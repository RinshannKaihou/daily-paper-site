> **一句话总结：** 针对企业级 RAG 的可靠性缺口，作者构建了覆盖 6 大领域、983 条专家标注样本的 EnterpriseRAG 基准，在检索噪声/知识缺失/事实冲突三种非理想场景下评测 13 个 LLM，发现"单约束满足率高但整体合规率低"的编排断层（最高 57pp 落差），并指出知识交互协议（拒答、冲突识别）是当前最大瓶颈。

> **TL;DR:** EnterpriseRAG is an enterprise RAG benchmark of 983 expert-validated instances across six domains that pairs complex multi-constraint instructions with three controlled non-ideal retrieval modes (noise, knowledge gaps, factual conflicts). Evaluating 13 SOTA LLMs reveals a 57-point orchestration gap — Loose IAS reaches 83.8% (Qwen3-235B-Thinking) while Strict IAS (all constraints satisfied) is only 26.8% — and that behavioral judgment under uncertainty, not formatting, is the dominant bottleneck: best rejection accuracy is just 42.7% (Claude-Opus-4.5) and best conflict recognition is 44.3% (DeepSeek-R1), both far below production-grade.

## 核心贡献

- **企业级 RAG 基准 EnterpriseRAG**：基于中国移动真实生产 RAG 日志构建，包含 6 个垂直领域（能源、医疗、法律、金融、党建、网络搜索）、491 条真实查询扩展为 983 条专家验证实例，平均每条 8.4 个约束，并提供可复现的脱敏数据生成流水线。
- **三维度复杂指令体系 + 三种非理想检索场景**：约束被组织为人格定义、输出约束、知识交互协议三个正交维度；同时系统化模拟检索噪声（45.5%）、知识缺失（23.1%）、事实冲突（31.4%）三种生产中真实存在的失败模式，弥补了现有基准"检索质量与指令遵循分开评估"的盲区。
- **新型评测指标体系**：区分 Loose IAS（单约束满足比例）与 Strict IAS（全约束同时满足的二元指标）以暴露组合性失效，并新增拒答准确率与冲突识别准确率，专门衡量安全关键场景下模型对"证据是否充分"的判断能力。
- **关键实证发现**：在 13 个 LLM 上发现 57pp 编排断层，最佳拒答仅 42.7%、冲突识别 <45%；推理增强模型显著优于标准模型（拒答 +18.1pp、冲突识别 +14.4pp），并证明显式知识交互协议能在全部 13 个模型上稳定提升冲突识别。

## Key Contributions

- **Enterprise-grade RAG benchmark**: EnterpriseRAG, built from China Mobile production RAG logs, comprises 983 expert-validated instances across six vertical domains (Energy, Medical, Legal, Financial, Party Building, Web Search), expanding 491 authentic queries with an average of 8.4 constraints per instance, plus a reproducible desensitized data-generation pipeline.
- **Three-dimensional constraint schema + three non-ideal retrieval modes**: Constraints are organized into Persona Definition, Output Constraints, and Knowledge Interaction Protocols; the benchmark systematically simulates Retrieval Noise (45.5%), Knowledge Gaps (23.1%), and Factual Conflicts (31.4%) — failure modes co-occurring in real deployments but evaluated separately by prior work (Table 1 shows EnterpriseRAG is the only benchmark ticking all six of Human-Curated, Vertical Domain, Natural User Queries, Complex Constraints, Negative Rejection, and Conflict).
- **Specialized evaluation framework**: Loose IAS (per-constraint satisfaction) vs Strict IAS (binary all-constraints-satisfied) exposes compositional adherence collapse, augmented with Rejection Accuracy and Conflict Recognition Accuracy to probe safety-critical judgment about evidence sufficiency.
- **Empirical insights**: Across 13 LLMs the authors find a 57pp orchestration gap, best rejection accuracy of only 42.7%, and conflict recognition below 45%; reasoning-enhanced variants substantially outperform standard ones, and explicit Knowledge Interaction Protocols reliably improve conflict handling across all 13 models.

## Methodology

**Benchmark construction.** Each instance is a triple ⟨q, I, D⟩: user query q, a fused multi-constraint instruction set I, and a retrieved document bundle D. The four-stage pipeline: (1) collect/filter 491 authentic queries; (2) synthesize I by fusing atomic constraints from a library (50–60 per task) and removing internal contradictions via conflict-detection and query-aware refinement; (3) retrieve documents with hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense) and assign one of three non-ideal modes; (4) two-stage expert verification of instruction–query consistency and context validity. The native language is Chinese; raw logs are desensitized (rule-based PII removal + manual review) and only the desensitized benchmark is released.

**Constraint taxonomy.** Three orthogonal dimensions:
- *Persona Definition* (Role, Audience) — virtual identity and target readers.
- *Output Constraints* (Format, Content, Negative, Ordering, Style) — structural/stylistic rules; 53.1% of all constraints.
- *Knowledge Interaction Protocols* (Conflict Handling, Knowledge Gap, Uncertainty Expression, Source Filtering, Citation) — behavioral rules for evidence handling; 29.2% of constraints and the identified bottleneck.

**Non-ideal retrieval scenarios.** Noisy Retrieval (topically similar but irrelevant documents, 100% natural), Knowledge Gaps (related but insufficient evidence, 5.3% natural / 94.7% augmented), Factual Conflicts (contradictory passages, 8.7% natural / 91.3% augmented). Sparse natural gaps/conflicts are augmented while preserving domain coherence; equivalence testing (Appendix B.2) confirms synthetic and natural conflicts are statistically indistinguishable on core robustness metrics.

**Metrics.** RAG quality: Faithfulness F = |C_sup|/|C_total|; Answer Coverage (Eq. 1, core claims weighted α=0.7 over supplementary). Instruction Adherence: Loose IAS (proportion of satisfied constraints) and Strict IAS (binary, all satisfied). Robustness: Rejection Accuracy (knowledge gaps) and Conflict Recognition Accuracy (factual conflicts). Structural constraints use rule-based checks; behavioral protocols use LLM-as-a-judge (Kimi-k2-thinking). Evaluator reliability validated via three-judge cross-comparison (ICC ≥ 0.809), human–LLM alignment (κ=0.77, 88% agreement), and conflict-recognition alignment (κ=0.93).

## Results & Highlights

- **57-point orchestration gap** on the noisy subset: Loose IAS reaches **83.8%** (Qwen3-235B-Thinking) but Strict IAS is only **26.8%** for the same model — models satisfy individual constraints yet fail holistic compliance.
- **Rejection accuracy (knowledge gaps) is alarmingly low**: the best model Claude-Opus-4.5 reaches only **42.7%**, while Qwen3-30B-Instruct achieves **6.6%** (hallucinating in 93.4% of unanswerable cases), exposing a pervasive helpfulness bias.
- **Conflict recognition is the hardest task**: top model DeepSeek-R1 detects only **44.3%** of contradictions, and GPT-4.1 just **18.5%** (Table 3); reasoning-enhanced models show a strong positive correlation between recognition and answer coverage (ρ = +0.90, p<0.01) whereas standard models show none (ρ = −0.50) (Figure 3).
- **Reasoning > standard variants**: Qwen3-235B-Thinking boosts rejection accuracy by **+18.1pp** and Strict IAS by **+6.1 to +10.6pp** (p<.01) over its Instruct twin; DeepSeek-R1 improves conflict recognition by **+14.4pp** over DeepSeek-V3.1 (Figure 5).
- **Scaling is non-linear for judgment, saturating for factuality**: within Qwen3-Thinking, Strict IAS rises **12.3% (8B) → 26.8% (235B)** while Faithfulness barely moves (64.8% → 67.1%), indicating orchestration is an emergent capability requiring substantial scale.
- **Explicit protocols help conflict handling universally**: Knowledge Interaction Protocols yield a large, significant gain in conflict recognition across **all 13 models**, but only modest (2/13 significant) gains in rejection accuracy — refusal requires harder evidence-sufficiency judgment than in-context contradiction spotting (Figure 6).
- **Knowledge Interaction Protocols are the dominant error source**: Figure 4 shows protocol constraints (especially Citation and Gap Identification) exhibit the highest failure rates and variance, and the largest reasoning gains concentrate precisely there.
- **Synthetic augmentation is ecologically valid**: synthetic vs. natural conflicts are statistically equivalent on Conflict Recognition (|Δ|=0.038, p=.120) and Strict IAS (|Δ|=0.004, p=.826), with 95% CIs within ±0.2 (Figure 8).
- **Domain difficulty tracks constraint density**: Medical (10.21 avg constraints) and Legal (8.61, highest protocol density 3.45) show lower Strict IAS than Web Search (Figure 9); Legal tasks are the hardest with 12 simultaneous constraints over 20 retrieved documents.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from Jiutian Research, China Mobile (Beijing) — a large telecom operator with genuine production RAG deployments, which lends strong ecological validity to the "enterprise" framing (the data is sourced from real operational logs rather than contrived scenarios). The paper is methodologically careful: it reports a three-judge cross-comparison (Kimi-k2-thinking, Qwen3-235B-Thinking, GPT-4o) with ICC ≥ 0.809, a human annotation study (n=150, IAA κ=0.85), and equivalence testing justifying the synthetic augmentation. The native language is Chinese and the benchmark is desensitized before release. The work is positioned clearly against prior benchmarks via Table 1 and is honest about the limitations of LLM-as-judge evaluation. No peer-review status is indicated (arXiv preprint, v1).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine. The core insight — that *compositional* instruction adherence (Strict IAS) collapses even when per-constraint adherence (Loose IAS) is high — and the joint evaluation of complex instructions with three orthogonal non-ideal retrieval modes fills a real gap left by FollowRAG (synthetic injection, clean contexts) and RARE (robustness without complex constraints). The Loose/Strict IAS split is a simple but effective diagnostic.
- **Rigor**: Strong on evaluation reliability (cross-judge ICC, human κ, McNemar/χ² with multiple-testing notes, bootstrap CIs). The synthetic-vs-natural equivalence test (Appendix B.2) directly addresses the obvious critique that augmented conflicts are artificial. One weakness: the protocol-presence analysis (Figure 6) is explicitly acknowledged as *observational, not randomized* — protocol presence is confounded with query/instruction properties, so the causal claim that "explicit protocols improve conflict handling" is weaker than presented; the authors mitigate this with domain-level breakdowns (D.1) but do not fully resolve it.
- **Reproducibility**: Partial. The desensitized benchmark and generation pipeline are promised "upon publication," but at submission time neither is public, and the raw logs (understandably) cannot be released. Independent verification therefore depends on the authors honoring the release commitment. Evaluation prompts and constraint libraries are documented in appendices, and all 13 models are named with versions, which is good.
- **Significance**: High for the RAG-evaluation subfield and for practitioners; the finding that even the best reasoning models recognize <45% of conflicts and refuse correctly <43% of the time is actionable and sobering. The China-centric domain mix (Party Building, Energy ERP) is unusual and valuable for diversity, though it may limit direct comparability with Western enterprise settings.
- **Red flags / overclaiming**: (1) The headline "27% meet all requirements simultaneously" / 57pp gap is computed on the *noisy* subset and on the single best model's Strict IAS — the abstract generalizes it as "only 27% of responses meet all requirements," which is a slight overstatement given it is the best-case model on one of three subsets. (2) Heavy reliance on a single reasoning LLM (Kimi-k2-thinking) as the primary behavioral-protocol judge introduces a known bias risk; the authors mitigate with sampling checks (κ=0.77) but Strict IAS alignment is the weakest dimension (κ=0.68), precisely where the central 57pp claim is measured. (3) The comparison in Table 2 marks "best/second-best" across models but the three non-ideal subsets have very different n (447/227/309) and the Strict IAS column conflates models with different numbers of protocol constraints per instance, which could be surfaced more transparently.

## Strengths

- First benchmark to **jointly** stress complex multi-constraint instructions and three non-ideal retrieval modes, capturing the compounding complexity of real enterprise workflows that prior work studied in isolation.
- The **Loose vs Strict IAS** distinction is an elegant, model-agnostic diagnostic that cleanly isolates the compositional/orchestration bottleneck from raw instruction-following ability.
- Exceptionally thorough **evaluator-reliability validation**: three-judge ICC, human IAA, LLM–human κ, and equivalence testing on synthetic data — a standard many RAG benchmarks do not meet.
- Practically useful, actionable findings: explicit protocols reliably help conflict handling; reasoning helps judgment but not factuality; scaling is non-linear for protocol adherence — these directly inform deployment and prompt design.
- Honest about the observational nature of the protocol analysis and the limitations of LLM-as-judge, rather than burying them.

## Limitations

- **Text-only RAG**: no multimodal contexts (charts, images, tables inside PDFs), which are common in real enterprise retrieval (e.g., financial reports).
- **Single-judge bias risk**: the primary behavioral-protocol judge is one reasoning LLM (Kimi-k2-thinking); while cross-judge checks are strong, Strict IAS (the central metric) has the lowest human–LLM alignment (κ=0.68).
- **Binary Strict IAS** is stringent and coarse; nuance in partial constraint satisfaction is lost, and the authors acknowledge future metrics could explore graded semantic satisfaction.
- **Observational protocol analysis** (Figure 6) cannot establish causality for the protocol→conflict-recognition claim without randomization.
- **Domain and language bias**: Chinese-language, China-Mobile-centric domains (Party Building, Energy) may not transfer cleanly to other enterprise contexts; release of the desensitized data is pending "upon publication."
- Small per-subset sample sizes for gaps (n=227) and conflicts (n=309), and very few *natural* conflicts (n=27) anchor the equivalence claims — the augmented majority dominates.

## Relevance

与企业级 LLM 应用的指令遵循与鲁棒性评测高度相关：该工作提出的 Loose/Strict IAS 指标体系与"知识交互协议是核心瓶颈"的结论，直接对 RAG 系统的 prompt 设计、拒答策略与冲突处理协议提供可落地的工程指导，也属于当前 cs.CL/cs.AI 中 RAG 评测的前沿方向。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would **randomized insertion** of Knowledge Interaction Protocols (rather than observational presence) confirm the causal gain in conflict recognition, or does the effect depend on correlated instruction properties?
- How do the Loose/Strict IAS gap and rejection accuracy change under **multimodal retrieval** (tables/charts in financial and medical PDFs), which this benchmark explicitly excludes?
- Is the **helpfulness bias** (93.4% hallucination on unanswerable queries) reducible via targeted RLHF/DPO on protocol-style constraints, or does it require architectural changes to evidence-sufficiency estimation?
- Can a **graded Strict IAS** (partial-credit semantic matching) reveal whether models fail "all-or-nothing" or accumulate small per-constraint deficits, and would that change the model ranking?
- How stable are the findings under **open-weight judges** other than Kimi/Qwen3/GPT-4o, and does the κ=0.68 Strict-IAS alignment degrade enough on borderline cases to reshape the 57pp headline?
