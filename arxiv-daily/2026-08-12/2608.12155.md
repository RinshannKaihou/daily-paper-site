> **一句话总结：** 通过 DDIM 反演深度扫描、频域互换、潜空间有效维度三方分析，论证视觉基础模型（DINOv3 / MetaCLIP2 等）之所以能稳健检测扩散生成图像，靠的不是语义缺陷也不是高频伪影，而是扩散生成过程在低-中频段留下的"分布性"差异——且再生图像的有效维度与方差随反演深度单调下降，说明扩散模型并未完整复现真实数据的变异性。

> **TL;DR:** This paper explains *why* frozen vision foundation models (DINOv3 ViT-7B/16, MetaCLIP2, OpenCLIP) make such effective and robust detectors of diffusion-generated images, an open question despite their state-of-the-art AUC. Using a DDIM-inversion depth sweep on 1,000 MS-COCO images regenerated by SD 1.4 and SD 2.1, the authors show the detector score rises from ~0 to ~1.0 *before* PSNR/LPIPS show any semantic change; a frequency-swap experiment on k=0.4T SD 2.1 hybrids proves the decision is driven by low-to-mid frequency components (injecting just the [0, 0.05] band flips fake→real); and a latent-space analysis shows normalized effective dimensionality and variance drop as inversion depth grows. The headline cross-generator result is that a DINOv3 ViT-7B/16+LoRA linear head trained only on SD 1.4 reaches average AUC 99.5 across ten generators including the very recent pixel-space PixelDiT (Nov 2025) and Scale-RAE (Jan 2026), outperforming OpenCLIP's 84.9 by ~14.6 AUC points.

## 核心贡献

- **DDIM 反演深度的"语义保持扫描"协议：** 给定一张真实图像，对其施加不同深度 k 的 DDIM 反演再生成，得到一系列在像素层不同但语义保持一致的合成副本。这样把"语义失败"和"非语义分布痕迹"这两个变量解耦——在 k≤0.6T 时 PSNR/LPIPS 仍接近原图，但检测器分数已大幅上升，从而把检测器的判据与语义脱钩。
- **频域互换的因果式证据：** 对真实图与其 k=0.4T SD 2.1 再生图在频域做低/高频互换构成混合图。结果显示 DINOv3、DINOv3+LoRA、MetaCLIP2 三类检测器的判据几乎完全由低频段驱动——仅在 [0, 0.05] 的极窄低频带注入"真实"成分就能把判定从 fake 翻转为 real，约在 0.25 处饱和。
- **潜空间有效维度/方差的量化分析：** 引入有效维度度（基于协方差矩阵特征值的熵，Roy & Vetterli 2007）与潜方差，在 Nb×s×s（s∈{1,2,4,8,16}）的块上计算。再生样本的归一化有效维度随 k 单调下降、约在 k≈0.75T 处达到最低，潜方差也单调下降——证明扩散生成过程把内容压缩到更少的主方向上，并未复现真实数据的全部变异性。
- **覆盖多基础模型的可迁移性表格：** 仅在 SD 1.4（GenImage）上训练一个线性头，跨十种生成器测试，DINOv3 ViT-7B/16+LoRA 平均 AUC 达 99.5，ViT-7B/16 达 98.8，MetaCLIP2 达 96.9；即便架构截然不同的像素空间 PixelDiT（2025-11）和 Representation Autoencoder 的 Scale-RAE（2026-01）也分别被 ViT-7B/16 检出 AUC=100 和 100，排除了训练数据泄漏的解释。
- **抗退化鲁棒性的统一解释：** DINOv3 ViT-7B/16 在模糊、缩放、JPEG、AWGN 四类退化下，平衡准确率在很宽的参数范围内保持稳定（如 JPEG QF=55 仍稳定）；SD 1.4 VAE laundering 仅把真阴性率从 97.9% 降到 95.5%。这些证据共同说明：检测器依赖的是中低频的深层分布差异，所以高频抹除型退化对其影响很小。

## Key Contributions

- **A DDIM-inversion-depth probing protocol** that decouples semantic change from non-semantic distributional traces. By regenerating a real image from intermediate timesteps k<T, the authors obtain a stream of images that remain semantically identical to the original (PSNR/LPIPS nearly flat for k<0.5T) yet already flip the detector score — directly showing the decision is not driven by semantic failures or visible artifacts.
- **A causal-style frequency-swap experiment** that localizes the discriminative cues. By exchanging low- and high-frequency components between a real image and its k=0.4T SD 2.1 regeneration, the paper shows that DINOv3, DINOv3+LoRA, and MetaCLIP2 decisions are governed almost entirely by the low-frequency band: replacing just the [0, 0.05] band of a fake image with the real one shifts the prediction from fake to real.
- **Latent-space effective-dimensionality and variance measures** that quantify the distributional gap. Normalized effective dimensionality (Roy–Vetterli entropy of covariance eigenvalues) drops monotonically with k to a minimum near k≈0.75T, and averaged latent variance (Nb×4×4 blocks) drops as k grows — empirical evidence that diffusion regeneration concentrates samples in a lower-dimensional, lower-variance region than real data occupies.
- **A cross-generator generalization benchmark** spanning ten generators including the very recent pixel-space PixelDiT (Nov 2025) and Scale-RAE (Jan 2026). A simple linear head on frozen DINOv3 ViT-7B/16+LoRA trained only on SD 1.4 reaches average AUC 99.5, near-perfect on most generators, which cannot be attributed to training-data leakage given the architecture and release-date differences.
- **A unified robustness account**: because the cues live in the low-to-mid frequency range, the detector stays largely stable under JPEG (down to QF=55), resizing, AWGN, and VAE laundering (TNR 97.9%→95.5%), explaining *why* foundation-model detectors generalize where high-frequency-artifact detectors collapse.

## Methodology

The paper has four analysis pillars, all built around frozen vision foundation models followed by a linear binary head trained on GenImage's SD 1.4 subset (162K fakes) versus ImageNet reals (162K), 2 epochs, AdamW, lr=1e-3, batch 128, inputs resized to 224×224 with no augmentation. Feature extractors compared include OpenCLIP, MetaCLIP1, MetaCLIP2, DINOv3 (ConvNeXt-B/L and ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16, ViT-7B/16), and a LoRA-adapted ViT-7B/16. Testing uses real images from the RAISE dataset (to avoid dataset bias) and ten synthetic sources (SD 1.4, SD 2, SDXL, SD3, Flux, DALL·E 3, Firefly, Midjourney, Scale-RAE, PixelDiT).

**Pillar 1 — DDIM-inversion depth sweep.** For each of 1,000 pristine 512×512 MS-COCO images, DDIM inversion is run to various depths k∈[0, T] in latent space, then DDIM generation regenerates the image. At small/intermediate k the regeneration is near-identical to the real image (verified by PSNR/LPIPS); at k≈T it deviates semantically. The detector score is recorded as a function of k for SD 1.4 and SD 2.1 (50 steps, guidance scale 1, text-conditioned on the MS-COCO caption). Two diffusion models are used because higher guidance scales degrade inversion semantic consistency.

**Pillar 2 — Frequency-swap analysis.** For each real image and its k=0.4T SD 2.1 regeneration, hybrid images are built in the Fourier domain by combining low frequencies of one source with high frequencies of the other across a sweep of cutoff frequencies fc∈[0, 0.5]. The analysis is restricted to samples whose regeneration is already classified fake >50% of the time, to ensure a meaningful starting signal. The detector prediction is plotted versus fc to reveal which band carries the decision.

**Pillar 3 — Latent-space analysis.** Effective dimensionality ED = exp(−Σ p_j ln p_j) with p_j = λ_j / Σλ_i is computed on the covariance of latent blocks of spatial size s∈{1,2,4,8,16} (dimension D=s²·Nb), normalized to the k=0 value for cross-block comparison. Averaged block variance (Nb×4×4) is also reported. Both are plotted as functions of inversion timestep k.

**Pillar 4 — Robustness characterization.** A DINOv3 ViT-7B/16 detector is stressed under Gaussian blur (std 0–7), resizing (50%–200%), JPEG (QF 100→55), and AWGN (std 0–25), reporting TPR, TNR, and balanced accuracy with 95% confidence intervals. A VAE-laundering test (passing real images through SD 1.4's VAE) measures the high-frequency-only contribution.

## Results & Highlights

- **Cross-generator AUC (Table 1, trained only on SD 1.4, tested on 10 generators):** DINOv3 ViT-7B/16+LoRA achieves **average AUC 99.5** with perfect 100.0 on SD 1.4, SDXL, DALL·E 3, Scale-RAE, and PixelDiT, versus OpenCLIP's average AUC **84.9** (a **+14.6 AUC gap**) and MetaCLIP2's **96.9**. On the hardest generator, Firefly, ViT-7B/16+LoRA reaches **97.4** vs OpenCLIP's **89.5** (+7.9) and MetaCLIP2's **92.9** (+4.5). On pixel-space PixelDiT, ViT-7B/16 already hits **100.0** without LoRA.
- **DDIM-depth detector response (Fig. 5, 1,000 MS-COCO images):** For SD 1.4 and SD 2.1 the detector prediction rises steeply with k *before* PSNR/LPIPS degrade. On the illustrative example of Fig. 1 the score climbs from **0.14 (real)** to **0.47 at k=0.04T, 0.54 at 0.16T, 0.60 at 0.40T, 0.71 at 0.60T, and 1.00 at 1.00T**, crossing the 0.5 fake threshold already at k≈0.04T despite near-identical semantics. The VAE-laundered-only version scores just **0.20**, confirming the VAE alone is a weak cue.
- **Frequency-swap decision driver (Fig. 7, k=0.4T SD 2.1):** Across DINOv3, DINOv3+LoRA, and MetaCLIP2 the prediction is governed by the low-frequency band. When low frequencies come from the fake and highs from the real, the prediction saturates to fake by **fc≈0.25**; when lows come from real and highs from fake, it drops toward real. Injecting real low-frequency content only in the narrow **[0, 0.05]** band already flips the decision from fake to real — the tightest localization of the discriminative cue reported.
- **Latent-space collapse (Fig. 8):** Normalized effective dimensionality decreases monotonically with k to a minimum near **k≈0.75T** for both SD 1.4 and SD 2.1, across all block sizes Nb×1×1 through Nb×16×16, then partially recovers but never returns to the k=0 baseline. Averaged latent variance (Nb×4×4) likewise drops steadily with k, with SD 1.4 starting higher (~47) and SD 2.1 lower (~41) at k=0 and both converging downward.
- **Robustness to high-frequency removal (Fig. 2, DINOv3 ViT-7B/16):** Balanced accuracy stays nearly flat under JPEG compression down to **QF=55** and resizing across **50%–200%**; significant TPR degradation appears only under strong blur (std≥5) and strong AWGN (std≥10). VAE laundering of real images with SD 1.4's VAE lowers the true-negative rate only from **97.9% to 95.5%** (−2.4 points), confirming the high-frequency VAE traces are not the primary cue.

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors (Davide Cozzolino, Giovanni Poggi, Luisa Verdoliva) are from the Image Processing/Vision group at University Federico II of Naples, one of the most active academic groups in image forensics and AI-generated-image detection. Verdoliva and Cozzolino are co-authors of the CLIP-based detector line of work (Cozzolino et al. CVPR-W 2024, ref [13]), the bias-free training paradigm (Guillaro et al. CVPR 2025, ref [23]), and the GenImage benchmark ecosystem, so the paper sits squarely inside the authors' own research program — they are explaining a phenomenon they helped create and benchmark. This gives them deep command of the experimental landscape and the citation set (64 references, well-curated, including the 2026 contemporaneous work on DINOv3-based detection [26,64]). The writing is measured: claims are hedged ("our results suggest", "this is consistent with"), the limitations section is explicit, and the analysis is presented as insight rather than as a new SOTA detector — there is no leader-board chasing, which is appropriate for an interpretability paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High and genuine. The DDIM-inversion-depth probing protocol is an elegant new tool for isolating non-semantic traces, and the frequency-swap experiment is the most direct causal-style test to date of *which band* foundation-model detectors actually use. The convergence of three independent analyses (depth sweep, frequency swap, latent ED) on the same low-to-mid-frequency conclusion is more convincing than any single one.
- **Rigor:** Solid but with real gaps. The frequency-swap analysis is explicitly restricted to "samples for which the regenerated image is classified as fake more than 50% of the total" (Section 4.3) — this selection on the dependent variable could exaggerate the apparent dominance of the low-frequency cue, and no sensitivity analysis on the threshold is given. The "non-semantic" claim leans on PSNR/LPIPS, but LPIPS is known to be insensitive to certain distributional shifts; a perceptual/multimodal judge or a human study would tighten the semantic-identity argument. No formal statistical significance testing is reported for the key crossover claims (e.g., the [0,0.05] flip), despite 95% CIs being drawn.
- **Missing negative control:** The paper never runs the *same* DDIM-depth / frequency-swap protocol on a deliberately high-frequency-reliant detector (e.g., a spectral-peak detector or NPR-style method) to show what the opposite curve looks like. Such a contrast would make the "foundation models differ because they use low frequencies" claim falsifiable rather than illustrative.
- **Scope of the mechanistic claim vs. the detection table:** Table 1 covers ten generators including the architecturally distinct pixel-space PixelDiT and the Representation-Autoencoder Scale-RAE, but the *entire mechanistic analysis* (DDIM sweep, frequency swap, latent ED) is run on only **two latent-space Stable Diffusion variants (SD 1.4, SD 2.1)**. The authors flag this honestly as future work, but it means the central "why" explanation is not actually verified on the very generators (pixel-space, non-VAE) that would most stringently test whether the low-frequency-distributional-gap story generalizes. The detection-AUC numbers on PixelDiT/Scale-RAE are correlational evidence only.
- **Latent-space confound:** The effective-dimensionality and variance drop could in principle be a generic property of passing an image through *any* lossy bottleneck (a JPEG codec, a VAE, an autoencoder), not specific to diffusion generation. A control at matched reconstruction error — e.g., VAE-laundered-only samples at comparable PSNR — is missing, so the latent collapse is attributed to diffusion without isolating diffusion from the autoencoder.
- **Reproducibility:** Reasonable. Models are off-the-shelf (OpenCLIP, MetaCLIP1/2, DINOv3 public weights + LoRA), datasets are public (GenImage, ImageNet, RAISE, MS-COCO), and the DDIM-inversion pipeline uses standard SD 1.4/2.1 checkpoints with stated hyperparameters (50 steps, guidance 1). However, no code release is mentioned in the body, and the exact sample-selection rule for the frequency experiment and the LoRA rank/configuration are not fully specified, which would hinder exact replication.
- **Significance:** Conceptually important for the forensic community — it reframes "foundation models see semantics" as "foundation models see low-frequency distributional mismatch," which has direct implications for adversarial robustness and for designing the next generation of laundering attacks. But it is an *understanding* paper, not a new detector, so practical impact depends on whether follow-up work turns the insight into better detectors or attackers.
- **Red flags:** None severe. No overclaiming of a new SOTA detector; the AUC table is presented as background motivation, not as the contribution. The main risk is interpretive overreach — readers may take "low-frequency distributional gap" as a fully characterized mechanism when the authors are careful to say the mathematical origin in the diffusion process remains an open problem.

## Strengths

- The DDIM-inversion-depth sweep is a genuinely novel and clean experimental design that separates semantic from non-semantic cues more rigorously than prior reconstruction-error methods (DIRE et al.), because it enforces semantic identity by construction at low k.
- Three independent analyses (depth sweep, frequency swap, latent effective dimensionality) triangulate the same conclusion, which is much stronger than any single line of evidence.
- Tests the effect across multiple foundation models (OpenCLIP, MetaCLIP1/2, four DINOv3 backbones plus LoRA), showing it is not idiosyncratic to one architecture.
- Includes the very recent pixel-space PixelDiT and Scale-RAE generators in the detection benchmark, preempting the "data leakage" critique that dogs cross-generator detection papers.
- Honest limitations section: explicitly names the two-generator scope of the mechanistic analysis and the absence of a mathematical characterization of the traces as open problems.

## Limitations

- The core mechanistic claims rest on only two latent-space Stable Diffusion models (SD 1.4, SD 2.1); pixel-space and non-VAE generators are detected well in Table 1 but never put through the DDIM / frequency / latent analyses, so the "why" is not verified for the hardest architectural cases.
- The frequency-swap experiment selects only on samples already classified fake >50% of the time, a potential selection bias; no robustness check on this threshold is provided.
- No negative control running the same protocol on a known high-frequency-reliant detector, which would make the contrast falsifiable.
- "Semantic identity" relies on PSNR/LPIPS, both of which can miss distributional shifts a human or VLM judge might catch.
- The latent-space collapse is not decoupled from the VAE/autoencoder bottleneck — a matched-PSNR VAE-only control is absent.
- No code-release statement in the paper body, and some key experimental details (LoRA rank, exact frequency-filter shape) are under-specified for exact replication.
- Statistical significance testing is absent despite narrow confidence-interval claims on the frequency crossover.

## Relevance

对理解大规模视觉基础模型的表征性质、以及扩散生成模型在数据分布层面的固有缺陷都有直接意义——这正契合"模型可解释性 / 生成模型分析 / 合成数据可靠性"这类研究方向，也为评估合成训练数据、检测对抗鲁棒性、设计更可解释的取证方法提供了新的机理层面依据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the low-to-mid-frequency distributional gap survive for pixel-space diffusion (PixelDiT) and Representation-Autoencoder (Scale-RAE) when put through the same DDIM-depth and frequency-swap protocol, or do those generators leave a spectrally different signature that the foundation model exploits for a different reason?
- What is the mathematical origin of the low-frequency mismatch in the diffusion process — is it the prior-horizon noise mismatch (finite forward process vs. Gaussian init), the score-model approximation error, the VAE decoder, or an interaction of all three? A controlled decomposition (e.g., oracle-score vs. learned-score, with and without the VAE) could isolate the source.
- Can the frequency-swap result be turned into an adversarial attack — i.e., does replacing only the [0, 0.05] band of a fake image with a real image's low frequencies reliably evade the DINOv3 detector at scale, and does it transfer to MetaCLIP2 and OpenCLIP?
- Is the effective-dimensionality collapse specific to diffusion, or does any lossy reconstruction bottleneck (JPEG, VAE-only, neural compression) produce a similar ED drop at matched reconstruction PSNR?
- How does the detector behave on hybrid real/generated pipelines (e.g., real photo partially inpainted by a diffusion model) — does the low-frequency cue localize to the inpainted region, and can it support localization rather than only image-level real/fake decisions?
- Would adding a deliberately high-frequency-reliant baseline (spectral-peak or NPR detector) through the same protocol sharpen the contrast and make the "foundation models are different" claim quantitatively falsifiable?
