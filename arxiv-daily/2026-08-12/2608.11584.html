01

Methodology

Each instance is a triple ⟨query, multi-constraint instruction set, retrieved documents⟩ built in four stages: (1) collect 491 authentic China Mobile production queries; (2) fuse atomic constraints from a 50–60-entry library, removing internal contradictions via conflict-detection; (3) retrieve with hybrid BM25 + dense, then inject one of three non-ideal modes; (4) two-stage expert verification (instruction–query consistency + context validity). Native language is Chinese; logs are desensitized before release.

Constraints span three orthogonal dimensions — Persona, Output (53.1%), and Knowledge Interaction Protocols (29.2%, the identified bottleneck). Three retrieval failure modes are simulated: noise, knowledge gaps, and factual conflicts. The diagnostic core is the Loose vs Strict IAS split — Loose measures per-constraint satisfaction; Strict is binary all-constraints-satisfied. Robustness is probed via Rejection Accuracy and Conflict Recognition Accuracy, with LLM-as-judge validated by three-judge ICC ≥ 0.809 and human κ = 0.77.

03

Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths

  • First joint stress test of complex multi-constraint instructions with three non-ideal retrieval modes — capturing compounding complexity prior work studied in isolation.
  • The Loose vs Strict IAS split is an elegant, model-agnostic diagnostic isolating the orchestration bottleneck from raw instruction-following.
  • Exceptional evaluator-reliability validation: three-judge ICC, human IAA, LLM–human κ, and synthetic-data equivalence testing.
  • Actionable findings — explicit protocols help conflict handling; reasoning helps judgment not factuality; scaling is non-linear.

− Limitations

  • Text-only RAG — no multimodal contexts (tables, charts, images in PDFs), common in real enterprise retrieval.
  • Single-judge bias risk — behavioral protocols judged by one reasoning LLM; Strict IAS has the lowest human–LLM alignment (κ = 0.68).
  • Binary Strict IAS is stringent and coarse; partial-credit nuance is lost.
  • Observational protocol analysis (Figure 6) cannot establish causality without randomization.
  • Domain/language bias — Chinese, China-Mobile-centric domains; desensitized data release is pending "upon publication."

Open Questions

For further exploration

Q1Would randomized insertion of Knowledge Interaction Protocols confirm the causal gain in conflict recognition — or does it depend on correlated instruction properties?
Q2How does the IAS gap shift under multimodal retrieval (tables/charts in financial and medical PDFs), which this benchmark excludes?
Q3Is the helpfulness bias (93.4% hallucination on unanswerable queries) reducible via targeted RLHF/DPO, or does it need architectural changes to evidence-sufficiency estimation?
Q4Would a graded Strict IAS reveal whether models fail "all-or-nothing" or accumulate small per-constraint deficits — and would that change the ranking?
