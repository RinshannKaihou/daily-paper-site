End-to-end research-paper generation, done as 13 composable skills inside an existing coding assistant — not a new standalone agent platform.

Why this matters

A practical, infrastructure-light blueprint for trustworthy long-horizon research agents — making the full research-to-paper loop auditable and fabrication-resistant without a heavyweight platform.

Critical edge

Citation validity ≠ correctness. Every headline number is self-measured on self-selected topics — no peer-review or expert check that the claims are actually true or novel.

Methodologystages 0–8

A lightweight orchestrator (ts-paper) runs Stages 0–8 inside Claude Code, reusing the assistant's native file, tool, and code-execution abilities — no orchestration server, scheduler, or graph DB. Stage 0 routes input into one of two integrity modes: Proposal Mode (results unobserved, values stay unspecified) or Data-Aware Mode (every quantitative claim traces to supplied data); that mode propagates through every downstream stage.

Stages 1–8 plan a structured blueprint, cite references verified by DOI/arXiv into BibTeX plus a claim–citation map (references are found, not recalled), write and refine the LaTeX manuscript, then assemble it under a venue template into a compilable project. Stage 5 runs isolated adversarial reviews where each issue must quote a passage and survive three checks before it is returned. Quantitative figures plot directly from measured data; method diagrams are rasterized, then reconstructed in editable HTML with iterative render-and-compare before vector-PDF export.

The system keeps model judgment for argumentation but delegates every mechanical check to deterministic scripts, and bounds a named Self-Refutation Loop to 7 cycles before logging a failure report and trying a new idea — never rewriting a failure as success.

Strengths+

  • Infrastructure-light. Skills inside Claude Code with no orchestration server / scheduler / graph DB — a real adoption and reproducibility advantage over AI Scientist-family systems.
  • Judgment-vs-execution split + six deterministic gates make most QA directly checkable and model-independent — a clean, reusable design pattern.
  • Evidence-precommitment (freezing the result-table structure before results are seen) is a cheap, principled anti-hacking mechanism borrowed from clinical preregistration.
  • Naming and bounding the Self-Refutation Loop, and refusing to rewrite failed trajectories as successes, directly counters the "forced success narrative" failure mode.
  • Pre-registered protocol, external citation verification, disclosed costs, and explicit positioning caveats raise the credibility bar for the subfield.

Limitations—

  • No controlled re-run of baselines. The head-to-head mixes system, backbone, topic set, and pricing era — suggestive, not decisive.
  • Tiny probe/sample sizes: 36 fabrication probes; review precision over just 57 issues. Generalization to broader hallucination/issue distributions is unestablished.
  • Unvalidated scientific value. Citation existence and figure editability are artifact metrics — whether claims are true or novel is not assessed.
  • Single-model, single-platform test (Claude in Claude Code). The model-/assistant-agnostic design claim is asserted, not demonstrated.
  • Arbitrary Self-Refutation bound (7) with no published failure/restart statistics — the amortized cost of "try a new idea" recovery is opaque.

Questions for further exploration

  1. How often is the 7-cycle Self-Refutation bound actually hit, and what is the end-to-end success rate — including failed trajectories in the amortized cost?
  2. Would the 14%→92% fabrication-detection gain hold on a larger, independently constructed probe corpus, or does it reflect the specific 36-probe distribution?
  3. Can the semantic claim-labeling be grounded by automated retrieval against cited source text, reducing reliance on the same model that wrote the claim?
  4. How does performance change across non-Claude backbones and non-Claude-Code assistants — is the skill decomposition robust to weaker tool-use?
  5. What is the HTML figure-reconstruction fallback rate, and how many match iterations reach an accepted vector PDF on average?
  6. Do the generated papers survive blind expert peer review, and how does expert-rated claim correctness compare to human-written preprints on the same topics?
