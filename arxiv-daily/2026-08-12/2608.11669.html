Rubric Dropout: A Simple Way to Mitigate Reward Hacking in Rubric-as-Reward RL

Minglai Yang, Xinyu Guo, Utkarsh Tyagi, Mian Zhang et al.
reinforcement-learning reward-hacking rubric-as-reward GRPO LLM-posttraining
Key finding

Rubric Dropout drops random rubric criteria each step, stopping the policy from exploiting a fixed proxy judge.

Key metric
+7.0pts

Why this matters

Directly relevant to LLM post-training & alignment: a zero-cost, one-line, plug-in regularizer for any GRPO / rubric-as-reward pipeline.

⚠ Critical edge

Every configuration is a single seed — the reported error bars are within-run checkpoint variation, not the across-seed effect size the headline +1–2 pt Medical gain rests on.

Methodology

SetupGRPO post-training of Qwen3-8B (and 4B) on rubric-graded prompts. Each rubric is K weighted criteria; one judge call returns binary verdicts, and the standard reward is the clipped weighted fraction satisfied.

Measuring OOD hackingEvery 20 steps the policy is graded twice — by the training proxy (gpt-4o-mini) and by a stronger cross-family gold judge (claude-sonnet-4-6). The signal is the divergence: proxy rising while gold falls is impossible under a mere fixed bias.

Rubric DropoutEach step, drop a random f-fraction of positive-weight criteria (always keep ≥3, never touch the protected safety set), compute reward on the remainder. Evaluation always uses the full rubric.

Group-shared maskOne mask per rollout group, re-seeded per step via SHA256(instance_id, step) — no cross-worker communication. Proposition 1 proves any mask-only normalizer cancels in GRPO's standardized advantage; Observation 1 shows the net effect is a variance regularizer, largest exactly when a response's edge hinges on a single high-weight criterion.

Authors & credibility. Scale AI (lead/corresponding Minglai Yang) with University of Arizona & UT Dallas. Explicitly marked "Work in Progress". Methodology is unusually careful about confounds: it refuses to read absolute proxy−gold gaps, uses window means + matched-checkpoint win counts, and ships a formal appendix (Proposition 1, Observation 1). Gold judge is deliberately cross-family vs the proxy to avoid shared blind spots.

Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • Divergence-based two-judge protocol is reusable beyond this paper.
  • Mitigation is genuinely minimal: one line, one hyperparameter, zero extra judge calls, with a formal correctness proof.
  • Two independent domain pairs and two model sizes give internal cross-checks.
  • Criterion-level breakdowns make "gold went up" concrete and inspectable.
  • Honest POW3R negative result and unresolved-mechanism admission raise credibility.

Limitations

  • Single seed — bars are checkpoint variation, not effect-size confidence.
  • Gold judge is not ground truth; distribution-dependent bias can't be ruled out.
  • "No in-domain cost" is measured on the training set only.
  • Narrow scope: one policy family, two domains, one algorithm (GRPO).
  • Mechanism unresolved — anti-co-adaptation vs implicit regularization undecided.
  • Self-described "Work in Progress"; numbers may change.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the effect survive seed replication, and how big is across-seed variance vs the +1–7 pt deltas?
  2. What does the Section 6 two-epoch frontier test show — the decisive experiment on mechanism?
  3. How does dropout interact with orthogonal mitigations (ensembles, ODIN, OnlineRubrics, GDPO)? Does stacking double-count variance?
  4. Does it still help when proxy & gold are same family, or with human gold grading?
  5. Would a faithful, category-aware, EMA-smoothed POW3R still lose out of distribution?
  6. Does the 30–50% sweet spot hold for rubrics with very few criteria? At what K is dropout infeasible?
