> **一句话总结：** 本文通过 pass@K/avg@K 的测试时扩展视角系统分析在策略蒸馏(OPD)，发现 OPD 只是提升了采样效率(让已会做的题更容易被采样到)，并未真正扩展学生的能力边界——在大 K 下学生反而被原始 base 模型反超，作者据此称其为"虚假的蒸馏 (illusory distillation)"。

> **TL;DR:** This analysis paper re-examines on-policy distillation (OPD) via test-time scaling, sweeping the sampling budget K and measuring pass@K and avg@K across three student-teacher settings (Qwen3, Skywork, JustRL) on AMC2023 and AIME2024/2025/2026. OPD-trained students win at small K and on avg@K (e.g., Qwen3 AMC23 pass@1 45.4% vs base 32.3%, +13.1pp) but are matched or beaten by the pre-OPD base at K=1024 (AMC23 95.0% vs base 100.0%, -5.0pp; AIME24 53.3% vs 70.0%, -16.7pp). Problem-level accounting shows OPD forgets more previously-solvable problems than it newly learns, and unlike off-policy distillation (DeepSeek-R1-Distill) it never expands the capability boundary — the authors dub this an "illusory distillation."

## 核心贡献

- **用测试时扩展 (test-time scaling) 重新审视 OPD**：通过同时考察 pass@K（预算相关的可达性，大 K 探测能力边界）与 avg@K（采样效率），首次把"OPD 到底扩展了能力还是只提升了采样效率"拆解成两个可独立测量的指标。
- **揭示"采样效率↑ vs 能力边界→/↓"的权衡**：在 Qwen3、Skywork、JustRL 三种学生-教师设置、四个数学基准上，OPD 学生在小 K 上全面胜过 base，但在大 K（如 K=1024）被 base 反超或追平。
- **问题级求解性 (solvability) 分析**：把题目分为 retained/learned/forgotten 三类，发现遗忘 (forgotten) 的可解题目多于新学会 (learned) 的题目——OPD 在能力边界层面是净负的，收益来自让"本就能解"的题更易解。
- **训练动态刻画**：追踪 pass@K 随 OPD 步数变化，发现小 K 持续提升而大 K 早早（约 80 步）就出现不稳定下降，印证了效率与边界的此消彼长。
- **横向对比说明"虚假蒸馏"独特性**：与 off-policy 蒸馏 (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B) 不同——后者在大小 K 上全面超越 base 并真正扩展能力边界，而 OPD 即便引入更强教师也未能做到。
- **多种改进型 OPD 同病相怜**：EOPD、ExOPD、Direct-OPD、纯 forward-KL 变体在测试时扩展下表现一致——小 K 和 avg@K 提升，大 K 仍未扩展边界（仅 forward-KL 在 AIME2026 上例外）。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a **test-time-scaling diagnostic** for OPD: jointly measuring pass@K (reachability; large-K ≈ capability boundary) and avg@K (sampling efficiency) lets the community separate "made correct paths easier to sample" from "genuinely learned new reasoning."
- Demonstrates a **sampling-efficiency vs capability-boundary trade-off** across three independent student-teacher settings (Qwen3 1.7B→4B-GRPO, R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B→Skywork-OR1-Math-7B, R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B→JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B) and four benchmarks (AMC2023, AIME 2024/2025/2026).
- Provides a **problem-level solvability decomposition** (retained / learned / forgotten) showing that under pass@1024 the forgotten set exceeds the learned set — OPD is net-negative on capability boundary, and its gains come from boosting success frequency on already-solvable problems (retained mean accuracy rises from 13.5%→25.9% for Qwen3, 42.3%→48.7% for Skywork, 42.0%→60.4% for JustRL).
- Tracks **pass@K trajectories during OPD training**, revealing that small-K gains are stable while large-K degradation appears early (~step 80) and is non-monotonic — pointing to OPD training instability.
- Contrasts OPD with **off-policy distillation (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B)**, which raises pass@K and avg@K at both small and large K, framing OPD's failure-to-expand as an "illusory distillation."
- Shows the pathology is **robust across OPD variants** (ExOPD, Direct-OPD, EOPD, pure forward-KL): all improve small-K pass@K and avg@K but do not consistently expand the large-K boundary.

## Methodology

The paper is an empirical analysis, not a new method. It builds no new architecture; it repurposes test-time scaling as a diagnostic.

- **Three OPD settings** (Table 1): (1) *Qwen3* — student Qwen3-1.7B-Base, teacher Qwen3-4B-Base-GRPO; (2) *Skywork* — student R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, teacher Skywork-OR1-Math-7B; (3) *JustRL* — student R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, teacher JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B.
- **OPD objective**: standard reverse-KL minimization between student and teacher next-token distributions over student-sampled trajectories (Eq. 1), implemented with top-k token KL.
- **Training data**: DAPO-Math-17k. Variant training configs in Table 2 (e.g., LR 1e-6 to 1e-5, cosine or constant schedule, batch sizes 64–1024).
- **Evaluation**: pass@K and avg@K for K ∈ {1, 2, …, 512, 1024}; temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, seed 0, 32,768-token context, 31,744-token output limit. Uses the math-vault benchmark snapshots and math-eval pipeline; correctness judged against the last boxed answer.
- **Problem-level analysis**: a problem is "solvable" at budget K if ≥1 of K samples is correct; classified as retained / learned / forgotten comparing pre-OPD base vs OPD-trained.
- **Perplexity probe (Fig 7)**: 16 problems × 32 trajectories per source model (base/OPD/teacher), scored under both base and teacher distributions to test whose trajectory manifold OPD moves toward.
- **Off-policy comparison**: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B (fine-tuned from Qwen-Math-1.5B/7B on teacher-generated data).

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen3 / AMC23 (pass@K, Table 3–4)**: At K=1 OPD = 45.4% vs Base = 32.3% (+13.1pp); at K=1024 the order reverses — Base = 100.0% vs OPD = 95.0% (−5.0pp). Same pattern on **AIME24**: K=1 OPD 12.0% vs Base 4.2% (+7.8pp), but K=1024 Base 70.0% vs OPD 53.3% (−16.7pp).
- **Skywork / AIME26 (pass@K)**: K=1 OPD 25.9% vs Base 20.6% (+5.3pp); K=1024 Base 86.7% vs OPD 80.0% (−6.7pp) — the large-K reversal holds even with a much stronger teacher (Skywork-OR1-Math-7B).
- **avg@K is uniformly higher for OPD (Table 5)**: e.g., Qwen3 AMC23 avg@1024 = 45.4% (OPD) vs 32.3% (Base), +13.1pp; JustRL AIME24 avg@1024 = 49.4% (OPD) vs 29.9% (Base), +19.5pp. OPD's real, consistent win is on sampling efficiency, not boundary.
- **Problem-level asymmetry (Sec 3.2, Fig 3)**: using pass@1024 as the solvability criterion, the *forgotten* set is larger than the *learned* set in all three settings — more previously-solvable problems become unsolvable than unsolvable problems become solvable. Mean accuracy on *retained* problems jumps 13.5%→25.9% (Qwen3), 42.3%→48.7% (Skywork), 42.0%→60.4% (JustRL).
- **Training dynamics (Table 6, Skywork)**: by Step 260 pass@1 beats base on all four benchmarks, but pass@1024 is lower on AMC23/AIME25/AIME26 and ties on AIME24. The decline starts early — by Step 80, pass@1024 on AIME2026 is already 10.0pp below the base, and is non-monotonic thereafter.
- **Off-policy distillation behaves oppositely (Fig 5)**: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B exceed their Qwen-Math base on both pass@K and avg@K across small *and* large budgets — i.e., it genuinely expands the capability boundary where OPD does not.
- **Variants do not escape the pathology (Fig 6)**: EOPD, ExOPD, Direct-OPD, and pure forward-KL all raise small-K pass@K and avg@K but stay at/below base on pass@1024; the only exception is forward-KL on AIME2026.

## Authors & Credibility

- Multi-institution team spanning Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai Innovation Institute, Ant Group, Hong Kong Baptist University, and UT Austin; senior authors include Bo Han, Jun Zhou, Jiangchao Yao, and Weiran Huang, all active in LLM/RL/distillation research. The first author also released the reproducible `math-eval` pipeline and `math-vault` benchmark snapshots used here (cited as Ge 2026a/b, Apache-2.0 / CC dataset), which materially improves reproducibility.
- Benchmark revisions are pinned (frozen HuggingFace commit hashes for AMC23, AIME 2024/2025/2026), and the exact decoding config (T=0.7, top-p=0.95, seed 0, token limits) is given — a level of rigor above typical analysis papers.
- The narrative is appropriately hedged: they call OPD an "illusory distillation" specifically "from the perspective of capability expansion," and explicitly acknowledge avg@K (real-world, single/few-sample deployment) gains are genuine.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The framing is the paper's main contribution — reframing an open methodological debate (does OPD transfer new capability?) as a measurable pass@K/avg@K decomposition, and coining "illusory distillation." The individual building blocks (pass@K as a capability-boundary probe, learned/forgotten accounting) are borrowed from the RLVR-criticism line (Yue et al. 2025, "Does RL really incentivize reasoning beyond the base model?"), so the novelty is synthesis + application to OPD, not a new technique.
- **Rigor**: Strong on aggregate. K sweeps up to 1024 with frozen seeds are expensive and well-executed, and the asymmetry finding (forgotten > learned) is a sharp, falsifiable claim backed by per-problem transitions (Fig 9). However, the **cap-boundary claim rests heavily on K=1024 = "the boundary"** — a modeling assumption, not a proof; the true asymptotic ceiling is unobserved, and the paper does not estimate it. A reader could argue that at K=1024 the base is still sampling-noise-limited and neither model has hit its true ceiling.
- **Reproducibility**: High for the *evaluation* side (public datasets, pinned revisions, released eval harness, stated decoding config). The *training* side is weaker: checkpoint release is not clearly promised, teacher checkpoints (Qwen3-4B-Base-GRPO, JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B) may not all be public, and the three settings are all small (≤7B) math-only pairs — generalization to code/general reasoning or to larger scales is untested.
- **Significance**: Practically important for practitioners: it warns that OPD's headline single-shot gains do not translate to best-of-large-N robustness, and that OPD can *erase* rare-but-correct reasoning modes. The contrast with off-policy distillation is the most actionable finding.
- **Red flags / weaknesses**:
  - **Math-only, small-scale**: every conclusion is on math (AMC/AIME) with ≤7B models and ≤1.7B students. Extrapolation to code, agentic, or ≥30B settings is unsupported.
  - **"Illusory" is somewhat overclaimed given the avg@K wins** — for real deployments that sample once or a few times, OPD is strictly better; the paper says this but the title/abstract lead with the negative framing.
  - **No causal mechanism for forgetting**: they observe forgotten > learned and show perplexity alignment, but do not isolate *why* reverse-KL collapses rare correct modes (e.g., is it mode-seeking KL, teacher-student coverage mismatch, or RL-style probability concentration?). The perplexity result (OPD trajectories still lowest-PPL under the *base*) actually complicates the "OPD = RL-style concentration" story rather than confirming it.
  - **No ablation on K-budget itself as a confound**: compute for K=1024 is enormous; whether the base's large-K edge survives with a verifier/best-of-N selection (the realistic test-time regime) is not tested.
  - **Off-policy baseline is a different data regime** (R1 teacher trajectories vs dense token KL), so the "off-policy expands, OPD does not" contrast conflates data source with supervision form.

## Strengths

- Clean, falsifiable central claim (pass@K boundary vs avg@K efficiency) tested across three genuinely different student-teacher pairs rather than one cherry-picked setup.
- The learned/forgotten/retained decomposition is a genuinely useful diagnostic that future OPD papers should adopt as standard reporting.
- Honest about its own scope: explicitly notes that OPD's avg@K (and hence real single-sample) gains are real, and that off-policy distillation is the contrasting positive case.
- Released eval tooling (math-eval, math-vault) and pinned dataset revisions lower the barrier to verification.

## Limitations

- Generalization is narrow: math-only, students ≤1.7B, teachers ≤7B. No code, no multi-turn/agentic reasoning, no frontier-scale models.
- The "capability boundary" is operationalized as pass@1024; the asymptotic ceiling is never estimated, so "does not expand the boundary" is really "does not raise pass@1024."
- No mechanism-level explanation for *why* OPD forgets more than it learns — the perplexity and case-study evidence is correlational.
- The off-policy comparison (DeepSeek-R1-Distill) differs from OPD in both data source and supervision form, so it cannot cleanly isolate the cause of the boundary difference.
- Variants are tested but hyperparameters follow each variant's own default repo (Table 2), so cross-variant comparisons are not compute- or tuning-matched.

## Relevance

对研究 LLM 推理后训练（蒸馏、RLVR）的用户高度相关：它直接质疑 OPD "扩展能力"的常见叙事，并提供了一套 pass@K/avg@K + 问题级求解性分解的可复用诊断方法，可作为评估任何蒸馏/对齐方法是否"真学新能力"的范式。对于做学生模型训练或测试时扩展选型的人，这是必读的"避坑"型分析。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the "illusory distillation" finding hold at frontier scale (e.g., 30B+ students) and in non-math domains (code generation, agentic tool use, multi-hop QA)?
- If OPD collapses rare correct modes, can an explicit **diversity/entropy regularizer** or trust-region constraint (cf. Xing et al. 2026) recover the large-K boundary without sacrificing small-K gains?
- The forgotten > learned asymmetry suggests reverse-KL's mode-seeking behavior is the culprit — would **forward-KL or symmetric KL OPD** systematically close the gap? (The single forward-KL exception on AIME2026 hints it might.)
- How does OPD interact with **best-of-N with a verifier** (the realistic test-time regime)? The paper measures raw pass@K; a verifier could erase or amplify the base's large-K edge.
- Is the early large-K degradation (by step 80) a sign of **catastrophic forgetting of rare reasoning skills**, and would a replay or KL-anchoring-to-base term prevent it?
- Can the learned/forgotten/retained accounting be turned into a *training* signal — e.g., up-weight forgotten problems — to make OPD genuinely expand the boundary?
