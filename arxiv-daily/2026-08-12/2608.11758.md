> **一句话总结：** AWARe 通过一个小校准集前向计算神经元的"激活显著度"，在下游微调时冻结显著度最高的 30% 自注意力/多模态投影参数，从而在 LLaVA-v1.5-7B 上以仅约 17.5% 可训练参数同时保住上游能力并取得更强下游性能。

> **TL;DR:** AWARe repurposes activation-aware parameter saliency (inspired by AWQ/Wanda) from compression to forgetting mitigation: it profiles neuron importance on a small calibration set, then freezes the top-30% highest-saliency rows of self-attention/multimodal-projector weights during fine-tuning. On LLaVA-v1.5-7B it attains the best stability-plasticity harmonic mean on IconQA (H=103.2, +4.6 over LoRASculpt's 98.6) and COCO-Caption (H=108.0, +3.8 over Model Tailor's 103.9), and raises the MLLM-DCL continual average by +3.18 (65.58 vs DISCO's 62.40), while training only ~17.5% of parameters and needing no architectural changes.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 AWARe 框架**：一种基于激活显著度的两阶段方法（Knowledge Profiling + Constrained Fine-tuning），通过冻结对上游能力最关键的神经元权重来缓解 MLLM 微调中的灾难性遗忘，不需修改模型结构、不需 replay buffer 或额外适配器模块。
- **激活显著度估计方法**：设计了三步聚合-归一化流程（序列维 L2 范数 → 样本内隐藏维 L2 归一化 → 批次平均），所得显著度由神经元的相对重要性而非绝对激活尺度决定，比基于权重幅值或随机选择的方法更准确。
- **行级选择性冻结 + AwareLinear 实现**：对自注意力 q/k/v 投影与 mm_projector 的权重矩阵按输出通道行级冻结，仅更新低显著度行；训练期用 `AwareLinear` 包装实现掩码梯度更新，训练后移除包装、保持原推理图兼容。
- **系统验证与鲁棒性分析**：在单任务下游适配（IconQA、COCO-Caption）与连续指令微调（MLLM-DCL 5 任务序列）上全面对比 8+ 基线，并通过消融确认了激活选择、全局 Top-30% 比例、Self-Attn+MM 目标、200 样本校准等关键设计；额外在 Qwen2.5-VL 上验证可迁移性。

## Key Contributions

- **AWARe framework** — a two-phase method (Knowledge Profiling + Constrained Fine-tuning) that mitigates catastrophic forgetting in MLLMs by freezing the most salient parameters identified from activation patterns, with no architectural change, no replay buffer, and no extra adapter modules.
- **Activation-based saliency estimation** — a three-step aggregation-and-normalization procedure (L2-norm over sequence length → per-sample L2-normalization over the hidden dimension → batch average) that yields a saliency score reflecting relative neuron importance rather than absolute activation scale, which the authors argue is why it beats weight-magnitude and random selection.
- **Row-wise selective freezing via AwareLinear** — only the self-attention q/k/v projections and the mm_projector are targeted; the top-ρ (30%) rows of these weight matrices are frozen while the rest are updated. A training-time `AwareLinear` wrapper realizes masked gradient updates and is removed afterwards so the original model configuration (and inference engines) stay intact.
- **Comprehensive validation and ablations** — comparisons against 8+ PEFT/anti-forgetting baselines on single-task adaptation (IconQA, COCO-Caption) and continual instruction tuning (MLLMM-DCL 5-task sequence), plus ablations isolating the selection strategy, retention ratio, training targets, calibration composition, and calibration-set size, and an additional Qwen2.5-VL transfer study.

## Methodology

AWARe sits on the observation (from AWQ and Wanda) that a small fraction of weights associated with large activation magnitudes is disproportionately important for model behavior. It repurposes this idea from quantization/pruning to forgetting mitigation.

**Problem setup.** Given a pre-trained MLLM Mθ (vision encoder V + LLM L + connector ρ) already trained on upstream tasks T_base, the model is sequentially adapted to downstream tasks. Parameters are partitioned into a fixed set θ_fixed (preserving core features) and a plastic set θ_active (learning new tasks).

**Phase 1 — Knowledge Profiling.** Using a small calibration set S_cal (200 samples each from OKVQA/OCRVQA/GQA/TextVQA, or 600 MMMU samples when upstream data is unavailable), for each target linear layer with weight W ∈ R^(dout × din) the activation tensor A ∈ R^(B×L×dout) is collected in a single forward pass. Saliency per output neuron k is computed in three steps:
1. L2-norm along sequence length (Eq. 1): a'_{i,k} = sqrt(Σ_j a²_{i,j,k}).
2. Per-sample L2-normalization across the hidden dimension (Eq. 2): a''_{i,k} = a'_{i,k} / ||a'_i||_2, which makes saliency reflect relative within-context importance rather than absolute scale.
3. Batch average (Eq. 3): s_k = (1/B) Σ_i a''_{i,k}.

**Phase 2 — Constrained Fine-tuning.** The retention ratio ρ controls the frozen fraction. Output neurons across all target layers are ranked globally, and the top-ρ set I is frozen via a binary row-wise gradient mask M_grad (0 on frozen rows, 1 elsewhere). Updates become W(t+1) = W(t) − η · (∇_W L_down ⊙ M_grad). The `AwareLinear` wrapper decomposes the linear into active and frozen sub-matrices for efficiency and is removed after training.

**Target layers.** Only q_proj, k_proj, v_proj of self-attention and the mm_projector are targeted — informed by the finding that updating self-attention causes less forgetting than MLP blocks. MLP blocks and the output projection W_o are kept entirely frozen. The authors show (Fig. 5) that fine-tuning MLP blocks collapses upstream performance below 30 points even with AWARe protection, while locking the mm_projector costs ~10 points of target-task performance. Trainable parameter ratio is ~17.5% (= 3·0.7·h² / 12h²).

**Training details.** LLaVA-v1.5-7B (and Qwen2.5-VL-7B in the appendix); vision tower frozen; 3 epochs, global batch 16, AdamW with cosine annealing (warmup 0.03); LR 2e-5 for LLM, 2e-4 for mm_projector; Global-Highest 30% retention. Results averaged over three random runs.

## Results & Highlights

- **Best stability-plasticity trade-off on IconQA (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, Table 1).** AWARe achieves H = 103.2 (R = 98.4, E = 108.4, Target = 86.92), beating the next-best LoRASculpt (H = 98.6) by +4.6 and SPIDER (H = 97.5) by +5.7; Full-FT collapses to H = 0.4 (R = 0.2) due to extreme forgetting, and plain LoRA only reaches H = 76.2.
- **Best trade-off on COCO-Caption (CIDEr, Table 1).** AWARe reaches H = 108.0 (R = 94.2, E = 126.5, Target CIDEr = 120.94), +3.8 over Model Tailor's H = 103.9 and +3.8 over LoRASculpt's H = 104.2.
- **State of the art on the MLLM-DCL continual benchmark (Table 2).** AWARe's 5-task Average is 65.58 (+3.18 over DISCO's 62.40) and Last is 62.10 (+1.77 over DISCO's 60.33); per-task it leads on RS (79.88), Med (55.42), AD (51.38), Sci (49.79), and Fin (91.45). On the detailed CL metrics (Table 7) it also reports the best MAA = 67.28 and a competitive BWT = −6.74 (DISCO's BWT = −5.57 is slightly less negative but at much lower accuracy).
- **Works without upstream data.** Using MMMU (600 samples) as a proxy calibration set, AWARe(MMMU) still gets H = 101.7 on IconQA (+3.1 over the sub-optimal baseline) and H = 107.9 on COCO-Caption (+3.7), confirming the general-purpose calibration fallback.
- **Activation selection beats alternatives (Table 3).** At 30% retention, Activation selection gives Source-Avg 60.13 vs Weight-Norm 58.51, hybrid 0.3W+0.7A 58.69, and Random 49.94 on IconQA.
- **Global-Highest 30% is the sweet spot (Table 4).** H peaks at 103.2 for 30% Global-Highest (R = 98.4); over-freezing at 90% collapses plasticity (Target 54.62, E = 68.1, H = 81.0). Calibration-set sensitivity is low (Table 6: std-dev ≈ 0.3 across 200-vs-400-sample, 3-seed runs).
- **Transfers to Qwen2.5-VL (Tables 9–10).** On MLLM-DCL Med/AD, AWARe preserves the strongest upstream retention among fine-tuned methods (e.g., DocVQA 0.913 vs SFT's 0.599, AI2D 0.730 vs SFT's 0.040) while keeping competitive downstream adaptation.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from Zhejiang University (School of Software Technology), Fudan University, Sun Yat-Sen University, and Hokkaido University; the corresponding author is Guoming Wang (NB21013@zju.edu.cn). The work is positioned in the active 2024–2025 PEFT / multimodal continual learning literature and compares against a genuinely strong and recent baseline set (DoRA, DARE, Model Tailor, LoRASculpt, SPIDER, Orth-Reg on the single-task side; SEFE, DISCO, HiDe-LLaVA, CL-MoE, O-LoRA, MoELoRA, ModalPrompt on the CL side) rather than only older methods like vanilla LoRA or EWC. Code is publicly released at https://github.com/kaln27/AWARe. Evaluation uses established benchmarks (IconQA, COCO-Caption, MLLM-DCL) with standard CL metrics (R/E/H, MFT/MFN/MAA/BWT), and most numbers are averaged over three random runs, which is a reasonable rigor bar for the venue.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate but well-motivated.** The core idea — repurpose activation saliency (AWQ/Wanda) to decide which rows to freeze during fine-tuning — is a clean and plausible transplantation rather than a fundamentally new principle. The paper is honest about this lineage and the main empirical contribution is the careful recipe (which layers, which ratio, which normalization).
- **Rigor: solid on ablations, weaker on baselines' tuning.** The ablation coverage is commendable (selection strategy, ratio 1–90%, target components, calibration composition, calibration size with 3 seeds). However, the paper does not report the compute budget or hyperparameter-tuning effort spent on each baseline; given that LoRASculpt, SPIDER, and Model Tailor each have their own sensitive hyperparameters, a reader cannot fully rule out under-tuned baselines. The single-task comparison also uses LLaVA-v1.5-7B as the only primary backbone (Qwen2.5-VL appears only in the appendix and only on two MLLM-DCL subtasks, with no single-task IconQA/COCO numbers).
- **Reproducibility: good.** Code is released, hyperparameters are tabulated (Table 11), calibration protocol is specified (200 samples/task or 600 MMMU), and the AwareLinear wrapper is described concretely. The "~17.5% trainable" claim is derived cleanly in Section C.
- **Significance: practically useful.** Freezing 30% of self-attention rows with a single forward pass of ~200–600 samples is cheap and deployable on top of existing LoRA/PEFT pipelines, and the gains over strong 2025 baselines (SPIDER, LoRASculpt, DISCO) are consistent across single-task and continual settings.
- **Red flags / things to probe.** (1) On MLLM-DCL, DISCO actually has a better (less negative) BWT (−5.57 vs AWARe's −6.74) and MFT (64.78 vs 67.49) — AWARe wins on MAA/MFN but the plasticity picture is not a clean sweep; the paper emphasizes Avg/Last which favors it. (2) The 90%-retention collapse row in Table 4 (Target drops to 54.62) suggests the method is brittle to over-freezing, which matters for users who do not sweep ρ. (3) No evaluation on larger backbones (>7B), longer CL sequences (>5 tasks), or pure-LLM (non-multimodal) forgetting, so generality beyond the studied regime is unproven. (4) The MMMU-proxy result is close to the upstream-data result (H 101.7 vs 103.2 on IconQA), which is encouraging but slightly surprising given MMMU's distribution is quite different from VQA-style upstream — a per-layer activation-overlap analysis would strengthen the claim that the frozen regions are truly "the same" critical neurons.

## Strengths

- Simple, architecture-agnostic, and cheap: one forward pass for profiling, no replay buffer, no extra modules, and the AwareLinear wrapper leaves the deployed model untouched.
- Strong and consistent gains over genuinely recent (2024–2025) anti-forgetting baselines on both single-task and continual settings.
- Thorough and well-isolated ablations (selection strategy, ratio sweep 1–90%, target components, calibration composition, calibration size with 3-seed std-dev) that directly support each design choice.
- Practical fallback: a general-purpose calibration set (MMMU) works nearly as well as upstream data, which matters when upstream data is unavailable.
- Clean ~17.5% trainable-parameter derivation and public code release.

## Limitations

- Method depends on the calibration set; its quality and coverage influence which regions are frozen, and the authors only study a few VQA-style / MMMU choices.
- Evaluation is limited to 7B MLLMs (LLaVA-v1.5-7B primarily; Qwen2.5-VL-7B only on two MLLM-DCL subtasks in the appendix) and a narrow set of multimodal tasks — no >7B models, no long CL sequences, no pure-LLM setting.
- Because AWARe explicitly freezes salient parameters, very large distribution shifts may require re-tuning the retention ratio ρ; the 90%-collapse row in Table 4 shows the operating range is not unbounded.
- On MLLM-DCL, AWARe's BWT (−6.74) is actually worse than DISCO's (−5.57), so under a pure "least forgetting" lens it is not strictly dominant even though it wins on average accuracy.
- No analysis of how the frozen-neuron set overlaps across different downstream tasks, which would clarify whether the same "critical" neurons are being protected or whether the choice is somewhat task-agnostic by luck.

## Relevance

高度相关。该工作直接面向多模态大模型（MLLM）在下游微调中的灾难性遗忘问题，提出了轻量、架构无关的激活显著度冻结方案，与我们关注的"大模型高效微调 / 持续学习 / 参数高效适配"研究方向紧密契合，可作为 PEFT 与多模态持续学习交叉点上的代表性新基线参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the frozen-neuron set overlap across different downstream tasks (e.g., IconQA vs COCO-Caption vs MLLM-DCL subtasks)? Are we protecting the same "critical" neurons, or is the choice largely task-agnostic?
- Does AWARe compose with LoRA-based updates on the trainable rows (i.e., freeze top-30% by activation, then apply LoRA to the remaining 70%)? Could that push trainable parameters well below 17.5%?
- How does the method scale to 13B/70B MLLMs and to longer (10+ task) continual sequences where interference compounds?
- Is the MMMU-as-proxy result robust to the choice of general calibration set — would a non-VQA distribution (e.g., pure text or pure image classification) work equally well, and why?
- Can the row-wise binary mask be relaxed to a soft/gradient-aware retention schedule that auto-tunes ρ per layer based on training dynamics, removing the need for a ratio sweep?
- AWARe's BWT on MLLM-DCL (−6.74) lags DISCO's (−5.57); can a small backward-transfer-aware regularizer be added without sacrificing the MAA/MFN lead?
