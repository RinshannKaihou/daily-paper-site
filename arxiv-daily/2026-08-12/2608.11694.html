Key Finding

Benchmark scores are a wording-dependent interval, not a point — drift spans 74.7pp and reverses direction with model strength.

The Wording Effect: Quantifying Two-Way Drift in LLM Benchmark Performance
Shailja Thakur, Sungeun An, Chad DeLuca, Hima Patel ·IBM Research (India & Almaden) ·2026-08-12
llm-evaluation benchmark-robustness prompt-sensitivity phrasing-drift measurement

Key Metric

74.7pp

Average worst-to-best accuracy gap across all 24 model–benchmark pairs — a single reported score sits inside this window.

Pearson r = 0.98 · drift asymmetry vs baseline

Why This Matters

Turns a single accuracy number into a defensible interval — every leaderboard rank is a wording-dependent range.

Critical Edge

The central asymmetry rests on LLM judges with no human validation — a directional bias could distort the keystone result.

Methodology

Four roles, one validated flip

500 problems × 3 benchmarks × 8 models (7B–34B) · vLLM 2×H100 · temp 0

A Generator (Mistral-Large-2411) proposes rephrasings along four meaning-preserving axes — linguistic, referential, pragmatic, structural. A Validator (GPT-OSS-120B) discards any candidate whose ground-truth answer moves. The Target answers the original and every surviving variation with chain-of-thought. A Judge (Llama-3.3-70B) scores correctness, accepting equivalent surface forms.

original: wrong → rewrite →POSITIVE DRIFT variant: correct
original: correct → rewrite →NEGATIVE DRIFT variant: wrong

Both rates share denominator N, yielding exact identities Best = Rep. + Pos. and Worst = Rep. − Neg. Each problem keeps on average 20.2 validated variations (range 3–92).

Each flip is attributed to the specific transformation that produced it — so fragility is traceable, not just observed.

A fourfold range. Damage tracks surface form, not comprehension — persona & reordering cost more answers than rewriting the answer's own tokens.

Drift direction reverses with strength. Below 60% baseline, weak models net-gain (Mistral-7B on MATH-Hard: +37.6% pos vs 9.0% neg); above it, strong models net-lose — the two strongest GSM8K models fall below 40% worst-case despite >93% baselines. Asymmetry vs baseline: r = 0.98.

Strengths

What holds up

  • Exact identities (Best = Rep. + Pos.) turn one score into a defensible interval — no extra modeling assumptions.
  • Sign-reversal regularity (r = 0.98) is striking, interpretable, and reshapes how to read leaderboards.
  • Limitations section names the most dangerous threat (directional judge bias) instead of burying it.
  • Transformation-level analysis is the load-bearing result — and reported as such.
  • Subsampling curve gives a cheap audit recipe: 8–10 variations recover ~90% of drift.

Limitations

Where it frays

  • No human validation of judge/validator — a shared directional blind spot could distort the central asymmetry.
  • Only three closed-form benchmarks; no open-ended or code-generation tasks.
  • Generator/validator/judge families overlap with target families (Mistral, Qwen).
  • Axis labels reconstructed post hoc from transformation IDs, not logged at generation time.
  • Higher cost than a standard eval; positioned for periodic audits, not routine runs.
  • 15-problem DSPy/GEPA comparison is underpowered; 74.7pp gap partly reflects weak models.

Open Questions

Where to push next

  1. Does the sign-reversal regularity hold on open-ended tasks, or on HumanEval/MBPP where correctness isn't binary?
  2. Would human annotation of the judge's directional permissiveness change the asymmetry — and by how much?
  3. Can the transformation-level fragility rankings (mean ρ = 0.77) transfer as a cheap probe across model generations?
  4. Does self-consistency across meaning-preserving variations recover best-case accuracy — converting drift into robustness?
  5. Do persona/reordering attacks specifically target instruction-following surface features — and can targeted fine-tuning close the worst-case gap?
