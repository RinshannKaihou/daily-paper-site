Orientation, not magnitude: the causal structure of task-vector interference in merged language models ↗ arXiv
Chencheng Zhu ·UNSW Sydney ·2026-08-12 ·arXiv:2608.11797v1
model-merging task-arithmetic mechanistic-interpretability activation-patching numerical-precision

Key finding

Task-vector interference is carried and amplified along a direction — not produced in magnitude.

Key metric
~20×

collapse in expressed interference when an Alpaca instruction wrapper is applied.

+6.91pp → +0.34pp
Why this matters

Instruction-template benchmarks read the format gate, not real interference — a hidden blind spot in how merged LLMs are evaluated.

Critical edge

The headline 20× gate is Qwen2.5-1.5B-scoped (Llama shows ~2×); behavioral recovery was falsified and no deployable directional metric exists.

Methodology

A four-corner decomposition, then a direction-only intervention.

Intervenes on the exact layerwise cross-term Iℓ = hαβ − hα0 − h0β + h00 of LoRA-merged models — Qwen2.5-1.5B primary; Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-7B replications, with predictions frozen before any adapter on those points existed. Six rank-16 LoRA task vectors (math, code, instruction, safety, summarization, translation), norm-matched, merged as θ0 + αΔA + βΔB over a 6×6 interior grid. An exact per-layer identity Iℓ+1 = Gℓ + Tℓ + Mℓ splits local generation from transport. The causal intervention replaces the residual with hαβ − λIℓ over a 5-point dose ladder against four norm-matched structural controls (wrong-pair, coefficient-mismatch, cross-prompt, random). Expressed interference is a ratio-of-means JSD; effect D = Rorig − Rpatched freezes the main-effect denominator so it moves for exactly one reason. Because Iℓ is a catastrophic cancellation (O(αβ) signal from O(α) paths), every estimator carries a propagated-ULP floor, determinism nulls, and bitwise equivalence audits. 46 predictions were preregistered — and 2 of 3 headline expectations were falsified and reported as such.

Strengths

  • Top-field rigor: per-estimator ULP floors, determinism nulls, bitwise audits that caught two real implementation bugs before any confirmatory data.
  • Preregistration with reported falsifications (2 of 3 headline expectations) — almost unheard of in the merging literature.
  • Four norm-matched structural controls plus a dose ladder; the cross-prompt derangement is a direct null for input-conditioning.
  • Multi-scale (7B) and multi-family (Llama) replications, predictions frozen before adapters existed — honest and more informative than a universal claim.
  • The bf16-as-rounding-roughness caution is a broadly useful, standalone warning for activation-difference work.

Limitations

  • LoRA-only, rank-16: full finetuning untested; the 13×/20× gate is explicitly a family property, not universal.
  • Behavioral endpoints failed: discrete scoring was a null instrument; the continuous NLL endpoint falsified behavioral recovery.
  • Three seeds as the outer unit, within-seed CIs only; the 14×–337× pair ratio is unstable.
  • No deployable directional metric found — all three preregistered features failed on held-out pairs; erasure-as-mitigation is unevaluated.
  • Narrow gating scope: one wrapper family (Alpaca + two phrasings), two core task pairs, first-token estimand only.
  • Single author, preprint, no peer review; load-bearing output numbers come from the companion paper (arXiv:2608.09490).

Open questions for further exploration

  1. Does the transport-dominance / attractor structure survive full finetuning, not just rank-16 LoRA?
  2. Can a cheap, deployable directional predictor be engineered without the four-corner overhead?
  3. Would late-depth erasure as a merging-repair method actually improve downstream task accuracy?
  4. What architectural property (GQA, tokenizer, width, norm scale) controls gate strength — is 7B’s total closure scale or family?
  5. Does the expression-gating critique generalize beyond merging to RLHF / safety evals?
  6. Can the mechanism predict which wrapper templates gate hardest, inverted into a design principle?
