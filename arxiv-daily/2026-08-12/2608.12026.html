SoftWater: Class-Aware Rate Allocation for Softmax Quantization

Joao V. Cavalcanti, Ashia C. Wilson — MIT EECS  ·  1B–32B evaluation  ·  weight-only, no finetuning

Key Finding

SoftWater recasts softmax-head quantization as a KL rate-distortion problem — allocating bits by class frequency and winning 59 of 60 tests.

A single calibration forward pass yields the class-side statistics; frequent low-variance classes get fine grids, rare ones get coarse grids.

Key metric · head-only wins
59/60
Head-only test points where SoftWater beats the released WaterSIC quantizer — across five models (1B–32B) at 2 / 3 / 4-bit heads.
Only 1 cell lost · 2-bit SW head ≈ 3-bit WS head on WT2 KL ("buys one bit")
Why this matters

The softmax head holds 15–30% of params (52–68% of post-quant bytes) yet usually stays fp16 — SoftWater targets this overlooked deployment bottleneck.

Critical edge

The 59/60 win is in-domain only — out-of-domain (C4) KL gains shrink to 3.5×–5.1×, and no SoftWater code is released for reproduction.

01

Methodology

SoftWater targets the LLM softmax head W ∈ R^{K×n}, whose output is a distribution — so the right distortion is KL divergence DKL(p‖q), not the WMSE that standard PTQ minimizes. A second-order Taylor expansion of the KL at δ=0 yields an error weight E[∇²f(0) ⊗ XXᵀ] with ∇²f(0)=diag(p)−ppᵀ, jointly weighting quantization error by feature covariance and per-class softmax curvature.

Two reductions make it tractable: (1) a subtractive-dither argument zeros the off-diagonal class terms, leaving the diagonal λ=diag(p⊙(1−p)); (2) a separability assumption (validated to ≤10% proxy error) splits the Kronecker product so the class factor λ̄ (K scalars) and feature covariance ΣX are estimated independently from a single forward pass — no backward pass, no second data pass.

The induced grid spacing α ∝ (λ̄ₖ|ℓᵢᵢ|)^(−1/2) gives fine grids to frequent, low-variance classes and coarse grids to rare ones. A smoothing prior p̃=(1−ε)p+ε/K (ε=0.1) caps spacing on unseen classes and reduces exactly to WaterSIC at ε=1 — so the prior WMSE treatment is a special case.

03

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths pro

  • Theory-driven and cheap: the entire extra cost is one K-vector accumulator in the existing calibration forward pass — no backward pass, no second data pass, no finetuning.
  • Unified: WaterSIC's WMSE head treatment falls out as the ε=1 special case — a clean conceptual story, not an ad-hoc patch.
  • Broad head-only wins across five models (1B–32B, Llama & Qwen) at every tested rate (2/3/4-bit).
  • Domain-targetable: the class-side statistic is a token frequency, so a head can specialize to its deployment domain via calibration alone (2×–5× lower KL).
  • Honest reporting: in-domain vs out-of-domain gaps, the single failing cell, and limitations are surfaced explicitly.

Limitations con

  • The separable surrogate is accurate to ≤10% on measured models but is not guaranteed in general; strong class-curvature / feature-anisotropy coupling would degrade it.
  • Deployed-model & zero-shot experiments use only Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct and only GuidedQuant/BlockLDLQ bodies — generalization to other sizes, body quantizers, and untied heads is untested here.
  • KL gains are larger in-domain (6.5×–8.3×) than out-of-domain (3.5×–5.1×); the "one free bit" claim is calibration-domain-dependent.
  • The simpler marginal p̄ₖ nearly ties λ̃ in-domain, so the full second-moment machinery pays off mainly in transfer settings.
  • No public code release for SoftWater; 2-bit heads on already-strong (4-bit) bodies still roughly double total KL even under SoftWater.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does class-aware allocation generalize beyond Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct to mid-size (7B–13B) and untied-head models, where the head's byte share and Zipf structure differ?
  2. The analysis claims to extend to MoE routers and vision/speech classifiers — is separability equally tight when the "vocabulary" is experts or classes rather than tokens?
  3. How does SoftWater compose with WaterSIC's omitted refinements (LMMSE shrinkage, residual-stream correction, finetuning) — are the gains genuinely additive?
  4. Since the simpler marginal p̄ₖ nearly ties λ̃ in-domain, is there a principled criterion for when the second-moment correction's out-of-domain benefit justifies the extra bookkeeping?
  5. The method is weight-only with a frozen body; how does head rate allocation interact with activation quantization or non-GuidedQuant bodies?
