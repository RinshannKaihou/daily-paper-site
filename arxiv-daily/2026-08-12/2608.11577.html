Methodology

two-stage co-design · DP + Best-first Dijkstra

Stage 1 — Per-function segmentation & mixed-degree optimization. For each of six activations, breakpoints are placed by coordinate descent on a discretized grid, then a Dynamic Programming pass assigns every segment an independent degree (0–3). The DP state is the cumulative degree distribution (cd₁, cd₂, cd₃) — additive and monotone — so Bellman recursion keeps only the minimum-error partial assignment per reachable state. Theorem 1 proves optimal substructure, guaranteeing a globally optimal (area, error) Pareto front for every hardware profile.

Stage 2 — Joint cross-function search. A Best-first Dijkstra explores the graph of all functions’ Pareto sets in increasing area; the first node that feasibly covers every function’s error budget is provably the minimum-area unified configuration. Crucially, area is scored by an RTL-derived analytical model (fit from 800+ GF 22nm syntheses, MAPE 0.83% on 30 held-out configs), replacing the segment-count proxy used by all prior unified SFUs.

Datapath · multi-lane

aBreakpoint Comparator — parallel compare + priority encoder, 2 cyc.
bPer-lane Local Cache (LRU, 4-entry) — 4×32-bit coeffs, 1–2 cyc.
cShared Global Multi-Level LUT — degree-banked (20/18/12/3), ≤6 cyc.
dExecution Unit — 3 parallel MAC-ADD pairs, 2/4/6 cyc by degree.
·Best-case latency 4 + Lexec; worst-case 10 + Lexec.

Strengths & Limitations

side-by-side verdict

Strengths

  • Joint mixed-degree + cross-function optimization is the right formulation — and it is solved optimally, not heuristically (Theorem 1).
  • RTL-derived area model is a meaningful upgrade over the segment-count proxy and is independently validated (MAPE 0.83%).
  • Comprehensive evaluation: 700 TIMM + 3 NLP models, inference + training, FP32 + FP16, single + multi-lane, plus a full NVDLA integration study.
  • Honest about metrics — re-derives sq-AAE comparisons, discloses the conflict of interest, separates best/worst-case latency.
  • Strong power efficiency (0.56–0.63 mW) at full FP32, relevant for both server and edge.

Limitations

  • No open-source release of RTL or fitted area-model constants — limits independent reproduction.
  • Headline area comparisons rest on scaled/estimated Flex-SFU baselines, not resynthesized competitor RTL.
  • Function set fixed at six activations; Mish, GLU variants, or true Softmax are future work.
  • Throughput drops to 0.86× at 4 lanes; low-locality NLP workloads (GPT-Neo) hit worst-case path — end-to-end LLM serving unmeasured.
  • Training fidelity validated on only 3 vision models, not the full 700; area model omits P&R-level optimizations by design.

Questions for Further Exploration

Q1How does the Pareto frontier shift when targets expand to Mish, Swish variants, or full Softmax — does joint search still converge quickly?
Q2What is the end-to-end token throughput in an autoregressive LLM serving path, especially for low-locality workloads like GPT-Neo?
Q3Could the RTL area model be replaced by a learned surrogate (e.g., a small GNN over the netlist) to avoid 800+ syntheses per node?
Q4How does Uni-SFU behave under INT8/FP8 quantization-aware training where activations are temporarily up-cast to FP32?
Q5Is Best-first Dijkstra still tractable when per-function Pareto sets grow large (8+ functions, 20+ segments), or does it need branch-and-bound?
Q6Does the 0.13% NVDLA global overhead hold under mixed-precision dataflow (e.g., Jetfire-style frameworks)?
