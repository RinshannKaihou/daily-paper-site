> **一句话总结：** 提出 A-CRC-QA，一个事后、免训练的阈值校准框架：把"被接受答案的错误率（SCER）≤α"重写为线性期望约束，再对非单调的经验风险取单调化上包络并加上渐近趋于零的有限样本修正，从而在 CoQA / MedMCQA 上 α=0.15 时把平均 SCER 控制在 0.143 / 0.138（均低于目标），同时保留 46.2% / 58.7% 的答案，比保守的置信界基线多保留约 7 个百分点的答案。

> **TL;DR:** A-CRC-QA calibrates a single uncertainty threshold for selective LLM question answering so that the error rate among accepted answers (SCER) is controlled to a user-specified α. It reframes SCER≤α as a bounded linear expectation loss (following LEC), but because that loss is non-monotone in the threshold, it monotonizes the *empirical* risk via an upper envelope and adds a vanishing correction, yielding an **asymptotic (not finite-sample) marginal** guarantee. On CoQA and MedMCQA with LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B at α=0.15, it holds SCER to 0.143 / 0.138 while retaining 46.2% / 58.7% of answers — beating the COIN-style Clopper–Pearson confidence-bound baseline (UCB-CLP) by ~7.4 pp in acceptance on MedMCQA and cutting the violation rate of LEC-Direct roughly in half (CoQA 25.5%→12.5%, MedMCQA 19.0%→8.0%) at a ~3.3 pp retention cost.

## 核心贡献

- **把选择性问答形式化为"被接受答案错误率（SCER）的控制问题"**：给定标量不确定性估计器与目标风险 α，要求在保留尽可能多答案的同时使 SCER(λ) ≤ α。
- **将 LEC 的线性期望约束与 CRC 对非单调损失的处理相结合**：定义实例级线性损失 L(λ)=S(λ)(E−α)（有界：−α ≤ L ≤ 1−α），证明 SCER≤α 等价于 E[L(λ)]≤0（只要选择概率为正）。
- **指出并解决非单调性难题**：接受正确答案使损失下降、接受错误答案使损失上升，故实例损失对阈值非单调，标准 CRC 的有限样本定理无法直接套用；作者转而**对经验风险（而非实例损失）取单调上包络** ĝ_n^↑(λ)=sup_{t≥λ} ĝ_n(t)，再加趋于零的修正 γ_n=(1−α)/(n+1)，选最大可行阈值。
- **给出渐近边际保证定理**（基于 Glivenko–Cantelli 一致收敛），并诚实声明这只是渐近、边际意义上的保证，不能当作有限样本/条件保证。
- **高效、免训练、模型无关**：只需在留出校准集上对唯一分数排序后做前缀最大值即可 O(n log n) 求解；可配合任意标量不确定性信号（白盒或黑盒）。
- **跨设置验证**：在开放式（CoQA，SemEnt/WSE）与封闭式（MedMCQA，PE/MSP）问答、两类模型族上均验证有效。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates uncertainty-aware selective QA as controlling the selection-conditioned error rate (SCER) among accepted LLM answers to a user-specified α.
- Combines LEC's linear expectation constraint with the monotonized-non-monotone-loss machinery of Conformal Risk Control: the instance loss L(λ)=S(λ)(E−α) is bounded but non-monotone, so the method monotonizes the *empirical* risk (upper envelope) rather than each instance loss, then adds a vanishing finite-sample correction γ_n=(1−α)/(n+1).
- Identifies and formally handles the non-monotonicity that blocks the standard finite-sample CRC theorem, and proves an **asymptotic marginal** SCER guarantee (Theorem 1, via Glivenko–Cantelli uniform convergence), with an explicit remark scoping it as asymptotic/marginal only.
- Provides an efficient post-hoc, training-free, model-agnostic procedure reducible to sorting calibration scores and computing a prefix maximum.
- Empirically validates across open-ended (CoQA) and closed-ended (MedMCQA) QA, two model families (LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B), and four uncertainty estimators (SemEnt, WSE, PE, MSP).

## Methodology

A-CRC-QA is a **post-hoc threshold calibrator** sitting between an LLM + uncertainty estimator and the accept/abstain decision.

1. **Setup.** A pretrained LLM G produces an answer ŷ for question x; a scalar uncertainty estimator U gives u (smaller = more confident), converted to a reliability score r=h(u) (e.g., r=−u) with any strictly decreasing h. A pre-specified correctness function A(y\*,ŷ)∈{0,1} defines the error indicator E=1−A (exact option match for MedMCQA; token-level F1≥0.5 for CoQA, fixed before calibration). Accept iff r≥λ.
2. **Objective.** Control SCER(λ)=Pr(E=1 | S(λ)=1) ≤ α while maximizing retention.
3. **Linear reformulation.** Define Z(λ)=S(λ)E. SCER(λ)≤α ⇔ E[Z(λ)] − α·E[S(λ)] ≤ 0 (when E[S(λ)]>0), giving the bounded instance loss L(λ)=S(λ)(E−α) with −α ≤ L ≤ 1−α and population risk g(λ)=E[L(λ)].
4. **Why finite-sample CRC fails.** L(λ) is non-monotone in λ: crossing a threshold that drops a *correct* example raises the loss (−α→0), while dropping an *incorrect* one lowers it (1−α→0). Standard CRC needs monotone instance losses.
5. **Monotonized empirical risk.** On calibration set D_cal, compute ĝ_n(λ)=⅟ₙ Σ S_i(λ)(E_i−α); define the non-increasing upper envelope ĝ_n^↑(λ)=sup_{t≥λ} ĝ_n(t). Requiring ĝ_n^↑(λ)≤0 enforces the constraint at λ and every more conservative threshold, removing spurious feasible points from calibration noise.
6. **Vanishing correction & threshold.** Add γ_n=(1−α)/(n+1) (the (1−α) upper bound of L, à la CRC's finite-sample term, which →0). Select the least conservative feasible threshold λ̂_n=inf{λ : ĝ_n^↑(λ)+γ_n ≤ 0}; if the set is empty, declare infeasible and abstain on everything.
7. **Efficient computation.** Risk changes only at observed scores: sort r^(1)≥…≥r^(n), compute prefix C_k=⅟ₙ Σ_{j≤k}(E^(j)−α) and prefix max M_k=max_{j≤k} C_j; accept the first k\*=max{k : M_k+γ_n≤0} examples, threshold λ̂_n=r^(k\*). Ties handled as a block (deterministic, no label-based tie-breaking).
8. **Guarantee.** Under i.i.d. calibration/test, bounded right-continuous losses, γ_n→0, and non-vanishing expected selection probability, Theorem 1 gives lim sup E[S_{n+1}(λ̂_n)E_{n+1}]/E[S_{n+1}(λ̂_n)] ≤ α — i.e., asymptotic marginal SCER control. The authors stress this is **not** a finite-sample or conditional-on-calibration guarantee; finite-sample behavior must be checked empirically via violation rates over repeated splits.

## Results & Highlights

All numbers at target risk α=0.15 unless noted; SCER is the error rate among accepted answers (lower better), AR the acceptance/retention rate (higher better), VR the fraction of random calibration–test splits whose test SCER exceeds α (lower better), IF the infeasibility rate.

- **Risk control holds at α=0.15.** Averaged over the two models, A-CRC-QA achieves SCER **0.143 on CoQA** and **0.138 on MedMCQA** — both below the 0.15 target — while retaining **46.2% (CoQA)** and **58.7% (MedMCQA)** of test answers (Table 2). By contrast, the uncalibrated Fixed-50 / Empirical thresholds violate the target in **61–93%** of splits despite reasonable AUROC, demonstrating that a good uncertainty ranking alone does not control accepted-answer risk.
- **Beats conservative confidence-bound baselines on retention.** Against UCB-CLP (the exact Clopper–Pearson bound used by the COIN selective-QA line), A-CRC-QA raises the acceptance rate by **~7.2 pp on CoQA** (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B: 35.4%→42.6%; Qwen2.5-7B: 42.7%→49.8%) and **~7.4 pp on MedMCQA** (LLaMA: 47.8%→55.3%; Qwen: 54.9%→62.1%), with the extra accepted answers being predominantly correct (power rises correspondingly) (Table 1).
- **Trades a little retention for much better stability vs LEC-Direct.** LEC-Direct has the highest retention among calibrated methods but is sensitive to local non-monotone fluctuations; A-CRC-QA cuts the average violation rate from **25.5%→12.5% on CoQA** and **19.0%→8.0% on MedMCQA**, at a cost of only **~3.3 pp** in acceptance rate (§4.2).
- **Ablations confirm both components matter (Table 5, α=0.15).** Removing monotonization pushes SCER to **0.164 (CoQA)** / **0.171 (MedMCQA)** with VR **65% / 72%**; removing the finite-sample correction gives SCER **0.151 / 0.154** with VR **41% / 46%**; the full method holds SCER **0.143 / 0.138** with VR **13% / 8%**. (Monotonizing each *instance* loss instead — the "instance-wise envelope" — is far too conservative: SCER 0.073 / 0.068 but AR only 12.8% / 18.9%.)
- **Calibration-set size behaves as the asymptotic theory predicts (Table 4).** On CoQA, SCER converges from below (0.127±0.036 at n=100 → 0.145±0.013 at n=1500), AR rises 30.8%→48.0%, and VR falls 26%→12% — but VR never reaches 0, consistent with an asymptotic-only guarantee.
- **Uncertainty-estimator-agnostic (Table 3).** All four estimators support average risk control after calibration; on CoQA WSE (AUROC 0.735 vs SemEnt 0.691 for LLaMA) accepts ~4 pp more answers, and on MedMCQA PE beats MSP, confirming calibration and uncertainty quality play complementary roles.
- **Across target levels (Table 2).** SCER stays below α from 0.05 to 0.25; CoQA acceptance rises from 11.6% (α=0.05) to 78.6% (α=0.25). Very strict targets become infeasible (IF 16% CoQA / 22% MedMCQA at α=0.05) when the model has no sufficiently reliable low-uncertainty subset.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — moderate and honestly scoped.** The two building blocks (LEC's linear expectation constraint; CRC's monotonization of non-monotone empirical risk from Angelopoulos et al. 2024) are both prior work; the contribution is their adaptation to selective QA plus the asymptotic SCER theorem. This is a legitimate synthesis, not a breakthrough, and is presented as such.
- **Rigor — strong on theory hygiene.** Theorem 1 has a real proof sketch (Glivenko–Cantelli uniform convergence of a 1-D threshold class), and the authors are unusually disciplined about scope: Remark 1 and §4.7 explicitly forbid presenting the result as finite-sample/conditional, and §4.7 prescribes a reporting protocol (always pair average SCER with VR, IF, and calibration-size sensitivity). The nonzero VR even at n=1500 is reported rather than hidden.
- **Reproducibility — reasonable.** Method reduces to sort + prefix-max; models (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), decoding (greedy, 64 tokens; 10 samples at T=0.7, top-p=0.9), estimators (SemEnt, WSE, PE, MSP), splits (100 random calibration–test), and α=0.15 are all specified. However, no code release is mentioned.
- **Missing ablations / narrow evaluation.** Only two datasets (one open, one closed) and two small (7–8B) models; no 70B+/frontier models, no math/reasoning QA where correctness is sharper, and no distribution-shift study (acknowledged as future work). The COIN baseline is approximated by UCB-CLP rather than the full COIN pipeline. There is no sensitivity analysis on the CoQA correctness threshold (F1≥0.5), even though that heuristic gate is upstream of every reported number.
- **Red flag — headline number is internally inconsistent.** The abstract and introduction claim A-CRC-QA improves acceptance by "approximately **6.1** percentage points on CoQA" over a "Hoeffding-based upper-confidence-bound method." But Table 1 shows the gap over UCB-HFD (the Hoeffding baseline) is ~12.5 pp (LLaMA: 30.1→42.6) and ~13.4 pp (Qwen: 36.4→49.8) on CoQA, while the gap over UCB-CLP is ~7.2 pp. Neither baseline yields 6.1 pp on CoQA, so the abstract figure appears to be an error; the §4.2 text ("7.2 pp on CoQA") contradicts it. Readers should rely on Table 1, not the headline.
- **Minor:** the "Zhangjiang University" typo and the unedited ACM template suggest light proofreading for a statistical-paper venue where precision matters.

## Strengths

- Genuinely useful operating point: a stable middle ground between the aggressive LEC-Direct (high retention, high VR) and conservative UCB methods (low VR, low retention), with both gains traced to specific mechanisms via clean ablations.
- Theoretically honest — the asymptotic/marginal scoping and the mandated VR+IF+calibration-size reporting protocol are exactly the right discipline for a method that cannot claim finite-sample guarantees.
- Practical and cheap: post-hoc, training-free, model- and UQ-agnostic, O(n log n) calibration, works with black-box uncertainty scores.
- Robustness section (Table 3) cleanly separates the roles of calibration vs. uncertainty discrimination.

## Limitations

- **Asymptotic only.** No finite-sample or conditional guarantee; VR stays at 8–13% even with 1500 calibration examples, and small calibration sets are infeasible at strict α (IF 16–22% at α=0.05).
- **Narrow empirical base.** Two datasets, two ≤8B models; no large models, no reasoning/math QA, no distribution shift, no real high-stakes deployment.
- **Depends on a pre-specified, heuristic correctness function** (token-level F1≥0.5 for CoQA) that itself injects noise into every error label; its sensitivity is unanalyzed.
- **Insular baselines:** the strongest competitors (COIN, LEC) come from the same research cluster; an independent re-implementation of COIN's full pipeline and a comparison against a learned selection/rejection function (e.g., SelectiveNet-style) are missing.
- **Headline improvement figure (6.1 pp on CoQA) is inconsistent with Table 1.**

## Relevance

高度契合用户对"LLM 可靠性 / 不确定性量化 / 选择性预测与弃答 / 共形（风险）校准"的研究兴趣：本文正是把共形风险控制（CRC）落地到 LLM 选择性问答的阈值校准，并提供可操作的接受率—错误率折中，是该方向可直接借鉴的方法学参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the asymptotic guarantee tighten meaningfully at 70B+ scale, and does the method behave well on reasoning/math QA (GSM8K, MMLU-STEM) where correctness is binary and uncertainty is harder to estimate?
- How brittle is SCER control under distribution shift between calibration and test (new domain, new model version), and can a weighted/weighted-split conformal variant restore approximate finite-sample control?
- Can the vanishing correction γ_n be replaced by a data-adaptive term to push VR toward 0 at finite n without LEC-Direct's instability?
- The paper proposes extending to model routing and human–AI deferral — does calibrating a *defer* threshold (not just accept/abstain) preserve the linear-loss structure and the asymptotic bound?
- How sensitive are the conclusions to the CoQA F1≥0.5 correctness gate — does a semantic-equivalence or LLM-judge correctness rule change the relative ranking of A-CRC-QA vs. UCB-CLP / LEC-Direct?
