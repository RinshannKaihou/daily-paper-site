Refusal behavior lives in MLP weights, not attention, concentrated in mid-network layers 8–11, and composes non-additively.

Localizing Safety Alignment: MLP Layers and Mid-Network Blocks Encode Refusal Behavior in Large Language Models Mingyu Zong, Sampad Mohanty, Bhaskar Krishnamachari — USC (ANRG) 2026-08-12
weight transplantation refusal behavior safety alignment mechanistic interpretability non-additive composition
Key Metric · MLP vs Attention
2.7×
MLP-only hybrids refuse ≥ 2.7× more malicious prompts than attention-only hybrids — across all 6 benchmark settings. On the 100-prompt validation the gap is 61 vs 8 refusals.
Why This Matters
why

Pinpoints where safety lives in the weights — a concrete mechanistic hook for why alignment is brittle, and for designing surgical safety edits & more robust redundant alignment.

Critical Edge statistical power

Main results use ≤ 30 prompts per condition; with single-digit refusal counts, many “Block X beats Block Y” rankings sit within noise — and the 2.7× headline divides by a near-zero attention baseline.

Methodology / how the claim is isolated

Selective weight transplantation between architecturally identical pairs.

RealSafe-R1-7B↔DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B 28-layer Qwen2
saferlhf_ultra_sft↔Llama-3.1-8B 32-layer Llama

Because each pair shares its architecture, any behavioral change is attributable solely to the transplanted weights — no cross-model confound.

Three granularities of transplant.

  • Component — replace all attention projections (attn) vs all MLP projections (mlp), across every layer.
  • Contiguous groups — first5 / mid5 / last5 / each half (both components).
  • 4-layer MLP blocks — B1…B7 (28-layer) / B1…B8 (32-layer), transplanted alone or in combination.

Four benchmarks, two manual metrics.

TwinPrompt paired SGXSTest paired AdvBench harmful OR-Bench over-refusal

Subsets are filtered to prompts where the aligned model refuses and the base complies (≤ 30 each), sharpening contrast; a separate 100/100 paired pool validates at scale. Block importance is mapped via greedy forward selection per model–dataset pair. Every refusal label is manually inspected.

▲ MR — malicious refusal (higher better) ▼ BOR — benign over-refusal (lower better)

Adding the last aligned block reduces refusal. 5 of 6 greedy trajectories show a negative marginal effect.

  • Block 3 (layers 8–11) is the locus — strongest single block in both families; selected first in all 6 greedy searches. On 100-prompt validation: 13/100 refusals (highest of 7 blocks) with only 1/100 over-refusal.
  • Layer 8 dominates within Block 3; subsets of Block-3 layers match full-block performance.
  • Refusal & over-refusal co-localize in MLP — full-MLP BOR 11/30 vs attention 0/30. Precision isn't free by transplanting more.

Strengths

what holds up
  • Matched-pair design makes weight-space attribution unambiguous — sidesteps the confounds of cross-model comparisons.
  • Manual inspection of every refusal label is unusually rigorous for an empirical safety paper; avoids auto-judge failure modes.
  • The non-additivity / negative-marginal-effect finding is the most provocative contribution — it directly contradicts “more aligned weights = safer.”
  • Honest about scope: explicitly flags n=2 pairs, filtered subsets, and within-distribution validation.
  • Connects localization to deployment-relevant framing — precision–coverage trade-off, refusal/over-refusal co-localization.

Limitations

where it frays
  • Only 2 model pairs, 7B–8B; no test of whether layers-8–11 is an absolute-index or relative-depth property; no MoE / larger scale.
  • Primary subsets ≤ 30 prompts, filtered → many block-ranking claims within noise; biased toward alignment-critical prompts, not the general distribution.
  • The saferlhf_ultra_sft family is too weak at block level (mostly 0–2 refusals), so fine-grained analyses run only on RealSafe-R1-7B.
  • Greedy forward selection misses interactions; no exhaustive/randomized search, no random-subset lower bound, no 4-layer block-size ablation.
  • Localizes where safety is stored, not how it is computed — no activation-patching / causal-tracing follow-up.

Questions for Further Exploration

Q1Does the layers-8–11 locus generalize as absolute depth or a relative depth fraction (~30–40% in)? Testing 1B / 13B / 70B / MoE would disambiguate.
Q2Would a randomized or exhaustive block search beat greedy subsets by a larger margin — or reveal structured higher-order interactions it obscures?
Q3Can non-additivity be predicted from representation-space structure — do interfering blocks share overlapping refusal directions (Wollschlager cones)?
Q4Is refusal/over-refusal co-localization breakable — a subset maximizing MR while keeping BOR ≈ 0 on an unfiltered distribution?
Q5How do localized weights interact with activation-level interventions (Arditi's direction, CAST, LED)? Combining the two could separate storage from computation.
Q6Does targeted fine-tuning / unlearning on only Block 3 degrade safety faster than uniform updates — as the “brittleness from localization” hypothesis predicts?
