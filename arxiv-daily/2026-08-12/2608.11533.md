> **一句话总结：** 在 NCSA Delta（AMD EPYC 7763 Milan，双路128核）上实测了"EPYC 猜想"：把块大小 M_C 从 M1 Pro 继承的 64 修正为按真实 512KB L2 推导出的 256 后，性能提升 30–59%；关键在于把缓存容量、访存模式、co-tenancy（同住线程数）都视为"形状（shape）"参数，而非抽象架构——例如在受控 NUMA 实验中，GEBP 因远程内存损失 13.9% 而 MoA-pipelined 仅损失 2.4%，尽管原始 STREAM 带宽差异不到 1%。

> **TL;DR:** This paper tests the "EPYC Conjecture" (per-CCD recalibration of MoA-guided dense matmul) on NCSA Delta (AMD EPYC 7763 "Milan", dual-socket 128 cores) after the originally-targeted Bergamo allocation was declined. Its central thesis is that performance differences trace to *shape* — cache capacity divided by data-type byte width and co-tenancy, plus the access pattern a kernel traces through memory — not abstract architecture. The cache-block size M_C = 256, derived from Delta's real 512KB private L2 with zero post-hoc fitting, beats the M1-Pro-inherited M_C = 64 by 30–59% across all sizes, and a controlled NUMA test shows MoA-pipelined loses only 2.4% to remote memory vs GEBP's 13.9% at <1% bandwidth difference. The headline result reproduces the M1 Pro's exact three-of-four win over a Strassen-GEBP hybrid (winning at n=1024/2048/4096 by 1.644×/1.108×/1.053×, losing only at n=8192 at 0.589×) — a pattern AWS Graviton4 did not achieve — on the NCSA Delta benchmark.

## 核心贡献

- **形状优先（shape-first）的统一解释框架：** 把缓存容量、co-tenancy（同住消费者数）、以及内核访存模式都形式化为"形状"参数。块大小不是事后调出来的常数，而是 $M_C = C_{L2}/(K_C \times 8)$ 的直接推导——把物理容量（字节）按数据类型字节宽（double = 8）归一化成可数数组维度。
- **M_C 的零拟合校准得到 30–59% 提升：** 从 Delta 真实的 512KB 私有 L2 推出 $M_C = 256$，在所有测试矩阵尺寸上击败 M1 Pro 继承的 $M_C = 64$（n=8192 处 +30%，n=1024 处 +59%），是该系列扩展工作中最大的单点提升。
- **co-tenancy 即形状参数的实验证据：** 把安全裕度 $\sigma$ 当作整个 CCD 32MB 的直接分数得到 1/64，与 M1 Pro 的 1/6 相差 10.7×；改为"先除以线程数得到每线程 4MB 名义份额再算 $\sigma'$=1/8"才与 M1 Pro 量级吻合——证伪了对 H2 假设的朴素解读。
- **受控 NUMA 实验分离"访存模式"与"带宽"：** 固定 P=64、只变内存位置（local vs remote），原始 STREAM 带宽仅差 0.9%，但 GEBP 损失 13.9%、recursive-classical 损失 15.1%、MoA-pipelined 仅损失 2.4%。MoA 的 DNF 派生访存模式在 NUMA 下天然更鲁棒——这是原猜想未预料、本文也未去寻找的发现。
- **在 Delta 上完整复现 M1 Pro 的"四中三胜"头条结果：** task-based MoA 在 cutoff=512 下对 Strassen-GEBP 混合算法赢 n=1024/2048/4096（1.644×/1.108×/1.053×），仅输 n=8192（0.589×），精确复刻 M1 Pro 模式——而 Graviton4 只达到四中二胜。
- **诚实地把一个未解问题点名：** Delta 上 MoA/GEBP 比值（n=4096 处 2.29×）远大于另两平台，但本文不给速度论解释，因为 P=8 在 M1 Pro/Graviton4 是整机、在 Delta 仅 1/16，无法在匹配利用率下公平比较——留作下一个可证伪的开放问题。

## Key Contributions

- **A unified, shape-first causal account** that treats cache capacity, co-tenancy, and kernel access pattern as *shape* parameters rather than independent tuning knobs, formalized in the companion theoretical paper's Mathematics of Arrays (MoA) / Γ-normalization and empirically tested here.
- **Zero post-hoc-fit calibration of the cache block size:** $M_C = 256$ derived directly from Delta's real 512KB private L2 (divided by $K_C$ and the 8-byte double width) beats the M1-Pro-inherited $M_C = 64$ by 30–59% at every size, the single largest improvement reported anywhere in this project's extensions.
- **Demonstration that co-tenancy is itself a shape parameter:** the naive margin-transfer reading of the safety-margin hypothesis H2 fails by an order of magnitude (1/64 vs M1 Pro's 1/6, a 10.7× mismatch); a per-thread-normalized reading ($\sigma' = 1/8$) survives, isolating exactly the falsifiable form of the per-CCD recalibration half of the EPYC conjecture.
- **A controlled NUMA experiment that decouples access pattern from raw bandwidth:** at P=64 with only memory placement varying, local vs remote differ by <1% in STREAM bandwidth yet GEBP loses 13.9% throughput and MoA-pipelined only 2.4% — establishing that the *shape each kernel's access pattern traces through memory*, not memory speed, determines NUMA penalty.
- **Full reproduction of the M1 Pro headline win pattern** (task-based MoA beats Strassen-GEBP hybrid at three of four sizes, losing only at the largest) on Delta — a result that weakened to two-of-four on AWS Graviton4 — alongside a roofline result (MoA up to 2.29× GEBP at n=4096) stronger than either prior platform.
- **An explicitly named, falsifiable open question** rather than an overclaim: whether Delta's larger MoA-over-GEBP margin reflects genuine speed or an unmatched utilization fraction, since P=8 is the whole machine on M1 Pro/Graviton4 but 1/16 of Delta.

## Methodology

The study runs the verified, correctness-checked C/OpenMP implementations from the companion paper [3] on NCSA Delta CPU nodes (dual-socket AMD EPYC 7763 "Milan", 128 cores total, eight CCDs of eight cores per socket with 32MB L3 per CCD, 512KB private L2 and 32KB L1 per core), compiled with `gcc -march=znver3` (the correct Zen3 target, distinct from `znver4`/Zen4c that the original Bergamo conjecture assumed) and scheduled under SLURM. Topology was verified directly via `lscpu -e`.

The methodology is built around *predicting then measuring* rather than fitting after the fact. The block size $M_C = 256$ is derived analytically from the real L2 capacity before any timing run, using the identical no-margin formula that exactly reproduced the M1 Pro's own $M_C = 64$ from its 128KB L1. The co-tenancy/safety-margin hypothesis H2 is tested by pinning execution to one CCD (`taskset -c 0-7`) to reproduce the M1 Pro's exact eight-way co-tenancy on a different microarchitecture. The contention sweep spans P=1 through P=128 (full node, both sockets) with parallel efficiency $\eta(P) = \text{achieved}(P)/(P \cdot \text{achieved}(1))$ computed directly.

The controlled NUMA test fixes P=64 and varies only memory placement via `numactl`: local (`--cpunodebind=0 --membind=0`, zero cross-socket traffic) vs remote (`--cpunodebind=1 --membind=0`, every access crosses the socket boundary), with STREAM bandwidth, GEBP, MoA-pipelined, recursive-classical, and the Strassen-GEBP hybrid all measured under both conditions. A variance characterization runs the identical configuration twice back-to-back to compare dedicated bare-metal noise shape against Graviton4's previously observed virtualization-induced shift. The roofline sweep covers five sizes by four thread counts (with three n=8192 configurations added after the first SLURM allocation was exhausted mid-sweep, verified by row-count against the expected twenty).

## Results & Highlights

- **Block-size correction, M_C = 256 vs inherited M_C = 64 (Section 3):** Confirmed at every size, ranging from **+30% at n=8192 to +59% at n=1024** — the largest single improvement reported across this project's extensions. Correcting M_C also shifted the optimal N_C: at M_C=64 every size preferred N_C=2048, while at the corrected M_C=256 the optimum drops to N_C=1024 for three of four sizes, confirming M_C and N_C are not independently optimizable.
- **Controlled NUMA test at fixed P=64 (Table 1):** STREAM bandwidth drops only **0.9% (27.24 → 26.99 GB/s)** under remote placement, while kernel throughput drops **GEBP 13.9% (104.90 → 90.27 GFLOP/s)**, **recursive-classical 15.1% (161.33 → 137.03 GFLOP/s)**, **hybrid 8.7% (156.44 → 142.75 GFLOP/s)**, and **MoA-pipelined only 2.4% (190.15 → 185.54 GFLOP/s)** — a fivefold smaller NUMA penalty for MoA than GEBP despite identical available bandwidth.
- **Headline task-based MoA vs Strassen-GEBP hybrid, cutoff=512 (Section 8):** MoA wins at **n=1024 (1.644×), n=2048 (1.108×), n=4096 (1.053×)** and loses only at **n=8192 (0.589×)** — the exact three-of-four pattern the M1 Pro reported (Graviton4 achieved only two-of-four). At the best per-size cutoff the n=4096 margin widens to 1.288× and n=8192 closes to 0.858×, the closest to parity on any platform tested.
- **Roofline MoA/GEBP ratio (Section 9):** MoA reaches **1.19× GEBP at n=1024, 2.07× at n=2048, and 2.29× at n=4096** (8 threads) — more than double GEBP's throughput, a ratio not approached on either M1 Pro (≈1.06× parity at n=8192) or Graviton4.
- **Parallel efficiency across the full P=1–128 sweep (Section 5):** $\eta$ declines from **0.744 at P=8** to **0.575 at P=16**, **0.468 at P=32**, down to **0.150 at P=128** (full node). The single steepest per-doubling drop is **P=32→64 (η ratio 0.543)** — entirely within one socket — while the actual cross-socket step P=64→128 gives η ratio 0.591 (459.4 vs 389.4 GFLOP/s), *not* distinctly worse. Optimal N_C plateaus at 2048 from P=16 through P=128.
- **Variance characterization (Section 10):** Two back-to-back identical Delta runs show a **15% mean absolute difference**, *larger* than Graviton4's ~10% shift — but Delta's variance is scattered/uncorrelated (17 of 25 points higher in one run, 8 in the other, swings up to ±29%) versus Graviton4's one-directional uniform shift, a qualitatively different noise shape.
- **Cross-machine efficiency at matched full utilization (Section 11):** Delta's $\eta(128) = 0.150$ is substantially worse than M1 Pro's $\eta(8) = 0.473$ or Graviton4's $\eta(8) = 0.540$ — using all of a 128-core node costs proportionally more efficiency than using all of an 8-core one, agreeing with the original conjecture's underlying concern.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: Lenore M. Mullin, College of Nanotechnology, Science, and Engineering, University at Albany, SUNY. The work is the empirical companion to a theoretical Mathematics-of-Arrays formalization [2] and an earlier experimental study submitted to ACM TOMS [3]. It runs on a peer-reviewed national resource (NCSA Delta via ACCESS allocation `bibg-delta-cpu`, partition `cpu`), uses SLURM-exclusive bare-metal allocation, and verifies topology directly with `lscpu -e`. The honesty of the framing is notable: the paper explicitly reports results on a *related but distinct* chip (Milan/Zen3, dual-socket) rather than the originally-named Bergamo (Zen4c, single-socket 128-core), after the REPACSS allocation was declined, and is careful to separate what confirms the conjecture from what only speaks to Milan's specific topology. The Mathematics of Arrays formalism is the author's own long-running research program (the $\Gamma$ operator), giving the work strong internal theoretical grounding rather than ad-hoc empiricism.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The conceptual move — treating cache capacity, co-tenancy, and access pattern uniformly as *shape* parameters rather than independent tuning knobs — is genuinely interesting and distinguishes this from conventional auto-tuning literature. The controlled NUMA experiment that decouples access pattern from raw bandwidth (P fixed, only placement varied) is a clean, well-isolated design.
- **Rigor:** Strong on falsifiability: predictions are stated before measurement, the per-CCD pinning reproduces matched co-tenancy on a different microarchitecture, and the SLURM-exclusive allocation plus back-to-back variance runs are methodologically careful. The single-socket vs dual-socket confound (Delta is dual-socket; the conjecture named a single-socket chip) is disclosed rather than hidden.
- **Reproducibility:** Mixed. Code is inherited verified C/OpenMP from the companion study and the hardware (Delta) is a national resource accessible via ACCESS — good. But the work is a single-author, single-machine extension tightly coupled to two companion papers ([2], [3]) that an independent reproducer would also need; precise SLURM/numactl flags are given, but per-run seeds, repetition counts, and full raw timing tables live in the figures rather than being tabulated in-text.
- **Significance:** Moderate and deliberately scoped. It confirms one half of the EPYC conjecture precisely (per-CCD recalibration), *refutes* the other half as stated (fork/join overhead does *not* become more pronounced at higher core counts — it saturates at N_C=2048 from P=16 onward), and surfaces an unanticipated property (MoA's NUMA resilience). None of this yet overturns established high-performance matmul (BLIS, LIBXSMM, oneMKL) — those are not baselines here.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) The headline three-of-four win is over a *self-built* Strassen-GEBP hybrid, not a production-grade library (BLAS/LIBXSMM/GotoBLAS) — so "beats the hybrid" does not mean "beats the SOTA." (2) The roofline ratio of 2.29× MoA-over-GEBP at n=4096 is unusually large and the paper itself flags it as possibly an unmatched-utilization artifact rather than genuine speed — an honest caveat, but it means the most striking number is the least explained. (3) The fork/join half of the conjecture is effectively falsified yet framed softly as "saturates rather than grows," which undersells the negative result. (4) The steepest efficiency drop (P=32→64, within one socket) is attributed to Milan's shared centralized memory channels but the exact mechanism is left open — plausible but unproven. (5) Absolute GFLOP/s figures (e.g., 459.4 at P=128) are not compared against a reference BLAS at the same sizes, so the reader cannot tell whether any of these kernels are fast in absolute terms or only fast relative to each other. (6) The 15% back-to-back run-to-run variance is large for a performance paper and somewhat undercuts the precision of every other percentage reported.

## Strengths

- Predict-then-measure discipline: every key parameter ($M_C$, $\sigma$, the win pattern) is derived before timing, not fitted after, which is the strongest defense against post-hoc rationalization.
- The controlled NUMA design (fix P, vary only placement) cleanly isolates access-pattern shape from bandwidth capacity — a genuinely instructive experimental template.
- Intellectual honesty about scope: the paper repeatedly distinguishes "confirms the conjecture" from "speaks only to Milan," names its open questions explicitly, and does not overclaim the large MoA/GEBP ratio.
- Cross-platform reproducibility of a qualitative pattern (the three-of-four win) across M1 Pro and now Milan, with the negative Graviton4 result reported rather than suppressed.
- The shape formalism gives a re-derivable recipe: changing the data type (e.g., to single precision) would shift every block size by exactly the byte-width ratio, a testable prediction.

## Limitations

- **Wrong target chip:** the conjecture named Bergamo (Zen4c, single-socket 128-core); Delta is Milan (Zen3, dual-socket), introducing a cross-socket NUMA confound the original chip does not have. Results are evidence about a related machine, not the one conjectured.
- **No production BLAS baseline:** comparisons are against the author's own GEBP / Strassen-GEBP hybrid kernels, not against BLIS, LIBXSMM, oneMKL, or vendor BLAS — so absolute competitiveness with deployed libraries is untested.
- **Negative result understated:** the fork/join-overhead half of the conjecture is falsified (overhead saturates rather than growing), but the framing ("saturates from P=16") softens what is fundamentally a refutation.
- **Unexplained flagship number:** the 2.29× MoA/GEBP ratio is flagged by the authors themselves as possibly an unmatched-utilization artifact, and no speed-based explanation is offered.
- **Variance is large:** 15% mean absolute difference between two back-to-back identical runs (larger than virtualized Graviton4's ~10%) raises questions about the precision of every percentage claim, though the noise *shape* differs usefully.
- **Single author, single machine, companion-paper dependency:** independent reproduction requires both the code from [3] and the formalism from [2]; the theoretical claims about Γ-normalization are developed elsewhere and only gestured at here.

## Relevance

对于关注高性能数值计算内核（稠密矩阵乘法、缓存分块、roofline/NUMA 调优）的研究者，本文提供了一套"形状优先"的可迁移调优方法论——把缓存容量、数据类型字节宽、co-tenancy 和访存模式统一当作形状参数推导块大小，而非逐机台经验拟合，这与 cs.PF（性能）方向的核心问题（跨架构可移植的高性能内核设计）直接相关，且其受控 NUMA 实验设计可作为分离"带宽"与"访存模式"的模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do MoA-pipelined and GEBP compare against production BLAS (BLIS, LIBXSMM, oneMKL, AMD AOCL) at the same sizes and thread counts on Delta, in absolute GFLOP/s — is the MoA advantage robust to a serious library baseline or only to the self-built hybrid?
- Does the MoA-over-GEBP ratio of 2.29× at n=4096 survive once both kernels are measured at a properly matched utilization fraction (e.g., both at their own peak-bandwidth thread count, not an arbitrary P=8)?
- Would re-running on the originally-conjectured Bergamo (Zen4c, single-socket 128-core) reproduce the per-CCD recalibration result without the dual-socket NUMA confound Delta introduces?
- What happens to the optimal $M_C$, $N_C$, and the MoA/GEBP ratio if the data type is changed to single precision (byte width 4) — the paper predicts a deterministic shift, but is this verified empirically?
- What is the exact mechanism behind the steepest efficiency drop at P=32→64 (within one socket) — is it Milan's centralized eight-channel memory subsystem saturating, and can a finer-grained non-power-of-two sweep pinpoint the thread count at which it begins?
- Can the back-to-back 15% variance be reduced (e.g., by frequency pinning, NUMA binding of the OpenMP runtime, or longer warm-up), and does the scattered/uncorrelated noise shape persist across more than two repetitions?
- Does MoA-pipelined's NUMA resilience (2.4% vs GEBP's 13.9% remote penalty) generalize to other bandwidth-bound kernels (e.g., tensor contractions, batched GEMM, stencils) beyond dense matmul?
