> **一句话总结：** 本文系统评估了三类单轮不确定性量化方法（白盒 token 概率、黑盒采样一致性、模型自评）在多轮工具调用 LLM 智能体上的迁移效果，提出多种轨迹级聚合与一致性度量（含基于判别器的轨迹等价率 TER），发现在 5 个模型、4 个数据集上迁移效果"有用但不均"——没有任何单一方法在所有模型与领域上可靠，且白盒得分对聚合规则极度敏感。

> **TL;DR:** The authors adapt three families of single-turn UQ scorers (white-box token probabilities, black-box trajectory-consistency, and reflexive self-assessment) to multi-turn tool-using LLM agents by adding cross-turn aggregators and new trajectory-level consistency measures (including a judge-based Trajectory Equivalence Rate, TER). Evaluated on BFCL-v4 and three τ²-bench domains across five LLMs, transfer is "useful but uneven": TER reaches 0.868 AUROC on airline (Qwen3.5-9B) versus a 0.5 chance baseline, but 91 of 240 white-box cells fall below chance and switching the aggregator for the same base score can swing AUROC from 0.725 to 0.228, so no single family is reliable across models and domains.

## 核心贡献

- **首次在匹配条件下系统比较三类 UQ 方法**：在 LLM 智能体的轨迹层面，统一评估白盒（token 概率）、黑盒（采样一致性）与反思（模型自评）三类打分器对轨迹成功与否的判别能力，填补了此前没有在多模型、多数据集上一致对比的空白。
- **提出轨迹级聚合与一致性度量**：将单轮白盒打分器通过 first/mean/min/last 等跨轮聚合扩展到轨迹级；提出新的黑盒一致性打分器——首动作一致性（FAC）、动作集合一致性（ASC）、动作分布一致性（ADC）、动作编辑一致性（AEC），以及基于 LLM 判别器的轨迹等价率（TER）。
- **给出迁移效果的"不均"结论与实用指引**：明确指出白盒方法对聚合规则高度敏感（同一基础打分器换聚合可让 AUROC 从 0.725 跌到 0.228），反思打分器是低成本强基线，而黑盒自一致性（尤其 TER 与 ASC）常是最强但需要额外采样轨迹；并给出按预算（额外轨迹数、辅助模型调用）的成本-判别力权衡表。

## Key Contributions

- **First controlled, matched-condition comparison** of the three single-turn UQ families (white-box, black-box consistency, reflexive) at the trajectory level for tool-using LLM agents, across five LLMs and four multi-turn datasets — prior agent-UQ work had not compared probability-, sampling-, and reflexive-based methods under matched conditions.
- **Trajectory-adapted scorers**: cross-turn aggregators (first/mean/min/last, plus position-weighted variants in the appendix) for white-box per-turn scores, computed over the action span; and new black-box consistency measures over the action structure — First-Action Consistency (FAC), Action-Set Consistency (Jaccard over action-type sets, ASC), Action-Distribution Consistency (1 − Jensen-Shannon, ADC), Action-Edit Consistency (normalized Levenshtein over ordered action-type sequences, AEC), and a judge-based Trajectory Equivalence Rate (TER) where a separate LLM reads two full transcripts and judges outcome equivalence (not correctness).
- **A nuanced, deployable "transfer is useful but uneven" finding** with concrete cost/benefit guidance: no single family or scorer is reliable across all models and domains; white-box scores are cheap but aggregator-sensitive; reflexive scorers are the strongest low-cost baseline (one extra pass); black-box self-consistency (especially TER and ASC) is often the strongest family but incurs the cost of m resampled trajectories.

## Methodology

The paper builds on the trajectory-UQ formalism of Oh et al. (2026): an episode is a trajectory F of up to T turns, each turn emits an action A_i (tool call or user message) and receives an observation O_i, ending with a binary reward r(F). A scorer is a map C: F → [0,1] (larger = more confident the trajectory succeeded). For black-box consistency the signature extends to C(F, F̃) where F̃ is a set of m resampled trajectories.

**White-box scorers** are computed over the *action span* of each turn (the committed action tokens, not reasoning). Single-generation base scores include Sequence Probability (SP), Length-Normalized Sequence Probability (LNSP, geometric mean), and Average Token Neg-entropy over top-K (ATN@K). Per-turn scores s_i are combined via cross-turn aggregators: g_first = s_1, g_mean = (1/T)Σ s_i, g_min = min_i s_i, g_last = s_T (position-weighted early/late variants in Appendix A).

**Black-box consistency scorers** sample m=3 trajectories at temperature 0.7 and compare the reference to the samples along three surfaces: (1) Final-Message Consistency via Non-Contradiction Probability (NCP) using a DeBERTa-large-MNLI NLI model; (2) Action-Structured Consistency comparing action-type sequences symbolically (FAC first-action match, ASC Jaccard over sets, ADC 1−JSD over distributions, AEC 1−normalized Levenshtein over ordered sequences); (3) Judged Consistency where a gemini-flash-lite judge reads two full transcripts and decides outcome equivalence (TER), never seeing the hidden task spec or reward.

**Reflexive scorers** ask the model to judge its own trajectory once, over the transcript prefix ending at the final action: P(True) (probability assigned to "True" that the trajectory succeeded) and Verbalized Confidence (VC, a Yes/No guess plus a 0–1 probability; confidence is the probability for "Yes" or its complement for "No").

**Evaluation**: primary metric is AUROC for predicting greedy-trajectory success, with task-clustered bootstrap intervals (1000 resamples); AUPRC, Prediction Rejection Ratio (PRR), and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) reported in appendices. Each task contributes one greedy reference trajectory (temperature 0, K=5 top logprobs per token) plus three sampled trajectories for consistency. The simulated user is fixed to gpt-4.1-mini at temperature 0.

## Results & Highlights

- **Black-box judged consistency (TER) is the single strongest cell**: TER reaches **0.868 AUROC on airline for Qwen3.5-9B** (chance = 0.5), and ASC reaches **0.819 AUROC for gpt-oss-20b on airline**; on BFCL-v4, action-/trajectory-level agreement reaches about 0.71–0.77 AUROC for four of five models.
- **Reflexive scorers dominate BFCL-v4 at low cost**: P(True) ranges **0.746–0.852 AUROC** across models on BFCL-v4 (gpt-oss-20b VC interval [0.749, 0.906] despite only 5% success rate), and VC reaches at least 0.75 on three models — making reflexive scoring (one extra pass) the strongest low-cost baseline.
- **White-box scores are alarmingly aggregator-sensitive**: for the *same* base score SPmean on Qwen3.5-9B telecom, switching the cross-turn aggregation moves AUROC **from 0.725 to 0.228**; across the white-box grid, **91 of 240 cells fall below 0.5 AUROC and 21 have bootstrap intervals entirely below chance**. The largest τ²-bench white-box AUROC is only 0.725 (SPmean, Qwen3.5-9B telecom).
- **SAUP-inspired propagation controls underperform**: the strongest propagation control averages only **0.434 AUROC across the fifteen τ²-bench model-dataset cells**, below even the strongest fixed naive aggregation (SPlast at 0.53).
- **Family-level cost/discrimination tradeoff (Table 2, m=3)**: White-box mean AUROC 0.628 (peak 0.725) at zero extra calls; Reflexive mean 0.691 (peak 0.885) at one extra pass; Action-consistency mean 0.705 (peak 0.849); Message-consistency (NCP) mean 0.603 (peak 0.832); Judged consistency (TER) mean 0.686 (peak 0.868) with the gemini-flash-lite judge costing only ~$0.002/task.
- **Metric agreement is high**: scorer rankings under AUROC correlate with AUPRC and PRR with **median Spearman rank correlation 0.94 (AUPRC) and 0.95 (PRR)** across model-dataset cells; bootstrap interval median half-width is 0.10 in columns with ≥20 minority-class trajectories.
- **Judge robustness for TER**: validated against database-state ground truth over 1,365 pairs the equivalence judge attains **0.88 precision and 0.93 recall**; swapping gemini-flash-lite for the larger gemini-flash agrees on 91% of pairs and moves TER AUROC by at most 0.062.
- **Selective-prediction sign instability**: LNSPmin attains **0.44 PRR on retail but −0.28 on telecom** for gpt-4o-mini, i.e. abstaining on its least-confident trajectories there retains a worse success rate than random.

## Authors & Credibility

Dylan Bouchard (corresponding author) and Mohit Singh Chauhan are established researchers in LLM uncertainty quantification: they co-authored the "UQ for Language Models: A Suite of Black-Box, White-Box, LLM Judge, and Ensemble Scorers" paper in TMLR (Bouchard & Chauhan, 2025), maintain the UQLM Python package (Bouchard et al., 2026b, JMLR), and have prior work on fine-grained long-form UQ (Bouchard et al., 2026a). This direct lineage means the scorer implementations and evaluation methodology are mature rather than prototypes. The paper is notably self-critical: it prominently flags minority-class cells († for n<20), reports where scorers anti-correlate with success as prominently as where they work, and includes a strong Ethical Considerations section warning against treating any unvalidated signal as a safety gate.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine)**: The systematic, matched-condition comparison is a real gap-filler — prior agent-UQ work (SAUP/UProp) proposed propagation but never compared the three families head-to-head. The new action-structured consistency scorers (FAC/ASC/ADC/AEC) and the outcome-equivalence judge (TER) are concrete, reusable contributions, not relabelings.
- **Rigor (strong)**: Task-clustered bootstrap intervals with explicit minority-class flags, a judge-dependence ablation (gemini-flash swap), an interface ablation (native tool calling on retail + gpt-4o-mini), and a prefix-conditioned ablation (resampling only the next action on airline, peaking at just 0.588 AUROC) all support the claims. The authors repeatedly tell the reader *not* to read small margins as established rankings.
- **Weak/missing comparisons**: The SAUP and UProp baselines are explicitly treated as *controls* rather than faithful reproductions (SAUP is defined for ReAct thought-action-observation traces and needs situational-state supervision unavailable here), so the headline "propagation underperforms" is suggestive rather than a knockout. The native tool-calling ablation covers only one model and one domain, yet white-box results are generalized as "deployment variables." The white-box main table reports only SP/LNSP/ATN@5; probability-margin and position-weighted aggregators are buried in the appendix.
- **Data quirks worth flagging**: Retail labels are partly generated by gpt-4.1 natural-language assertion judgments (Appendix B), so retail reflexive results inherit a judge dependence that is acknowledged only in a footnote. The black-box consistency trajectories resample the *joint agent-user system* (simulated user is resampled too), so "agent uncertainty" is conflated with agent+user variability; the agent-only airline ablation is the only direct mitigation.
- **Reproducibility**: Datasets (BFCL-v4, τ²-bench) and models are public; the numeric grid is promised alongside the code. Per-domain task counts are modest (airline = 50 tasks, several cells with minority n<20), so absolute numbers will shift with resampling — the authors are appropriately cautious, but readers wanting "the best scorer" will not find a single answer.
- **Significance**: Practitioners building agent abstention/escalation will find the cost/benefit table immediately useful; the negative result (white-box confidence is not a safe drop-in) is arguably more valuable than the positive ones.
- **Red flags**: None fraudulent. The main risk is over-reading point estimates in small-n cells; the authors mitigate this with † flags and bootstrap intervals.

## Strengths

- Comprehensive grid (5 models × 4 datasets × ~20 scorers × multiple aggregators) with honest, wide bootstrap intervals and explicit flags for underpowered cells.
- Novel trajectory-level scorers (ASC, TER) that reach strong AUROC (0.82–0.87) on airline where single-turn-style NCP also peaks at 0.832.
- Practical, provider-independent cost accounting (Table 2) that lets practitioners pick a scorer by budget rather than AUROC alone.
- Refreshingly self-critical framing: reports anti-correlated cells and sign-flipping aggregators as first-class findings, and includes a safety-oriented Ethical Considerations section.
- Clean ablations isolating the action-interface confound and the next-action-vs-full-trajectory signal source.

## Limitations

- **Narrow benchmark scope**: only tool-use agents (BFCL-v4, three τ²-bench text domains); web-browsing, embodied, and open-ended agents are explicitly untested.
- **Small per-domain task counts** (airline = 50; many minority classes n<20) yield wide intervals; many "best scorer" margins are within noise.
- **Text-action interface**: white-box logprob results rely on the agent emitting actions as text; native tool calling is ablated on only one model+domain and is not uniformly usable across the model ladder.
- **Simulated-user confound**: trajectories are produced against a fixed gpt-4.1-mini user, and consistency trajectories resample the joint agent+user system, so scorers measure joint-system variability rather than pure agent uncertainty.
- **Single judge for TER**: only gemini-flash-lite (validated with one gemini-flash swap); more distant judge models untested.
- **No end-to-end test**: scores are evaluated for *after-the-fact* success classification only — whether acting on them (abstention, clarification, escalation) improves outcomes is out of scope.
- **Action-only scope**: observation uncertainty, white-box scores over reasoning tokens, and step-level labels by action type/tool family are all left to future work.

## Relevance

高度相关：本文直接研究 LLM 智能体在多轮工具调用中的不确定性量化与可靠性，涵盖自一致性、校准与选择性预测等核心议题，对构建可安全部署（能在执行错误动作前选择弃权或升级人工）的智能体系统具有直接的工程与研究方向价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do these scorers retain (or improve) their ranking when used *online* to trigger abstention, clarification requests, or human escalation mid-trajectory, rather than only for after-the-fact success classification?
- Would step-level success labels (per action type / tool family) reveal that uncertainty is concentrated in specific tools (e.g., state-changing vs. read-only), enabling targeted calibration?
- Can a cheap ensemble — e.g., reflexive P(True) as a prior gated by TER on the uncertain tail — beat any single family at fixed compute budget?
- How do the results transfer to web-browsing or embodied agents where action spaces and observation noise differ sharply from API tool-use?
- How much of the black-box consistency signal is genuinely agent uncertainty versus joint agent+user variability, given that the simulated user is resampled alongside the agent?
- Is there a principled, data-driven way to *select* the cross-turn aggregator per model+domain, rather than validating each one post hoc?
