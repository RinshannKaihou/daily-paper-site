Key Finding · arXiv 2608.11552

Single-turn uncertainty quantification transfers to multi-turn LLM agents — but unevenly, with no scorer universally reliable.

Why This Matters

Quantifies whether an agent can safely abstain or escalate to a human before executing a wrong action — a prerequisite for trustworthy deployment.

Critical Edge · the floor

White-box confidence is alarmingly unstable — switching the aggregator alone swings AUROC from 0.725 to 0.228, and 91 / 240 cells fall below chance.

Not a safe drop-in signal

Methodology

Three scorer families, adapted to the trajectory

Built on the trajectory-UQ formalism of Oh et al. (2026): an episode is a trajectory of up to T turns, each emitting an action and an observation, ending with a binary reward. A scorer maps a trajectory to a confidence in [0, 1]. The primary metric is AUROC for predicting greedy-trajectory success, with task-clustered bootstrap intervals (1000 resamples); AUPRC, PRR, and ECE appear in the appendices.

White-box

Token-level scores (Sequence Probability, length-normalized, token neg-entropy) computed over each turn's action span, then aggregated across turns via first / mean / min / last. Zero extra calls.

Black-box consistency

Sample m=3 trajectories (temp 0.7) and compare along message (NCP via DeBERTa-NLI), action-structure (FAC, ASC, ADC, AEC), and judged equivalence (TER). Strongest family, costs m samples.

Reflexive

The model self-judges its own transcript once: P(True) and Verbalized Confidence. One extra pass — the strongest low-cost baseline.

Strengths

  • Comprehensive grid — 5 models × 4 datasets × ~20 scorers × aggregators, with honest bootstrap intervals and explicit underpowered-cell (†) flags.
  • Novel trajectory scorers (ASC, TER) reaching 0.82–0.87 AUROC on airline where message-level NCP peaks at 0.832.
  • Provider-independent cost accounting (Table 2) — pick a scorer by budget, not AUROC alone.
  • Self-critical framing — anti-correlated cells and sign-flipping aggregators reported as first-class findings; includes a safety-oriented Ethics section.
  • Clean ablations isolating the action-interface confound and next-action-vs-full-trajectory signal source.

Limitations

  • Narrow scope — only tool-use agents (BFCL-v4, 3 τ²-bench domains); web, embodied, and open-ended agents untested.
  • Small per-domain counts (airline = 50; many n<20) → wide intervals; most "best scorer" margins sit within noise.
  • Text-action interface — white-box logprobs need text actions; native tool-calling ablated on only one model+domain.
  • Simulated-user confound — consistency resamples the joint agent+user system, not pure agent uncertainty.
  • No end-to-end test — after-the-fact classification only; whether acting on scores (abstain / clarify / escalate) improves outcomes is out of scope.
  • Single TER judge (gemini-flash-lite; one swap to gemini-flash); more distant judges untested.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Do rankings hold when scorers trigger online abstention, clarification, or escalation mid-trajectory?
  2. Would step-level labels reveal uncertainty concentrated in specific tools (state-changing vs. read-only)?
  3. Can a cheap ensemble — reflexive P(True) as a prior, gated by TER on the uncertain tail — beat any single family at fixed compute?
  4. How do results transfer to web-browsing or embodied agents with different action spaces and observation noise?
  5. How much of the consistency signal is genuine agent uncertainty vs. joint agent+user variability?
  6. Is there a principled, data-driven way to select the cross-turn aggregator per model+domain, rather than post-hoc?
