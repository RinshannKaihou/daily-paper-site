> **一句话总结：** 在 S&P 500 截面波动率预测上系统评估 560 个 walk-forward 模型后发现：8-bit 与仅权重量化的 4-bit 几乎无损，但默认 abs-max 校准下的静态 W4A4 会抹去 11–62% 的全精度 IC；改用百分位校准可在四个最受损架构中恢复 53–94% 的损失，且最优校准范围随市场波动率体制漂移（2020 COVID、2022 加息与 2021 平静期方向相反）。

> **TL;DR:** A systematic walk-forward study (8 test years 2018–2025, 560 trained models) of post-training activation calibration for cross-sectional 5-day volatility forecasting on the S&P 500 finds 8-bit and weight-only 4-bit quantization nearly harmless, but static W4A4 under default abs-max calibration strips 11–62% of the full-precision mean IC across seven architectures; replacing abs-max with percentile calibration recovers 53–94% of this loss in the four most affected models (e.g., Transformer +0.123 → +0.007 IC, range-recoverable share R=94%), while SegRNN/TimeMixer retain large residual damage, and the optimal range itself flips between COVID-stress and calm years.

## 核心贡献

- 首个在 walk-forward 协议下系统研究金融预测中 PTQ 激活校准的工作，覆盖 7 种代表性神经网络架构（DLinear、TSMixer、TimeMixer、vanilla Transformer、PatchTST、iTransformer、SegRNN）、2018–2025 八个测试年、共 560 个独立训练模型。
- 揭示 4-bit 激活量化损伤可分解为"范围可恢复损失"与"架构相关残余损失"两部分：改进校准范围可在 Transformer/TSMixer 等架构上恢复大部分损伤，但循环（SegRNN）与多尺度（TimeMixer）架构仍有大量不可恢复残余。
- 把激活范围选择拆解为"范围–分辨率"权衡：窄范围提升常规日内分辨率但裁剪尾部，宽范围反之；并据此解释为何 abs-max 在多数架构上处于高损伤侧。
- 证明最优校准范围随市场体制漂移：测试年比校准年更波动时（2020 COVID、2022 加息）窄百分位代价更高；更平静时（2021 复苏期）abs-max 反成最差设置，二者方向相反。
- 把上述现象工程化为可落地、无需测试期后见之明的部署流程（表 4），并用校准包络（calibration envelope）作为运行期监控信号——越界日的量化损伤是包络内日的 1.5–3.6×。

## Key Contributions

- First systematic study of activation calibration for PTQ in financial forecasting under a walk-forward protocol; spans 7 architectures, 8 test years (2018–2025), and 560 independently trained S&P 500 models, isolating the effect of activation-range selection by comparing each quantized model only against its own FP32 checkpoint.
- Decomposition of W4A4 damage into a range-recoverable share R and a residual loss L: percentile calibration recovers 94% (Transformer), 80% (TSMixer), 73% (SegRNN), and 53% (TimeMixer) of the abs-max damage, while recurrent and multi-scale architectures remain substantially sensitive.
- A formal range–resolution/clipping trade-off analysis (Eq. 1, Figure 2) showing default abs-max sits on the high-damage side of the curve for all four most-affected architectures, with the optimum typically between p98 and p99.9.
- A layerwise attribution: for the vanilla Transformer, quantizing only the convolutional token-embedding layer reproduces 72% of full-model W4A4 damage, and protecting that single layer at 8 bits drops damage from +0.123 to +0.015 IC — essentially eliminating it; TSMixer has no comparable single-layer bottleneck.
- A market-regime sensitivity analysis using the daily cross-sectional return dispersion σ_t and a calibration envelope (max σ_t of the preceding validation year): out-of-envelope test days suffer 1.5–3.6× the p99 damage of inside-envelope days; matched 2020 recalibration removes 27–44% of the COVID-year excess damage.
- A hindsight-free deployment rule: choose the percentile on the validation year and apply it to the following test year; mean regret vs. the oracle is 0.007 IC (median 0.002), matching or beating a fixed p99 rule in 27 of 32 architecture–fold cells.

## Methodology

The study targets cross-sectional prediction of 5-day-forward volatility on a daily panel of the 501 current S&P 500 constituents from June 2008 to December 2025 (~2.1M asset-days). Each input sample is a 64-day history of 11 standardized features (5/10/20-day mean returns, 20-day volatility, 14-day RSI, MACD and its 9-day signal, 20-day max/min/skewness). The supervised target is the within-date cross-sectional z-score of log 5-day realized volatility; skill is the daily cross-sectional information coefficient IC_t = Spearman_i(ŷ_{i,t}, y_{i,t}), averaged over all test dates.

The walk-forward protocol fixes the training window at 7 years (Y−8 … Y−2), validation at Y−1, and test at Y, producing 8 folds for Y ∈ 2018–2025. Each architecture is trained with 10 seeds per fold, giving 8×10×7 = 560 runs. Sample-split leakage is prevented by assigning each sample to a split via the last date of its 5-day outcome window t+5, while the 64-day input window may reach backward across a boundary. Hyperparameters are selected once on a dev pseudo-fold (train 2009–2015, val 2016) via equal-budget grid search over d_model ∈ {16,32,64,128,256} and dropout ∈ {0.1,0.3,0.5}, then frozen. Models are trained with AdamW (batch 1024, MSE loss, early stopping patience 20, max 300 epochs).

Quantization uses simulated symmetric round-to-nearest with 15 representable levels at 4 bits and per-tensor activation ranges with per-channel weight scales. Treatments are FP32 (reference), W8A8 (static INT8/INT8), W4 (INT4 weights, FP32 activations), W4A4 (INT4/INT4 static), plus Dynamic INT8 as a complementary reference. Every learned matrix multiplication (linear projections, conv token embeddings, GRU recurrent products with unrolling) is quantized so coverage is comparable across architectures; biases, normalization layers, and elementwise nonlinearities stay FP32; attention activation-only products stay FP32. Two calibration statistics are compared on a 1,024-window sample from the validation split: abs-max A = max(|x|) and percentile A = P_p(|x|). Quantization damage Δ is the paired per-day FP32-minus-quantized IC, summarized as mean ± s.d. over fold-by-seed cells; interpretation is via effect size against HAR and persistence baselines rather than formal hypothesis tests.

## Results & Highlights

- **Static W4A4 under default abs-max strips 11–62% of FP32 IC.** On the 5-day cross-sectional volatility IC, Transformer loses +0.123 IC (25% of its FP32 IC of 0.490), dropping to 0.367 — below the HAR econometric baseline (0.408). SegRNN loses +0.271 IC (59% of 0.458), dropping to 0.187 — below even the persistence baseline (0.315). TSMixer loses +0.096 IC (20%), TimeMixer +0.188 (62%), iTransformer +0.023 (12%), PatchTST +0.019 (11%).
- **8-bit and weight-only 4-bit are nearly free.** Under static W8A8, six of seven architectures lose ≤0.0007 mean daily IC (<0.2% of FP32 signal for five of them); weight-only W4 costs ≤2.5% for every architecture (TimeMixer and SegRNN largest at ~2.5%, Transformer and TSMixer ≤0.5%).
- **Percentile calibration recovers 53–94% of W4A4 damage in the four most-affected architectures.** Transformer abs-max +0.123 → best percentile +0.007 (R=94%); TSMixer +0.096 → +0.019 (R=80%); SegRNN +0.271 → +0.072 (R=73%); TimeMixer +0.188 → +0.095 (R=53%).
- **Single-layer protection can beat range tuning.** For Transformer, quantizing only the convolutional token-embedding layer reproduces 72% of full W4A4 damage; keeping that one layer at 8 bits while quantizing the rest to 4 bits reduces damage from +0.123 to +0.015 IC, whereas protecting any single TSMixer layer leaves damage near +0.086.
- **Calibration–test mismatch explains 27–44% of the 2020 COVID excess damage.** Matched p99 recalibration of the 2020 fold on 2020 data cuts TSMixer damage from +0.077 to +0.043 (−44%) and SegRNN from +0.164 to +0.119 (−27%); the rest is irreducible clipping of stress-year tails.
- **Narrow ranges fail exactly when reliability matters most.** Under p99, out-of-envelope (stress) test days suffer 1.5–3.6× the quantization damage of inside-envelope days (TimeMixer 1.5×, TSMixer 2.2×, SegRNN 2.5×, Transformer 3.6×); abs-max carries no such penalty.
- **A no-foresight validation rule works.** Selecting the percentile on the validation year and applying it to the test year gives mean regret 0.007 IC (median 0.002) vs. the oracle percentile, and matches or improves on a fixed p99 rule in 27 of 32 architecture–fold cells; the largest failure is SegRNN 2021 (regret 0.057 IC), where a COVID-stress validation percentile is applied to a calm test year.

## Authors & Credibility

The two authors are at the School of Computing, Montclair State University (a public university in New Jersey, USA). The methodology is unusually careful for an empirical PTQ study: it fixes the training-window length across folds to avoid confounds, prevents target-window leakage across split boundaries while allowing input windows to reach backward, selects hyperparameters once on a dev pseudo-fold rather than per-fold, controls quantization coverage across architectures (quantizing the unrolled GRU so SegRNN's recurrent core is actually tested), and uses paired FP32-vs-quantized IC on identical stocks/dates to isolate range selection. The honest framing of the percentile sweep as "diagnostic, not deployable" — followed by a separate validation-year prospective test — is a methodological strength rare in this literature.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely fills a gap. Prior PTQ calibration work targets vision/language (Banner 2019, SmoothQuant, QuaRot, LLM.int8) and prior low-precision time-series work uses retraining or QAT rather than static activation calibration; the closest precedent (Pavlova et al. 2026, trajectory-based sensitivity score) motivates mixed precision but does not systematically study activation-range selection under historical-vs-future distribution shift. The R/L decomposition and the calibration-envelope monitoring signal are novel and reusable.
- **Rigor:** Strong on isolation (each quantized model compared only to its own FP32 checkpoint) and on leakage control. However, the headline percentile optima in Section 6 are selected using test-year outcomes (acknowledged as an upper bound); the deployable validation-year rule in Section 8 is the honest result and is reported with its worst-case failure (SegRNN 2021, 0.057 IC regret).
- **Reproducibility:** Architectures, hyperparameter grid, splits, features, and quantization protocol are fully specified; data sources (Yahoo Finance, Tiingo backfill) are public. Reproducibility is limited by (a) simulated quantization in FP32 rather than a real INT backend, so backend accumulation/efficiency effects are not measured, and (b) per-tensor symmetric round-to-nearest only — no per-channel activations, no stronger PTQ methods (SmoothQuant, QuaRot) tested, which the authors admit may shrink the SegRNN/TimeMixer residual.
- **Significance:** Practically important for any team deploying quantized forecasting models: it shows W4A4 with default calibration can erase a neural model's entire edge over HAR, and gives a concrete, deployable diagnostic table (Table 4). The regime-shift framing (envelope as monitoring signal) is a useful operational idea.
- **Red flags / caveats:** (1) Survivorship bias — the panel uses current S&P 500 constituents, tilting IC levels up; the authors argue this cancels in paired comparisons but it bounds generality of absolute numbers. (2) Only two major stress episodes (2020 COVID, 2022 rate hikes) in eight test years, so regime conclusions rest on few events. (3) Skill is measured by IC alone — no portfolio-level PnL or risk-adjusted return, so the economic value of the recovered IC is unquantified. (4) DLinear's FP32 IC (0.026) is so low that its percentage damage figures are numerically unstable and it functions more as a low-signal control than evidence.

## Strengths

- Clean experimental isolation: only the activation-range statistic varies in the W4A4 columns, so attribution to calibration is unambiguous.
- The R (range-recoverable) / L (residual) decomposition plus the layerwise attribution give an actionable diagnosis, not just a leaderboard.
- Honest separation of diagnostic (test-year oracle) and deployable (validation-year) results, with the worst-case failure reported.
- The calibration-envelope idea turns a known fact (calibration lags the market) into a concrete runtime monitoring signal with quantified damage ratios.
- Practical deployment table (Table 4) maps observed patterns directly to a setting choice (default range / percentile W4A4 / protect-one-layer / fall back to W8A8 or W4).

## Limitations

- Quantization is simulated in FP32 via Q/DQ; real-hardware INT4 backends, accumulation order, and latency/memory savings are not measured.
- Only symmetric round-to-nearest with per-tensor activations and per-channel weights is tested; per-channel activations, affine/asymmetric quantizers, and stronger methods (SmoothQuant, QuaRot, rotations) are left to future work and plausibly reduce the SegRNN/TimeMixer residual.
- Survivorship-biased panel (current S&P 500 constituents) bounds how far the absolute IC numbers generalize, even though paired comparisons cancel the bias.
- Only eight test years with two major stress episodes; regime conclusions (2020 vs 2021 vs 2022) are based on few events and could be coincidence.
- Single dataset (S&P 500), single task (5-day cross-sectional volatility), single metric (IC); no other markets, no return prediction, no portfolio-level evaluation.
- The percentile sweep in Section 6 uses test-year outcomes, making R/L optimistic upper bounds; the deployable validation-year rule is the operationally relevant result.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would per-channel activation quantization or affine/asymmetric quantizers shrink the large residual damage in SegRNN and TimeMixer, or is the residual fundamental to recurrent/multi-scale dynamics?
- How do stronger PTQ methods (SmoothQuant, QuaRot rotations, LLM.int8 mixed-precision decomposition) behave on these financial time-series architectures under the same walk-forward protocol?
- Does the recovered IC translate into portfolio-level economic value (e.g., volatility-managed portfolios per Moreira & Muir 2017), or do the out-of-envelope stress days wipe out the average gain in PnL terms?
- Does the calibration-envelope monitoring signal generalize to other markets (international equities, crypto, FX) and to other tasks (return prediction, drawdown forecasting) where dispersion dynamics differ?
- Can the layerwise attribution (Transformer's conv token embedding as the bottleneck) be automated into a sensitivity score that predicts W4A4 damage before deployment, avoiding the test-year sweep entirely?
- With only two stress episodes in eight years, how sensitive are the regime-flip conclusions (2020 narrow-percentile hurts, 2021 abs-max hurts) to adding more history or bootstrapping across sub-periods?
