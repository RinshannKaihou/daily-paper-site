> **一句话总结：** 针对学习型图像压缩（LIC）模型在低比特部署下的精度损失与跨平台编解码失配问题，本文提出 HAMP-LIC——一种基于 Hessian 迹的二阶敏感度估计、并结合率失真目标的混合精度 PTQ 框架，在 4.85× 权重压缩下仅产生 0.59% BD-rate 损耗，并彻底消除了 CPU/GPU 跨平台解码错误。

> **TL;DR:** HAMP-LIC is a four-stage mixed-precision post-training quantization (PTQ) framework for learned image compression (LIC) that estimates per-block sensitivity via the Hessian trace, refines it with rate-distortion task loss, allocates bit-widths through a Pareto-frontier search (reducing the search space from exponential to polynomial), and performs block-wise reconstruction. On the Cheng2020 model it achieves 4.85× weight compression with only 0.59% BD-rate loss on Kodak (vs. 0.89% for the FMPQ mixed-precision baseline at a lower 3.99× compression), and completely eliminates the 24/24 Kodak and 98/100 Tecnick cross-platform decoding failures that plague the FP32 model.

## 核心贡献

- 提出混合精度 PTQ 框架 HAMP-LIC，首次将基于 Hessian 迹的二阶敏感度估计与率失真（R-D）感知优化结合，用于预训练 LIC 模型的低比特部署。
- 设计任务感知的敏感度细化指标 Ω_l = Tr(H_l)·|L_qp(b_l)−L_int8|/|L_int8|，将纯几何敏感度与各 block 量化后实际 R-D 损失退化耦合，使位宽决策直接服务于压缩目标而非通用重建误差。
- 将位宽分配形式化为带全局平均位宽预算（ε=0.75）的约束整数优化，并引入基于敏感度排序的单调连续划分 Pareto 前沿搜索，把搜索空间从 k^L（如 3^50≈7.12×10²³）降至多项式规模（1326 个候选），同时保留渐进式 block 细化。
- 在 Minnen2018 与 Cheng2020 上验证：最高 4.85× 模型压缩、最低 0.59% BD-rate 损耗，全面消除 CPU/GPU 跨平台浮点编解码失配（错误率从 24/24、98/100 降至 0）。

## Key Contributions

- Proposes **HAMP-LIC**, a mixed-precision PTQ framework that integrates Hessian-trace second-order sensitivity with rate-distortion-aware optimization specifically tailored to the hyperprior/analysis-synthesis structure of LIC models.
- Introduces a **task-aware sensitivity refinement** that modulates the Hessian-trace metric by each block's relative R-D loss degradation under quantization, so bit-width choices reflect the compression objective rather than generic reconstruction error.
- Formulates bit allocation as a **constrained integer optimization** with a global average-bit-width budget, and solves it via a Pareto-frontier search over monotonically non-increasing partitions of the sensitivity-ranked block list—collapsing an exponential (k^L) search space to polynomial scale (1326 candidates for L=50, k=3).
- Performs **task-constraint block-wise reconstruction** combining sequential scaling optimization (minimizing R-D loss discrepancy) with AdaRound-style adaptive rounding under a joint task+quantization loss.
- Demonstrates up to **4.85× weight compression with ≤0.59% BD-rate loss** and **zero cross-platform decoding errors**, outperforming RAQ, RDO-PTQ, FPQ, and FMPQ across Kodak/Tecnick/CLIC benchmarks.

## Methodology

HAMP-LIC is a four-stage PTQ pipeline (Fig. 1, Algorithm 1) operating on a pre-trained FP32 LIC model partitioned into L blocks {B_1,…,B_L} with per-channel uniform weight quantization W_b = Clip(⌊W/s⌉, n, p):

1. **Hessian-based sensitivity (Step 1).** For each block B_l, the sensitivity is taken as the Hessian trace Tr(H_l) = ∂²L/∂W_l², which captures loss-landscape curvature and predicts output change under quantization perturbation—superior to first-order gradients. The full Hessian is intractable, so the trace is estimated via **Hutchinson's method** (Hessian-vector products) using 5 Rademacher random vectors per block over the 12-image calibration set.

2. **Task-aware sensitivity list (Step 2).** The geometric Tr(H_l) is modulated by a task term: Ω_l(b_l) = Tr(H_l)·|L_qp(b_l)−L_int8|/|L_int8|, where L_qp(b_l) is the R-D task loss when block l is quantized to b_l bits and L_int8 is the reference uniformly-8-bit quantized loss. The LIC R-D loss is J = λ·D + R_Ŷ + R_Ẑ (distortion + latent bitrate + hyperprior bitrate). This couples second-order curvature with the downstream compression objective.

3. **Mixed-precision allocation (Step 3).** Bit assignment minimizes ΣΩ_l(b_l) subject to an average-bit-width budget (1/8L)Σb_l ≤ ε (ε=0.75, with 10% tolerance), over an admissible set B={2,4,8}. Rather than exhaustive k^L search, blocks are sorted by sensitivity and the problem is reformulated as choosing j contiguous monotone partitions (Eq. 7), yielding |B| = Σ_j C(k,j)·C(L−1,j−1) candidates—polynomial in L. Each candidate is scored by Eq. 6 and the minimizer under the size budget is selected.

4. **Block-wise reconstruction (Step 4).** Two interleaved optimizations per block, processed sequentially front-to-back with preceding blocks frozen at their quantized params: (i) **scaling optimization** finds weight/bias scale factors s_w^l, s_b^l minimizing the R-D loss gap between quantized and FP32 block outputs (Eq. 8); (ii) **rounding optimization** uses an AdaRound-style learnable variable V with a regularization term βf_reg(V) (Eqs. 9–10). The joint objective is min_{s,V} λ_t·L_task + L_q^l (Eq. 11, λ_t=1).

Training uses only 12 CLIC calibration images (256×256 crops, batch 4, 20,000 iterations/block, ADAM lr=1e-3). This article extends the authors' DCC 2026 paper MPP-LIC by adding the Hessian-based second-order sensitivity (vs. the original first-order/reconstruction-error metric) and the Pareto-frontier constrained allocation.

## Results & Highlights

- **BD-rate vs. FP32 anchor (Table I), Cheng2020, Kodak:** HAMP-LIC (w=6.6, a=32) achieves **0.59%** BD-rate loss vs. **0.89%** for the FMPQ mixed-precision baseline, **2.05%** for FPQ, **2.95%** for RDO-PTQ, and **27.84%** for RAQ—at a higher **4.85×** weight compression versus FMPQ's 3.99× and the 4× of the fixed-precision methods.
- **BD-rate vs. FP32 anchor (Table I), Cheng2020, Tecnick:** HAMP-LIC (w=6.6, a=32) achieves **1.79%** vs. FMPQ's **2.68%**, RDO-PTQ's 5.09%, and RAQ's 29.95%.
- **Cross-platform decoding error rate (Table II), Cheng2020:** the FP32 model fails on **24/24** Kodak images and **98/100** Tecnick images when encoding/decoding are split across CPU and GPU (both directions); HAMP-LIC reduces this to **0/24** and **0/100** in both CPU/GPU and GPU/CPU configurations—fully eliminating floating-point-induced mismatches.
- **Fully-quantized config (w=6.6, a=8):** BD-rate loss rises to 2.99% (Kodak) / 6.33% (Tecnick) on Cheng2020—i.e., going from FP32 to 8-bit activations costs an additional +2.40% / +4.54% BD-rate—in exchange for reduced compute and deterministic integer decoding.
- **BOPS analysis (Table III), 512×768 input, a=8:** mixed-precision BOPS of **2007.5G (Q3) / 4628.2G (Q4) / 4652.6G (Q5) / 4661.9G (Q6)**, a **94.3%/94.1%/94.1%/94.1%** reduction versus the FP32 baseline (35138.5G / 78780.0G) and an additional **8.6%/6.0%/5.5%/5.3%** reduction beyond uniform W8A8 quantization, because the less-sensitive hyper-path layers (h_a, h_s) are pushed to 4-bit.
- **Minnen2018 (Table I), Kodak:** HAMP-LIC (w=6.6, a=32) BD-rate **1.23%** at 4.85× compression—here roughly on par with FMPQ (1.2%) and RDO-PTQ (1.19%, but only 4× compression); the dominant wins are on Cheng2020 and on compression ratio + cross-platform consistency.
- **Subjective quality (Fig. 3) at Q3:** bitrates drop to 0.296 bpp (CLIC), 0.162 bpp (Kodak), 0.078 bpp (Tecnick) while retaining MS-SSIM of **0.951 / 0.960 / 0.972** respectively, with no severe blocking or over-smoothing.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Moderate-to-good. Hessian-trace sensitivity (HAWQ/HAWQ-V2 lineage, [7],[23]) and AdaRound/BRECQ-style block reconstruction are well-established in general mixed-precision quantization; the genuine contribution is the LIC-specific coupling of the Hessian trace with the R-D task-loss degradation term (Eq. 4) and the monotone-partition Pareto search that makes the constrained allocation tractable. The extension over the authors' own MPP-LIC is real but incremental.
- **Rigor:** Strong on the Cheng2020 results and the cross-platform error analysis (Table II is a compelling, concrete demonstration of the FP32 numerical-robustness problem). However, the headline "consistently outperforming existing … methods across multiple datasets" is **slightly overstated for Minnen2018**: on Kodak the 1.23% BD-rate is marginally worse than FMPQ's 1.20% and RDO-PTQ's 1.19%—HAMP-LIC's advantage there is the higher compression ratio, not the BD-rate itself.
- **Reproducibility:** Reasonable. Hyperparameters (ε=0.75, λ_t=1, 5 Rademacher vectors, 20k iterations/block) and the calibration protocol (12 CLIC images) are specified, and the anchors come from the public CompressAI library. Missing: the code/data are not stated to be released, and the exact Cheng2020/Minnen2018 layer-partitioning into the 57 quantizable blocks is only partially described.
- **Significance:** Practically relevant—the cross-platform decode-failure problem (24/24 and 98/100 images failing) is a real deployment pain point that integer PTQ cleanly resolves, and the 4.85× compression at <1% BD-rate is a solid result for resource-constrained LIC deployment.
- **Red flags / weak points:** (1) Only **two** LIC models are evaluated, both older CNN/VAE architectures (Minnen2018, Cheng2020); no modern transformer-based or attention-heavy LIC (e.g., the very Cheng2020 attention module is present but no Swin/ELIC-style backbone) is tested—the authors themselves flag transformer-LIC as future work. (2) The **calibration set is tiny (12 images)**; while efficient, generalization across content types is not stress-tested (e.g., no out-of-distribution medical/screen-content imagery). (3) The **ε budget is fixed empirically at 0.75** with no automation or sensitivity analysis across multiple ε values (only a single ε=0.65 ablation point in Fig. 5). (4) No comparison against any **QAT** baseline, so the accuracy/cost trade-off versus retraining-based quantization is uncharacterized. (5) Activation quantization is only explored at 8-bit; the framework's behavior at sub-8-bit activations is unknown.

## Strengths

- The task-aware sensitivity metric (Eq. 4) is a principled, low-cost way to bias bit allocation toward the actual R-D objective rather than generic MSE—a genuinely useful idea for entropy-model-heavy architectures.
- The Pareto-frontier monotone-partition search provably collapses an exponential space (3^50≈7.12×10²³) to 1326 candidates with minimal accuracy sacrifice, a clean algorithmic contribution.
- The cross-platform decoding-error analysis (Table II) quantitatively exposes a severe, under-discussed FP32 robustness failure mode (up to 98% decode failures) and shows integer PTQ eliminates it entirely.
- Strong empirical result on Cheng2020: best-in-class BD-rate (0.59% Kodak) at the highest compression ratio (4.85×) among all compared PTQ methods.
- Honest ablations—including the Hessian-vs-Fisher comparison (Fig. 4) and the bit-allocation-vs-threshold comparison (Fig. 5)—directly justify each design choice.

## Limitations

- Evaluated on only two CNN-based LIC models (Minnen2018, Cheng2020); generalization to transformer/attention-heavy LIC architectures is unverified and explicitly deferred to future work.
- 12-image calibration set raises questions about robustness on out-of-distribution content, though it is an advantage for deployment efficiency.
- The compression-ratio hyperparameter ε is selected empirically (0.75) and not automated; only one alternative value (0.65) is studied.
- No QAT comparison and no sub-8-bit activation results; the lowest activation precision explored is 8-bit.
- The Minnen2018 BD-rate win is not a clean sweep—on Kodak it is marginally behind FMPQ/RDO-PTQ in absolute BD-rate (only the compression ratio is better), so the "consistently outperforming" framing should be read as compression-efficiency-dominant rather than BD-rate-dominant across all model/dataset cells.
- Single-author, under-review manuscript; broader peer validation is pending.

## Relevance

与用户关注的"Computation Reliability & Precision Errors"（量化误差、浮点精度、混合精度、硬件级算术误差）高度契合——本文核心正是量化误差最小化、混合精度位宽分配，以及最引人注目的 CPU/GPU 跨平台浮点编解码失配（Silent numerical non-determinism）的彻底消除。同时也呼应"ML Reliability & Mathematical Foundations"，因为其用二阶 Hessian 信息对 LIC 模型的数值脆弱性做了显式建模与缓解。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the Hessian-trace sensitivity ranking remain stable when the calibration set is expanded or replaced with out-of-distribution content (e.g., screen content, medical imagery), given that only 12 CLIC images are used?
- How does HAMP-LIC transfer to transformer-based LIC backbones (e.g., ELIC, SwinT-based codecs), where attention layers may have very different sensitivity profiles than the ga/gs/h_a/h_s CNN blocks analyzed here?
- Can the Pareto-frontier search be extended to a joint weight-AND-activation bit allocation (currently activations are fixed at a single 8-bit setting), and what is the achievable frontier at sub-8-bit activations?
- The FP32 cross-platform decode failures (24/24, 98/100) are attributed to CPU-vs-GPU floating-point non-determinism—can the specific failure locations be localized (entropy model? GDN layers?) to understand whether cheaper targeted fixes could partially mitigate them without full quantization?
- How does HAMP-LIC compare against a QAT baseline at equivalent compression ratios, in terms of both BD-rate and the total engineering cost (calibration iterations vs. full retraining)?
