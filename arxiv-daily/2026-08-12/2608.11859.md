> **一句话总结：** 本文论证小模型之所以"看不到"scaling law，根因在于超参敏感而非规模本身——只要对 4M 参数的小模型做 256+ 次随机搜索充分调参，scaling law 就会浮现；并揭示随规模增大，超参损失面的内禀维度会降至 1，由此提出"小规模充分调参 + 定性诊断 + 简单规则上推"的研究范式，在 pre-norm vs post-norm 案例中复现了大模型结论。

> **TL;DR:** Scaling laws at small scales (down to 4M parameters) are not absent, just obscured by hyperparameter sensitivity: on FineWeb-Edu, going from 16 to 256 random-search configurations collapses the held-out (268M) test MSE from 6.01e-03 to 3.70e-06 (~1600x), while decaying the learning rate cuts test MSE a further 98%. The authors show the effective number of hyperparameters drops to 1 as models grow, and demonstrate a holistic small-scale methodology that recovers the pre-norm-over-post-norm result — a question that originally took the field years and a dedicated theoretical analysis to settle.

## 核心贡献

- **定位了小模型 scaling law 失效的真正元凶**：不是规模本身，而是超参敏感。小模型的超参损失面是高维的、挑剔的，必须经过远超常规的搜索（数百次配置）才能触达"完全调参前沿"，scaling law 只在该前沿上才浮现。
- **量化了"充分调参"的含义**：在 4M–34M 训练、67M–134M 验证、268M 测试的设定下，4 个配置时 law 完全消失，16 个时仍未成形，64 个时勉强可见，**256 个配置才得到准确的外推 law**（测试 MSE 从 6.01e-03 降至 3.70e-06）。
- **揭示超参敏感随规模下降的几何机制**：随参数与数据同比例增长，近最优构型占据的空间比例变大，超参损失面的**内禀维度 γ 单调下降至 1**；其中参数（而非数据）是降维的主驱动。"参数的维度之福治愈了超参的维度之祸。"
- **提出小规模研究的整体方法论**：整合三个文献工具——noisy quadratic limit（是否充分调参）、scaling laws（loss 如何随规模变化）、perplexity–capability correspondence（loss 是否代理下游能力），把每个强假设变成可检验的诊断。
- **用案例验证方法论的威力**：在 pre-norm vs post-norm 这一历史上耗时近三年、需理论分析才解决的问题上，仅用 4M/34M/134M 三档少量预算（等效于"几次十亿参数 run"的算力）就从定性诊断中恢复出 pre-norm 更优的大模型结论。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies hyperparameter sensitivity (not scale) as the true culprit** behind unreliable small-scale scaling laws. Small models sit on a high-dimensional, unforgiving hyperparameter loss surface; the law only emerges on the "fully tuned frontier," which requires hundreds of configurations — far beyond what most studies run.
- **Quantifies what "fully tuned" means.** Under a train(4M–34M)/val(67M–134M)/test(268M) split on FineWeb-Edu: with 4 configs the law is absent (test MSE 1.30e-02), with 16 it is still missing (6.01e-03), with 64 it is visible but imprecise (2.79e-03), and only at 256 configs is the law accurate (test MSE 3.70e-06).
- **Explains the geometric mechanism** by which tuning gets easier with scale: the intrinsic dimension γ of the hyperparameter loss surface drops to ~1 as parameters grow, driven primarily by parameters rather than data. The "blessing of dimensionality" in weights cures the "curse" in hyperparameters.
- **Synthesizes a holistic small-scale methodology** from three literature tools — the noisy quadratic limit (are HParams fully tuned?), scaling laws (how does loss scale?), and perplexity–capability correspondence (does loss proxy capability?) — turning each expected assumption into a falsifiable diagnostic.
- **Validates the methodology on a hard historical case.** The pre-norm vs post-norm question took the field ~3 years and a theoretical analysis (Xiong et al., 2020) to settle; the authors recover the pre-norm-wins result from small-scale experiments costing the equivalent of a handful of billion-parameter runs.

## Methodology

The paper is an empirical/methodological study of the hyperparameter loss surface across scales, not a new architecture. Its backbone has four components:

**Experimental design.** Models are Llama-variants (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, pre-norm by default) implemented in Meta Lingua, pretrained on the 100B-token subset of FineWeb-Edu with the p50k_base tokenizer and context length 1024. Scales are powers of two from 2^22 ≈ 4M to 2^28 ≈ 268M *effective parameters* (a FLOP-based count that excludes embeddings, includes unembedding + attention, so c = 6pd holds exactly). Each configuration trains for 32 tokens/parameter (~50% past the Chinchilla-optimal ~20 ratio) under a warmup-stable-decay (WSD) schedule, branching decay phases off checkpoints at 1/8 … 8/8 of training — so one sweep yields stable and decayed evaluations across eight token budgets cheaply.

**Search.** Hyperparameters (lr, weight_decay, batch_size, betas, warmup, rope_theta, and for the "random" experiment also depth/width/heads) are sampled by random search with log/logit-appropriate scales. Three experiments are run: a hand-crafted *model ladder* (867 configs at 4M down to 64 at 268M), a *random-architecture* sweep (128 per scale, 4M–134M), and a *post-norm* expansion (511 at 4M, 512 at 34M, 128 at 134M) to match pre-norm from the random experiment.

**Analysis tools.** (1) The *noisy quadratic limit* (Lourie et al., 2025a): near the optimum, the validation-score distribution converges to a noisy quadratic Q_min(α, β, γ, σ), where γ is the intrinsic hyperparameter dimension; the authors fit it with the `opda` library to estimate γ and detect whether the search has reached the asymptotic regime. (2) *Scaling laws*: Hoffmann-style L(p,d)=ε + ζ/d^η + κ/p^ι, fit via `scipy.optimize.differential_evolution` to the best loss at each parameter–data budget, with scales partitioned into train/val/test to measure extrapolation. (3) *Perplexity–capability correspondence*: evaluating six lm-evaluation-harness tasks (MMLU, ARC-Easy/Challenge, BoolQ, OpenBookQA, PIQA) to confirm equal-loss ⇒ equal-capability holds with data fixed.

**Methodology prescription.** Tune thoroughly and measure at the small scale, build a *qualitative* understanding via the diagnostics (is it hard to tune? is it getting easier? does loss track capability? does the law emerge?), then carry the winner up with simple rules — because at large scale the loss surface is low-dimensional and forgiving. The authors stress this is holistic: pure quantitative extrapolation eventually just compares estimates of the irreducible error ε, whose sampling variance is large until the law saturates.

## Results & Highlights

- **Hyperparameter search size is the single decisive factor.** On the held-out 268M test scale, scaling-law test MSE falls from **1.30e-02 (4 configs) → 6.01e-03 (16) → 2.79e-03 (64) → 3.70e-06 (256)** — i.e., 256 vs 16 configurations is a ~1600x reduction in extrapolation error. With ≤16 configs "there is nothing to fit."
- **Methodology refinements help but do not enable the law.** Moving from "best single run per scale" to "best configuration per parameter–data budget" cuts test MSE by **~50%** (2.20e-03 → 1.09e-03); adding learning-rate decay cuts it a further **~98%** (1.09e-03 → 1.85e-05). Tying the exponents ι=κ gives inconsistent results (val MSE 1.81e-05 vs 4.47e-05 free) — no clear benefit.
- **Parameter definitions barely matter.** All six combinations of {±Attention, ±Embedding, ±Unembedding} yield comparable held-out MSE (test MSE ranging 4.30e-07 to 2.37e-04), confirming the long-running Kaplan-vs-Chinchilla counting debate is a second-order effect.
- **Tuning difficulty collapses with scale, driven by parameters.** The effective hyperparameter count γ (intrinsic dimension of the loss surface) drops from ~3 at 4M toward **1 at 268M**; increasing data alone has much less effect on γ than increasing parameters. Correspondingly, the gap between the 25th-percentile and minimum validation loss collapses only when *both* parameters and data are large.
- **Scaling laws extend remarkably far down.** A recognizable law is recoverable at **4M effective parameters**, where a model trains in under an hour on a single A100 — well within academic budgets.
- **Pre-norm beats post-norm, recovered at small scale.** Post-norm's noisy-quadratic asymptotic regime is markedly smaller than pre-norm's (an early signal of tuning difficulty); post-norm also retains a persistent suboptimal peak in its score distribution across scales. Pooling all sizes, pre-norm scales better over the explored range, and maintains its advantage when irreducible error ε is shared across architectures.
- **Cost framing.** A single 1B-parameter run costs as much as **64 runs at 134M, 1,024 at 34M, or 65,536 at 4M** — so the entire pre-norm/post-norm case study costs the equivalent of "a handful of billion-parameter runs."

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** is genuine and well-scoped: the central empirical claim — that γ → 1 and that 256 configs (not 16) are needed to see a small-scale law — is a concrete, falsifiable mechanism for the "small-model scaling laws are unreliable" finding of Li et al. (2025c) and Pearce & Song (2024). The framing as "the confounding factor is hyperparameters" is a useful reframing rather than a new technique.
- **Rigor** is high in aggregate: thousands of runs (e.g., 867 at 4M, 934 at 34M) and a clean train/val/test split by scale make the headline curves credible. The noisy-quadratic fits come with confidence bands (LD Highest Density bands), not point estimates.
- **Reproducibility caveats.** The central γ estimates are sensitive to the per-checkpoint threshold θ chosen *per parameter–data budget* (Appendix A.3 lists bespoke 3%–50% percentiles), and the post-norm asymptotic regime is so small (3%–10%) that γ for post-norm rests on very few tail points — a fragility the authors acknowledge indirectly ("could likely benefit from even further hyperparameter tuning"). Re-fitting γ with a different θ-schedule would be an informative ablation that is absent.
- **Weak/confounded comparison in the case study.** The pre-norm-vs-post-norm conclusion is partly an artifact of the shared-ε assumption: when ε is left *free*, post-norm actually appears to eventually win beyond the tested range (Figure 15, left). The authors rescue pre-norm by arguing (a) post-norm was harder to tune "at every turn," and (b) near the data — where the law is most trustworthy — pre-norm wins. This is reasonable but rests on a qualitative "holistic" judgment rather than a decisive quantitative gap, and the paper is candid about it.
- **Missing ablations.** There is no test of whether the γ→1 result survives an alternative parametrization (e.g., μP / maximal-update parametrization), which the authors flag as a limitation but do not probe; nor is there a check of whether the 256-config threshold is stable under a different search distribution (log-scale choices, search-space bounds).
- **Extrapolation scope is modest.** Extrapolation is only validated up to 268M — about 1.5 orders of magnitude above the smallest training scale. The paper honestly admits (§5.2) that beyond this, extrapolation mostly reflects the noisy estimate of ε, so the "small scale predicts large scale" promise is demonstrated over a bounded range.
- **Red flags.** Minor overclaiming in the abstract ("scaling laws exist in small models") is tempered in the body, where the honest position is "they exist but extrapolate with care." No evidence of cherry-picked benchmarks; all six eval tasks are standard and reported together.

## Strengths

- Provides a clear, actionable mechanism (hyperparameter sensitivity → γ → 1) for a long-standing community pain point, replacing a vague "small models are unreliable" with a diagnosable condition.
- Exceptionally well-written for a methods paper: each section is organized as claim → diagnostic → figure, making the argument auditable.
- The cost framing (1B run = 65,536 runs at 4M) makes the affordability case concretely and is a useful budgeting rule for academic labs.
- Honest about the limits of pure extrapolation and explicit that the methodology is restricted to *model-centric* research (data fixed).

## Limitations

- **Data-centric research is out of scope.** Changing the pretraining corpus breaks perplexity–capability correspondence, so the methodology cannot predict outcomes of data-mixing or data-quality experiments — a major real-world use case.
- **Extrapolation range is bounded** (~1.5 orders of magnitude to 268M); beyond it, comparisons degenerate into comparing noisy ε estimates.
- **γ estimates depend on hand-tuned θ percentiles** that differ per scale and per architecture, introducing analyst degrees of freedom.
- **The flagship case study (pre/post-norm) is a settled question.** Recovering a known answer is a proof-of-concept, not evidence the method works on open problems; its power on genuinely undecided architectural questions is untested.
- **No alternative parametrizations** (μP, etc.) are tried, leaving open whether γ→1 is an artifact of naive LR/AdamW scaling.
- All experiments are decoder-only LMs on a single English web corpus (FineWeb-Edu); generalization to other modalities or multilingual data is unverified.

## Relevance

高度相关于 scaling-law 与高效 LLM 训练方法学研究：直接回答"小模型实验何时可信、需调多少次超参"这一日常工程问题，并给出可操作的 γ/noisy-quadratic 诊断工具，对在有限算力下做架构与训练超参决策的研究者（如关注 cs.LG 的 scaling、调参、模型设计）有直接指导价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the γ→1 result hold under μP / maximal-update parametrization, or does a scale-invariant parametrization flatten the γ-vs-scale curve and change the "small scales need more search" prescription?
- How stable is the "256 configs" threshold to the *shape* of the search distribution (e.g., narrower log-uniform bounds, or a quasi-Monte-Carlo / Sobol scheme instead of pure random search)?
- Can the methodology be extended to data-centric research (data mixing, deduplication, curriculum) by replacing perplexity–capability correspondence with a data-conditioned capability proxy?
- On a genuinely open architectural question (e.g., placement of SwiGLU vs parallel-FFN, choice of RoPE base, depth-vs-width at fixed FLOPs), does the holistic diagnostic agree with later large-scale confirmations — or does it produce false positives?
- The irreducible-error ε estimates are wildly unstable across 128-config samples (Figure 10); could a paired/bayesian scaling-law fit that shares information across configs reduce the extrapolation variance enough to make far-range comparisons trustworthy?
