> **一句话总结：** NetlistBench 提出一个基于结构等价校验的 SPICE 网表识别与编辑基准（2,342 个用例、24 个任务族，用规范化电路 IR 做精确匹配判分），评测六个非推理 LLM 发现局部删除/参数编辑可达 96%-100%，但器件添加降至 41%-83%、等价判断仅 49%-90%，多步复合编辑随步数从 ~80% 急剧退化为 0%，表明网表结构保持是 LLM 电路设计自动化中被忽视的独立瓶颈。

> **TL;DR:** NetlistBench is a structure-verified benchmark of 2,342 cases across 24 SPICE netlist task families (recognition + manipulation), graded by a canonical-circuit-IR oracle rather than string matching. Across six non-thinking LLMs, device removal/parameter edits reach 96%-100% but device addition drops to 41%-83% and equivalence judgment to 49%-90%; overall accuracy spans 41% (Qwen3-30B-A3B) to 82% (Claude Sonnet 4.6), a 41-point gap, and long-horizon compound editing collapses from ~80% at 3 steps toward 0% at 15 steps even with reasoning enabled.

## 核心贡献

- 将 SPICE 网表可靠性形式化为**表示层评测问题**，明确把"底层网表结构操作（识别/编辑）"与"高层设计推理"分离——指出既有电路 LLM 评测（CIRCUIT、AMSbench、AnalogCoder、SPICEPilot、SPICED 等）都将二者混在端到端管线里，无法定位失败来源。
- 构建 **NetlistBench**：2,342 个用例、24 个任务族，覆盖参数/连接性识别、单步编辑、层次化操作、等价判断与 3-15 步长程复合编辑；全部由确定性模板生成，每个用例为可复现的三元组 `(源网表, 显式指令, 目标)`，并在构造期自动校验。
- 提出**结构感知评测管线**：把模型输出与参考都解析为规范化电路中间表示（canonical IR：器件集合、有序端子-节点绑定、参数数值归一化、顶层指令、子电路端口接口），要求精确匹配（仅允许 R/C/L 无序端子等语义等价归一化），从而能捕获字符串匹配或最终仿真都漏掉的失败（节点误重命名、器件遗漏/重复、端子错绑、编辑局部性丢失）。
- 等价判断族的 ground truth 用 **VF2 图同构**额外审计正负样本标签，确保标签本身可靠（且明确说明 VF2 仅用于审计标签、不参与评分，否则会掩盖评分要检测的命名/局部性违规）。
- 用六个非推理 LLM（前沿/flash/开源三档）系统刻画可靠性随"操作结构复杂度"与"编辑时域长度"的变化规律，并附带缓解分析（PySpice 表示、原生思考、CoT）；数据与代码公开（Apache-2.0 / CC BY 4.0）。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates SPICE netlist reliability as a representation-level evaluation problem, isolating low-level netlist structure operations from high-level design reasoning — a gap that existing circuit-LLM benchmarks (CIRCUIT, AMSbench, AnalogCoder, SPICEPilot, SPICED) leave conflated inside end-to-end pipelines.
- NetlistBench: 2,342 cases across 24 task families spanning recognition (7 structural-extraction families + equivalence judgment) and manipulation (6 single-edit, 5 flat compound at 3/6/9/12/15 steps, 5 hierarchical families), all deterministically templated with reproducible ground truth.
- A structure-aware evaluation oracle built on a canonical circuit IR (devices, ordered terminal-node bindings, parameters, top-level directives, subcircuit port interfaces) with semantics-preserving normalizations; it directly encodes the name-preservation and locality constraints of edit tasks that surface-string or final-simulation checks miss.
- VF2 graph-isomorphism audit of the equivalence-judgment ground-truth labels (used only to validate labels, deliberately excluded from scoring so it does not mask the name/locality violations the edits are designed to detect).
- Systematic reliability characterization across six non-thinking LLMs (frontier/flash/open-weight tiers) plus controlled mitigations (SPICE vs PySpice representation; native thinking vs CoT vs direct prompting), with the benchmark, oracle, generation scripts, and templates publicly released.

## Methodology

The benchmark draws on two complementary SPICE netlist sources: **AnalogGenie** (flat CMOS analog netlists from topology-discovery work) yielding 2,838 flat circuits (~58,000 device instances; Simple 492 / Medium 1,752 / Complex 594, with median 5 / 20 / 36 devices and max 69), and **ALIGN** (hierarchical analog netlists from a layout-automation flow) yielding 931 circuits across 21 topology families with 3-4 subcircuits each. Each evaluation instance is a deterministic triplet `(source netlist N_src, explicit instruction T_inst, target Y_target)`; family-specific transformation rules, syntax perturbations, and prompt templates produce reproducible cases, and construction-time validation rejects manipulation targets with unintended edits and inconsistent recognition/equivalence targets.

The **manipulation modality** (1,542 cases, 16 families) covers 6 single-edit families (connectivity edit, device add/remove/replace, parameter edit, rename propagation), 5 flat compound families chaining 3/6/9/12/15 mutually dependent edits, and 5 hierarchical families (subcircuit inline-expansion, port-swap, multi-step internal editing). The **recognition modality** (800 cases, 8 families) covers device parameters, ordered/semantic terminal connectivity, node incidence, subcircuit ports, instance port mapping, terminal-neighbor incidence, and structural equivalence judgment.

Evaluation parses each model output and the reference into the canonical IR and requires exact match up to normalizations (same named device set with no missing/extra device; identical terminal-node bindings; parameters equal up to numeric normalization such as 1k=1000; identical top-level directives; symmetric passives compared with unordered terminals; matching subcircuit port interfaces, internal devices, and directives). Six non-thinking models are evaluated — Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5 Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.6-Flash, Qwen3-30B-A3B — via official APIs with deterministic decoding, one query per case, binary pass/fail, and Wilson 95% confidence intervals; DeepSeek-V4-Flash with native thinking is reported separately as a reasoning reference. Two paired stratified mitigation analyses (n=718, 30 cases per family) compare SPICE vs PySpice output representation and direct vs native-thinking vs CoT prompting, using McNemar's test.

## Results & Highlights

- On NetlistBench's **device-removal** family, Claude/GPT/Gemini/DeepSeek all hit **100%** (Qwen3.6-Flash 98%, Qwen3-30B 97%), and **parameter editing** spans 96%-100%; in contrast the **device-addition** family drops to **83% (Claude) down to 41% (DeepSeek-V4-Flash)** — a >40-point spread within a single benchmark, showing local deletion/substitution is reliable but structural attachment is not.
- **Overall NetlistBench accuracy** (2,342 cases, non-thinking) ranges from **82% (Claude Sonnet 4.6, strongest) to 41% (Qwen3-30B-A3B, weakest)**, a 41-point gap; GPT-4.1 71%, Gemini 2.5 Flash 64%, DeepSeek-V4-Flash 55%, Qwen3.6-Flash 47%.
- **Long-horizon flat compound editing collapses monotonically**: Claude falls from **80% (3-step) to 26% (15-step)**, GPT-4.1 from **71% to 34%**, Gemini from **70% to 6%**, DeepSeek-V4-Flash from **44% to 0%**, and both Qwen3.6-Flash / Qwen3-30B from 34%/28% to **0%**; even DeepSeek-V4-Flash with reasoning drops from **74% to 31%**.
- **Structural equivalence judgment** spans **49% (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) to 90% (Claude)** across models on the recognition modality.
- On the paired stratified subset (n=718), **native thinking raises DeepSeek-V4-Flash from 52% to 81% and Qwen3.6-Flash from 45% to 85%** (both p < 10^-6, McNemar); switching SPICE to PySpice gives a smaller, inconsistent effect (DeepSeek 52% to 58%, p<0.001; Qwen3.6 45% to 45%, p=0.51).
- **Terminal-neighbor incidence** is the single hardest recognition family: 2% (Qwen3-30B) to 20% (Gemini) across non-thinking models (Claude an outlier at 97%), rising to 83%-100% only under thinking — relational/implicit-connectivity queries are far harder than explicit attribute extraction.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing is genuinely useful: most prior circuit-LLM work evaluates inside end-to-end generation/simulation/reasoning pipelines and cannot tell whether a failure came from bad design reasoning or from corrupting the netlist at the representation layer. NetlistBench is one of the first to isolate that layer with a structure-verified oracle, and the canonical-IR exact-match-up-to-normalization rule is well-motivated by the name-preservation/locality constraints that text-similarity or final-simulation checks would miss.

**Rigor.** Strong on deterministic construction (templated, construction-time-validated, VF2-audited labels) and on statistics (Wilson CIs, McNemar). However, the single-sample-per-case protocol is a real weakness: the authors concede the numbers characterize API snapshots rather than output distributions, and the reasoning-mode results are explicitly flagged as single-sample with run-to-run variance — yet some headline comparisons (e.g., DeepSeek+R 81% vs Claude 82%) sit within plausible sampling noise and are still read as "does not consistently surpass."

**Reproducibility.** Good in principle (public repo, deterministic case generation, code+data released), but the evaluated proprietary models (Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5 Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.6-Flash) are API snapshots with no version pinning, so exact numerical reproduction will decay over time regardless of the released scripts.

**Significance / overclaiming.** Claims are mostly calibrated and the conclusion ("reasoning improves but does not make LLMs reliable unverified editors") is well-supported. One mild overclaim: the abstract's "device addition drops to 41%-83%" is presented as a model-agnostic finding, but the 41% floor is specific to DeepSeek-V4-Flash and the open-weight Qwen3-30B (43%); frontier models are markedly higher, so the range conflates model tier with task difficulty. A conspicuously missing ablation is any breakdown of error mode or pass rate by circuit size (AnalogGenie-Simple 5 devices vs Complex 36-69 devices), leaving it unclear whether the structure-preservation failures are scale-driven or purely operation-driven.

**Red flags.** The scope is narrower than the framing implies. The corpus is exclusively analog CMOS from AnalogGenie + ALIGN and explicitly excludes symbolic `.param`, complex `.model`, behavioral/controlled sources, `include` hierarchies, extracted parasitics, and RF/power/digital/mixed-signal syntax. So "netlist reliability as a distinct bottleneck" is established only for one circuit family, and generalization to the industrial post-layout decks where LLM-EDA tools must actually operate is entirely untested.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated isolation of representation-level netlist operations from high-level design reasoning — fills a genuine evaluation gap left by end-to-end circuit-LLM benchmarks.
- Deterministic, validated, VF2-audited ground-truth construction; fully reproducible via a public repo.
- The structure-aware canonical-IR oracle is the right tool: it catches exactly the failure modes (node rename, dropped/duplicated device, terminal rebinding, locality loss) that string or simulation checks miss.
- Thoughtful decomposition — recognition vs manipulation, single vs compound, flat vs hierarchical — plus a long-horizon sweep (3-15 dependent edits) that yields a striking, interpretable degradation curve.
- Honest about mitigations: reports both that reasoning helps substantially and that it does not close the gap, and that PySpice helps inconsistently (significant for one model, null for the other).
- Statistical care (Wilson CIs, McNemar paired tests) above the typical LLM-benchmark standard.

## Limitations

- Narrow circuit domain (analog CMOS only; no `.param`/`.model`/behavioral/parasitic/PDK syntax; no RF/power/digital/mixed-signal) — external validity is limited.
- Block-level netlists that fit within context windows; no long-context retrieval, hierarchical partitioning, or million-statement extracted-deck evaluation.
- Single query per case; sampling/temperature variance not quantified; reasoning results are single-sample and carry run-to-run noise.
- Proprietary API models without version pinning undermine long-term numerical reproducibility.
- Templated, unambiguous instructions do not reflect the linguistic ambiguity of real designer prompts, so instruction-following reliability (distinct from structural reliability) is under-tested.
- No error-mode breakdown by circuit size or by specific structural-perturbation type.

## Relevance

若你的研究兴趣涵盖大模型在 EDA/电路设计自动化中的应用、LLM 对结构化（图语义）代码生成的可靠性评测，或 SPICE 网表层面的工具构建，本文直接相关——它系统量化了当前 LLM 在网表识别与编辑上的结构保持短板，并提供了一套可复用的结构感知评测方法与公开基准，对设计"分解-编辑-校验"型 LLM-EDA 工作流有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do pass rates and error modes scale with circuit size (5 vs 36-69 devices) within the same operation family?
- Would a graph-structured circuit representation (explicit terminal-to-terminal links) meaningfully outperform raw SPICE/PySpice text, and by how much on compound edits?
- Can a decompose-edit-verify loop (structural check after each atomic edit, with feedback on detected unintended changes) recover long-horizon reliability, and at what compute overhead?
- Do the observed structure-preservation failures transfer to digital/RF/power/mixed-signal netlists and to extracted post-layout decks with parasitics?
- How stable are the model rankings under multi-sample decoding and across API model versions, given the single-sample protocol used here?
