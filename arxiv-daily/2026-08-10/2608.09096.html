Evo-Bench · arXiv:2608.09096

Fix everything but the evolver LLM: the harnesses it writes reach 46.3 — just shy of human-engineered 47.5.

Evo-Bench: Can Language Models Improve Agent Harness?
Lisheng Huang et al.· 2026-08-10· Renmin University & BOSS Zhipin

Key metric

46.3

Overall score on the held-out 448-task eval — GPT-5.6-Sol, the top of 9 evolvers.

+16.6 vs CodeAct seed (29.7)  ·  −1.2 vs human-engineered composite (47.5). Every evolver beats the seed; none beats the human baseline.

Why this matters

First benchmark decoupling a model's harness-evolving skill from its base-model strength — gains appear only where the seed lacks capabilities, and stay near-zero for specialized workflows.

Critical edge

“We run all experiments one time.” With a self-reported ~2.2-point noise floor on byte-identical revisions, the top-3 ranking and the −1.2 gap to the human baseline are not statistically defensible.

Methodology

方法

The policy agent At = (π, Ht) carries an editable harness Ht. Across a fixed budget the evolver Aevo = (E, Hevo) rewrites Ht using cumulative validation evidence — aggregate scores, per-task outcomes, policy trajectories, diagnostics — then spends one of its 20 iterations to re-evaluate. On termination HT is frozen and run once on the disjoint eval suite.

frozen: policy = DeepSeek-V4-Flash (256K ctx, max reasoning)  |  seed = CodeAct (run_shell_command + finish only)  |  evolve harness  |  budget = 20 iters / 1,000 steps / 48 h  →  only the evolver LLM varies.

Tasks come from five benchmarks (BrowseComp, HLE, GDPval, APEX-Agents, Claw-Eval). The novel filter is harness-sensitivity Sens(x): the Pearson correlation between a task's score and leave-one-out harness quality across K=12 auxiliary harnesses. Tasks with Sens ≤ 0 are dropped; within each difficulty stratum the highest-Sens tasks form a 160-task validation set and a disjoint 448-task eval set (Search : Office : General = 2 : 2 : 1). Two metrics — Overall (frozen final on held-out) and AnytimeVal (mean best-so-far over the budget) — separate peak discovery from what gets frozen. Integrity: sandbox isolation, answer-key pattern scanning, and a Codex-based semantic audit of full trajectories for encoded/paraphrased leakage.

Strengths

  • Isolation design done carefully. Fixed policy, seed, evolve harness and budget make the leaderboard measure the evolver alone — the single most valuable contribution.
  • Adversarial integrity controls. A semantic audit of full trajectories catches encoded/paraphrased answer leakage, not just regex — and the paper names the model caught (MiniMax-M3).
  • Two metrics turn a anecdote into a phenomenon. Overall + AnytimeVal make “early saturation” measurable; per-iteration failure traces for the weakest evolvers are candid.
  • Null results reported prominently — Office regressions, ±0.0 General ties, budget underuse, and “still below the human baseline.”
  • Cross-policy transfer is the right experiment against the strongest objection (“you overfit one model's flaws”) and answers it convincingly.

Limitations

  • n = 1 for every cell with a self-documented ~2.2-point noise floor; the leaderboard ordering is not statistically defensible.
  • Val and eval split instances of the same five benchmarks, so genuine cross-suite generalization is untested — task-ID routing can pay off.
  • No coding or scientific-research tasks; no multimodal. Coverage = 5 benchmarks, 3 domains.
  • The CodeAct seed is deliberately impoverished; gains partly measure distance from a crippled start, not a production baseline.
  • Evolvers edit the harness only — no weight updates, no co-optimization. A narrow slice of “self-improvement.”
  • Cost figures omit policy/judge spend; the Pareto plot understates true run cost.
  • Only 2 models in the budget-scaling ablation; no evolve-harness ablation, so harness/model fit is an uncontrolled confound.

Open questions

待探索
  1. Does Sens(x) — estimated from only K=12 auxiliary harnesses — stay predictive under the harnesses evolvers actually produce? Without that check, task selection may fit noise.
  2. How much of the leaderboard survives 3–5 seed replicates, given the measured 2.2-point byte-identical range? Is GPT-5.6-Sol vs. Opus-4.8 distinguishable at all?
  3. A true cross-suite protocol (validate on Search+Office, hold out a sixth benchmark): would gains survive, or did task-ID routing carry them?
  4. Why is Office so resistant? Missing workflow knowledge, coarse rubric feedback, or genuinely low harness sensitivity in that domain?
  5. Would protocol scaffolding — best-snapshot restore, mandatory preflight smoke tests, one falsifiable mechanism per iteration — close the peak-to-freeze gap?

Credibility. Corresponding author Wayne Xin Zhao (RUC) is a highly-cited figure in LLM/agent survey and RAG work; co-authors from BOSS Zhipin bring production agent systems. Prompts, seeds, model configs, a token-level cost formula and selection procedures are documented; code/data/leaderboard are promised. Caveat: every evaluated model is a closed or rapidly-deprecating endpoint, and 9 × 48 h single runs make replication expensive and noisy.
