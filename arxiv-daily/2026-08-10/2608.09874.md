> **一句话总结：** ArchAgent v2 用「级联式分治进化」(先逐级进化 L1D→L2→LLC 再全局联合优化) 加上「硬件可实现性存储反馈回路」(编译期用 `prefetcher_size()` 直接拒绝超预算设计)，在 DPC4 官方规则下自动演化出三级预取器，整体几何平均 IPC 相对基线 (Berti@L1D + Pythia@L2) 提升 3.82%，比人工设计的冠军方案 BertiGO (3.52%) 高 0.3 个百分点；但优势几乎全部来自单核低带宽场景 (4.56% vs 2.57%)，多核跨套件混合负载上反而跌到基线以下 (0.9902)。

> **TL;DR:** ArchAgent v2 extends the AlphaEvolve-based ArchAgent framework to multi-level data prefetching by (1) cascading evolution across cache levels (evolve-and-freeze L1D, then L2, then LLC, then joint) and (2) adding a compile-time storage-budget feedback loop that rejects over-budget designs. Under identical Fourth Data Prefetching Championship (DPC4) rules and starting from the Berti@L1D + Pythia@L2 baseline, the evolved three-level prefetcher reaches a 3.82% geomean IPC speedup vs. the 3.52% of the hand-designed champion BertiGO (+0.3 pts overall), with the win concentrated in single-core limited-bandwidth runs (4.56% vs. BertiGO's 2.57%). The flip side: on multi-core it trails BertiGO (1.33% vs. 2.85%) and on the cross-suite 4-core mix it is actually 0.98% *below* baseline. Worth reading if you care about LLM-driven hardware design-space search or about what an evolutionary coding agent actually does across 12,000+ candidates; skip it if you want a genuinely novel prefetching mechanism, since the authors themselves concede the discovered components offer "limited novelty."

## 核心贡献

- **级联分治进化搜索 (Cascaded Evolutionary Search)**：不再同时进化三级预取器，而是先只演化 L1D (冻结 Pythia@L2 与空 LLC)，性能饱和后冻结最佳 L1D 再演化 L2，随后冻结 L1/L2 演化 LLC；期间只用单核配置以压缩搜索空间，最后才进入单核跨级联合优化与多核联合优化两个阶段。
- **硬件可实现性存储反馈回路**：为每级预取器实现 `prefetcher_size()` 接口，在编译/评估阶段自动核对 32KB (L1D) / 128KB (L2) / 256KB (LLC) 预算，超预算候选直接被拒绝并作为负反馈；提示词中明确要求 LLM 同步更新逻辑与容量函数，并用 C++ 位掩码保证类型宽度与硬件位宽一致。
- **跨进化运行的种子注入机制**：在联合优化运行中，随机时刻把其他仍在运行的单级进化中最优候选及其 lineage/summary 注入提示词，让上游搜索与下游联合搜索并行互相影响；并把低带宽单核配置加权两倍 (geomean(lowBW, lowBW, highBW)) 作为昂贵多核仿真的廉价代理。
- **12,000+ 候选设计的进化过程剖析**：定义 "What"(10 类结构性修改) 与 "Why"(8 类动机) 双重分类法，用 LLM judge 给每个改动按百分比归因，并用自定义 DAG 遍历把「单块大跳跃」递归拆解为子级 top ideas。
- **一个完整的竞赛级产物**：给出 L1D (Berti + Global Stride Tracker + Global Delta History Table + RL 仲裁器，31.1/32KB)、L2 (Pythia 特征 2→5、动作 15→18、sum-pooling + 置信阈值 + 带宽节流，110.0/128KB)、LLC (指针追逐序列重放 + 线性跨步双引擎 + 滑动 delta 唯一步长比仲裁，230.3/256KB) 的完整结构与容量拆解表。

## Key Contributions

- A divide-and-conquer "cascade" that sequentially evolves and freezes prefetchers per cache level, making a design space that ArchAgent v1 could not navigate tractable under ~12-hour-per-candidate ChampSim evaluations.
- An automated hardware-realizability loop: a per-prefetcher `prefetcher_size()` function seeded from the literature for Berti/Pythia, checked at compile time against DPC4's per-level budgets, turning constraint violations into negative rewards rather than post-hoc human area estimation.
- Empirical evidence that an agentic evolutionary system can beat the human-designed championship winner under identical competition rules, not just a weak academic baseline.
- A large-scale profiling study (12,000+ candidates over ~40 days for the first L1D evolution alone) with a two-axis taxonomy and a lineage/DAG decomposition of which ideas actually produced the gains.
- An honest post-mortem on mechanism novelty: the authors classify RL arbitration, changing-stride tracking, and long-lived metadata as recombination of known ideas rather than new science.

## Methodology

The substrate is AlphaEvolve driving C++ policy code inside ChampSim, with a distributed simulation cluster and an evolutionary program database (MAP-Elites / island-style populations). Starting point is the DPC4 baseline: Berti at L1D, Pythia at L2, nothing at LLC.

Two additions define v2. First, the **storage feedback loop**. ChampSim measures IPC but not area, and the authors observed the classic failure mode: an LLM optimizer left unconstrained proposes elaborate structures that violate physical budgets. They therefore prompt the exact per-level limits (Figure 3 shows the prompt, ending in "FALSE REPORTING OR EXCEEDING THE STORAGE BUDGET WILL BE REJECTED"), require the model to maintain `prefetcher_size()` in lockstep with logic changes, and enforce masking so a `uint64_t` standing in for a 3-bit field is billed at 3 bits. Compliance was audited by offline LLM judges during the run and by a manual check of the final output.

Second, the **cascade** (Figure 4). Phase 1 evolves each level in isolation, single-core only, weighting low- and high-bandwidth configurations equally: L1D from Berti → freeze best → L2 from Pythia → freeze → LLC from nothing. This ordering matches the observation that DPC4 gains (including BertiGO's) are L1D-dominant, and that near-core levels have the most latency-hiding potential. Phase 2 runs two joint optimizations: a single-core cross-prefetcher run (with low-bandwidth double-weighted as a multi-core proxy) and finally a multi-core-inclusive run seeded from the best joint candidates.

Train/validate separation is deliberate: evolution uses 60M instructions after 15M warmup (single-core) and 12.5M after 5M (multi-core, over 100 randomly generated 4-workload mixes); final validation uses the competition's held-out longer traces at 200M/50M and 50M/20M with unseen workloads and novel mixes. Total evolution time to beat SoTA: about two months.

The profiling study reuses the "What" taxonomy from Pelleriti et al. and introduces a "Why" taxonomy, with an LLM judge assigning fractional attribution per change (e.g., 50% architectural / 50% hyperparameter).

## Results & Highlights

- **Overall geomean IPC (Fig. 8, normalized to Berti@L1D/Pythia@L2):** ArchAgent 1.0382 (+3.82%) vs. BertiGO 1.0352 (+3.52%), Global Berti 1.0212, EDP 1.0160. The headline win over the hand-designed champion is **+0.30 pts** — real but thin.
- **Single-core limited bandwidth (Fig. 8):** ArchAgent 1.0456 (+4.56%) vs. BertiGO 1.0257 (+2.57%), i.e. **+1.99 pts**, the paper's strongest and most defensible result. This is where cache pollution and bus contention bite, and where the bandwidth-throttling and confidence-filtering machinery pays off.
- **Single-core full bandwidth (Fig. 8):** ArchAgent 1.0561 vs. BertiGO 1.0516 — only **+0.45 pts**, so the "wins on single-core" claim is really "wins on *bandwidth-starved* single-core."
- **Multi-core (Fig. 8):** ArchAgent 1.0133 vs. BertiGO 1.0285 — a **loss of 1.52 pts**, and even below Global Berti (1.0178). The authors attribute this to multi-core feedback entering only in the final cascade stage.
- **Multi-core cross-suite 4-core mix (Fig. 10, harmonic geomean):** ArchAgent **0.9902 — a 0.98% regression below the baseline** — while BertiGO reaches 1.0147 and even EDP/Global Berti sit at ~0.999. This is the single most damaging number in the paper and it appears only in a figure, not in the abstract.
- **Per-suite single-core wins (Fig. 9):** Graph low-BW 1.0507 vs. BertiGO 1.0330 (+1.77 pts); Google low-BW 1.0515 vs. 1.0215 (**+3.00 pts**, the largest per-suite margin); Google high-BW 1.0318 vs. 1.0113 (+2.05 pts).
- **Per-suite single-core losses (Fig. 9):** AI-ML high-BW 1.1978 vs. BertiGO **1.2771 — a 7.93 pt deficit**, by far the largest gap in either direction in the paper; SPEC low-BW 1.0240 vs. 1.0337 (−0.97 pts); SPEC high-BW 1.0423 vs. 1.0455 (−0.32 pts). Multi-core Graph is worse still: 0.9165 vs. BertiGO 0.9712 (−5.47 pts, and −8.35% vs. baseline).
- **Search statistics:** >12,000 candidates in the first L1D evolution over ~40 days, split roughly **50/50 between useful and discarded** (failed compiles, budget violations, worse performance). Attribution: 50% of all contributing changes are "architectural changes," rising to **65% along the winning main lineage**; motivations split as ~35% pattern detection and ~14% each for hardware efficiency, execution-history disambiguation, bandwidth management, and cache pollution.
- **Top individual ideas (Table V, % IPC gain over their parent):** Velocity Aware Delta Engine **+11.0%**, Execution Velocity Berti Prefetcher +10.9%, Feedback Guided Stride Prefetcher +7.7%, Cognitive Genome Hybrid +4.6%, Context Reward Based Arbitration +4.5%. The biggest main-lineage jump (~days 20–30, +4% over its parent) came from merging three separately-evolved island programs ("Resonance Aware Dynamic Arbitrator" +4.5%, "Path Aware Arbitrated Prefetcher" +4.3%, "Refractory Mask Prefetch Filter" +4%).
- **Budget utilization (Table II):** 31.1/32 KB at L1D, 110.0/128 KB at L2, 230.3/256 KB at LLC — the feedback loop produced designs that sit near but under the caps, with the L2 Q-Value store alone consuming 99 KB.

## Authors & Credibility

A strong, credible team spanning Google (Gonzalez, Jain, Alam, Dixon, Levy, Ranganathan — Ranganathan is a Google Fellow/VP and a well-known systems architect), Google DeepMind (Novikov, Balog, Huang, Eisenberger, Shirobokov, Vũ — the AlphaEvolve authors themselves), and UC Berkeley (Gupta, Nikolić, Karandikar — Nikolić and Karandikar are core Chipyard/FireSim people). This is essentially the ArchAgent v1 team plus the AlphaEvolve team, which is both a credibility signal and a conflict-of-interest note: the framework, the baseline framework it improves on, and the evaluation are all in-house.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (methodological, moderate; mechanistic, low).** The cascade is a sensible and clearly-motivated schedule, but it is a scheduling heuristic — evolve-and-freeze per subsystem — not a new search algorithm; it is closer to greedy coordinate descent over subsystems than to anything AlphaEvolve did not already support. The storage feedback loop is the more transferable idea, and it addresses a real failure mode. On mechanism novelty the paper is refreshingly candid: Section VI states outright that the L1D's RL arbitration, changing-stride GST, and long-lived metadata "provide limited novelty" relative to Pythia, prior RL prefetchers, and Jain & Lin's work, and that only the LLC unique-step-ratio arbitration is "incrementally novel."

**Rigor (mixed).** The train/validate split (60M-instruction evolution traces vs. 200M-instruction held-out validation, plus unseen workloads and novel mixes) is genuinely good practice and better than many agentic-discovery papers. But the evaluation has a specific asymmetry that the paper does not fully own: ArchAgent was given ~2 months of cluster time and 12,000+ evaluations *targeting the official DPC4 score*, and the comparison to BertiGO is a comparison against a fixed artifact whose authors did not get a second optimization pass. Given that the overall margin is 0.30 pts and the multi-core cross-suite result is a 0.98% regression below baseline, the framing "outperforms the winning hand-designed solution" is doing heavy lifting for a result that is arguably a wash outside low-bandwidth single-core.

**Missing ablations (the biggest gap).** There is no experiment isolating the two claimed contributions. We never see (a) ArchAgent v2 with the cascade but without the storage feedback loop, (b) with the feedback loop but evolving all three levels jointly, or (c) a matched-compute v1 run to confirm that v1 truly "fails to scale" rather than merely converging slower. The claim that v1 "does not scale" is asserted from prior work and narrative, not measured here. Similarly, the low-bandwidth double-weighting trick (geomean(lowBW, lowBW, highBW)) is presented as a multi-core proxy but never validated as one — and the multi-core results suggest it is a *poor* proxy, since the policy that this weighting selected for is exactly the one that regresses on 4-core mixes.

**Data quirks worth flagging.** The AI-ML high-BW suite shows BertiGO at 1.2771 vs. ArchAgent at 1.1978 — a 7.9 pt gap on the workload family arguably most relevant to the sponsoring organization, discussed in the text only as "mixed gains." Multi-core Graph at 0.9165 is a 8.35% slowdown versus baseline that would be disqualifying in many deployment contexts. The profiling section also depends on an LLM judge assigning fractional attributions to 12,000 candidates with no inter-rater agreement study, human spot-check statistics, or judge-model disclosure, so the "50% architectural changes / 35% pattern detection" numbers should be read as suggestive rather than measured. Likewise, storage-budget compliance was verified by "offline LLM judges" during the run with only one manual check at the end — for a competition whose entire premise is a hard area budget, that is thinner verification than the contribution deserves.

**Reproducibility (low).** No code, prompts (beyond one illustrative figure), evolved policy source, or evolutionary database is released. The compute is substantial and non-public: a distributed ChampSim cluster, individual evaluations "up-to or exceeding 12 hours," two months wall-clock, an unnamed set of "SoTA LLMs," and Google's internal AlphaEvolve. No cost, GPU-hour, or token accounting is given. An external reader cannot reproduce the search, and cannot even reproduce the *artifact* without the policy source.

**Significance (real but bounded).** As a case study, "an evolutionary coding agent beat the human championship winner under the championship's own rules and budgets" is a meaningful data point for the architecture community, and the failure analysis is more useful than the win. As a prefetching contribution, the artifact is a well-tuned recombination that regresses on multi-core.

**Red flags.** (1) Abstract-level asymmetry: the +4.6%/+2.6% low-bandwidth win is headlined while the below-baseline multi-core cross-suite result is buried in Figure 10. (2) "winning the competition" phrasing in Section IV-B for a post-hoc evaluation under DPC4 rules, which is not the same as having won DPC4. (3) Self-comparison throughout — the baseline framework, the compared framework, and the evaluation harness all come from the same group.

## Strengths

- Genuinely honest limitations sections: the authors state the mechanism novelty is limited, that ~50% of candidates were wasted, that the mean proposal quality does *not* improve over time (Fig. 12), and that multi-core evolution remains unsolved.
- The hardware-realizability loop is the most portable idea in the paper and generalizes beyond prefetching to any agentic hardware search with a hard resource budget; making constraint violation a compile-time rejection rather than a post-hoc human audit is the right design.
- Careful train/validate protocol with held-out longer traces and unseen workload mixes, plus 100 randomized multi-core mixes during training — this is above the norm for LLM-discovery papers.
- The 12,000-candidate profiling, DAG decomposition of top ideas, and the observation that the largest jump came from *merging three separately-evolved island lineages* is a concrete, actionable insight for anyone building evolutionary harnesses: cross-island idea injection mattered more than any single mutation.
- Full storage breakdown (Table II) and per-engine architectural description make the artifact auditable at the design level even without code.

## Limitations

- Multi-core is unsolved: −1.52 pts vs. BertiGO overall and a 0.98% *regression* below baseline on cross-suite 4-core mixes, driven by 12-hour simulations forcing multi-core feedback into only the last cascade stage.
- The cascade is greedy: freezing L1D before L2 exists precludes co-designed solutions, and the paper never tests whether a different ordering (or no freezing) would do better.
- No ablation separating the cascade from the storage feedback loop, and no matched-compute ArchAgent v1 control.
- Single evolution run reported per configuration; no seeds, no variance bars, so the +0.30 pt overall margin has no error estimate.
- Storage compliance and idea attribution both rest on LLM judges with minimal human validation.
- Search targets the DPC4 score directly over 2 months and 12,000+ evaluations against a static human artifact — an unequal-effort comparison.
- Nothing is released: no code, no evolved policy, no prompts, no compute accounting.
- Only area is modeled as a realizability constraint; timing, power, and the 1-cycle L1 hit requirement appear only as an LLM-side heuristic ("avoid monolithic tables"), never as an enforced check.

## Relevance

如果你关注 LLM/agentic 系统在真实工程搜索空间中的落地 (AlphaEvolve 类框架的实际工程约束、如何把硬约束编码为编译期负奖励、如何在慢评估下做搜索空间分解)，这篇是少见的、带完整失败分析和 12,000 候选剖析的一手案例。对 ML-for-Systems / 硬件-软件协同设计方向也直接相关：它同时给出了「agent 能赢人类冠军」和「赢得很窄且多核会退化」两个同样重要的结论。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a matched-compute ArchAgent v1 (no cascade, no storage loop) actually fail, or just converge more slowly? Without that control, "does not scale" is a claim, not a finding.
- Does cascade ordering matter? L1D-first was justified by BertiGO's L1D-dominant gains — a circular justification that bakes the human prior into the "automated" search. What does LLC-first or a round-robin cascade discover?
- Can the multi-core regression be fixed by cheaper proxies (sampled/checkpointed multi-core simulation, learned IPC surrogates) rather than by double-weighting a single-core low-bandwidth configuration that demonstrably mis-predicts multi-core behavior?
- Why does the evolved policy lose 7.9 pts to BertiGO specifically on AI-ML high-bandwidth workloads? Is this a training-mix coverage artifact or a structural weakness of the GST/GDHT arbitration under streaming tensor access?
- Given that the largest lineage jump came from combining three island programs, would explicitly scheduling cross-island merge rounds (rather than random prompt injection) improve the ~50% discard rate?
- Could the `prefetcher_size()` idea be extended to a real timing/power model (e.g., CACTI-backed) so the agent is constrained by the full realizability envelope rather than area alone?
- How much of the 12,000-candidate cost is irreducible? The finding that mean proposal quality does not improve over time suggests the program database is not exploiting its own history well — is that an AlphaEvolve limitation or a prompt/context limitation?
