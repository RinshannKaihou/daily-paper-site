> **一句话总结：** ReliableNet 把"高置信度且预测错误"（Joint Confident-Wrong, JCW）的概率显式写成机会约束（chance constraint）加进 ERM 训练目标，用 Geletu-Hoffman 平滑内逼近 + 两时间尺度原始-对偶更新求解；在 6 个数据集上它是唯一在全部 5 个随机种子上都通过 Clopper-Pearson 认证的方法（如 CIFAR-10 上 JCW 0.0061 vs 温度缩放 0.0319，α=0.03；Adult 上 0.0271 vs 0.0520），代价是覆盖率明显下降（CIFAR-10 0.52 vs 0.68）。

> **TL;DR:** ReliableNet reformulates classifier training as a chance-constrained ERM problem that bounds the *Joint Confident-Wrong* probability — P(confidence ≥ ε AND prediction wrong) ≤ α — using a smooth conservative inner surrogate plus a two-timescale primal-dual solver, and certifies the final model with a held-out Clopper-Pearson bound. Across UCI Adult, German Credit, UNSW-NB15, a synthetic ambiguity band, Colored-MNIST-10 and CIFAR-10, it is the only method certified on 5/5 seeds for every dataset (baselines: ERM 0/5 almost everywhere, temperature scaling 1–3/5, CRC 1–3/5, SelectiveNet/Deep Gamblers 1–3/5). Headline deltas: CIFAR-10 JCW 0.0061 vs 0.0319 for temperature scaling (α = 0.03, a 5.2× reduction) and Adult 0.0271 vs 0.0520 (~2×). The honest caveat the paper itself flags: ReliableNet buys this partly with abstention — coverage drops to 0.52 on CIFAR-10 (vs 0.68) and 0.39 on German Credit (vs 0.50) — though matched-coverage risk-coverage curves (AURC 0.0159 vs 0.0211 on Adult) suggest at least part of the gain is genuinely better confidence ranking. Read it if you care about training-time (not post-hoc) reliability constraints; skip if you need large-scale vision/LLM evidence.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化 JCW 概率**：把"置信度 c_θ(x) ≥ ε 且 ŷ ≠ y"这一部署中最危险的失效事件定义为一个联合概率 JCW(θ)，并证明它可以被标量违反函数 g_θ(Z) = min{c_θ(X) − ε, ℓ_θ(X,Y)} 在测度零集之外**精确编码**（Corollary 3.5），从而把两个事件的交集化归为一个标准机会约束。
- **理论连接**：给出分解式 JCW(θ) = Cov(θ)·(1 − Acc_hc(θ))，由此推出选择性风险上界 P(错误 | 接受) ≤ α/Cov(θ)（Corollary 3.9），以及在模型过自信时高置信区间上的加权校准误差界 WCE_hc(θ) ≤ α（Proposition 3.11）——这解释了为什么约束 JCW 会"顺带"改善 ECE。
- **可微内逼近 + 有限样本保证**：采用 Geletu-Hoffman 参数族 ζ(τ,s) 构造保守的内逼近（ψ_τ(θ) ≤ α ⟹ JCW(θ) ≤ α），并配一个外逼近 Π(τ,s) 仅用于计算包络宽度 Δ_τ 作为停机诊断；给出一致收敛定理（Theorem 4.5/4.6）把经验代理可行性与总体可行性联系起来，以及独立留出集上的 Clopper-Pearson 硬事件认证（Theorem 4.7）。
- **实用训练算法**：Algorithm 1 用 10 epoch ERM 热启动确定阈值 ε*（选择折上误分类样本置信度的 80 分位数），随后以 τ 连续下降（τ₀=0.5→τ_min=0.01，γ=0.65）分阶段求解，模型参数做梯度下降、乘子 λ 做投影上升并带 EMA 平滑与上限 λ_max=50。
- **系统性对比**：与 ERM、温度缩放、置信度阈值、Conformal Risk Control、SelectiveNet、Deep Gamblers 六个基线在 4 个表格 + 2 个图像数据集、5 个种子、5 类分布漂移（人口学、模糊性、伪相关、新类别、协变量）下比较。

## Key Contributions

- Defines the **Joint Confident-Wrong (JCW) probability** as the training-time object to bound, rather than a post-hoc diagnostic, and shows it is exactly encodable (up to a P-null tie set) by a single scalar violation function, converting an intersection of two events into one standard chance constraint.
- Establishes population identities that tie JCW to two neighboring literatures: the decomposition JCW = Cov · (1 − Acc_hc) yields a selective-risk bound α/Cov and a coverage-weighted high-confidence calibration bound under overconfidence — i.e. the constraint provably subsumes weaker versions of what selective prediction and calibration target.
- Adapts the inner/outer analytic approximation framework of Geletu et al. (2017) to deep classifiers, with a cheap two-forward-pass "bracket width" Δ_τ stopping diagnostic (tolerance 10⁻⁴) replacing the expensive practice of solving both approximation problems at every smoothing level.
- Provides both a uniform-concentration finite-sample feasibility theorem for data-dependent outputs and a distribution-free Clopper-Pearson certificate on the *hard* JCW event, and uses the latter (not the former) as the reported certification metric.
- Empirically demonstrates that a training-time constraint transfers better under shift than post-hoc threshold selection, with per-subgroup and severity-sweep analyses (education subgroups on Adult, age subgroups on German Credit, attack families on UNSW-NB15, γ/ρ/σ sweeps).

## Methodology

The setup is standard multiclass classification with softmax confidence c_θ(x) = max_k p_θ(k|x). Fixing a threshold ε ∈ (1/K, 1), the target failure event is {c_θ(X) ≥ ε} ∩ {ŷ_θ(X) ≠ Y}. Using the multiclass margin ℓ_θ(x,y) = max_{k≠y} f_θ(x)_k − f_θ(x)_y, the authors define g_θ(Z) = min{c_θ(X) − ε, ℓ_θ(X,Y)} so that {g_θ > 0} coincides with the failure event almost surely (Assumption 3.4 rules out exact ties). The learning problem becomes min_θ E[L] + R(θ) s.t. P{g_θ(Z) > 0} ≤ α.

Because the indicator is discontinuous and the constraint nonconvex, they replace the Heaviside function with the Geletu-Hoffman family ζ(τ,s) = (1 + m₁τ)/(1 + m₂τ e^{−s/τ}), which is bounded, dominates 1{s ≥ 0} pointwise (given 0 < m₂ < m₁/(1+m₁)), and converges to it as τ ↓ 0. The inner surrogate ψ_τ(θ) = E[ζ(τ, g_θ(Z))] therefore over-estimates JCW, giving the conservative implication ψ_τ(θ) ≤ α ⟹ JCW(θ) ≤ α, and the feasible-set nesting F_τ^in ⊆ F ⊆ F_τ^out. Only the inner problem is optimized; the outer surrogate is evaluated purely to report the enclosure width Δ_τ = ϕ_τ − (1 − ψ_τ), which → 0 as τ ↓ 0 by dominated convergence.

Optimization uses the Lagrangian L_τ(θ,λ) = E[L] + R(θ) + λ(ψ_τ(θ) − α) with λ ∈ [0, λ_max]. This is nonconvex-concave, motivating gradient descent on θ and projected ascent on λ; the violation is normalized as v = (ψ̂_τ − α)/max(α, 10⁻²) and smoothed with an EMA (β = 0.75) before the dual step (η_λ = 1.1). Hyperparameters: τ₀ = 0.5, τ_min = 0.01, γ = 0.65, δ_tol = 10⁻⁴, λ_max = 50, m₁ = 0.5, m₂ = 0.3, Adam lr 10⁻³, weight decay 10⁻⁴, batch 256, grad-norm clip 5. Architectures: 2×128 MLP (tabular), 5-layer CNN (Colored-MNIST), ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10). Data are split into train / selection / certification folds; ε* and all baseline operating points are fixed on the selection fold, and the certification fold is untouched until the model is frozen. α = 0.05 (tabular), 0.03 (CIFAR-10), 0.01 (Colored-MNIST), ρ = 0.05, 5 seeds.

The important design choice for comparability: ERM, temperature scaling and ReliableNet are all evaluated at the *same* threshold ε*, while methods with native scores (CRC, SelectiveNet, Deep Gamblers, confidence thresholding) get the most permissive selection-fold operating point that satisfies the empirical JCW budget. Since resulting coverages differ, AURC and full risk-coverage curves are reported as the coverage-matched comparison.

## Results & Highlights

- **In-distribution certification (Tables 3–4, 5 seeds):** ReliableNet is certified 5/5 on all six datasets. Best baseline certification rates are 2/5 (German Credit: CRC, SelectiveNet, Deep Gamblers), 2/5 (Adult), 3/5 (SyntheticBand: TempScaling/ConfThresholding/CRC), 3/5 (Colored-MNIST), 2/5 (CIFAR-10: SelectiveNet), 3/5 (UNSW-NB15). ERM is 0/5 everywhere except Colored-MNIST (1/5).
- **CIFAR-10 (α = 0.03):** JCW 0.0061 ± 0.0021 vs 0.0319 ± 0.0029 for temperature scaling, 0.0321 for SelectiveNet, 0.0330 for Deep Gamblers, and 0.1692 for ERM — a 5.2× reduction over the strongest baseline (JCW/α of 0.20 vs 1.06). Accuracy also improves: 0.853 ± 0.011 vs 0.831 ± 0.009 for ERM. But coverage falls to 0.52 ± 0.06 vs 0.68 for the post-hoc baselines, and AURC is 0.0530 vs 0.0319 reported for Deep Gamblers in the same table — i.e. ReliableNet does *not* have the best in-distribution AURC on CIFAR-10.
- **Adult (α = 0.05):** JCW 0.0271 ± 0.0144 vs 0.0517–0.0520 for TempScaling/ConfThresholding/CRC/SelectiveNet/DeepGamblers (~1.9× better), accuracy 0.821 vs 0.809–0.816, ECE 0.015 vs 0.018 (TempScaling), AURC 0.0159 vs 0.0211 (ERM-score methods) — a genuine ranking gain, not just abstention. Coverage 0.52 ± 0.12 vs 0.60–0.63.
- **SyntheticBand:** JCW 0.0005 ± 0.0003 (JCW/α = 0.01) vs 0.0437 for Deep Gamblers and 0.0515 for temperature scaling — two orders of magnitude, but coverage collapses to 0.42 vs 0.67–0.76, and AURC is 0.0712 vs 0.0683 for SelectiveNet (slightly worse). In the 2D visualization at ε* = 0.66, ERM makes 19 confident-wrong predictions inside the irreducible-noise band while ReliableNet makes 0.
- **German Credit:** JCW 0.0274 vs 0.0421 (Deep Gamblers) and 0.0463 (CRC); high-confidence accuracy 0.928 vs 0.912 (SelectiveNet); ECE 0.101 vs 0.090 for temperature scaling (*worse*). Notably overall accuracy **drops** to 0.749 ± 0.039 vs 0.762 for ERM and 0.773 for Deep Gamblers, and coverage to 0.39 vs 0.44–0.50 — this is the one dataset where the reliability gain visibly costs predictive quality.
- **Colored-MNIST-10 (α = 0.01):** JCW 0.0023 ± 0.0006 vs 0.0085 (SelectiveNet), accuracy 0.992 vs 0.986, AURC 0.0076 vs 0.009, and coverage is *not* sacrificed (0.98 vs 0.86–0.99).
- **UNSW-NB15:** JCW 0.0154 vs 0.0482 (Deep Gamblers), high-confidence accuracy 0.982 vs 0.950, but coverage 0.85 vs 0.96 and AURC 0.0139 vs 0.0133 for Deep Gamblers (marginally worse).
- **Under shift, CIFAR-10 Gaussian noise:** at σ = 0.05 ReliableNet stays inside the budget at JCW ≈ 0.022 (α = 0.03) while every baseline has already violated at 2–2.7α (≈0.06–0.081) and ERM sits at 0.34; the 2–3× advantage over the strongest baseline persists at all severities. Baseline coverage *increases* between σ = 0.1 and 0.25 while their JCW rises — the signature of corruption-induced overconfidence.
- **Ambiguity sweep (SyntheticBand):** all baselines violate α = 0.05 from γ = 0.5 onward and reach 4–5α at γ = 1.3, where ReliableNet is at JCW ≈ 0.001 (2% of budget) and stays feasible up to γ ≈ 1.1, roughly twice the severity tolerated by any baseline. Coverage is invariant to γ for all methods (the sweep perturbs p(y|x), not p(x)), so this gap is pure constraint slack, not extra abstention — the cleanest experiment in the paper.
- **German Credit age shift (train ≥30, test <30):** ReliableNet is the only method under α = 0.05 on both the 20–25 and 25–30 subgroups (JCW ≈ 0.03 at ~25% coverage); temperature scaling, confidence thresholding, CRC, SelectiveNet and Deep Gamblers all land at 0.06–0.09 and ERM at 0.32–0.37.
- **UNSW-NB15 unseen attack families:** ReliableNet retains only 45% coverage on Reconnaissance and 37% on Shellcode (vs 78–100% for baselines) but reaches JCW 0.012 and 0.007 there (ERM: 0.19 and 0.13); on Backdoor and Worms it keeps 90–96% coverage with the lowest JCW — evidence that the abstention is targeted rather than uniform.
- **Where it fails:** under full Colored-MNIST color inversion (ρ_test = 0.1), *no* method meets the α = 0.01 budget; ReliableNet reaches ≈0.17 vs 0.24–0.26 for the strongest baselines and 0.37 for ERM, degrading most slowly but still ~17× over budget.

## Authors & Credibility

Five authors from the University of KwaZulu-Natal (South Africa) and the African Institute for Mathematical Sciences (AIMS) Research and Innovation Centre, Kigali. Abebe Geletu is a co-author and is also the author of the inner-outer analytic approximation framework (Geletu et al., 2017) that the method builds on, which lends real credibility to the optimization-theoretic core — the smoothing family, the bracket, and the continuation scheme are used correctly and the proofs in Sections 3–4 and Appendix A are elementary but sound (Corollary 3.5, Lemma 4.4, Theorems 4.5–4.7 all check out on inspection). This is an applied-mathematics/optimization group rather than a mainstream deep-learning lab, which shows in both directions: the constrained-optimization machinery is handled with care, while the deep-learning experimental practice (small datasets, weak CIFAR-10 backbone, no released code) is below the standard of a top-tier ML venue.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-high).** Framing confident-wrong error as a chance constraint solved *during* training is a genuinely different position from calibration, conformal risk control, and selective prediction, and the exact-encoding trick g_θ = min{c_θ − ε, ℓ_θ} is neat. That said, the constraint P(confident ∧ wrong) ≤ α is, by their own Corollary 3.7, just selective risk × coverage; the conceptual distance from SelectiveNet's coverage-penalized selective risk is smaller than the framing suggests. The novelty is more in the certification story (Clopper-Pearson on the hard event) and the transfer of the Geletu-Hoffman machinery than in the objective itself.

**Rigor — the central worry is coverage.** JCW = Cov · (1 − Acc_hc), so it can be driven to zero by abstaining. The authors are commendably upfront about this (Remark 3.8 explicitly says to report coverage and throughput alongside JCW), and the risk-coverage/AURC analysis is the right defense. But the defense only partly holds: ReliableNet has the best in-distribution AURC on Adult (0.0159 vs 0.0211), Colored-MNIST (0.0076 vs 0.009) and German Credit (0.2145 vs 0.2289), yet is *worse* on SyntheticBand (0.0712 vs 0.0683) and UNSW-NB15 (0.0139 vs 0.0133) and appears far worse on CIFAR-10 (0.0530 vs 0.0319 for Deep Gamblers). So on 3 of 6 datasets the headline JCW win is not backed by a better confidence ordering in-distribution — meaning it *is* substantially the abstention. The paper's Section 5.3.2 claims the lowest AURC on CIFAR-10 (0.053 ± 0.004) under the stress test, which does not contradict Table 4 but is easy to misread as an overall claim. Also worth flagging: the Deep Gamblers CIFAR-10 AURC entry (0.0319 ± 0.0029) is numerically identical to the temperature-scaling CIFAR-10 JCW entry in the same table, which looks like a copy-paste error the authors should verify.

**Missing ablations (the biggest gap).** There is no ablation section at all. Nothing isolates: (a) the choice of ε* as the 80th percentile of misclassified-example confidences — an arbitrary heuristic with no sensitivity study, despite ε being the hyperparameter that defines the whole problem; (b) the two-timescale/EMA dual update vs a plain penalty with fixed λ; (c) the τ continuation schedule (γ = 0.65, Δ_τ < 10⁻⁴) vs a single fixed τ; (d) m₁ = 0.5, m₂ = 0.3, λ_max = 50. Most importantly, **the obvious control is absent**: take the ERM model and set its threshold to match ReliableNet's coverage (0.52 on CIFAR-10, 0.39 on German Credit) and report JCW. The confidence-thresholding baseline is matched on *JCW budget*, not on coverage, so this comparison is never made directly. Without it, one cannot cleanly separate "trained a better model" from "abstained more."

**Weak backbone / weak baselines.** ERM ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 reaches only 83.1% accuracy — roughly 11 points below a standard ResNet-18 CIFAR-10 baseline (~94–95%). No augmentation is described, and the optimizer is Adam at lr 10⁻³ for 80 epochs. Undertrained networks are dramatically more overconfident, which inflates the headline ERM JCW of 0.1692 and makes the reliability problem easier to "fix." Relatedly, four of six datasets are small tabular problems (German Credit has only 1000 records total, and its ±0.03–0.04 std bars swallow several of the between-method differences). Despite two full paragraphs of related work on Bayesian and ensemble uncertainty (MC dropout, deep ensembles, ODIN, Mahalanobis, energy scores), **none of these appear as baselines** — deep ensembles in particular are the standard strong comparator for confident-error reduction under shift.

**Reproducibility (weak).** No code or data-release statement anywhere in the paper. Hyperparameters and the algorithm are specified in enough detail to reimplement, and the seeds are given ({0, 37, 42, 123, 2026}), but certification fold sizes m are never reported — and the Clopper-Pearson bound U_CP(K_m, m; ρ) depends directly on m, so the "5/5 certified" headline is not fully auditable. The per-dataset α values (0.05 / 0.03 / 0.01) are also chosen without justification; a reader cannot tell whether they were set before or after seeing results.

**Significance.** Real but bounded. The strongest evidence — the γ sweep, where coverage is provably invariant so the entire gap is constraint slack, and the CIFAR-10 σ = 0.05 result where every baseline violates and ReliableNet does not — makes a convincing case that training-time constraints leave the solution in the *interior* of the feasible set, and that this interior slack is what buys robustness under shift. That is a genuinely useful insight, and it is the paper's best contribution. But no large-scale vision, no foundation models, no LLM setting, and the finite-sample surrogate theorem (Theorem 4.5) is effectively vacuous for an 11M-parameter ResNet since δ_n scales with √(d ln 3n / n) and the global Lipschitz constant L_g — the authors concede this in the limitations.

**No red flags for dishonesty.** The paper reports its losses (coverage drops, Colored-MNIST full-inversion failure where nothing meets budget, German Credit accuracy regression to 0.749, ECE worse than temperature scaling on German Credit, UNSW-NB15 AURC tie/loss), and Remark 3.8 pre-empts the abstention critique rather than hiding it. The overclaiming that exists is mild and mostly in phrasing ("stronger or very competitive").

## Strengths

- The problem framing is genuinely sharp: "confident and wrong" is the failure mode that defeats every confidence-triggered safeguard, and the paper makes a persuasive argument that ECE, conformal coverage, and selective risk each miss it for a specific, articulable reason (e.g. citing Chidambaram & Ge 2025 that a constant-confidence predictor achieves near-zero ECE).
- The theory is honest about what it buys: the inner approximation is *conservative* by construction, so feasibility of the surrogate implies feasibility of the real constraint — one-directional, which is the correct direction for a safety claim.
- The evaluation protocol is disciplined: three disjoint folds, all thresholds and checkpoints fixed on the selection fold, certification fold untouched, and every method evaluated with the same hard JCW event and the same certificate.
- The severity sweeps are the right experiments, and the γ sweep in particular is a well-designed control (perturbing p(y|x) with p(x) fixed makes coverage non-adaptive and isolates constraint slack).
- The subgroup analyses are informative and self-critical — the UNSW-NB15 per-family breakdown surfaces that marginal JCW feasibility does not imply subgroup feasibility, which the authors then name as an open direction rather than bury.
- Limitations section is specific and accurate rather than boilerplate (dimension-dependent bound conservatism, threshold-selection/checkpoint/certification budget entanglement, group-conditional control).

## Limitations

- Classification-only, with a single scalar confidence threshold; regression, structured prediction and sequential decision-making are untouched.
- Scale: the largest experiment is CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, and that backbone is undertrained (83.1% ERM accuracy). No ImageNet, no transformers, no foundation models.
- The reliability gain is entangled with reduced coverage on most datasets (CIFAR-10 0.52 vs 0.68; German Credit 0.39 vs 0.50; UNSW-NB15 0.85 vs 0.96), and no coverage-matched ERM control is run.
- The uniform finite-sample certificate (Theorem 4.5/4.6) is unusable in practice for deep networks; only the Clopper-Pearson certificate carries real weight, and it requires an independent certification sample drawn from the distribution one wants to certify — which is exactly what is unavailable under the distribution shifts the paper stress-tests.
- Certification is in-distribution only. Under shift, the paper reports *empirical* JCW comparisons, not certificates; and under full spurious-correlation inversion no method (including ReliableNet, at ≈0.17 vs α = 0.01) is anywhere near feasible.
- Two extra hyperparameters (ε and α) are exposed to the practitioner as "interpretable," but ε is actually set by an unablated 80th-percentile heuristic, and per-dataset α values vary without stated justification.
- No ablation study, no released code, no reported certification-fold sizes.

## Relevance

如果你（用户）关心 LLM/模型可靠性、幻觉抑制与拒答策略，这篇的核心思想很值得借鉴：把"自信地答错"作为一个可训练的概率约束而非事后校准问题，其 JCW = Cov·(1 − Acc_hc) 分解和 Clopper-Pearson 认证流程可以直接迁移到 LLM 的 abstention/routing 场景（例如把 ε 换成 verbalized confidence 或 logit-based score）。但要注意它的实验规模停留在 CIFAR-10 及表格数据，且覆盖率下降与"少答"的混淆没有被彻底切开，迁移到大模型时需要自己补上覆盖率匹配的对照实验。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is ERM's JCW when its threshold is raised to match ReliableNet's coverage exactly (0.52 on CIFAR-10, 0.39 on German Credit, 0.85 on UNSW-NB15)? This single control would settle how much of the headline gain is representation change vs abstention.
2. How sensitive is everything to ε*? The 80th percentile of misclassified-example confidence is arbitrary; sweeping it to the 50th/90th/95th percentile would show whether the constraint is doing something robust or whether ε* is being implicitly tuned.
3. Would deep ensembles or MC dropout — discussed at length in Section 2 but never run — close the JCW gap? Ensembles are the standard strong baseline for confident-error suppression under shift and their absence is the most conspicuous hole in the comparison.
4. Does the advantage survive a properly trained CIFAR-10 backbone (SGD + augmentation, ~94% accuracy)? Well-trained networks are less pathologically overconfident, so the ERM JCW of 0.1692 should shrink substantially — by how much, and does ReliableNet still win?
5. Can the constraint be made group-conditional (as the authors suggest) without a coverage collapse, given that the UNSW-NB15 breakdown already shows marginal feasibility masking per-family failure at 37–45% coverage?
6. How does the two-timescale primal-dual scheme behave when the constraint is infeasible for the given architecture — does λ saturate at λ_max = 50 and effectively become a fixed penalty, and is that detectable at training time?
7. Could the same formulation be applied to token-level or sequence-level confidence in autoregressive models, where the "confident and wrong" event is exactly the hallucination failure mode, and where the margin ℓ_θ has a natural per-token analogue?
