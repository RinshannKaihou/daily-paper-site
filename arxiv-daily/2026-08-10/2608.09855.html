Methodology — argued, not run

No experiments — the "method" is a structured analogy plus a proposed protocol for others. The case is built in four moves:

  1. Establish the control-loop isomorphism. Fuzzer and research agent both propose → execute → observe → choose next. Scoped strictly to search within a declared problem.
  2. Observability. Scientific outcomes are sparse; the agent needs a coverage-analogue measuring how the search state moved (ruled out a rival, sharpened a prediction), not an early verdict.
  3. Feedback-directed search. Guidance is worthless unless the policy acts on it — AFL energy allocation, Angora's gradient constraints, directed fuzzing's distance schedule.
  4. Protected validation. Because the search games its proxy, the verdict must come from evidence shielded from optimization — hidden test, replication, independent review.

To its credit, it cites evidence against its own analogy: coverage does not rank fuzzers by bug count (Böhme, ICSE'22), and coverage-unique crashes collapse to a few distinct bugs (Klees, CCS'18) — both folded into requirement C3.

Strengths

  • Crisp, low-cost rule. Stop your judge steering and certifying — failures become attributable to a component, not "retrain the judge".
  • Honest scoping. Explicitly excludes the abductive act of redefining the problem.
  • Substantive defense. Six counter-arguments (BOED, quality-diversity, bitter lesson…) each met with a real distinction.
  • Strong framing arc. Gödel → Friedberg → Gödel machine → NAS overfit → learned judges, all as one missing-component diagnosis.
  • Cites against itself and converts the counter-evidence into requirement C3.
  • Three concrete falsifiable predictions, each with controls fixed.

Limitations

  • Zero original empirics. Every number borrowed; the three predictions are proposed, not tested.
  • Core construct unoperationalized. "Epistemic progress" has C1–C3 and a menu, but no metric, pilot, or toy example.
  • Transfer asserted at its weakest joint. Angora's differentiable byte-predicates have no obvious hypothesis-space counterpart.
  • Cost accounting hand-waved. C1 ("cheap") sits in tension with "guidance and oracle need not differ in cost" — never reconciled.
  • Scope excludes the hard part: open-ended reformulation — largely the regime where validation is already easy.
  • No toy demo. A synthetic bandit showing a dense-but-gameable proxy beating terminal-only feedback would have made the essay compelling.

Open questions worth chasing

  1. Smallest signal-prediction test? Instrument one hyperparameter loop with 4–5 proxies, measure rank correlation with a held-out outcome — why didn't the authors just run it?
  2. Implicit oracle leakage. The proposer is pretrained on the validator's literature; fuzzing's oracle is causally independent. How do you wall off a validator that isn't?
  3. Coverage plateau analogue? Does auto-research have one, and is detecting it a useful redirect rule for a campaign scheduler?
  4. Cost exchange rate. Under what conditions is an expensive dense signal still worth it vs. final validation?
  5. Does "not BOED" survive? If the best proxy turns out to be cheap disagreement-based expected information gain, the distinction from Bayesian OED may collapse.
  6. What is the "target site"? Directed fuzzing needs a target to compute distance to — does requiring one reintroduce the goal-model the paper attacks QD for needing?
