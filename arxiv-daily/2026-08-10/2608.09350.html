01

Methodology, distilled

 · 方法

A β-Variational Autoencoder is trained on roughly 1,000 LRO Narrow Angle Camera frames (~0.5–2 m/pixel), tiled into 52 million 64×64-pixel patches (~32×32 m). Because lunar regolith is overwhelmingly homogeneous, the VAE learns a latent prior of the “statistically average lunar surface.”

The β term deliberately degrades reconstruction to favour latent disentanglement: the decoder emits what the model expects a patch to look like, not a copy. The per-patch anomaly score is the pixel-wise reconstruction discrepancy — high score means structure the prior cannot explain.

Evaluation has two prongs: (1) targeted recovery of two community-flagged “anomalies of interest” (Plaskett Crater, Paracelsus C); (2) technosignature retrieval at scale over 13 known landed/crashed assets, scored via a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and a precision–recall curve against a random ranker. No retraining, fine-tuning, or hyperparameter search — patch size and β are inherited verbatim from the 2024 model.

Strengths & limitations

 · 优势 / 局限

+  Strengths

  • Tests on unseen targets rather than showcasing training-set reconstructions — a genuinely rare contribution type in planetary ML.
  • Reports a PR curve and an explicit 3.02× factor instead of only cherry-picked thumbnails; the numbers are there to be criticized.
  • Volunteers its own biases — patch-fill dominance and label noise (negatives may still contain anomalies).
  • Resolves the “mysteries” to mundane geology instead of courting sensationalism — honest given a technosignatures venue.
  • Fully unsupervised at real scale (52M patches); public code; a concrete roadmap (multi-scale ensembles, β sweeps, transfer to Mars/Mercury).

!  Limitations

  • Absolute AP of 4.79×10−5 — not a standalone detector; tens of thousands of false positives per true positive.
  • Single baseline (random). No classical-CV, supervised, or simpler unsupervised (PCA / plain AE) comparator.
  • No ablation on patch size or β, despite both being named as the dominant sources of bias.
  • Fixed 64×64 scale structurally disadvantages meter-scale artificial objects — the exact class the framing cares about.
  • Evaluated only on target-containing images; global false-positive rate over the whole Moon is unmeasured.
  • Tiny positive set, no CIs, no seed variation; illumination/incidence-angle confounds undiscussed.

Questions for further exploration

 · 待探究
Q1
How much of the 3.02× survives a non-trivial baseline — pixel-variance, gradient-energy, or shadow-length heuristics on the same patches?
Q2
What does a multi-scale ensemble (32 / 64 / 256) do to the artificial-vs-natural score gap? The patch-fill hypothesis is directly falsifiable here.
Q3
How does the score correlate with solar incidence angle, slope, albedo? If shadows dominate error, the ranking may be an illumination ranking.
Q4
What is the false-positive rate on a global random sweep — i.e. how many candidates must a human review to build the proposed global map?
Q5
Would modern self-supervised features (DINOv2 + kNN / Mahalanobis) beat pixel-space reconstruction here, and at what compute cost?
Q6
Can the proposed self-supervised bootstrap loop avoid collapse — or does training on anomalies teach the model to reconstruct them, lowering future scores?
