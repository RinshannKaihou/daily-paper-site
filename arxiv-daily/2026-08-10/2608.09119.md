> **一句话总结：** Motif Technologies 发布 Motif 3——314B 总参 / 13.2B 激活的细粒度 MoE（每层 384 路由专家、top-8），核心是把 Grouped Differential Attention 与 MLA 融合成 GDLA（10B 对照实验中比 MLA 少用 9.2% token 即达到 loss 3.2），配合改良 mHC、Expert-Specific PolyNorm、MXFP8 与窗口感知上下文并行训练 12.5T token，并用「六个 GRPO 专家教师 + 一个 SFT 软工教师 → 多教师在线策略蒸馏（MOPD）」的后训练管线；结果在 agentic 类基准上最强（τ³-Banking 35.3 对最强基线 DeepSeek-V4-Pro 30.1，ITBench-AA 51.5 对 Qwen-3.7 Max 42.5），但在科学编码与科学推理上明显落后（SciCode 40.6 vs 53.5，CritPt 6.6 vs 12.9）。

> **TL;DR:** Motif 3 is a 314B-total / 13.2B-active MoE (384 routed experts per layer, top-8) built on Grouped Differential Latent Attention (GDLA = grouped differential attention + MLA's compressed KV), trained on 12.5T tokens with MXFP8 experts, Muon + QK-Clip, and per-layer context-parallel selection (Ulysses for full-attention layers, window-aware Ring halo for the 128-token sliding-window layers, a 1,024× KV-volume reduction). Post-training uses six GRPO specialist teachers plus one SFT software-engineering teacher consolidated by Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation. It leads the comparison table on agentic tool use — τ³-Banking 35.3 vs 30.1 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T-A49B) and 15.3 for MiniMax-3, ITBench-AA 51.5 vs 42.5 for Qwen-3.7 Max (public subset only) — and matches on SWE-bench Verified (76.2 vs 76.4/76.2 for GLM-5.1/Kimi-K2.6) while trailing on SciCode (40.6 vs 53.5), GPQA Diamond (83.4 vs 92.9), and CritPt (6.6 vs 12.9). Worth reading for the architecture and systems sections; the post-training gains are asserted without a single MOPD ablation.

## 核心贡献

- **GDLA（Grouped Differential Latent Attention）**：把 Differential Transformer 的「信号-噪声两路相减」与 MLA 的低秩 KV 压缩合并。KV latent 只做一次 RMSNorm + 上投影展开成 16 个 KV 头，被信号路（64 头）和噪声路（16 头）共享；差分系数 λ_t = σ(x_t W_λ) 改为 token 相关且被 sigmoid 约束在 (0,1)，只能抑制噪声不能反转放大；输出前再加一个来自 query latent 的逐元素 sigmoid 门。
- **修改版 mHC**：4 条并行残差流，Hres 用 Sinkhorn-Knopp 投影到 Birkhoff 多面体（双随机），并把 post-mapping 的乘子从 2 退火到 1（st: 2→1），以避免深层反复放大导致激活离群值累积。
- **Expert-Specific PolyNorm**：每个专家学自己的三阶多项式激活系数（sigmoid 约束到 (0,1)，bias clip 到 ±0.5），比 SwiGLU 保持更高的门控权重有效秩。
- **训练系统**：MXFP8 只用于专家 GEMM（DeepGEMM 后端），并在 EP dispatch 之前预量化（通信减半、量化成本降到 1/top-k=1/8）；FSDP 改造（多 in-flight Reduce-Scatter、跨 microbatch 抑制 DP-replicate All-Reduce、22/51 层专家权重常驻不 reshard）；Parallel Muon 对 MoE 扩展（专家无需 gather/scatter）；QK-Clip 用非对称 γ 分配（key 侧比例 r = 1/(1+√G)）防止共享 key 塌缩，并改 FlashAttention-4 kernel 直接吐出每头最大 logit。
- **长上下文**：逐层选择上下文并行算法——full attention 用 Ulysses，sliding-window 层用「halo 交换」式 Ring；并按 unmasked score 数估计工作量、用 LPT 贪心在 DP × 微批槽位间重排 packed sequence。
- **后训练**：13 个 verifier 域归并成 6 个 GRPO 专家教师 + 1 个 SFT 软工教师，再由 MOPD（只用被采样 token 的标量 teacher log-prob，配 ICE-POP 重要性权重过滤 [0.5, 5.0]）合成单一学生模型；MTP 头与路由参数全程冻结。

## Key Contributions

- **GDLA**: fuses Grouped Differential Attention (asymmetric signal/noise head allocation, g=4) with MLA's low-rank KV latent so both attention paths share one compressed KV state; adds a token-dependent, sigmoid-bounded differential coefficient and a query-latent-derived output gate. Claimed to reach loss 3.2 with 9.2% fewer tokens than MLA in a controlled ~10B comparison.
- **Modified manifold-constrained hyper-connections**: 4 residual streams, doubly-stochastic (Sinkhorn) residual mixing, and a post-mapping multiplier annealed 2→1 during pretraining to stop layer-wise outlier accumulation without hard clipping.
- **Expert-Specific PolyNorm**: per-expert learned polynomial activation coefficients, preserving higher effective rank in expert gate weights than SwiGLU.
- **MoE stabilization stack**: FP32 routing, auxiliary-loss-free selection bias, sequence-wise load-balancing loss with layer-specific weights (2.4e-4 at layer 2), cosine-decayed Gaussian router noise, a soft FFN magnitude penalty instead of gpt-oss-style clamping, and a six-metric expert-health monitor with explicit healthy/unhealthy thresholds (dispatch min/median > 0.7 healthy, < 0.3 starving; max expert-output cosine similarity < 0.5 healthy, > 0.8 redundant).
- **Systems**: selective MXFP8, memory-efficient MoE permutation with the routing weight folded into the permuted activation, Liger fused linear cross-entropy for the 220K vocabulary, Parallel Muon for experts, optimizer-state CPU offload driven from activation-checkpoint hooks, and per-layer context-parallel algorithm selection at 256K.
- **Post-training**: multi-teacher on-policy distillation from seven specialists into one student, with per-domain reward designs (binary outcome verifiers for terminal tasks; four-grade abstention ladder 1/0.666/0.333/0 for the long-context teacher; bidirectional LLM-judge preference for professional work; a Korean rubric with eight anti-patterns and piecewise length shaping for chat).

## Methodology

**Architecture.** 53 layers (2 dense + 51 MoE), d_model 4,096, 384 routed + 1 shared expert per MoE layer with top-8 routing, expert FFN dim 1,280, dense FFN dim 12,288. Attention is a 1-full / 3-sliding-window (W = 128) hybrid, 80 query heads and 16 KV heads, d_qk = 192, d_v = 128, 64-dim rotary component, query/KV low-rank dims 1,024/512. Signal:noise = 64:16 (g = 4). A 1-layer MTP head supports self-speculative decoding. Max context 262,144.

**Pretraining.** ~12.5T tokens under the Motif tokenizer (220,160 vocab, trained with SuperBPE two-stage pre-tokenization so tokens can span whitespace-delimited words, e.g. "of the", "수 있다"). ~70% of the corpus is retained NVIDIA Nemotron public data; the rest is in-house web/STEM/synthetic, with dedicated Korean, legal, and financial content. Knowledge cutoff March 2026. Dynamic mixture scheduling grows the pool from 19 to 57 datasets with linear/cosine interpolation of category ratios. WSD schedule: LR 3e-4 → 7.5e-5 during decay (4K → 32K sequences) → 3e-5 for the 256K stage; global batch up to 75M tokens; full-attention layers use DeepSeek-YaRN with extension factor 64, sliding-window layers keep local RoPE. Reasoning-focused data is deliberately capped below 5%.

**Distributed training.** EP = 8 inside a node over NVLink (HybridEP), DP-shard = 8, remaining ranks DP-replicate; no pipeline parallelism thanks to B200 memory plus optimizer-state offload. CP = 8 reuses the EP process dimension for the long-context stage. Gradients travel in BF16 but reduce locally in FP32; only the row-wise MXFP8 weight is All-Gathered and the column-wise copy is transcoded locally.

**Post-training.** A preliminary SFT model is used only to mine failure-prone decisions in agentic trajectories; the real student restarts from the pretrained checkpoint (batch 33M tokens, LR 3e-5 → 1.5e-6 cosine, max length 256K, assistant-only loss masking on both NTP and MTP). RL uses TorchTitan wired in as an external NeMo RL backend with NeMo Gym verifiers, asynchronous GRPO with one-step trajectory-age bounds, in-flight vLLM weight refits at wave boundaries, and truncated importance sampling in [0.2, 5.0]. DAPO-style asymmetric clipping [0.8, 1.28], token-level loss, advantages clipped to [-4, 4], LR 3e-6, rollouts up to 192K tokens. Prompts are filtered offline to empirical pass rate 0 < p ≤ 0.8 using 4–8 pre-rollouts. MOPD then runs a 512-trajectory on-policy batch with Muon, LR 5e-6 → 3e-6, max sequence 163,840, environment rewards and reference-KL both removed.

## Results & Highlights

- **τ³-Banking: 35.3 for Motif 3 (314B-A13B) vs 30.1 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T-A49B), the strongest baseline (+5.2).** The rest of the table is far behind — Kimi-K2.6 23.3, MiniMax-3 15.3, GLM-5.1 13.6, Qwen-3.7 Max 12.0 — so this is the paper's single cleanest win, and it is achieved with roughly 1/5 the total parameters of the best baseline.
- **ITBench-AA: 51.5 vs 42.5 for Qwen-3.7 Max (+9.0)** — but the Motif 3 number carries an asterisk meaning "public subset only," while the baselines are leaderboard numbers presumably on the full set. The paper still calls it "the highest among the available results," which the footnote does not support.
- **Terminal-Bench 2.1: 74.9 vs 75.0 for Qwen-3.7 Max (−0.1), and +9.0 over Kimi-K2.6 (65.9) and +13.1 over GLM-5.1 (61.8).** Essentially tied for best, and the strongest evidence for the "terminal-based problem solving" claim.
- **SWE-bench Verified: 76.2, statistically indistinguishable from GLM-5.1 (76.4) and Kimi-K2.6 (76.2), but 4.2 points behind Qwen-3.7 Max (80.4).** Note Motif 3 is evaluated with mini-SWE-agent, 16K tokens/step, 250 steps — the baselines' scaffolds are not stated.
- **SciCode: 40.6 vs 53.5 for both Kimi-K2.6 and Qwen-3.7 Max (−12.9)** — the worst gap in the table, and the paper acknowledges it. **CritPt: 6.6 vs 12.9 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro (−6.3)** and 11.4 for Qwen-3.7 Max, i.e. roughly half the frontier score on research-level physics.
- **GPQA Diamond: 83.4 vs 92.9 for MiniMax-3 (−9.5); HLE: 37.0 vs 41.4 for Qwen-3.7 Max (−4.4); IMO-AnswerBench: 83.2 vs 90.0 for Qwen-3.7 Max (−6.8); Apex Shortlist: 75.5 vs 85.8 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro (−10.3).** Knowledge and olympiad math are the model's weakest axis relative to peers.
- **AA-Omniscience: accuracy 30.1 vs 42.9 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro (−12.8); non-hallucination 71.6 vs 81.6 for MiniMax-3 (−10.0) and 74.0 for Qwen-3.7 Max.** The report describes 71.6 as "among the highest results listed" — it is third of six. The genuinely striking datapoint is DeepSeek-V4-Pro's non-hallucination score of 5.9 alongside its 42.9 accuracy, i.e. it buys accuracy with rampant guessing.
- **Long context / IF: AA-LCR 72.3 vs 80.3 for MiniMax-3 (−8.0); IFBench 78.2 vs 82.9 for MiniMax-3 (−4.7).** GDPval-AA v2 38.7 vs 44.4 for MiniMax-3 (−5.7). τ²-Bench Telecom 94.7 vs 97.7 for GLM-5.1 (−3.0).
- **Base model (no baselines given):** MMLU 86.20 (5-shot), MMLU-Pro 68.56 (5-shot CoT), ARC-C 94.71, GSM8K 93.93, MATH 70.58, HumanEval 73.70 pass@1, MBPP 84.60.
- **Tokenizer (Table 2, bytes/token, higher better):** English 5.68 vs 4.73 for DeepSeek-V4 (+20%), Korean 5.31 vs 4.03 for Qwen3.5 (+32%), math 4.55 vs 3.86, code 4.07 vs 3.92 — but Japanese 3.87 vs 4.22 (Qwen3.5) and Chinese 3.51 vs 4.20 (DeepSeek-V4) are clearly worse. The 12.5T-token budget is claimed to be "more than 15T" under comparison tokenizers, which is a probe-based estimate, not a retokenization.
- **Systems measurements:** window-aware Ring halo reduces rank-average received KV volume by an analytic 1,024× at L = 256K, W = 128 (1.1 MB vs Ring-full's 3.5× KV bar), with a 1,536× communication-intensity gap; in one sampled packed sequence of 473 documents, rank 7 carried 3.54× the mean attention FLOPs, motivating the LPT rebalancing. MXFP8 pre-quantization before dispatch halves dispatch communication and cuts quantization cost 8× at top-k = 8.

## Authors & Credibility

Twenty-seven authors from Motif Technologies (Korea), alphabetically ordered with an explicit contributions section naming Joon Son Chung, Sungmin Lee, and Junghwan Lim as technical/management leadership. Joon Son Chung is a well-known researcher in speech and multimodal learning. The group has a credible track record on exactly this line: they authored the Grouped Differential Attention paper (arXiv:2510.06949) that GDLA extends, and the Motif 2 12.7B technical report (arXiv:2511.07464) whose Parallel Muon and dynamic mixture scheduling are reused here. The work is funded by Korea's MSIT/NIPA under the Sovereign AI Foundation Model Project, which is consistent with the heavy Korean-language emphasis (tokenizer, chat rubric, corpus). A training-system reference repository is cited (github.com/MotifTechnologies/motif3-training-example).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-good, mostly compositional).** GDLA is a real and sensible synthesis — sharing one MLA KV latent between the differential signal and noise query paths is the non-obvious trick, and making λ token-dependent and sigmoid-bounded is a clean improvement over the original Differential Transformer's static per-layer coefficient. The QK-Clip modification (asymmetric burden split r = 1/(1+√G), justified by the observation that a shared key's recovery gradient scales as 1/√G because contributions from G query heads partially cancel) is a genuinely useful, hard-won detail. Nearly everything else — MLA, sigmoid routing, auxiliary-loss-free bias, MTP, mHC, Muon, DAPO clipping, on-policy distillation — is assembled from prior work, which is fine for a technical report but should temper "novel architecture" readings.

**Rigor (the weakest dimension).** The headline post-training contribution, MOPD, has **zero ablation**: there is no table comparing the general SFT student, the individual teachers, and the MOPD student on any benchmark. We are told MOPD "consolidates complementary capabilities," but nothing rules out that a simpler SFT-on-teacher-outputs baseline, or even one of the seven teachers alone, would score similarly on Table 6. Likewise the seven-teacher decomposition into 6 GRPO jobs is justified by prose ("verifier latency and reward variance differ") rather than an experiment against a single mixed-verifier policy. The architectural diagnostics (Figures 2 and 6) are all at ~10B scale, single curves, no seeds, no downstream evaluation — the only quantitative claim from them is "loss 3.2 with 9.2% fewer tokens than MLA," a training-loss proxy that says nothing about task quality, and the mHC 2→1 annealing is supported only by the qualitative statement that outliers accumulated. The FFN magnitude regularizer is reported honestly as a partial fix ("we no longer observed isolated extreme outliers... however, the activation growth became distributed across a broader set of channels, and the overall FFN output RMS continued to increase"), which is refreshing but also means the stated problem is not actually solved.

**Comparison hygiene (problematic).** Section 5.3.1 states outright that baseline numbers are "scores reported on the corresponding benchmark leaderboards" — none were re-run. Motif 3's own runs use temperature 1.0 / top-p 0.95 with specific harnesses (Terminus 2 with 250 episodes and 2-hour timeouts; mini-SWE-agent with 250 steps and 4-hour timeouts; Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507 as the τ²-Bench user simulator). Agentic benchmarks are notoriously scaffold-sensitive, so the +9.0 ITBench and +5.2 τ³-Banking margins should be read as "Motif's harness beat other teams' harnesses," not as a clean model comparison. The ITBench asterisk (public subset for Motif 3 only) makes that particular row not comparable at all, yet it is claimed as the highest. The AA-Omniscience non-hallucination "among the highest" phrasing is also softer than the numbers warrant (third of six). MiniMax-3 is missing entirely from ITBench-AA, IMO-AnswerBench, and Apex Shortlist, so "highest among the models listed" is doing quiet work.

**Reproducibility (partial).** The training-system optimizations have a reference repo and the hyperparameters are unusually complete (auxiliary loss weights per layer, FFN threshold 128 with 1,024 for the final layer, QK-Clip τ = 100 pretraining / 200 long-context every 10 steps, importance-weight bounds). But: no weight release is stated anywhere in the report despite the framing against "open-weight models"; ~30% of the pretraining corpus is in-house and unspecified; the router-collapse remedy — manually copying a neighboring layer's router parameters and selection biases mid-run when balance collapsed — is an unlogged manual intervention that makes the run non-reproducible in the strict sense (credit for disclosing it, though). No GPU-hours, MFU, wall-clock, or throughput numbers are reported anywhere, which is a notable omission for a report whose central pitch is efficiency; there are also no inference latency or MTP draft-acceptance-rate numbers despite the claim of "no measurable degradation" in acceptance rate.

**Significance.** A 314B-A13B model matching or beating a 1.6T-A49B model on banking tool use and a 1T-A32B model on terminal tasks is a meaningful efficiency result, and the per-layer context-parallel selection (Ulysses for full attention, halo Ring for 128-token windows) is a transferable systems insight that other hybrid-attention trainers can adopt immediately. The knowledge/science deficit (GPQA 83.4, SciCode 40.6, CritPt 6.6) is large enough that this is a specialist agentic model, not a frontier generalist.

**Red flags.** (1) No MOPD ablation for the paper's main post-training claim. (2) Public-subset-vs-full-set comparison presented as a win on ITBench-AA. (3) Leaderboard-sourced baselines with undisclosed scaffolds on scaffold-sensitive agentic benchmarks. (4) Chat reward includes explicit length shaping peaking at 512–1024 tokens, a verbosity incentive that IFBench (78.2) does not directly probe. (5) March 2026 knowledge cutoff with no contamination analysis for GPQA/HLE/IMO-AnswerBench — only SWE-bench base commits were excluded for the SFT teacher.

## Strengths

- Unusually complete disclosure of training-stability machinery, including thresholds that practitioners can actually act on (dispatch min/median > 0.7 healthy vs < 0.3 starving; expert output-weight min/median > 0.8 healthy, < 0.5 dying; max pairwise expert-output cosine similarity < 0.5 healthy, > 0.8 redundant).
- Honest negative reporting: the FFN magnitude penalty is described as suppressing spikes without preventing global RMS drift; row-to-column MXFP8 requantization error is acknowledged; Appendix B explicitly says communication volume is *not* a latency proxy and that the Ring-vs-Ulysses comparison reverses depending on whether you take the mean or the busiest rank.
- The systems section is the strongest part: folding routing weights into permuted activations to shrink retained activations, exposing the fused unpermute-combine op to the selective checkpoint policy, eliminating expert All-Gathers for 22 of 51 layers, and driving optimizer-state reload from activation-checkpoint hooks are all concrete and reusable.
- The abstention-aware reward ladder (1 / 0.666 / 0.333 / 0 with zero reward for truncated generations so the policy cannot farm the abstention reward by never finishing) is a well-designed guard, and the resulting 71.6 non-hallucination score against DeepSeek-V4-Pro's 5.9 shows the design has teeth.
- Limitations section names real gaps (text-only, evaluation coverage, long-horizon state tracking) rather than boilerplate.

## Limitations

- No ablation isolating MOPD, the seven-teacher decomposition, or the frozen-router/frozen-MTP choice; the entire post-training story rests on one aggregate table.
- All architectural evidence (GDLA, PolyNorm effective rank, router noise) comes from ~10B proxies with training-loss or spectral proxies, never downstream task accuracy at that scale.
- Baselines are leaderboard-copied, not re-run; harness and sampling settings differ; ITBench-AA compares a public subset against full-set numbers.
- Weak on scientific coding (SciCode 40.6 vs 53.5) and research-level physics (CritPt 6.6 vs 12.9), and notably behind on knowledge breadth (AA-Omniscience accuracy 30.1 vs 42.9; GPQA Diamond 83.4 vs 92.9).
- Text-only; no vision. Long-context capability is trained to 256K but AA-LCR (72.3) trails MiniMax-3 (80.3) and Kimi-K2.6 (76.7), so the 256K investment does not visibly convert into leading long-context reasoning.
- No throughput, MFU, GPU-hour, inference-latency, or MTP acceptance-rate numbers, so the efficiency claims are architectural rather than measured end-to-end.
- Manual mid-run router replacement on collapsed layers means the recipe is not fully hands-off at this scale.
- Tokenizer gains are asymmetric: strong for English/Korean/code/math, worse than Qwen3.5 on Japanese and DeepSeek-V4 on Chinese, so the "more than 15T equivalent tokens" framing is language-dependent.

## Relevance

如果你关注 MoE 架构细节（差分注意力与 MLA 的融合、专家级激活函数、mHC 残差流）或大规模训练系统工程（MXFP8 选择性量化、Parallel Muon、混合注意力下的逐层上下文并行选择），这篇报告的第 2–4 节信息密度很高、可直接借鉴。若你更关心 RL/蒸馏方法论，则要注意它的 MOPD 部分只有配方没有消融，对照价值有限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of Table 6 comes from MOPD versus the general SFT checkpoint alone? Without that delta, the seven-teacher pipeline's cost-benefit is unknowable — would a single mixed-verifier GRPO run have landed in the same place?
- Does GDLA's advantage over MLA persist at 314B, or is the 9.2%-fewer-tokens result a 10B-scale artifact? Differential attention's benefit is usually framed as noise suppression, which may matter less as model capacity grows.
- Why is SciCode (40.6) so much weaker than SWE-bench Verified (76.2) when a dedicated code-and-science teacher exists? Is the code-and-science teacher being diluted during MOPD by the software-engineering teacher, and would per-domain routing during distillation be verifiable through teacher-level scores?
- Does the piecewise length reward (peak 1.5 at 512–1024 tokens) inflate response length on non-Korean, non-chat tasks, and is any of the IFBench score explained by verbosity?
- Is the ITBench-AA public subset systematically easier than the full set? Publishing the full-set number would settle whether +9.0 over Qwen-3.7 Max survives.
- What is the actual end-to-end MFU and inference throughput relative to a dense-equivalent or to DeepSeek-V3-style MoE, given the mHC Sinkhorn iterations and per-expert PolyNorm add non-trivial FP32 work per layer?
- Does annealing the mHC post-mapping from 2 to 1 cost anything in final loss, and would starting at 1 have worked as well? The report asserts the early-training benefit of the 2× range but never tests it.
