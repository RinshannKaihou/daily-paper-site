> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 WarehouseReliabilityBench（400 个冻结任务、约一半的正确行为是澄清/弃答/拒绝）并构建 QueryProof——一个用语义层+物理目录规则决策、对每个答案做执行后确定性校验的 7B 智能体；在 80 条冻结测试集上其 Business Truth Rate 达 0.537（未路由版 0.562），比直接提示的 32B 基线 0.300 高 +0.237 [+0.112, +0.375]，每正确答案成本低 71.0%，假成功率从 0.754 降到 0.351，且在 24 个可答任务上没有返回过一个错数；但作者同时诚实报告：按模板族（而非任务）重采样后两个置信区间都跨越 0，学习式置信模型（AURC 0.436）输给了它想取代的手工启发式（0.164），且测试集在运行前已被一份"污染词表"暴露过。

> **TL;DR:** This paper reframes analytics-agent evaluation from SQL execution match to "Business Truth Rate" (BTR), introducing WarehouseReliabilityBench (WRB) — 400 frozen tasks over two synthetic DuckDB warehouses where roughly half of correct responses are a clarification, abstention, or refusal. QueryProof, a 7B Qwen2.5-Coder agent that decides behaviour by deterministic rules over a semantic layer + physical catalog and gates every answer on post-execution checks, hits BTR 0.537 (routed) / 0.562 (unrouted) on the 80-task test split versus 0.300 for a direct-prompted 32B baseline (+0.237 [+0.112, +0.375] paired) and 0.275 for a cost-matched 7B few-shot baseline (+0.262 [+0.137, +0.400]), while cutting False Success Rate from 0.754 to 0.351 and cost per correct answer by 71.0% ($0.0017 vs $0.0058). The paper is unusually self-critical: a family-level bootstrap widens both intervals to include zero, the routing layer *hurts* on test (0.537 vs 0.562 unrouted), the fitted confidence model loses to the heuristic it replaced (AURC 0.436 vs 0.164), and a contaminated synonym lexicon had matched the entire 16-task test ambiguous stratum before being removed. Read it if you care about abstention-aware evaluation of LLM data agents or about how to write an honest single-author empirical paper; skip it if you want SOTA on Spider/BIRD (none is claimed and no external benchmark is run).

## 核心贡献

- **WarehouseReliabilityBench (WRB v0.2.0)**：400 个冻结任务，覆盖电商与 SaaS 两个由固定种子确定性生成的合成数仓，分为 standard / ambiguous / unanswerable / schema-drift / adversarial 五类。其中仅 184/400 可答并携带可执行的 accepted result，其余用"行为契约"打分——这正是 execution-match 类指标无法覆盖的部分。切分 240/80/80，并且**按模板族互斥切分**，测试题的改写句不会出现在开发集。
- **以可靠性为先的评测框架**：Business Truth Rate（分母=全部任务）、False Success Rate（分母=被标记为 ANSWER 的输出）、coverage 与 answer accuracy 分离、ECE、cost per correct answer（分母=可答任务上的正确返回答案）。每个分母都被显式钉死，附录 E 给出公式；配对 bootstrap 与"允许使用哪个动词"的规则在任何测试数字产生之前就预注册了。
- **执行落地的确定性校验层**：一台状态机先于模型调用判定拒绝，用词表最长匹配在语义层的 metric 定义上判定歧义（"revenue"匹配两个 metric 即触发澄清），AST 白名单静态校验（禁 DML/DDL、未知表列、废弃列、被禁 join），只读连接执行，然后执行后校验（过滤条件是否真的应用、时间窗是否应用、结果是否非空、是否含不可能值）。这一层的**两个方向的错误都被测量**：140 条 golden SQL 上 0 次误拒；但漏检率 dev 14/86=0.163、validation 8/24=0.333。
- **三个被如实报告的负结果**：路由层在验证集有用（0.738 vs 0.725）但在测试集因过度弃答而变差（0.537 vs 0.562）；拟合的 logistic 置信模型在同一批 37 条答案上输给手工启发式；后训练被一条预注册的 gate 拒绝执行（可用训练样本池 20 条，阈值 100 条）。
- **可复现工程**：所有六个系统的原始输出入库，`./scripts/reproduce.sh` 一条命令、零模型调用即可重放打分/自助法/表格/图；论文中每个数字都是从 `results.json` 生成的宏而非手打，图表由 `make -C paper all` 构建，26 个输入各带内容哈希。

## Key Contributions

- **A benchmark whose correct answer is often "no answer."** WRB is explicitly designed so that no exact-match or execution-match metric can score roughly half its tasks. Ambiguity, unanswerability and schema drift are *designed in* from a pinned seed rather than hoped for in scraped data.
- **Metric definitions with pinned denominators.** The paper documents six scoring-harness defects found over the project's life (Table 5), three of which were denominator errors (FSR over all tasks instead of over ANSWER outputs; CPCA computed as cost-per-success, which flatters a system that earns its score by declining; ECE pooled over all tasks instead of returned answers). Two moved reported numbers and forced the v0.2.0 bump.
- **"The model proposes, rules dispose."** Table 6 assigns each decision to a rule or to the model: safety, period, warehouse coverage, dimension-value existence and "does this term have one definition" are all rule-decided; the model is asked only two narrow, re-validated questions (which candidate metric is meant, what SQL computes it) plus "what is missing from the schema," which is re-checked and discarded if hallucinated.
- **A pre-registered one-run protocol.** The metric list, table/figure set, the two permitted paired comparisons, the claim-verb rule, and content hashes of every frozen artifact were fixed before any test-split model call. The test split was evaluated exactly once and is declared spent; unresolved differences are left unresolved rather than re-measured with a better threshold.
- **Adversarial disclosure of the paper's own contamination.** Five hand-authored synonym-lexicon phrases occurred zero times in dev/validation questions and zero times in the semantic layer, yet matched 16 test tasks — the entire test ambiguous stratum. They were removed before any test-split model call (dev/validation lexicon output bit-identical), a regression test now guards it, and the author still refuses to call the split clean.

## Methodology

**System.** Six systems, all built on the Qwen2.5-Coder family (7B and 32B), all quantised and run locally on one machine at temperature zero against the same pinned DuckDB warehouses over read-only connections. Four baselines: 7B direct, 7B few-shot (the cost-comparable reference), 7B with retrieval over the semantic layer, and 32B direct (the larger-model reference). Two treatments: QueryProof with and without the routing layer, both over the same 7B model.

**QueryProof's pipeline** (Figure 7): PARSE → SEMANTIC_LOOKUP → RESOLVE → AMBIGUITY_CHECK → DRAFT_SQL → AST_VALIDATE → EXECUTE → VERIFY → (REPAIR, ≤2 rounds) → SCORE → ROUTE → {RETURN, ESCALATE, ABSTAIN, CLARIFY, REFUSE}. Refusal is checked *before* the model is called, so an injection payload never reaches it. Abstention fires on rule conditions: requested period outside warehouse coverage, filter literal absent from a closed dimension domain, requested concept with no metric/dimension/column. Clarification fires when a phrase matches ≥2 defined metrics without disambiguation, or when it matches only concepts the semantic layer leaves undefined. Notably, a conditional state that lets the model pick between candidate metrics **exists and is off by default on evidence**: on dev it recovered a few over-clarifications but produced 4× as many false successes on genuinely ambiguous questions ("asked to pick, a 7B picks").

**Confidence and routing.** The first attempt — a hand-weighted heuristic over the state machine's own observations — could not rank, for a structural reason worth noting: by the time a candidate is eligible to return it has already passed static validation and post-execution verification, so the heuristic's signals are nearly always identical and isotonic regression collapsed them to a constant. The shipped replacement is an L2-regularised logistic regression over four inference-time features (join count, SQL length, repair attempts, verification-issue count), fitted on dev, threshold selected on validation, generalisation estimated leave-one-family-out (a row-wise estimate is ~5× more optimistic). The verification-issue feature carries zero weight because it never varies among returned answers.

**Cost model.** Local generation billed by generation time at an amortised ~$0.50/hour hardware rate; execution billed per CPU-second for exactly one execution of the returned SQL *whether or not the system executed it itself* — otherwise baselines that never execute their own SQL would be billed nothing. Escalated answers bill both model calls. Cost is measured once, serially, quantised to 1e-6 USD and sealed into the run record, because re-measuring wall-clock drifted reported cost by ~6e-6 USD with evaluator thread contention.

**Statistics.** Paired percentile bootstrap, 2000 resamples, seed 9901, over per-task outcomes; the pre-registered rule permits the verb "outperforms" only when the interval excludes zero. CPCA is a ratio of sums, so it cannot be bootstrapped as a mean; the paired test resamples numerator and denominator over the same task indices and reports usable-resample counts.

## Results & Highlights

- **Business Truth Rate, test split (80 tasks):** QueryProof routed 0.537 [0.425, 0.638] and unrouted 0.562 [0.450, 0.675], versus **32B direct 0.300** [0.200, 0.400], 7B retrieval 0.300, 7B few-shot 0.275 [0.175, 0.375], 7B direct 0.100 [0.037, 0.163]. Paired difference vs 32B: **+0.237 [+0.112, +0.375]** (27 wins / 8 losses / 45 ties); vs few-shot: **+0.262 [+0.137, +0.400]** (28/7/45).
- **False Success Rate (over returned answers) falls from 0.754 (32B) to 0.351 (QueryProof routed)** — a 13/37 numerator against 49/65. Few-shot is 0.797, retrieval 0.806, and 7B direct is a perfect 1.000 (it returns 72 answers, none correct). Per-task false-success share drops −0.450 [−0.575, −0.337] vs 32B and −0.525 [−0.650, −0.400] vs few-shot, both resolved.
- **Coverage/accuracy decomposition:** QueryProof answers 0.600 of tasks and is correct on **1.000** of the answerable questions it chose to answer; every baseline answers 0.900–1.000 of tasks and is correct on 0.325–0.444. Table 7 makes it concrete: QueryProof returns **0 wrong numbers on answerable tasks (0 of 24)**, while 32B returns 4 and few-shot returns 13.
- **Cost per correct answer resolves in exactly one direction.** vs 32B: $0.0017 vs $0.0058, **−71.0%**, −0.00414 [−0.00752, −0.00250], resolved. vs few-shot: only −6.5%, −0.00012 [−0.00142, +0.00078], **not resolved** — and on validation the same comparison ran the *other* way with the routed agent 155.1% more expensive and resolved. The paper also states plainly that QueryProof costs *more per task* than few-shot: +0.00020 [+0.00007, +0.00037], resolved.
- **The gain lives entirely in the non-answer strata (Table 4).** On the 32 standard questions QueryProof scores 0.500 against 32B's **0.500 — exact parity**. On 16 unanswerable questions: 0.438 vs 0.000 for every baseline. On 8 schema-drift questions: 1.000 vs 0.000. On 8 adversarial questions every system scores 1.000 with 0 prohibited executions, so that stratum ranks nothing. Ambiguous: 0.250 vs 0.000 (retrieval 0.188). "A benchmark of standard questions alone would have reported near-parity."
- **Cluster robustness kills the magnitude claim.** Resampling the 10 template families instead of the 80 tasks leaves the point estimates unchanged but widens the intervals to **+0.237 [−0.125, +0.562]** vs 32B and **+0.263 [−0.075, +0.575]** vs few-shot — both include zero (Table 8).
- **Negative result 1 — routing does not transfer.** Validation 0.738 (routed) vs 0.725 (base); test **0.537 vs 0.562**. Coverage falls 0.700→0.600 while answer accuracy rises 0.929→1.000: the routed config withheld answers that were correct. Not re-tuned, because that would require a second evaluation of a spent split.
- **Negative result 2 — the fitted confidence model loses to the heuristic it replaced**, on the same 37 returned answers: **AURC 0.436 vs 0.164**, ECE 0.337 vs 0.189, Brier 0.336 vs 0.253. Cause is diagnosed: it was fitted on dev answers to *answerable* questions only, so it never learned to doubt an answer given where a clarification was required.
- **Negative result 3 — validation was optimistic.** Five of six systems drop from validation to test: routed agent −0.200, 32B −0.175, base −0.162, few-shot −0.037, 7B direct −0.062; only 7B retrieval rises (+0.050). The drop was predicted in writing before the run.
- **Verification layer measured in both directions:** 0 false rejections over 140 golden SQL programs (observed 0.000, explicitly not a soundness proof), but false-clean rates of **0.163 on dev (14/86)** and **0.333 on validation (8/24)** — passing the checks raises the probability an answer is right without establishing it.
- **Label reliability:** blind re-adjudication of 50 tasks gives raw agreement 0.920, Cohen's κ 0.887 — but it is test–retest by the same person, and all four disagreements fell in one template family (ambiguous, 11/15 agreed), so the effective independent sample is well below 50.
- **Post-training gate (Appendix D):** dev failures 33 → 28 after the deterministic defect sweep, agent defects 21 → 8, model-capability pool 12 → **20 against a threshold of 100**. Reported as untested-by-design, not as a negative result. One striking finding: running each answerable task's own golden SQL through the agent's static validator rejected two template families because an invalid-join rule read "no direct key between these tables" as "these tables may never co-occur."

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Morris Lee, listed as an Independent Researcher with no institutional affiliation, and a public repository at `github.com/k-w-lee/query_proof`. There is no track record signalled in the paper and no co-author check on any design decision — which the author flags himself as a validity threat ("One person designed the warehouses, the task taxonomy, the semantic layer, the deterministic rules, the labels and the evaluation harness; co-design of that kind can favour the system in ways no single check detects"). Credibility here rests on process rather than pedigree, and the process is unusually strong for a solo arXiv preprint: pre-registered protocol with artifact hashes, a one-run rule on the test split, a full changelog of scoring-harness corrections including four made *after* results had been reported internally, a self-disclosed contamination incident that a less scrupulous author would simply have deleted, and a one-command replay path with all six systems' raw outputs committed. The related-work coverage (Spider, BIRD, Spider 2.0, TrustSQL, AmbiQT, test-suite accuracy, selective prediction, dbt/LookML/Cube/Malloy semantic layers) is competent and correctly positions the paper against TrustSQL as the nearest prior.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** The individual pieces are all standard and the paper says so explicitly: "No new decoding algorithm or training method is claimed." Rule-based ambiguity detection over a semantic layer, AST allowlists, post-execution verification, and abstention-aware scoring all exist. TrustSQL already penalises confidently wrong answers. The genuine novelty is the *combination plus the measurement discipline*: making the semantic layer the arbiter of what a business term means, which converts ambiguity detection from a model judgement into a lexicon lookup, and building a benchmark where half the tasks have no reference SQL at all.

**Rigor (high on process, weak on evidence base).** The pre-registration, pinned denominators, sealed cost measurements and byte-identical rescoring are better than most industrial-lab papers. But the evidence itself is thin in ways the author does not hide: 80 test tasks from 10 template families; the family-level bootstrap swallows both headline intervals; and the **primary confound is unresolved — the 32B baseline is prompted directly and receives none of the semantic layer, verification, repair or catalog access**. This is a system-vs-system comparison dressed in a title that names parameter counts ("A Rule-Gated 7B Agent Outperforms a Direct-Prompted 32B Baseline"). The title is technically accurate but invites exactly the size-vs-scaffolding reading the Limitations section forbids. The obvious missing experiment — the same scaffold over the 32B model — is named as future work but not run.

**Specific weaknesses worth naming.** (1) *The 7B direct baseline is a strawman*: BTR 0.100, FSR 1.000, zero correct answers, CPCA undefined. It contributes nothing except to make the plot look dramatic. (2) *No component ablation.* The paper's central thesis is "the gain comes from the deterministic layer," yet the only ablation run is routing on/off; static validation, post-execution verification, the ambiguity lexicon and the abstention rules are never independently removed. The Conclusion concedes this ("Attributing the gain to those components specifically would need a component-wise ablation, which this paper does not report") — an admirable admission that nonetheless leaves the headline claim supported by stratum-level correlation rather than by ablation. (3) *The test ambiguous stratum never exercises the agent's primary mechanism.* Its 16 tasks come from two e-commerce families that both test the *undefined-concept* path; every "two defined metrics" family landed in dev/validation. So the mechanism the paper builds its ambiguity story around — "revenue matches two metrics, therefore clarify" — is never evaluated on held-out data, and its test ambiguous score is a mediocre 0.250. (4) *Contamination.* Five lexicon phrases matched exactly the 16-task test ambiguous stratum and could only have been written from the held-out split. Removal restores nothing about the development decisions taken with that knowledge; the author says so and readers should take him at his word. (5) *Single-reviewer labels* with test–retest, not inter-annotator, agreement. (6) *Adversarial stratum is a non-result*: 8 tasks, all six systems at 1.000.

**Reproducibility (excellent).** Public repo, pinned seeds, committed raw outputs from all six systems, `reproduce.sh` with no model calls, per-artifact content hashes in `results.json`, run commit `3fa9b5d` and analysis commit `7116b07` stamped into every figure footer, and prose numbers generated as macros so a figure cannot disagree with the table beside it. The author correctly distinguishes *replay* reproducibility (guaranteed) from *generation* reproducibility (not guaranteed — needs same quantised weights, backend and hardware).

**Significance (narrow but real).** For practitioners shipping analytics agents, the practical takeaway is concrete and cheap: build the deterministic layer first, measure its false-clean rate, and do not fit a confidence model on the subpopulation that layer already admits. Whether any of this transfers to a real warehouse with real schema debt, a different model family, a different SQL dialect, or Spider/BIRD is entirely untested.

**Red flags.** Mild overclaiming lives in the title rather than the body — the abstract and Limitations both immediately disclaim the size comparison. The genuine risk is that the abstract's "+0.237 at 71.0% lower cost" travels while "family bootstrap includes zero" and "no component ablation was run" do not. Also note the cost model is an amortised local hardware rate, not a market price, so only *ratios* between systems mean anything — a 71% CPCA reduction against a locally-quantised 32B says little about a hosted-API deployment.

## Strengths

- Frames the right problem: the failure mode that reaches a dashboard undetected is the query that runs and returns a wrong business number, and no execution-match metric can score it.
- Pre-registration with a claim-verb rule fixed before the numbers existed, and visible discipline in honouring it (routing vs base is *not* claimed as a difference because that pair was not pre-registered and the intervals overlap).
- Both error directions of the verification layer are measured rather than asserted — including the embarrassing 0.333 false-clean rate on validation.
- Three negative results are promoted into the body rather than buried in Limitations, and the confidence-model failure comes with a mechanistic explanation (fitted only on answerable-question answers) that is a genuine caution for the selective-prediction literature.
- The self-disclosed lexicon contamination, with the predicted consequence written down before the run and compared against the outcome, is exemplary scientific conduct.
- Metric definitions with explicit denominators (Appendix E), plus two explicitly-named near-misses (per-task false-success share ≠ FSR; cost-per-success ≠ CPCA) that many papers conflate silently.
- Table 7 gives every test task in one mutually exclusive outcome bucket for all six systems — the residual failures are auditable, not summarised away.

## Limitations

- 80 test tasks, 10 template families, two synthetic domains from a single seed, one model family, one SQL dialect, no external benchmark. Family-level resampling puts zero inside both accuracy intervals.
- Headline comparison confounds scale with scaffolding; the matched-scaffold 32B run is not reported.
- No component ablation, so "the gain tracks the deterministic layer" is inference from stratum breakdowns, not from removals.
- Test split had disclosed prior exposure through a contaminated synonym lexicon covering the entire test ambiguous stratum.
- The routing threshold over-abstains on test and was not re-tuned (one-run rule), so the shipped operating point is "an illustration rather than a recommendation."
- Ambiguous tasks count as correct when the clarification contains a distinguishing token — checkable, but no human study establishes that an analyst could act on it.
- Result equivalence orders columns by content and excludes headers, so a result with right values under wrong column roles can pass when the column count matches.
- Temperature-zero decoding everywhere removes within-configuration sampling variance and says nothing about robustness across seeds or decoding schemes.
- Labels rest on one reviewer; the 0.920/κ=0.887 figure is test–retest, and all disagreements clustered in one family.
- Post-training remains untested by design (pool of 20 against a gate of 100), so the paper cannot say whether execution-verified traces would help.

## Relevance

如果你关注 LLM agent 的可靠性评测、弃答/校准（selective prediction）、或把 LLM 接入生产数据栈（semantic layer / dbt / text-to-SQL）的工程实践，这篇值得精读：它给出了一个"正确答案往往不是答案"的基准设计范式，以及"确定性校验层优先、学习式置信度后置"的可迁移工程结论。此外，它本身是一份关于预注册、单次运行规则、污染披露与可复现构建的优秀方法论样本，对写实证类论文（尤其是 agent/benchmark 方向）有直接借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- The decisive missing experiment: same scaffold over the 32B model. Does the +0.237 survive when the baseline gets the semantic layer, catalog and verification? The author names this as future work — how much of the gap is information asymmetry rather than reasoning?
- Component-wise ablation: what happens to BTR if you keep the rule-based behaviour decision but drop post-execution verification, or vice versa? The 0.163/0.333 false-clean rates suggest verification's marginal contribution may be smaller than the abstention rules'.
- Does the ambiguity-by-lexicon mechanism survive a real semantic layer with hundreds of metrics, overlapping synonyms and stale definitions? The two-defined-metrics path is literally never tested on held-out data here.
- The confidence-model failure diagnosis implies a fix: fit on the *full* behaviour distribution (including cases where clarification was required) rather than on answerable-question answers only. Would that beat the hand-weighted heuristic, or is the post-verification subpopulation just structurally unrankable?
- How does maintenance cost enter the ledger? The deterministic layer needs no retraining when a metric definition changes, but the semantic layer and rule set must be maintained — and the golden-SQL-rejects-two-families incident shows the rules themselves carry defects.
- Would a schema-drift stratum built from real warehouse migration logs (rather than designed-in deprecations) still yield 1.000 vs 0.000, or is the 8-task drift result an artifact of synthetic drift being unambiguously detectable from the catalog?
- Can BTR/FSR be ported to Spider 2.0's enterprise workflows, where reference queries exist but ambiguity and unanswerability are not labelled? What is the minimum annotation needed to add a behaviour contract to an existing text-to-SQL suite?
