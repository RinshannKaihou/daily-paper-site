Methodology

distilled · ~150 words

Motif 3 is a 53-layer hybrid-attention MoE (2 dense + 51 MoE; 384 routed + 1 shared expert per layer, top-8) at 314B total / 13.2B active. Its GDLA attention fuses Differential Transformer's signal/noise subtraction with MLA's low-rank KV compression: a single KV latent serves both paths, with a token-dependent sigmoid-bounded differential coefficient and a query-latent output gate. The 1-full / 3-sliding-window (W=128) hybrid routes long context through per-layer parallelism — Ulysses for full-attention layers, a halo Ring for window layers. Pretraining covers 12.5T tokens (~70% retained NVIDIA Nemotron public data) under MXFP8 expert GEMM, Parallel Muon, and asymmetric QK-Clip. Post-training consolidates seven teachers (6 GRPO specialists + 1 SFT software-engineering) into one student via Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD), using only sampled-token teacher log-probs with ICE-POP importance weighting.

Strengths & limitations

side-by-side

+ Strengths

  • Actionable stability disclosure — real thresholds: dispatch min/median >0.7 healthy vs <0.3 starving; expert-output cosine <0.5 healthy vs >0.8 redundant.
  • Honest negative reporting — the FFN penalty is admitted as suppressing spikes without stopping global RMS drift; comms volume is explicitly said to be ≠ latency.
  • Systems section is the strongest part — routing-weight folding, 22/51 layers expert-resident, checkpoint-hook-driven optimizer offload: concrete & reusable.
  • Abstention-aware reward ladder (1 / 0.666 / 0.333 / 0, zero for truncated) prevents abstention-farming — the 71.6 non-hallucination vs DeepSeek-V4-Pro's 5.9 shows teeth.

— Limitations

  • No MOPD ablation — the seven-teacher decomposition and frozen-router/MTP choices all rest on one aggregate table.
  • All architecture evidence is ~10B-scale, using training-loss or spectral proxies — never downstream task accuracy at that scale, let alone 314B.
  • Comparison hygiene is problematic — leaderboard-copied baselines, undisclosed scaffolds on scaffold-sensitive agentic benchmarks, public-subset-vs-full-set on ITBench-AA.
  • Specialist, not generalist — SciCode 40.6, CritPt 6.6, GPQA 83.4, AA-Omniscience 30.1 all lag frontier by 7–13 points.
  • Manual mid-run router replacement on collapsed layers makes the recipe non-hands-off; the 256K context investment doesn't visibly convert into leading long-context reasoning (AA-LCR 72.3).

Open questions for further exploration

How much of Table 6 comes from MOPD versus the general SFT checkpoint alone? Without that delta the seven-teacher cost-benefit is unknowable.
Does GDLA's 9.2%-fewer-tokens edge over MLA persist at 314B, or is it a 10B-scale artifact as noise suppression matters less with capacity?
Why is SciCode (40.6) so much weaker than SWE-bench Verified (76.2) when a dedicated code-and-science teacher exists — is it diluted during MOPD?
Does the piecewise length reward (peak 1.5 at 512–1024 tokens) inflate verbosity on non-chat tasks, and does IFBench (78.2) partly reflect that?
Is the ITBench-AA public subset systematically easier? Publishing the full-set number settles whether +9.0 over Qwen-3.7 Max survives.
What is the actual end-to-end MFU & inference throughput given the Sinkhorn iterations and per-expert PolyNorm add FP32 work per layer?
