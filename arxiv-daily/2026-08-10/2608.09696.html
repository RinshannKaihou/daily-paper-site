01 Methodology — distilled

The world is a controlled, intervenable state-space model. An experiment design ξ=(ι,a,x) splits into inputs (ι,x) to a fixed mechanism and an intervention do(a) that alters the mechanism's parameters θ itself — Pearl level-2 causality; counterfactuals are explicitly out of scope.

The outer loop is a sequential Bayesian design loop. Inference (Alg.2) keeps Nm LLM-proposed structure particles, scoring each by marginal likelihood log Ẑ from a per-structure adaptive-tempered SMC over Np=200 parameter particles (Alg.3, random-walk Metropolis rejuvenation), forming p(m|D)=softmax(log Ẑ). Marginalizing parameters supplies the automatic Occam factor behind the accuracy–complexity Pareto frontier.

Design maximises the Lindley objective I(M;Yξ|D); small menus are enumerated, the 7-D ChemBench box uses CMA-ES (48 evals). Likelihoods come in three flavours: exact Gaussian (deterministic), a bootstrap particle filter (Alg.4, stochastic latents), and a synthetic likelihood on learned summary statistics when traces are too noisy. An M-open check on held-out residuals re-prompts the LLM to expand the hypothesis space; an ESS-adaptive rule evicts near-duplicate structures.

LLM runs at temp 0.2–0.4, JSON mode + caching; priors & SMC settings tabulated (Table 2); prompts in Appendix G.

04 Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Design-menu control (Opus + MDA's menu = 22%, worse than free choice) directly kills the most obvious confound.
  • Un-throttled reproduction of DiscoverPhysics's nMSE≈0.01 validates the whole pipeline against the original paper.
  • Intellectual honesty: concedes the Fable-5 tie/lead and names a fix (parsimony rule, 74%→78%).
  • Particle-filter spot-check is a reusable safety pattern — detects when the cheap summary statistic is insufficient and falls back to the assumption-free filter.
  • Yukawa Pareto-frontier narrative gives a crisp, falsifiable illustration of why design and discovery couple.

Limitations

  • All evaluation is on synthetic simulators with known ground truth — no wet-lab, instrument, or real observational data.
  • No compute/token/wall-clock accounting; MDA makes up to ~112 LLM calls per run vs ~8 for the baseline agent it beats on experiment count.
  • Thin run counts — 9 runs/world physics, 2 seeds chemistry, 3 ICs biology — with ±1 SE bars, not CIs.
  • Advantage over Fable 5 is confined to the numeric axis; the unaided agent leads on structure recovery.
  • ChemBench head-to-head uses a different task subset and a partially upgraded baseline — not a like-for-like reproduction.
  • VoI's benefit is domain-dependent and near-zero on stochastic neurons; the "design" half is carried mainly by physics.

05 Open Questions

  1. What is the real token/dollar cost of MDA vs the LLM agent per unit of held-out accuracy — does it still win when experiments are cheap and inference is not?
  2. Does the M-open trigger recover the truth when the proposer genuinely cannot reach it (Z-REBOUND is flagged but not isolated)?
  3. The thresholds τr and τp are hand-tuned per domain — how sensitive is performance, and can they be set adaptively from the noise floor?
  4. Is there a base-model capability level at which the Bayesian layer stops paying for itself on the predictive metric?
  5. Would VoI still beat the LLM's own design in a genuinely large, unstructured design space — not a curated 13-launch menu?
  6. Can the learned summary sφ be trained jointly across an expanding hypothesis pool, avoiding Algorithm 2's refits?
