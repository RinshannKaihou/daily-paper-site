> **一句话总结：** 提出 TCS-Bench——一个从 STOC/FOCS/SODA 论文中抽取 300 个自包含定理证明任务、通过依赖图掩码控制难度的基准；用 4 次 Gemini 3.1 Flash 多数表决验证器（人类标注集准确率 >90%）评测前沿模型，GPT 5.6 Pro 取得 68% 的最高分，且跨模型批评选择可使 Colosseum 智能体达到 67.7%。

> **TL;DR:** TCS-Bench is the first benchmark for research-level Theoretical Computer Science proof generation, built from 300 self-contained theorem-proving tasks scraped from STOC/FOCS/SODA papers (2020–2026) with difficulty scaled by hiding intermediate lemmas in a dependency DAG. Evaluated with a calibrated verifier (>90% accuracy on 100 human-labeled proofs), GPT 5.6 Pro tops the leaderboard at 68% accuracy on TCS-Bench versus 30.3% for Gemini 3.1 Pro (a +37.7-point gap), and a cross-model Colosseum selection harness reaches 67.7% (oracle upper bound 72.3%).

## 核心贡献

- **首个研究级 TCS 证明基准**：从 STOC、FOCS、SODA（2020–2026）论文中构造 300 个自包含的定理证明任务，填补了竞赛数学（如 IMO）与真实科研之间的空白——任务要求模型在论文自定义的定义、记号与前置引理构成的依赖语境中进行推理，而非解孤立题目。
- **基于依赖图的难度可控机制**：用确定性 LaTeX 解析器 + LLM 分析构建论文的形式陈述有向无环图（DAG），按最长依赖路径定义"秩"；通过掩码不同层级的中间引理，可从"提供全部依赖"逐步加难到"证明论文主定理"，实现难度谱的连续扩展。
- **可扩展的自动化证明验证器**：验证器对每个候选证明做 4 次 Gemini 3.1 Flash 调用，取 ≥3/4 多数判定为正确；在 GEPA 提示优化流水线上用 50 正/50 负人工标注校准，参考验证器在 100 条人类专家标注集上准确率 >90%，使大规模评测无需昂贵人工。
- **严格的自包含性与质量过滤**：任务经结构检查（图/表引用、标签泄漏、10K token 上限、依赖覆盖、悬空引用）与语义检查（良定义性、唯一性与正确性、上下文连贯性）双重把关；仅采用 CC-0/CC-BY-4.0 许可的 arXiv LaTeX 源，保证法律可分发。
- **前沿模型系统评测与跨模型洞察**：评测 Gemini 3.1 Pro、Gemini 3.1 DeepThink、Opus 5、GPT 5.6 Pro 及 Colosseum 智能体壳；发现"自我拒绝"比"自我接受"更具信息量，且跨模型批评具方向性不对称（Gemini 3.6 Flash 评判 Gemini 3.1 Pro 的 AUC 为 0.854，反向仅 0.637）。

## Key Contributions

- **First research-level TCS benchmark.** TCS-Bench provides 300 self-contained theorem-proving tasks extracted from STOC/FOCS/SODA papers (2020–2026). Unlike competition-math benchmarks, tasks require reasoning within a paper's bespoke definitions, notation, and chains of prior lemmas, closing the gap between IMO-style problems and genuine research.
- **Dependency-DAG difficulty scaling.** A deterministic LaTeX parser plus an LLM analysis pass builds a directed acyclic graph over all formal statements; statement rank is the longest dependency path. Masking intermediate lemmas of increasing rank yields a continuous spectrum from "all dependencies provided" up to "prove the paper's main theorem."
- **Calibrated automated verifier.** The verifier makes 4 calls to Gemini 3.1 Flash and accepts a proof iff at least 3/4 verdicts are correct, tolerating stylistic omissions but rejecting logical gaps. Calibrated via the GEPA prompt-evolution pipeline on 50 correct + 50 incorrect human-labeled proofs, it exceeds 90% accuracy on the 100-proof human-expert set.
- **Self-contained, legally distributable tasks.** Only CC-0/CC-BY-4.0 arXiv LaTeX sources are used; tasks are pruned (iterative section removal + LLM shortener) to fit a 10,000-token context window and pass both structural checks (figure refs, label leakage, token limit, dependency coverage, dangling refs) and semantic checks (well-definedness, unambiguous correctness, context coherence).
- **Systematic frontier-model evaluation plus cross-model insight.** The authors benchmark Gemini 3.1 Pro, Gemini 3.1 DeepThink, Opus 5, GPT 5.6 Pro, and the Colosseum agentic harness, and show that self-rejection is far more informative than self-acceptance and that cross-model critique is strongly directional (AUC 0.854 one way vs. 0.637 the other).

## Methodology

TCS-Bench is constructed in four stages. (1) **Data acquisition**: LaTeX sources are pulled from arXiv for FOCS/STOC/SODA papers (2020–2026) under permissive licenses (CC-0/CC-BY-4.0); statements whose proofs invoke unstated external results are skipped. (2) **Dependency-structure construction**: a deterministic LaTeX parser identifies all theorem-like environments and assigns unique IDs, then an LLM pass maps proof environments to the statements they prove and builds dependency edges, forming a DAG. Acyclicity is programmatically verified (cycles trigger rejection as extraction errors). Each statement's rank = length of the longest directed path terminating at it (definitions/axioms have rank 0), enabling difficulty stratification and leakage prevention.

(3) **Task construction**: for a rank-r target statement, the context is assembled from condensed external-reference digests plus the paper text truncated at the start of the statement's proof, with all rank-≥r statements hidden and all proof environments removed; missing dependencies are re-inserted. Scalable difficulty is created by additionally withholding intermediate lemmas. Context is compressed to ≤10,000 tokens via iterative section pruning (LLM flags irrelevant sections) followed by an LLM "shortener" that condenses verbose passages while preserving mathematical content. (4) **Quality filtering** rejects tasks with figure/image references, label leakage, oversized contexts, uncovered dependencies, or unresolved refs (structural checks), and uses three independent LLM calls to verify well-definedness, unambiguous correctness, and context coherence (semantic checks).

The **verifier** takes the task context, target statement, candidate proof, and the ground-truth proof, issues 4 Gemini 3.1 Flash calls, and accepts the proof iff ≥3/4 mark it correct. Its prompt was tuned with the GEPA reflective-evolution pipeline on 50 correct + 50 incorrect human-labeled proofs to reach >90% accuracy. Each released task gives the solver a self-contained LaTeX context (≤10,000 tokens) and a target statement; the solver must output a single complete LaTeX proof using only context results, with the ground-truth withheld for evaluation.

## Results & Highlights

- **Overall leaderboard (Table 1, TCS-Bench, 300 tasks).** GPT 5.6 Pro (max) is the strongest single model at **68% accuracy (204/300)**, versus **Gemini 3.1 Pro at 30.3%** — a **+37.7-point gap** — with Gemini 3.1 DeepThink at **52%** and Opus 5 at **32.77%**. Opus 5's low score is partly artifactual: it exhausts its 128K-token budget on **162 of 300 tasks** before producing an answer.
- **Verifier reliability.** The reference verifier achieves **>90% accuracy on the 100 human-expert-labeled proof set**, calibrated via GEPA on 50 correct + 50 incorrect proofs, enabling scalable grading without prohibitive manual review.
- **Colosseum agentic harness (Table 2, TCS-Bench).** Colosseum + Gemini 3.1 Pro scores **54.0% (162/300)** and Colosseum + Gemini 3.6 Flash scores **46.7% (140/300)**; **cross-model selection between the two reaches 67.7% (203/300)** — a **+13.7-point gain** over the stronger single arm — with an oracle best-of-two upper bound of **72.3% (217/300)**.
- **Self-rejection is informative, self-acceptance is not.** Routing purely on Gemini 3.1 Pro's self-rejection raises its accuracy from **54.0% to 63.7% (+9.7 points)**; among its self-accepted proofs, **93.4% receive unanimous verifier acceptance**, leaving little discriminative signal in acceptance.
- **Asymmetric cross-model critique.** Gemini 3.6 Flash critiquing Gemini 3.1 Pro separates correct from incorrect proofs at **AUC 0.854**, whereas the reverse direction (Pro judging Flash) yields only **AUC 0.637**; performance is insensitive to the critique threshold (67.3%–67.7% across 0/8–5/8 cutoffs).
- **Per-arm accuracy after filtering.** The selection rule picks Gemini 3.1 Pro on 167 problems at **84% accuracy (vs. 54% before filtering)** and Gemini 3.6 Flash on 133 problems at **47% accuracy**.

The work is closely tied to a prior Google effort on accelerating scientific research with Gemini (ref [34], Woodruff et al. 2026), indicating an institutional program rather than a one-off study. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High and well-motivated. TCS-Bench is genuinely the first benchmark to target *research-level theoretical* proof replication (as opposed to competition math, formal tactic generation in Lean/Coq, or empirical paper replication à la PaperBench). The DAG-based dependency-masking mechanism for scalable difficulty is elegant.
- **Rigor:** Mixed. The benchmark-construction pipeline (deterministic parser + LLM + acyclicity checks + structural/semantic filters) is thorough, but the headline difficulty-scaling claim is **not quantitatively validated**: no table reports accuracy as a function of rank or number of masked lemmas, so "scalable difficulty" is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **Verifier circularity / family bias (red flag):** The verifier is built on **Gemini 3.1 Flash**, the same family as several solvers (Gemini 3.1 Pro, 3.1 DeepThink, 3.6 Flash). A same-family grader may systematically favor Gemini-style phrasings or proof structures, and the verifier is *given the ground-truth proof*, biasing it toward proofs that mimic the reference structure. This is a non-trivial conflict of interest for a benchmark whose leaderboard ranks Gemini models.
- **Calibration set ambiguity (data quirk):** Section 4.2 calibrates the prompt on 50 correct + 50 incorrect proofs, while the introduction reports ">90% accuracy on a held-out set of 100 human-labeled proofs." It is unclear whether the 100-proof "held-out" set is disjoint from the 100 used for GEPA calibration; if they overlap, the >90% figure is essentially in-sample, and a truly independent human evaluation is absent.
- **Selection rule tuned on the test set (mild overfitting):** The cross-model selection's direction (Flash→Pro) and threshold were "selected using cross-model critique accuracy measured on TCS-Bench" — i.e., the 67.7% number reflects hyperparameters chosen on the same 300 tasks it is reported on, with no held-out split.
- **Reproducibility:** Weak. All evaluated solvers are proprietary frontier models accessed via API/web with future-dated names (Gemini 3.1/3.6, GPT 5.6 Pro, Opus 5, DeepThink) and **no open-weight models** (no Llama/Qwen/DeepSeek/Mistral) are tested, so the community cannot independently reproduce the leaderboard. Colosseum's internal details are deliberately omitted.
- **Confound in ranking:** Opus 5's 32.77% conflates proof ability with token-budget/length handling (it ran out on 162/300 tasks), so the model ordering partly reflects output-length policy rather than pure reasoning.
- **Significance:** Still substantial. As a durable, license-clean, difficulty-stratified probe of long-chain mathematical reasoning, TCS-Bench is a meaningful contribution even given the caveats; the cross-model-critique finding is the most novel and reusable empirical insight.

## Strengths

- First benchmark bridging competition math and genuine research-level TCS proof work; clear and important gap in the literature.
- Dependency-DAG masking gives an elegant, principled mechanism for generating a continuous difficulty spectrum from a single paper.
- Strong quality-filtering pipeline (structural + semantic) and a strict self-containment contract (≤10,000 tokens, no external access) make tasks clean and shareable.
- Honest reporting: explicitly states the lack of human baselines and exposes the oracle ceiling (72.3%) to frame the cross-model gain realistically.
- Genuinely interesting and counter-intuitive empirical finding that self-rejection dominates self-acceptance as a filtering signal, and that cross-model critique is strongly directional — useful for any proof-search/agentic harness design.
- Designed for longevity via continual addition of post-cutoff papers and rank-based stratification, resisting contamination.

## Limitations

- Only **300 tasks** from three venues (STOC/FOCS/SODA); coverage of TCS is broad but not exhaustive (e.g., no complexity-theory-specific or distributed-computing-specific venues beyond what those conferences carry).
- **Proprietary-only models**, no open weights, so reproducibility for the broader community is limited; Colosseum internals are undisclosed.
- **Verifier–solver family overlap** and verifier access to the ground-truth proof create grading-bias risk.
- **No human baseline**, so it is unclear how 68% compares to a working TCS researcher on the same tasks.
- Difficulty scaling is described but **not quantitatively evaluated** (no accuracy-vs-rank or accuracy-vs-masked-lemmas table).
- The cross-model **selection rule is tuned on the test set**, slightly inflating the 67.7% result.
- TCS-only focus; generalization to pure mathematics, physics, or other formal domains is untested.

## Relevance

该论文属于 cs.CL/cs.AI，核心是评测大语言模型的研究级数学推理与证明生成能力，与用户对 LLM 推理、评测基准及智能体（agentic harness）方向的兴趣高度相关；其依赖图掩码控难度与跨模型批评选择的方法，对构建更难的推理基准和自我验证/自我纠错智能体具有直接的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does accuracy degrade as a function of target-statement rank or the number of masked intermediate lemmas? Is the "scalable difficulty" curve monotonic and well-separated across models?
- Would the leaderboard change if the verifier used an orthogonal model family (e.g., an open-weight model or a non-Google frontier model) instead of Gemini 3.1 Flash, to remove same-family grading bias?
- What is a human TCS-expert baseline on TCS-Bench, and how long does an expert take per task — i.e., is 68% near, at, or far below competent-researcher performance?
- How do open-weight reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-Math, Llama-based math variants) perform, and does the self-rejection-vs-self-acceptance asymmetry generalize across families?
- Does providing the verifier the ground-truth proof bias grading toward structurally mimicking proofs, and would a blind verifier (no ground truth) change which models win?
- Can the cross-model-critique selection rule, when re-tuned on a held-out split, retain its ~13.7-point gain, or is the 67.7% partly an artifact of test-set tuning?
- How contamination-resistant is TCS-Bench in practice as new papers keep entering the pool — do frontier models memorize proofs of highly cited recent theorems?
