Methodology

Two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. An LLM (Llama-3.3-70B) decomposes each query via the CREDENCE procedure into five typed sub-queries — appearance, action, objects, scene, short summary — plus a concatenated caption. Seven retrieval branches run over three backbones (VisMin-tuned CLIP, EVA02-CLIP, Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B), split into three anchors (full / concat / short captions) and four low-weight facet correctives (0.006–0.03). Per-query percentile calibration plus a fixed-denominator fusion prevents thinly-supported candidates from being over-scored; a reciprocal-rank agreement term adds soft consistency.

Stage 1 · Retrieval

Anchor branches gate the candidate pool (union ∩ anchors); facet branches only correct scores. Calibrated scores fuse into S_SCA, yielding the shared top-K=10 pool sent to all experts.

Stage 2 · Verify + Consensus

Three parallel, non-debating experts — LoRA Qwen3-VL-8B reranker, AnomalyLMM cloze, SSDC Detective→Analyst→Writer — update a shared prior. An uncertainty gate g(q) interpolates static late fusion ⇄ credibility-weighted log-opinion pool; g=0 recovers the baseline exactly.

Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths

  • Anchor / corrective split is a clean, well-motivated fix for facet-only false positives, backed by a dedicated ablation rather than asserted.
  • Calibration is thoughtful: percentile scaling + clipping bounds each expert; fixed-denominator fusion has a clear justification.
  • The gate strictly generalizes the baseline (g=0 recovers late fusion) — limits downside risk, a rare and honest engineering property.
  • Explicit, repeated validation-only hyperparameter selection; authors flag that correlated experts may agree on a wrong candidate.
  • Hyperparameter disclosure (model IDs, LoRA r=16, LRs, gate percentiles, branch weights) is far above the challenge-report norm.

– Limitations

  • No compute / latency / GPU-hour accounting, despite efficiency being the stated core motivation for the coarse-to-fine design.
  • Missing pairwise ablations — AnomalyLMM's solo gain over the Qwen3 reranker is just +0.037 mAP; its value inside the full system is never isolated.
  • Single-run, aggregate-only evaluation: no seeds, no error bars, no per-query analysis of what consensus actually fixed.
  • Gate thresholds calibrated on synthetic data then applied to real queries — Sim2Real calibration drift conceded but unmeasured.
  • No comparison to competing AI City 2026 Track 4 entries or published PAB SOTA; 95.41% sits only against the authors' own baselines.
  • Generalization beyond PAB untested — no CUHK-PEDES / ICFG-PEDES results.

Open Questions

Q1

What is the actual wall-clock / GPU cost per Stage-2 query, and how does mAP@10 vary with K ∈ {5, 10, 20}?

Q2

With only +0.23 mAP from consensus, what fraction of real test queries actually cross the g(q) > 0 threshold — and does it match the synthetic-validation 30th percentile?

Q3

Would a two-expert system (Qwen3 reranker + SSDC) with re-tuned fusion weights close most of the 3.4-point gap, making AnomalyLMM — and its extra Qwen3-14B + Qwen3-VL-8B forwards — droppable?

Q4

Does the anchor-constraint transfer to standard text-based person re-ID (CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES) where captions are less action-heavy, or is it PAB-specific?

Q5

Since verifiers boost R@1 but destroy R@5, could an explicitly rank-aware objective recover both better than credibility-weighted opinion pooling?

Q6

How much of the 8.92-point Stage-1 gain survives if facet decomposition uses a small open model instead of Llama-3.3-70B?
