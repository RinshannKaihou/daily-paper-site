A Tight Lower Bound for Smooth Nonconvex Stochastic Optimization with Bounded Gradient Noise

Jikai Jin · 2026-08-10 · arXiv:2608.09004

Provenance: single author, no affiliation listed. Abstract states the proof was independently generated with GPT-5.6 (Codex Ultra mode, ~2-hour session); human role limited to prompt, checking & polishing. No peer-review status indicated.
Key Finding

Bounded gradient noise yields zero speedup — nonconvex SGD stays pinned at the ε⁻⁴ minimax rate.

一句话总结：在梯度噪声几乎必然一致有界（半径 σ）的预言机模型下，任何随机自适应算法寻找 ε-稳定点的查询数下界为 Ω(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ²/ε⁴)，与 SGD 标准上界紧匹配 —— 更强的"有界噪声"假设并不能改善最坏情况复杂度。

Why This Matters

Caps how fast nonconvex SGD can ever be: gradient clipping or quantization (bounded noise) buys no worst-case speedup — faster rates require structural assumptions, not a tighter noise bound.

Critical Edge

The hard oracle is discontinuous in x and not a sample gradient (Remark 3.2) — so the bound does NOT cover the samplewise-smooth, finite-sum models modern ML actually uses.

Only the K=1 fresh-sample model is settled; K≥2 shared-sample (SPIDER ε⁻³) regime untouched. Constants extreme (c ≈ 10⁻¹⁶) — purely asymptotic. Machine-generated proof places the full verification burden on the reader.

How the proof is built

01Methodology

Construct a hard-instance family F_θ ∈ F_d(Δ,L) with a fixed-law bounded-noise oracle, then show no N-query randomized adaptive algorithm can drive E‖∇F(x̂_N)‖ below ε. Five steps:

  1. Scalar zero-chain (Carmon et al.) of length T ≍ ΔL/ε² — information passes only from coordinate j to j+1.
  2. Sign-vector encoding: hide each coordinate in a D-dim block via tanh of hidden signs θⱼ ∈ {±1}^D; a log cosh penalty blocks escape to saturation points.
  3. Bounded oracle: realize the next-sign contribution as the mean of ±σ/2 random signs → unbiased, ‖noise‖ < σ almost surely.
  4. Information control: each response leaks ≤ KDε²/σ² KL; a fixed-length comparison interaction + conditional KL chain rule ⇒ Ω(σ²/ε²) relevant responses per link.
  5. Sum over links: with N ≤ Tn₀/2, the chain completes w.p. ≤ 9/128 ⇒ E‖∇F‖ > 2ε w.p. ≥ 119/128.

The balance sheet

03Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Settles a stated open problem (Arjevani et al. 2023) with a tight, rate-matching answer.
  • Biased-sign encoding cleanly decouples "bounded noise" from "information leakage" — reusable for heavy-tail, finite-sum, and distributed oracle lower bounds.
  • Fully tracks constants and gives an explicit dimension formula (Eqs. 85–86), uncommon for minimax proofs.
  • Honest disclosure of AI generation and precise scope boundary (discontinuous oracle, K=1 only).

Limitations

  • Hard oracle is discontinuous and not a sample gradient — samplewise-smooth / finite-sum / conservative models uncovered.
  • Only K=1 fresh-sample; K≥2 shared-sample (SPIDER ε⁻³) regime untouched.
  • Constants extreme (c ≈ 10⁻¹⁶) — purely asymptotic, not a practical finite-precision bound.
  • Single author, AI-generated proof, no code, no peer review — verification falls on the reader.
  • No empirical validation (expected for theory, but practical tightness for finite-sum is unaddressed).

Open directions

04Questions for Further Exploration

  • Extend to samplewise-smooth oracles g(x,z)=∇f(x,z) — the model ML actually satisfies — or does the rate improve there?
  • Does the biased-sign encoding transfer to K≥2 shared-sample or the heavy-tailed (bounded p-th moment) setting of Fradin et al.?
  • Is there a matching algorithm (beyond generic SGD) that is minimax-optimal with constants near the lower bound?
  • How sensitive is the construction to oracle discontinuity? Would joint continuity or conservativity change the rate?
  • Which machine-generated steps — Section 9 constants, Lemma 7.1 measurability, Lemma 8.6 union bound — most need formal computer-assisted verification?
