Why this matters

A large-scale map of which rhetorical levers move AI-reviewer scores — and clear evidence that strict prompting won't close the vulnerability.

Critical edge

The reward-hack title oversells it — the paper's own body finds the effects are selective, configuration-dependent, and sometimes counterproductive.

Title vs. conclusion tension

Methodology

Controlled, content-preserving rewrites · blind multi-model review · within-paper paired analysis
① SEED
120 ICLR 2026 papers
OpenReview-sampled across 6 human-rating strata; arXiv LaTeX matched & anonymized
→
② REWRITE
4,200 full-paper variants
6 rhetorical dims × 2 directions, by GPT-5.5 & Opus 4.8; structural-anchor checks preserve content
→
③ REVIEW
42,396 blind reviews
5 LLM reviewers × 2 protocols; reviewers unaware of rewrite condition
  • Six prespecified dimensions — novelty stance, scope framing, evidence framing, contribution salience, technical register, linguistic complexity — each rewritten in two opposing directions.
  • Four workflows — single-dimension (2,880), joint (240), recursive joint over 3 rounds (480), reviewer-guided (480).
  • Content preservation enforced by a programmatic checker guarding citation keys, cross-refs, labels, URLs, graphics paths, math/code/algorithm envs, and the bib file — plus compile-and-repair; uncompilable outputs discarded.
  • Statistical design — paired (each variant vs. its prompt-matched original), stratified by human-OA and AI-original-OA; 5,000 paper-level bootstrap resamples for 95% percentile intervals; missing outcomes retained as missing.
  • Direct API cost $29,165.89 — a scale rarely seen in LLM-evaluation studies.

  • Starting score beats human quality as the driver of direction: positive evidence averages +1.05 OA for AI scores in [1,3] but −0.19 for [8,10].
  • Strict ≠ desensitized. Strictness lowers severity but leaves reviewers still responsive to content-preserving rhetoric (effect ρ only .081 vs. .862 for absolute OA).
  • More ≠ better. Reviewer guidance underperforms a plain second pass in all four averages; gains saturate after round 2.
Roles split: the rewriter shapes positive–negative contrast; the reviewer decides magnitude, sign, and which rubric dimension absorbs the change.
Strengths
  • Large-scale, controlled, content-preserving design isolates rhetoric from merit far more cleanly than observational studies.
  • Bidirectional rewriting per dimension separates directional preference from generic rewriting artifacts.
  • Honest reporting of null/heterogeneous results (GPT-5.5 near-zero; guidance ≠ repetition) — no cherry-picking.
  • Stratification by AI starting score reveals regression-to-the-mean — non-obvious and important.
  • LaTeX-level structural-anchor verification gives a credible content-preservation guarantee.
Limitations
  • Restricted to ICLR 2026 with recoverable full-paper source — generalization to other venues untested.
  • Six dimensions are non-orthogonal; effects are not independent coefficients for single features.
  • Most configs use a single AI review per manuscript — reviewer stochasticity not fully characterized.
  • Non-random missingness (safeguard-triggered review failures) could bias aggregates beyond the bootstrap.
  • Sparse [8,10] strata; ~$29k replication barrier; proprietary models are moving targets.

Questions for further exploration

Open threads the paper leaves behind
  1. Does the dimensional hierarchy (evidence > novelty > scope) replicate on non-ML venues or under different reviewer guidelines (ARR/ACL)?
  2. How much of the Opus 4.8 vs. GPT-5.5 gap is faithful content-preservation versus aggressive rhetoric strengthening?
  3. What is the within-reviewer OA variance for identical manuscripts — does averaging multiple reviews shrink or preserve the effects?
  4. Could a reviewer-side intervention (quoted-evidence per rubric score, abstract+figures-only scoring) beat the strict prompt at reducing sensitivity?
  5. Can a within-paper anchoring correction recover the intended directional signal at the score extremes?
  6. Are Sonnet 5's downward shifts and safeguard-triggered failures correlated with specific topics — and would their removal change the aggregates?
