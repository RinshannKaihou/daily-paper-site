> **一句话总结：** 该文用 Legendre 能量函数替换经典强凸/Lipschitz 光滑中的二次项 ‖·‖²₂，提出互为共轭对偶的 H(ψ)-凸性与 H(Ψ)-光滑性，据此定义"广义 GD/SGD"（更新方向为 ∇Ψ*(∇G)，最优学习率恒为 1），并把 DNN 训练重写为复合优化问题，证明经验风险被"梯度能量 / Jacobian 极端奇异值"上下夹逼；在 MNIST/CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/TREC/SST-2 上用 LeNet、ResNet-18、ViT、TextCNN 等验证风险与理论上下界的滑窗 Pearson 相关系数趋近 1，但广义 GD 本身只在 100 维合成函数 ‖x‖ʳ₂ 上做过实验，从未真正用于训练神经网络。

> **TL;DR:** Qi generalizes strong convexity and Lipschitz smoothness by replacing the quadratic energy ‖·‖²₂ with arbitrary Legendre "energy functions", yielding a conjugate-dual pair H(ψ)-convexity / H(Ψ)-smoothness that subsumes non-convex and non-smooth objectives, plus generalized GD/SGD whose optimal learning rate is provably exactly α = 1 (vs. the tuned 1/L of classical GD, which is recovered as the special case Ψ = L/2‖·‖²₂). DNN training is recast as composite optimization, giving two-sided bounds L(θ,z) ∈ [‖∇θL‖²₂/(2σ²max(Jθf)), ‖∇θL‖²₂/(2σ²min(Jθf))] for MSE (and an 8 ln2 / 4 min_i p_i variant for softmax CE). Empirically: on synthetic ‖x‖ʳ₂ (r ∈ [1.1, 2.0], 100-dim) α = 1 always beats α ∈ {0.1…1.8}, stagnates at α = 2 and diverges for α > 2; on MNIST/CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/TREC/SST-2 with LeNet, ResNet-18, ViT, TextCNN and 6+ loss functions the sliding-window (w=50) Pearson correlation between log risk and both bounds approaches 1, while a false-label control breaks it; final gradient energy separates capacity regimes (LeNet on CIFAR: 2⁰–2¹⁰; ResNet-18: ≈0; over-parameterized custom nets on MNIST: 2⁻²⁰–2⁻³⁰). Worth reading if you care about generalized-smoothness theory (L₀,L₁-smoothness, ℓ-smoothness, NTK alternatives); skip if you want a new optimizer you can run — generalized GD is never applied to a real network.

## 核心贡献

- **能量函数与广义凸/光滑**：定义能量函数 Ψ（Legendre 函数、最小值在原点、有径向表示 Ψ(µ)=Ψ∘(‖µ‖)），并用一阶 Taylor 余项 S_G(µ,ν)=G(µ)−G(ν)−⟨∇G(ν),µ−ν⟩ 的上下夹逼定义 H(Ψ)-光滑（S_G ≤ Ψ(µ−ν)）与 H(ψ)-凸（S_G ≥ ψ(µ−ν)）。关键设计是常数项：ψ(0)≠0 允许非凸，Ψ(0)≠0 允许非光滑，从而在同一形式下统一凸/非凸、光滑/非光滑。
- **对偶性**：证明 G 是 H(Ψ)-光滑 ⟺ 其共轭 G* 是 H(Ψ*)-凸（Prop 3.2），把经典"强凸-光滑对偶"（Kakade et al. 2009）推广到非凸非光滑情形；并给出缩放、平移与 Fenchel–Young 损失下的性质继承。
- **广义 GD/SGD 与恒等于 1 的学习率**：更新式 θ_{k+1}=θ_k−α∇Ψ*(∇G(θ_k))，Theorem 4.1 证明最优 α=1，且当 Ψ=L/2‖·‖²₂ 时退化为经典 GD 且恢复 α=1/L 与 O(κ log(1/ε)) 速率。广义 SGD 额外引入梯度相关因子 M 与模型容量风险 R_G(s,m)，迭代复杂度 O(n/(mΨ*(ε)−(n−m)M))。
- **复合优化视角解释可训练性**：把 ERM 写成 G=F∘h，Theorem 5.1 给出 Φ*(∇θG/‖J‖) ≤ G−G* ≤ ϕ*(∇θG·‖J⁺‖)，即"降低梯度能量 + 维持 Jacobian 诱导范数"两项即可优化 DNN；进而用 Prop 5.1（随机初始化 + 过参数化收窄谱间隙）与 Prop 5.2（跳连阻止 Jacobian 随深度指数衰减）解释架构技巧。
- **跨设置的经验一致性验证**：在图像/文本、CNN/ResNet/ViT/Transformer、SGD/Adam/RMSprop、MSE/KLDiv/PoissonNLL/‖·‖ᵏₖ（k=2..6）等组合上验证风险与理论上下界高度同步，并用"假标签"对照证明界非平凡。

## Key Contributions

- **A single formalism for convex/non-convex and smooth/non-smooth objectives.** By bounding the first-order Taylor remainder above by Ψ and below by ψ (both Legendre energy functions with radial representation), the constants Ψ(0) ≥ 0 and ψ(0) ≤ 0 act as continuous "degrees" of smoothness and convexity rather than binary properties. Non-differentiable points (ReLU kinks) are handled by declaring ∇G := 0 there, matching autodiff practice.
- **Conjugate duality between generalized smoothness and generalized convexity** (Prop 3.2.1), extending the classical Kakade–Zhou duality; plus inheritance rules under scaling, sample averaging, and Fenchel–Young loss construction — the latter covering MSE, softmax cross-entropy, hinge and perceptron losses through their generating functions.
- **Generalized GD/SGD with a parameter-free step size.** The update follows ∇Ψ*(∇G) rather than ∇G; Theorem 4.1 proves α = 1 is optimal, and Prop 4.1 shows the update direction coincides with the raw gradient direction iff the energy is built on the L2 norm. Prop 4.2 shows that when G is H(Ψ)-smooth but not Lipschitz smooth, classical GD has *no* universally optimal constant step size — a time-varying LR schedule is exactly equivalent to generalized GD.
- **Two new architecture-aware quantities**: the gradient correlation factor M = max_{k,s_k}[G(θ_{k+1}, s\s_k) − G(θ_k, s\s_k)] capturing cross-batch parameter coupling, and the model capacity risk R_G(s,m) = G(s)* − E[G(s_m)*], proved monotonically non-increasing in batch size m (Prop 4.4). Convergence of generalized SGD requires mΨ*(ε) > (n−m)M, so gradient energy provably need *not* reach zero.
- **Concrete two-sided risk bounds for DNN training** (Cor 5.3) in terms of measurable quantities only: gradient norm and extreme singular values of the Jacobian — no Hessian, no Lipschitz constant, no infinite-width limit.

## Methodology

The construction is: (1) *Energy functions.* Ψ: R^m → R is Legendre, minimized at 0, and radial: Ψ(µ) = Ψ∘(‖µ‖). Prop 3.1 shows conjugates of energy functions are energy functions, Ψ(0) + Ψ*(0) = 0, and for the power family Ψ = ‖a_Ψ µ‖^{r_Ψ}/r_Ψ + c_Ψ the conjugate exponents satisfy 1/r_Ψ + 1/r_{Ψ*} = 1, a_Ψ a_{Ψ*} = 1, c_Ψ + c_{Ψ*} = 0.

(2) *Generalized properties.* H(Ψ)-smooth means S_G(µ,ν) ≤ Ψ(µ−ν); H(ψ)-convex means S_G(µ,ν) ≥ ψ(µ−ν). Theorem 3.1 then gives Ψ*(∇G(µ)) ≤ G(µ) − G* ≤ ψ*(∇G(µ)) — suboptimality sandwiched by the *gradient energy* under conjugation, with no convexity or differentiability assumed. Cor 3.1 recovers ‖∇G‖²₂/(2k_Ψ) ≤ G − G* ≤ ‖∇G‖²₂/(2k_ψ) for the quadratic case. Prop 3.5 instantiates: Hessian-eigenvalue criterion (λmax/λmin), MSE is both H(‖·‖²₂/2)-convex and -smooth in f_θ(x), and softmax CE is H(min_i p_i ‖·‖²₂)-convex and H(2 ln2 ‖·‖²₂)-smooth.

(3) *Algorithms.* Generalized GD is θ_{k+1} = θ_k − α ∇Ψ*(∇G(θ_k)), i.e. a mirror-descent-flavoured step in the dual geometry induced by Ψ*. Theorem 4.1 gives descent G(θ_{k+1}) ≤ G(θ_k) − Ψ*(∇G(θ_k)) at α = 1, iteration count O(1/Ψ*(ε)), and a linear rate O(κ log(1/(ψ*(ε)−ψ*(0)))) with κ = −1/log(1−t) when Ψ*(µ) ≥ tψ*(µ)+b. The SGD version (Theorem 4.2) folds in M and R_G(s,m), yielding O(n/(mΨ*(ε)−(n−m)M)) and a condition number κ = −1/log(1−mt/n). Prop 4.3 shows the variance of the empirical-PMF difference between s_k and its complement is p_a(1−p_a)·n²/(m(n−m)(n−1)), minimized at m = ⌊n/2⌋.

(4) *Composite reduction.* G(θ,z) = F(h_θ(z)) with F being H(ϕ)-convex/H(Φ)-smooth in the *output*. Theorem 5.1 / Cor 5.1 push the bounds through the Jacobian: Φ*(∇θG/σmax(J)) ≤ G − G* ≤ ϕ*(∇θG/σmin(J)). Prop 5.1 (assuming Jacobian columns are i.i.d. uniform on a radius-ε ball — the "approximate gradient independence assumption") gives σmin ≥ (1 − 2log d/m)ε and σ²max/σ²min ≤ ζ(m,d)+1 with probability ≥ 1 − O(1/d), where ζ(m,d) = 2d√(6 log d)/√(m−1) · (1−2log d/m)⁻². Prop 5.2 argues residual blocks turn the depth-wise product ∏(∇h) into ∏(∇h + I) ≈ I, converting exponential decay into growth.

(5) *Empirical protocol.* Two experiment families. First, generalized GD on F(x) = ‖x‖ʳ₂ (r = 1.1…2.0, 100-dim, θ₀ ~ N(0,1)) with α ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5, 1.8, 2.0, 3.0}, tracking log₂‖∇F‖²₂; Lemma 6.1 supplies the tight energy Φ = 2^{2−r}‖·‖ʳ₂. Second, DNN training (PyTorch 2.2.2, one RTX 2080 Ti) where the *classical* optimizer is run and three scalars are logged per step: log L(θ,z), bound_up = −log σ²min(J) + log‖∇θL‖²₂, bound_low = −log σ²max(J) + log‖∇θL‖²₂; agreement is assessed by a sliding-window (w = 50) local Pearson correlation. Sweeps cover architecture (4 custom nets, LeNet, ResNet-18, ViT, TextCNN, residual MLP+LayerNorm, light Transformer), datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, TREC, SST-2), optimizers/schedulers (SGD lr 0.1, Adam 1e-4, RMSprop 1e-4 × StepLR(30, γ=0.1) / cosine annealing, 80 epochs, BN + Dropout 0.5), losses (CE, MSE, KLDiv, PoissonNLL, ‖·‖ᵏₖ for k=2..6), and scale (ResNet-18/34/50/101/152). A width sweep (n_w: 16 → 1600 at n_d = 1) and depth sweep (n_d: 1 → 100 at n_w = 64) probe Jacobian spectra at initialization, with and without skip connections. A **false-label control** (train on true labels, evaluate the bound on random wrong labels) tests non-triviality.

## Results & Highlights

- **Optimal learning rate α = 1 holds uniformly on the non-Lipschitz family.** Across all ten exponents r ∈ {1.1, 1.2, …, 2.0} of F(x) = ‖x‖ʳ₂ in 100 dimensions, α = 1 gives the fastest and most stable decay of log₂‖∇F(θ_k)‖²₂; performance degrades monotonically as α moves away from 1 in either direction (tested α = 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.5, 1.8), optimization **stagnates exactly at α = 2**, and **diverges for α > 2** (α = 3.0). This is the theoretically predicted stability boundary, and it is the one place where the paper's *algorithm* (not just its bounds) is actually run. Baseline comparison here is implicit: classical GD is the Ψ = L/2‖·‖²₂ special case, and Prop 4.2 asserts no constant step size is universally optimal for it on these objectives.
- **Convergence speed increases monotonically with r at α = 1**: larger r yields both faster decay and a lower final gradient energy in Fig. 4, matching the theoretical statement that the order of the energy function sets the rate.
- **Final gradient energy separates capacity regimes by ~30–40 orders of magnitude in log₂.** LeNet on CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 plateaus at gradient energy in the range **2⁰ to 2¹⁰**, ResNet-18 on the same data drives it **≈ 0**, and the heavily over-parameterized custom nets on MNIST reach **2⁻²⁰ to 2⁻³⁰**. The paper reads this as direct evidence for the gradient correlation factor M: simple models on complex data have large M, so mΨ*(ε) > (n−m)M fails and the gradient energy cannot converge to zero. Note this is the paper's *only* cross-model quantitative comparison, and no test accuracy is reported anywhere.
- **Sliding-window Pearson correlation between log risk and both bounds approaches 1** (window length 50) in *every* setting reported — 4 custom nets on MNIST, LeNet/ResNet-18/ViT on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, TextCNN / residual-MLP / light-Transformer on TREC and SST-2, ResNet-18 through ResNet-152, six loss functions, and three optimizer × two scheduler combinations. The gap between bound_up and bound_low is reported to narrow over training with both converging toward zero. The paper gives no numeric correlation value beyond "approaches 1", and no comparison correlation for a degenerate predictor (e.g. gradient energy alone without the Jacobian term).
- **False-label control breaks the bound**, which is the strongest evidence of non-triviality: parameters and Jacobian are identical to the true-label run, but the gradient energy blows up, so bound_up no longer tracks the risk. The bounds are therefore informative only in the regime where SGD has actually reduced gradient energy — an honest and useful negative result.
- **Depth without skip connections is catastrophic for the Jacobian spectrum; with skip connections it is beneficial.** Sweeping depth n_d from 1 to 100 at fixed width n_w = 64 on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, model (a) without skips shows extreme singular values decaying rapidly (exponentially) with depth, while model (b) with skips shows **log σ² growing linearly in depth** (i.e. exponential growth in σ²) — exactly the ∏(∇h + I) ≈ I prediction of Prop 5.2.
- **Width narrows the spectral gap.** Sweeping n_w from 16 to 1600 at n_d = 1, the gap log₂σ²max(J) − log₂σ²min(J) decreases monotonically while both extremes drift upward and stabilize — consistent with Prop 5.1's σ²max/σ²min ≤ ζ(m,d)+1 being decreasing in the parameter count m for fixed output dimension d, and with (but not requiring) the NTK lazy-training picture.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-good, but narrower than advertised).** Replacing ‖·‖²₂ with a Legendre function and reading off the Fenchel dual is a natural and elegant move, and the *symmetric* treatment — the same object bounding the Taylor remainder from above and below, with Ψ(0)/ψ(0) as continuous knobs — is genuinely cleaner than the asymmetric literature ((L₀,L₁)-smoothness bounds only curvature; ℓ-smoothness only generalizes upward). But "generalized GD" is recognizably mirror-descent/Bregman machinery, and the relationship to mirror descent, natural gradient, or Bregman proximal methods is *never discussed anywhere in the paper* — a conspicuous omission for a work whose central update rule is a dual-space step.

**The α = 1 headline is close to a definitional artifact.** Generalized GD absorbs the step size into ∇Ψ*: when Ψ = L/2‖·‖²₂, ∇Ψ*(g) = g/L, so "learning rate 1" *is* classical learning rate 1/L wearing a different hat. The paper says this eliminates "the need to tune the learning rate", but tuning is merely relocated into constructing Ψ — and the author explicitly concedes that computing the optimal Ψ is likely NP-hard, by analogy to exact Lipschitz-constant computation (Virmaux & Scaman 2018). The practical claim is therefore substantially weaker than the abstract implies.

**Proposition 3.3 is nearly vacuous as stated.** "The objective function of neural network training is both H(Ψ)-smooth and H(ψ)-convex" is asserted with no proof in the main text, and by the paper's own preceding argument any function can be sandwiched by inflating a_Ψ, r_Ψ, c_Ψ. All content lives in the *tightness* of (ψ, Ψ), which is exactly what the framework cannot compute. Proposition 3.4 acknowledges this ("ψ(0), Ψ(0) as close to 0 as possible") but offers a design heuristic, not a procedure.

**The empirical validation does not test the paper's algorithm on any neural network.** Every DNN experiment runs *classical* SGD/Adam/RMSprop and merely logs correlations. Generalized GD is tested only on 100-dimensional synthetic ‖x‖ʳ₂ objectives. So the most eye-catching claim (parameter-free α = 1) has zero evidence at DNN scale, and the paper never reports what Ψ would even be for ResNet-18.

**The correlation metric is weak and partly self-fulfilling.** Since ∇θL = J_θ f · ∇_h F, the quantity log‖∇θL‖²₂ − log σ²min(J) is algebraically constructed from the same objects that drive the loss; for MSE, ∇_h F = f − y, so ‖∇θL‖²₂ ≈ σ²·(2L) almost by identity, and a near-1 correlation is close to arithmetic rather than a prediction. The paper's own false-label check is the right instinct, but the missing ablation is the obvious one: **what is the correlation using log‖∇θL‖²₂ alone, with the Jacobian term removed?** If that is also ≈ 1, the Jacobian half of the theory carries no empirical weight. This ablation is absent.

**Batch-size prescription contradicts standard practice, and the paper claims the opposite.** Section 5.2 recommends "first use a larger m to converge quickly, then reduce m to further decrease the gradient energy" and asserts "this is consistent with the batch size scheduling strategy commonly employed in practice". Common practice is the reverse — batch size is typically *increased* over training (Smith et al.'s "don't decay the learning rate, increase the batch size"; large-batch fine-tuning schedules). This is an overclaimed and factually shaky alignment with practice. Prop 4.3's separate conclusion that m = n/2 minimizes distributional discrepancy is also far from any batch size used in the experiments (32).

**Prop 5.2's depth claim is one-sided.** Concluding that skip connections make deeper always better because σ(J) grows exponentially with depth ignores that exploding Jacobians are themselves a pathology; the paper treats "larger induced norm ⇒ tighter bounds ⇒ better trainability" monotonically, with no discussion of instability, and offers no experiment where a very deep residual net actually trains better in loss/accuracy terms.

**Other specifics:** the convention ∇G := 0 at non-differentiable points makes "zero gradient at global optimum" true by construction, which is convenient for Theorem 3.1 and deserves more scrutiny than the one paragraph given. Prop 5.1's "approximate gradient independence assumption" (Jacobian columns i.i.d. uniform on a ball) is idealized and never verified directly — the width sweep is consistent with it, but many hypotheses would be. M and R_G(s,m) are, by the author's own admission, uncomputable for DNNs and function as theoretical placeholders like σ² in classical SGD analysis; the LeNet-vs-ResNet gradient-energy gap is interpreted post hoc through M without independent measurement.

**Reproducibility:** no code link, no seed counts, no error bars, no repeated runs; the custom architectures are specified only via a figure. The synthetic α = 1 experiment is trivially reproducible; the correlation experiments would take real effort to reconstruct.

**Significance:** if you accept the framing, this is a coherent and unusually broad-coverage theoretical story that gets DNN trainability without infinite width, convexity, or differentiability — a real advance over NTK-style and Fenchel–Young-restricted accounts. But it explains rather than predicts: nothing in the paper produces a better optimizer, a better architecture, or a measurable performance number.

## Strengths

- The conjugate-duality framing is clean and the Ψ(0)/ψ(0) constants are a genuinely nice device for making convexity and smoothness continuous rather than binary properties.
- Every generalization is checked against the classical special case (Cor 3.1, Cor 4.1, Cor 4.2 all recover textbook GD/SGD results under Ψ = L/2‖·‖²₂, ψ = σ/2‖·‖²₂), which is the right sanity discipline for a theory paper.
- The false-label control is a well-designed non-triviality test that many theory-validation papers omit; it holds the Jacobian fixed and perturbs only the gradient energy.
- Experimental breadth along *nuisance* axes is real: two modalities, six datasets, eight-plus architectures, three optimizers, two schedulers, six loss functions, five ResNet depths. The invariance claim is well-supported even if the metric is weak.
- Explicit acknowledgment that computing the optimal Ψ is likely NP-hard, and that the gradient energy need not converge to zero (mΨ*(ε) > (n−m)M), is more honest than the typical "our theory explains deep learning" paper.
- The depth/width Jacobian-spectrum sweeps (n_d up to 100, n_w up to 1600) give a crisp, falsifiable prediction — decay without skips, growth with skips — and the prediction holds on two datasets.

## Limitations

- Generalized GD/SGD is never run on a neural network; the algorithmic contribution is validated only on 100-dim ‖x‖ʳ₂ toys.
- Constructing (ψ, Ψ) for a real DNN is conjectured NP-hard, so the framework predicts qualitatively but cannot be instantiated quantitatively.
- No connection drawn to mirror descent / Bregman divergence / natural gradient literature despite the update rule being dual-space.
- The validation metric (sliding-window Pearson correlation between log risk and algebraically related bound components) is near-tautological for MSE, and the key ablation (drop the Jacobian term) is missing.
- No accuracy or task-performance numbers anywhere; no error bars, seeds, or code release.
- The batch-size scheduling recommendation (large → small) is presented as matching practice when practice generally goes the other way.
- Prop 5.1 relies on an unverified i.i.d.-uniform-columns assumption on the Jacobian; Prop 5.2's "deeper is better" conclusion ignores exploding-Jacobian failure modes.
- Generalization is entirely out of scope — the framework says nothing about why low-gradient-energy solutions generalize, which the conclusion lists as future work.

## Relevance

对关注优化理论、非凸/非光滑分析与"为什么 SGD 能训练深网"这类基础问题的研究者高度相关：它给出一个不依赖无限宽度、凸性或可微性的统一框架，并把架构设计（跳连、过参数化、宽度）直接落到 Jacobian 极端奇异值这一可测量上。若关注点是可直接落地的优化器或大模型训练配方，则参考价值有限——文中的广义 GD 从未在真实网络上跑过，且不报告任何准确率指标。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the sliding-window Pearson correlation between log risk and log‖∇θL‖²₂ *alone*, with the Jacobian singular values removed? Without this ablation it is impossible to tell whether the composite-optimization half of the theory adds explanatory power over plain gradient-norm tracking.
- How does H(Ψ)-smoothness relate formally to mirror descent with Bregman divergence D_Ψ, and does generalized GD reduce to a known mirror-descent variant for the power family Ψ = ‖a µ‖ʳ/r? If so, do known mirror-descent rates recover Theorem 4.1?
- Can a *cheap surrogate* for ∇Ψ* be estimated online (e.g. from gradient-norm history, in the spirit of (L₀,L₁)-adaptive clipping) to actually run generalized SGD on a ResNet or transformer and test whether α = 1 works without tuning?
- The theory predicts that lower parameter coupling (smaller M ⇒ sparser parameterization) improves attainable gradient energy. Does this hold for MoE / sparsely-activated models, where coupling is architecturally reduced? That would be a genuinely falsifiable prediction the paper does not test.
- Prop 5.2 implies σ(J) grows exponentially with residual depth. At what depth does this become an exploding-Jacobian pathology rather than a trainability benefit, and does the bound tightness/optimization stability tradeoff have an optimum?
- Can R_G(s,m) or M be estimated even crudely (e.g. by measuring loss change on held-out-batch complements during training) to turn them from theoretical placeholders into diagnostics for capacity vs. data complexity?
