Why This Matters

Elevates when to refine as a first-class design axis for block-wise PTQ — a near-free drop-in for existing CBQ/GPTQ pipelines that rescues diverged sub-2-bit runs.

Critical Edge

The 5752.9 baseline is a diverged run — without prefit it scores 47.28, so the advertised 190× gain is really a 12% honest gap.

01

Methodology

ICBQ is weight-only PTQ of an L-block Transformer with fp16 activations, no rotation. An inner quantizer Q — ternary DBF (1.58-bit) or GPTQ (W3 / W2g128) — converts each fp16 block into a low-bit student. On top, a fixed two-block CBQ objective refines adjacent pairs by minimizing MSE to the fp16 teacher output two blocks ahead, with a rollback safeguard.

The Sequential CBQ baseline quantizes all blocks, then sweeps each adjacent pair once, left-to-right; errors at a pair propagate forward and are never revisited. ICBQ replaces the sweep order: quantize in chunks of K, and after each chunk close, re-open a two-block window whose left endpoint is offset by −1 — pulling the previous chunk's last pair back in so every chunk seam is refined twice while interior pairs are refined once. Activations are re-rolled from a stored layer-0 buffer each step.

Chunk size K is the single dial: smaller K ⇒ more seams ⇒ more revisits. Theorem 4.1 gives each seam an extra γ contraction factor in the depth-wise error upper bound — explicitly a bound-vs-bound statement (Remark B.7), not a realized-perplexity guarantee.

03

Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths
  • Genuinely cheap & drop-in: no new objective, codebook, or params — 1.21× time, depth-independent memory constant.
  • Includes the no-prefit ablation (Table 4) that deflates its own headline — to the authors' credit.
  • Single interpretable dial K; theory predicts the trend, experiments confirm on 6/7 models.
  • Candid theory: Remark B.7 flags it as bound-vs-bound; reported γ̄ ≈ 0.67 brackets the empirical range.
! Limitations
  • No head-to-head vs QuIP#, AQLM, QTIP, or OmniQuant at matched bit-width — no absolute sub-2-bit Pareto placement.
  • Single seed, no variance bars, while several claimed wins are ≤1% — the sign of the effect is not established.
  • Weight-only, fp16 activations, no rotation — untested with QuaRot/SpinQuant bases or W8A8 pipelines.
  • Window-width vs chunk-size interaction never explored, though both are schedule parameters — the paper's whole thesis.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How much of the gain is the seam double-refinement versus the activation re-roll alone? An ablation with re-roll on but no seam overlap (window starting at b_cs, not b_cs−1) would isolate this — and is conspicuously absent.
  2. Why is Qwen3-14B non-monotone in K (24.67 at K=4 vs 25.89 at K=2)? Does over-revisiting seams overfit the 256-sample calibration set?
  3. Would non-uniform / adaptive chunking help? Errors are not uniform across depth; placing seams where measured mismatch is largest is a natural extension the fixed-K schedule leaves on the table.
  4. Does the schedule effect survive when composed with rotation preprocessing (QuaRot/SpinQuant), where the propagated term the theory targets is already suppressed?
