> **一句话总结：** 作者用 Active Attention Probing（在 system 区插入固定探针，提供与提示内容无关的测量坐标）配上「同一 base goal 配对 plain/wrapped + 真实生成 + 判定器打标」的析因设计，证明一个在 BeaverTails 上有害意图 AUROC 0.985、JailbreakBench 上 0.936 的内部安全分数，对 Llama-3.1-8B 上真实越狱成功与否的排序 AUROC 只有 0.220（CI [0.109, 0.343]，即成功攻击的分数系统性**低于**失败攻击）；在 5% 良性误报预算下该过滤器拦掉 42.5% 的失败攻击却只拦掉 14.8% 的成功攻击（gap −27.7 点），而同样内部特征改用成功标签监督训练可达 0.930 out-of-fold，说明信息存在、只是分数「量错了东西」。

> **TL;DR:** Internal prompt-safety scores are validated on harmful-*intent* labels, but deployed as predictors of jailbreak *success* — two different estimands. Using Active Attention Probing (a fixed, content-independent measurement coordinate in the system region) plus a matched plain-vs-wrapped factorial design with real generations, the authors show on JailbreakBench × Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct that wrapping raises harmful generation 0.05 → 0.27 while harmful-intent AUROC falls 0.936 → 0.803, and outcome AUROC is **0.220** (CI [0.109, 0.343]) — successful attacks are ranked *below* failed ones. Llama Guard 3 as a comparison detector sits at 0.507 (chance) on the same cohort, and a pooled clustered bootstrap over 7 attack families × 3 targets × 2 judges gives 0.313 (CI [0.212, 0.422], p = 0.0006). Yet an outcome-supervised readout on the *same* internal features reaches 0.930 out-of-fold, beating the strongest oracle-ish factorial baseline by +0.156, so the failure is one of construct validity, not of information availability.

## 核心贡献

- **区分两个被长期混淆的量**：提示的「有害意图」H(x) 是提示属性；「越狱成功」Y_m(x) 是由目标模型 m、解码策略与判定器共同产生的结果。论文形式化为三层效度：construct validity（能否排序自己的训练标签）、outcome validity（能否排序 Y_m）、decision validity（阈值是否保持工作点），并论证前者不蕴含后者。
- **提出 Active Attention Probing (AAP) 作为测量工具而非防御方法**：把 K=3 行连续探针插入 chat template 物化后的 system 区，读取最后输入位置 q 对探针位置的注意力 z_{ℓ,h} = Σ_{j∈P} A^{ℓ,h}_{qj}，由 XGBoost 映射为标量分数。关键点是被动注意力检测器（Attention Tracker / AttentionDefense 式）读的是**提示决定的位置**，wrapper 同时改变了被测内容和测量点；AAP 固定坐标以消除该混淆。
- **匹配析因审计设计**：对每个 base goal 同时构造 plain 与 wrapped 版本，交叉 H×W，定义 τ_H、τ_W、τ_HW 三个平衡效应，并对每个提示**真实生成**补全后打标，而不是把成功当作提示属性。
- **给出反转的广度证据**：rare-token、common-token、norm-matched、system-span、passive attention、refusal-logit 六条测量通道，三个目标模型（Llama / Mistral / Qwen），七个攻击族（GCG、AutoDAN、AutoDAN-Turbo、PAIR、TAP、Best-of-N、Many-shot），两个独立判定器（Llama Guard 3、Qwen3-14B rubric）上复现。
- **把「效度失效」拆成排序 / 校准 / 阈值迁移三段**：RQ2 显示分布漂移下阈值最先坏，校准次之，排序最后坏；并给出双类同时变换（both-sided）设计，防止「变换本身即标签」的捷径。
- **量化对部署的影响**：在固定良性误报预算下测量拦截配额如何在成功攻击与失败攻击之间分配，并把泄漏归因为「原本就漏」「wrapper 主效应」「逆序排名」三部分。

## Key Contributions

- Separates prompt harmfulness H(x) from target-indexed realized success Y_m(x), and argues that the standard validation ritual (harmful vs. benign AUROC) licenses only the first reading while deployments assume the second.
- Introduces **Active Attention Probing (AAP)**: a K=3 continuous probe inserted into the system region after template materialization, giving a fixed content-independent coordinate so that a prompt and its wrapped version are read at a common reference point. Explicitly framed as a *measurement instrument*, not a defense or a steering method.
- Runs a matched 2×2 factorial (harmful intent × wrapper) over fixed base goals with **real completions** from each target, so the outcome label is generated rather than assumed.
- Demonstrates the anti-ranking result across six measurement channels, three targets, seven attack families, and two judges, with a per-head decomposition — and then honestly discounts that decomposition against a mechanical null.
- Decomposes shift-induced failure into ranking loss, calibration loss, and threshold-transfer loss, showing they fail in a specific order and that a single fixed-threshold metric cannot distinguish them.
- Quantifies deployment cost: how a benign-quantile filter allocates its blocking budget between attacks that would have succeeded and attacks that would have failed anyway.
- Releases an artifact (analysis code, configs, tests, numeric aggregates) at `github.com/kuangren777/aap-audit`, and includes a full postmortem of a checkpoint-resume defect the authors found in their *own* pipeline.

## Methodology

**Setting.** A pre-generation internal score computed from a single eager-attention prefill pass; it sees attention and hidden states of the prompt x but never the completion. Primary target: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; Mistral, Qwen, Gemma, and Phi test transfer across attention grids.

**Probe and readout.** `I(x, p_θ)` inserts a K=3 row continuous probe into the system region; user tokens are untouched. The per-head response is `z_{ℓ,h}(x,p) = Σ_{j ∈ P(x,p)} A^{ℓ,h}_{qj}(I(x,p))`, concatenated over monitored heads and mapped by XGBoost to S(x) ∈ [0,1]. The probe is initialized from safety-semantic token embeddings, trained with Adam (lr 1e-3, 50 updates, batch 20/class) on the **BeaverTails training split only**, then **frozen before every audit**. The objective maximizes the safe-vs-attack attention gap with a safe-attention penalty (0.1) and a row-norm penalty (0.01). No JailbreakHub / WildJailbreak / JailbreakBench prompt selects the probe, heads, or readout.

**Data.** BeaverTails curated to 1774 safe / 3671 harmful after removing duplicates and label conflicts; 1000 per class for training, held out 774 safe / 2671 harmful. Source prompts are split *before* creating paraphrase, multilingual, or encoding variants.

**Estimands.** With μ_hw = E[S(x) | H=h, W=w], the paper reports τ_H (harmfulness effect), τ_W (wrapper effect), and the interaction τ_HW = μ11 − μ10 − μ01 + μ00. R_H is harmful-intent AUROC; R_Y(S, m) is outcome AUROC restricted to harmful prompts, separating effective (W=H=Y=1) from ineffective (W=H=1, Y=0) attacks.

**Outcome generation.** All completions use the **original prompt without the probe** and greedy decoding; scores use `inputs_embeds` with the probe present. The insertion effect is audited separately: median next-token symmetric KL 0.22, refusal flips on 7.5% of prompts, and a generation audit estimating an attack-success change of −0.03 (95% CI [−0.070, 0.007]). Llama Guard 3 provides primary outcome labels; a frozen Qwen3-14B rubric relabels cached completions; a StrongREJECT-style rubric is reported but excluded from inference because the available substitute marks ~30% of verbatim refusals as successful.

**Controls.** (i) Head selection — sparse 20-head subsets are high variance (min OOD AUROC 0.60 ± 0.15 over 40 random draws), so RQ2 uses the dense all-head read; (ii) prompt surface — a length-only classifier gets 0.49–0.62 AUROC even where encoding makes prompts 5× longer; (iii) source correlation — a bootstrap clustered by source prompt yields intervals 0.94× the unclustered width; (iv) norm-matched random probes with identical row norms, coordinate, and dtype; (v) passive channel that inserts no tokens at all; (vi) attention-dilution normalization (system-region attention drops 0.383 → 0.225 under wrapping, and the paper divides by it, since that region holds a constant 13 tokens while the wrapper lands in the user turn).

**Metrics.** AUROC for ranking; Brier / ECE / logistic recalibration slope for calibration; fixed vs. refitted F1 for threshold transfer. DeLong tests plus bootstrap CIs for paired AUROC differences; a benign-only quantile rule with a DKW finite-sample bound for the deployment audit.

## Results & Highlights

- **The headline reversal (Llama-3.1-8B, JailbreakBench 100 benign + 100 harmful goals):** wrapping raises harmful generation **0.05 → 0.27** while harmful-intent AUROC **falls 0.936 → 0.803**; among the 100 wrapped harmful goals the score ranks the 27 successes *below* the 73 failures, giving outcome AUROC **0.220 (CI [0.109, 0.343])** versus 0.5 chance. Factorial effects: τ_H = 0.521 (CI [0.458, 0.584]), τ_W = −0.094, τ_HW = **−0.257** (CI [−0.359, −0.158]) — wrapping lowers the score selectively for harmful requests.
- **Not an artifact of wrapping:** plain harmful prompts alone already show inverse ranking, R_Y = **0.128** (CI [0.025, 0.259]); induced successes (23 of 95 goals Llama refuses plain become successful after wrapping, McNemar p = 3×10⁻⁶) give R_Y = 0.254 (CI [0.129, 0.396]); within-family centering gives 0.333 (CI [0.236, 0.444]). Ranking by the *plain→wrapped score difference* gives only 0.421, so it is the wrapped score **level** that carries the (wrong-signed) signal.
- **Deployment cost at fixed benign budget:** at a nominal 5% FPR (achieved 5.7%) the filter removes **42.5% of failed attacks but only 14.8% of successful ones — a gap of −27.7 points (CI [−44.8, −8.8])**, admitting 23 of 27 realized jailbreaks; the harmful rate among admitted prompts *rises* 0.270 → 0.354. At 10% nominal (11.1% achieved) it blocks 80.8% of failures vs 29.6% of successes, gap **−51.2 points**. On the strong-attack cohort 92% of successful attacks pass under either judge. Leakage decomposition at 5%: 53% pre-existing misses, 23% wrapper main effect, 24% inverse ranking — the inverse share rises to 53% at the 10% point. Fair caveat the authors state themselves: absolute harm still falls 27 → 23, and the score removes ~2.6× as many successful attacks as random blocking at the same benign budget.
- **The information is there — the score just measures the wrong thing.** An outcome-supervised readout on the *same* internal features (grouped CV, 25 repeats, base goal never spanning folds) reaches **0.930** out-of-fold on wrapped harmful prompts, 0.939 on all harmful prompts, 0.875 (CI [0.800, 0.939]) leave-one-wrapper-family-out, and 0.725 (CI [0.680, 0.788]) on WildJailbreak; label-permutation null is 0.497 ± 0.072. The strongest factorial baseline — an oracle handed each wrapper family's empirical success rate — trails by **+0.156**; on WildJailbreak a word-level TF-IDF logistic model is the strongest baseline at **0.651** and the internal readout beats it by a paired margin of +0.016 to +0.139. Swapping to this readout drops the admitted-harm rate from 0.354 to **0.062** at matched coverage.
- **Channel robustness:** rare-token, passive, system-span, and refusal-logit channels retain wrapped-harmfulness AUROC 0.751–0.828 while their outcome AUROC spans **0.145–0.248**, every interval below chance, each with a negative H×W interaction. The passive channel inserts no tokens, ruling out probe insertion as the cause. Across five probe seeds, harmfulness AUROC stays 0.770–0.861 and outcome AUROC 0.130–0.246.
- **External detector comparison:** Llama Guard 3 remains a strong harmfulness discriminator but gets outcome AUROC **0.507** (exactly chance) on the same matched cohort. On 350 *natural* WildJailbreak prompts (133 effective attacks) AAP gets 0.359 while Llama Guard reaches **0.625** — the paper flags this as the one place an external detector beats every internal channel, including its own matched-design value; rare-token 0.374, passive 0.449, perplexity 0.487 on the same cohort.
- **Targets and score-source cross (Table 1, 9 cells):** with score source in rows and generator in columns, R_Y = .220/.491/.163 (Llama score), .292/.470/.190 (Mistral score), .172/.386/.078 (Qwen score). **Six of nine intervals lie below chance and none lies above.** Mistral's near-chance .491 is explicitly labeled uninformative because Mistral already complies with 0.68 of *plain* harmful requests (McNemar p = 0.85 for wrapping's effect). Pairwise native score correlations are only 0.42–0.54, so native recomputation does not rescue the ranking.
- **Strong attacks (Table 2, Llama):** PAIR raises success .05 → .52 with R_Y = .48; Best-of-N .10 → .94 with R_Y = **.35**; GCG .11 → .17 with R_Y = **.19**; AutoDAN .05 → .34 with R_Y = **.20**; TAP .02 → .16 with R_Y = .31; AutoDAN-Turbo .02 → .38 with R_Y = .28; Many-shot .10 → .18 with R_Y = .48. Six of fourteen attack×judge cells lie below chance, none above; the goal-and-family-clustered bootstrap pools to **0.313 (CI [0.212, 0.422], p = 0.0006)**. Pooled logistic models controlling the plain-goal score and attack family retain negative score coefficients of −0.54 (Llama Guard) and −0.35 (Qwen3).
- **RQ2 — thresholds break before rankings (Table 3):** in-distribution AUROC 0.985 / ECE 0.045 / slope 0.54 / F1@0.5 0.925; WildJailbreak 0.850 / **0.366** / 0.51 / **0.559** (F1 recovers to 0.664 by relocating only the threshold); paraphrase 0.858 / 0.203 / 0.32 / 0.734; Chinese 0.802, French 0.778; English round-trip control 0.987 / 0.041 / 0.76 / 0.950. Platt scaling on a frozen source slice moves the slope 0.545 → 1.019 and ECE 0.045 → 0.009, and the calibrated source rule reaches WildJailbreak F1 0.667 against an oracle 0.678 — **without target labels** — but the same map *harms* the English round-trip control (ECE 0.041 → 0.171, slope 0.761 → 1.488).
- **Both-class encoding destroys harmfulness separation entirely:** encoding *only* harmful prompts gives AUROC 0.85–0.89 and a model-free character-entropy statistic reaches **0.997–1.000** (vs 0.513 on unencoded prompts) — i.e. the single-class design measures a transformation cue, not safety. Encoding **both** classes collapses AAP to **0.51 / 0.50 / 0.52** for base64 / rot13 / caesar, and Mistral, Qwen, Gemma, Phi reproduce 0.50–0.51.
- **Readout family matters a lot under shift:** logistic regression and linear SVM match XGBoost in distribution (~0.98) but their mean shifted-domain AUROC is **0.55 and 0.64 vs 0.75** for XGBoost; on Japanese the linear readouts reach 0.01–0.08 vs XGBoost's 0.228.
- **Benign-tail stability is a separate failure mode:** the benign-only quantile rule holds its nominal FPR in distribution and nearly matches oracle recall on paraphrase and English, but under benign shift its WildJailbreak recall collapses to **0.03 against an oracle 0.25**.
- **Risk–coverage:** harmful-generation rate among *admitted* prompts monotonically rises with blocking fraction — 0.270, 0.289, 0.300, 0.329, 0.383, 0.420 at 0/10/20/30/40/50% coverage — the cleanest single demonstration that the score spends its budget on the wrong cohort.

## Authors & Credibility

Eleven authors, led by Fudan University's College of Computer Science and AI (Mingyu Luo, Ming Deng, Zilang Qiu, Ci Tao, Xue Tan) with the Institute of Big Data at Fudan (Ping Chen, corresponding), plus Shanghai University, Beijing Normal University, Tsinghua (Yiming Cheng), A*STAR's Institute of Advanced Intelligence and Computing (Sijin Sun), East China Normal University (Yangfu Li), and Worcester Polytechnic Institute (Jun Dai, Xiaoyan Sun — both established security researchers). Two co-first authors. The paper is not from a frontier-lab safety team, and no author is a household name in mechanistic interpretability, but the methodological discipline on display (pre-registered cells, frozen probes, clustered bootstraps, mechanical nulls, a public defect postmortem) is well above the median for the jailbreak-detection literature. The artifact URL is concrete and the paper states exactly which analyses are pre-specified versus exploratory.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (high on framing, moderate on mechanism).** The core insight — that harmful-intent validation does not license success prediction — is not entirely new: the paper itself cites Ball et al. and Lin et al. showing jailbreaks suppress internal harmfulness features, and Kirch et al. showing success probes transfer below chance across attack families. What is genuinely new is (a) turning this into a *measurement-validity* audit with a matched factorial design where the base goal is held fixed, and (b) the AAP fixed-coordinate trick, which is the right response to a real confound: passive attention detectors read from prompt-determined locations, so a wrapper moves both the content and the measurement point. The negative result — successful attacks scoring *below* failed ones rather than merely uninformatively — is stronger than "detectors degrade."

**Rigor (unusually good, with specific soft spots).** Genuine strengths: the both-sided transformation design that prevents the transformation from becoming the label; refusing to rest homogeneity on a non-significant interaction test after showing via power simulation that the test's rejection rate is ≈0.16 under both null and a sign-flipped alternative ("its power equals its type-I rate, so a non-rejection carries no information" — this is the most intellectually honest sentence in the paper); reporting a design effect of 9.3 (~2.5 independent units across 23 cells) and therefore *declining* to run a sign test over cells; and discounting the striking per-head anti-correlation (Spearman −0.849) against a mechanical null that already produces −0.71 to −0.82, noting the null covers the observation outright along an alternative aggregate direction.

Now the soft spots, all paper-specific. **(1) The positive control is judge-fragile.** Under Qwen3 relabeling the outcome-supervised ceiling drops 0.930 → 0.883, and Qwen3 identifies only 14 successes — a strict subset of Llama Guard's 27, κ = 0.61. Three-judge Fleiss κ ≈ 0.3 with **no blinded human audit anywhere**. Every headline number is a function of an automated judge whose agreement with other automated judges is barely better than chance-adjusted moderate. **(2) 27 positives.** The entire Llama outcome AUROC rests on 27 successful attacks (Qwen: 10), which is why three of five wrapper families yield only two successes each and no per-family AUROC is estimable. The CI [0.109, 0.343] excludes chance, but this is a small-event-count estimate. **(3) Mistral is a weak cell dressed as coverage.** Mistral complies with 0.68 of plain harmful requests, so its R_Y ≈ 0.49 is uninformative — the authors say so, but Table 1's "6 of 9 below chance" headline is partly carried by cells that couldn't have shown much either way. **(4) The comparison detectors are ports, not reimplementations.** HiddenDetect is a logit-lens readout ported from multimodal to text-only, GradSafe is described but the "detector-derived channels" are the authors' own re-readouts; the paper is upfront ("They do not reimplement the published systems that motivated them"), but that means no published defense is actually run head-to-head, which weakens the generalization from "AAP and its channels anti-rank" to "internal safety scores anti-rank." The one true external system, Llama Guard 3, gets **0.625 on natural WildJailbreak — beating every internal channel**, which cuts against the broadest reading of the title. **(5) Single-draw shift results.** Only the factorial is checked under five sampled decodes (R_Y 0.202–0.252); strong-attack and shift results are single-draw. **(6) The Japanese inversion is an acknowledged artifact** (2.4× character-trigram similarity of the source; orientation-free AUROC 1 − 0.228 = 0.772), and the *lowest* OOD numbers depend on readout family — so the most dramatic RQ2 rows are the least trustworthy ones.

**Reproducibility (strong).** Public artifact with code, configs, tests, and publication-aligned numeric aggregates; frozen splits, fixed constants with no validation search for the harmfulness readout, prespecified search budgets (GCG 40 rounds with NLL<0.10 early stop; TAP/AutoDAN-Turbo at depth/width/branching 2 for cost), and per-head extraction verified to reproduce cached scores bitwise (r = 1.000, max abs deviation 0). Appendix B's checkpoint-defect disclosure — a length-validated resume displacing records by exactly k = 2(B−s) positions, corrupting 240 completions and 320 scores across 5 of 12 cells, with the fitted k matching in every artifact — is the kind of self-report almost nobody publishes. After repair, GCG's R_Y moved to 0.189/0.264 and its Qwen3 interval newly excludes chance, while the count of below-chance cells stayed at six (GCG–Qwen3 in, TAP–Llama Guard out).

**Significance (high for safety evaluation practice).** If internal prompt-safety filters systematically spend their false-positive budget on attacks that would have failed anyway, then the standard "our detector gets 0.98 AUROC on harmful vs benign" claim is close to meaningless for deployment. The concrete recommendation — a complete audit must report harmful-intent ranking on held-out prompts, realized outcomes for named targets/decoding/judges, *and* blocking at a fixed benign budget — is actionable and cheap.

**Red flags.** No serious ones in the sense of overclaiming: the paper repeatedly narrows its own claims ("These results do not show that prompt safety detectors fail at their stated task"), labels the representational-decoupling story explicitly as a hypothesis, and calls WildJailbreak and strong attacks "uncontrolled external checks." The one place the framing outruns the evidence is the unqualified title: "Internal Harmfulness Scores Anti-Rank Successful Jailbreaks" is demonstrated for AAP and five closely related attention/logit channels on one primary target with 27 positives, not for the internal-safety-score family in general — and the Llama Guard WildJailbreak result is a direct counterexample to the broad reading.

## Strengths

- The audit design is the contribution: matching on base goal, applying every transformation to both classes, and generating real completions removes three separate shortcuts (corpus identity, transformation-as-label, success-as-prompt-property) that the prior literature routinely leaves open.
- The positive control (0.930 out-of-fold from the same features under success supervision) converts a negative result into a *diagnosis*: the information is present in the internal state, the harmfulness-trained readout simply has the wrong orientation and operating meaning.
- Separating ranking / calibration / threshold transfer is a real methodological upgrade — the WildJailbreak row (AUROC only 0.850 but F1@0.5 collapsing to 0.559, recovering to 0.664 with threshold relocation alone) shows why a single F1 number hides which component broke.
- Repeated, explicit self-limitation: the per-head mechanical null, the power simulation that voids the homogeneity test, the design-effect calculation that forbids a sign test, the exclusion of StrongREJECT from inference, and the Appendix B defect postmortem.
- The passive-channel and refusal-logit replications close off the most obvious objection (that probe insertion itself creates the effect), reinforced by the insertion audit (median symmetric KL 0.22, attack-success change −0.03 with CI spanning zero).

## Limitations

- Outcome labels are entirely automated with **no blinded human audit**; three-judge Fleiss κ ≈ 0.3, and Llama Guard guides several attack searches, leaving only rubric judges independent for those rows.
- Small positive counts: 27 successes on Llama, 10 on Qwen; targets are not difficulty-matched, so cross-target comparison shows a range of behaviors rather than a controlled contrast.
- Primary outcomes use one greedy decode; only the factorial is repeated over five sampled decodes. Strong-attack and distribution-shift results are single-draw.
- Strong attacks and derived channels are evaluated on Llama only; just Best-of-N and Many-shot were repeated on Mistral and Qwen.
- Attacks do not share a common goal list (n = 200 rows pool JailbreakBench + HarmBench; GCG/AutoDAN/PAIR use 100; TAP/AutoDAN-Turbo use 50), so plain-prompt success rates differ by row and rows must be compared to their own plain column, not to each other.
- The Japanese transform contains a known register-collapse artifact, and the lowest OOD results depend on the readout family (linear 0.01–0.08 vs XGBoost 0.228).
- Probe insertion perturbs prefill (7.5% refusal flips), even though all reported outcomes come from unmodified prompts.
- No published defense system is reimplemented head-to-head; the "detector-derived" rows are the authors' ports with deviations listed in the appendix.
- The representational-decoupling account is offered as a hypothesis — the audit does not identify a unique causal pathway.

## Relevance

如果你（用户）关注 LLM 安全评测的方法论、可解释性探针（probing / attention readout）作为测量工具的效度问题，或者在做越狱检测 / 输入侧过滤的落地，这篇提供了一个可直接复用的审计模板：把「检测器在提示分类上的 AUROC」与「它在固定良性误报预算下对真实越狱结果的排序能力」分开报告。对做 LLM-as-a-judge 标注可靠性、以及关心「benchmark 指标与部署目标错配」这一类问题的研究，本文的 both-sided 变换设计、mechanical null、以及 checkpoint 缺陷复盘都有很高的方法论借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the anti-ranking survive a **blinded human-labeled** outcome set? With Fleiss κ ≈ 0.3 across judges, a few hundred human-adjudicated completions would settle whether R_Y = 0.220 is a property of the score or of Llama Guard's failure mode on wrapped text.
- Is the reversal a *causal* consequence of wrappers weakening the specific features the harmfulness readout reads, while compliance is governed by a partly disjoint mechanism? Activation patching or causal tracing on the ~22 heads that survive max-statistic permutation correction (R_Y > 0.5) would test the decoupling hypothesis the paper deliberately leaves open.
- Can a *hybrid* readout — harmfulness-supervised for construct coverage plus outcome-supervised for orientation — retain OOD robustness? The outcome-supervised probe drops to 0.725 on WildJailbreak versus 0.930 in-cohort, so it may simply be overfitting to wrapper-family surface.
- Why does Llama Guard 3 reach 0.625 on natural WildJailbreak while anti-ranking is absent (0.507) on the matched factorial? Does natural-attack outcome prediction rely on lexical/pragmatic cues that internal prefill attention discards?
- Does the result hold for **reasoning models and non-greedy deployment settings**, where the outcome depends on a long CoT trajectory rather than a single prefill-conditioned continuation?
- Practically: if outcome labels are needed for valid filter training, what is the cheapest sufficient outcome-labeling loop for a new deployment, given the paper's finding that new targets require new outcome audits?
- Would a conformal or benign-tail-stabilized threshold rule fix the decision-validity failure (WildJailbreak recall 0.03 vs oracle 0.25 under benign shift), or is upper-tail instability an intrinsic property of these attention statistics?
