01

Methodology, distilled

MMDiff warm-starts a base-LM sparse autoencoder (LLaMA-Scope / Gemma-Scope / Qwen-Scope) and fine-tunes it on a frozen MLLM's residual-stream activations from 50k VQAv2 image–question pairs (Adam, lr 7e-5, one job per layer). Text-only masking wins on reconstruction (FVU 0.005 vs 0.032–0.050 for the other regimes) and on alignment with the base dictionary — the argument being that multimodal capability emerges in the language backbone as text tokens attend to visual context, so text-token activations retain the LM basis while still carrying the multimodal change.

Adapted features are the intersection of low decoder-cosine (geometric rotation, bottom 25%) and high visual energy E_v — 5% (Llama) to 20% (Gemma) of the dictionary. Per-task features are then carved out by per-token contrastive firing against a VQAv2 baseline (Fisher exact, OR ≥ 3, Δp ≥ 0.05, BH-corrected) plus a lexical-invariance filter that strips spatial vocabulary. Removal projects the unit-norm decoder direction out at every layer and three insertion points (attention / MLP / residual out), text-token positions only; steering (MMDiff-CAA) injects the feature's own decoder direction at its home layer on top of multi-layer backbone CAA.

base-LM SAE (warm start)→ fine-tune on MLLM activations→ decoder-cosine ∩ visual energy→ per-token contrastive firing→ lexical-invariance filter→ ablate / steer

Every ablation variant except the full pipeline destroys general capability (ΔVQA ≈ −25). A conventionally-trained SAE on MLLM activations does not recover causally effective features — its top-10 "spatial" features fire on 100% of VSR samples (odds-ratio saturates) and ablate to +0.22.

Strengths

  • Unusually complete control suite for an interpretability paper: random-feature ablation, from-scratch-dictionary control, cross-seed stability, full-dictionary index correspondence (0.00% relocation), image counterfactuals.
  • Self-undercutting steering decomposition (Table 14) — reporting that multi-layer CAA alone reaches +10.78 of their +12.59 — reads as intellectual honesty.
  • One pipeline, three behaviors (spatial / safety / OCR) by varying only the target distribution and a five-prompt neutral bank.
  • Cross-stage pt-448 vs mix-448 ablation shows spatial features are acquired by instruction tuning, not inherited (effects amplify ~3×).
  • Three backbone families × three SAE objectives (TopK / JumpReLU / TopK) × three vision encoders for the spatial results.

Limitations

  • Safety & OCR are PaliGemma-2-only; the multi-family claim covers spatial reasoning. All backbones are 1.7B–8B dense — no MoE, no frontier-scale.
  • Per-feature VSR subsets are built from each feature's own top-activating samples, biasing effect sizes upward.
  • MMDiff-CAA needs an instruction-tuned reference model; without it, degrades to weak SAE steering (+2.63 / +3.66).
  • ASR judged solely by Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, no human validation; MSSBench control n = 100 (one sample = 1%).
  • No code or SAE checkpoints released; replication means re-training per-layer SAEs on 8× A100.
  • Ablation removes capability ≠ improves safety; no refusal-rate or over-refusal metric is reported.
05

Open questions for further exploration

  1. Do the spatial effect sizes survive a fixed, feature-independent VSR split rather than each feature's own top-activating subset? What fraction of −10 to −15 remains?
  2. Why does adding the lexical-invariance filter increase the ablation effect from −1.0 to −12.3 when it can only remove candidates? Top-k artifact?
  3. Does safety-feature ablation produce refusal or capability loss? What are the over-refusal rates on benign-but-sensitive prompts?
  4. Since multi-layer CAA alone recovers +10.78 of +12.59 — is layer selection the actual contribution? Would attribution patching suffice with no SAE at all?
  5. Do the discovered features transfer across MLLMs sharing a backbone (e.g., different LLaVA variants), turning per-model discovery into a reusable catalog?
