The first benchmark for research-level theorem proving finds frontier models solve only 68% of 300 TCS proofs.

TCS-BENCH: Benchmarking State-of-the-Art Generative AI Theoretical Computer Science Research Ability
Vincent Cohen-Addad, Dimitris Paparas, Ernest van Wijland · et al. (16 authors) · Google Research & DeepMind, CNRS/IRIF, Princeton · 2026-08-10
benchmarktheorem-provingtheoretical-csllm-evaluationproof-verification
Key metric · top score
68%
GPT 5.6 Pro on TCS-Bench — 204 / 300. A +37.7-pt gap over Gemini 3.1 Pro (30.3%).
Why this matters

Calibrates a difficulty-tunable probe of long-chain mathematical reasoning, and yields cross-model critique methods reusable for self-verifying proof agents.

Critical edge

A Gemini-family verifier grades its own sibling models while being handed the ground-truth proof — a built-in conflict on a leaderboard that ranks Gemini.

01Methodology

Built in four stages. (1) Acquisition — pull CC-0 / CC-BY-4.0 LaTeX from FOCS/STOC/SODA papers (2020–2026), dropping statements that invoke unstated external results. (2) Dependency DAG — a deterministic LaTeX parser IDs every theorem-like environment; an LLM pass maps proofs to the statements they prove, forming an acyclic graph (cycles rejected as extraction errors). A statement's rank is the longest dependency path into it. (3) Task construction — for a rank-r target, assemble context from reference digests plus text up to the proof, hide all rank-≥r statements and proof environments, re-insert missing dependencies, then compress to ≤10,000 tokens via iterative pruning and an LLM shortener. (4) Quality filtering applies structural and semantic checks. The verifier issues 4 Gemini 3.1 Flash calls per proof, accepting iff ≥3/4 agree, GEPA-calibrated on 100 human-labeled proofs to >90% accuracy.

03Strengths

  • First benchmark bridging competition math and genuine research-level TCS proof work.
  • Dependency-DAG masking yields an elegant, principled continuous difficulty spectrum from a single paper.
  • Strict self-containment contract (≤10K tokens, no external access) keeps tasks clean and shareable.
  • Honest reporting: admits the missing human baseline and exposes the 72.3% oracle ceiling.
  • Counter-intuitive, reusable finding — self-rejection beats self-acceptance; cross-model critique is directional.
  • Designed for longevity via continual post-cutoff paper addition and rank-based stratification.

04Limitations

  • Only 300 tasks across three venues — broad TCS, but not exhaustive.
  • Proprietary models only; no open weights tested; Colosseum internals undisclosed.
  • Verifier–solver family overlap plus ground-truth access create grading-bias risk.
  • No human baseline, so 68% is unanchored against a competent researcher.
  • Difficulty scaling is asserted, not quantified — no accuracy-vs-rank table.
  • Cross-model selection rule tuned on the test set (mild overfitting of the 67.7% figure).
  • TCS-only; generalization to pure math, physics, or other formal domains untested.

05Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does accuracy fall monotonically with target-statement rank or number of masked lemmas?
  2. Would an orthogonal, non-Google verifier (open-weight or other frontier model) reorder the leaderboard?
  3. What is a human TCS-expert baseline — is 68% near, at, or far below it, and how long does an expert take per task?
  4. How do open-weight reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-Math, Llama-math) perform, and does the self-rejection asymmetry generalize?
  5. Does a blind verifier (no ground-truth proof) change which models win?
  6. Does the 67.7% cross-model gain survive re-tuning on a held-out split?
  7. How contamination-resistant is TCS-Bench as new, highly-cited papers keep entering the pool?
