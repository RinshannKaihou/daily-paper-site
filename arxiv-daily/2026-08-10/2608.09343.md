> **一句话总结：** 该文提出用"事件级仿真轨迹"替代黑箱标量反馈来驱动 LLM 智能体改写可执行调度策略代码——manager 智能体回放得分最低的那次复现、从轨迹数据库中诊断瓶颈（强制充电、空驶、下游缓冲拥堵），多个 editing 智能体并行改代码，10 次重复仿真取均值做 best-so-far 择优；在三产线动态生产+AGV 调度 DES 中把初始规则策略从 62.49 提升到 78.61 分（0-100 制），在 100 个配对种子上逐一击败 rolling-MILP（62.97）、最优规则组合（62.62）和 GA/DE/PSO（63.22/63.58/63.46），中位差约 14-15 分。

> **TL;DR:** Instead of feeding an LLM only aggregate KPIs, this paper hands it a queryable event-level discrete-event-simulation trace — replayed from the *worst* of R=10 replications — so a manager agent can hypothesize *why* a scheduling policy fails and dispatch parallel editing agents to rewrite the policy code. On a three-line production + AGV scheduling DES (adapted from the SUPCON/FreezoneX smart-factory hackathon environment), the best Gemini-3.1-Pro run lifts the best-so-far mean score from 62.49 to 78.61 on a 0-100 scale (five-run mean 77.51), versus 62.97 for a two-stage rolling MILP, 62.62 for the best of 135 rule combinations, and 63.58 for the best metaheuristic (DE). On 100 matched seeds the LLM policy wins on *every* seed (median paired gaps 13.6-25.8 points, Holm-adjusted p ≈ 1.2e-17). Ablations: removing the trace database costs 1.35 pts for Gemini-3.1-Pro (77.51→76.16, ranges overlap) but 3.56 pts for GPT-5.4-mini (72.84→69.28); removing parallel candidates costs 3.86/6.62 pts and halves search length. Caveat the authors themselves flag: the LLM policies control proactive charging, which no baseline's decision space contains — so part of the ~15-point gap is a wider action space, not trace guidance.

## 核心贡献

- **把仿真器从"黑箱评分器"变成"可查询证据源"**：常规 SBO 只从仿真器取标量目标值，本文额外暴露事件级轨迹数据库（订单事件、资源状态变迁、队列/缓冲状态、AGV 任务与充电事件、故障与恢复、时变 KPI），由 manager 智能体按需查询而非把整库塞进上下文。
- **诊断性回放（diagnostic replay）机制**：每个候选跑 R=10 次计分复现，再用得分最低那次的随机输入 ξ_{r(π)} 做一次确定性回放生成轨迹；该回放的分数被明确排除在选择用的均值之外，避免重复计数。
- **manager–editor 多智能体循环**：manager 基于轨迹证据提出 3-4 条互异的修改方向，每条由一个独立上下文的 editing 智能体在隔离工作区并行实现；通过执行检查（失败最多 3 次修复）后重复评估，best-so-far 只保留改进。LLM 只在评估批次之间介入，单次仿真内策略固定，因而策略可审计、可版本化、无推理延迟。
- **完整的三族基线对照**：rolling-MILP（原始版与两阶段版）、135 组规则组合穷举、以及在 14 维规则权重单纯形上搜索的 GA/DE/PSO（带稳定性惩罚 λ=0.35 的适应度），并给出 100 配对种子的 Wilcoxon+Holm 检验、故障扰动下的免再优化测试、以及两种改变设置下的再优化测试。
- **组件消融**：分别关闭"并行候选生成"和"轨迹数据库访问"，在 Gemini-3.1-Pro 与 GPT-5.4-mini 两个骨干上各跑 5 次完整优化。

## Key Contributions

- **A trace-aware feedback interface for simulation-based optimization.** The evaluation operator returns a pair `(mean score, diagnostic evidence)`: the mean over R replications drives selection, while a queryable event-level trace drives *generation*. Prior LLM heuristic-design work (EoH, FunSearch, ReEvo, AlphaEvolve) conditions only on scores or textual reflections; Eureka uses reward-component statistics and OPTO uses a computational dependency graph — neither exposes DES event-level operations.
- **Worst-replication diagnostic replay.** Because a DES run is deterministic given (policy, stochastic input), replaying ξ from the lowest-scoring replication exactly reproduces it and yields the trace; its score is excluded from the selection mean.
- **Snapshot discipline for data-driven parameter estimation.** Each candidate gets a frozen copy of the incumbent's trace database for pre-run parameter estimation; all R scoring replications read the same snapshot, and only the diagnostic replay writes the trace retained for the next iteration — preventing within-batch leakage.
- **Independent-seed promotion.** Candidate evaluations use independent seed sets and the incumbent keeps its stored estimate rather than being re-scored on candidate seeds, so the search does not overfit one fixed realization set. A separate matched-seed re-evaluation (seeds 123-222) provides the headline comparison.
- **A case study with an explicitly enlarged action space**, covering line selection, transport-task ranking, AGV assignment, and proactive charging, evaluated under dynamic orders, finite buffers, re-entrant flow, quality rework, and optional random faults.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** Maximize J(π) = E_ξ[S(π; ξ)] over executable policies π, estimated by the sample mean of R=10 independent replications. `Evaluate_R(π)` returns both the mean score and τ(π), the diagnostic evidence bundle (queryable trace database + grouped KPI summaries). No decomposition of S is required — the framework is agnostic to how the simulator scores.

**Loop (Algorithm 1).** Starting from an initial rule-based policy π₀, at each iteration the manager reads the incumbent policy code, its mean score, retained trace evidence, and prior search history, then emits k_t ∈ [3,4] distinct revision plans. Each plan goes to a fresh-context editing agent (role, tools, environment description, trace schema, plan details) working in an isolated workspace. Candidates that pass load/runtime checks — with up to N_repair=3 repair-and-restart attempts — are evaluated with 10 scoring replications plus 1 diagnostic replay. The best candidate replaces the incumbent only if it strictly improves the stored mean. Stopping: T=10 iterations, target score S_tar=80, or C_stall=3 non-improving iterations. Manager capped at 20 execution steps, editors at 25.

**Simulator.** A three-line factory DES adapted from the SUPCON/FreezoneX 2025 smart-factory hackathon environment (workstations A/B/C, conveyors, upper/lower CQ buffers, quality-check station, raw-material and finished-goods warehouses, line-specific AGVs). The total score is a fixed weighted sum of eight normalized metrics: production efficiency 40% (on-time completion 16%, weighted production cycle 16%, equipment utilization 8%), quality & cost 30% (pass rate 12%, cost-benefit ratio 18%), AGV efficiency 30% (charging-strategy efficiency 9%, AGV energy efficiency 12%, AGV utilization 9%). Note that "charging-strategy efficiency" is literally the fraction of charging events that are *active* rather than *forced* — a metric the policy can move directly. Default horizon T_sim=500 min (justified in Appendix A.5: the score curve plateaus after ~500 min, with a ±2-point 5-95% band over 1000 replications); orders arrive every 10 min.

**Baselines.** (1) Rolling MILP over the AGV transport subproblem, re-solved when new tasks or rework appear, with a 2 s solver limit and a greedy fallback on any non-optimal status; a two-stage variant (greedy line assignment, then per-line MILP) is used throughout because it beats the monolithic one. Battery/charging constraints are excluded from the MILP. (2) 135 rule combinations (5 line-selection × 9 task-ranking × 3 AGV-assignment rules), each run 100 replications. (3) GA / DE(best/1/bin) / PSO over a 14-dimensional simplex of rule weights, initial population = all 64 one-hot rule combinations, 10 generations, 20 replications per candidate, fitness = mean − 0.35·std.

## Results & Highlights

- **Default setting, headline comparison.** Best Gemini-3.1-Pro run reaches **78.61** mean total score vs **62.97** for the two-stage rolling MILP (+15.6 pts), **62.62** for the best of 135 rule combinations (DEFAULT/LWKR/NVF), and **63.58** for the best metaheuristic (DE; GA 63.22, PSO 63.46). Averaged across five independent runs the framework still reaches **77.51** — i.e. the *worst* of five LLM runs (min 76.10) beats the *best* baseline by ~12.5 pts.
- **Matched-seed re-evaluation (100 seeds, 123-222).** The LLM policy scores higher on **all 100 seeds in all 12 comparisons**, so W⁻ = 0, asymptotic two-sided p = 3.90e-18, Holm-adjusted 1.17e-17. Median paired gaps: default 14.45 (heuristic) / 14.36 (MILP) / 15.11 (metaheuristic); under random faults 14.25 / 14.57 / 14.02; at T_sim=3000 min 19.50 / 25.79 / 14.18; under U(5,15)-min interarrivals 13.82 / 13.56 / 13.85.
- **Optimization trajectory (RQ2).** Best-so-far mean rises 62.49 → 70.28 (iter 1, after trace-diagnosed proactive charging for idle low-battery AGVs + nearby-task preference) → 75.98 (iter 5, after prioritizing tasks that clear downstream buffers) → **78.61** (iter 8, after rebalancing toward upstream feeding because the policy had over-corrected into upstream starvation). That is +25.8% over the initial policy; iterations 9-10 produced no further improvement.
- **Changed settings with re-optimization.** At T_sim=3000 min the framework averages **74.16** (range 72.37-78.16 over five runs) vs GA 63.06 / PSO 62.96 / DE 62.91 / best heuristic 57.67 / MILP **50.03** — the MILP degrades badly over the long horizon (−12.9 pts vs its 500-min score), which is where the largest median gap (25.79) comes from. Under U(5,15)-min interarrivals the framework averages **76.34** (74.71-78.97) vs GA 63.42 / DE 63.15 / PSO 62.82 / heuristic 62.72 / MILP 62.71.
- **Fault robustness without re-optimization.** With per-line fault generators (inter-fault U(80,120) min, recovery U(20,60) min) applied *after* optimization, the frozen default policy keeps a ~14-point median advantage over all three baseline families, all significant at p ≤ 1e-4.
- **Ablations (Table 2).** Removing the trace database: Gemini-3.1-Pro 77.51 → **76.16** (−1.35, run ranges overlap: 76.10-78.61 vs 72.27-77.61); GPT-5.4-mini 72.84 → **69.28** (−3.56, non-overlapping means, ranges 70.94-75.80 vs 66.26-71.84). Removing parallel candidates: Gemini 77.51 → **73.65**, min collapses from 76.10 to **62.36** (i.e. one run never improved on the 62.49 initial policy) and average iterations drop 9.2 → 5.8; GPT-5.4-mini 72.84 → **66.22**, min 70.94 → 62.29, iterations 8.0 → 4.6. Notably, the single-candidate *best* run still reaches 78.56 ≈ 78.61 — parallelism buys reliability, not peak quality.
- **Backbone spread (Figure 3).** Five-run mean final scores: Gemini-3.1-Pro 77.51 > GPT-5.5 76.13 > GLM-5 73.83 > GPT-5.4-mini 72.84. Even the cheapest backbone beats every classical baseline by ~9 pts.

## Authors & Credibility

Jinbo Li and Chuanhao Li (corresponding, chuanhao-li@tsinghua.edu.cn), Department of Industrial Engineering, Tsinghua University — a group with a visible line of work in this space (they cite their own Chen et al. 2026 on DEVS-formalism specification-driven generation of discrete-event world models). The related-work coverage is genuinely well-calibrated: it separates the classical AGV/job-shop scheduling literature (Bilge & Ulusoy 1995, Han et al. 2024), the SBO literature (Fu 2002, Amaran et al. 2016, simheuristics), and the LLM heuristic-design line (EoH, FunSearch, ReEvo, AlphaEvolve, Eureka, OPTO), and states precisely how the feedback interface differs from each. The closest competitor, May et al. (2026)'s actor-critic LLM dispatching-rule generator with DES evaluation, is named and differentiated rather than buried. Appendices are unusually complete for an arXiv preprint: full MILP formulation, rule tables, metaheuristic hyperparameters, metric weights, facility parameters, and the exact statistical protocol.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-good).** The core idea — condition LLM code revision on structured event-level traces rather than a scalar — is a natural but genuinely under-explored extension of the EoH/ReEvo/Eureka family, and the worst-replication diagnostic-replay trick (deterministic re-execution of the failing seed, with the replay score excluded from the selection mean) is a clean, transferable piece of engineering. It is not a new optimization algorithm: the outer loop is a (1+k) hill-climber with best-so-far selection, which the authors honestly frame as a metaheuristic whose variation operator acts on code.

**Rigor (mixed, but with real strengths).** The matched-seed Wilcoxon protocol with Holm correction, independent seed sets for promotion, and the frozen-snapshot rule for parameter estimation are all above the norm for LLM-agent papers. Two specific problems remain:

1. **The action-space confound is acknowledged but never resolved.** None of the baseline decision spaces includes charging control — the 135 rule combinations cover only line selection / task ranking / AGV assignment, and the MILP explicitly drops battery and charging constraints (Appendix B.1, assumption three). Meanwhile *9% of the total score is "charging-strategy efficiency," defined as the share of active vs. forced charging events*, and the very first LLM revision adds proactive charging for a +7.8-point jump. So a large, quantifiable slice of the ~15-point gap is available to any method allowed to touch charging. The authors point to the ablations as the control, but the no-database ablation still keeps charging control — it isolates trace evidence, not action space. The honest missing baseline is a rule/metaheuristic search over a rule space *extended with a charging threshold parameter*; without it the headline "beats MILP and metaheuristics on every seed" is partly a comparison between different problems.
2. **The trace-database ablation is confounded by design and weak for the strong model.** Disabling the database removes both the manager's diagnostic evidence *and* the policy's data-driven parameter estimation; the paper says so explicitly, but that means the −1.35-point Gemini effect cannot be attributed to trace diagnosis at all. And with n=5 runs and overlapping ranges (76.10-78.61 vs 72.27-77.61), the Gemini result is not statistically distinguishable — the only convincing trace effect is on GPT-5.4-mini (−3.56). The paper's framing ("consistent direction of the decline") is appropriately hedged, but the abstract's flat "removing … trace-database access reduced final mean scores" is stronger than the Gemini evidence supports.

**Missing ablations.** The single most load-bearing design choice — selecting the *lowest-scoring* replication for the diagnostic trace — is never ablated against random-replication, median-replication, or multi-replication-aggregate selection. The authors list this as a limitation, which is honest, but it is the paper's named contribution. Similarly absent: a scalar-reflection control (ReEvo-style textual reflection without traces) that would separate "richer text feedback" from "trace-grounded evidence"; and any cost accounting — LLM token/compute cost is never reported, even though the sim budgets are wildly asymmetric in the framework's favor (roughly 10 iters × 3.5 candidates × 11 runs ≈ 400 simulation runs, versus 64 × 10 × 20 = 12,800 for each metaheuristic). That asymmetry actually flatters the framework's sample efficiency and deserved a plot.

**Reproducibility (weak).** No code, prompts, or trace-schema dumps are released; the underlying simulator is a hackathon repo (25-AdventureX-SUPCON-Hackathon) that the authors then "instrumented" in unspecified ways, so even the evaluation target is not reconstructible. Two of the four backbones (Gemini-3.1-Pro, GPT-5.5) are proprietary and versioned only by name; no temperature, seed, or token settings are given. Manager/editor prompts are described only in prose.

**Significance (solid within scope, N=1 environment).** All evidence comes from a single DES model with three parameter variations. The discussion names warehouse logistics, port operations, and intra-hospital logistics as plausible transfers, but nothing is tested there. For OR/simulation practitioners the result is a credible existence proof that trace-grounded code revision beats fixed-dimensional rule search; for the LLM-agent community the contribution is the feedback interface, not the benchmark.

**Red flags (minor).** The abstract's "outscored representative rolling-MILP, rule-based, and metaheuristic policies on every seed" is true as stated but leans on a MILP that is a deliberately tractable abstraction (single-product tasks, no battery, 2 s solver cap with greedy fallback, optimistic inspection-pass assumption) — a weak-by-construction optimality baseline. The 62.49 initial policy is exactly the DEFAULT/DEFAULT/DEFAULT rule combination (62.33 in Table 13), so "improvement of approximately 25.8%" is measured from a deliberately mediocre start. GLM-5 and GPT-5.5 appear only in Figure 3 and are excluded from the ablations, so cross-backbone claims rest on two models.

## Strengths

- The evaluation protocol is the best part of the paper: independent seed sets for promotion, the incumbent keeping its stored estimate (avoiding re-evaluation bias toward the incumbent), a separate frozen matched-seed re-evaluation on 100 seeds, and paired non-parametric tests with multiplicity correction. Many LLM-for-optimization papers report a single run against a single seed.
- The horizon choice T_sim=500 min is empirically justified (Figure 8: 1000 replications, score plateaus after ~500 min, ±2-point 5-95% band) rather than picked for convenience.
- The qualitative trajectory in Section 4.3 is a genuinely informative narrative — forced charging → empty travel → downstream buffer clearing → *over-correction into upstream starvation* → rebalance. The fact that the agent diagnosed and then repaired its own over-correction is the strongest evidence in the paper that traces carry causal information a scalar cannot.
- The parallel-candidate ablation's finding that peak quality is preserved (78.56 vs 78.61) while variance explodes (min 62.36) and search length halves is a subtle, well-explained mechanism: with one plan per iteration, an unproductive candidate triggers the stall counter.
- Limitations section is specific and self-aware (no structured memory of past revisions, single-incumbent search without branch diversity, unvalidated trace-selection strategy) rather than boilerplate.
- Appendices actually enable partial replication of the *baselines* (full MILP formulation, GA/DE/PSO hyperparameters, all 135-combination top-10 results) even though the framework itself is not released.

## Limitations

- Single simulation environment; generalization to warehouse/port/hospital logistics is asserted, not tested.
- No code, prompts, or instrumented simulator released; proprietary backbones with unstated decoding settings.
- Trace-selection strategy (worst replication) is unablated, as are alternative promotion criteria.
- The no-database ablation conflates trace-based diagnosis with data-driven parameter estimation.
- Baselines cannot control charging, while 9% of the score directly rewards active-vs-forced charging.
- Search maintains a single incumbent — no population or branch diversity; five runs is a small sample for the min/max intervals reported.
- Optimization history is retained only as raw context, not distilled into structured memory of what worked.
- No LLM cost/latency accounting, and no wall-clock comparison against the metaheuristics' 12,800 simulation runs.
- T=10 iterations with target score 80: the best run stopped at 78.61 without hitting the target, so the ceiling of the method is unknown.

## Relevance

这篇文章对关注 **LLM-as-optimizer / 代码级策略搜索**（EoH、FunSearch、ReEvo、AlphaEvolve、Eureka 一线）的读者很有价值：它把"反馈富化"这一维度从标量分数、文本反思推进到结构化事件轨迹，并给出了一个可迁移的工程原语——最差复现的确定性回放 + 回放分数排除在选择均值之外 + 快照式参数估计防泄漏。同时对做 **多智能体系统评测与消融设计**的人是一个正面样本（配对种子 Wilcoxon + Holm、独立种子晋升）与一个反面教材（消融把轨迹诊断和参数估计混在一起、基线动作空间比方法窄）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does worst-replication trace selection actually beat random or median-replication selection? If the gain comes mainly from *any* trace rather than the *failing* trace, the paper's central mechanism claim weakens considerably.
- What happens with a charging-augmented rule space for the metaheuristic baselines — how much of the ~15-point median gap survives once the action spaces are matched?
- Can the trace-based diagnosis be separated from the data-driven parameter estimation in the ablation, e.g. by keeping the estimator but blinding the manager to the trace?
- How does the framework compare against a ReEvo-style textual-reflection controller on the same simulator and the same ~400-run simulation budget? That is the correct apples-to-apples LLM baseline and it is absent.
- What is the token/dollar cost per point of score improvement, and how does it scale with T_sim=3000 (where each replication is 6× longer)?
- Does the trace advantage grow or shrink with weaker backbones? The GPT-5.4-mini result (−3.56 without traces vs −1.35 for Gemini) hints that traces substitute for model capability — a testable scaling claim worth a dedicated study.
- Would a population/branching variant (retaining several promising policies, as the authors suggest) break the 78.61 plateau, or is the ceiling set by the simulator's score decomposition?
- Do the discovered policies transfer to a *structurally* different factory layout (four lines, different buffer topology) without re-optimization, or only across horizon/arrival-rate changes?
