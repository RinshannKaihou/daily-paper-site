> **一句话总结：** 在 MedMCQA 的 500 道题上系统评测五个 LLM（GPT-5/GPT-4o/GPT-4o-mini/LLaMA-3-8B/Mistral-7B）发现，随着提示中加入不确定性线索或正确答案被移除，模型准确率大致不变但置信度持续偏高；当正确选项被删除时，模型极少弃答（最高仅 38.2%）而以高置信度编造答案，Mistral-7B 的 ECE/UCER 高达约 0.76–0.49，LLaMA-3 在弃答设置下的 UCER 更飙升至 0.828。

> **TL;DR:** On 500 MedMCQA questions, the authors probe GPT-5, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 with graded linguistic uncertainty cues (C0–C3) and an answer-removal setting where the gold option is replaced by an "Insufficient information" choice. Accuracy barely moves under uncertainty framing (e.g., GPT-5 stays 0.828→0.836) yet confidence stays high, so ECE/UCER barely improve; when the correct answer is removed the best model abstains only 38.2% of the time (GPT-4o) and GPT-5 hallucinates in 80.8% of cases, with LLaMA-3 reaching a UCER of 0.828 — evidence that current LLM confidence is decoupled from epistemic uncertainty and unsafe for clinical deployment.

## 核心贡献

- 设计了双设置评测框架：(i) 不确定性框架化（Uncertainty Framing，C0–C3 四档渐进式语言提示，从无提示到"信息可能不足以确诊"），(ii) 答案移除设置（移除正确选项并替换为"信息不足无法确定"弃答选项），从而把"提示层模糊性"与"信息显式缺失"两类不确定性解耦评估。
- 在五个模型（GPT-5、GPT-4o、GPT-4o-mini、LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct、Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3）上同时测量准确率、自报告置信度、logit 置信度、calibration gap、ECE、UCER、弃答率、幻觉率与"不安全预测率"，构成多维度可靠性画像。
- 揭示系统性失效模式：准确率随不确定性变化很小，但置信度几乎不随之下降；正确答案被移除时模型仍偏向强行作答而非弃答，且幻觉答案常伴随高置信度——表明准确率与置信度之间存在"解耦"。
- 提供严格统计分析：使用 McNemar 检验比较准确率、Wilcoxon 符号秩检验比较置信度，并施加 Holm–Bonferroni 多重比较校正，使"闭源 vs 开源"等差异具备统计显著性背书。

## Key Contributions

- A **dual-setting evaluation framework** for clinical-LLM reliability: (a) *uncertainty framing* with four graded linguistic cues (C0–C3, from no statement to "the information may be insufficient for a fully certain diagnosis"), and (b) *answer removal*, where the gold option is replaced by an explicit "Insufficient information to determine the correct answer" abstention choice. The candidate options and label are kept fixed across framing levels so that the only varying factor is the uncertainty signal.
- A **multi-metric behavioral probe** applied uniformly to five models spanning proprietary (GPT-5, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini) and open-weight (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) families: accuracy, self-reported confidence, logit-based confidence, calibration gap, ECE, UCER (Unsafe Confident Error Rate), abstention rate, hallucination rate, and an "unsafe rate" (incorrect with confidence > 0.8).
- A **systematic failure-mode finding**: under both settings, confidence stays high while accuracy is largely unchanged (Setting A) or collapses (Setting B), so the confidence–accuracy gap widens; when the correct option is removed, models exhibit a "forced-answering" bias, rarely abstaining and frequently emitting high-confidence hallucinated answers.
- A **statistically grounded comparison**: McNemar tests for accuracy, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for confidence, both with Holm–Bonferroni correction (α = 0.05), establishing which inter-model differences are robust.

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** Given a medical multiple-choice dataset D = {(xᵢ, Aᵢ, yᵢ)}, a model f_θ outputs a predicted answer ŷᵢ ∈ Aᵢ and a confidence cᵢ ∈ [0,1]. Calibration is checked against the identity P(ŷᵢ = yᵢ | cᵢ = p) = p.

**Setting A — Uncertainty framing.** Four prompt variants xᵢ^(k) = gₖ(xᵢ) inject graded ambiguity (C0: none; C1: "Some clinical details may be incomplete"; C2: warning of ambiguous/missing info; C3: "information may be insufficient for a fully certain diagnosis"). Crucially, the answer options Aᵢ and ground truth yᵢ are unchanged across C0–C3, isolating the effect of the linguistic cue.

**Setting B — Answer removal.** The gold option is removed and replaced with an abstention option: A′ᵢ = (Aᵢ \ {yᵢ}) ∪ {a_insufficient}. Selecting a_insufficient counts as abstention; any other choice is a *hallucinated prediction* (the correct answer is no longer present). The position of the abstention option is randomized per question to neutralize positional bias.

**Confidence signals.** Two complementary measures are collected: (i) *self-reported* cᵢ (an explicit numerical score elicited via a structured prompt), and (ii) *log-probability* p_ŷᵢ computed by softmax over the token logits for the option labels A/B/C/D at the decoding step. GPT-5's logits are unavailable via API, so only its self-reported confidence is used.

**Metrics.** ECE over M=10 equal-width bins; calibration gap (mean confidence − accuracy, signed); UCER_τ = (1/n)·Σ 𝟙[ŷᵢ ≠ yᵢ ∧ cᵢ ≥ τ] with τ the high-confidence threshold; abstention and hallucination rates; "unsafe rate" = incorrect answers with confidence > 0.8. Inference is deterministic (temperature = 0); open-weight models run in 4-bit quantization on an NVIDIA T4.

**Data.** 500 single-answer 4-option questions randomly sampled from MedMCQA (Indian medical entrance exams) with a fixed random seed, yielding 2,000 instances for Setting A (500 × 4 framing conditions) plus 500 for Setting B.

## Results & Highlights

- **Uncertainty framing barely changes accuracy but confidence barely drops either (Table II).** GPT-5 stays the most accurate model across C0→C3 at 0.828 → 0.836 while its self-reported confidence is actually *lower* than accuracy (negative calibration gaps, −0.019 to −0.038) and its UCER stays tiny (0.028–0.034). In stark contrast, Mistral-7B's accuracy is only ≈0.496–0.504 yet it reports confidence ≈0.971–0.987, giving ECE ≈ 0.473–0.489 and UCER ≈ 0.488–0.498 — essentially half of its answers are confident errors.
- **Open-weight models are badly miscalibrated under framing (Table II).** LLaMA-3-8B reaches moderate accuracy (0.750–0.774) but reports confidence ≈0.895–0.933, with ECE 0.148–0.171 and UCER 0.212–0.226; GPT-4o (ECE 0.079–0.119) and GPT-4o-mini (ECE 0.175–0.193) sit between the two extremes. Importantly, *strengthening the uncertainty cue from C0 to C3 does not meaningfully reduce confidence in any model* — they fail to internalize the prompt-level signal.
- **When the correct answer is removed, abstention is uniformly poor (Table IV).** The best abstention rate is only 0.382 (GPT-4o), with GPT-4o-mini at 0.368, Mistral-7B at 0.238, GPT-5 at just 0.192, and LLaMA-3 lowest at 0.156. Hallucination rates are correspondingly high, peaking at 0.808 for GPT-5 (i.e., it confidently picks a wrong option in ~81% of cases) and 0.798 for LLaMA-3.
- **Self-reported confidence massively overshoots logit-based belief (Table III).** In Setting B, LLaMA-3 reports mean confidence 0.970 while its logit-based confidence is only 0.280 — a gap of 0.814; Mistral-7B reports 0.998 against logit 0.657 (gap 0.760). GPT-4o (self 0.863 vs logit 0.883) and GPT-4o-mini (0.871 vs 0.913) are far better aligned. This shows the verbalized number is not a reliable readout of internal uncertainty.
- **UCER — the safety metric — is alarming in Setting B (Table III).** LLaMA-3 reaches UCER 0.828 and ECE 0.814, Mistral-7B 0.760/0.760, GPT-4o-mini 0.491, GPT-4o 0.486, and GPT-5 0.600 (with its low UCER of 0.186 only because its overall confidence is lower, not because it abstains — it does the opposite).
- **Statistical significance (Table V).** GPT-4o vs GPT-4o-mini is *not* significant (p = 0.603), and GPT-5 vs LLaMA-3 (p = 0.133) and GPT-5 vs Mistral-7B (p = 0.077) are not significant after Holm–Bonferroni; but GPT-4o vs GPT-5 (p < 0.001), GPT-4o vs open-weight models (p < 0.001), and LLaMA-3 vs Mistral-7B (p = 0.002) are robust.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from the School of Computing, Macquarie University (Sydney), a credible NLP/services group; the work was supported by Macquarie's iRTP and iMQRES scholarships. The methodology is reproducible in spirit (fixed random seed, temperature 0, public dataset, named model versions and quantization), and the paper self-reports its own limitations rather than overselling. The framing is a behavioral/empirical analysis rather than a new model, so credibility rests on experimental design and statistical rigor — both of which are above average (paired tests with multiple-comparison correction). No external clinical validation or clinician review is involved, which is appropriate for a model-behavior study but limits real-world safety claims.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate.** The finding that LLMs are overconfident in medical QA is well established (the authors cite [12] Bentegeac et al. 2025 and [15] Groot & Valdenegro-Toro 2024). The genuine contribution is the *specific* decomposition into uncertainty framing (C0–C3) vs answer-removal, and the simultaneous measurement of self-reported *and* logit-based confidence — the LLaMA gap of 0.970 vs 0.280 is a concrete, citable data point.
- **Rigor: mixed.** Statistics are properly handled (McNemar/Wilcoxon + Holm–Bonferroni, α=0.05), and the fixed-seed 500-sample design is reproducible. However the **sample size (n=500, a single random draw from MedMCQA) is small** and no bootstrapped confidence intervals are reported on ECE/UCER/gap — only on accuracy via McNemar. The headline ECE numbers (e.g., 0.486 vs 0.814) could be noisy at this n.
- **Abstract vs body inconsistency.** The abstract claims "although accuracy degrades under increasing uncertainty, model confidence remains misaligned," but Table II and the body text state accuracy is *stable* across C0–C3 ("accuracy remains relatively stable across uncertainty conditions for all models"). The real story is "accuracy flat, confidence flat-too-high" — the abstract's "degrades" framing overstates the framing effect.
- **Missing ablations.** No analysis of *why* uncertainty framing fails: no test of whether the cues are even read (e.g., attention/answer-distribution shifts), no comparison of cue phrasings, no test of whether chain-of-thought or confidence-elicitation prompting (the very methods cited [14] Tian et al.) would change the picture. The "unsafe threshold" τ=0.8 is asserted without sensitivity analysis.
- **Weak baseline framing.** The answer-removal "abstention" baseline is just one option among four; there is no comparison to principled abstention methods (e.g., calibrated thresholds, ensemble disagreement [16][17], or the abstention benchmark [18] the authors cite), so "models can't abstain" is shown only against a naive single-shot prompt, not against the best available abstention technique.
- **Data quirk.** MedMCQA is Indian medical-entrance exam material with heavily factual recall questions; the authors themselves flag that generalization to "real clinical decision-making" is limited. The overconfidence finding may be amplified or attenuated on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (e.g., MedQA-USMLE clinical-vignette style), which are not tested.
- **Reproducibility: good but incomplete.** Random seed, temperature, model versions, quantization, and the abstention-option randomization are specified; but the exact 500-question subset, the four framing strings (Table I gives paraphrases), and the prompt template (Fig. 3 is a figure, not text) are not released as artifacts, making exact replication hard.
- **Significance: clinically relevant but interpretive.** The core safety message (do not trust self-reported confidence from open-weight models in medicine) is well-supported and actionable; the GPT-5 finding that lower overall confidence buys lower UCER even at high hallucination rate is a useful design insight.

## Strengths

- Clean two-axis experimental design (linguistic ambiguity × information absence) that cleanly separates two failure modes.
- Reports both self-reported *and* logit-based confidence, exposing the verbalization/logit gap that single-signal studies miss.
- Strong safety-oriented metric (UCER) that directly counts "confident wrong" answers, more decision-relevant than accuracy or ECE alone.
- Proper paired statistics with Holm–Bonferroni correction — rare in this kind of LLM behavioral paper.
- Honest, specific limitations section (single dataset, synthetic cue-based uncertainty, fixed model set, multiple-choice-only).
- Deterministic protocol (temperature 0, fixed seed, randomized abstention position) that controls obvious confounds.

## Limitations

- **Small, single-dataset sample (n=500 from MedMCQA)** — no bootstrapped CIs on ECE/UCER/gap; effects could be sample-dependent.
- **Synthetic uncertainty:** the C0–C3 cues are injected via prompt engineering rather than drawn from genuinely ambiguous real clinical notes, so external validity to real patient data is untested.
- **No principled abstention baselines** — only a naive single-shot "pick the insufficient-information option" prompt; abstention methods from [16]–[18] are cited but not compared.
- **Multiple-choice only** — no free-form, open-ended reasoning, interactive, or multimodal clinical settings.
- **Fixed model set** (5 models, snapshot in time); no instruction-tuned variants, no medical-specialized models (e.g., Med-PaLM, Meditron), no newer reasoning models.
- **No threshold sensitivity** for τ in UCER or for the "unsafe" confidence cutoff of 0.8.
- **GPT-5 logit confidence unavailable** (API limitation), so its logit-vs-verbalized comparison is missing.
- **Abstract/body mismatch** on whether accuracy "degrades" under framing.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"评估 LLM 在医学场景下的可靠性/可信度"高度相关：它直接量化了模型置信度与真实正确率之间的解耦，并用 UCER 等"不安全预测率"指标揭示当前模型在信息缺失时的危险行为，对研究 LLM 校准、不确定性估计与医疗 AI 安全部署的工作有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the overconfidence/UCER findings hold on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (MedQA-USMLE, PubMedQA) or on free-form clinical-vignette generation rather than 4-option MCQ?
- Can cheap post-hoc interventions — chain-of-thought, self-consistency [17], verbalized-confidence elicitation [14], or token-probability calibration [12] — actually move the Setting-B abstention rate above ~40%, or do they merely redistribute errors?
- Is the self-reported-vs-logit gap (LLaMA 0.970 vs 0.280) a general property of RLHF/instruction-tuned open-weight models, and does it shrink with scale, base vs instruct, or different decoding temperatures?
- How sensitive are the UCER rankings to the high-confidence threshold τ (0.8 here) — does GPT-5's favorable safety profile survive at τ = 0.6 or 0.9?
- Does fine-tuning on an explicit "abstain when uncertain" objective (with calibration-aware loss) transfer to the answer-removal setting, or does the forced-answering bias re-emerge on out-of-distribution medical content?
- Would including medical-specialized LLMs (Meditron, Med-PaLM-2, BioMistral) change the proprietary-vs-open-weight calibration gap story?
