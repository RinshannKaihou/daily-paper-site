> **一句话总结：** 本文证明双曲多模态表征要避免遗忘，等价于所有模态共享同一个双曲等距变换（旋转），据此推导出对 Lorentz 线性层空间分量做正交投影、并冻结时间分量（block-wise 约束）的闭式更新规则 HMCL；在 15 个任务的连续多模态流上，MERU-L 的整体 BWT 从 −3.49 改善到 −1.07、分类 BWT 从 −6.46 改善到 −0.74（遗忘减少 88.5%），HyCoCLIP-B 的 image-to-text R@1 从 45.34 提升到 57.55。

> **TL;DR:** HMCL casts forgetting in hyperbolic vision-language models as violation of a *shared Lorentz isometry* across modalities, and enforces it with a zero-parameter closed-form update: project the space-like block of the Lorentz linear head onto the orthogonal complement of the old-task representation subspace (∆Wₛ = δₛ(I − VVᵀ)) and set the time-like update to zero. On a 15-task stream (CIFAR-10/100, Food-101, Caltech-101, ..., COCO, Flickr30K) with three frozen hyperbolic backbones, HMCL beats sequential fine-tuning (Vanilla) and EWC/GEM/C-FLAT: on MERU-L overall performance 41.36 vs 38.22 (Vanilla, +8.2% rel.) with overall BWT −1.07 vs −3.49; on HyCoCLIP-B image-to-text R@1 57.55 vs 45.34 (+12.2 pts) and BWT −0.43 vs −5.42. Worth reading if you care about geometry-aware continual learning; the theory is clean but the method is essentially GPM/Adam-NSCL-style orthogonal projection ported to Lorentz space, and the closest Euclidean projection baselines are never run.

## 核心贡献

- **首个双曲多模态连续学习的几何刻画。** 提出三条保持条件 (P1) 模态内 Gram 矩阵不变、(P2) 跨模态 Gram 矩阵不变、(P3) 空间分量范数（即到原点的双曲半径，编码语义层级）不变；并证明 Theorem 1：在非退化假设下，(P1)–(P3) 同时成立 **当且仅当** 存在一个所有模态**共享**的分块旋转 R = diag(1, R̃)，R̃ ∈ SO(d)，使得 Z^{m,t}_{t−1} = Z^{m,t−1}_{t−1} Rᵀ。
- **一阶层级保持条件。** Corollary 1：若要求时间分量一阶不变，则空间更新必须与当前嵌入的空间方向正交，z⊤_{[1:d]} ∆z_{[1:d]} = 0，即容许更新被限制在切空间内。
- **从几何条件到参数更新的闭式解。** Proposition 1 给出容许更新的充要条件（存在反对称 Ω 使 Z^{m,*}_{t−1}[1:d] ∆Wₛᵀ = Z^{m,t−1}_{t−1}[1:d] Ωᵀ）；Corollary 2 给出典范解 ∆Wₛ = δₛ(I − P_{t−1})，配合分块约束 ∆w₀ = 0。
- **HMCL 算法：零额外参数、O(d²) 常数内存。** 通过 SVD/PCA 得到旧任务空间表征的低秩正交基 V，每阶段只计算一次；只维护一个 d×d 协方差摘要而非每个历史任务的基，内存与任务数无关。
- **把"遗忘"分解为可测量的几何漂移。** 定义 radial / angular / cross-modal / paired-distance 四种漂移，分别对应 (P3)/(P1)/(P2)/(P2+P3)，为"遗忘到底破坏了什么几何量"提供了可证伪的度量。

## Key Contributions

- **Necessary-and-sufficient geometric condition for representation preservation in Lorentz space.** Preservation of intra-modal, inter-modal, and hierarchical structure holds iff a *single shared* rotation R = diag(1, R̃), R̃ ∈ SO(d), acts identically on every modality's old-task embeddings. This is a sharper statement than "keep the loss small": it says cross-modal preservation is a rigidity condition, not a regularization target.
- **First-order hierarchy constraint.** Requiring the time-like coordinate (which encodes abstraction level via entailment-cone aperture) to be first-order invariant forces the spatial update to be orthogonal to the embedding's spatial direction — updates must live in the tangent space.
- **Closed-form canonical update (Corollary 2).** ∆Wₛᵐ = δₛᵐ(I − P_{t−1}) with P_{t−1} the orthogonal projector onto the old-task spatial subspace, plus a block-wise constraint ∆w₀ᵐ = 0 that freezes the time-like row of the Lorentz linear layer.
- **HMCL, a drop-in training procedure.** No new learnable parameters; projection basis V computed once per stage from a fixed-size d×d covariance summary, so memory is constant in the number of tasks. Backbones stay frozen; only a single bias-free linear Lorentz head per modality is trained.
- **A drift taxonomy that operationalizes the theory.** Four drift metrics map one-to-one onto (P1)–(P3), letting the paper test *why* forgetting happens rather than only *that* it is reduced.

## Methodology

**Setup.** M modalities pass through frozen pretrained encoders Eₘ, then per-modality learnable Lorentz transformation layers T⁽ᵗ⁾ₘ: f(W; z) = [√(K⁻¹ + ‖Wz‖²); Wz], with W = [w₀ Wₛ] split into a time-like row and a space-like block, curvature fixed at K = 0.1. Training uses the symmetric hyperbolic contrastive loss (similarity = negative geodesic distance) plus the Ganea entailment-cone loss with weight 0.2. Tasks arrive sequentially with no replay.

**Theory chain.** (i) State preservation as three Gram/norm invariants (P1)–(P3). (ii) Theorem 1 converts them into "one shared SO(d) rotation," using a Lorentzian analogue of Witt's extension theorem (Appendix E.1) — non-degeneracy is stated as an *identifiability* condition, not an algorithmic requirement. (iii) Corollary 1 linearizes (P3) into an orthogonality constraint on ∆z. (iv) Proposition 1 lifts this from representations to parameters: ∆Wₛ is admissible iff its action on old-task spatial features equals an infinitesimal rotation (skew-symmetric Ω). (v) Corollary 2 picks the canonical (minimum-interference) solution: orthogonal projection away from the old-task row space.

**Algorithm 1 (HMCL).** Compute low-rank orthonormal basis V of the old-task spatial representations Z^{m,*}_{t−1,s} via SVD/PCA; project the unconstrained optimizer direction, ∆Wₛᵐ = δₛᵐ(I − VVᵀ); set ∆w₀ᵐ = 0; update Wₛ^{m,t} = Wₛ^{m,t−1} − η∆Wₛᵐ, leaving w₀ untouched. Retained-variance ratio of the projector is tuned per backbone (0.9 for MERU-L, 0.8 for MERU-B, 0.9 for HyCoCLIP-B).

**Finite-step correction ("step pullback").** Because the theory is first-order and AdamW takes finite steps, the authors snapshot the head W_{t,0} at the start of each task and after every projected optimizer step apply W ← (1 − β)W + βW_{t,0}, with β = 0.10 (MERU-L/B) and 0.20 (HyCoCLIP-B). Gradients are accumulated over an epoch, so this is one step per epoch. This heuristic sits outside the theory and is applied only to the shared bias-free head.

**Benchmark.** 15 datasets interleaved into one fixed stream: 13 image–text classification tasks (CIFAR-10/100, Caltech-101, Food-101, Flowers-102, EuroSAT, DTD, CLEVR, FGVC-Aircraft, MNIST, PCAM, SST-2, Country211) and 2 retrieval tasks (COCO, Flickr30K). Metrics: average accuracy, Recall@K, and signed BWT = final − right-after-task (negative = forgetting), averaged over 5 seeds. Baselines: Vanilla sequential fine-tuning, EWC, GEM, C-FLAT — all re-run under the *same* hyperbolic contrastive objective, backbone, optimizer, and schedule, so only the update mechanism differs. Hardware: a single RTX A6000.

## Results & Highlights

- **MERU-L, overall stream metric:** HMCL 41.36 ± 0.10 vs Vanilla 38.22 ± 0.18 (+3.14 pts, +8.2% rel.); the strongest non-Vanilla baseline GEM reaches only 38.25 ± 0.14, i.e. +0.03 over doing nothing. Overall BWT: −1.07 (HMCL) vs −3.49 (Vanilla) and −6.02/−6.04 for GEM/EWC — **both EWC and GEM are strictly worse than naive fine-tuning on overall BWT**, which is the single most informative number in Table 1.
- **MERU-L, classification forgetting:** BWT_A improves from −6.46 ± 0.23 (Vanilla) to −0.74 ± 0.08 (HMCL), an 88.5% relative reduction; C-FLAT, the strongest dedicated continual-VLM baseline, only reaches −6.23 while *losing* accuracy (39.28 vs 40.59 Vanilla).
- **HyCoCLIP-B retrieval:** image-to-text R@1 57.55 ± 0.31 vs 45.34 ± 0.35 (Vanilla) = +12.21 pts (+26.9% rel.); retrieval BWT −0.70 vs −5.46, and overall BWT −0.43 vs −5.42 (+92.1% rel.). EWC/GEM/C-FLAT land at 45.62/45.67/45.31 R@1 — within ~0.3 pts of Vanilla, i.e. effectively inert.
- **MERU-B is the weakest case and the honest one:** classification accuracy 46.15 vs 44.34 Vanilla (+1.81 pts, only +4.1% rel.), overall 44.21 vs 41.82 (+5.7%), overall BWT −2.36 vs −5.43 (+56.5%). Also note GEM/C-FLAT retrieval here has huge variance (29.06 ± 3.00 and 28.20 ± 3.16) and is *below* Vanilla's 33.66 — the Euclidean baselines actively destabilize under the hyperbolic objective.
- **Representation drift (Table 7, averaged over old tasks, HMCL vs Vanilla):** radial drift −61.0%, angular drift −57.8%, cross-modal drift −73.8%, paired-distance drift −59.5%, all with p ≤ 3.1 × 10⁻⁴. This supports the paper's claim that forgetting is *both* hierarchy distortion and relational drift, not radial collapse alone.
- **Optimizer-mismatch stress test (Table 5, AdamW with weight decay 10⁻²):** Vanilla degrades sharply (overall BWT −11.94, R@5 24.67), while HMCL holds at −3.62 BWT and 35.50 R@5 (+43.9% rel. R@5, +69.7% rel. BWT). Gains are *larger* under this mismatch, i.e. the baseline gets worse faster than HMCL does.
- **Shuffled task orders (Table 6, seq2/seq3, seed 42):** overall gains persist (+7.4%, +9.7%, +5.7% on seq2; +1.0%, +10.9%, +2.8% on seq3), but **classification accuracy actually drops below Vanilla on seq3 for MERU-L (43.20 vs 43.83, −1.4%) and HyCoCLIP-B (42.34 vs 43.32, −2.3%)** — the accuracy advantage is order-dependent even though the BWT advantage (+75.3%, +82.2%) is not. The paper's text ("retains consistent gains") glosses over these two negative cells.
- **Ablation (Figure 5):** removing the block-wise time/space separation ("w/o block") consistently worsens BWT on all three backbones while still beating Vanilla — reported only as a bar chart, with no table of numbers.
- **Qualitative (Figure 4, Flickr30K traversals):** HMCL traverses hat → fashion → style → [ROOT], while Vanilla jumps to the unrelated "volleyball → interest → fashion", indicating a broken specific-to-generic ordering.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate, front-loaded in the theory).** The geometric characterization — that preserving intra-modal, inter-modal, *and* hierarchical structure is equivalent to a single shared Lorentz rotation acting across all modalities — is a genuinely new and non-obvious statement, and the reduction of (P3) to a tangent-space orthogonality constraint is elegant. But the resulting algorithm, ∆Wₛ = δₛ(I − VVᵀ), is *exactly* Gradient Projection Memory / Adam-NSCL / OGD-style null-space projection, applied to the space-like block of a linear layer. The genuinely new algorithmic ingredient is the one-line block constraint ∆w₀ = 0. The paper never says this, and never compares against Euclidean orthogonal-projection baselines (GPM, Adam-NSCL) — it only compares to EWC, GEM, and C-FLAT. Since the "w/o block" ablation *is* essentially the Euclidean projection method and already beats Vanilla substantially, the honest framing is "projection does most of the work, the hyperbolic block constraint adds the rest," and Figure 5 provides no numbers to quantify that split.

**Weak baselines.** This is the paper's biggest vulnerability. EWC and GEM are 2017-era methods, and here they are not merely weak — on MERU-L they are *worse than doing nothing* on overall BWT (−6.04 and −6.02 vs Vanilla's −3.49), and on MERU-B GEM's retrieval R@5 (29.06 ± 3.00) is 4.6 points below Vanilla. When your baselines underperform the null method, the comparison mostly demonstrates that Euclidean methods transplanted into a Lorentz objective misbehave — which the paper does argue — but it does not establish that HMCL beats a competently-tuned modern continual-VLM method. Notable absences despite being in the paper's own bibliography: DER++ (Buzzega et al.), ZSCL (Zheng et al.), MoE-Adapters (Yu et al. 2024b), C-CLIP (Liu et al. 2025a), and Ayoughi et al. 2025 "Continual hyperbolic learning of instances and classes" — the last is the single most relevant prior work to the "first hyperbolic continual learning study" framing and is cited but not compared.

**Rigor (good in places, uneven in others).** Five seeds with std, significance marking at p < 0.05, an optimizer-mismatch test, two shuffled streams, and four mechanistic drift metrics with p ≤ 3.1 × 10⁻⁴ is above-average for this subfield, and the drift analysis is a real attempt to falsify the mechanism rather than just claim the headline. Three specific concerns:
1. **A suspicious data quirk in Table 1.** For every backbone, the Vanilla row has classification BWT_A and retrieval BWT_RK *identical to the decimal and to the std*: MERU-L −6.46 ± 0.23 / −6.46 ± 0.23; MERU-B −7.26 / −5.43 with the retrieval value equal to the Overall BWT; HyCoCLIP-B −5.46 ± 0.14 / −5.46 ± 0.14. Two different metrics on two different task types should not coincide to two decimals *and* to the same standard deviation. This looks like a column-copy error in the Vanilla rows. Additionally, MERU-L Vanilla has both component BWTs at −6.46 but an Overall BWT of −3.49, which is not reconcilable as any average of the two even accounting for the t2i-R@5 substitution.
2. **Untuned-for-baselines hyperparameters that only HMCL uses.** The step-pullback coefficient β (0.10/0.20) and the retained-variance ratio (0.9/0.8/0.9) are set per backbone, with no sensitivity study. Step pullback is an interpolation toward the task-start weights — i.e. an *additional* stability mechanism outside the theory, and arguably a form of implicit regularization the baselines never get. A "Vanilla + step pullback" control is missing and would be cheap to run.
3. **All headline deltas are relative percentages.** "+26.9% image-to-text R@1" is 45.34 → 57.55; "+8.2% overall" is 38.22 → 41.36, i.e. +3.14 points on a 15-task average. Figure 1's teaser "↓74% Less Forgetting" does not correspond to any single number in the paper (the overall-BWT reductions are 69.3/56.5/92.1%, averaging 72.6%), so the reader cannot trace it.

**Scope limitation that constrains the whole claim.** Backbones are frozen; only a *single bias-free linear layer per modality* is trained. The entire theory (a shared SO(d) rotation, a null-space projection on one weight matrix) rests on this linearity, and the paper's own limitations section concedes that extending to full-backbone adaptation is future work. Absolute numbers reflect this: 40–46% average classification accuracy across 13 datasets. There is also no measurement of zero-shot retention on held-out datasets — the standard failure mode for continual VLM training — even though the introduction motivates the work with CLIP-style zero-shot transfer.

**Reproducibility (good).** Single A6000, frozen encoders, one linear layer, fully specified optimizer/epochs/LR/batch size/curvature/entailment weight/pullback β/variance ratios, standard public datasets, released implementation referenced. The projection is a few lines of code. The main unstated item is the fixed task order (described but not enumerated in the extracted text), which matters given the seq3 accuracy regressions.

**Significance.** Solid contribution to a small but growing niche. The geometric framing and drift decomposition are reusable ideas that would transfer to any Lorentz-space adapter setting. The practical impact is bounded by the linear-head-only setting and the fact that the algorithm reduces to a known projection scheme.

**Red flags summary:** (a) duplicated BWT values in Vanilla rows; (b) baselines worse than no-op; (c) closest algorithmic relative (Euclidean gradient projection / GPM) never named or benchmarked; (d) "consistent gains under alternative task orders" contradicted by two negative accuracy cells in the authors' own Table 6; (e) untraceable "74% less forgetting" teaser; (f) step pullback given to HMCL only.

## Strengths

- Theory and algorithm are genuinely coupled: Theorem 1 → Corollary 1 → Proposition 1 → Corollary 2 → Algorithm 1 is a clean derivation chain where each step visibly constrains the next, rather than a post-hoc justification of a heuristic.
- The drift analysis (Table 7, Figure 3) is a mechanistic test, not a vanity metric: each of the four drift measures is tied to a specific preservation condition, and reductions of 57.8–73.8% with p ≤ 3.1 × 10⁻⁴ substantiate the "forgetting is both relational and hierarchical" claim the abstract makes.
- Cheap and practical: zero additional learnable parameters, one SVD per stage, a d×d covariance summary so memory is constant in task count, no replay buffer, and no privacy-sensitive data retention.
- Honest experimental controls within the chosen comparison set — all baselines share the hyperbolic objective, backbone, optimizer, and schedule, which genuinely isolates the update rule.
- The rebuttal-driven additions (weight-decay mismatch, shuffled streams, drift statistics) are explicitly labeled as such, and the weight-decay result (HMCL degrades far less than Vanilla) is arguably stronger evidence than the main table.
- Three backbones of two different families (MERU-L/B and HyCoCLIP-B) rather than a single cherry-picked model.

## Limitations

- Frozen backbones, single linear Lorentz head per modality; the theory's first-order and linearity assumptions do not obviously survive full-backbone adaptation, which the authors acknowledge.
- No replay, no comparison to replay-based methods (DER++, ER), and no comparison to modern continual-VLM methods (ZSCL, MoE-Adapters, C-CLIP) or to Euclidean null-space projection (GPM/Adam-NSCL), the method's closest algorithmic relative.
- No comparison to the one existing hyperbolic continual learning paper the authors cite (Ayoughi et al. 2025), which weakens the "first geometric study" positioning.
- Theory is first-order only; the finite-step gap is patched with an unprincipled step-pullback interpolation whose coefficient is hand-tuned per backbone and never ablated.
- Curvature fixed at K = 0.1 with no sensitivity analysis, despite curvature being the central geometric hyperparameter of the whole framework.
- Zero-shot transfer retention — the property that motivates CLIP-style models in the introduction — is never measured.
- Absolute accuracies (40–46%) are low, and gains on MERU-B (+1.81 pts classification) are much smaller than the headline MERU-L/HyCoCLIP-B numbers suggest.
- The ablation of the block-wise constraint is presented only as a bar chart with no numeric table, so the marginal value of the paper's distinctly hyperbolic ingredient cannot be quantified by the reader.

## Relevance

如果你关注非欧几何表征、多模态对齐的几何不变量，或者持续学习中的正交/零空间投影类方法（GPM、Adam-NSCL、O-LoRA 一脉），这篇给出了把"遗忘"翻译成可证伪几何量（径向漂移=层级失真、跨模态漂移=关系失真）的清晰范式，Theorem 1 的"共享等距变换"刻画本身可以迁移到任何 Lorentz 空间 adapter 设计。反过来，如果你更关心大规模 VLM 的实际持续微调，本文冻结骨干、只训一层线性头的设定与真实工程场景差距较大，参考价值主要在方法论而非可直接落地的收益。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How much of the gain survives when the "w/o block" variant is compared head-to-head with a Euclidean GPM/Adam-NSCL baseline on the same stream? If the gap is small, the contribution is the theory rather than the algorithm — which is fine, but should be stated.
2. Why are the Vanilla classification and retrieval BWT values numerically identical (including std) for MERU-L and HyCoCLIP-B in Table 1? Is this a column-copy error, and does correcting it change the relative-improvement percentages?
3. Does "Vanilla + step pullback" (or baselines + step pullback) close a meaningful fraction of the gap? Without this control, part of HMCL's stability may be attributable to the interpolation heuristic rather than the projection.
4. How does the method behave when curvature K is learned or varied? The whole construction depends on a fixed Lorentz manifold; a curvature-adaptive variant would either strengthen or break the shared-isometry argument.
5. Under what conditions does the non-degeneracy assumption fail in practice? Hyperbolic contrastive learning is known to suffer dimensional collapse (Zhang et al. 2025, cited here), which would shrink the old-task subspace, making the projection weaker exactly when it is most needed — is there a measurable rank collapse over the 15-task stream?
6. As the number of tasks grows, the projected null space shrinks monotonically. At what stream length does HMCL lose plasticity entirely, and does the retained-variance ratio (0.8–0.9) need to decay with t?
7. Can the shared-isometry condition be relaxed to a *per-modality* isometry plus a slowly drifting alignment, trading some cross-modal preservation for plasticity — and would that recover the seq3 classification accuracy that HMCL loses relative to Vanilla?
8. Does zero-shot accuracy on datasets never seen in the stream degrade under HMCL, and how does that compare to ZSCL, which is explicitly designed for that objective?
