Why this matters

Step-scoped, just-in-time guidance beats dumping everything in the system prompt — and existing Claude Code and Codex hooks are enough to ship it.

Critical edge

Own ablation: ~71% of the gain survives by dumping the same mined hints into the system prompt — no hooks or FSM needed.

01 Methodology — two stages, one FSM

Initialization. An LLM parser (GPT-5.4) converts the natural-language skill document into a DSL — steps, depends_on, tool/parameter constraints, and a termination set — validated for unique ids, acyclic dependencies, and termination ⊆ step set. In parallel, a miner reads successful traces T⁺ (validated action patterns per step) and failed traces T⁻ (failure patterns + reasons), diagnosing each field as FP / FN / MC / OK and applying auditable ADD / UPDATE / REMOVE edits.

Runtime assurance. depends_on forms an FSM. Before each action runs, a pre-action hook classifies it and may soft-deny once. A termination checker blocks completion until every required step is matched. The checker is fail-open — if it errors, the action proceeds.

Evaluation. 15 SkillsBench skills × 4 configs × 5 repeats; 80 augmented queries/skill split 50 evolution / 30 held-out; guidance evolves 10 iterations; results report mean ± SD with paired t-tests.

04 Strengths

  • Honest ablation. Authors publish the system-prompt-delivery number that most weakens their own framing.
  • Deployment-realistic. Pure hook integration into two shipping agents; fail-open, soft-deny, ~0.8% in-loop compute.
  • Variance is the real win. SD drops in 50 of 60 skill×config pairs — reliability, not peak capability.
  • Auditable guidance. FP/FN/MC diagnosis makes updates inspectable, not a black-box prompt rewrite.
  • Cross-model transfer retains 94.2% of native performance; guidance amortizes across backbones.

05 Limitations

  • No learning-based baseline. ExpeL / Reflexion / Trace2Skill skipped, despite being closest related work.
  • Assumes correct skill selection & skill doc; no assurance for interleaved multi-skill workflows.
  • Selection bias. Skills failing 5/5 were removed — evaluation is restricted to where the method works by construction.
  • Self-augmented data. 1200 queries are really 120 distinct tasks; paraphrase-level replication inflates n.
  • Offline evolution cost (50 executions + GPT-5.4 mining per skill/config) is never quantified.

06 Open questions for further exploration

  1. If mined experience were just appended to the skill document (zero infrastructure), how much of 77.7% survives? Is the hook layer worth its complexity outside high-stakes skills?
  2. What does a "specification-only" ablation (DSL + FSM, no mined hints) score? That missing row is needed to justify the DSL as the central contribution.
  3. In the 8 of 60 pairs where SD increased, what failed — does soft-deny occasionally push the agent into a worse re-planning basin?
  4. How does the FSM handle legitimate alternative decompositions (steps fused into one tool call)? Unmatched actions are allowed but don't advance the FSM — spurious termination denials?
  5. Does mined guidance overfit the 5 evolution instances? A cross-task-family transfer test would beat paraphrase-level generalization.
  6. Can the FP/FN/MC diagnosis use a cheaper non-LLM signal (coverage stats) instead of GPT-5.4 over full traces?
