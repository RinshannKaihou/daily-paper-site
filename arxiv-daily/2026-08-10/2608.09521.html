05Methodology

UAB takes each model's residual-stream activation at the final token of a matched prefix, cut at half depth, and routes it through a per-model path: normalize → linear encode → shared 3072-d bus → linear decode → denormalize. Two globally-shared residual MLPs sit at the bus entrance and exit.

Normalization is a one-shot z-score + regularized ZCA whitening (ρ = 10⁻²) estimated from ~200K activations, then frozen. The loss fuses three terms in normalized space: self-reconstruction, cross-reconstruction (a state decoded into model n's space must match n's own activation on the same prefix — readability, not identity), and InfoNCE contrastive (λ = 0.1, τ = 0.07).

Trained jointly on the 4 sources over 1.42M byte-offset-matched positions (≥99.83% leave-one-out tokenizer coverage). Then the bus and all adapters are frozen. A new model onboards by fitting only its own adapter pair on 10K unlabeled positions, aligned to the frozen source consensus center. Existing coordinates, paths, and tools provably cannot shift — persistent parameters scale O(N), not O(N²).

07Strengths & limitations

▲ Strengths

  • Right design for the claim. Freeze-then-onboard with leave-one-out onboarding of all five models — more thorough than the headline needs.
  • Negative results reported, not buried: −0.550 self-FVE at 3K, OLMo's 6% sentiment recall, rejected candidates, mismatched-input control (~0.328).
  • Cherry-picking disclosed before showing features — SAE Table 3a drawn from the median-Jaccard 0.569 group.
  • Hard-to-fake result: OLMo excluded from SAE training yet lands inside the source-pair Jaccard range.
  • Alignment & reconstruction genuinely decouple — near-perfect shared retrieval needs no faithful native reconstruction.
  • Clean tokenizer fix: byte-offset matched prefixes, 99.83% leave-one-out boundary coverage.

▼ Limitations

  • No pairwise baseline. "One pair beats O(N²) connectors" is never measured against the incumbent method class.
  • "Universal" is conditional: the screen rejected half the candidates, incl. Gemma, Mistral, Phi-3.5-mini — with no diagnosis.
  • Fixed D = 3072 caps wide models (OLMo 0.734, Qwen3-14B 0.591 self-FVE); D is never ablated.
  • Axes mostly saturated, so probe-transfer 0.966 vs 0.963 is a weak test; bias shows real loss (0.853 vs 0.950–0.990).
  • Execution is single-position at one half-depth cut, KV cache intact — not model stitching.
  • Steering cited as motivation but never tested; source models all ≤4B English-tuned.
08Open questions worth chasing
Why do Gemma-3-4B, Mistral-7B and Phi-3.5-mini fail the ridge screen so catastrophically (1.0–2.6%) while Falcon3 passes at 99.3%?
Does raising D above max(dm) — say 4096 or 8192 — recover OLMo's and Qwen3-14B's self-FVE, or is the bottleneck adapter linearity?
How does UAB compare head-to-head with a directly-trained pairwise translator on the same probe / SAE / execution tasks?
Does the interface survive multi-position translation — replacing the full prefix of states rather than only the final position?
Do steering vectors computed once in Z transfer as cleanly as probes? Applying them through Dm is a one-experiment addition.
Does the frozen interface degrade as onboarded models grow into the dozens, each aligned to a consensus from only four sources?
