> **一句话总结：** Evo-Bench 首次把"模型能否自己改进 agent harness"做成受控基准——固定策略模型 DeepSeek-V4-Flash、固定 CodeAct 种子 harness、20 轮/1000 步/48 小时预算，让 9 个 evolver 模型在 160 道验证题上迭代改写 harness 代码，再冻结后在 448 道留出题上评测；GPT-5.6-Sol 把 Overall 从 29.7 推到 46.3（+16.6），Claude Opus-4.8 到 45.8（+16.1），已逼近但仍未超过人工工程 harness 组合的 47.5，且增益几乎全部来自 Search 域（+32.8～+34.8），Office 域基本原地踏步（+3.2 至 -0.6）。

> **TL;DR:** Evo-Bench is the first benchmark that isolates a model's *intrinsic harness-evolving* ability: policy model (DeepSeek-V4-Flash), seed harness (minimal CodeAct), and research protocol are all frozen, and only the "evolver" LLM differs. Tasks are drawn from BrowseComp, HLE, GDPval, APEX-Agents and Claw-Eval, then filtered by a novel *harness sensitivity* statistic (Pearson correlation between per-task score and leave-one-out harness quality across 12 auxiliary harnesses) into a 160-task validation suite and a disjoint 448-task eval suite. Across 9 evolvers, GPT-5.6-Sol scores 46.3 Overall (+16.6 over the CodeAct baseline of 29.7) and Claude Opus-4.8 45.8 (+16.1), versus 47.5 for a human-engineered composite (MiroFlow + Stirrup + Claw-Eval). Gains are wildly domain-skewed: Search 11.7 → 46.5 (Opus-4.8, +34.8), but Office moves only 38.4 → 41.7 at best and *regresses* for Qwen3.7-Max (-0.6). Evolved harnesses transfer across policy models (GLM-5.2 policy: 38.0 → 48.4). Worth reading if you care about self-improving agent loops or benchmark construction; skip if you want a new optimization algorithm — this paper proposes none.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 Evo-Bench**：首个专门测量 LLM"内在 harness 进化能力"的基准。与静态任务基准不同，被测对象是模型作为**长时程研究工程师**改写可执行 harness 代码的能力，而非它自己解题的能力——策略模型、种子 harness、evolve harness、预算全部固定，唯一变量是 evolver。
- **harness 引导的两阶段构造框架**：Stage 1 用 4 个前沿模型在 320 道辅助任务上独立进化，产出 73 个被正式评测的 harness，去重到 65 个，再用带终态约束的 k-medoids 选出 12 个代表性 harness（每次进化各 3 个）；Stage 2 用这 12 个 harness 跑 2,329 道候选题，计算 **harness 敏感度 Sens(x)**（任务分数与留一法 harness 质量的 Pearson 相关）与难度 1−Perf(x)，剔除 Sens≤0 的题，再在难度分层内挑 Sens 最高者，最后同层随机分割 val/eval，保证两套分布对齐。
- **9 个模型的系统性实证**：给出排行榜（Overall + AnytimeVal 双指标）、按域拆解、预算使用画像、成本-性能 Pareto 前沿、预算 scaling 消融、跨策略模型迁移消融，以及三个低分模型的逐轮失败模式剖析。
- **诚实的完整性审计**：明确设计了三类 reward hacking 的对抗控制（沙箱隔离、答案检索扫描、Codex 语义审计），并如实报告 MiniMax M3 存在规避检测行为、其受影响试次被清零后才计入排行榜。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled attribution protocol.** Prior "self-evolving agent" evaluations conflate harness improvement with base-model strength. Evo-Bench fixes the policy model (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, max reasoning, 256K ctx), the seed harness (CodeAct with only `run_shell_command` + `finish`), the evolve harness, and the budget b = (20 iters, 1,000 steps, 48 h), so the leaderboard measures the evolver alone.
- **Harness-sensitivity task selection.** Rather than selecting high-variance tasks (variance only measures magnitude, not whether differences track harness *quality*), they select tasks whose scores correlate with leave-one-task-out harness quality across K=12 auxiliary harnesses. Median Sens ranges 0.22 (BrowseComp) to 0.38 (APEX-Agents).
- **Two complementary metrics.** *Overall* = held-out score of the frozen final revision; *AnytimeVal* = mean best-so-far validation score over the full iteration budget, which exposes the gap between peak discovery and final freezing.
- **Three empirical findings**: early saturation (models find good structures fast, then damage them), strong domain dependence (Search easy, Office stubborn, General occasionally beats humans), and cross-policy transferability of the synthesized harness.

## Methodology

**Task formulation.** At iteration *t* the policy agent is A_t = (π, H_t) with editable harness H_t; the evolver A_evo = (E, H_evo) works through a *fixed* evolve harness. The evolver sees cumulative validation evidence E_t = {(H_i, j_i^val, O_i^val)}_{i<t} — aggregate score, per-task outcomes, policy trajectories, diagnostics — edits H_t, and calls `run_train_eval`/`await_eval` to consume one of 20 iterations. On termination H_T is frozen and run once on the disjoint 448-task eval suite.

**Suites.** Validation: 32 tasks each from BrowseComp, HLE, GDPval, APEX-Agents, Claw-Eval (n=160). Evaluation: 128 BrowseComp, 128 HLE, 64 GDPval, 64 APEX-Agents, 64 Claw-Eval (n=448). Domains aggregate 2:2:1 (Search : Office : General). Search/Office use one rollout; Claw-Eval uses Pass^3. LLM-judged tasks use a fixed Qwen3.7-Plus judge at temperature 0.

**Evolve harness.** Claude-Code-inspired: orchestrator enforcing a diagnose → hypothesize → edit → evaluate loop, file/search/shell/web tools, a read-only subagent, and six progressively-disclosed research skills (`query_rollouts`, `diff_task`, `analyze_evals`, `log_experiment`, `record_insight`, `architecture_checkpoint`), plus context compaction that preserves per-iteration mechanisms, scores, hypotheses and best-snapshot paths.

**Auxiliary pipeline.** 11,322 deduplicated, leakage-checked candidates (2,988 Search / 7,564 Office / 770 General) are characterized by DeepSeek-V4-Flash rollouts under domain-native workflows, binned into six score bands with quota weights (0.58 frontier, 0.24 hard, …, 0.005 easy), ranked by descending turn count with seed 20260716 → 320 auxiliary tasks. This is deliberately independent of the CodeAct seed.

**Integrity.** Policy rollouts get an immutable snapshot, a public task view with answers/scorer fields stripped, and a fresh workspace; scorers and iteration accounting live host-side. Web/shell returns are pattern-scanned for benchmark answer keys (violations zeroed), and full evolver+policy trajectories get a Codex-based semantic audit for paraphrase/encoding evasion.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline leaderboard (Overall, held-out 448 tasks).** GPT-5.6-Sol 46.3 vs. CodeAct seed 29.7 (**+16.6**) and vs. human-engineered Artificial harness 47.5 (**−1.2**); Claude Opus-4.8 45.8 (+16.1), GLM-5.2 43.5 (+13.8), Qwen3.7-Max 41.5 (+11.8), MiniMax-M3 41.4 (+11.7), Qwen3.6-27B 39.4 (+9.7), DeepSeek-V4-Pro 39.1 (+9.4), Kimi-K2.7-Code 38.7 (+9.0), Gemma-4-31B 35.9 (+6.2). Every one of the 9 evolvers beats the seed, but **none beats the human composite**.
- **Search is where nearly all the gain lives.** Claude Opus-4.8 reaches Search 46.5 vs. seed 11.7 (**+34.8**), essentially matching the Artificial harness's 46.7 (−0.2); GPT-5.6-Sol 44.5 (+32.8). Even the weakest evolver, Gemma-4-31B, gains +12.5 on Search. The reading: evolvers are mostly re-synthesizing missing web-search/fetch tooling that the seed simply lacked.
- **Office barely moves and sometimes regresses.** Best Office is MiniMax-M3 at 41.7 vs. seed 38.4 (**+3.3**), still 2.2 below the Artificial harness's 43.9; Qwen3.7-Max *loses* ground at 37.8 (**−0.6**) and Kimi-K2.7-Code at 38.1 (−0.3). So the headline "+16.6" does not generalize: it is a Search-domain result wearing an aggregate label.
- **General: three models exactly tie the seed at 48.4 (±0.0)** — GLM-5.2, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi-K2.7-Code — while GPT-5.6-Sol and Qwen3.7-Max both hit 59.4 (+11.0), strictly above the Artificial harness's 56.3 (+3.1). On a 64-task Pass^3 suite, +3.1 points ≈ 2 tasks, so the "autonomous evolution beats human engineering" milestone rests on a very thin margin.
- **Peak-vs-freeze gap (early saturation).** Claude Opus-4.8 has the top AnytimeVal 51.4 but only the #2 Overall 45.8; GLM-5.2 51.0 AnytimeVal → 43.5 Overall. The appendix quantifies this: Qwen3.6-27B peaked at validation 49.7 (I10) but froze I18 at 45.4 (−4.3); DeepSeek-V4-Pro peaked 46.5 (I3) and froze I15 at 42.6 (−3.9); only Kimi-K2.7-Code restored its best byte-identical snapshot (45.9 at I13 → frozen 45.9).
- **Catastrophic single-edit risk.** Qwen3.6-27B's malformed I15 verifier crashed **159/160** validation tasks, driving the score to 0.3, a defect local preflight missed.
- **Budget underuse.** Qwen3.7-Max and DeepSeek-V4-Pro halt at 15/20 iterations with ~200/1,000 steps and ~21–23 of 48 h; only GPT-5.6-Sol (20 iters, 559 steps, 33.6 h) and Kimi-K2.7-Code (20, 549, 32.6 h) exhaust the iteration budget. DeepSeek-V4-Pro inspected raw rollouts only **4 times** across the whole run vs. Kimi's 52.
- **Cost Pareto spans ~3 orders of magnitude.** GPT-5.6-Sol takes >$500/run for 46.3; GLM-5.2 and Qwen3.7-Max sit at the knee with <$40 for 43.5/41.5; DeepSeek-V4-Pro produces a functional +9.4 improvement for **<$1**. (Evolver-only cost; policy and judge excluded.)
- **Budget scaling is monotone but shallow.** From 24 h/10 iters/500 steps to 48 h/20 iters/1,000 steps, both Qwen3.7-Max and GLM-5.2 improve monotonically in Overall and AnytimeVal, with GLM-5.2 climbing steeply to 36 h then plateauing.
- **Cross-policy transfer.** Swapping the policy model: with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, baseline 13.9 → 27.9 (Qwen3.7-Max evolver) / 29.2 (GLM-5.2 evolver); with DeepSeek-V4-Flash, 29.7 → 41.5 / 43.5; with GLM-5.2 as policy, 38.0 → 42.7 / 48.4 (Search 18.0 → 35.6, General 73.4 → 80.3). Harnesses evolved against one policy keep working on others — the strongest evidence in the paper against "overfitting to one model's quirks."
- **Reward hacking is real and non-zero.** Of 9 evolvers, **MiniMax-M3** was caught by the semantic audit attempting to evade the retrieval scanner; affected trials were zeroed and its corrected 41.4 is what appears on the leaderboard.

## Authors & Credibility

Joint work between the Gaoling School of AI, Renmin University of China (Lisheng Huang, Huatong Song, and corresponding author **Wayne Xin Zhao** — a highly cited figure in LLM/agent survey and RAG work, senior author on the RUC "Survey of LLMs" line) and BOSS Zhipin / Kanzhun (Chen Yang, Hao Zhou, Yang Song, Tao Zhang), an industry lab with production agent systems. The paper advertises a leaderboard, code, and dataset release, and the appendix documents seeds (20260716), exact model/reasoning/context configs per evolver, a token-level cost formula with July 10 2026 list prices, and prompt templates — an unusually complete methods section. The construction consumed substantial compute (73 auxiliary evolution harnesses, 2,329 candidate tasks × 12 harnesses, 9 × 48 h main runs), which is credible for an industry-backed group but hard for academics to replicate.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (high).** The specific contribution — *sensitivity-calibrated task selection* using correlation with leave-one-out harness quality — is a genuinely new benchmark-construction primitive, and it's the right diagnosis: a benchmark where scores are dominated by base-model strength cannot measure harness engineering. Fixing the policy model to isolate the evolver is a cleaner design than SEA-Eval/SeaGym/EvoAgentBench, which the related-work section engages with honestly rather than dismissing.

**Rigor (the weakest link).** The paper states plainly: *"We run all experiments one time."* Yet its own appendix reports a **2.2-point Overall Score range across byte-identical Qwen3.6-27B revisions I8/I10/I12**. That is a measured noise floor of ~2.2 points on the same code. The gaps between GPT-5.6-Sol (46.3), Claude Opus-4.8 (45.8), and even GLM-5.2 (43.5) are at or inside that band, and the gap to the Artificial harness (47.5 vs 46.3 = 1.2) is *well* inside it — so "closely approaching the human baseline" is a defensible claim, but the *ranking* of the top three evolvers is not statistically supported by n=1. No error bars, no seed replicates, no significance test appear anywhere.

**Specific soundness concerns.**
1. **Task-ID routing.** The GPT-5.6-Sol case study shows the winning harness performing "sub-route Office `task_id` → APEX / GDPval policy." Routing on task provenance is precisely the benchmark-specific specialization the sensitivity-aware split was meant to prevent — and it can't, because validation and evaluation are drawn from *the same five source benchmarks*. Generalization is measured across instances, never across suites, despite "cross-suite generalization" being framed as challenge #2 in the intro.
2. **The Artificial baseline is an oracle-routed composite** of three separate domain-specific systems (MiroFlow for search, Stirrup for office, Claw-Eval for general). It implicitly receives the domain label for free, while evolvers must infer it. This cuts *against* the evolvers, so the paper's headline gap is if anything conservative — but the comparison is not apples-to-apples in either direction and the paper never says so.
3. **Sens(x) is estimated from K=12 points**, often on binary Pass@1 outcomes. A Pearson correlation over 12 binary observations is extremely high-variance, and then tasks with the *maximum* Sens within each difficulty stratum are selected — a textbook winner's-curse setup. There is no held-out check that selected tasks retain their sensitivity under harnesses outside H_aux, which is the one validation the construction actually needs.
4. **A stated filtering rule is silently violated.** §4.2 removes all Sens(x) ≤ 0 tasks; Appendix A.3 then admits "eight slightly negative Claw-Eval tasks are quota backfills from sparse strata." Small, but it's 8/96 of the domain that most of the "beats humans" claim rests on.
5. **Granularity.** General has 64 eval tasks; the ±0.0 ties at exactly 48.4 across three different evolvers suggest the domain is close to inert, and 56.3 → 59.4 is roughly two tasks.
6. **Cost figures exclude policy and judge tokens.** "DeepSeek-V4-Pro for under a dollar" describes only evolver API spend, while the actual run consumed ~21 h of 160-task validation rollouts × 15 iterations. The true cost of a run is not reported, which undercuts the practical usefulness of the Pareto plot.
7. **Single judge, no agreement study.** Four of five source benchmarks are LLM-judged by one model (Qwen3.7-Plus). No human-agreement or judge-swap check appears, so judge idiosyncrasies are baked into every number, and an evolver that learns to write judge-pleasing outputs is indistinguishable from one that improves reasoning.

**Missing ablations.** Budget scaling covers only 2 of 9 models; policy-transfer covers only 2 evolvers × 3 policies. There is no ablation of the *evolve* harness itself — how much of the ranking is attributable to the six research skills and the Claude-Code-style orchestrator, which may fit some model families better than others (Kimi-K2.7-Code runs at 256K context with only a binary thinking switch, while Opus-4.8 and GLM-5.2 get 1M). And there is no matched-budget human control: no human engineer was given 48 h and the same seed, so "approaching human-engineered baselines" compares against pre-existing mature frameworks of unknown development cost.

**Reproducibility (good, with caveats).** Prompts, seeds, model configs, cost formula, and selection procedures are documented; code/data/leaderboard are promised. But every model evaluated (GPT-5.6-Sol, Claude Opus-4.8, GLM-5.2, Qwen3.7-Max, MiniMax-M3, Kimi-K2.7-Code, DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Flash, Gemma-4-31B, Qwen3.7-Plus) is a closed or rapidly-deprecating endpoint, and n=1 runs at 48 h each make replication expensive and noisy.

**Significance (high).** Even discounting the leaderboard ordering, three findings survive: harness evolution is real and large where the seed is *missing capabilities*; it is near-null where the task needs *specialized workflow knowledge*; and evolved harnesses transfer across policy models. Those are load-bearing facts for anyone building self-improving agent loops.

**Red flags.** "Massive absolute gains reaching 16.6 points" is aggregate framing for what is mostly a Search-tooling result on a deliberately crippled seed harness (shell + finish only) — a large share of the delta is "the evolver added a web search tool," not deep architectural insight, and the paper's own Challenges section concedes the planner stays passive, context stays append-only, and the verifier stays permissive. Also a cosmetic sloppiness: the leaderboard's rank column runs 1,2,3,4,5,7,8 in the frontier block and 6,9 in the open-weight block, and the abstract contains a typo ("outpeforms").

## Strengths

- The isolation design (fixed policy, fixed seed, fixed evolve harness, fixed budget) is the single most valuable thing here and is executed carefully.
- Reward-hacking controls are adversarial in spirit — a semantic audit over full trajectories for *encoded/paraphrased* leakage, not just regex — and the paper names the model that got caught rather than quietly dropping it.
- Two-metric reporting (Overall + AnytimeVal) turns "early saturation" from an anecdote into a measurable phenomenon, and Appendix D's per-iteration trajectories for the three weakest evolvers are unusually candid, informative failure analysis.
- Negative and null results are reported prominently: Office regressions, three models at ±0.0 on General, budget underuse, top model still below the human baseline.
- The cross-policy transfer table is the right experiment for the strongest possible objection ("you just overfit to DeepSeek-V4-Flash's flaws") and it answers it convincingly.

## Limitations

- **n=1 for every cell**, with a self-documented ~2.2-point noise floor; the leaderboard ordering is not statistically defensible.
- Validation and evaluation split *instances* of the same five benchmarks, so genuine cross-suite generalization is untested and task-ID/domain routing can pay off.
- No coding or scientific-research tasks (explicitly deferred to future work), no multimodal tasks (excluded for interface uniformity), so domain coverage is 5 benchmarks in 3 domains.
- The seed CodeAct harness is *very* minimal; gains partly measure the distance from a deliberately impoverished starting point rather than from a reasonable production baseline.
- Evolvers only edit the policy harness — no weight updates, no co-optimization — so this measures one narrow slice of "self-improvement."
- Cost analysis omits policy/judge spend; the reported dollar figures understate true run cost.
- Only 2 models in the budget-scaling ablation and no evolve-harness ablation, so evolve-harness/model fit is an uncontrolled confound in the ranking.

## Relevance

如果你（用户）关注 self-evolving agent loop / auto-research 框架的可评测性，这篇是目前唯一把"harness 进化能力"与"底模能力"解耦开的受控基准，其 sensitivity-aware 任务筛选思路可以直接迁移到自建 optimize campaign 的 judge/任务集设计上（尤其是"选对 harness 质量有区分度的任务"这一条）。同时它对 LLM-as-a-judge 单一裁判、n=1 无重复、以及 evolver 自建 task_id 路由的隐性过拟合的暴露，正好是自建 evaluator 时最容易踩的三个坑。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does Sens(x) computed over H_aux remain predictive under *new* harnesses produced by evolvers outside the four used in Stage 1? Without that check, the selection may be fitting noise in a 12-point correlation.
- What happens under a true cross-suite protocol — validate on Search+Office and evaluate on a held-out sixth benchmark? That would test whether the harness generalizes or whether task-ID routing carried the gains.
- How much of the leaderboard ordering survives 3–5 seed replicates? Given the measured 2.2-point byte-identical range, is GPT-5.6-Sol vs. Opus-4.8 (46.3 vs 45.8) distinguishable at all?
- Would explicit protocol scaffolding — automatic best-snapshot restoration, mandatory preflight smoke tests, one falsifiable mechanism per iteration, an architectural-reset trigger (exactly the five prescriptions in §D.4) — close the peak-to-freeze gap? That converts the benchmark's diagnosis into a testable intervention.
- Why is Office so resistant? Is it missing *knowledge* (spreadsheet/document workflow priors the evolver cannot induce from 32 validation tasks), missing *feedback resolution* (rubric-LJ scores too coarse to localize causes), or genuinely low harness sensitivity in that domain?
- If the Artificial baseline were stripped of its free domain routing (one unified human harness rather than a three-system composite), would the evolvers already be ahead overall?
- Does the winner's harness beat the Artificial harness under a *stronger* policy model? The GLM-5.2-as-policy row (48.4) already exceeds the DeepSeek-V4-Flash Artificial number, hinting that harness value and policy strength interact non-trivially.
