01Methodology, in two moves

Twelve pages of self-contained probability. No code, no experiments. The argument never recurses, which is exactly how the logarithm dies.

The setting is a sum of weakly-interacting, doubly-centered summands gi with E[gi|Z−i]=0|E[gi|Zi]|≤Moscj(gi)≤β. BKZ's 2020 bound carried a ⌈log₂ n⌉ because their proof recursed over coordinate blocks. This paper replaces recursion with a single-shot combinatorial estimate.

① Rademacher cube

Double centering forces an antisymmetric form hi(ε)=εiqi(ε−i). The MGF is split via eᵘ ≤ (1+2u)^½·e^{4u²} into a product term and a square-function term. The product term is tamed by a novel fixed-point counting lemma (Lemma 2): read E∏(1+εiai) as the expected fixed-point count of a random self-map of the cube, bounded by a second-moment (Chebyshev-style) argument to ≤ 2e+1.

② Two-copy transference

Pair each Zi with an independent copy, form Wi=(Zi,Z'i), and symmetrize over random signs. Exchangeability of pair-swaps makes the cube estimate carry over to arbitrary product measures; a bounded-differences moment bound cleans up the residual centering defect. Neither step introduces log n.

03Strengths & limitations

+ Strengths

  • Answers a precisely stated, citable open question (BKZ §6) — no ambiguity about what was accomplished.
  • Short (12 pp), elementary, mostly self-contained: McDiarmid + discrete Poincaré + Cauchy–Schwarz. Verifiable in an afternoon.
  • Fully explicit constants throughout (c⋆, C₀, K, λ, final 16 and 7) — including the arithmetic that produces them.
  • Lemma 2's fixed-point counting device is genuinely novel and reusable for weak cross-coordinate dependence.
  • Intellectual honesty: cleanly separates the upper-bound question solved from the lower-bound realizability question left open.

− Limitations

  • Zero empirical or numerical component — not even a five-line cube simulation to confirm the removed log n is real.
  • Constants far from the lower bound (factor 16 on pnβ); proof makes no optimization attempt, several steps visibly lossy.
  • Corollary 1 is not self-contained — depends on BKZ's Lemma 7 reduction and its 2γn comparison error, quoted without proof.
  • Matching holds only for κ ≤ p ≤ n; behaviour for p > n is untouched.
  • Limited practical impact — uniform-stability parameters for SGD/Adam/LLM tuning are too loose for log n to matter.
  • Thin related work (4 refs); no positioning vs. entropy-method or transportation-cost approaches.

04Questions for further exploration

  1. Can the constant 16 be pushed toward the lower-bound constant 1 — e.g. by counting out-of-S constraints in Lemma 2, or via a direct hypercontractivity route?
  2. Does the fixed-point lemma generalize beyond the unbiased cube — biased signs, q-ary alphabets, or Dobrushin-type weakly dependent measures?
  3. Does the improvement survive relaxing uniform stability to on-average or hypothesis stability, where BKZ-style reductions are more delicate?
  4. What happens for p > n, outside BKZ's lower-bound construction — is 16pnβ + M√(2pn) still tight?
  5. Can two-copy randomization give log-free bounds for excess risk of stable algorithms, not just the generalization gap?
  6. Is there a moderate-(n, p) regime where the new bound is actually worse than BKZ's due to the 16 vs. 12√2·⌈log₂n⌉ trade-off? The paper never checks the crossover.
