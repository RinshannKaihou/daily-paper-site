> **一句话总结：** 该研究将 120 篇 ICLR 2026 投稿匿名化后，用两位 LLM 改写者沿六个修辞维度双向改写出 4,200 篇"科学内容保留"的稿件，并由五位 AI 审稿人在标准/严格两种协议下打分；结果显示"证据框架"与"新奇性立场"最容易左右 AI 评分（正向证据最多可提升 OA +0.93，负向新奇最多降 −0.73），但这种效应高度依赖改写者–审稿者组合，并不是普遍的"奖励黑客"。

> **TL;DR:** Using 120 ICLR 2026 submissions rewritten into 4,200 content-preserving variants along 6 rhetorical dimensions by GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8, then scored by 5 LLM reviewers under standard and strict protocols (42,396 valid reviews), the authors show that positive evidence framing raises overall assessment (OA) by up to +0.93 while negative novelty stance lowers it by up to −0.73, strict review cuts mean OA by 1.36 points (6.296→4.934) without reducing rhetorical sensitivity, and Opus 4.8 joint rewriting yields +0.289 (standard) / +0.463 (strict) OA gains versus near-zero for GPT-5.5 — rhetoric influences AI review in a selective, configuration-dependent way rather than as a universal reward hack.

## 核心贡献

- **端到端受控分析框架**：构建了一个结合"受控修辞改写 + 多模型 AI 审稿 + 配对分析"的整体流水线，支持单维度、联合、递归与审稿人引导四种改写干预方式，系统地诊断 AI 审稿对内容保留性修辞变化的敏感度。
- **基于完整 LaTeX 工程的智能体级改写工具**：GPT-5.5（经 Codex CLI）与 Opus 4.8（经 Claude Code）可改写全文叙述（包括题注、表格、结果讨论），同时通过结构性锚点（引用键、交叉引用、标签、URL、图片路径、数学/代码环境、bib 文件）的程序化校验 + 编译修复来"软约束"科学内容不被篡改。
- **大规模受控语料**：以 120 篇匿名化 ICLR 2026 投稿为种子，生成 4,200 篇全文改写稿、累计 42,396 条有效审稿记录（直接 API 成本 $29,165.89），覆盖 2 位改写者 × 5 位审稿者 × 2 种协议 × 6 维度 × 2 方向。
- **结构化的修辞敏感性结论**：明确给出维度层级——证据框架与新奇性立场效应最大，scope（范围）框架次之，其余三维度效应较小或不稳定；并揭示改写者主要决定正负变体的分离度，审稿者主要决定分数变化的幅度与符号。
- **反直觉的工程结论**：更复杂的改写流程（联合、递归、审稿人引导）并不带来稳定的更大增益——联合改写对 Opus 4.8 有效（+0.289/+0.463），但对 GPT-5.5 接近零；审稿人引导并不稳定优于无引导的第二轮；增益多集中在第二轮之后递减。

## Key Contributions

- An end-to-end controlled framework that pairs content-preserving rhetorical rewriting with multi-model AI review and within-paper paired analysis, supporting single-dimension, joint, recursive, and reviewer-guided interventions.
- An agentic source-level rewriting harness operating on complete LaTeX projects: it propagates each intervention through the full narrative while enforcing soft content-preservation via structural-anchor checks (citation/cross-reference keys, labels, URLs, graphics paths, math/code/algorithm environments, bibliography) plus compile-and-repair, discarding outputs that violate constraints.
- A large controlled corpus: 120 anonymized ICLR 2026 originals expanded into 4,200 full-paper manuscripts (2,880 single-dimension + 240 joint + 480 recursive + 480 reviewer-guided rewrites), evaluated to 42,396 valid structured review records at a direct API cost of $29,165.89.
- A dimensional hierarchy of rhetorical sensitivity: evidence framing and novelty stance dominate (with scope a weaker second tier), shown to be stable across human-assessed quality strata while the AI reviewer's own starting score governs the direction of movement.
- A separation of roles between rewriter and reviewer: the rewriter mainly shapes the positive–negative separation between variants, whereas the reviewer determines the magnitude and sign of the resulting OA change and which rubric dimension (contribution vs. soundness) it shows up in.
- An honest negative result on "more is better" rewriting: joint rewriting is strongly rewriter-dependent (substantial for Opus 4.8, near-zero for GPT-5.5), reviewer guidance does not beat an unguided second pass, and gains saturate after the second round.

## Methodology

The pipeline has three stages. (1) **Seed corpus & baseline**: 120 ICLR 2026 submissions are retrieved via the OpenReview API, sampled as 20 papers from each of six human-rating intervals ([1,3), [3,4), [4,5), [5,6), [6,7), [7,8.5]); public arXiv LaTeX sources are matched to each submission by token-cosine + 5-gram-Jaccard similarity, then an agentic anonymization harness strips names/affiliations/acknowledgments/identity links with manual verification. (2) **Rhetorical intervention**: six prespecified dimensions (novelty stance, scope framing, evidence framing, contribution salience, technical register, linguistic complexity) are each rewritten in two opposing directions ("positive"/"negative") from the same baseline, with each variant generated independently so the design separates directional sensitivity from generic rewriting movement. The agentic harness (GPT-5.5 via Codex CLI; Opus 4.8 via Claude Code) edits prose, captions, tables, and result discussion under soft preservation of methods, settings, reported values, and evidence boundaries; a programmatic checker guards structural anchors and returns violations for repair or discards unfixable/ uncompilable outputs. Four workflows are studied: single-dimension (2,880 rewrites), joint (all six positive objectives in one prompt, 240 rewrites), recursive joint (three rounds, 480 rewrites), and reviewer-guided (joint rewrite → standard review by the same backbone → second rewrite conditioned on the validated review JSON, 480 rewrites). (3) **AI review**: each compiled PDF is scored blind (reviewers are not told the rewrite condition or rewrite model) by Gemini 3.5 FL, Qwen 3.5 F, GPT-5 mini, GPT-5.5, or Sonnet 5 under a standard conference rubric or a strict prompt that demands concrete evidence for high ratings and instructs not to reward presentation unless it changes the scientific assessment. The primary outcome is the overall rating; soundness, presentation, contribution are secondary; weak-accept probability is defined as the share of ratings ≥ 6. Analysis is paired (each rewrite vs. its prompt-matched original for the same paper/reviewer/prompt), stratified across the six human-score strata with 5,000 paper-level bootstrap resamples for 95% percentile intervals; missing outcomes are retained as missing rather than imputed, and no separate multiple-testing correction is applied—effects are judged by magnitude, direction, and cross-configuration consistency.

## Results & Highlights

- **Evidence framing and novelty stance dominate.** Across all 2×5×2 configurations, evidence framing produces the largest positive–negative OA contrast (positive up to +0.93, e.g. Opus 4.8 + Evidence + Strict Gemini 3.5 FL = +0.933; negative novelty stance lowers OA by up to −0.73, e.g. Opus 4.8 + Novelty + Strict Gemini 3.5 FL = −0.733 in Table 3).
- **Weak-accept probability tracks the same hierarchy.** Positive–negative contrasts reach 13.0 percentage points for evidence framing, 12.0 for novelty stance, and 9.0 for scope framing (Appendix Table 16); evidence framing moves weak-accept probability by ~13 pp on average.
- **Starting score drives direction more than human quality.** Pooling both rewriters and all reviewers under standard evaluation, positive evidence variants average +1.05 OA for AI original scores in [1,3] but −0.19 for scores in [8,10]; joint-rewrite mean OA change declines from +1.42 (initial score [1,3]) to −0.88 (initial score [8,10]), while staying comparatively flat across human-OA strata (Tables 5 and 7).
- **Strict review recalibrates but does not desensitize.** Strict review lowers mean OA from 6.296 to 4.934 (−1.36 points), with 95.0% of papers receiving a lower score on average, yet the cross-protocol Spearman correlation of paper-level rewrite effects is only .081 (vs. .862 for absolute OA) — strictness shifts severity without making reviewers invariant to content-preserving rhetoric (Table 8).
- **Joint rewriting is rewriter-dependent.** Opus 4.8 joint rewrites give reviewer-averaged OA gains of +0.289 (standard) and +0.463 (strict); GPT-5.5 only +0.021 and +0.045 (Table 6). Relative to the mean of the six positive single-dimension rewrites, Opus 4.8 gains +0.160 OA while GPT-5.5 falls −0.074 below it.
- **Reviewer guidance ≠ better than repetition.** Guided-minus-unguided contrasts are negative in all four rewriter–prompt averages (−0.035/−0.042 for GPT-5.5; −0.008/−0.067 for Opus 4.8); Opus 4.8 still improves OA from +0.289→+0.396 (standard) and +0.463→+0.557 (strict), but the lift comes from the second pass rather than the feedback itself.
- **Recursive rewriting saturates by round 2.** For Opus 4.8 the reviewer-averaged OA gain rises from +0.289 to +0.410 (standard) and +0.463 to +0.624 (strict) at round 2 with little added at round 3; GPT-5.5 reaches only +0.080 (standard) and +0.153 (strict) by round 3 (Figure 3).
- **Secondary-score mapping is reviewer-specific.** ΔOA correlates more with contribution and soundness than with presentation; Qwen 3.5 F aligns OA mainly with contribution, strict Gemini 3.5 FL with both contribution and soundness, and Sonnet 5 is weaker/less stable (Table 9).
- **Reviewer/rewriter roles split.** Reviewers share the same preferred rhetorical direction but disagree on whether it raises or lowers scores (Qwen 3.5 F and GPT-5 mini widest response distributions; Sonnet 5 shifts effects downward); Opus 4.8 produces wider positive–negative separation than GPT-5.5 rather than a uniform upward shift (Figure 4).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (high).** Prior work (Kaneko 2026; Baumann et al. 2026; Yang et al. 2026b; Li et al. 2026b) showed that presentation-only revisions can raise AI-review scores, but none systematically decomposed which rhetorical dimensions matter and whether their effects are directionally stable across evaluators. The six-dimension bidirectional taxonomy plus the separation of rewriter vs. reviewer roles is a genuinely new contribution, as is the LaTeX-level harness with structural-anchor preservation.
- **Rigor (good, with caveats).** The paired within-paper design, stratification by both human-OA and AI-original-OA, 5,000-resample bootstrap intervals, and cross-model triangulation are methodologically sound. However, the six dimensions are explicitly acknowledged as non-orthogonal, so single-dimension estimates are not independent coefficients for isolated linguistic features. Most configurations use only one AI review per manuscript (repeated reviews appear only in an auxiliary audit), so per-paper sampling noise from the reviewer's own stochasticity is not fully characterized. Some strata are very sparse (the [8,10] human-OA range is marked † with fewer than five papers in many cells, and several entries are exactly 0.000 because no papers fall there), making the high-quality-range estimates fragile.
- **Reproducibility (medium).** Inputs are public (OpenReview metadata, arXiv LaTeX sources) and the project page is released, but the exact proprietary model versions (GPT-5.5, Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, Gemini 3.5 FL, Qwen 3.5 F) are moving targets that providers can silently update, and the ~$29k API cost is a substantial replication barrier.
- **Significance (high).** The findings directly inform the live debate on AI in peer review: they show strict prompting is not a defense against rhetorical bias, that score inflation from rewriting is real but selective, and that rewriter–reviewer compatibility can masquerade as scientific improvement.
- **Red flags.** The "reward-hack" framing in the title is more provocative than the results support — the paper's own conclusion stresses that rhetoric is "selective, configuration-dependent, and sometimes counterproductive," not a universal exploit. Non-random missingness (some reviews failed, plausibly because paper topics triggered model safeguards) could bias aggregate estimates in ways the bootstrap cannot capture. The cross-batch comparisons (joint vs. six single rewrites; guided vs. unguided) are explicitly descriptive rather than causal, which the authors state but readers may overlook.

## Strengths

- Large-scale, controlled, content-preserving design that isolates rhetoric from merit far more cleanly than cross-paper observational studies.
- Bidirectional rewriting per dimension cleanly separates directional preference from generic rewriting artifacts.
- Honest reporting of heterogeneous and null results (e.g., GPT-5.5 joint rewriting near zero; reviewer guidance no better than repetition) rather than cherry-picking favorable configurations.
- Stratification by both human-assessed quality and the AI reviewer's own starting score reveals that starting score, not human quality, drives direction of movement — an important and non-obvious finding.
- Structural-anchor verification + compile-and-repair gives a credible content-preservation guarantee without forcing sentence-level semantic equivalence.
- Clear role separation (rewriter shapes contrast, reviewer shapes score effect) is a usable mental model for future work.

## Limitations

- Benchmark is restricted to ICLR 2026 submissions with recoverable full-paper source and public review metadata; generalization to other venues or scientific fields is untested.
- Full-paper rewriting and multi-model evaluation are computationally expensive; some planned reviews did not return valid structured records, and this missingness is likely non-random (topic-related safeguard triggers).
- The six rhetorical dimensions are not orthogonal — each rewrite is a full-paper intervention, so effects should not be read as independent coefficients for single linguistic features.
- Most configurations use a single AI review per manuscript, with repeated reviews examined only in an auxiliary audit; results characterize the tested models and prompts, not human review or the full distribution of repeated-model sampling.
- Sparse high-quality strata ([8,10]) make some stratified estimates noisy; the "reward hack" framing in the title overstates the universality that the body of the paper carefully tempers.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to anyone studying LLM-as-a-judge robustness, AI-assisted peer review, or evaluation biases in scientific NLP: it provides a concrete, large-scale map of which rhetorical levers move AI-reviewer scores and shows that neither strict prompting nor more elaborate rewriting reliably closes the vulnerability. For research on building evaluation systems robust to content-preserving variation, the controlled corpus and dimensional taxonomy are immediately reusable.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the dimensional hierarchy (evidence > novelty > scope) replicate on non-ML venues (e.g., biology, physics) or on venues with very different reviewer guidelines such as ARR/ACL?
- How much of the rewriter-dependent gap (Opus 4.8 vs. GPT-5.5 joint rewriting) is attributable to differences in how faithfully each model obeys the content-preservation constraint versus how aggressively it strengthens rhetoric?
- Since most configs use a single review per manuscript, what is the within-reviewer variance of OA for identical manuscripts, and does averaging multiple reviews shrink or preserve the rhetorical effects?
- Could a reviewer-side intervention (e.g., requiring quoted-evidence for each rubric score, or scoring on anonymized abstract+figures only) reduce sensitivity to evidence/novelty framing more effectively than the strict prompt did?
- The starting-score regression-to-the-mean pattern is large; can a within-paper normalization or anchoring correction recover the intended directional signal at the extremes?
- Are the Sonnet 5 downward shifts and the non-random missingness (safeguard-triggered review failures) correlated with specific paper topics, and would their removal change the aggregate estimates materially?
