> **一句话总结：** 本文将含缺失数据的监督学习重新建模为一个闭环自适应动力系统，把数据缺失视为对参数误差动力学可控性（actuation authority）的结构性损失，并基于类 Gramian 的可观测性矩阵推导出方向感知（directional observability-aware）的自适应更新律 `w_{t+1} = w_t - η B_t^{-1} ∇ℓ_t`，在有限窗口持续激励下给出 ISS 型的残差到状态（residual-to-state）界，在 HAR 人体活动识别与北极海冰浓度遥感任务的重度缺失场景中显著优于掩码 SGD。

> **TL;DR:** The authors recast supervised learning under missing data as a closed-loop adaptive control problem where missingness is a structured loss of actuation, and propose a directional observability-aware update `w_{t+1}=w_t-η B_t^{-1}∇ℓ_t` with a Gramian-like EMA matrix `B_t`; a Lyapunov/ISS analysis yields `‖e_t‖≤A q^{⌊t/T⌋}‖e_0‖+Lδ` under windowed excitation. On the HAR dataset the directional method beats masked SGD in all 24 logistic-regression class/step cells (mean gain +0.0765 F1, +69% relative for "sitting" at t=100: 0.430→0.727), and on the ASID-v2 sea-ice concentration benchmark the controlled learner reaches validation MAE 7.59 vs masked SGD 12.13 vs full SGD 9.97 over the first 5k steps of rotation A.

## 核心贡献

- **将含缺失数据的学习重构为闭环控制问题**：把模型参数视为系统状态，把数据缺失建模为对参数状态可控性（actuation authority）的结构性、方向性损失，而非统计噪声；学习率、更新方向与正则强度构成控制输入。
- **指出标准掩码 SGD 的失效机理是动力学的而非统计的**：缺失并不通过移动未观测参数来破坏学习，而是因为被观测参数"像未观测参数也在协同演化一样"地自由移动，导致内部不一致、参数漂移与"幻觉式"更新。
- **提出标量与方向两种可观测性感知（observability-aware）自适应律**：标量律用 `α_t=η/(ϵ+γ_t)`（`γ_t=‖x̃_t‖²`）做各向同性节流；方向律用类 Gramian 矩阵 `B_t=β`-EMA(`A_t=g_t g_t^T`)+`ϵI`，更新律 `w_{t+1}=w_t-η B_t^{-1}∇ℓ_t`，按近期激励的方向结构调节更新。
- **给出 Lyapunov/ISS 型稳定性证明（命题1）**：在持续（有限窗口）激励、扇区有界标量反馈与有界闭环残差失配 `|δ_t|≤δ` 前提下，得到残差到状态界 `‖e_t‖≤A q^{⌊t/T⌋}‖e_0‖+Lδ`（`q∈(0,1)`），适用于线性、核方法与梯度网络。
- **可扩展的实用近似**：对角、块对角（按层/注意力头分块）、低秩三种近似，使方法可部署到 36 万参数卷积模型；明确指出无穷维 RKHS 上不做全空间有限窗口收缩保证。
- **从合成线性回归到真实多模态任务的三组实验验证**：在重度缺失（同时缺失多个传感模态）下持续保持学习连贯性与稳定性，并在若干区间甚至超过完全观测的 SGD 基线。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframing learning-with-missing-data as closed-loop adaptive control**: model parameters are the system state, and data missingness is modeled as a structured, directional loss of actuation authority over the parameter error dynamics rather than as stochastic noise; the learning rate, update direction, and regularization are the control inputs.
- **Identifying a dynamical (not statistical) failure mode of masked SGD**: masking does not destabilize learning by moving hidden parameters, but by letting observed parameters move as if the hidden ones were also evolving consistently, producing internal inconsistency, parameter drift, and "hallucinated" updates (Section 2).
- **Two observability-aware adaptation laws**: a scalar controller `α_t=η/(ϵ+γ_t)` with `γ_t=‖x̃_t‖²` (isotropic throttling), and a directional controller using a Gramian-like EMA matrix `S_t=βS_{t-1}+(1-β)A_t`, `A_t=g_t g_t^T`, `g_t=∇_w ŷ_t(w_t,x̃_t)`, `B_t=S_t+ϵI`, with update law `w_{t+1}=w_t-η B_t^{-1}∇ℓ_t` that normalizes current actuation against its recent directional scale (Eq. 19).
- **A Lyapunov/ISS-style stability theorem (Proposition 1)**: under bounded instantaneous sensitivity, sector-bounded scalar feedback, a bounded closed-loop residual mismatch `|δ_t|≤δ`, and windowed effective observability, the parameter error satisfies `‖e_t‖≤A q^{⌊t/T⌋}‖e_0‖+Lδ` with `q∈(0,1)`; specialized to linear regression, finite-dimensional kernel realizations, and gradient networks.
- **Scalable approximations and honest scope boundaries**: diagonal, block-diagonal (per-layer/head), and low-rank realizations; explicit admission that no full-space finite-window contraction is claimed on a genuinely infinite-dimensional RKHS, and that the guarantee holds only on a finite-dimensional invariant active subspace.
- **Empirical validation from synthetic to real multimodal regimes**: three experiments (synthetic linear regression, HAR activity recognition, ASID-v2 sea-ice concentration) showing the controller maintains learning coherence and stability under heavy, concurrent modality loss and sometimes surpasses the fully-observed SGD baseline.

## Methodology

The paper builds the framework in three layers.

**1. Diagnostic prototype (Section 2).** For masked linear regression `y_t=⟨w*,x_t⟩`, `x̃_t=M_t x_t`, the residual decomposes as `r_t=-⟨e_t,x̃_t⟩ + d_t`, where `d_t=⟨w*,(I-M_t)x_t⟩` is an unobservable disturbance from masked components that the learner cannot separate using the masked loss alone. The authors argue standard masked SGD applies control inputs designed for a fully actuated system to a partially actuated one: some coordinates evolve while others are frozen, even though the residual implicitly couples them, inducing relative misalignment and instability. They introduce a scalar observability-aware gain `α_t=η/(ϵ+γ_t)` and, via the Lyapunov function `V_t=‖e_t‖²`, derive an ISS-style bound `‖e_t‖≤ρ^t‖e_0‖+C·sup|d_τ|`.

**2. Directional observability-aware learning (Sections 4–5).** The key object is the rank-one actuation operator `A_t=g_t g_t^T` with `g_t=∇_w ŷ_t(w_t,x̃_t)` (the model-output Jacobian row transposed), which captures both the energy and the directions of current actuation. Because `A_t` is volatile and rank-one, they aggregate it into an EMA state `S_t=βS_{t-1}+(1-β)A_t` and form the regularized Gramian-like matrix `B_t=S_t+ϵI`. The proposed update is `w_{t+1}=w_t-η B_t^{-1}∇ℓ_t` (Eq. 19). Decomposing along eigenvectors `v_i` of `S_t` (eigenvalue `λ_i`) shows the per-direction update scales as `-η·√λ_i/(λ_i+ϵ)·ζ_{i,t}·∂ℓ/∂ŷ`, so strongly/repeatedly excited directions are normalized (preventing aggressive repeated motion) and vanishingly excited directions produce vanishing updates while the ridge `ϵI` guards singularity. A variational interpretation (Eq. 21) recovers the update as the minimizer of linearized loss reduction plus motion measured in the observability geometry. Scalar throttling is recovered as the isotropic special case `B_t≈(ϵ+γ_t)I`.

**3. Stability analysis (Section 5, Proposition 1).** Writing the scalar-output gradient as `∇ℓ_t=s_t g_t`, the closed-loop error dynamics are `e_{t+1}=e_t-η B_t^{-1} g_t s_t`. Under Assumption 1 (bounded sensitivity `‖g_t‖≤G`), Assumption 2 (sector-bounded scalar feedback `m z²≤zφ(z)≤M z²`), Assumption 3 (a priori bounded closed-loop residual mismatch `|δ_t|≤δ`), and Assumptions 4–5 (windowed observability in raw and update geometry, requiring recurrent excitation with window `T≥d` in `R^d`), Lemmas 1–4 yield the windowed residual-to-state bound `‖e_t‖≤A q^{⌊t/T⌋}‖e_0‖+Lδ` with explicit (conservative) constants. Lemma 2 gives a sufficient bridge from raw to effective excitation when the inverse metric varies coherently over the window (`Gν_T<√(λ_g/b̄)`). Practical approximations (diagonal, block-diagonal per layer/head, low-rank) preserve the conditioning principle; the sea-ice CNN uses a block-diagonal `A_t`.

**Coherence and stability metrics (Section 3).** Beyond `‖e_t‖`, the authors define update coherence (windowed cosine similarity of consecutive updates), trajectory smoothness (second-order differences), final stability (std of `‖w_t‖` late in training), and a fitted Lyapunov contraction rate, to make the qualitative notions empirically visible.

## Results & Highlights

The paper reports three experiments, all under scalar-output supervision and heavy missingness, evaluating on the *clean, fully-observed* test/validation set.

- **Experiment 6.1 — synthetic linear regression, d=40, 15 repetitions.** Across all 8 missingness/step-size regimes (block/iid × moderate/heavy × η∈{0.020,0.008}), the directional method drastically cuts "severe misalignment" occupation time (Coherence(t) ≤ −0.04) versus masked SGD. For block missingness with η=0.020 the directional method spends only **3.6%** of steps in severe misalignment versus **25.7%** for masked SGD and **12.9%** for scalar-aware (Table 1); directional severe-misalignment ranges 3.6%–7.7% across all regimes versus 8.7%–26.3% for masked SGD. The authors explicitly include a larger-vs-smaller learning-rate comparison to rule out the "this is just slower SGD" explanation: reducing η improves the masked baseline but does not reproduce observability-aware behavior.

- **Experiment 6.2 — HAR dataset, heavy missingness (4 of 6 sensing families hidden per sample, plus 0.8 within-group retention), logistic regression and a 561→12→1 shallow net, 6 one-vs-rest classifiers, full unmasked test set.** The directional method **exceeds masked SGD in all 24 class/step entries** for logistic regression, with an **average gain of +0.0765 F1 (~9.4% relative improvement in overall mean F1)**, and exceeds fully-observed full SGD in 7/24 entries (Table 2). The largest masked→directional improvement is the "sitting" class at t=100: mean per-class F1 rises from **0.430 (masked) to 0.727 (directional)** — a +0.297 gain (~69% relative) — while full SGD sits at 0.660. For the shallow neural network, directional beats masked SGD in 23/24 entries and beats full SGD in 19/24 entries; where masked training collapses for "sitting" (0.015/0.004/0.005 at t=200/500/1000), the directional method recovers to 0.034/0.388/0.636.

- **Experiment 6.3 — ASID-v2 sea-ice concentration (Sentinel-1 SAR + AMSR2, convolutional two-branch regressor with 368,753 trainable parameters, block-diagonal controller), clean full-feature validation MAE across three split rotations.** In rotation A over 0–5k steps the controlled learner reaches window-averaged validation MAE **7.59 versus 12.13 for masked SGD and 9.97 for full SGD**, beating masked SGD at 90% of checkpoints, with a **+34.0 pp gain in "good-region" occupation time and a +36.0 pp reduction in "bad-excursion" occupation time**, and a calmer trajectory (mean checkpoint-to-checkpoint |ΔMAE| **1.39 vs 4.47** for masked). In the earliest window (rotation A, 0–1k) the controlled learner's MAE is **8.65 vs masked 22.80 vs full 14.08**, and it is the best of the three at 90% of checkpoints; rotation B's 0–1k window shows a **+90.0 pp bad-excursion reduction** relative to masked SGD (Table 3).

- The authors cautiously interpret the cases where the controller *surpasses* the fully-observed SGD baseline as evidence that the observability-aware geometry can also act as a beneficial preconditioner/conditioner of the optimization dynamics, not merely a missing-data repair.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The reframing is genuinely fresh — treating missing data as a directional loss of actuation and importing Gramian/observability concepts from adaptive control is distinct from imputation, masked losses, Bayesian marginalization, DRO, and bandit-style online learning (Sections 7.1–7.5). The diagonal approximation is superficially reminiscent of RMSProp/Adam, but the authors correctly distinguish it: `B_t`'s diagonal accumulates coordinatewise *model-sensitivity (Jacobian) actuation*, not uncentered second moments of loss gradients.

- **Rigor**: The Lyapunov analysis is complete and the constants are explicit. However, **the empirical baselines are weak**: every comparison is against fixed-step "full SGD" and fixed-step "masked SGD" — there is *no* comparison against Adam/RMSProp (the most natural competitors, given the diagonal realization), against any imputation method, or against Bayesian/ensemble uncertainty baselines the authors critique in Section 7. This leaves open how much of the gain is the observability-aware geometry vs. simply a better-conditioned per-coordinate adaptive step. The "controlled beats full SGD" claims in 6.2–6.3 are striking but should be read against a possibly under-tuned full-SGD baseline (fixed step size, no modern optimizer, no tuning reported).

- **Reproducibility**: Hyperparameters are reported (ϵ=1 for scalar, ϵ=0.05, β=0.99 for directional in 6.1; full-order and block-diagonal realizations described in Appendices C.1–C.2). Datasets are public. But **no code or configuration repository is referenced**, and the sea-ice pipeline "differs from the original pixelwise sea ice mapping objective" in unspecified appendix details, making exact replication nontrivial.

- **Significance**: The contribution is positioned at the "workflow-enabling" level (learning in pathologically sparse, sensor-fusion, cyber-physical regimes) rather than as a SOTA accuracy claim. This is a defensible and arguably under-served niche, but the impact is presently limited to scalar-output, small-to-mid-scale models (largest is 368K params); the transformer/autoencoder extensions are explicitly left as future work.

- **Red flags / caveats specific to this paper**:
  - **Only scalar-output models are analyzed or tested** (the authors concede multivariate output is future work), so applicability to real multi-task/decoder outputs is unproven.
  - **The bounded-residual-mismatch assumption (Assumption 3, `|δ_t|≤δ`) must be "certified independently"** of Proposition 1 — e.g., via projection, saturation, or a separate invariant-set argument; for a merely state-dependent estimate `|δ_t|≤c_δ‖e_t‖+d` the proposition "does not by itself close that dependence" and requires an additional small-gain argument that is *not* provided.
  - **Windowed observability (Assumptions 4–5) requires `T≥d`** for a full-space guarantee; persistently unobserved directions make the method stall (acknowledged as a feature, but it bounds practical applicability when missingness is chronic).
  - **The Table 3 "good-region" / "bad-excursion" thresholds are defined per-rotation from pooled validation MAE (lower quartile and 90th percentile)**, an internally-constructed metric that makes the percentage-point gains look strong but is not a standard, externally comparable metric.
  - The analytical constants are acknowledged to be conservative ("not literal plug-in tuning prescriptions").

## Strengths

- Principled, control-theoretic reframing that yields *provable trajectory bounds* — rare in the missing-data literature, which is dominated by statistical-consistency or likelihood arguments.
- Model-agnostic: the same `B_t^{-1}∇ℓ` machinery instantiates cleanly for linear regression, kernel/RKHS methods, and gradient networks, with the Jacobian `g_t` as the unifying object.
- Never imputes or hallucinates missing values — it only regulates parameter motion in evidence-supported directions, which is genuinely safer in pathologically sparse regimes (missing 4/6 modalities) where imputation would be guessing.
- Empirical gains in heavy-missingness regimes are large and consistent (24/24 logistic-regression cells in HAR; controlled MAE roughly half of masked SGD in early sea-ice windows).
- Honest and unusually detailed treatment of scope limits (Section 5.5): finite-dimensional restriction, stall behavior under chronic missingness, distinction between trajectory controllability and functional/identifiability properties.

## Limitations

- **Scalar-output only**; extension to multivariate/multi-task outputs and modern decoders is left open.
- **Small-to-mid-scale experiments only**; the largest model is a ~368K-parameter CNN. No transformer, autoencoder, or large-scale pretraining evaluation despite these being named as motivation.
- **Baselines are thin**: no Adam/RMSProp, no imputation, no Bayesian/ensemble baselines — so the marginal contribution of the observability geometry over standard adaptive optimizers is unknown.
- **Theoretical constants are conservative** and the key bounded-mismatch assumption `|δ_t|≤δ` and windowed-excitation condition (`T≥d`) need independent certification that the paper does not always provide.
- **No released code**, and some sea-ice pipeline details are deferred to the appendix.
- The most eye-catching results (beating full SGD) rest on an under-tuned full-SGD reference and an internally-defined occupation-time metric, tempering strong claims.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the directional controller compare against Adam/RMSProp and against modern imputation/autoencoder baselines (e.g., ReMasker) on the same HAR and sea-ice tasks — is the gain over masked SGD actually attributable to the observability geometry or partly to per-coordinate adaptive scaling?
- Can the analysis be extended to multivariate/decoder outputs and to transformer-scale models while retaining the `B_t^{-1}` machinery under a block-diagonal or low-rank `A_t`?
- The Assumption 3 bound `|δ_t|≤δ` must be certified externally; for realistic deep models, can a practical state-dependent small-gain or projection mechanism make this bound verifiable rather than assumed?
- Does the "controlled beats full SGD" preconditioning effect persist when the full-SGD baseline is properly tuned (e.g., with Adam, cosine schedules, tuned step size), or does it vanish?
- The windowed-excitation condition requires `T≥d` for a full-space guarantee; how does the method behave empirically when a meaningful parameter subspace is chronically unobserved (chronic sensor failure), and can a low-rank active-subspace realization recover useful guarantees there?
- Could the framework be composed with self-supervised/streaming objectives, where temporally correlated missingness is the norm, as the authors suggest?
