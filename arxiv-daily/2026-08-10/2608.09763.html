Why this matters

A clean coordinate-consistent framework for second-order Muon, a reusable 20% step-time saving — and a grokking-delay collapse worth replicating.

Critical edge

The 8–20.5× grokking claim rests on one seed per modulus with asymmetric LR grids — Muon was never even run at the LR that won for GO-Muon.

01 Methodology

The support problem maximizes ⟨M, D⟩ subject to ‖P_B⁻¹ D P_A⁻¹‖_op ≤ ρ. Operator/nuclear-norm duality gives the maximizer D = ρ P_B Q(P_B M P_A) P_A, where Q(·) is the polar factor. The paper's central move decouples this exactness from estimation quality: the oracle is exact for whatever SPD pair (P_A, P_B) you supply — whether that geometry is useful is a separate question.

GO-Muon instantiates P_A = A_t⁻¹ᐟ⁴ from layer-input second moments and P_B = B̄_t⁻¹ᐟ⁴ from OAS-shrunk observed-label backward-gradient second moments. The quarter power is the operator-norm analogue of the K-FAC block's square root; α = 0, ¼, ½ recover Muon, GO-Muon, and full-factor map-back respectively. A Frobenius graft restores Muon's update energy while preserving the oracle's ray and sign.

The systems contribution is refresh amortization: curvature factors recompute every K = 4 steps via armed forward/backward hooks; momentum, the polar map, graft, and the parameter update still run every step. Prop. 6 proves this trades ≈4× stationary variance for only ≈0.04 steps of extra lag.

03 Strengths

  • Clean decoupling. "Oracle is exact for supplied geometry" vs "geometry may be bad" — Corollary 2 turns this into a usable guarantee: cached factors can never yield negative source alignment.
  • Concretely convincing §2.2 counterexample showing inside-only preconditioning (used by several concurrent Muon variants) can be sign-wrong.
  • Prop. 6 is a real result: lazy refresh at β=0.97, K=4 costs 3.995× variance for only 0.038 steps extra lag — reframes staleness as compute-for-variance, not denoising.
  • Unusually candid reporting: post-hoc selection-rule change, unmeasured Θ_t, un-imputed missing window, mod-107 retention failure, and 2/4 win rates are all disclosed.
  • Practical guard rails: Eq. (20) four-term error decomposition + OAS conditioning bound for K-FAC-style optimizer builders.

04 Limitations

  • Training loss only on sub-1M-parameter, 3-layer/128-width models for 1000 updates — no validation, no test, no scaling study.
  • Unmatched learning rates (0.020 vs 0.080 on PTB); grokking LR grids barely overlap — Muon was never evaluated at 0.08.
  • Grokking is a "screen": one seed per modulus, two moduli (103, 107) only.
  • No comparison against closest concurrent methods (Mousse, FISMO) or classical baselines (Shampoo, SOAP), despite discussing all of them.
  • Missing ablations: α exponent sweep, K beyond {1,4}, OAS vs plain damping, graft vs no-graft.
  • Prop. 7's guarantee depends on drift Θ_t, which is never measured — so the cached-geometry bound is untested in practice.

05 Open questions

  1. Does the 8–20.5× grokking speedup survive a symmetric LR grid (both optimizers over {0.04, 0.08, 0.16, 0.32}) and 5+ seeds per modulus? Muon was never run at 0.08 — how much of the gap is just LR?
  2. Why does grokking delay collapse while memorization time is unchanged? Is the quarter-power geometry acting as implicit weight-decay on the read-out, or genuinely reshaping the route to the generalizing circuit?
  3. What happens at α = ½ (full-factor map-back) or α = ⅛? The paper parameterizes α but only ever runs ¼.
  4. Does the 0.38% PTB gain hold or grow at 100M–1B parameters — and does the 20.2% step-time saving survive when the (un-skipped) polar map dominates cost at large widths?
  5. Can Θ_t be cheaply instrumented online to make K adaptive, refreshing only when coordinate drift exceeds a threshold?
  6. The mod-107 run leaves the 99% region near update 4470 — is post-grok instability a systematic cost of the quarter-power geometry, or a single-seed artifact that onset-only metrics hide?
