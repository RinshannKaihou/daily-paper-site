> **一句话总结：** MDA 把 LLM 降级为"结构提案器"，把模型选择、参数推断和实验设计全部交还给标准贝叶斯机器（SMC 求证据 + VoI 选实验 + SBI 处理难解似然），在 ForceBench 上仅用 8 次实验就达到 93% 数值通过率 / 74% 精确形式恢复率（纯 Opus 4.7 智能体同预算下仅 31% / 31%，需 ~41 次实验才能达到同等 nMSE≈0.01），在 ChemBench 上以 55.6% 符号准确率超过前 SOTA LLM-AutoSciLab 的 41.7%，且 ~8 次实验即触顶（对方需 60 次）。

> **TL;DR:** The Model Discovery Agent (MDA) uses an LLM only as a *proposer* of candidate mechanistic structures and wraps it in classical Bayesian machinery — SMC over structures and parameters for model evidence, value-of-information (VoI) for experiment design, and simulation-based/synthetic likelihoods for intractable cases — plus an M-open escape hatch that re-prompts the LLM when a predictive check fails. On ForceBench (a wrapper on DiscoverPhysics, 6 two-particle worlds, B=8 experiments, Opus 4.7) MDA hits 93% numeric pass (nMSE<0.1) and 74% exact-form recovery vs 31%/31% for the matched pure-LLM agent, and reaches the DiscoverPhysics paper's nMSE≈0.01 with 8 experiments where the un-throttled Opus agent needs ~41 (≈5× data efficiency). On ChemBench (ActiveSciBench-Chem, 36-task stratified subset, B=60) MDA reaches 55.6% symbolic accuracy / 80.6% ExAcc vs 41.7% / 69.4% for the re-run LLM-AutoSciLab SOTA (35.1% as published with gpt-4o-mini). A new NeuronBench (6 "mystery neuron" Hodgkin–Huxley worlds, partially observed, optionally stochastic) shows the Bayes-forecaster beating an in-context LLM forecaster by >2 orders of magnitude (0.15 vs 46 spikes² MSE). Worth reading if you care about agentic scientific discovery; note the honest caveat that with the strongest proposer (Fable 5) the unaided agent closes most of the gap (81% numeric, 93% exact-form).

## 核心贡献

- **方法：MDA 循环。** LLM 只负责"提出候选结构 m"，其余全部是精确贝叶斯计算：Alg.2 用 SMC 在结构空间上按边际似然（证据）重加权，Alg.3 用自适应退火 SMC 求每个结构的参数后验与证据 Z_m（自动带 Occam 惩罚），Alg.4 在随机潜动力学下用 bootstrap 粒子滤波给出无偏似然估计。
- **M-open 处理机制。** 当 MAP 模型的留出残差 r* 超过阈值 τ_r 时，触发 R_max 轮"扩张"：提示 LLM 提出全新未命名机制（赋以宽先验），加入粒子池后按证据裁剪；反之当后验集中（p* > τ_p）且拟合良好时收缩粒子池 N_m，防止近重复假设泛滥。
- **发现与设计相互强化。** VoI 选出的实验专门用来识别刚提出的新机制；机制一旦被识别，预测变准，残差中更细微的错误才暴露出来，从而触发下一轮发现（Yukawa 世界的"顿悟"：只有长程探针才把真核推到 Pareto 凸角）。
- **新基准 NeuronBench / NeuronBenchStoch。** 6 个基于广义 Hodgkin-Huxley 的"神秘神经元"，每个在 Na+K+leak 之上加一个作者自造的新通道 I_Z（防止 LLM 直接背书），设计空间为 9 种电流波形 × 4 种通道阻断剂 = 36 个离散选项；随机版本加入 Fox-Lu 门控噪声，似然不可解析。
- **三域统一引擎。** 同一套 MDA 引擎不加修改地应用于动力学 ODE（ForceBench）、静态代数律（ChemBench，R^7 连续设计空间）与部分可观测 ODE/SDE（NeuronBench）。

## Key Contributions

- **A clean division of labour**: the LLM proposes structures; SMC evidence does model selection; VoI does design; SBI handles intractable likelihoods. The paper's thesis is that the *inference layer*, not the proposer, is what buys data efficiency — and it tests this directly with a base-model sweep and a design-space control.
- **M-open extension of SMC over structures**: a predictive check (held-out interventional forecast residual) triggers hypothesis-space *expansion* via LLM re-prompting conditioned on the residuals of the *entire* particle pool (SMC-S style, not single-ancestor ModelSMC style), plus an ESS-adaptive *shrink* rule to evict near-duplicates.
- **New SOTA on two existing interactive-discovery benchmarks** (DiscoverPhysics/ForceBench, ActiveSciBench-Chem/ChemBench), against re-run rather than straw-man baselines.
- **NeuronBench**, a new benchmark adding partial observability, stochasticity, and mechanism-level interventions (channel blockers) that the existing benchmarks lack; released at github.com/murphyk/neuronbench.
- **Practical SBI engineering**: a learned 1-D CNN summary statistic gives ~10⁴× speedup over nested particle filtering, with a particle-filter *spot-check* that detects when the cheap summary is insufficient and falls back to the ~7× slower but assumption-free filter.

## Methodology

The world is modeled as a controlled, intervenable state-space model: z_{t+1} ~ p(z_{t+1} | z_t, x_t; do(a,θ)), y_t ~ p(y_t | z_t; θ), z_0 ~ p(z_0 | ι). Crucially, an experiment design ξ = (ι, a, x_{1:T}) is separated into inputs to a *fixed* mechanism (ι, x) versus an intervention do(a) that strikes θ itself and changes the mechanism. This is Pearl's level-2 causality; the paper explicitly disclaims level-3 counterfactuals.

The outer loop (Algorithm 1) is a standard sequential Bayesian experimental design loop with two additions. Inference (Algorithm 2) maintains N_m structure particles proposed by the LLM, scores each by its marginal likelihood log Ẑ_i from a per-structure adaptive-tempered SMC over N_p parameter particles (Algorithm 3, with random-walk Metropolis rejuvenation at each temperature rung), and forms p(m|D) = softmax(log Ẑ). Marginalizing parameters supplies the automatic Occam factor that produces the accuracy–complexity Pareto frontier in Fig. 1b. Design uses the Lindley objective ξ* = argmax_ξ I(M; Y_ξ | D); for deterministic dynamics with Gaussian noise this reduces to an analytic expression that picks the design with maximum posterior-predictive variance, dominated in practice by cross-model disagreement. Small design spaces are enumerated; the 7-D continuous ChemBench box uses CMA-ES at 48 objective evaluations (better than 48-sample Monte Carlo).

Likelihoods come in three flavours: exact Gaussian per-timestep for deterministic dynamics; a bootstrap particle filter (Algorithm 4) for stochastic latents; and a synthetic/factored likelihood on summary statistics s(y_{1:T}) when individual traces are too noisy for a per-timestep likelihood (NeuronBench uses spike counts with a Poisson likelihood, and a 6-feature Gaussian synthetic likelihood in the stochastic battery). Section A.4 explains why learning s by "fitting the data" collapses to a constant, and adopts the standard anti-collapse fix: train s_φ on a held-out bank of prior simulations to be predictive of (θ, m).

Three forecasters are compared: Bayes-forecast (MAP or Bayes-model-averaged model), LLM-forecast (LLM writes code for a model, as in DiscoverPhysics), and ICL-forecast (in-context LLM predicts outputs directly with no explicit model). The two canonical agents are MDA = VoI design + Bayes-forecast, and "LLM agent" = LLM design + LLM-forecast, run through an identical B≤8 protocol.

## Results & Highlights

- **ForceBench, 6 two-particle worlds, B=8, Opus 4.7 on both arms (Table 8, 9 runs/world):** MDA reaches **93% numeric pass rate (nMSE<0.1) and 74% exact-form recovery, versus 31% and 31%** for the budget-matched pure LLM agent. Grand-mean nMSE is **9.2×10⁻⁴ (MDA) vs 5.4×10⁻¹ (LLM agent)** — nearly three orders of magnitude.
- **Data-efficiency multiplier:** run in its native un-throttled batched protocol the Opus agent consumes **~41 experiments to reach nMSE 0.013**, reproducing the DiscoverPhysics paper's reported 0.01 (a useful pipeline-validation check). MDA reaches the same accuracy with **8 one-per-round experiments — a ~5× data-efficiency advantage**.
- **Design-menu control (the most convincing ablation):** an Opus agent handed MDA's identical curated 13-launch design menu scores only **22% numeric pass and 17% exact-form — worse than the same agent choosing freely (31%)**, and far below MDA's 93%. So the hand-built menu is not the source of the gain; SMC-evidence selection is.
- **Base-model sweep (Fig. 12, the honest caveat):** MDA is nearly proposer-agnostic — **89–94% numeric pass and 74–83% exact-form across Opus 4.7, Fable 5, DeepSeek v4** — while the unaided agent swings **26% (DeepSeek) → 31% (Opus) → 81% (Fable 5)**. Against Fable 5, MDA still wins numerically (**94% vs 81%**, reaching nMSE≈10⁻³ in ~2 experiments where the agent needs B=8) but **loses on pure exact-form recovery (78% after a parsimony submission rule, 74% before, vs 93%)**, and the joint exact-and-numeric metric **ties at 74% each**. On coulomb the Fable agent scores 100% exact-form but only 11% numeric, vs MDA's 67% numeric.
- **ChemBench, 36-task stratified subset, 2 seeds, B=60 (Table 9):** MDA (VoI) **55.6% symbolic accuracy / 80.6% ExAcc@0.05** vs LLM-AutoSciLab re-run with the same Opus 4.7 proposer at **41.7% / 69.4%** (and 35.1% / 50.9% as published with gpt-4o-mini). MDA wins **every difficulty tier**, including hard (**54.2% vs 41.7% SA**). Data-efficiency-wise MDA hits its ~56% SA ceiling **within ~8 experiments; LLM-AutoSciLab needs B=60 to reach 42%**.
- **Overfitting diagnosis on the hard tier (Table 1):** LLM-AutoSciLab's PySR returns `10^(0.87·log(0.5·√(Enz/(C_I+·))))` at **RMSLE 0.001** — passing even the strict 0.01 threshold — yet symbolically meaningless; MDA returns Hill×noncompetitive at RMSLE 0.018 with the correct mechanism. Low error ≠ right mechanism.
- **ChemBench ablation ladder (Fig. 16):** ModelSMC base **36% SA → +M-open exploration 50%** (easy tier **42→75%**, and the only route to any compound-mechanism recovery, **0→11%**) **→ +CMA-ES VoI** (hard tier **33→42%**, ExAcc 36→42%) **→ +ESS-adaptive pool 56% overall, hard 54%**. Each component earns a distinct tier.
- **NeuronBench (Fig. 4, 6 worlds, 3 ICs):** the Bayes-forecaster beats the in-context LLM forecaster on **every** world; within the Bayes family **VoI and LLM-proposed design perform similarly and both beat random**. In the stochastic version the gap is stark: **Bayes ≈0.15 vs ICL ≈46 spikes² MSE**, with the Bayes-forecast falling 15–20× from its N_a=0 prior.
- **Repeat-aware VoI under noise (Fig. 32):** promoting the repeat count r into the design (cost-normalized VoI) raises mean posterior on the true mechanism to **0.91 vs 0.80** for each-once/random at the noisiest rung (N=50 channels), while the policies **tie at N≥1000**.
- **Hidden-mass world (Fig. 15):** trans-dimensional selection excludes K=0 and Occam-rejects K=2; a single VoI-chosen probe drives p(K=1)→1.0 and collapses the position posterior from **σ≈0.11 to ≈0.01**.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by **Kevin Murphy**, listed at Dept. of Computer Science, University of British Columbia. Murphy is the author of the canonical *Machine Learning: A Probabilistic Perspective* / *Probabilistic Machine Learning* textbooks and a long-standing authority on exactly the machinery used here (SMC, state-space models, Bayesian model selection). The Bayesian components are textbook-correct, the pseudocode (Algorithms 1–4) is unusually complete for a conference-length paper, priors and SMC hyperparameters are tabulated (Table 2), prompts are reproduced in full in Appendix G, and LLM calls are run at temperature 0.2–0.4 in JSON mode with caching for reproducibility. NeuronBench is released. The flip side of single authorship is single-lab execution: all baselines were re-implemented or re-run by the author.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-good).** None of the individual pieces are new — Lindley VoI dates to 1956, adaptive-tempered SMC and pseudo-marginal particle filters are standard, and LLM-as-proposer is by now a crowded field (ModelSMC, SMC-S, AutoSciLab, DiscoverPhysics all pre-date this). The genuinely new bits are narrow but real: the M-open trigger (residual threshold τ_r → LLM re-prompt with pool-wide residuals) and the ESS-adaptive pool shrink. The ablation shows these two contribute 36%→56% SA on ChemBench, so they are not decorative.

**Rigor (good, with sample-size caveats).** The design-menu control and the base-model sweep are exactly the two ablations a skeptical reviewer would demand, and the author runs them and *reports the unflattering answer*: against Fable 5 the joint metric ties at 74% and the unaided agent leads on exact-form recovery 93% vs 78%. The paper says so plainly ("not an unbounded lead over any conceivable agent"). That said, run counts are thin — 9 runs per ForceBench world (3 ICs × 3 draws), **2 seeds** on ChemBench, 3 ICs on NeuronBench — and error bars are ±1 SE, not CIs. A 55.6% vs 41.7% SA gap on 36 tasks × 2 seeds is roughly 5 tasks of difference; that is suggestive rather than decisive.

**Baseline comparability (the weakest link).** The ChemBench head-to-head is not a like-for-like reproduction: the author's LLM-AutoSciLab run uses a **different, self-chosen 36-task stratified subset** (the published one is unreleased), swaps gpt-4o-mini for Opus 4.7, but **keeps Qwen2.5-7B for the method's adaptive ensemble**. So "42% vs their published 35.1%" is a re-run under altered conditions, and the 55.6-vs-41.7 comparison inherits that uncertainty. Meanwhile MDA's own proposer is Opus 4.7 throughout the headline chemistry result.

**Missing ablation — compute, not data.** The paper optimizes *experiment* efficiency and is silent on inference cost. Table 2 makes the price visible: ForceBench uses N_m≤14 structures × R_m=8 refinement rounds, i.e. **O(N_m·R_m) ≈ up to ~112 LLM proposal calls per run**, plus an inner N_p=200-particle SMC per structure per round, against an LLM agent that makes ~8 calls. There is no token, wall-clock, or dollar accounting anywhere. The claim "MDA has negligible cost" (Section C.8) is asserted, not measured. In domains where experiments are cheap relative to Opus-class inference, the trade flips entirely.

**Structural advantage on the self-built benchmark.** On NeuronBench, MDA "assumes the data can be represented by some kind of HH model" — which is exactly the family the benchmark generates from — while the ICL baseline gets no such prior. Beating an in-context forecaster by 2 orders of magnitude under those conditions is less a discovery result than a demonstration that a correctly-specified simulator beats a black-box regressor. The truly M-open case (Z-REBOUND, where the LLM omits the low-threshold inward current) is acknowledged but not quantified separately.

**The design half of the thesis underperforms in biology.** The paper's headline framing is that "discovery and design reinforce," but on deterministic NeuronBench **VoI and LLM-proposed design perform similarly**, and on stochastic NeuronBench random designs are "similar to (and arguably a tiny bit better than) VoI designs." On ChemBench, MDA(Mean) — the exploit/peak-seeking acquisition, not the discriminating one — actually **beats MDA(VoI) on the medium tier (58.3 vs 45.8 SA)**. VoI is decisive on ForceBench and on the repeat-aware noise ladder; elsewhere the forecaster axis (model-based vs LLM) matters more than the acquisition axis, which the author states explicitly. The honest reading is that the *Bayesian model selection*, not the *Bayesian design*, is doing most of the work.

**Reproducibility (mixed).** Prompts, priors, SMC settings, and the design menus are all published, and NeuronBench is on GitHub. But there is **no stated code release for MDA itself**, and all three base models named (Opus 4.7, Fable 5, DeepSeek v4 Pro) are checkpoints an external reader cannot pin down or re-run identically.

**Red flags.** (1) Every benchmark is synthetic with a known ground truth; there is no real-lab or real-instrument evaluation, which matters because the entire motivation is that "experiments are expensive." (2) The main text has **no limitations or conclusion section** — it ends at Related Work on page 9 — so the caveats live buried in Appendix C.8. (3) The "new SOTA" framing on ForceBench rests on the Opus-4.7 comparison, which the Fable-5 sweep partially deflates.

## Strengths

- The best kind of ablation: the curated-design-menu control (Opus agent with MDA's menu: 22% pass, *worse* than free choice) directly kills the most obvious confound, and the un-throttled reproduction of DiscoverPhysics's own reported nMSE≈0.01 validates the pipeline against the original paper.
- Genuine intellectual honesty about where the advantage narrows: Section C.8 concedes that the Fable-5 agent ties MDA on the joint metric and leads on exact-form, and names the mechanism (Bayesian evidence occasionally prefers a slightly over-flexible form that fits noise better) plus the fix (a parsimony submission rule, 74%→78%).
- The critique of the DiscoverPhysics LLM-judged explanation metric is well evidenced: the score is flat or non-monotonic in the number of experiments (Coulomb 0.83→0.77→0.73, Yukawa 0.37→0.70→0.60), and the paper argues convincingly that interventional out-of-distribution forecasting is the better instrument, grounding it in Richens & Everitt (2024).
- The particle-filter spot-check (Fig. 30) is a genuinely useful safety pattern: on D-TYPE the fixed-feature synthetic likelihood picks T-type Ca and the learned s_φ picks slow-Na, while only the assumption-free filter recovers the true D-type K — and the auto-selector detects the disagreement and falls back.
- The Yukawa Pareto-frontier narrative (short-range probes leave the true kernel mid-frontier; one long-range probe drops it to the convex corner) is a crisp, falsifiable illustration of *why* design and discovery couple, complete with a Bayesian description-length x-axis rather than a syntactic complexity proxy.

## Limitations

- All evaluation is on synthetic simulators with known ground truth; no wet-lab, instrument, or real observational data.
- No compute/token/wall-clock accounting, despite MDA making an order of magnitude more LLM calls than the baseline agent it beats on *experiment* count.
- Small run counts (9 runs/world on physics, 2 seeds on chemistry, 3 ICs on biology) with ±1 SE error bars.
- The advantage over a very strong free-form agent (Fable 5) is confined to the predictive/numeric axis; on structure recovery the unaided agent leads.
- ChemBench head-to-head uses a different task subset and a partially upgraded baseline configuration than the published SOTA numbers.
- MDA's hypothesis space is handed a strong correct inductive bias per domain (Green's-function force law, algebraic rate law, HH ODE); the paper does not test what happens when that outer assumption is wrong.
- VoI's benefit is domain-dependent and near-zero on the stochastic neuron battery relative to random design; the "design" half of the title is carried mainly by physics and by the repeat-count extension.
- Level-2 causality only; explicitly out of scope for counterfactual (level-3) reasoning.

## Relevance

如果你关注 LLM agent 用于科学发现、或者在做"LLM 提案 + 显式概率推断"的混合架构，这篇给出了一个干净且可复现的分工范式：把 LLM 限制在结构提案上、把模型选择和实验设计交给 SMC 证据与 VoI，并用 ForceBench 上的设计菜单对照实验证明增益来自推断层而非提示工程。它同时是一份关于"低预测误差 ≠ 正确机制"的实证材料（ChemBench 上 RMSLE=0.001 却符号错误的例子），对任何用 held-out loss 做 LLM-as-judge 或自动评估的工作都有直接借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the actual token/dollar/wall-clock cost of MDA versus the LLM agent per unit of held-out accuracy? At N_m≤14 × R_m=8, does MDA remain preferable when experiments are cheap and inference is not?
2. Does the M-open trigger work when the LLM's proposal distribution genuinely cannot reach the truth (Z-REBOUND is flagged as such a miss but not isolated)? What is the recovery rate as a function of how far the truth sits outside the proposer's prior?
3. The residual threshold τ_r (0.18 on NeuronBench, 0.05 on ChemBench) and concentration threshold τ_p=0.9 are hand-tuned per domain. How sensitive is performance to them, and can they be set adaptively from the noise floor?
4. With Fable 5 the unaided agent reaches 81% numeric pass. Is there a base-model capability level at which the Bayesian layer stops paying for itself on the predictive metric, or does the parsimony/evidence machinery keep a persistent structure-quality edge?
5. Would VoI beat the LLM's own design choices in a domain where the design space is genuinely large and unstructured, rather than a curated 13-launch menu or a 36-option protocol grid?
6. Can the learned summary s_φ be trained *jointly* across an expanding hypothesis pool without the repeated refits that Algorithm 2 currently requires whenever the pool changes?
7. How does MDA behave when the observation model itself is misspecified — e.g. heteroscedastic or heavy-tailed noise — given that the evidence-based selection is precisely the component most sensitive to likelihood misspecification?
