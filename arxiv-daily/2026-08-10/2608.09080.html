Forensic brief · LLM calibration · clinical safety

Confidence is decoupled from truth — strip away the right answer and clinical LLMs confidently hallucinate rather than abstain.

When Confidence Fails: Overconfidence in LLMs under Uncertainty and Missing Clinical Information / Maryam Tahermazandarani et al. / Macquarie University / 2026-08-10 llm-calibration medical-qa overconfidence uncertainty-estimation
Key metric
80.8%

of cases where GPT-5 confidently picks a wrong option after the gold answer is removed (Setting B). The strongest model hallucinates the most.

Why this matters

Self-reported confidence from clinical LLMs does not track real uncertainty — a direct safety warning against trusting them in medical decisions.

Critical edge

n = 500 questions from a single dataset, with no confidence intervals on the headline ECE / UCER numbers — they could be statistical noise.

Methodology

Two probes that isolate distinct failures

Setting A · uncertainty framing Setting B · answer removal

Setting A injects four graded linguistic cues — C0→C3, from silence to “information may be insufficient for a certain diagnosis” — while the answer options and gold label stay fixed. Only the wording changes, so the test isolates how well models read prompt-level ambiguity.

Setting B deletes the correct option and replaces it with an explicit “Insufficient information” abstention choice (position randomized). Any non-abstention answer is, by construction, a hallucination.

Two confidence signals are logged per response: a verbalized self-report and a logit-based softmax over A/B/C/D. Reliability is scored with ECE (10 bins), a signed calibration gap, and the safety metric UCER — the share of answers that are both wrong and confident (τ = 0.8).

500 MedMCQA items · temperature 0 · 4-bit open weights on T4 · McNemar + Wilcoxon with Holm–Bonferroni correction.

Strengths
  • Two-axis design cleanly separates linguistic ambiguity from information absence.
  • Dual confidence signal (self-report + logit) exposes a gap single-signal studies miss.
  • UCER directly counts “confident wrong” answers — more decision-relevant than accuracy or ECE alone.
  • Paired statistics with Holm–Bonferroni correction — rare in LLM behavioral work.
  • Deterministic protocol: temp 0, fixed seed, randomized abstention position.
Limitations
  • n = 500 from one dataset; no bootstrapped CIs on ECE / UCER / gap.
  • Synthetic C0–C3 cues, not genuinely ambiguous real clinical notes.
  • No principled abstention baselines — only a naive single-shot prompt.
  • Multiple-choice only; no free-form, interactive, or multimodal settings.
  • Fixed 5-model snapshot; no medical-specialized LLMs (Med-PaLM, Meditron).
  • Abstract claims accuracy “degrades” under framing; the body shows it is stable.
Open questions for follow-up
01Do the overconfidence / UCER findings hold on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (MedQA-USMLE, PubMedQA) or free-form vignettes?
02Can cheap post-hoc fixes (CoT, self-consistency, verbalized elicitation) push abstention above ~40%, or just redistribute errors?
03Is the self-vs-logit gap a general property of instruction-tuned open-weight models — does it shrink with scale or decoding temperature?
04How sensitive are UCER rankings to the threshold τ (0.8 here) at, say, 0.6 or 0.9?
05Would fine-tuning on an explicit “abstain when uncertain” objective transfer to answer-removal, or does forced-answering bias re-emerge OOD?
06Would medical-specialized LLMs (Meditron, Med-PaLM-2, BioMistral) change the proprietary-vs-open-weight calibration story?
