> **一句话总结：** 作者证明"预训练是可选的"：只要给一个权重完全随机、从未训练过的单层 softmax attention 网络前置一段显式构造的 soft prompt（作为线性系统 $\widetilde{W}p_i=v_i$ 的最小范数解），它就能在紧致流形上逼近任意 Hölder 函数——因为该 prompt 迫使 attention 的前向传播复现 Nadaraya-Watson 核估计器，从而继承 $\tilde{O}(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d_X)})$ 的 minimax 最优率（只依赖内蕴维度 $d_X$，与环境维度无关），代价是 prompt token 范数随 $n^{2/(2\alpha+d_X)}$ 增长。

> **TL;DR:** A single-layer softmax-attention network with i.i.d. Gaussian frozen weights is a universal approximator *purely through its prompt*: the authors solve a small linear system to build a soft prompt of length $n$ (one token per support point) that makes the frozen network emulate the Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimator, inheriting the minimax rate $\tilde{O}(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d_X)})$ on a $d_X$-dimensional manifold. On the paper's only benchmark — regression of a degree-4 spherical-harmonic function on $S^2 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ ($\alpha=1$, $d_X=2$, so the predicted slope is $n^{-1/2}$) with $d_h=256$ and $n$ up to 1024 — the constructed prompt's MSE is visually indistinguishable from the oracle empirical NW estimator (baseline B1) and tracks the $n^{-1/2}$ slope, while the "trained weights, no prompt" baseline (B3) stays flat at MSE $\approx 1$ for every $n$; the sup-squared error to the oracle kernel falls from $\sim10^{1}$ at $n=8$ to $\sim10^{-16}$ at $n=1024$ (the predicted $n^{-2}$). Read it if you care about the *theory* of prompting expressivity / reachability; it is a pure approximation-theory paper with synthetic toy experiments and no real LLM.

## 核心贡献

- **Prompt 可复现 NW 核估计器（Theorem 1）：** 给定任意满足秩条件的单层 softmax attention transformer 和任意 $n$ 个支撑点，显式构造长度为 $n$ 的 soft prompt，使输出逼近以这些点为基础的 Nadaraya-Watson 估计器，误差 $O\big(\frac{1}{n^2 e^{2\beta}}\exp(3R^2/h^2)\big)$。构造方法是把 attention logit 与高斯核指数逐项配平，得到线性系统 $\widetilde{W}p_i = v_i$，取 Moore-Penrose 伪逆的最小范数解。
- **用 logit shifting 抑制 query 自注意力：** 标准 self-attention 中 query 会与自身做注意力，这一项在核估计器里不存在。作者引入 logit 偏移 $\beta\ge0$（吸收进齐次坐标那一行的约束 $W_{d_{in}+1}p_i = -\|x_i\|^2/2h^2 + \beta$），使该干扰项以 $e^{-\beta}$ 指数级衰减。
- **秩条件是"通有"的（Theorem 3, 5）：** Condition 1（$\widetilde{W}$ 行满秩）在 Gaussian 初始化下几乎必然成立，用随机矩阵论的奇异值尾界证明；进一步推广到 embedding/decoding 也是随机仿射映射的"完全随机 transformer"。
- **定量万有逼近定理（Theorems 2, 4, 6）：** 通过调节带宽 $h=n^{-1/(2\alpha+d_X)}$ 与 logit shift $\beta$，得到 $\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{G}(H_{P^*,x})-f(x))^2]=O(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d_X)}(\log n)^{1+3d_X/2})$，即噪声非参回归在流形上的 minimax 最优率（差对数因子），主导项只依赖内蕴维度 $d_X$ 而非环境维度 $d_{in}$。
- **量化 prompting 的代价：** token 范数在三种设定下分别为 $O(n^{2/(2\alpha+d_X)})$（确定性权重）、$O(n^{2/(2\alpha+d_X)}/\sqrt{d_h}+1)$（随机 attention）、$O(n^{2/(2\alpha+d_X)}/d_h+\sqrt{d_h})$（完全随机），揭示 prompt 范数、prompt 长度、隐藏维度三者的权衡；完全随机情形的 $\sqrt{d_h}$ 项来自必须用更大的 $\beta$ 去抵消被放大的 query 自注意力。

## Key Contributions

- **Pretraining is optional, in an approximation-theoretic sense.** Prior prompting-expressivity results (Wang et al. 2023; Hu et al. 2025; Petrov et al. 2024a; Nakada et al. 2025) all hand-pick transformer weights that training may never reach. This paper removes that crutch: the weights are i.i.d. Gaussian and frozen, and only a rank condition — provably satisfied almost surely — is needed.
- **A constructive, closed-form prompt.** The soft prompt is not obtained by optimization but by a pseudoinverse: $p_i^* = \widetilde{W}^\dagger v_i$ with $v_i = [x_i/h^2,\; -\|x_i\|^2/2h^2+\beta,\; y_i]^\top$. It is one prompt per target function, independent of the query, and prepended like an ICL context.
- **Logit shifting as a mechanism.** A clean trick to kill the query-self-attention term at rate $e^{-\beta}$ without modifying the architecture (no causal mask, no cross-attention), trading token magnitude for prompt length — argued to match real-world preferences.
- **Rates that inherit kernel-regression optimality.** Because the network becomes an NW estimator, all the classical bias/variance/noise machinery (Bickel & Li 2007 style) transfers, giving intrinsic-dimension-dependent minimax rates.
- **Three-regime cost accounting** (deterministic attention, random attention, fully random transformer), including the observation that random dense embeddings spread the linear constraints across all $d_h$ coordinates, improving $\sigma_{\min}(\widetilde{W}_{\text{aff}})$ from $\Omega(\sqrt{d_h})$ to $\Omega(d_h)$ — but at the cost of a much larger required logit shift.

## Methodology

The hypothesis class is deliberately minimal: $G = D \circ A \circ E$, with one single-head softmax self-attention layer $A(H)=\mathrm{softmax}(HW_Q(HW_K)^\top/\sqrt{d_h})HW_V$, no feedforward block, no residual, no layer norm. In the base setting the embedding is homogeneous with zero padding, $E(x)=[x^\top,1,0,\dots,0]$, and the decoder reads the bottom-right scalar of the attention output.

The key algebra: after expanding the last row of $A(H_{P,x})$, the numerator is $\sum_i \exp(x_{\text{emb}}Wp_i)p_i^\top w_V + \exp(x_{\text{emb}}Wx_{\text{emb}}^\top)x_{\text{emb}}w_V$ with $W=W_QW_K^\top/\sqrt{d_h}$ and $w_V$ the last column of $W_V$. Meanwhile the Gaussian-kernel NW estimator, after cancelling the $i$-independent $\exp(-\|x\|^2/2h^2)$ factor, has exponents $x^\top x_i/h^2 - \|x_i\|^2/2h^2$ and weights $y_i$. Matching term by term gives three constraints per prompt token: $W_{1:d_{in}}p_i = x_i/h^2$ (kernel direction), $W_{d_{in}+1}p_i = -\|x_i\|^2/2h^2 + \beta$ (bias/normalization plus logit shift), and $w_V^\top p_i = y_i$ (value readout). Stacking these into $\widetilde{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{(d_{in}+2)\times d_h}$ yields the linear system; full row rank (Condition 1) guarantees solvability, and $\|p_i^*\|\le\|\widetilde{W}^\dagger\|\|v_i\| = O(\beta+h^{-2})/\sigma_{\min}(\widetilde{W})$.

The universal-approximation theorems decompose the squared error into four terms: (I) prompt-to-kernel-estimator gap (Theorem 1), (II) noisy-vs-noiseless empirical estimator (Lemma 1), (III) empirical-vs-integral variance (Lemma 2), (IV) kernel bias on the manifold (Lemma 3). Only term I involves the transformer. Assumptions: $X$ a compact $d_X$-manifold with positive reach $\tau_X$ isometrically embedded in $\mathbb{R}^{d_{in}}$, $\|x\|\le R$; $f\in C^\alpha_L(X)$ with $0<\alpha\le1$; bounded mean-zero label noise $|\varepsilon_i|\le S_{\text{noise}}$; support points and query i.i.d. uniform on $X$. Setting $h=n^{-1/(2\alpha+d_X)}$ and $\beta = \frac{3R^2}{2}n^{2/(2\alpha+d_X)}$ (plus $\log^+ C_G$ in the random cases, where $C_G$ is an a.s.-finite random constant depending on $\|W\|,\|w_V\|$) balances term I to $\le n^{-2}$.

Experiments: $X=S^2\subset\mathbb{R}^3$ ($d_X=2$, $d_{in}=3$, $R=\tau_X=1$), target = a fixed unit-norm combination of the nine degree-4 real spherical harmonics ($\alpha=1$, $S_f\approx2.6$ measured on a 200,000-point Fibonacci lattice), labels corrupted with $\mathcal{U}([-1,1])$ noise, all frozen weights $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, softmax computed in log-space, 10 seeds. Architecture A = fixed homogeneous embedding/readout decoder; Architecture B = fully random affine embedding and decoding.

## Results & Highlights

- **Kernel-emulation rate in prompt length (Fig. 2a, Architecture A, $\beta=8$, $d_h=256$):** supremum squared error between the prompted network and the *oracle* empirical NW estimator falls from $\sim10^{1}$ at $n=8$ to $\sim10^{-16}$ at $n=1024$, matching the theoretical slope $n^{-2}$ across bandwidths $h\in\{0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8\}$. The one deviation is $h=0.2$, where the curve drops *steeper* than $n^{-2}$ at small $n$ — attributed to queries lacking nearby support points so the self-attention term dominates.
- **Exponential suppression via logit shift (Fig. 2b, $n=d_h=256$):** sup squared error decays from $\sim10^{0}$ at $\beta=0$ to $\sim10^{-18}$ at $\beta\approx12$, i.e. the predicted $e^{-2\beta}$, and the authors report the scaling "matches almost exactly across all bandwidths."
- **Minimax regression rate vs. four baselines (Fig. 4, $d_h=256$, $h=n^{-1/4}$, $\beta=3\sqrt{n}/2+\log^+C_G$, MSE over 1000 uniform test queries/seed, 10 seeds):** in both architectures the constructed prompt is "virtually indistinguishable" from B1, the oracle empirical NW estimator, and tracks the predicted $n^{-1/2}$ slope ($=2\alpha/(2\alpha+d_X)$ with $\alpha=1,d_X=2$). B3 (no prompt, trained weights) is flat at MSE $\approx1$ for all $n\in[8,1024]$ — the authors correctly note this is structural, since one softmax head over a single token collapses to an affine map.
- **Where the construction is beaten:** in Architecture A, B2 (frozen random weights, *trained* prompt) shows a *slightly steeper* slope than the closed-form construction, and B4 (frozen random prompt, trained weights) is better still — the authors concede "the construction is sufficient, but likely not tight." In Architecture B, by contrast, B2 and B4 "do not learn anything meaningful," with B2 worse than the flat B3 at small $n$ (MSE above $10^3$ on the plot); the authors attribute this to unit-variance init blowing up attention logits beyond what gradient methods can handle — a numerical failure, not evidence of superiority.
- **Prompt-norm tradeoffs (Figs. 3, 5):** max token norm grows linearly in $\beta$ as predicted, peaking around $2.0$ at $d_h=n=256$ over $\beta\in[0,20]$, with a non-monotone dip for $h=0.2$ explained exactly by the coordinate $\beta - h^{-2}/2 = \beta-12.5$ crossing zero. Sweeping $d_h\in[8,1024]$, Architecture A's max token norm decreases monotonically and flattens (matching $O(d_h^{-1/2}+1)$, roughly $10^2\!\to\!10^1$), while Architecture B traces a U-shape (matching $O(d_h^{-1}+\sqrt{d_h})$, minimum around mid-range $d_h$, rising to $\sim10^3$).
- **Rank condition empirically generic (Fig. 1):** sweeping $d_h\in[8,1024]$, $\sigma_{\min}(\widetilde{W})/\sqrt{d_h}$ concentrates near $\approx0.5$–$0.7$ (Arch. A) and $\sigma_{\min}(\widetilde{W}_{\text{aff}})/d_h$ near $\approx0.4$–$0.8$ (Arch. B), with no points near zero, consistent with a.s. full rank.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (high, but narrower than the title suggests).** The genuinely new move is dropping the "assume favorable weights" crutch: the rank condition holds a.s. under Gaussian init, so the result applies to *almost every* random network rather than a measure-zero hand-picked one. The kernel/attention correspondence itself is well-trodden (Tsai et al. 2019; Shen et al. 2026), and the paper is candid that it reuses Shen et al.'s NW target while changing the vehicle from weights to prompt.

**Rigor (solid, with a load-bearing caveat).** Proofs are explicit and self-contained; assumptions (compact manifold with positive reach, bounded noise, i.i.d. uniform design, $\alpha\le1$) are standard. But the "universal approximation" is essentially the NW estimator's statistical power, not the transformer's: terms II–IV of the error decomposition are bounded "independently of the transformer." The network contributes term I only, i.e. it is a computational substrate, not a source of statistical efficiency. Calling this "training-free universal approximation *by* prompting" is defensible but oversells how much the transformer is doing.

**Reproducibility (moderate).** The experimental setup is specified precisely enough to reimplement (unit sphere, degree-4 spherical harmonics, $\mathcal{U}([-1,1])$ noise, $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ init, 10 seeds, log-space softmax, Fibonacci lattices, Adam for trained baselines with $1/d_h$-variance init). No code URL appears in the text, and all results are plots — there is not a single numeric table, so exact values must be read off log-log axes.

**Specific weaknesses.**
1. **B3 is a straw man.** The authors themselves explain that a single softmax head attending over one token collapses to an affine map, so its flat MSE $\approx1$ carries no information. Of the four baselines, only B1 (oracle NW) is a real yardstick, and matching it is by construction.
2. **The Architecture-B comparison is contaminated.** The claim "there are settings where an explicit construction can outperform direct computational optimization" rests on B2/B4 failing in Arch. B — which the paper concedes is "almost certainly a numerical failure" caused by their own choice of $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ frozen init. A fair comparison would rerun those baselines under a conditioning-friendly init.
3. **Prompt length $=$ number of support points.** There is no compression: to reach error $\epsilon$ you need $n\sim\epsilon^{-(2\alpha+d_X)/2\alpha}$ tokens, and each token's norm grows as $n^{2/(2\alpha+d_X)}$. In practice a length-1024 real-valued prefix that literally stores $(x_i,y_i)$ pairs is closer to a lookup table than to what "prompting an LLM" means. The authors flag the open question of whether fewer tokens suffice.
4. **The logit shift is astronomically large in the fully random case.** $C_{G,\text{aff}} = \exp(O(d_h^{1.5}))$, so $\beta = O(n^{2/(2\alpha+d_X)} + d_h^{1.5})$. At $d_h=1024$ that is a logit shift on the order of $10^4$–$10^5$ — physically unrepresentable in bf16 and the direct cause of the Architecture-B numerical breakdown. The theory is asymptotically clean but numerically hostile.
5. **White-box, function-specific construction.** Building $P^*$ requires the realized $W_Q,W_K,W_V$ (to form $\widetilde{W}^\dagger$) *and* the labeled samples. This is not black-box prompting and not in-context learning in the usual sense — the paper is honest about this distinction, but readers skimming the abstract may not catch it.
6. **Missing ablations.** One manifold ($S^2$), one intrinsic dimension ($d_X=2$), one $\alpha$ ($=1$), one target function (a single fixed draw of degree-4 harmonics). The headline rate's dependence on $d_X$ — arguably the most interesting claim, since it is what makes the bound ambient-dimension-free — is never varied experimentally. Nor is $\alpha<1$ tested, nor a manifold with small reach $\tau_X$ (the bounds require $h\le\tau_X/2$).
7. **Architectural fragility.** No layer norm, no residual, no FFN, no multi-head, one layer. LayerNorm in particular would rescale prompt tokens and plausibly break both the logit-shift trick and the unbounded-norm construction; the authors list this as future work rather than arguing it is benign.

**Red flags:** none of the fabrication/cherry-picking variety. The main overclaim risk is rhetorical — "pretraining is optional" is true only in the sense that a random reservoir plus an oracle-computed, weight-dependent, sample-sized prompt can express a kernel estimator.

## Strengths

- The construction is genuinely explicit and interpretable: three matching conditions, one pseudoinverse, no optimization loop, no hidden constants that cannot be computed.
- The a.s.-full-rank argument via Gaussian random-matrix singular-value bounds is the right way to close the "but will training ever find these weights?" gap that plagues prompting-expressivity theory, and Fig. 1 backs it empirically.
- Cost accounting for prompt token norms across three regimes is unusual and useful — most expressivity papers report only existence, not the magnitude of the object they construct.
- Intellectually honest reporting: the authors explicitly state their bounds are not tight (B2 beats them), explain why B3 is uninformative, and diagnose their own Architecture-B numerical failure rather than claiming a win.
- Remark 1 is a nice piece of scholarship: it enumerates alternatives to logit shifting (prompt duplication, masked/cross-attention giving exact reconstruction) and justifies the choice made.

## Limitations

- Toy architecture: single layer, single head, no FFN/residual/LayerNorm/causal mask; extension to realistic transformers is open and non-obvious in the prompting regime.
- Scalar-output, single-task regression on a compact manifold; no sequence-to-sequence, no discrete tokens, no language.
- Prompt length scales with the number of support points; no lower bound is proven, so the necessary prompt length is unknown.
- Token norms are unbounded in $n$ and (for fully random transformers) in $\sqrt{d_h}$; combined with $\beta=O(d_h^{1.5})$, the construction is numerically infeasible at realistic widths.
- Requires full white-box access to the frozen weights and to labeled samples of the target function.
- Only $\alpha\le1$ (no higher smoothness), bounded noise, uniform sampling on the manifold, and $h\le\tau_X/2$.
- Empirical validation is confined to one function on one 2-dimensional sphere; no table of numbers, all evidence read from log-log plots.

## Relevance

如果你关注 prompting/ICL 的表达能力理论边界（"任务能力必须存在权重里，还是可以在推理时由 prompt 诱导"），这篇给出了目前最干净的一个极端答案：随机冻结权重 + 显式构造 prompt = 流形上的 minimax 最优核回归。同时它也是 attention-as-kernel 这条线的一个漂亮增量，适合作为理解 soft prompt 容量、prompt 长度/范数权衡的参考，但不要期望能直接迁移到真实 LLM 的 prompt engineering 实践。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the construction survive LayerNorm? RMSNorm/LayerNorm would rescale each prompt token, apparently destroying both the $O(\beta+h^{-2})$ norm growth and the logit-shift suppression — is there a norm-invariant reparameterization of $v_i$?
2. Is there a *lower* bound on prompt length for a target accuracy $\epsilon$? The current one-token-per-support-point scheme is presumably far from optimal; can $O(\log n)$ or $O(n^{\gamma})$ tokens realize a comparable estimator by, e.g., encoding a compressed/quadrature representation of the kernel weights?
3. Can multi-head attention or depth $>1$ be exploited to reduce the required logit shift from $O(d_h^{1.5})$ to something numerically representable — or, as the paper hints, do additional components not automatically increase expressivity in the prompting-with-random-weights regime?
4. Benchmark 4 (frozen *random* prompt, trained weights) scaled surprisingly well in Architecture A. Is random context genuinely acting as a computational scratchpad for attention, and can that be characterized theoretically the way the constructed prompt is here?
5. How do the rates degrade under width-dependent (Xavier/Kaiming, variance $\Theta(1/d_h)$) initialization, which the paper deliberately avoids? Remark 3 says the asymptotic prompt-norm bounds change — in which direction, and does it fix the Architecture-B numerical collapse?
6. Does the construction extend to sequence-to-sequence targets, or to $\alpha>1$ smoothness where kernel bias saturates and higher-order (e.g. local-polynomial) estimators are needed — and can attention emulate those too?
