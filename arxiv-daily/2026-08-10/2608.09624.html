Why this matters

The standard "0.98 AUROC harmful-vs-benign" detector claim is near-meaningless for deployment — its false-positive budget gets spent blocking attacks that would fail anyway.

Critical edge

The headline reversal rests on just 27 successful attacks — every figure comes from an automated judge with no blinded human audit (three-judge Fleiss κ ≈ 0.3).

01

Methodology, distilled

Setting. A pre-generation internal score computed from a single eager-attention prefill pass — it sees only the prompt's attention and hidden states, never the completion. Primary target: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Mistral / Qwen / Gemma / Phi test transfer).

Active Attention Probing (AAP). A K=3 continuous probe is inserted into the system region after chat-template materialization, giving a fixed, content-independent coordinate so a prompt and its wrapped version are read at a common reference point. The per-head response z = Σ Aqj over probe positions is mapped by XGBoost to S(x) ∈ [0,1]. Framed explicitly as a measurement instrument, not a defense. Trained on the BeaverTails split only, then frozen before every audit.

Matched 2×2 audit. Each base goal is built in both plain and wrapped form (harmful intent H × wrapper W), with real greedy completions per target — the outcome label is generated by a judge, never assumed. Estimands: τ_H (harmfulness), τ_W (wrapper), τ_HW (interaction); R_H intent AUROC vs R_Y outcome AUROC restricted to harmful prompts.

Controls. Norm-matched random probes, a passive channel that inserts no tokens, both-class encoding, head-selection variance checks, and source-clustered bootstraps. Two independent judges: Llama Guard 3 + a frozen Qwen3-14B rubric.

Strengths

  • The audit design removes three shortcuts prior work leaves open: corpus identity, transformation-as-label, and success-as-prompt-property.
  • The 0.930 positive control converts a negative result into a diagnosis — the information is there; the harmfulness readout has the wrong orientation.
  • Separating ranking / calibration / threshold-transfer failure explains why a single F1 number hides which component broke.
  • Relentless self-limitation: a mechanical null, a power simulation that voids the homogeneity test, a design-effect calculation, and a public checkpoint-defect postmortem.
  • Passive-channel and refusal-logit replications rule out probe insertion as the cause.

Limitations

  • No blinded human audit; three-judge Fleiss κ ≈ 0.3, and Llama Guard guides several attack searches.
  • Small event counts: 27 successes on Llama, 10 on Qwen; targets are not difficulty-matched.
  • One greedy decode for primary outcomes; only the factorial is repeated over five sampled draws.
  • Strong attacks and derived channels are evaluated on Llama only.
  • No published defense is reimplemented head-to-head — the "detector-derived" rows are authors' ports.
  • The representational-decoupling account is offered as a hypothesis, not a proven cause.
03

Open questions for further work

  1. Does the anti-ranking survive a blinded human-labeled outcome set, or is RY = 0.220 a property of Llama Guard's failure mode on wrapped text?
  2. Is the reversal causal — wrappers weakening the specific features the harmfulness readout reads while compliance follows a disjoint mechanism? Activation patching on the ~22 surviving heads would test it.
  3. Could a hybrid readout (harmfulness-supervised for coverage + outcome-supervised for orientation) keep OOD robustness, given the outcome probe drops to 0.725 on WildJailbreak?
  4. Why does Llama Guard 3 reach 0.625 on natural WildJailbreak yet sit at chance (0.507) on the matched factorial — does natural-attack prediction lean on cues prefill attention discards?
  5. Does the result hold for reasoning models and non-greedy settings, where outcome depends on a long CoT rather than a single prefill-conditioned continuation?
  6. Would a conformal / benign-tail-stabilized threshold rule fix the decision-validity failure (WildJailbreak recall 0.03 vs. oracle 0.25 under benign shift)?
