Key Finding · DPC4 Prefetching

An LLM-driven evolutionary agent beats the human DPC4 prefetching champion by just 0.30 points — and only when bandwidth is scarce.

一句话总结：ArchAgent v2 以「级联式分治进化」+「编译期存储预算反馈回路」，在 DPC4 规则下自动演化出三级预取器，整体几何平均 IPC 提升达 3.82%，比人工冠军 BertiGO 高 0.3 个百分点；但优势几乎全部来自单核低带宽场景，多核跨套件反而跌到基线以下 (0.9902)。

Key Metric

+3.82%

overall geomean IPC speedup vs. the DPC4 baseline (Berti@L1D + Pythia@L2).

…but only +0.30 pts over the hand-designed champion BertiGO (3.52%), in a single, seedless run.

Why This Matters

A rare first-hand case of an LLM agent winning a real engineering search under hard physical budgets — with full failure analysis and a 12,000-candidate profiling study attached.

Critical Edge

On cross-suite 4-core mixes the evolved prefetcher regresses 0.98% below baseline (0.9902) — a number buried in Figure 10, never mentioned in the abstract.

Methodology

distilled

ArchAgent v2 drives AlphaEvolve to mutate C++ prefetcher policy code inside ChampSim, starting from the DPC4 baseline (Berti at L1D, Pythia at L2, nothing at LLC). Two additions define v2:

① Storage feedback loop

Each prefetcher exposes prefetcher_size(); the agent must keep it in lockstep with logic. Candidates exceeding per-level caps (32 / 128 / 256 KB) are rejected at compile time — a hard physical constraint turned into negative reward, not a post-hoc human area estimate.

② Cascaded evolution

Evolve L1D alone (single-core) & freeze the best → evolve L2 against frozen L1D → evolve LLC → single-core joint → multi-core joint. Low-bandwidth configs are double-weighted as a cheap multi-core proxy.

Evolution uses 60M-instruction traces (200M held-out for validation, plus unseen workloads and novel mixes); the first L1D run alone explored >12,000 candidates over ~40 days. Total wall-clock to beat state of the art: roughly two months of distributed ChampSim cluster time, with individual evaluations running up to or exceeding 12 hours.

Strengths & Limitations

two-sided

▲ Strengths

  • Unusually honest limitations: authors concede limited mechanism novelty, ~50% wasted candidates, and that mean proposal quality does not improve over time.
  • The hardware-realizability loop is the most portable idea — generalizes to any agentic search with a hard resource budget. Compile-time rejection beats post-hoc human audit.
  • Careful train/validate split with 200M-instruction held-out traces, unseen workloads, and 100 randomized multi-core mixes — above the norm for LLM-discovery papers.
  • Concrete, actionable insight: the biggest lineage jump came from merging three island programs — cross-island injection mattered more than any single mutation.
  • Full storage breakdown makes the artifact auditable at the design level even without released code.

▼ Limitations

  • Multi-core is unsolved: −1.52 pts vs. BertiGO and a 0.98% regression below baseline on cross-suite 4-core mixes.
  • No ablation isolating the cascade from the storage loop; no matched-compute ArchAgent v1 control. "v1 does not scale" is asserted, not measured.
  • Greedy cascade: freezing L1D before L2 exists precludes co-designed solutions; no test of alternative orderings.
  • Single seed per config — the +0.30 pt overall margin has no error estimate.
  • Storage compliance and idea attribution both rest on LLM judges with minimal human validation.
  • Unequal-effort comparison: ~2 months and 12,000+ evaluations against a static human artifact with no second pass.
  • Nothing released: no code, no evolved policy, no prompts, no compute accounting. Reproducibility is low.

Questions for Further Exploration

open
  1. Would a matched-compute ArchAgent v1 actually fail, or just converge more slowly? "Does not scale" is a claim, not a finding, without that control.
  2. Does cascade ordering matter? L1D-first was justified by BertiGO's L1D-dominant gains — a circular argument that bakes the human prior into the "automated" search.
  3. Can the multi-core regression be fixed by cheaper proxies (sampled/checkpointed simulation, learned IPC surrogates) rather than the single-core low-BW weighting that demonstrably mis-predicts multi-core?
  4. Why does the policy lose 7.9 pts to BertiGO specifically on AI-ML high-BW — training-mix artifact, or a structural GST/GDHT weakness under streaming tensor access?
  5. Given the biggest jump came from combining three island programs, would explicitly scheduling cross-island merge rounds cut the ~50% discard rate?
  6. Could prefetcher_size() extend to a real timing/power model (CACTI-backed) so the agent is constrained by the full realizability envelope, not area alone?
  7. If mean proposal quality is flat over time, is the program database failing to exploit its own history — an AlphaEvolve limitation or a prompt/context limitation?
