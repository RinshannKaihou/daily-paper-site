> **一句话总结：** Emotion2Skill 从 Qwen3 残差流中抽取 27 维 GoEmotions 情绪向量，经一个 <27K 参数的 MLP 编码器映射为「情绪模板 + 置信度」文本片段注入技能路由 prompt，并用情绪轨迹突变点定位有问题的技能调用来指导 SOP 重写；在 ALFWorld 上 Qwen3-8B 平均成功率 47.4%（Zero-Shot 21.9%，最强基线 MASA 43.3%），WebShop 成功率 29.7%（MASA 27.1%）——即相对 Zero-Shot 的 +26.9pp 头条数字，换成最强基线只剩 +2.6pp。

> **TL;DR:** Emotion2Skill reads a 27-dim GoEmotions emotion vector out of the LLM's residual stream (layer 24 of Qwen3-8B, picked by GoEmotions 27-way accuracy: 37.2% vs 8.2% random), compresses it via a 3-layer MLP into one of 12 natural-language state templates plus a confidence score, and injects that into the skill-routing prompt behind a confidence gate (τ=0.3; 72% of steps admitted). Emotion-trajectory shift points (cosine distance δ_t > mean+1σ) then localize which steps to blame during SOP rewriting. On ALFWorld it reaches 47.4% avg success with Qwen3-8B vs 21.9% Zero-Shot (+25.5) and 43.3% for the strongest baseline MASA (+4.1); on WebShop 29.7% Succ. vs 2.8% Zero-Shot (+26.9) and 27.1% MASA (+2.6), plus Score 52.1 vs 44.7. Worth reading if you care about using interpretability artifacts as *control* signals rather than analysis; skip if you need black-box-deployable methods, since it requires white-box activation access.

## 核心贡献

- **把可解释性向量从「事后分析/输出 steering」变成「agent 级决策信号」。** 之前的情绪方向研究（Sofroniew et al. 2026）只做读出与因果 steering，本文首次把它作为技能路由与技能演化的在线输入。
- **情绪编码器 + 置信门控。** 原始 27 维情绪向量不能直接塞进 prompt；作者训练一个 27→64→128→128 的 MLP（<27K 参数），输出（i）12 个自然语言情绪模板中的一个（原型余弦最大），（ii）标量置信度 c_t；c_t < τ 时整段情绪信息从 prompt 中撤掉，退化为纯文本路由。
- **情绪驱动的技能演化。** 用逐步情绪余弦距离 δ_t = 1 − cos(e_t, e_{t−1}) 定位「内部状态突变点」，取 3 步窗口的观测/技能/模板/δ 作为诊断上下文喂给 SOP 重写器，替代以往只有 episode 级二元成功/失败的粗信号。
- **配套数据与分析。** 自建 Emotion Stories 语料（27 概念 × 100 篇短故事 = 2700 篇，CC BY 4.0 承诺发布）；情绪-技能共激活热图给出 4 组语义自洽配对（curiosity+desire→ProductSearch、confusion+nervousness→QueryRephrase 等），GPT-4o 判定一致率 76.5%。

## Key Contributions

- First use of linear emotion directions as an **active routing signal** for skill-based LLM agents, rather than post-hoc probing or output steering.
- A confidence-gated **emotion encoder** that converts a 27-dim activation readout into prompt-compatible text while preserving magnitude/co-activation structure (raw top-3 label injection is shown to be strictly worse).
- **Emotion-trajectory change detection** for skill evolution: temporally localized diagnostics replace the episode-level binary outcome signal used by SkillOpt/MASA-style rewriters.
- Empirical package: 2 interactive benchmarks × 2 backbones × 5 baselines, 3-component ablation, τ sweep, layer sweep, Fisher-ratio analysis, out-of-domain transfer to MATH/MBPP, plus an appendix with an (elementary) mutual-information justification and case studies.

## Methodology

**Offline extraction.** For each of K=27 GoEmotions concepts, Qwen3-8B generates N=100 short stories embodying the emotion without naming it (filtered: 2–8 sentences, target emotion in a zero-shot classifier's top-3; no intra-concept cosine > 0.95; mean 4.2 sentences / 67.3 tokens; 89% human-identifiable on a 50-story sample). Activations are mean-pooled over tokens t ≥ 50; the direction for concept k is the within-class mean minus the global mean (contrastive averaging, Eq. 2), then PCA-denoised by projecting out the top-10 principal components of 100 neutral-story activations. Stacking gives an extractor matrix W_e ∈ R^{27×d}. The extraction layer L* is chosen by GoEmotions 27-way accuracy: L*=24 of 36 for Qwen3-8B (0.67 depth), L*=26 of 40 for Qwen3-14B.

**Online state.** At each decision step, the residual activation at the `Assistant:` delimiter token is projected through W_e and ℓ2-normalized to give e_t ∈ R^27. The encoder f_θ (3-layer MLP) maps e_t to r_t ∈ R^128, from which a template index T*_t = argmax_j cos(r_t, q_j) over 12 learnable prototypes and a confidence c_t = σ(w_c·r_t + b_c) are derived. Training uses ~3,200 tuples (e_t, s_t^sel, y_t) collected from 500 warm-up ReAct+skill episodes, with a supervised-contrastive loss (positives = same skill choice *and* successful episode, κ=0.07) plus a template-classification loss over k-means pseudo-labels (λ=0.5). Training takes <10 min on 2×H800 and is done once per backbone, reused across all benchmarks.

**Selection.** The prompt is `x_t; {D_i}; T*_t; c_t` if c_t ≥ τ, else `x_t; {D_i}` (Eq. 8), and the skill is chosen by constrained generation over skill identifiers. τ=1 recovers vanilla text-only routing.

**Evolution.** Over R=3 rounds of 100 episodes each, skills with success < η=0.4 are flagged; for each, the transition points P = {t : δ_t > δ̄ + σ_δ} restricted to that skill yield 3-step diagnostic windows d_t, which are verbalized into Δ_i and appended to the rewriter prompt alongside the current SOP and episode outcome. The rewriter is the same backbone, greedy decoding, SOP capped at 200 words.

## Results & Highlights

- **ALFWorld avg success (Qwen3-8B): 47.4% ±1.6 vs 43.3% ±0.9 for MASA (+4.1 pp), 37.0% ExpeL, 31.5% Reflexion, 30.7% ReAct, 21.9% Zero-Shot.** The abstract's headline "+25.5%" is measured against Zero-Shot; against the strongest baseline the margin is 4.1 pp.
- **WebShop (Qwen3-8B): Succ. 29.7% ±1.6 vs 27.1% ±0.8 for MASA (+2.6 pp) and 2.8% Zero-Shot (+26.9 pp); Score 52.1 vs 44.7 MASA (+7.4).** The Score gap is notably larger than the Succ. gap, i.e. the method buys partial-credit quality more than binary task completion.
- **Qwen3-14B: ALFWorld avg 52.3% vs 49.2% MASA (+3.1); WebShop Succ. 30.7% vs 24.8% MASA (+5.9), Score 53.8 vs 46.8 (+7.0).** But on 4 of 6 ALFWorld task types the 14B model *loses* to MASA — Pick 79.8 vs 81.0, Clean 48.9 vs 50.5, Heat 38.8 vs 36.5 (win), Cool 35.7 vs **56.2** (a 20.5 pp loss) — yet the reported average is higher. See Critical Assessment: the 14B average does not reconcile with its own per-task row.
- **Per-task concentration (8B):** Heat 9.6% → 56.9% (+47.3 pp) and Pick2 4.4% → 31.3% (+26.9 pp) vs Zero-Shot; Pick, where Zero-Shot is already at 43.4%, moves to 67.9%. The paper attributes the Heat gain largely to one SOP edit ("verify the appliance is open; if closed, open it first") produced by the emotion-guided rewriter.
- **Ablations (Qwen3-8B, Table 2):** w/o Emotion Extraction (plain class means instead of contrastive W_e) −4.7 pp WebShop Succ. / −8.6 pp ALFWorld Avg; w/o Emotion Encoder (raw top-3 labels as text) −3.1 / −5.5; w/o Emotion Evolution (outcome-only rewriting) −0.9 / −3.1. Extraction quality matters most; the evolution component is the weakest contributor on WebShop.
- **τ sensitivity:** 26.6 / 28.1 / 27.8 / 20.0 WebShop Succ. at τ = 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.9 — flat within [0.1, 0.6], collapsing at 0.9 (gate almost always closed). 28% of WebShop steps fall below τ=0.3 and use the text-only fallback.
- **Evolution rounds (WebShop Succ., 8B):** outcome-only 26.8 → 27.5 → 28.1 vs emotion-guided 27.2 → 28.9 → 29.7, i.e. the advantage compounds from +0.4 to +1.6 pp over three rounds.
- **Representation quality:** GoEmotions 27-way hit-any accuracy peaks at 37.2% (layer 24) vs 8.2% random, non-monotonic across layers (15.8 → 24.3 → 21.7 → 30.5 → 37.2 → 33.6 → 35.1); Fisher ratio peaks at F=2.08 but decouples from accuracy (layer 20: F=1.52 with 21.7% accuracy; layer 22: F=1.21 with 30.5%). Qwen3-8B-**Instruct** drops to 28.1%, implying re-extraction is needed per checkpoint.
- **Out-of-domain (8B, emotion vectors reused from L*=24 without re-extraction):** MATH 500-problem accuracy 54.8% → 69.2% (+14.4 pp) and MBPP-374 pass@1 60.0% → 71.8% (+11.8 pp) — but the ∆ is quoted against **Zero-Shot only**; the plotted MASA/ExpeL bars sit at roughly 67.6/65.2 on MATH and 69.1/67.3 on MBPP, so the real margin over the best baseline is ≈1.6 and ≈2.7 pp.
- **Interpretability check:** conditional entropy of skill selection for QueryRephrase drops 1.84 → 1.31 bits when conditioning on the emotion vector (0.53 bits CMI, 22.3% uncertainty reduction); GPT-4o rates 76.5% of 200 sampled emotion–skill pairings semantically coherent (Cohen's κ=0.81 vs human on 50 events), and 61% of the "inconsistent" residue has max(e_t) < 0.15.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-high).** The conceptual move — take a causally-validated interpretability direction and use it as an *input to an agent's control loop* rather than as an analysis artifact or a steering vector — is genuinely new for skill-based agents, and the evolution half (using δ_t spikes as a credit-assignment localizer) is a nicer idea than the selection half. But mechanically, the pipeline is: read activations, classify into one of 12 canned templates, paste one sentence into a prompt. The information actually reaching the LLM is a template ID and a float. That is a very low-bandwidth channel for a 27-dim state, and the paper never tests whether the same gains survive if the template is replaced by a *random* template (a placebo control) or by a text-derived uncertainty proxy such as token entropy or self-verbalized confidence. Without either control, "emotion vectors specifically" is not isolated from "any extra step-level confidence token".

**Rigor (mixed, with one hard arithmetic problem).** Three concrete issues:
1. **The Qwen3-14B ALFWorld average does not reconcile with its own per-task numbers.** The row is Pick 79.8, Look 43.5, Clean 48.9, Heat 38.8, Cool 35.7, Pick2 34.6 — unweighted mean 46.9, and episode-weighted over the standard 134-episode `json_2.1.1` split it is ≈47.0 — yet the reported Avg is **52.3**, the number the +28.9 pp headline is built on. MASA's 14B row has a smaller (~2 pp) but same-signed discrepancy. This should be checked before citing the 14B result.
2. **Table 1 and Table 2 disagree on the same configuration.** Table 1 reports Emotion2Skill (8B) at 29.7 WebShop Succ. / 47.4 ALFWorld Avg; Table 2's "Emotion2Skill (Full)" row reports 28.1 / 46.9, and all ∆ values are computed against the latter. Table 12 then lists 29.7 as the round-3 number, so 28.1 appears to be an earlier-round or differently-seeded run silently used as the ablation reference.
3. **Baseline handling.** MASA is reimplemented from the paper description ("No public Qwen3 code exists"), and ReAct's retrieval augmentation is dropped as "incompatible with our evaluation protocol" — both weaken the comparison in the authors' favor. Zero-Shot on WebShop scores 2.8% Succ., which is essentially a broken agent; anchoring the abstract's "+26.9%" to it is overclaiming when the real headroom over MASA is 2.6 pp on the same metric.

**Reproducibility (good on paper).** Hyperparameters are unusually complete (layer grids, PCA top-10, τ, η=0.4, R=3, λ=0.5, κ=0.07, κ'=0.1, seed 42, encoder shape, warm-up episode counts), the encoder is tiny and cheap, and code plus the story corpus are promised. The main obstacles are the missing exact ALFWorld per-type episode counts (needed to reproduce the Avg column) and the fact that emotion directions must be re-extracted for every checkpoint — the Instruct-variant accuracy drop from 37.2% to 28.1% is a real portability tax the paper reports honestly but does not evaluate end-to-end.

**Significance (moderate).** If the numbers hold, the practical takeaway is that a <27K-parameter probe on frozen activations buys 2.6–5.9 pp over a strong skill-routing baseline at near-zero inference cost — a good ratio. The out-of-domain MATH/MBPP transfer without re-extraction is the most interesting result in the paper and is under-sold, though it is also the one presented with the weakest baseline framing.

**Red flags.** (a) The "+26.9% / +25.5%" abstract framing against Zero-Shot; (b) the 14B average that does not equal its own row; (c) the Table 1 / Table 2 inconsistency; (d) GPT-4o used to grade the semantic coherence of the paper's own emotion–skill mapping (76.5%), with the "conservative lower bound" argument resting on the authors' own low-activation filter; (e) an appendix of "theory" (Propositions on I(S*; X, E) ≥ I(S*; X) via the chain rule, plus a convergence sketch that assumes the diagnostic fixes a failure mode with probability α > 0) that is mathematically trivial and adds the appearance of rigor without constraining anything; (f) no per-task-type significance testing despite 134 total ALFWorld episodes spread over 6 types — roughly 20 episodes per cell, so the eye-catching Heat 9.6 → 56.9 swing rests on ~23 episodes.

## Strengths

- The framing is clean and the gap it identifies is real: every prior skill-selection method conditions only on text, and the internal state at the moment of routing is genuinely unobserved.
- Component ablations are well designed as *degradations* rather than removals (plain class means vs contrastive directions; raw labels vs encoder), which isolates the contribution of each design choice rather than just "does the module exist".
- The confidence gate is not decorative: 28% of steps fall back to text-only, τ=0.9 collapses performance to 20.0%, and the paper reports the fallback rate instead of hiding it.
- The evolution case study is concrete and falsifiable — a specific SOP line ("verify the appliance is open; if closed, open it first") traced to a specific T9 transition, credited with much of the Heat gain.
- Limitations section is candid: white-box access required, Cool tasks lose to MASA, Instruct variants need re-extraction, warm-up costs 500 episodes.
- Low run-to-run variance (≤1.6 pp) versus ExpeL ±4.7 and Reflexion ±7.0 is a legitimate secondary claim, and it is reported rather than buried.

## Limitations

- **White-box only.** Requires residual-stream access, so it cannot be applied to API-served frontier models; distillation to black-box settings is left as future work.
- **Per-checkpoint re-extraction.** Directions are model-specific and even instruction-tuning degrades them (37.2% → 28.1% GoEmotions accuracy), so the pipeline is not a drop-in across model versions.
- **Warm-up cost.** ~500 episodes under a ReAct+skill baseline are needed before the encoder can be trained, and the encoder's contrastive positives are defined using *episode outcomes*, so the "purely internal" signal is partly supervised by task reward.
- **Not uniformly better.** ALFWorld Cool tasks lose to MASA on Qwen3-8B, and on Qwen3-14B the per-task row loses on Pick, Clean, and badly on Cool; the paper's explanation (stereotyped action sequences benefit less) is plausible but untested.
- **Only two interactive benchmarks and one model family.** Everything is Qwen3 (8B/14B, non-thinking mode, greedy decoding); no Llama/Mistral/GPT-family replication of the emotion-direction geometry.
- **Emotion framing may be a label of convenience.** Nothing in the method requires the 27 directions to be *emotions*; they function as a learned uncertainty/state basis, and the paper offers no ablation replacing GoEmotions concepts with an arbitrary 27-concept basis.

## Relevance

若你（用户）关注 LLM agent 的技能库/路由设计，或关注「可解释性产物如何转化为可用的控制信号」这条线，这篇给出了一个成本极低（<27K 参数探针 + 一次性 10 分钟训练）的具体范式，且置信门控与轨迹突变点定位这两个机制可以直接迁移到自己的 agent 框架里。但引用其数字时务必用 MASA 而非 Zero-Shot 作为参照，并回避 Qwen3-14B 的 ALFWorld 平均值（该数字与其自身分项无法对上）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- **Placebo control:** does injecting a *random* template + random confidence at the same rate recover part of the gain? Without this, "emotion" vs "any auxiliary token" is unresolved.
- **Text-space baseline:** how does the emotion channel compare against a purely textual uncertainty signal (token entropy, logit margin over skill IDs, or asking the model to self-report confidence) that requires no white-box access?
- **Why does the 14B ALFWorld average exceed its per-task mean by 5.4 pp?** Is there a different episode weighting, a different split, or a transcription error?
- **Is the 12-template bottleneck necessary?** Feeding a quantized 27-dim vector or the top-k with magnitudes directly (the paper only tests raw top-3 *labels*) might preserve more information than a 12-way argmax.
- **Does the evolution mechanism need emotions at all?** δ_t is just a change-point detector on a 27-dim projection — how does it compare to change-point detection on the raw hidden state, or on per-step reward/observation novelty?
- **Closed loop:** the conclusion proposes letting executed skills update emotion embeddings. Does bidirectional coupling risk a feedback pathology where the agent learns to induce "confident" internal states rather than solve the task — the representational analogue of reward hacking that Sofroniew et al. tie to these same directions?
