> **一句话总结：** 这是一篇立场论文（position paper，无自有实验），主张自动科研 agent 的瓶颈不是"生成更多候选再用 learned judge 排序"，而是反馈架构——应照搬 greybox fuzzer 的控制回路：每次实验暴露一个廉价稠密的"认识论进展信号"（相当于 coverage）来引导下一步干预，同时把"最终验证"（相当于 crash/sanitizer oracle）严格隔离在引导路径之外以防自适应过拟合；作者用 Angora（xmlwf 上梯度求解 97.0% 分支约束 vs 随机变异 77.4%）、MLE-bench（选 test-best 节点相比 validation-best 可多拿 9–13 个百分点的 medal rate）、MLR-Bench（约 80% 案例含伪造或无效实验结果）等外部数据支撑三条可证伪预测。

> **TL;DR:** A cs.AI position paper (no new experiments) arguing that autonomous research agents should be built like coverage-guided greybox fuzzers rather than as generate-and-rank pipelines: expose a cheap dense *epistemic progress* signal on every experiment (the coverage analogue), let the search policy act on it (the mutation/power-schedule analogue), and keep *protected* final validation (the sanitizer-oracle analogue) out of the guidance path. The baseline it targets is explicitly repeated sampling + learned-judge ranking as used by The AI Scientist-v2, AI co-scientist (Elo tournament) and Agent Laboratory. Its quantitative ammunition is borrowed: Angora's ablation on **xmlwf** solves **97.0%** of branch constraints by gradient descent vs **77.4%** for random mutation; on **MLE-bench**, counterfactually picking the test-best node instead of the validation-best node would raise medal rate by **9–13 percentage points**; on **MLR-Bench**, ~**80%** of coding-agent cases contain fabricated or invalidated experimental results. Read it if you design auto-research loops or evaluation harnesses and want a crisp vocabulary (guidance vs. oracle) plus three falsifiable experiment designs; skip it if you want empirical results — there are none of the authors' own.

## 核心贡献

- **提出一个逐组件的类比表（Table 1）**：种子语料↔既往结果与文献；变异算子↔LLM 实验提案器；coverage↔中间进展信号；crash/sanitizer oracle↔经验证的发现；盲目 vs. 定向变异↔重复采样 vs. 有向搜索；power schedule↔跨研究方向的算力分配。作者明确标出加粗的两行（可观测的中间进展 + 用它来引导搜索）为核心设计选择。
- **"拆开 evaluate 这个盒子"（Figure 2）**：批评当下主流系统用一个分数同时承担"引导搜索"和"认证结果"两种角色；fuzzing 领域成熟的标志正是把两者拆开——廉价的 coverage 负责引导，受保护的 oracle 负责认证。
- **给出进展信号的三条设计要求 C1–C3**：C1 每个候选上都廉价且有信息量（且必须显著便宜于它所调度的最终验证）；C2 与真实进展相关（而非只与信号自身增大相关）；C3 在被优化时仍然稳健。并指出已知失败模式：prediction-error 好奇心的 "noisy TV" 陷阱违反 C3；coverage-unique crash 会高估进展（大量唯一 crash 可能塌缩为少数几个真 bug）。
- **三条可证伪预测（本文的"实验计划"）**：(1) Signal prediction——固定提案器/任务/候选池时，好的中间指标应比无信息或仅终局信号预测出更多受保护验证确认的发现；(2) Search prediction——固定提案器/任务/验证器/总预算时，用反馈选择下一步干预应比重复采样带来更高的"单位成本验证发现数"；(3) 固定搜索轨迹时，受保护验证应比直接报告被优化的 proxy 降低 false discovery rate。
- **系统回顾六种反对意见**（LLM 无法完成 abductive jump、这只是 BOED 换皮、这只是 novelty search / quality-diversity、bitter lesson 说该 scale 生成器、开放科研没有稳定搜索空间所以 coverage 无定义、科学没有 oracle 所以类比失效），并逐条划定自己主张的边界：只覆盖"已声明的研究问题内部"的搜索，不覆盖改变"什么算问题/候选/发现"的溯因行为。
- **一条从 1930s 到今天的失败史串联**：Gödel/Church/Turing/Rice 说明验证不可完全机械化；Friedberg 的随机码变异说明无引导采样不比穷举快；Schmidhuber 的 Gödel machine 是"只有受保护判决、没有廉价信号"因而一步都迈不出去；NAS 用同一个静态 benchmark 既引导又认证，于是过拟合了自己优化的 benchmark；今天的 learned research judge 在论文尺度上重复同一个混淆。

## Key Contributions

- A component-by-component mapping (Table 1) between coverage-guided greybox fuzzing and automated research, with the two boldfaced rows — *make intermediate progress observable* and *use it to direct search* — identified as the load-bearing design choices.
- An explicit architectural prescription: split the single "evaluate" box into a **cheap progress signal that steers** and a **protected validator that certifies and never steers** (Figure 2a vs 2b).
- Three requirements (C1 cheap/informative per candidate, C2 correlated with genuine progress, C3 robust under optimization) that any candidate epistemic-progress proxy must satisfy, plus candidate proxies to try: surprise against a model, novelty against prior work, verifier confidence, cross-replicate disagreement, and distance to a stated target.
- Three falsifiable, operationally specified predictions (signal prediction, search prediction, protected-validation prediction) that would refute the position if they fail, each with the controls fixed (proposer, task, validator, budget).
- A diagnosis argument: separating the three roles makes campaign failures *attributable* (uninformative guidance vs. bad search policy vs. miscertifying validator), whereas a conflated score leaves "retrain the judge" as the only remedy.
- A defense against six named alternative views, including a specific distinction from quality-diversity: a QD behavior descriptor is hand-designed to describe progress *toward the goal*, whereas coverage is read automatically off the candidate's own execution and describes *what it did* — a property of the process, not an estimate of the outcome, which is what open research needs because the outcome is exactly what cannot yet be modeled.

## Methodology

There is no empirical methodology in the usual sense; this is a position/argument paper in cs.AI (cross-listed cs.CL) whose "method" is a structured analogy plus a proposed experimental protocol for others (and presumably the authors' future work) to run.

The argument proceeds in four moves. **(1) Establish the isomorphism of control loops** (§2): both a fuzzer and a research agent propose a candidate, execute it, observe feedback, and choose what to try next; the paper stresses that the correspondence is about *control structure*, not literal equivalence, and explicitly scopes itself to search *within* a declared research problem. **(2) Lesson one — observability** (§3): final scientific outcomes are sparse (most experiments establish nothing, and stronger validation may require replication, expert review, or a physical assay), so the agent needs a coverage-analogue that measures *how the state of the search changed* — e.g. whether an experiment ruled out a competing explanation, located a boundary, sharpened a held-out prediction, or changed which experiment should run next — rather than an early verdict on the current candidate. The paper deliberately declines to name a specific metric and instead ships C1–C3 as a specification, arguing the signal should be cheaper and less model-intensive than full Bayesian expected information gain (which needs an explicit likelihood, prior, design domain, and utility). **(3) Lesson two — feedback-directed search** (§4): guidance is worthless unless the policy acts on it; the fuzzing exemplars are AFL/AFL++ energy allocation from execution history, Angora's taint-analysis + numerical-gradient constraint solving, and directed greybox fuzzing's distance-based power schedule (Figure 4 shows pruning directions D3/D5 to free budget for D2 under a fixed supply). The research analogues suggested are sensitivity analysis to find the premise driving an outcome, scheduling compute toward under-explored directions, and generating an experiment that discriminates surviving explanations. **(4) Protected validation** (§5): because the search adaptively optimizes the proxy, the verdict must come from evidence protected from that optimization — a hidden test, replication, physical assay, or independent review — connecting to the reusable-holdout / adaptive-data-analysis and reward-overoptimization literatures.

The proposed evaluation design, if executed, would instrument a *fixed* research loop with competing progress proxies, run repeated trials, and measure (a) how well each proxy predicts protected outcomes, (b) how well it allocates a fixed validation budget, and (c) validated discoveries per unit cost versus repeated sampling. This mirrors the fuzzing benchmarking pattern where coverage correlates with bug-finding across programs yet the highest-coverage fuzzer is not necessarily the best bug-finder.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — the paper runs no experiments of its own. The evidence it offers is entirely borrowed numbers from prior work, marshalled to support each of its three lessons, plus qualitative system-by-system positioning (AI co-scientist, AI Scientist-v2, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, Agent Laboratory, AIGS, Dolphin, RE-Bench, MLAgentBench). The concrete figures it leans on:

- **Feedback-directed search beats blind mutation (Angora ablation, cited for Lesson two):** on **xmlwf** with a fixed input corpus, gradient-descent-guided constraint solving solved **97.0%** of branch constraints versus **77.4%** for random mutation (**+19.6 points**), and the paper notes gradient descent won on *every* tested program in that ablation. This is the paper's single strongest quantitative anchor — but note it is a 2018 software-fuzzing result, not an auto-research result, so the transfer is asserted rather than demonstrated (the paper concedes "research agents will not inherit Angora's gains automatically").
- **Optimizing the steering proxy ≠ progress under protected evaluation (MLE-bench, cited for Lesson three):** agents that search using cross-validation scores and return the validation-best solution leave **9 to 13 percentage points** of medal rate on the table relative to the counterfactual test-best node. The paper is careful that this gap cannot be closed by querying the hidden test repeatedly — it is offered as a *measurement of proxy–oracle divergence*, not a proposed method.
- **The cost of no protected validation (MLR-Bench):** roughly **80%** of cases under its coding-agent conditions contain fabricated or invalidated experimental results — the empirical stand-in for "learned judges cannot substitute for independent validation."
- **Diminishing returns from generate-and-rank (Brown et al., Large Language Monkeys):** the fraction of problems solved grows approximately **log-linearly** with sample count over the measured range, and without automatic verification, majority voting and reward-model selection **plateau after a few hundred samples**. The paper explicitly refuses to over-read this ("these benchmark results do not establish a universal law of discovery").
- **Executable-objective systems as the easy case:** ALE-Agent, iteratively optimizing an executable contest score, placed **21st among more than 1000 participants** in a live AtCoder contest — used to argue that grounded, machine-executable evaluation is where feedback-directed search already works, and that open science is the hard remainder.
- **Human judgment of LLM ideas (Si et al.):** human reviewers rate machine-proposed ideas as *more novel* but *markedly less feasible* than human ideas — the motivating imbalance for "generate faster than we can validate."
- **Counter-evidence the paper itself surfaces (to its credit):** coverage correlated with bug-finding across many programs, yet the fuzzer with the most coverage did not necessarily find the most bugs (Böhme et al., ICSE'22), and many coverage-unique crashes collapse to a small number of distinct bugs (Klees et al., CCS'18). Both are cited as reasons a progress proxy can be systematically misleading.

## Authors & Credibility

The author list is well matched to the analogy's software-testing half. **Hao Chen** (The University of Hong Kong, previously UC Davis) is a senior security/fuzzing researcher and co-author of **Angora** (IEEE S&P 2018) — the very ablation the paper uses as its strongest quantitative anchor — and of Prompt Fuzzing for Fuzz Driver Generation (CCS 2024). **Yifeng He** (UC Davis / ARA Lab) is first author of "Content Fuzzing for Escaping Information Cocoons on Digital Social Media" (Findings of ACL 2026), which is cited here as evidence that the fuzzing architecture transfers beyond software testing. Affiliations span UC Davis, Zhejiang University (intern work), ARA Lab, and HKU; correspondence lists both an academic and an ARA Lab address. Self-citation is present but disclosed and load-bearing rather than padding (2 of ~60 references). The weaker half is the science-of-science side: none of the authors are, on this evidence, practitioners of the wet-lab or open-ended scientific discovery loops the paper prescribes for, so the "epistemic progress" construct is argued from analogy rather than from experience running such campaigns.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-good).** The specific contribution is not "research is search" — which is old — but the *architectural* claim that the steering signal and the certifying oracle must be different objects with different protection properties, and the concrete import of fuzzing's design vocabulary (seed corpus, mutation operator, power schedule, directedness) into auto-research. The distinction drawn against quality-diversity is genuinely sharp: a QD behavior descriptor models the goal, whereas coverage is read off the execution and models the process. The distinction from Bayesian OED is also honest — the paper positions itself as what you do when you *cannot* specify likelihood, prior, design domain and utility, which is a real regime.

**Rigor (mixed, and this is the paper's main weakness).** The central construct, "epistemic progress," is never operationalized. §3 lists candidate proxies (surprise against a model, novelty against prior work, verifier confidence, cross-replicate disagreement, distance to a stated target) and then says each "balances C1 through C3 differently" — no scoring, no pilot, no worked example on even a toy research loop. Given that the paper's own C3 warns that a search can game any signal it maximizes, and given that its own cited evidence shows coverage over-counting progress (coverage-unique crashes collapsing to few bugs) and highest-coverage fuzzers not finding the most bugs, the paper is in the awkward position of demanding a property (C2 ∧ C3) that the analogy's *source domain* only partially satisfies. It never engages with what auto-research's equivalent of the "coverage plateau" would be.

**Paper-specific soft spots.** (a) The **Angora xmlwf 97.0% vs 77.4%** number is the only head-to-head "feedback beats blind sampling" figure in the paper, and it comes from numerical gradient descent over *byte-level branch predicates* — arguably the least transferable part of fuzzing to scientific hypothesis space; the paper acknowledges this in one sentence ("not to use gradients for every scientific hypothesis") and then keeps the number as its headline evidence. (b) The **MLE-bench 9–13 pp** gap is evidence that validation-best ≠ test-best, which supports the *protected validation* lesson but is orthogonal to the *feedback-directed search* lesson; the paper cites it as a general warning without noting that the same gap is also an argument that *any* cheap steering proxy (including the one it advocates) will leak. (c) The comparison class is somewhat straw-adjacent: "generate-and-rank" is attributed to AI Scientist-v2 and AI co-scientist, but AI Scientist-v2 uses *agentic tree search* over experimental nodes, which is already history-dependent search — the paper's own §5 concedes systems "fall at different points on a spectrum," which undercuts the framing that the field is stuck at repeated sampling. (d) **No ablation, no simulation, no toy environment.** For a paper whose entire thesis is "run these three controlled comparisons," even a synthetic bandit-style demonstration that a dense-but-gameable proxy beats terminal-only feedback at fixed validation budget would have converted a plausible essay into a compelling one. Its absence is the single biggest missed opportunity.

**Reproducibility.** Not applicable in the code/data sense — there is nothing to reproduce. The proposed protocols are specified at the level of "fix the proposer, task, validator, and budget; measure validated discoveries per unit cost," which is a real design but leaves the hardest choices (what is a validated discovery, what is the cost unit, who is the protected validator, how many trials for significance) entirely open. Anyone trying to run these comparisons will have to invent the benchmark themselves.

**Significance.** If you build auto-research systems, the guidance/oracle separation is a genuinely useful lens and cheap to adopt — it costs nothing to stop letting your learned judge both steer and certify, and the MLR-Bench 80% number is a strong motivator. The framing also makes failures diagnosable, which is a real engineering benefit. But the paper hands the field its hardest problem ("whether a scientific experiment can offer intermediate guidance ... is the open question we hand the field") rather than making progress on it.

**Red flags.** Nothing dishonest — the paper is unusually careful about over-reading its borrowed numbers (explicitly disclaiming that Brown et al. establishes "a universal law of discovery" and that agents will "inherit Angora's gains automatically"), and it dedicates a full section to six counter-arguments including ones that could sink it. The overclaiming risk is at the title level: "Agentic Auto-Research **is** Fuzz Testing" is a much stronger statement than the body's carefully scoped "the correspondence concerns control structure, not literal equivalence, and covers search within a declared research problem."

## Strengths

- The guidance/oracle separation is a crisp, actionable, low-cost design rule with a clear failure story attached (adaptive reuse of an optimized proxy → false discoveries), and it makes campaign failures attributable to a specific component instead of "retrain the judge."
- Intellectually honest scoping: the paper repeatedly and explicitly declines to cover the abductive act of changing what counts as the problem, candidate, or discovery, and it names the boundary in both §2 and §7.
- The six alternative-views section is substantive rather than performative — the BOED, quality-diversity, and bitter-lesson responses each identify a real difference (model-richness, process-vs-outcome descriptors, general execution feedback vs hand-coded rules) rather than dismissing the objection.
- The historical arc (Gödel/Church/Turing/Rice → Friedberg → Gödel machine → NAS benchmark overfitting → learned research judges) is an unusually good framing device: it recasts several unrelated failures as instances of the same missing-component diagnosis (no cheap signal, unaffordable oracle, or one score playing both roles).
- It cites evidence *against* its own analogy (coverage does not rank fuzzers by bug count; coverage-unique crashes over-count bugs) instead of hiding it, and folds that into requirement C3.
- Falsifiability is stated concretely three times, including the explicit "If no candidate signal predicts protected progress, this claim is wrong."

## Limitations

- **Zero original empirical content.** Every number in the paper is borrowed; the three predictions are proposed, not tested. Readers looking for evidence that feedback-directed auto-research beats repeated sampling will not find it here.
- **The core construct is unoperationalized.** "Epistemic progress" gets requirements (C1–C3) and a menu of candidate proxies but no metric, no pilot measurement, and no guidance on how to choose among the candidates for a given domain.
- **Transfer of the fuzzing analogy is asserted at its weakest joint.** Angora's gradient-guided constraint solving depends on a differentiable-ish numeric structure over program inputs that has no obvious counterpart in hypothesis space; the paper says as much but does not propose what replaces it.
- **Cost accounting is hand-waved.** C1 requires the progress signal to cost "substantially less" than final validation, but the paper elsewhere insists guidance and oracle "need not run at different frequencies or have inherently different costs" — these two statements sit in tension and are never reconciled.
- **Scope excludes the part many people care about**: open-ended problem reformulation, hypothesis-space revision, and the abductive jump are explicitly out of scope, which means the framework applies to the "declared problem, execute and measure" regime — largely the same regime (executable objectives) where the paper concedes validation is already easy.
- **No treatment of multi-agent or human-in-the-loop cost structure** beyond the closing remark that humans "declare the problem at the front and ground the verdict at the back."

## Relevance

如果关注 LLM agent 的自动科研/自我改进循环、reward hacking 与 judge 可靠性，这篇提供了一个可直接落地的架构分区（引导信号 vs. 受保护验证）和三个可证伪的实验设计，适合作为设计 auto-research evaluation harness 时的 checklist。但它没有任何自有实验，价值在于概念框架与文献地图（MLE-bench 9–13pp、MLR-Bench 80% 伪造率等数字很适合直接引用），不适合期待方法论 SOTA 的读者。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the smallest concrete instantiation that could test the "signal prediction"? E.g. fix a hyperparameter-search or ablation-design loop, instrument it with 4–5 competing proxies (surprise, novelty, verifier confidence, cross-replicate disagreement), and measure rank correlation with a held-out protected outcome — why did the authors not run this?
- How do you prevent the protected validator from being *implicitly* leaked into the search, given that the agent's proposer was pretrained on the literature that the validator's domain draws from? Fuzzing's oracle is causally independent of the mutator; a scientific validator often is not.
- Is there an auto-research analogue of the coverage plateau, and would detecting it be a useful stopping/redirect rule for a campaign scheduler?
- The paper's C1 (cheap) and its claim that guidance and oracle need not differ in cost are in tension. Under what conditions is an *expensive* dense signal still worth it — i.e. what is the right exchange rate between guidance cost and validation cost?
- Cross-replicate disagreement is listed as a candidate proxy; it is also the classic ingredient of Bayesian active learning by disagreement. Does the paper's "not BOED" position survive if the best-performing proxy turns out to be a cheap disagreement-based approximation of expected information gain?
- Directed greybox fuzzing needs a *target* to compute distance to. What is the research analogue of a target site, and does requiring one reintroduce exactly the goal-model that the paper criticizes quality-diversity for needing?
- Given MLE-bench's 9–13 pp validation-vs-test gap, how much of a "feedback-directed search wins" result would be attributable to better search rather than to the proxy simply being a better-calibrated estimate of the protected outcome — and how would you disentangle the two experimentally?
