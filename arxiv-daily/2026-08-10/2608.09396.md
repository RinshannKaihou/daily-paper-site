> **一句话总结：** 本文把不变学习中"某个预测模式是否被学到"重新表述为铁磁相变问题——模式载荷 β 是序参量、正则强度 λ 是温度、环境敏感度 γ 是外场——通过消去非临界参数得到 Landau 有效自由能 F=r·w²/2+u·w⁴/4+…，证明目标函数的二次修正 R2 会移动相边界（在有限强度 λ₂,c=(a²/μ−μ)/γ 处把 shortcut 精确抹零），而四次修正 R4 只能连续压缩载荷（|β*|=[|a|−√(μ(μ+λ₂γ))]₊/(m+λ₄γ)），并据此把 IRM、V-REx、GroupDRO、CORAL、MMD、IGA、Fishr、Fish/MLDG、DANN、Mixup 归入七种"正则化表型"；合成四环境实验中解析 onset 与实测早期增长率零点最大偏差仅 4.7×10⁻⁶。

> **TL;DR:** A physics-style theory paper (no real benchmarks) that reduces invariant-learning objectives to a Landau free energy in which a feature's learned loading is the order parameter and regularization strength is temperature. The key structural claim: only objectives with a genuine *quadratic* (R2) correction can eliminate a shortcut at finite strength (critical strength λ₂,c = (a²/μ−μ)/γ), while quartic-leading objectives (IRMv1, V-REx, Fishr, CORAL-cov) can only shrink it asymptotically and leave residual loading. Validation is on synthetic 4-environment scalar/3-feature constructions plus 1–2 hidden-layer ReLU MLPs (width 32): analytic vs. measured onset agrees to max abs. deviation 4.7×10⁻⁶; unfitted Landau loading curves track trained |β| over bF ∈ [0,2]; a theory-blind critical-strength calibration over 52 settings and 3125 bootstrap draws lands on the y=x line. A finite-sample audit shows MMD-mean/CORAL-full leak onto the stable mode with γ̄_c = 0.0286 ± 0.0078 vs. γ̄_s = 5.092 ± 0.212 (ratio 5.7×10⁻³) versus the oracle R2's exact (0, 1), quantitatively explaining why those methods destroy the stable feature at λ ≳ 10. Caveat: the best-performing curves use an *oracle* environmental-sensitivity matrix Γ_E that the authors themselves admit is not identifiable from finitely many observed environments.

## 核心贡献

- **铁磁相变视角**：把"预测模式的习得"映射为磁化过程——模式载荷为序参量 w（或 β=wθ），正则强度为温度型控制参数，环境依赖支撑为模式相关的外场；纯靠环境场支撑的 shortcut 类似顺磁响应，自发有序的模式类似铁磁相，因果干预对应改变外场。
- **Landau 约化与"目标签名"**：利用双线性参数化的 Z₂ 对称性 F(−w,−θ)=F(w,θ)，通过 Lyapunov–Schmidt 消去非临界方向 θ（θ*(w)=κ(λ)w+O(w³)），得到只含偶次项的有效自由能；其低阶系数 r（有效质量）与 u（四次刚度）构成目标函数的"低阶签名"，可直接预测相边界、后临界载荷与正则化路径形态。
- **R2 与 R4 的角色分离（可证明）**：定理 4.1 给出精确相边界 |a|² > μ(μ+λ₂γ)，即 λ₂,c=(a²/μ−μ)/γ；命题 4.3 给出稳态载荷闭式解 |β*|=[|a|−√(μ(μ+λ₂γ))]₊/(m+λ₄γ)。R2 让载荷在有限强度处精确归零，R4 只增大分母、连续压缩，永远留残余。若 γ=0，二次环境正则完全不移动该模式的相边界。
- **选择性保留窗口**：推论 4.2 指出，当 shortcut 与 stable 模式的临界强度满足 λ₂,c^(s) < λ₂,c^(c) 时，区间 (λ₂,c^(s), λ₂,c^(c)) 内 shortcut 被稳定在原点而 stable 方向仍处有序相——保留与否取决于任务信号相对环境敏感度之比，而非边际环境关联。
- **多分量集体模**：定理 5.1 给出谱型相边界判据 λ_max[(1/μ)Cᵀ(μI+λ₂Γ_E)⁻¹C] > 1；两个单独看都环境敏感的特征可以组成补偿型集体稳定方向。推论 5.2 证明矩阵版 R4（作用在 β 上）能直接惩罚 predictor-space 泄漏 β₁−β₂，而矩阵 R2（作用在 w 上）不能，因为 w₁=w₂ 不蕴含 β₁=β₂。
- **对现有方法的结构化归类**：Table 1/2 把十余种目标函数按局部展开归为七类表型——有限强度消除（显式 R2、MMD-mean、CORAL-full、IGA）、连续收缩（CORAL-cov、Fishr、V-REx）、大强度全局塌缩（IRMv1、Gaussian MMD）、非零平台（GroupDRO）、非单调/振荡（DANN）、方向依赖后失稳（Fish/MLDG）、无固定环境修正（ERM、Mixup）。

## Key Contributions

- **A ferromagnetic Landau description of invariant learning.** Mode loading = order parameter, regularization strength = temperature, environmental support = external field; mode onset, suppression, and selective retention become statements about r = 0 phase boundaries.
- **Objective signatures as a predictive methodology.** A variational (Lyapunov–Schmidt) elimination of the noncritical direction θ yields r(λ) = F_ww − F_wθ²/F_θθ and a quartic stiffness u(λ); these two numbers, computable directly from an objective's local Taylor expansion, are claimed to determine the whole regularization phenotype.
- **Exact closed forms for a canonical bilinear model.** Phase boundary λ₂,c = (a²/μ − μ)/γ, quartic stiffness u = 2a²B/μ², loading |β*| = [|a| − √(Aμ)]₊/B with A = μ+λ₂γ, B = m+λ₄γ, mean-field critical exponent 1/2 (|w*| ∝ (λ_c−λ)^{1/2}), and a second-derivative jump in the minimum free energy (a textbook second-order transition).
- **Selective-retention window** as a corollary, plus a spectral collective-mode criterion for the multi-feature case.
- **A phenotype table for 13 objectives** (ERM, IRMv1, V-REx, GroupDRO, IGA, Fish/MLDG, Fishr, Mixup, MMD-mean, Gaussian MMD, CORAL-cov, CORAL-full, DANN) derived from their local expansions (Appendix H, Table 2).
- **Controlled empirical validation** in bilinear models, 1- and 2-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs, and a compensatory-collective-mode construction, including a theory-blind critical-strength calibration protocol and a finite-sample operator-alignment audit.

## Methodology

The setting is deliberately stripped down: supervised learning over |E_tr| = 4 training environments with identifiable low-dimensional predictive modes, so that "which mode was learned" is directly measurable and not confounded by feature discovery, optimization, or model selection.

**Reduction.** For the bilinear parameterization β = wθ, the objective is invariant under (w,θ) → (−w,−θ), which is exactly the Z₂ symmetry of a zero-field Landau free energy — so the reduced expansion contains only even powers, and the absence of a cubic term is a symmetry consequence rather than a truncation assumption. Assuming ∂_θθF(0,0;λ_c) > 0, the implicit function theorem slaves θ to w and gives F_eff(w;λ) = L₀ + r(λ)w²/2 + u(λ)w⁴/4 + O(w⁶). Crucially, r subtracts the curvature relief from θ's readjustment: r = F_ww − F_wθ²/F_θθ. The paper stresses that the mere presence of a w², wθ, or θ² monomial in an objective does *not* determine the sign of the mass shift — the correction must be projected onto the critical soft mode.

**Canonical model.** F_can(w,θ;λ₂,λ₄) = L₀ − awθ + (m+λ₄γ)w²θ²/2 + (μ+λ₂γ)w²/2 + μθ²/2, with a the task-predictive signal, γ ≥ 0 the environmental sensitivity, μ the baseline (weight-decay) curvature. For this model, strict convexity in θ makes the 1-D reduction *exact*, not merely local, so the phase boundary and loading formula are theorems rather than asymptotics. BCE is shown (Appendix B.3) to share the same local normal form as squared loss.

**Mapping to real objectives.** Appendix H expands each published objective around the same controlled bilinear reference state. Examples: MMD-mean contributes a pure gate-block R2 (λ_μM₂w²); CORAL-full = MMD-mean's R2 plus CORAL-cov's R4 (λ_ΣS₂w⁴); IRMv1 gives λ[c_e²β² − 2c_eq_eβ³ + q_e²β⁴], i.e. R4-leading with explicit sixth- and eighth-order terms and *no* R2; V-REx gives R2 only when null risks are unequal, otherwise R4-leading; IGA gives a PSD matrix-R2 (λzᵀΔH_eᵀΔH_e z) plus sign-indefinite R4 and nonnegative R6 gradient couplings; Fish/MLDG gives a *sign-indefinite* quadratic form G_Fish whose negative directions imply finite-strength local instability; DANN is handled through a locally profiled *linear* discriminator surrogate (PSD gate R2 plus a saturating negative R4) which the authors explicitly flag as a surrogate, not the real nonlinear adversary.

**Dynamics link.** Gradient flow near the boundary reduces to Model-A relaxational dynamics τ_c dq/dt = −r q − u q³ + …, whose linear term changes sign exactly at r = 0. This justifies measuring the *static* boundary via the zero crossing of the early-time growth rate, and predicts critical slowing down τ_relax ∝ |r|⁻¹.

**Experimental constructions.** All data are synthetic with four environments, q_e = (−3,−1,1,3)/√5 (zero mean, unit second moment). (i) Single-feature scalar probe X_F = a_F Y + b_F q_e + σ_F ε with a_F = 0.6, σ_F = 1, scanning b_F ∈ [0,2]; a matched correlation-heterogeneity probe X = (0.6 + Δ_a q_e)Y + ε for IGA/V-REx. (ii) Stable–shortcut path experiments: X_c = a_cY + σ_cε_c, X_s = (a_s + Δ_a q_e)Y + δq_e + σ_sε_s with (a_c,a_s,δ,Δ_a,σ_c,σ_s) = (0.6,0.8,1,0.3,1,0.5) plus four independent noise features; 256 samples/env train, 2048/env test; OOD test uses q = ±2 and flips a_s → −a_s. (iii) Nonlinear transfer: plain ReLU MLPs, width 32 or (32,32), 2000 steps, Adam lr 0.01/0.005; "functional loading" = mean absolute Jacobian of the prediction w.r.t. each input coordinate on OOD samples, normalized by the same method's λ = 0 value. (iv) Collective mode: three features where X₁ + X₂ cancels the environmental background while X₁ − X₂ is the leakage direction, with oracle Γ_E = d_pair d_pairᵀ + d_short d_shortᵀ; diagonal(Γ_E) baselines vs. full matrix R2/R4. Five seeds ({0,1,2,3,4}), full-batch updates, no annealing/scheduling/early stopping, λ grids fixed before training.

**Theory-blind calibration protocol (Fig. 4).** To avoid circularity, empirical critical strengths are estimated without touching λ_c^theory: a fixed 24-point coarse grid, a "resolved ordered" test (b̄_λ − 1.96 s_λ > b̄_floor + 1.96 s_floor) to bracket the transition, a 9-point (21 for IGA) refinement, linear extrapolation of the loading to zero from the five strengths closest to the transition, and all 5⁵ = 3125 five-seed bootstrap resamples for the interval. Quality control rejects nonnegative slopes, R² < 0.95, or roots more than half a bracket width outside the bracket.

## Results & Highlights

- **Onset prediction is essentially exact in the population limit (Fig. 2).** Across the mean-shift scan b_F ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2} and the IGA correlation-heterogeneity scan Δ_a ∈ {0,…,0.8}, the analytic Landau onset and the measured zero crossing of the early-time growth rate agree with **maximum absolute deviation 4.7 × 10⁻⁶**. The qualitative split is exactly as predicted: ERM, IRM, V-REx, GroupDRO, CORAL-cov, and explicit R4 produce a visually flat (≈0) onset shift relative to ERM, whereas explicit R2, R2+R4, MMD-mean, CORAL-full, and profiled DANN produce a finite shift that grows monotonically with b_F (up to ≈1 on the plotted axis at b_F = 2). This is the paper's cleanest quantitative claim, and it is a *population/Hessian* computation with no finite-sample training error.
- **Unfitted loading law tracks trained models (Fig. 3).** Objective-specific low-order coefficients derived analytically — never fitted to the trained paths — are inserted into the same reduced Landau equilibrium and reproduce trained |β_F| across ERM, R2, R4, R2+R4, MMD-mean, and CORAL-full over b_F ∈ [0,2] (final loading falling from ≈0.28 at b_F = 0 to 0 at the predicted finite b_F for R2-type objectives, versus a nonzero residual for R4-type), with mean ± s.e.m. over five models. Retaining the sextic coefficient v₆ (s* = (−u + √(u²−4v₆r))/(2v₆)) extends the same construction to V-REx and IGA in panel (b).
- **Theory-blind critical strengths land on y = x across 52 settings (Fig. 4).** Twelve settings each for explicit R2, R2+R4, MMD-mean, CORAL-full (enumerating a_F ∈ {0.5,0.6}, μ ∈ {0.15,0.2}, b_F ∈ {0.5,0.7,0.9}) plus 4 matched-IGA settings. Empirical λ_c estimates track λ_c^theory over roughly 0.4–0.85 (MMD-mean/CORAL-full), 1.5–8 (R2 and R2+R4), and 0.30–0.55 (IGA), with 95% bootstrap bars from 3125 draws. The decisive within-family contrast: **adding R4 to R2 changes the amplitude and near-critical slope of the loading branch but does not move the R2-controlled critical point** — R2+R4 shares R2's transition, exactly as Eq. (12) requires.
- **A pre-training finite-sample audit quantitatively predicts where MMD/CORAL fail (Table 3).** For MMD-mean and CORAL-full, the empirical gate-block R2 coefficients are **γ̄_c = 0.0286 ± 0.0078 (stable mode) vs. γ̄_s = 5.092 ± 0.212 (shortcut), ratio (5.73 ± 1.57) × 10⁻³**, compared with the oracle R2's exact (γ_c, γ_s) = (0, 1). At the largest plotted strength λ = 100 the stable-mode contribution is λγ̄_c ≈ **2.86, i.e. ~14× the gate weight-decay coefficient μ = 0.2** — negligible at intermediate λ but enough to visibly suppress the stable mode at large λ. This is the paper's best piece of evidence, because the audit uses only the five pre-training datasets (no trained paths, no performance values) and still predicts the shape of the Fig. 5 curves.
- **Regularization paths match the phenotype table (Fig. 5).** Oracle R2 and R2+R4 keep the shortcut suppressed while preserving the stable mode out to the largest tested strength (λ = 10³); MMD-mean and CORAL-full show a selective-retention plateau at intermediate strengths but lose stable retention at large strength (consistent with the γ̄_c leakage above); V-REx follows R4-leading shrinkage with only a mild nonlinear tail (consistent with weak R6/R8 corrections); Fish/MLDG curves **terminate at the first observed instability**, matching the sign-indefinite G_Fish prediction. OOD AUC (axis range ≈0.3–0.8) improves *only* when shortcut suppression preserves the stable mode; IID AUC stays in a narrow ≈0.76–0.90 band.
- **Depth shifts scale but not phenotype (Fig. 6).** Transferring the same data and objectives to 1- and 2-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs preserves the qualitative ordering (R2-like objectives retain finite-strength shortcut control; quartic-dominant ones leave residual shortcut loading), but the transition interval **shifts and broadens toward larger λ** because a nominal λ is distributed across many factorization paths. Increasing depth *accentuates* the large-strength distinction, with quartic-dominant paths showing rebound and non-monotone tails. No numerical agreement metric is reported for this section — it is an eyeball comparison.
- **Collective modes require coupled regularizers (Figs. 7–8).** In the compensatory three-feature construction, coordinate-diagonal R2/R4 destroy the stable combination while suppressing the shortcut; coupled matrix R2/R4/R2+R4 built from oracle Γ_E suppress the shortcut *and* preserve the collective mode while improving OOD AUC (panel axis ≈0.3–0.7). Matrix R4 is the most effective at suppressing predictor-space background leakage (leakage panel, ≈0–0.15), consistent with Corollary 5.2's argument that w₁ = w₂ does not imply β₁ = β₂. The supplementary full-baseline comparison (Fig. 8) shows no non-oracle method simultaneously wins all four diagnostics — several curves are clipped (retention at 1.45, leakage at 0.268, shortcut loading at 0.383), i.e. they blow up.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (high, with an honest caveat).** Applying Landau theory to learning is not new — the paper cites the committee-machine and jamming literature itself — but the specific move of expanding *an invariant-learning regularizer* around the origin and reading off whether it contributes to r (mass) or u (stiffness) is genuinely new and, more importantly, *useful*: it explains why IRMv1 and V-REx behave qualitatively differently from CORAL-full even though both are "invariance penalties." The observation that r = F_ww − F_wθ²/F_θθ, so that the presence of a w² monomial does not determine the mass shift, is the kind of point that would save practitioners real time.

**Rigor (strong on the theory side, weak on the evaluation side).** The theorems are correct and the canonical model's reduction is genuinely exact (strict convexity in θ), not just local. But the empirical program is entirely synthetic. There is **no Colored MNIST, no PACS/VLCS/OfficeHome, no DomainBed, no WILDS** — despite Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz and Koh et al. being cited as the motivating pathology. Every experiment uses hand-designed four-environment Gaussian-ish constructions with q_e = (−3,−1,1,3)/√5, identifiable single features, and networks of width 32. That is a legitimate choice for a *controlled* structural study, and the authors say so, but it means the paper demonstrates that its theory describes its own toy models, not that the phenotypes survive real feature learning.

**The oracle problem is the central weakness.** The curves that actually achieve the advertised "selective retention" — oracle R2, oracle R4, matrix R2+R4 — are constructed from the ground-truth environmental sensitivity operator Γ_E. Section 5 concedes outright that "oracle Γ_E is not uniquely identifiable from observational associations in finitely many training environments alone; uniquely recovering it requires additional structural assumptions, an environmental generative model, or causal priors." So the paper's most impressive empirical result (Fig. 7: shortcut suppressed, collective mode preserved, OOD AUC up) is not achievable by any deployable algorithm, and the honest reading of Fig. 8 is that *every actual method fails at least one of the four diagnostics*. The framework is diagnostic, not prescriptive; the abstract's claim that it predicts "what models learn" is on firmer ground than any implied claim that it improves OOD generalization.

**Asymmetric evidentiary standards across methods.** Only R2, R2+R4, MMD-mean, CORAL-full, IGA, and V-REx receive quantitative tests (loading curves and/or critical-strength calibration). IRM, GroupDRO, Fishr, Gaussian MMD, DANN, Fish/MLDG, and Mixup get only qualitative "the path looks like the phenotype we predicted" agreement, judged visually on log-scale plots. The phenotype predictions for these methods are hedged with "may show," "may undergo," "may retain," "or large seed variability" — which makes them nearly unfalsifiable. DANN is the worst case: the theory is applied to a *locally profiled linear discriminator surrogate*, and Table 2 admits "the nonlinear adversary is measured rather than globally identified with this surrogate," so the DANN row is a prediction about a different object than the DANN curve it is compared against.

**Non-independent evidence.** Table 3 reports *identical* γ̄_c = 0.0286 and γ̄_s = 5.092 for MMD-mean and CORAL-full — necessarily, since CORAL-full's leading R2 operator is literally MMD-mean's. Their two marker sets in Fig. 4 therefore are not two independent confirmations of the critical-strength prediction; effectively the calibration covers three distinct R2 mechanisms (explicit R2, mean-matching, IGA gradient alignment), not five.

**Missing ablations.** The number of training environments is fixed at 4 throughout with a single hand-chosen q_e; there is no sweep over |E_tr|, no test of how the phase boundary degrades as environments become fewer or less diverse — precisely the regime where Rosenfeld et al. (2021) and Kamath et al. (2021), both cited, show invariance objectives break. Width is fixed at 32, depth at ≤2, seeds at 5 with s.e.m. (not std) error bars. The nonlinear section reports no quantitative agreement statistic at all, only that the qualitative ordering "persists." There is also no sample-size ablation, even though the entire γ̄_c ≠ 0 leakage story is a *finite-sample* effect that should shrink with n.

**Equilibrium vs. training reality.** The theory is about equilibria; the experiments train Adam for 2000–3000 steps with no early stopping and treat the endpoint as the equilibrium. Appendix F argues the early-growth diagnostic measures the same static boundary near criticality, and the 4.7×10⁻⁶ agreement supports that *for the population Hessian computation*. But the trained regularization paths (Figs. 5–7) are not verified to be converged, and critical slowing down (τ ∝ |r|⁻¹, which the paper itself predicts) means the runs *closest to the transition* are exactly the ones most likely to be under-converged — a self-inflicted confound the paper does not address.

**Reproducibility (mixed).** Appendix J is unusually good: full data-generating equations, all constants, λ grids, seeds {0,1,2,3,4}, optimizer settings, and a pre-registered, explicitly theory-blind calibration protocol with stated rejection criteria (R² < 0.95, slope ≥ 0, root outside bracket). The bootstrap over all 3125 five-seed resamples is more careful than typical. However, **no code URL or anonymous repository link appears anywhere in the paper**, despite repeated references to "the corresponding output directory's config.json" and "provided with the code." Everything is reimplementable from the appendix, but nothing is directly runnable.

**Red flags.** (1) The oracle-dependence of the headline collective-mode result, stated honestly in the text but not in the abstract. (2) Phenotype predictions phrased with "may," making Table 1 hard to falsify for half its rows. (3) DANN's surrogate mismatch. (4) Zero real-world data. (5) Figure 8's clipped upper tails (1.45 / 0.268 / 0.383) indicate several baselines diverge, which is reported but not analyzed.

## Strengths

- The physics is done correctly and not decoratively: Z₂ symmetry is used to *justify* the absence of a cubic term rather than assuming truncation; the reduction is a proper Lyapunov–Schmidt elimination; the mean-field exponent 1/2 and second-derivative jump are derived, not asserted; Appendix E bounds the truncation error as O(|λ−λ_c|) relative to the leading free-energy difference.
- The R2/R4 distinction is a genuinely actionable insight: "does your penalty move the onset or just compress the amplitude?" is a question practitioners can now answer by expanding their loss, and it explains a real folk observation (that IRM-family penalties leave residual shortcut reliance no matter how hard you crank λ).
- The theory-blind critical-strength protocol (Fig. 4, Appendix J.3.3) is methodologically exemplary — the theoretical value is used neither to select the bracket, nor the fitting points, nor the fit, and the quality controls are pre-specified.
- Table 3's operator-alignment audit is a rare case of a paper predicting *its own baselines' failure mode from objective structure alone*, before training, and then observing that failure.
- Corollary 5.2's predictor-space-vs-parameter-space distinction (w₁ = w₂ ⇏ β₁ = β₂) is a sharp, non-obvious observation with immediate implications for where a matrix penalty should be applied.
- Intellectual honesty in the body text: the non-identifiability of Γ_E, the surrogate status of DANN, the asymptotic-only nature of predictions away from the canonical model, and the "cannot establish an exact finite-sample phase transition" disclaimer are all stated explicitly.

## Limitations

- **No real-world or standard OOD benchmark.** All evidence is from synthetic constructions with identifiable, low-dimensional, pre-specified modes; feature discovery, representation collapse, and high-dimensional confounding are excluded by design.
- **Oracle Γ_E is required for the best results and is admittedly unidentifiable** from finite observed environments without extra causal or generative assumptions. The paper offers no estimator.
- **Fixed at four environments, width-32 networks, ≤2 hidden layers, 5 seeds.** No ablation over environment count, network width, sample size, or noise level.
- **Quantitative agreement does not transfer to nonlinear networks** — only qualitative phenotype ordering does; the paper explicitly reports depth-dependent shifts in transition scale with no correction factor.
- **Half the phenotype table is only qualitatively validated**, and DANN's row applies to a linear profiled surrogate rather than the trained adversary.
- **Equilibrium theory vs. finite-step training**, with critical slowing down making near-critical runs the most suspect.
- **No released code**, only a detailed appendix.
- The theory is local (near a *simple continuous* transition with u > 0); first-order transitions, u < 0 regimes, and multi-critical points are outside scope, as are objectives whose behavior is dominated by terms of order 6+ over the whole path.

## Relevance

如果你（用户）关注 OOD 泛化 / 不变学习 / shortcut learning 的理论基础，这篇给出了一个少见的"从目标函数形式直接预测正则化路径行为"的可操作框架，尤其是 R2 vs R4 的角色分离能解释为什么 IRM/V-REx 类惩罚无论怎么加大强度都留残余 shortcut。同时它也是统计物理方法用于理解深度学习训练结果（而非仅训练动力学）的一个干净范例，适合作为 Landau/相变视角建模学习行为的方法论参考——但要注意其全部实验都是合成构造，最好结果依赖不可辨识的 oracle Γ_E。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can Γ_E be *estimated* rather than oracled? The paper's own audit shows the empirical mean-difference operator Γ̂_μ has γ_c/γ_s ≈ 5.7×10⁻³ — that is already a 175:1 selectivity. What accuracy of Γ̂_E is actually needed for the selective-retention window to survive, and does a shrinkage/denoised estimator close the gap to oracle?
- How does the phase boundary λ₂,c = (a²/μ−μ)/γ degrade as |E_tr| shrinks from 4 to 2, where Rosenfeld et al.'s impossibility results bite? A sweep over environment count would test whether the framework explains *why* invariance methods fail in the few-environment regime, which would be a much stronger result than the current toy validation.
- Does the R2/R4 taxonomy predict anything measurable on Colored MNIST or a WILDS shift — e.g., does the residual shortcut reliance of IRMv1 at large λ show the predicted non-vanishing floor, and does adding an explicit R2 term to IRMv1 create a finite elimination threshold?
- The mean-field exponent 1/2 and critical slowing down τ ∝ |r|⁻¹ are testable *dynamical* signatures. Measuring the relaxation-time divergence near λ_c would be a much sharper (and harder to fake) confirmation than loading curves.
- What happens with feature *discovery* rather than fixed identifiable modes? If the modes themselves emerge during training, the reference point for the expansion moves — does the Landau picture become a moving-target problem, or can the soft-mode construction of Theorem B.2 track it?
- Fish/MLDG's predicted finite-strength instability from a negative direction of G_Fish is a falsifiable, method-specific prediction. Can the instability threshold be predicted numerically (not just "terminates at first instability") and matched against observation, the way λ₂,c was in Fig. 4?
- Since matrix R4 uniquely penalizes predictor-space leakage (β₁−β₂), is there a practical, oracle-free regularizer that operates on β rather than w — e.g., penalizing environment-conditioned variance of the *Jacobian* rather than of the weights — and does it inherit the predicted phenotype?
