A soft prompt turns a frozen random transformer into a minimax-optimal kernel regressor — no training.

Training-Free Universal Approximation by Prompting Random Transformers
Alexander Hsu · Rongjie Lai · Purdue, Mathematics · 2026-08-10
transformer-theory prompt-tuning universal-approximation kernel-methods random-networks
Key metric
10−16
Sup-squared error between the prompted network and the oracle NW estimator at n=1024 — down from ~101 at n=8, tracking the predicted n−2 slope (Fig. 2a).
Why this matters

Sharpest answer yet to whether task power must live in weights — a prompt alone can induce minimax-optimal estimation.

Critical edge

Building the prompt needs white-box weight access and one token per data point — closer to a lookup table than prompting.

01

Methodology

方法

The hypothesis class is deliberately minimal: G = D ∘ A ∘ E — one single-head softmax self-attention layer, with no feedforward, no residual, no layer norm. The construction matches attention logits to Gaussian-kernel Nadaraya-Watson (NW) exponents term by term, yielding three linear constraints per prompt token (kernel direction, bias-plus-logit-shift, and value readout). Stacked into W̃ and solved by the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, this gives a closed-form prompt pi* = W̃†vi — one prompt per target function, no optimization loop. A logit shift β ≥ 0 suppresses the spurious query-self-attention term at rate e−β. The rank condition needed for solvability holds almost surely under i.i.d. Gaussian weights (random-matrix singular-value bounds; Fig. 1). Setting bandwidth h = n−1/(2α+dX) and β = 3R²⁄2 · n2/(2α+dX) yields the intrinsic-dimension-dependent minimax rate Õ(n−2α/(2α+dX)) on any compact dX-manifold.

03

Strengths & Limitations

优势与局限

Strengths

  • Explicit & interpretable: three matching conditions, one pseudoinverse, no optimization loop, no hidden constants.
  • Closes the "will training ever reach these weights?" gap — rank condition holds a.s. under Gaussian init (Fig. 1).
  • Rare cost-accounting of prompt token norms across three regimes, not just existence proofs.
  • Intellectually honest: concedes bounds aren't tight (B2 beats them), flags B3 as uninformative, self-diagnoses the Arch.-B numerical failure.
  • Remark 1 enumerates alternatives (prompt duplication, masked/cross-attention) and justifies the design choice.

Limitations

  • Toy architecture: one layer, one head, no FFN/residual/LayerNorm — extension to real transformers is open.
  • Scalar single-task regression on a compact manifold — no seq2seq, no discrete tokens, no language.
  • Prompt length = # support points; no lower bound is proven, so the necessary length is unknown.
  • Numerically hostile at width: token norms unbounded in n and √dh; β = O(dh1.5) is unrepresentable in bf16.
  • White-box & sample-hungry: needs the realized frozen weights and labeled samples — not black-box ICL.
  • Thin validation: one function on one 2-sphere, α ≤ 1 only; all evidence read from log-log plots, no numeric tables.

04

Questions for Further Exploration

待探究
  1. Does the construction survive LayerNorm / RMSNorm, which rescale each prompt token?
  2. Is there a lower bound on prompt length for accuracy ε — could O(log n) tokens suffice?
  3. Can multi-head or depth > 1 cut the O(dh1.5) logit shift to something representable?
  4. Why does frozen-random-prompt (B4) work well in Arch. A — is random context a computational scratchpad?
  5. How do rates change under Xavier / Kaiming (1/dh-variance) init, which the paper deliberately avoids?
  6. Does it extend to seq2seq targets, or to α > 1 where local-polynomial estimators beat kernels?
