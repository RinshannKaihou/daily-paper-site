> **一句话总结：** 本文证明：在梯度噪声几乎必然一致有界（半径 σ）的 K=1 新鲜样本预言机模型下，任何随机自适应算法寻找 ε-稳定点所需的查询数下界为 Ω(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ²/ε⁴)，与 SGD 标准上界和有界方差下的最优率完全一致——从而（负面地）解决了 Arjevani 等人 2023 年提出的开放问题：更强的"几乎必然有界噪声"并不能改善最坏情况复杂度。

> **TL;DR:** In the K=1 fresh-sample oracle model with almost-surely uniformly bounded gradient noise (radius σ), any randomized adaptive algorithm needs **Ω(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ²/ε⁴)** queries to reach an ε-stationary point of an L-smooth nonconvex objective — exactly matching both the classic SGD upper bound of Ghadimi-Lan and the bounded-variance minimax lower bound of Arjevani et al. (zero rate improvement), thereby resolving their open question of whether uniform boundedness of the oracle error beats bounded variance. The key device replaces the rare large-amplitude reveals used in prior lower bounds with many bounded, slightly biased random signs that leak only O(Dε²/σ²) KL information per response.

## 核心贡献

- 在 K=1 新鲜样本、固定分布、梯度噪声几乎必然一致有界（半径 σ）的预言机模型下，证明了 ε-稳定点的查询复杂度下界为 Ω(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ²/ε⁴)（定理 4.1），与 SGD 的标准上界 O(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ²/ε⁴) 紧匹配。
- 解决了 Arjevani 等（2023, *Mathematical Programming*）明确列出的开放问题：把"有界方差"加强为"几乎必然有界误差"是否会带来更优的率——答案是否定的，最坏情况率不变。
- 提出全新的"编码链 + 偏置符号"硬实例构造：用未知的随机符号向量 θⱼ ∈ {−1,+1}ᴮ 编码下一个链坐标，把梯度贡献分散为许多有界、轻微偏置的随机符号的均值；每个响应都几乎必然有界（‖·‖=σ/2），但仅泄漏 O(Dε²/σ²) KL 信息。
- 用条件 KL 链式法则加上一个"定长比较交互"（fixed-length comparison interaction），把单响应 KL 界转化为"穿越每条链需要 Ω(σ²/ε²) 次相关响应"的自适应查询下界，并显式处理了自适应查询历史带来的复杂性。

## Key Contributions

- Establishes the **tight minimax lower bound Ω(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ²/ε⁴)** for finding an ε-stationary point of an L-smooth nonconvex objective under an unbiased oracle whose noise is uniformly almost-surely bounded by σ, in the K=1 fresh-sample model (Theorem 4.1).
- **Resolves the open problem of Arjevani et al. (2023):** strengthening the standard "bounded variance" assumption to "almost-surely bounded error" yields no improvement in worst-case query complexity, even though the latter is a strictly smaller oracle class.
- Introduces a novel hard-instance construction that **encodes each chain coordinate as a correlation with a hidden sign vector θⱼ ∈ {−1,+1}ᴮ**; the gradient contribution is realized as the mean of many bounded, slightly biased random signs, so every response has fixed norm σ/2 while leaking only O(Dε²/σ²) KL information about the hidden sign.
- Converts the single-response KL bound into an **adaptive query lower bound** via a conditional KL chain rule plus a fixed-length comparison interaction (Definition 8.5), proving each of the T ≍ ΔL/ε² links requires Ω(σ²/ε²) "relevant" responses (Lemma 8.8).
- Adds a **log cosh penalty term** that prevents algorithms from escaping to large-norm points where the tanh encoding would saturate and make the chain spuriously look stationary (Lemma 6.2: an unfinished coordinate forces ‖∇F‖ > 2ε).

## Methodology

The proof builds a hard instance family F_θ inside the function class F_d(Δ,L) together with a fixed-law oracle g_θ(x,z) of uniformly bounded noise, then shows no N-query randomized adaptive algorithm can drive E‖∇F(x̂_N)‖ below ε. It proceeds in five steps:

1. **Scalar zero-chain (Section 5).** Start from the smooth scalar zero-chain of Carmon, Duchi, Hinder, Sidford [2] of length T ≍ ΔL/ε². Its nearest-neighbor structure lets information pass only from coordinate j to j+1, and Lemma 5.1 guarantees the first unfinished coordinate has |∂ⱼ f| ≥ 2, with uniformly bounded derivatives (‖∇f_T‖∞ ≤ 64, Lip(∇f_T) ≤ 2400).
2. **Encoding + escape prevention (Section 6).** Hide each scalar coordinate inside a D-dimensional block via qⱼ(x) = (1/D)Σ θⱼℓ tanh(√D xⱼℓ/δ) against an unknown sign vector θⱼ (Eq. 15). The objective F_θ = A·f_T(q(x)) + (8A/D)Σ log cosh(yⱼℓ) (Eq. 16) is verified to be globally L-smooth with F_θ(0)−inf F_θ ≤ Δ (Lemma 6.1); the log cosh term forces a large gradient whenever any encoded coordinate is unfinished, including at large-norm points (Lemma 6.2).
3. **Uniformly bounded oracle (Section 7).** Decompose ∇F_θ(x) = Bⱼ(x) + hⱼ(x), where Bⱼ is determined by earlier sign vectors and the small remainder hⱼ depends on the next sign vector (Eq. 29). Realize hⱼ as the mean of bounded ±r (r=σ/2) random signs by thresholding uniform coordinates on Z=[0,1]^{TD} (Eq. 31). Lemma 7.1 proves joint measurability, unbiasedness, and ‖g_θ(x,z)−∇F_θ(x)‖ < σ for all x,z.
4. **Information accumulation (Section 8).** Each response has KL divergence ≤ KDε²/σ² (K=2²⁴) from a sign-independent reference distribution (Lemma 8.3c). A comparison interaction (Definition 8.5) restricts the algorithm to cross at most one link per step and agrees with the real interaction except on event J of probability ≤ 1/128 (Lemma 8.6). A Chernoff-style correlation bound (Lemma 8.1: Pr[⟨Ξ,V⟩ > √D/4] ≤ 2e^{−D/128}) plus the conditional KL chain rule show crossing one link needs n₀ ≍ σ²/ε² relevant responses (Lemma 8.8).
5. **Summing over links (Section 9).** With N ≤ Tn₀/2 total queries, the probability of completing all T links is ≤ 9/128, so E‖∇F_Θ(x̂_N)‖ > 2ε with probability ≥ 119/128 (Proposition 8.9). Averaging over the uniform prior on sign vectors and selecting the worst θ* yields the deterministic-instance lower bound; the explicit dimension is d = T·D with D = max(2¹⁵, 256·log(16(N+1)T)) (Eq. 85).

## Results & Highlights

This is a pure theory paper; the quantitative results are oracle-complexity bounds rather than empirical benchmark scores.

- **Tight query-complexity lower bound (Theorem 4.1).** Metric: number of first-order oracle queries to achieve E‖∇F(x̂)‖ ≤ ε on an L-smooth nonconvex objective in the K=1 bounded-noise model. Paper's number: **Ω(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ²/ε⁴)** for every randomized adaptive algorithm. Strongest matched upper bound / baseline: **O(ΔL/ε² + ΔLσ²/ε⁴)** for SGD (Ghadimi-Lan, 2013). Delta: **zero** — the lower bound is tight, closing the gap with the standard upper bound exactly.
- **No rate gain over bounded variance.** Metric: minimax rate exponent in ε. Paper's number: **ε⁻⁴** (the ΔLσ²/ε⁴ term dominates in the theorem's regime). Strongest baseline: Arjevani et al.'s (2023) sharp **ε⁻⁴** minimax lower bound under bounded variance. Delta: **the rate is identical** even though "almost-surely bounded noise" is a strictly smaller oracle class — this is a negative resolution of their open question.
- **Per-link response cost and failure probability (Lemma 8.8 / Proposition 8.9).** Metric: oracle responses needed to cross one chain link with bounded probability. Paper's number: **n₀ ≍ σ²/ε² relevant responses per link**; with T ≍ ΔL/ε² links the necessary total budget is Tn₀/2 ≍ ΔLσ²/ε⁴. Under this budget the output fails stationarity with **E‖∇F(x̂)‖ > 2ε at probability ≥ 119/128 ≈ 0.93**; the "mismatch" event J has probability ≤ 1/128.
- **Per-response information leakage (Lemma 7.1, Eq. 36).** Metric: KL divergence between the true response and a sign-independent reference. Paper's number: **D_KL ≤ KDε²/σ²** with K = 16H² = 2²⁴, H = 1024. An independent query crosses a new link with probability ≤ **2e^{−D/128}** (Lemma 8.1).
- **Regime and constants.** The theorem holds for **0 < ε ≤ c₀·min{σ, √(ΔL)}** with c₀ = 2⁻²⁰ and overall prefactor **c = 1/(2560·10⁴·2³⁹) ≈ 10⁻¹⁶**; the construction dimension is d = T·D (Eq. 85).

No empirical experiments are reported (none are expected for a pure lower-bound paper).

The result sits squarely inside the well-established Carmon-Duchi-Hinder-Sidford / Arjevani-et-al. minimax program and correctly cites the relevant upper bounds (Ghadimi-Lan SGD, 2013) and lower bounds (Arjevani et al. 2023 for bounded variance; Fradin et al. 2026 for heavy tails). The construction is internally detailed (every constant is tracked across nine sections), and the core mechanism — biased-sign encoding in place of rare reveals — is a clean, verifiable idea rather than a black-box claim, which makes the theorem independently checkable despite its AI-generated origin.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely new mechanism. The bounded-variance lower bound of Arjevani et al. [1] relies on a rare Bernoulli reveal whose amplitude scales as 1/ρ, which *inherently* violates any uniform bound; the paper replaces this with a hidden-sign-vector encoding whose per-response KL is O(Dε²/σ²) while every realization stays bounded at σ/2. This biased-sign-vs-rare-reveal substitution is the crux that makes the extension to bounded noise possible.
- **Rigor:** The argument is laid out as an explicit five-step roadmap with constants pinned down (H=1024, K=2²⁴, the 119/128 probability in Proposition 8.9, the 1/128 bound on event J). Extending the response laws to *every* (p,x) pair (Definition 8.2) so the conditional KL chain rule applies to adaptively generated queries is a careful, non-trivial step that prior rare-reveal proofs did not need.
- **Scope limitations (the main weaknesses):** (a) The constructed oracle g(·,z) is **generally discontinuous in x** and is **not the gradient of any sample function** f(x,z) — Remark 3.2 states this explicitly. Therefore the theorem does **not** settle the samplewise-smooth, finite-sum, or conservative-oracle variants, which are the models most relevant to modern ML. (b) Only the K=1 (one query per fresh sample) model is covered; the K≥2 shared-sample regime that lets SPIDER reach ε⁻³ is explicitly out of scope. (c) The constants are astronomically loose — prefactor c ≈ 10⁻¹⁶ and ε must satisfy ε ≤ 2⁻²⁰·min{σ,√(ΔL)} — so this is an asymptotic-rate statement, not a practical finite-precision bound.
- **Reproducibility:** The construction is fully specified and the random signs are even fixed to a deterministic worst-case θ* in Section 9, so a reader can in principle re-derive it; however there is no code, the proof is long and machine-generated, and the discontinuous-oracle measurability claims (Lemma 7.1) place the verification burden entirely on the reader.
- **Significance:** Conceptually important for practice — it tells algorithm designers that switching from a bounded-variance to a bounded-noise assumption (e.g., gradient clipping or quantization that caps per-sample error) yields **no** worst-case SGD speedup, and that faster rates require *structural* assumptions (oracle smoothness, shared samples, finite-sum structure) rather than a tighter noise bound.
- **Red flags:** (1) The proof was AI-generated in two hours with the human author's role limited to "checking and polishing" — readers should independently verify the constant bookkeeping (Section 9) and the measurability/discontinuity handling rather than trust the headline. (2) The discontinuous, non-sample-function oracle weakens the connection to real stochastic optimization where g(x,z)=∇f(x,z). (3) No peer-review status is indicated; it appears to be a fresh arXiv preprint.

## Strengths

- Settles a precisely stated open problem from a leading minimax-optimization paper with a clean, rate-matching (tight) answer.
- The biased-sign encoding is an elegant, reusable technique: it cleanly decouples "uniform boundedness of the noise" from "information leakage about the hidden coordinate," a separation likely useful for other oracle-model lower bounds (heavy-tail, finite-sum, distributed).
- Fully tracks constants and provides an explicit dimension formula (Eqs. 85–86), which is uncommon for minimax lower-bound proofs.
- Honest disclosure: openly states the AI generation of the proof and the precise scope boundary (discontinuous oracle, K=1 only), rather than overclaiming applicability.

## Limitations

- Does **not** cover samplewise-smooth, finite-sum, or conservative oracles — the hard oracle is discontinuous and not a sample gradient (Remark 3.2).
- Only the K=1 fresh-sample model; the shared-sample (K≥2) variance-reduced regime exploited by SPIDER for an ε⁻³ rate is untouched.
- Constants are extreme (c ≈ 10⁻¹⁶, ε ≤ 2⁻²⁰·min{σ,√(ΔL)}); the result is purely asymptotic.
- Single author, AI-generated proof, no code, no peer review yet — the full verification burden falls on the reader.
- No empirical validation (expected for theory, but it means practical tightness of the rate for realistic finite-sum problems is unaddressed).

## Relevance

这篇论文属于非凸随机优化的极小化极大（minimax）复杂度理论，是理解 SGD 类方法在最坏情况下为何无法突破 ε⁻⁴ 率的基础性结果。对于关心随机梯度方法、噪声建模（有界噪声 / 梯度裁剪 / 量化）以及优化下界理论的研究者具有直接参考价值；但它不提供任何新算法或经验性基准测试，对追求可用工程方法的工作价值有限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the lower bound be extended to **samplewise-smooth oracles** (g(x,z)=∇f(x,z) with f smooth in x) — the model most ML applications actually satisfy — or does the achievable rate improve there?
- Does the biased-sign encoding technique transfer to the **K≥2 shared-sample model** to recover an SPIDER-style ε⁻³ lower bound under bounded noise, or to the heavy-tailed (bounded p-th moment) setting of Fradin et al.?
- Is there a **matching algorithm** (beyond the generic SGD upper bound) that is minimax-optimal under the bounded-noise oracle with constants closer to the lower bound's?
- How sensitive is the construction to the discontinuity of g(·,z)? Would requiring joint continuity or conservativity of the oracle change the rate?
- Given the proof is machine-generated, which steps — the constant bookkeeping in Section 9, the measurability argument in Lemma 7.1, the union bound in Lemma 8.6 — are most fragile and would most benefit from formal computer-assisted verification?
