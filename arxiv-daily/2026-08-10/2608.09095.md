> **一句话总结：** 该文把多语言安全的机制分析从"孤立安全神经元"推进到"跨层安全通路"，通过共激活（phi 系数差分）与激活传播（因果干预）两种视图构造语言级安全通路，发现高资源语言（EN）与非高资源语言之间存在稀疏的**共享安全通路**——屏蔽它可让 Qwen3-8B 在 AdvBench-x 上的攻击成功率从 32.54% 飙到 91.67%（等量随机通路仅 −0.21）——并据此只微调 0.46%–0.71% 参数，就把 Llama-3.1-8B-it 在 AdvBench-x 上的平均 ASR 从 24.39% 降到 1.00%（最强基线 SS-Neuron 为 1.81%），同时 MGSM/MMMLU 基本无损。

> **TL;DR:** This paper argues that multilingual safety is carried by sparse *cross-layer pathways* rather than isolated safety neurons, and that HR (English) and non-high-resource languages share a thin intersection of those pathways that acts as the transfer bridge. Masking that shared intersection raises Qwen3-8B's AdvBench-x attack success rate from 32.54% to 91.67% (+59.13) while a size-matched random pathway moves it by −0.21, establishing causality. Fine-tuning only the HR-pathway parameters (0.46%–0.71% of weights) on a translated HR→NHR safety corpus gives the lowest average ASR on both AdvBench-x and MultiJail across Gemma-2-9B-it and Llama-3.1-8B-it — e.g. Llama AdvBench-x avg ASR 24.39% → 1.00% vs 1.81% for the strongest baseline SS-Neuron and 3.11% for MPO — while MGSM moves 40.67→40.93 and MMMLU 51.85→51.40 (LoRA loses 7.7/3.1 pts on the same axes). Worth reading if you care about mechanistic interpretability of safety or parameter-efficient multilingual alignment; the margin over the closest neuron-level baseline is under 1 ASR point, and the GPT-4o judge agrees with Llama-Guard-3 only 65–67% on Swahili/Nepali/Bengali — exactly the languages carrying the headline gains.

## 核心贡献

- **从"点"到"路"的机制视角转变。** 已有跨语言安全可解释性工作（如 SS-Neuron, Zhang et al. 2026）把安全归因到孤立神经元；本文认为安全信号是在层间传播中形成"功能通路"的，并给出可操作的通路构造方法：共激活通路 $P^A_\lambda$（相邻层安全神经元的 phi 系数在有害/良性语境下的差分 $\Delta\phi$ + 置换检验）与激活传播通路 $P^B_\lambda$（把源神经元激活替换为良性均值，测量下一层安全神经元的激活变化，并对随机非安全神经元干预分布做标准化），最终 $P_\lambda = P^A_\lambda \cup P^B_\lambda$。
- **发现"跨语言共享安全通路"这一内部桥梁。** 定义 $P^\star_\lambda = P_\lambda \cap P_{\text{HR}}$，虽然只占单语通路的一小部分，但屏蔽它造成的安全崩塌远超等量随机通路；且与 HR 共享通路越多的语言，其原生安全性越强、被屏蔽后退化越剧烈。
- **通路定向的跨语言对齐方法。** 用 HR 语言作为安全锚点，把已验证的有害查询与安全回复翻译成目标 NHR 语言构成 $D_{\text{train}}$，然后用二值参数掩码 $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta(m\odot\nabla_\theta L)$ 只更新 HR 安全通路对应的 FFN 参数，冻结注意力头、embedding 与非通路单元。
- **一阶理论分析 + 经验验证。** 附录 A 用一阶泰勒展开证明 NHR 安全损失的下降量集中在共享通路上，取决于 HR/NHR 安全梯度在 $P^\star$ 上的内积；图 7 用 Cohen's d 构造"安全方向向量"，验证所有 NHR 语言在共享通路上与 EN 的余弦相似度显著高于随机通路。
- **零样本跨语言迁移证据。** 留一语言实验（Gemma-2-9B-it）表明，即使训练数据中完全不含目标语言，AdvBench-x 平均 ASR 仍从 3.66% 降到 0.66%。

## Key Contributions

- **Pathway-level rather than neuron-level localization of safety.** Safety neurons are first isolated by contrastive gradient-activation attribution (top-3% per layer under unsafe context minus top-3% under benign context, on 802 jailbreak queries vs 1,000 Alpaca-style benign queries per language), then linked across adjacent layers by two complementary criteria: (i) co-activation, retained only if joint activation rate $p^-_{\ell ij}\ge s_{\min}$, phi coefficient $\phi^-\ge\phi_{\min}$, permutation-test $q<\alpha$, and safety-specific difference $\Delta\phi=\phi^--\phi^+\ge\rho$; (ii) activation-propagation, a causal mean-ablation intervention standardized against a reference distribution built from interventions on random non-safety neurons.
- **Identification and causal validation of a cross-lingual shared safety core** $P^\star_\lambda=P_\lambda\cap P_{\text{HR}}$, with the size-matched-random-pathway control as the key falsification test in both Table 1 (monolingual) and Table 2 (shared).
- **A pathways-targeted alignment recipe** that updates 0.46%–0.71% of parameters and beats both Full FT (100% params) and LoRA (1.05%–1.18% params) on MultiJail across all three backbones, supporting the paper's claim that *where* you optimize matters more than *how many* parameters you touch.
- **A theoretical account (Appendix A)** deriving $\Delta L_{\text{NHR}}\approx-\eta(\langle\nabla_{P^\star}L_{\text{NHR}},\nabla_{P^\star}L_{\text{HR}}\rangle+\alpha\|\nabla_{P^\star}L_{\text{NHR}}\|^2)$, which correctly predicts that pathway overlap alone is insufficient — gradient agreement on the shared subspace is required.
- **A control experiment separating "pathway" from "important neurons" (Appendix G):** importance-matched neurons that do *not* participate in pathway connections, when masked, raise Qwen3-8B AdvBench-x ASR by only 9.3% and MultiJail by ~0, vs +61.1% / +36.4% for the pathway neurons.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Three instruction-tuned backbones (Gemma-2-9B-it, Llama-3.1-8B-it, Qwen3-8B), one HR language (EN) and nine NHR languages (ZH, KO, BN, TH, SW, HU, AF, IT, NE). A "neuron" is a channel of the FFN intermediate activation feeding the down-projection (standard Geva et al. 2021 convention). Safety is measured by attack success rate (ASR) on AdvBench-x and MultiJail with GPT-4o as automated judge.

**Step 1 — safety neurons.** For each language $\lambda$, response-level gradient×activation attribution is aggregated over response tokens to give unsafe importance $I^-_\lambda(\ell,i)$ and benign importance $I^+_\lambda(\ell,i)$. The safety neuron set is the contrastive subtraction $S_\lambda=\bigcup_\ell (T^-_{\lambda,\ell}\setminus T^+_{\lambda,\ell})$ with per-layer top-3%.

**Step 2 — cross-layer pathways.** Two views, unioned. Co-activation binarizes each neuron's mean response-token activation and keeps adjacent-layer edges whose unsafe-context phi coefficient is both statistically significant (permutation test, $\alpha=0.05$) and *unsafe-specific* ($\Delta\phi\ge\rho=1.5$). Activation-propagation performs a causal intervention — replace the source neuron's activation with its benign mean on a subset of unsafe samples, measure the induced change in each next-layer safety neuron, z-standardize against interventions on random non-safety neurons ($z_{\min}=2.0$), and normalize by the target neuron's mean unsafe activation ($r_{\min}=0.05$).

**Step 3 — shared pathways.** Intersect NHR and HR pathway edge sets. Validation is by mean-value masking with a size-matched random-pathway control.

**Step 4 — alignment.** Translate verified HR harmful queries and their correct safe answers into each NHR language to build a semantically aligned $D_{\text{train}}=\{(x_{\text{HR}},y_{\text{HR}}),(x_{\text{NHR}},y_{\text{NHR}})\}$; train with NLL on safe responses for 3 epochs, effective batch 16, lr 2e-5, Adafactor, max length 512, gradient masked to the HR safety-pathway parameters. Notably the update targets the *full* $P_{\text{HR}}$, not only $P^\star_\lambda$ — the authors justify this in Appendix G on the grounds that $P^\star$ is too sparse to encode the complete HR refusal mechanism.

**Baselines.** SFT, DPO, IPO, rDPO, CPO, KTO, ORPO, R-DPO, SimPO, MPO (multilingual safety alignment), and SS-Neuron (the shared-safety-*neuron* predecessor, explicitly named as the strongest baseline). Efficiency comparisons add Full FT, LoRA (r=32, α=64, all linear), and R-P FT (random size-matched pathways).

## Results & Highlights

- **Causal validation of pathways (Table 1).** On Qwen3-8B, mean-value masking the identified safety pathways raises AdvBench-x ASR from 32.54% to 82.71% (+50.17), whereas a size-matched *random* pathway mask moves it only to 32.67% (+0.13). On MultiJail the same contrast is 43.87% → 77.36% (+33.49) vs +1.21. Llama-3.1-8B-it shows +28.88 (pathway) vs +2.84 (random) on AdvBench-x. This is the cleanest evidence in the paper.
- **Shared-pathway masking is disproportionately damaging (Table 2).** Masking only $P^\star_\lambda$ pushes Qwen3-8B AdvBench-x ASR to 91.67% (+59.13) and MultiJail to 79.02% (+35.15), while the random control gives −0.21 and +0.19 respectively — i.e. a strict subset of the monolingual pathway does *more* damage (+59.13) than the full pathway did (+50.17), which the authors read as evidence for a genuine cross-lingual bridge rather than a coincidental intersection.
- **Main safety results (Table 3, 5 languages EN/ZH/KO/BN/SW).** On Gemma-2-9B-it the method reaches **0.19% average AdvBench-x ASR vs 1.00% for SS-Neuron (−0.81 pts), 2.19% for MPO and 3.80% for the untouched model**; on MultiJail, 2.29% vs 3.30% (SS-Neuron), 7.36% (MPO), 12.32% (default). On Llama-3.1-8B-it: AdvBench-x 1.00% vs SS-Neuron 1.81% and MPO 3.11% (default 24.39%); MultiJail 4.58% vs SS-Neuron 5.27% and MPO 6.22% (default 34.92%). So the gain over the *strongest* baseline is 0.69–1.01 absolute points, while the gain over preference-optimization baselines is large.
- **Biggest wins are in the weakest languages.** Llama-3.1-8B-it Swahili AdvBench-x ASR falls 48.56% → 1.35% and Bengali 40.12% → 2.12%; on MultiJail the same pair falls 37.78% → 5.71% and 49.52% → 10.79%. Note the residual 10.79% on Bengali/MultiJail — the method does not fully close the gap on the hardest language-benchmark cell.
- **Parameter efficiency (Table 5, 12 languages).** Updating 0.46%–0.71% of parameters, the method gets Qwen3-8B MultiJail ASR to 8.29% vs 24.90% (LoRA, 1.05% params), 24.48% (Full FT, 100% params), 26.94% (random-pathway FT, 0.59% params) and 28.11% default — a 16.6-point margin over LoRA that the random-pathway control attributes to *pathway selection*, not sparsity. Caveat: on Llama-3.1-8B-it AdvBench-x, LoRA actually edges the method out (0.69% vs 0.88%).
- **Capability preservation (Figure 5).** Llama-3.1-8B-it: MGSM 40.67% → 40.93%, MMMLU 51.85% → 51.40%; Qwen3-8B: MGSM 34.13% → 35.33% (+1.20) and MMMLU 58.85% → 59.25% (+0.40). LoRA on Llama loses 7.7 MGSM and 3.1 MMMLU points; Full FT is worse still.
- **Ablation (Table 4).** On Qwen3-8B, combined pathways give 1.82% / 8.29% (AdvBench-x / MultiJail) with 5,038 pathways, vs 5.45% / 23.88% for activation-propagation only (1,812), 6.05% / 23.49% for co-activation only (4,773), and 5.51% / 23.67% for tuning the full 7,050-neuron safety-neuron set — i.e. *more* trainable units but ~3x worse ASR, the paper's central argument for pathway structure over neuron sets.
- **Leave-one-language-out zero-shot transfer (Table 6, Gemma-2-9B-it).** Holding out BN/KO/AF/HU/NE from training entirely: average AdvBench-x ASR 3.66% → 0.66% and MultiJail 10.10% → 3.87%, with per-language MultiJail drops such as KO 10.16 → 1.90 and NE 13.65 → 2.86.
- **Data-composition ablation (Table 11).** HR-only training is much weaker on Qwen3-8B: 5.88% / 23.39% vs 1.82% / 8.29% for HR+NHR — meaning the pathway mask alone does not accomplish transfer; translated NHR supervision is doing substantial work.
- **Over-refusal (Table 12).** XSTest refusal rates: Gemma 0.80 → 0.40, Llama 6.40 → 4.00, but **Qwen3-8B 1.60 → 5.60 (3.5x increase)** — described in text as "comparable to the base model," which understates the Qwen result.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-high).** The pathway construction is a genuine methodological step past neuron-level localization, and the Appendix G control — importance-matched non-pathway neurons producing +9.3% ASR vs +61.1% for pathway neurons on Qwen3-8B AdvBench-x — is the specific experiment that distinguishes "pathway" from "just better neuron selection." That control is what makes the contribution credible rather than cosmetic. However, conceptually this is one delta away from SS-Neuron: same problem, same evaluation, same intersection-as-bridge story, with edges added.

**Rigor (mixed).** Strong points: the size-matched random-pathway control appears in every causal claim (Tables 1, 2, 5), the hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (Figure 8) honestly reports that the safety-specificity threshold $\rho$ *does* change the pathway (Jaccard 0.60 at $\rho{=}1.75$, 0.41 at $\rho{=}2.0$), and Appendix A's theory makes a falsifiable prediction that Figure 7 tests. Weak points:

1. **Inconsistent "Default" numbers across tables.** Qwen3-8B default AdvBench-x ASR is 32.54% (Table 1), 22.79% (Table 4) and 14.42% (Table 5); Llama-3.1-8B-it MultiJail default is 43.93% (Table 1), 34.92% (Table 3) and 31.91% (Table 5). Table 10 attributes this to different language sets per experiment (5 / 10 / 12 languages), which is a legitimate explanation but makes cross-table reasoning impossible and gives the authors latitude in which language mix supports which claim.
2. **Qwen3-8B is missing from the main comparison table.** Table 3 — the only table with the full baseline suite including SS-Neuron and MPO — covers only Gemma-2-9B-it and Llama-3.1-8B-it. Qwen3-8B appears in ablations and efficiency tables where the competitive baselines (SS-Neuron, MPO, DPO family) are absent. Since Qwen3 is where the method's pathway-masking effects are largest and where the over-refusal regression shows up, its omission from the head-to-head is the single most conspicuous gap.
3. **Judge reliability is weakest exactly where the gains are largest.** Appendix G reports GPT-4o vs Llama-Guard-3 agreement of 97% (EN), 94% (IT), 92% (ZH), but 65% (SW), 66% (NE), 67% (BN), 74% (KO) — average 80.3%. The headline improvements (Swahili 48.56 → 1.35, Bengali 40.12 → 2.12) live in the 65–67% agreement band. The authors also claim human agreement "exceeded 86%" across ten languages but give no per-language table or annotator details for that number, while the machine-agreement number they *do* tabulate is 80.3%. ASR deltas of 0.7–1.0 points against SS-Neuron are well inside this measurement noise.
4. **No variance reporting.** No seeds, no confidence intervals, no repeated runs anywhere — for sub-1-point margins against the strongest baseline this matters a lot.
5. **Missing ablation:** the paper never reports updating *only* $P^\star_\lambda$ (the shared bridge) instead of the full $P_{\text{HR}}$. Appendix G answers the question rhetorically ("$P^\star$ is too sparse") but provides no number, which is odd given that the shared pathway is the paper's titular finding. Similarly, MPO and SS-Neuron are not evaluated under the leave-one-language-out protocol, so the strongest generalization claim (Table 6) has no baseline at all.

**Reproducibility (medium).** Hyperparameters for training, pathway identification, and both FFT/LoRA baselines are fully tabulated (Tables 7–9), the probing datasets are public (Aegis2.0/JailbreakBench for unsafe, Alpaca for benign), and the benchmarks are standard. But no code or released pathway masks are mentioned, the translation model/pipeline used to build $D_{\text{train}}$ is unspecified, and "we evaluate checkpoints periodically and use the best-performing checkpoint" implies selection on the evaluation signal without a stated held-out split.

**Significance (medium-high).** If the pathway abstraction holds, "discover shared structure → reinforce it under a mask" is a cheap and general recipe for cross-lingual alignment, and the leave-one-out result (unseen-language ASR 3.66 → 0.66) is the most practically interesting number in the paper. The capability-preservation advantage over LoRA (+7.7 MGSM points on Llama) is also a real selling point for deployment.

**Red flags.** (a) The claim that over-refusal is "comparable to the base model" while Qwen3-8B's XSTest refusal rate more than triples (1.60 → 5.60). (b) "State-of-the-art NHR safety" framing when the delta over the in-group predecessor is <1 ASR point under an 80%-agreement judge. (c) Table 5 quietly shows LoRA beating the method on Llama/AdvBench-x (0.69 vs 0.88), unremarked in the text. (d) A block of self-citations to blockchain and image-generation papers with no topical relation to multilingual safety.

## Strengths

- The size-matched random-pathway control is applied consistently — to monolingual pathways, shared pathways, and to the fine-tuning procedure (R-P FT) — which rules out the most obvious confounds (sparsity, parameter count) rather than just asserting them away.
- The importance-matched non-pathway neuron control (Appendix G) directly answers the skeptical reading of the whole paper ("is this just a good neuron ranking?") with a concrete number.
- Theory and experiment are actually coupled: Eq. (16) predicts that overlap is insufficient without gradient agreement, and Figure 7 measures that agreement via Cohen's-d safety-direction cosines, with the honest observation that Nepali — the lowest-coverage language — shows the weakest alignment and thus depends more on direct NHR supervision.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity is reported with a genuinely negative-ish finding ($\rho$ substantially reshapes the pathway set) rather than a uniformly flat sweep.
- Utility preservation is checked on two axes (MGSM, MMMLU) *and* on over-refusal (XSTest), which many safety-alignment papers skip; the qualitative Afrikaans arithmetic/knowledge cases in Appendix F are a reasonable sanity check.
- The leave-one-language-out protocol is a strong generalization test and it is reported at the language level, not just averaged.

## Limitations

- Self-acknowledged (Appendix H): the "discover shared pathway → reinforce" strategy is validated only on refusal-style safety behaviour, which may be unusually sparse and localizable; reasoning, retrieval, and creative-writing capabilities likely rely on denser, less separable pathways.
- Pathways are restricted to FFN down-projection input channels and to *adjacent-layer* edges only — attention heads, residual-stream routing, and skip-layer dependencies are excluded by construction, so "pathway" here means a chain of neighboring-layer FFN neurons, not a full circuit.
- Only one HR anchor (English) is tested; whether a different anchor (e.g. Chinese for ZH-adjacent languages) yields different bridges is unexplored.
- The training corpus is machine-translated from HR data, so both the harmful queries and the safe responses inherit translation artifacts; the Table 11 result showing HR-only training fails suggests the NHR text distribution matters, which makes translation quality a live confound.
- Evaluation is limited to two jailbreak benchmarks (AdvBench-x, MultiJail) with a single primary judge; no adaptive/adversarial attacks, no multi-turn jailbreaks, and no test on the multilingual safety benchmarks the related-work section itself highlights (LinguaSafe, CultureGuard).
- Model scale caps at 9B; nothing about whether shared pathways remain sparse at 70B+ or in MoE architectures.

## Relevance

如果你（用户）关注 LLM 安全的机制可解释性、或者对"稀疏子网络 / 电路级定位 + 掩码微调"这类参数高效对齐范式感兴趣，这篇提供了一个可复用的模板：共激活 + 因果传播两视图构图、等量随机对照、以及重要性匹配对照来区分"通路"与"重要神经元"。对多语言 LLM 部署方而言，0.46%–0.71% 参数更新即可显著压低低资源语言越狱率且不损伤 MGSM/MMMLU，这个 tradeoff 比 LoRA 更值得一试——但要自行验证低资源语种上的 judge 可靠性。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What happens if you update **only** $P^\star_\lambda$ (the shared bridge) rather than all of $P_{\text{HR}}$? The paper's own narrative predicts this should be nearly as good and even cheaper, yet the number is never reported.
- Do the shared pathways survive an adaptive attacker who knows them? If the safety mechanism is this sparse, a white-box adversary could plausibly craft NHR prompts that route around $P^\star$ — the mean-ablation experiments essentially demonstrate the attack already works when you have weight access.
- Are the identified pathways language-family-structured or resource-level-structured? Figure 7 hints at resource level (Nepali weakest), but a controlled comparison (e.g. IT vs SW at matched pretraining token counts) would separate typology from data volume.
- How much of the 0.7–1.0 point margin over SS-Neuron survives (i) multiple seeds, (ii) Llama-Guard-3 as the sole judge, and (iii) human annotation on the low-agreement languages (SW/BN/NE)?
- Does the method compose across NHR languages, or does sequentially aligning many languages cause pathway interference/forgetting? All results train one multilingual corpus jointly; a continual setting is untested.
- Would the same construction identify "capability pathways" for MGSM-style reasoning, and does masking them degrade reasoning as cleanly as masking safety pathways degrades refusal? This is the direct test of whether pathway sparsity is special to safety.
- The over-refusal regression on Qwen3-8B (1.60 → 5.60 on XSTest) — is that a Qwen-specific artifact, a function of its higher default ASR, or the beginning of a safety/helpfulness frontier that pathway targeting cannot escape?
