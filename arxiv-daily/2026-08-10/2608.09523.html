Why this matters

A unified theory of why SGD trains deep nets — without infinite width, convexity, or differentiability — grounding skip connections and over-parameterization in measurable Jacobian spectra.

Critical edge

The algorithm is never run on a neural network. Every DNN experiment uses classical SGD/Adam and merely logs correlations — leaving the α = 1 headline with zero evidence at network scale.

01

Methodology

distilled from ~50 pp. + appendix

The framework replaces the quadratic energy ‖·‖²₂ in strong-convexity / Lipschitz-smoothness with arbitrary Legendre "energy functions" Ψ, ψ. Bounding the first-order Taylor remainder above by Ψ and below by ψ defines H(Ψ)-smoothness and H(ψ)-convexity — the constants Ψ(0), ψ(0) act as continuous knobs that admit non-convex and non-smooth objectives inside a single form.

Conjugate duality (Prop 3.2) makes generalized smoothness the exact dual of generalized convexity. Generalized GD steps θ ← θ − ∇Ψ*(∇G) with a provably optimal α = 1; classical GD is recovered as the special case Ψ = L/2‖·‖²₂.

For DNNs, ERM is written as a composite G = F ∘ h_θ, pushing the bounds through the Jacobian: risk is sandwiched by gradient energy over extreme singular values σ_min/max(J_θ f) — measurable quantities, with no Hessian, no Lipschitz constant, no infinite-width limit.

Two architecture lemmas explain trainability: random over-parameterization narrows the Jacobian spectral gap (Prop 5.1); skip connections convert depth-wise exponential decay of σ into growth, ∏(∇h + I) ≈ I (Prop 5.2).

03

Strengths & Limitations

side by side
+ Strengths
  • Clean duality. Ψ(0)/ψ(0) make convexity & smoothness continuous, not binary properties.
  • Sanity discipline. Every generalization recovers textbook GD/SGD under Ψ = L/2‖·‖²₂.
  • Real breadth along nuisance axes: 2 modalities, 6 datasets, 8+ architectures, 6 losses.
  • False-label control is a well-designed non-triviality test many theory papers omit.
  • Honest that optimal Ψ is likely NP-hard and gradient energy need not hit zero.
  • Falsifiable depth/width Jacobian predictions — and they hold on two datasets.
— Limitations
  • Algorithm untested at scale. Generalized GD runs only on 100-dim ‖x‖ʳ₂ toys — never a network.
  • Instantiation is NP-hard. (ψ, Ψ) for a real DNN can't be computed; framework predicts qualitatively only.
  • No mirror-descent link. The dual-space update is never related to Bregman / natural-gradient literature.
  • Weak metric. r → 1 is near-tautological for MSE; the drop-the-Jacobian ablation is missing.
  • No accuracy, no error bars, no seeds, no code release.
  • Batch-size call (large → small) contradicts common large-batch practice; "deeper is better" ignores exploding Jacobians.
  • Generalization out of scope — listed only as future work.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

what would actually test the theory
  1. Drop the Jacobian term. What is r between log risk and log‖∇θL‖²₂ alone? If still ≈ 1, the composite half of the theory carries no empirical weight.
  2. Formalize the mirror-descent link. Does generalized GD reduce to a known Bregman-divergence variant, and do its rates recover Theorem 4.1?
  3. Cheap ∇Ψ* surrogate? From gradient-norm history — could generalized SGD actually run on a ResNet and test α = 1 without tuning?
  4. Test it on MoE. Does "lower coupling ⇒ lower attainable gradient energy" hold for sparsely-activated models — a falsifiable prediction the paper never runs?
  5. Exploding-Jacobian optimum? At what residual depth does growing σ(J) tip from trainability benefit into pathology?
  6. Estimate M / R_G(s,m)? From held-out-batch loss changes — turning placeholders into live capacity diagnostics.
