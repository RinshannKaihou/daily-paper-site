> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 ICBQ（交错式跨块量化）——把逐块 PTQ 的深度轴切成大小为 K 的 chunk，在每个 chunk 结束后插入一次短的两块 CBQ 精修，使 chunk 边界（seam）上的相邻块对被优化两次；在三值 DBF（1.58-bit）设置下，七个模型的 C4 困惑度全部优于匹配的 Sequential CBQ 基线，其中 Qwen3-8B 从 5752.9 降到 29.90、Mistral-7B 从 31.1 降到 16.8，代价仅为平均 1.21× 的墙钟时间和一个与深度无关的常数显存缓冲（7B 上约 4.3 GB）。

> **TL;DR:** ICBQ is a *scheduling* change to block-wise PTQ, not a new objective or codebook: quantize blocks progressively in chunks of size K, and after each chunk run a short cross-block (two-block) refinement sweep whose window overlaps the previous chunk, so every chunk seam pair gets refined twice. Against a matched Sequential CBQ baseline (a fixed two-block weight-only adaptation of CBQ, Ding et al. ICLR'25) with a ternary DBF inner quantizer, ICBQ lowers C4 perplexity on all 7 models — Qwen3-8B 5752.9 → 29.90, Mistral-7B 31.1 → 16.8, Llama-3-8B 48.98 → 42.32, Llama-2-7B 13.6 → 12.5 — and lifts average zero-shot accuracy on 6 of 7 (Qwen3-8B 0.4032 → 0.5513). With GPTQ as inner quantizer it is best-or-tied on 5/7 models at both W3 and W2g128 (e.g. Llama-3-8B W2g128 18.51 → 17.37 vs Seq., 60.65 GPTQ-only). Cost: 1.21× mean wall-clock and an L/K-independent activation buffer (~4.3 GB at 7B). Theory contributes a depth-wise upper-bound comparison giving ICBQ an extra γ^S(L,K) contraction, S(L,K) = ⌈L/K⌉ − 1.

## 核心贡献

- **把"何时优化"而非"优化什么"作为设计维度。** 现有块级 PTQ（BRECQ、OmniQuant、CBQ、QEP、LPCD）改进的是重建单元、目标函数或权重码本；ICBQ 保持两块 CBQ 目标和内层量化器完全不变，只改调度顺序，因此可与任意兼容的内层量化器组合。
- **Seam（缝）机制。** 将深度轴切为大小 K 的 chunk，chunk c 的最后一对 (cK−1, cK) 恰好是 chunk c+1 的第一对，于是每条 seam 被精修两次，非 seam 对只精修一次；seam 数量 S(L,K) = ⌈L/K⌉ − 1。
- **Activation re-roll（激活重卷）。** 由于精修被交错插入到量化过程中间，缓存激活会失效；ICBQ 从保存的第 0 层输入重算 student/teacher 激活流，保证每步看到一致状态。额外显存只有一个常数大小的 depth-0 激活缓冲，与 L、K 无关。
- **Seam 摊销误差收缩定理（Thm 4.1）。** 在局部收缩与光滑性假设下，深度 L 的激活失配上界满足 E‖e_L^int‖ ≤ γ^{S(L,K)} B_L^seq + C(L,K,ρ,γ)ε_sub，即每条 seam 贡献一个额外的乘性收缩因子；推论 4.2 给出摊销增益随 seam 数几何增长。
- **跨内层量化器与跨调度超参的实证验证。** 在三值 DBF 与 GPTQ（W3 / W2g128）两类内层量化器、K ∈ {2,4,8,L} 的 chunk 尺寸消融、以及有/无 prefit 两种设置下均给出结果。

## Key Contributions

- **A schedule-level axis for block-wise PTQ.** ICBQ leaves the two-block CBQ objective and the inner quantizer untouched and changes only *when* refinement windows are applied, making it composable with GPTQ, ternary DBF, and in principle other block-local quantizers.
- **The seam construction.** Partitioning depth into chunks of K blocks and running an interleaved CBQ sweep at each chunk close makes the boundary pair (cK−1, cK) the last pair of one chunk and the first pair of the next, so it is refined twice; interior pairs are refined once.
- **Activation re-roll** recomputes student and teacher streams from stored layer-0 inputs so interleaved refinement never operates on stale caches; peak memory grows by Δmem = N·T·d_hidden·bytes(dtype), an L- and K-independent constant (~4.3 GB at N=256, T=2048, d_hidden=4096, fp16; ~1.0 GB for TinyLlama-1.1B, ~5.2 GB at 13B).
- **Theorem 4.1 (seam-amortized error contraction)** formalizes the intuition as an upper-bound comparison, and **Corollary 4.2** shows the ICBQ-over-Sequential bound ratio is exponential in the number of seams when additive noise is not dominant.
- **Empirical validation across 7 base models (3B–14B), two inner quantizers, chunk-size and prefit ablations**, plus wall-clock and peak-memory accounting on 11 models.

## Methodology

The setting is weight-only PTQ of an L-block Transformer with fp16 activations, no rotation and no activation quantizer. An *inner quantizer* Q (ternary DBF at 1.58-bit with an optional 50-step float32 AdamW "prefit"; or GPTQ at W3 per-channel / W2g128) converts one fp16 block into a low-bit student block. On top of that, a fixed two-block CBQ-style objective refines adjacent pairs: minimize ‖f^{θ_{i+1}}_{i+1}(f^{θ_i}_i(X̂_i)) − Y*_{i+2}‖²_F over the calibration set, where Y*_{i+2} is the fp16 teacher output two blocks ahead, with a rollback safeguard that restores parameters if MSE does not decrease.

The **Sequential CBQ baseline** quantizes all blocks first, then sweeps windows (0,1), (1,2), …, (L−2, L−1) once left-to-right. Errors introduced at a pair are propagated forward and never revisited.

**ICBQ** replaces this with an outer driver that sweeps b = 0…L−1, applies the usual per-block quantization (with optional prefit), and keeps a chunk-start pointer b_cs. When b+1 − b_cs ≥ K (or the model ends), it launches an interleaved CBQ call over the window range [max(0, b_cs − 1), min(b+1, L−1)) — note the −1, which is what pulls the previous chunk's last pair back into scope. The right block of a boundary pair may still be unquantized at that moment and is handled as a provisional copy until its own quantization step. After the refinement, activations are re-rolled from stored layer-0 inputs and the driver advances. Chunk size K is the single control knob: smaller K ⇒ more seams ⇒ more revisits.

The theory tracks the boundary mismatch m_i = ‖X̂_{i+1} − X*_{i+1}‖_F. Non-seam pairs satisfy a damp-and-inject recurrence m_i^out ≤ τ m_{i−1}^out + (1+γ)ε_sub with τ = γρ < 1; seams get an extra cleanup pass, yielding m_i^out ≤ γτ m_{i−1}^out + (1+γ+γ²)ε_sub. Chaining gives Theorem 4.1. The authors are explicit that this is a bound-vs-bound comparison, not a guarantee about realized perplexity (Remark B.7), and report a measured aggregate γ̄ ≈ 0.67 on Llama-2-7B (implying γ^{−7} ≈ 16× at L=32, K=4) versus a conservative γ = 0.92 giving ≈ 1.8×, bracketing the empirical 1.08–14.5× range.

## Results & Highlights

- **Ternary DBF (1.58-bit), C4 PPL, Table 1 — ICBQ wins on all 7 models.** Qwen3-8B: 5752.9 (Seq. CBQ) → **29.90** (ICBQ), with WikiText-2 PPL going from >1e4 → 25.86; the no-refinement DBF-only baseline is also >1e4. Mistral-7B: 31.1 → **16.8** (no-refinement 143.22; fp16 reference 7.99). Llama-3-8B: 48.98 → **42.32**. Qwen3-14B: 44.82 → **24.67** (−45%). Llama-2-7B: 13.6 → **12.5**; Llama-2-13B: 10.48 → **10.09**; Llama-3.2-3B: 40.63 → **37.01**.
- **Zero-shot accuracy (7-task average) improves on 6/7.** Qwen3-8B 0.4032 → **0.5513** (+14.8 pts), Mistral-7B 0.4586 → **0.5404** (+8.2 pts), Llama-3-8B 0.5026 → **0.5215**, Llama-2-7B 0.5330 → **0.5533**. Qwen3-14B ties at 0.6871; Llama-2-13B is the single loss, where Seq. CBQ beats ICBQ by 0.0002 (0.6503 vs 0.6501) — a difference the authors correctly decline to spin.
- **GPTQ transfer is real but noticeably weaker (Table 2).** At W2g128 ICBQ leads on Llama-3-8B (17.37 vs 18.51 Seq. and 60.65 GPTQ-only) and Llama-3.2-3B (22.37 vs 24.03 / 58.16), but **loses** to Sequential CBQ on Mistral-7B (9.76 vs 9.74) and Llama-2-13B (8.76 vs 8.64). At W3, ICBQ loses to Seq. CBQ on Mistral-7B (8.86 vs 8.77) and, more awkwardly, **both refinement schedules lose to plain GPTQ-only on Qwen3-14B** (13.63 / 13.80 vs 13.54). Best-or-tied on 5/7 at each precision, so the headline "transfers" claim is defensible but the margins are ~1–3% not ~50%.
- **Chunk-size ablation (Table 3) largely confirms the theory's monotonicity prediction — with one exception.** Mistral-7B C4 PPL: K=L (Seq.) 31.1 → K=8 17.33 → K=4 16.8 → K=2 **14.85**. Qwen3-8B: 5752.9 → 34.01 → 29.90 → 29.80. But Qwen3-14B is non-monotone: 44.82 → 29.02 → **24.67** (K=4) → 25.89 (K=2), i.e. more seams hurt, which Theorem 4.1 alone does not predict.
- **Prefit ablation (Table 4) is the most informative table and partly undercuts the headline.** Without prefit (T_pre = 0), Sequential CBQ on Qwen3-8B scores 47.28 — not 5752.9. So the dramatic 190× improvement in Table 1 is mostly a story about prefit + Sequential CBQ being catastrophically unstable on that model, and the honest no-prefit gap is 47.28 → 41.59 (−12%). Same pattern on Llama-3-8B (68.53 → 66.35, −3%) and Llama-2-13B (11.85 → 11.80, −0.4%). ICBQ still wins 7/7 without prefit, but the effect size shrinks by roughly an order of magnitude on the models that produced the flashiest numbers.
- **Cost accounting is honest (Table 5, 11 models, 1× H100-80GB).** Mean wall-clock ratio 1.21× (range 1.09×–1.34×); Qwen3-14B goes 15:00:02 → 19:38:56 (+279 min). Peak memory overhead is 4.3 GB at 7B, under 15% of the 32 GB ternary-DBF peak.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-good).** The insight — that block-wise PTQ literature has optimized *what* is reconstructed and ignored *when* — is genuinely underexplored, and the seam construction is a clean, almost embarrassingly simple realization of it (an off-by-one in the window's left endpoint). It is not deeply novel in the abstract: revisiting subproblems is standard in block-coordinate descent and multigrid V/W-cycles, which the authors acknowledge. The contribution is the specific instantiation plus the seam-counting bound.

**Rigor (mixed).** Strong points: matched-baseline design (same calibration set, same inner quantizer, same optimizer budget), the no-prefit ablation, the wall-clock table, and Remark B.7 explicitly flagging that Theorem 4.1 compares an *upper bound* against realized ICBQ error rather than realized sequential error — that is unusually candid for a paper leaning on a theorem. Weak points: (1) the Sequential CBQ baseline is the authors' own two-block adaptation of CBQ, not CBQ as published, so no external number is directly comparable — there is no head-to-head against QuIP#, AQLM, QTIP, or even OmniQuant/AWQ at matched bit-width, which makes it impossible to place ICBQ on the absolute sub-2-bit Pareto frontier. (2) Single seed, no variance bars anywhere, while several claimed wins are ≤1% (Llama-2-13B 10.48 → 10.09) and the single loss is 0.0002 — at that resolution the sign of the effect is not established. (3) The Qwen3-8B 5752.9 baseline is a diverged run, and quoting it in the abstract ("yields finite perplexity where the baseline has severe degradation") without immediately noting that the same baseline scores 47.28 without prefit is the paper's main overclaim; the reader must reach Table 4 on page 9 to learn this.

**Reproducibility (fair).** Hyperparameters are unusually complete (dbf.iters 200/400, cbq_epochs 20, cbq_lr 5e-5, GPTQ percdamp 0.01, block size 128, actorder off, prefit η=1e-4 for 50 steps, seed 0, K=4). But no code URL appears in the paper, and references to "the repository" and "conf/config.yaml" imply an unreleased internal codebase. There is also a real inconsistency: Section 5.1 and all main tables state C4 calibration, while Appendix C.3 "Calibration" says 128/256 samples "from WikiText-2" — one of these is wrong. Appendix D additionally cites "Table 6" for three different things (WikiText-2 main-set results, four small models, and 11-model zero-shot), so the supplementary tables are mislabeled.

**Significance (moderate).** For anyone already running CBQ-style progressive PTQ, this is close to free: +21% time, +4 GB, no objective change, and it removes catastrophic divergence in the sub-2-bit regime. That last property — turning a diverged run into a usable one — is worth more in practice than the average PPL delta. But the gains outside the ternary-DBF-with-prefit setting are small (GPTQ margins ~1–3%, two outright losses, one case where refinement is worse than doing nothing), so this reads as a useful engineering knob rather than a new state of the art.

**Red flags.** Abstract-level emphasis on the diverged Qwen3-8B configuration; no external SOTA comparison; Qwen3-14B W3 where GPTQ-only beats both schedules is reported but explained away as "the propagated term is less dominant," which is unfalsifiable post-hoc; the non-monotone K=2 result on Qwen3-14B contradicts the theory's prediction and gets one hedge word ("or near-monotonically") rather than analysis; no ablation isolating the re-roll mechanism from the seam revisit itself, so it is unclear how much of the gain comes from double-refining seams versus simply having fresher activations.

## Strengths

- The intervention is genuinely cheap and drop-in: no new objective, no new codebook, no extra learned parameters, measured 1.21× time and a depth-independent memory constant.
- The no-prefit ablation (Table 4) is the ablation that a defensive author would omit, and including it is to the authors' credit even though it deflates the headline.
- Chunk size K gives a single interpretable dial that the theory predicts and the experiments mostly confirm (6/7 models monotone or near-monotone).
- Explicit acknowledgment (Remark B.7, Section 4 closing) that the theorem is a bound-vs-bound statement, plus reported γ̄ diagnostics bracketing the empirical range, is more epistemically careful than typical "we prove our method is better" theory sections.
- Broad model coverage: 7 main models spanning 3B–14B, including grouped-query (Qwen3) and sliding-window attention, plus 11 models in the timing/zero-shot supplements. Appendix C.2 documenting WikiText-2 calibration bias on non-standard-attention models is a useful practical finding on its own.

## Limitations

- Still block-local; it reduces but does not eliminate the gap to true global end-to-end reconstruction, as the authors state.
- Language models only; no vision or multimodal evidence.
- Weight-only, fp16 activations, no rotation — so it is untested in combination with QuaRot/SpinQuant-style bases and W8A8-type pipelines, despite the related-work section suggesting compatibility.
- Fixed two-block window throughout; the interaction between window width and chunk size is never explored, even though both are schedule parameters and the paper's whole thesis is about schedules.
- No comparison to published sub-2-bit baselines outside the authors' pipeline, and no seeds/error bars for sub-1% deltas.
- The re-roll's added forward-pass compute is bundled into the 1.21× figure and never separated from the refinement compute, so it is unclear which half of the cost is essential.

## Relevance

如果你关注 LLM 极低比特（≤2-bit）部署、PTQ 误差传播理论、或者块级/子模块重建的调度设计，这篇值得读——它把"调度顺序"提升为与目标函数、码本并列的设计轴，并给出了一个可直接叠加到现有 CBQ/GPTQ 流水线上的低成本改动。若你主要关心 QAT、激活量化或已达 SOTA 的 4-bit 部署，参考价值有限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How much of the gain is the seam double-refinement versus the activation re-roll alone? An ablation with re-roll enabled but no seam overlap (window range starting at b_cs instead of b_cs − 1) would isolate this, and it is conspicuously absent.
2. Why is Qwen3-14B non-monotone in K (24.67 at K=4 vs 25.89 at K=2)? Does over-revisiting seams overfit the 256-sample calibration set, and would the effect disappear with more calibration data?
3. Would non-uniform chunking help? Errors are not uniformly distributed across depth; adaptively placing seams where measured m_i is largest is a natural extension the fixed-K schedule leaves on the table.
4. Does the schedule effect survive when composed with rotation-based preprocessing (QuaRot/SpinQuant), where the propagated term the theory targets is already suppressed? The GPTQ results hint that the benefit shrinks exactly when propagation is not dominant.
5. How does ICBQ + ternary DBF compare against published sub-2-bit methods (AQLM, QuIP#, QTIP, LittleBit) at matched bits-per-weight and matched calibration budget?
6. Is the 1.21× wall-clock overhead better spent on more CBQ epochs in the Sequential schedule? A compute-matched comparison (Seq. CBQ with ~1.2× the optimizer budget) would separate "better schedule" from "more optimization."
