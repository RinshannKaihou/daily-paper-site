One recomputed probability tile feeds all four gradients — AlphaFold3-style triangular attention made faster, more accurate, and bitwise deterministic on H100s.

TurboPairFormer: Fast and Stable Protein Folding Model Training with an Optimized Triangle Attention Kernel

Yide Ran, Chelsea Lowman, Jan Domanski, David Hartmann, et al. | 2026-10-05 |

GPU kernels Protein folding Deterministic training AlphaFold3 / OpenFold3

Key metric · determinism sweep

600/600

Bitwise-identical gradients across 5 repeated calls in every fixed-input test — cuEquivariance, OpenFold3-Triton and DeepSpeed-Evo pass none. Fastest of 8 backends: 12.58 s/step at crop 768 on 16×H100 (1.13× cuEquivariance, 1.73× Triton).

Why this matters

Speed, mixed-precision error, and reproducibility are usually traded off in large-model training — this kernel shows all three can be won at once, with the recipe open-sourced.

把 AlphaFold3 训练中最贵的三角形注意力 kernel 同时做到更快、更准、逐位确定，对大模型训练效率与混合精度稳定性研究很有参考价值。

Critical edge

The 1.73× headline is against the weakest baseline; versus NVIDIA’s cuEquivariance the real gain is only 6.1–11.7% per step — and bitwise determinism itself costs up to 13.7% backward time.

01Methodology — the mechanism

forward S=QKᶜ/√D+bias+M → P=softmax → O=PV, save O₁₆ + rounding residual E₁₆ backward / CTA = 1 key tile (64) × 4 outer slices: recompute S,P once → dS = P ∘ (dP − δ̂) → dV in FP32 registers (in-block) · dQ partial (BF16) · dbias partial (fixed order) reduce separate kernels sum partials in fixed FP32 order → no atomics, no N³ score-gradient
  • Three reduction axes, one partition: dQ reduces over keys, dK/dV over queries, dbias over slices — all four reuse the same P and dS, so one recomputation serves them all (prior kernels recompute 3×, use atomics, or store cubic gradients).
  • Determinism by construction: one-writer BF16 partials summed in ascending fixed FP32 order; Proposition D.1 proves bitwise reproducibility for fixed shapes and schedules.
  • Output-residual compensation: backward rebuilds the softmax correction δ̂ in FP32 from the saved BF16 output plus residual E₁₆ — the single change behind most of the accuracy win.
  • Hopper-native: TMA loads, wgmma, two warpgroups (256 threads), 16-key probability chunks; drop-in OpenFold3 (TriMul) integration, open-sourced on PyPI as turbopairformer.

Lowest of all eight backends at every crop: 17.4–42.1% below Triton, 6.1–11.7% below cuEquivariance.

  • Gradient accuracy vs FP64 reference (10 seeds × 6 shapes): lowest mean RMSE for dQ, dK, dbias in all six shapes — 31.4–44.4% / 28.7–42.4% / 16.9–37.9% below cuEquivariance; dV only tied.
  • Stability: all runs finite; OpenFold3-Triton hits NaN by updates 7–10; mean raw loss 1.640 / 1.603 / 1.603 vs cuEquivariance 1.646 / 1.608 / 1.598 (crops 384/640/768).
  • Task quality preserved, not improved: 9,297 kinase PDB complexes — model-selection means 0.715–0.723, differences vs 7 of 8 baselines statistically indistinguishable from zero; MegaFold is significantly higher (+0.0082).
  • The determinism tax, self-reported: the kernel’s own non-deterministic variants are 2.8–4.1% faster at D=32 and 11.0–13.7% at D=16 in backward.
  • Kernel-level: backward wall-clock 35.6–45.6% below cuEquivariance; 7.4% faster to 3.6% slower than Proteinx; forward changes cut forward time 25.5–27.5% at D=32.

Strengths

  • Speed, numerical accuracy and bitwise determinism simultaneously — with a clean mechanism (one recomputation, one-writer partials, fixed orders) and a formal proof.
  • Output-residual compensation is elegantly cheap: one extra BF16 tensor, public outputs and dV bitwise unchanged.
  • Unusually thorough for a kernel paper: end-to-end training, FP64 gradient audits, 600-case determinism sweep, 9,297-complex fine-tuning equivalence.
  • Fully open source, integrated into OpenFold3, and candid about its own trade-offs.

Limitations

  • Query-partial buffer scales cubically with crop N — very long crops stay memory-bound; named as the main open problem.
  • Determinism costs 11.0–13.7% backward time at D=16 vs the kernel’s own non-deterministic variants.
  • Hopper-only (PTX 8.8 / CUDA 12.9.1), crops ≤768, D∈{16,32}; no Blackwell or AMD results.
  • Stability evidence is a 400-update window, one trajectory per crop — rules out NaN blowups, not long-run convergence.
  • Fine-tuning cohort is kinase-only, and dV accuracy is not improved over cuEquivariance.

03Questions for further exploration

  1. Can the cubic query-partial buffer become a hierarchical or streaming reduction that keeps fixed accumulation order without the N³ allocation?
  2. Does output-residual compensation transfer to standard (non-triangular) attention and to FP8/BF16 training, where output-conversion error also corrupts δ?
  3. How large is the determinism tax on Blackwell-class hardware, and does the partial ordering remain optimal there?
  4. Would current OpenFold3-Triton builds erase the NaN stability gap — kernel numerics vs tooling (Appendix G.2)?
  5. Does bitwise-deterministic training measurably help downstream workflows enough to justify the 3–14% backward-time cost in production folding pipelines?
