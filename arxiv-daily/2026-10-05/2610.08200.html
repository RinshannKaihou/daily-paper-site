Causally validated attention heads let LLMs retrieve network records from context — heads transfer, single neurons don’t.

Finding the Heads and the Neurons Responsible for Network Information Retrieval in Language Models

Md Abdul Kadir · Md Mohasin Hossain · Daniel Sonntag DFKI / Univ. of Oldenburg / Saarland Univ. 2026-10-05
mechanistic-interpretability causal-ablation attention-heads knowledge-neurons LLM-agent-safety
Key metric
AUC · 1.000

Head-level detectors hit 99.5–100% held-out accuracy in all five models — and flag 500/500 CAIDA reverse-DNS records with zero retraining.

Why this matters

A reusable, causally validated detector recipe for agent safety: catch an LLM about to act on network data — plus a warning that neuron-level findings won’t transfer.

为 LLM Agent 安全监控提供了可复用的头级检测器思路；“头部结论可迁移、神经元结论不可迁移” 是机制级监控的重要反面警示。

Critical edge

On both Gemma models the detector still flags 100% of context-free questions — partly a wording detector — and the headline accuracies come entirely from the training template; out of template it degrades toward a generic “short-token-retrieval” signal.

Methodology how the heads were found

  1. Task & controls Each prompt shows a domain / IP record and asks the model to copy one field back (DeepURLBench positives). Matched negatives — entity, vocabulary, arithmetic questions on the same record — plus context-free stress tests.
  2. Algorithm A · HeadSuppressor Ablate each of 128–1,152 heads → drop in answer log-prob; necessity/sufficiency activation patching on the top-50; selectivity filter Sel(h) = Δ_net − ¼(Δ_ent+Δ_voc+Δ_ord) keeps 1–9 “clean” heads per model.
  3. Algorithm B · greedy circuit discovery A structurally different joint search recovers heads overlapping only Jaccard 0.12–0.20 — yet equally damaging when suppressed: the signal isn’t an artifact of one algorithm.
  4. Neuron probes Single-column ablation (causal ranking) vs. information-gain ranking; logistic probes; 20-random-neuron controls per head.
  5. Transfer, no retraining Detectors tested on 500 CAIDA reverse-DNS records (+500 TriviaQA negatives), then DNS-TTL, MAC-address, and naturalistic monitoring stress tests.

Models: Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma-4-E4B-it, Gemma-4-E2B-it — five attention architectures (hybrid linear, dense, sliding-window/global, GQA).

  • The filter matters more than accuracy. The unfiltered top-20 flags 100% of context-free prompts in all five models; the clean subset cuts this to 0.00 on the three Qwens — but Gemma stays at 1.000.
  • Neuron concentration is the exception, not the rule. Only Qwen3.5-4B collapses into one neuron (L15·H1·D0: 99.4% acc, AUC 1.000; causal and information-gain rankings agree exactly). In Qwen3-8B the best causally ranked neuron reaches AUC 0.665 vs 1.000 for its full head — indistinguishable from the random-neuron control (0.667±0.186).
  • Neuron-level transfer fails. Single-neuron detectors drop to AUC 0.285–0.568 on CAIDA — one below chance despite a perfect in-distribution score.
  • Generalization is asymmetric. MAC fields flagged ≥0.985 in 4/5 models; DNS-TTL 0.990 (Qwen3.5-4B) but 0.000 (Gemma-4-E4B). Patching shows the heads carry those values regardless (sufficiency S = 10.02 nats vs −0.03 random) — the gap is a linear-classifier artifact.
  • Realistic monitoring isn’t ready. All 25 naturalistic positives flagged (P ≥ 0.883), but only 48% of matched negatives are left unflagged — a flight-gate number is flagged at P = 0.998.

Strengths

  • Causal validation at every level — ablation plus necessity/sufficiency patching, not just correlational probes; where the two rankings disagree is itself a finding.
  • Two structurally independent discovery algorithms cross-validate; matched negatives (entity / vocabulary / arithmetic) are a model of confound control for localization claims.
  • The zero-retraining CAIDA evaluation is the strongest single piece of evidence: different institution, different methodology, still 500/500 in four of five models.
  • Intellectual honesty — the paper foregrounds its own negative results (neuron spread, transfer failure, Gemma wording sensitivity, 48% specificity) instead of burying them.

Limitations

  • One retrieval task, five models, one run each — no variance estimates on headline numbers, and architecture is varied only in a confounded way; neuron-concentration model-specificity is observed, not explained.
  • Single-neuron findings are unsafe out of distribution (AUC as low as 0.285), limiting the practical payoff of the finer-grained analysis.
  • Out-of-template behavior is weak: 48% negative specificity on naturalistic text, wording-driven false flags in Gemma, and a generic “retrieval happening” confound on non-network numeric fields.
  • The proposed monitoring application remains a proof of concept — no deployment-ready system, no naturalistic-negative retraining attempted.
  • Algorithm overlap is only Jaccard 0.12–0.20: effect-equivalence is established, but which heads matter is largely algorithm-dependent.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Is neuron-level concentration predictable from architecture (GQA group size, residual width, head dimension) or training recipe — and does it shift with scale within one family?
  2. Does ablating the top neuron promote the next-ranked one? The unrun knockout-cascade test would directly adjudicate the “partial concentration” account.
  3. Can retraining the detector with naturalistic negatives restore out-of-template specificity above 48% without losing in-template accuracy or CAIDA transfer?
  4. Do the same clean heads carry other network-entity types (certificates, ASNs, ports) — is there a shared “address-like field” circuit?
  5. Would head-level transfer survive at frontier scale, and could suppressing the heads become a real-time steering signal rather than a post-hoc classifier?

Relevance 相关性
