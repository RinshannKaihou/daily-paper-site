> **一句话总结：** 提出 TRACE 框架，用 rollout 阶段真实产生的 FP4 量化码字（仅缓存后半层专家激活与 KV 的 1-bit 尾数+scale，通信量降约 6 倍）来指导训练侧的 FP4 舍入决策，直接缩小训练- rollout 两条执行路径的分歧：在 Qwen3.5-35B-A3B 联合 NVFP4 W/A+KV rollout 的推理 RL 中，平均分从最强基线 QUADS 的 68.8 提升到 75.3（+6.5），追平 BF16 rollout 的 74.9，同时保持最高 5.4× 的 rollout 解码加速，端到端每步 RL 仅增加 7.4% 开销。

> **TL;DR:** Existing FP4 RL methods (QAT, QaRL, QUADS) minimize quantization error on each execution path independently, which does not minimize the train–rollout discrepancy and can collapse training. TRACE records the actual FP4 codewords produced during rollout (routed-expert activations + FP4 KV) and uses them to pick the training-side rounding decision, communicating only 1-bit mantissa codes plus scales from the latter half of layers (~6× cache reduction, near-vanilla FP4 throughput). On Qwen3.5-35B-A3B reasoning RL under joint NVFP4 weight/activation/KV rollout, TRACE reaches 75.3 average over LiveCodeBench/AIME24/AIME25/HMMT25 vs 68.8 for the strongest baseline QUADS (+6.5), matching BF16 rollout (74.9); rollout decoding keeps up to 5.4× speedup over BF16 with only 7.4% end-to-end RL step-time overhead.

## 核心贡献

- **问题诊断：** 指出现有 FP4 RL 方法（QAT、QaRL、QUADS、Rollout-ResQ）各自独立优化训练/rollout 路径的量化精度，这并不等于最小化两路径间的分歧——用两个反例说明 QUADS 降低了每条路径的量化误差，却仍留下 0.10/0.99 的 train-rollout 偏差；BF16 下仅差 0.48 的两个激活值（60.24 vs 59.76）经不一致 FP4 舍入后差距被放大到 24（72 vs 48）。
- **Rollout 引导的 QAT：** rollout 时记录 routed experts 激活与 FP4 KV 的真实量化码字，训练时在同一 scale 下从两个相邻 E2M1 码字中选取最接近 rollout 码字的那个，构造上保证局部偏差不超过标准 RTN。
- **仅尾数（mantissa-only）缓存方案：** 利用 ">95% 不一致码字仅相差一个相邻码"和"向低码字的舍入修正集中在深层"两个观察，只对后 20/40 层缓存 1-bit 尾数+scale（约 7.5 KB/token，比全量信息降约 6 倍，全量约 50 KB/token、单步 RL 高达 51 TB）。
- **大规模验证：** 在 4 个 MoE 模型（Qwen3.5-35B-A3B、Qwen3.5-122B-A10B、Qwen3.8-Flash-Next、Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B）上覆盖推理、代码、长程三类 RL 任务，联合 FP4 W/A+KV rollout 下性能追平 BF16 rollout，并给出逐层比特/层数的模块化敏感性消融、MXFP4 格式泛化、与 Score Centering 及 post-hoc PTQ 流水线的对比。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes the FP4 RL problem:** shows per-path quantization accuracy is the wrong objective — two worked examples demonstrate QUADS reduces per-path error yet leaves train–rollout discrepancy of 0.10 and 0.99 (vs 1.0 for vanilla FP4), and inconsistent FP4 rounding amplifies a 0.48 BF16 activation gap into a 24-gap quantized discrepancy (Figure 4a).
- **Rollout-guided rounding:** for each captured training-side activation, select among the two adjacent FP4 E2M1 codewords the one closest to the rollout-side codeword (q_TRACE = argmin |q − q_rollout|), with a constructive local guarantee |q_TRACE − q_rollout| ≤ |q_RTN − q_rollout|; only routed experts and attention-layer KV are quantized, all other modules stay BF16.
- **Mantissa-only quantization-information caching:** motivated by the fact that full rollout-side records would be ~50 KB/token (≈51 TB per RL step at 4096 trajectories for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B), TRACE caches 1-bit mantissa codes + amortized scales for only the latter 20 of 40 layers (~7.5 KB/token, ~6× reduction), preserving decoding throughput close to vanilla FP4 rollout.
- **Comprehensive evaluation:** four MoE models (up to Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B) across reasoning, coding, and long-horizon RL; ablations over isolated W/KV quantization, mantissa bits and layer coverage, MXFP4 variants, Score Centering, and post-training quantization pipelines; plus rollout-time breakdown (86% model forward, 4% weight guidance, 2% KV guidance, 8% KV dequantization).

## Methodology

The framework targets GRPO-style RL (with R3 for routed-expert replay, token-level importance ratios clipped at 5, max_version_diff=3) implemented on VeRL + Megatron for training and SGLang for asynchronous rollout.

**Rollout-guided quantization-aware training.** During rollout generation, TRACE records, at each quantization site (routed-expert activations and the FP4 KV cache of softmax-attention layers), the actual FP4 codeword produced by the rollout engine. During the QAT forward pass, for a training-side activation x captured at the same (trajectory, token, site), it normalizes x with the rollout scale, brackets the two adjacent E2M1 codewords {q−, q+}, and selects q_TRACE = argmin over this set of |q − q_rollout| (Eq. 2). Because the rollout codeword q_rollout always lies within [q−, q+], this choice never increases the local quantized train–rollout discrepancy relative to round-to-nearest (Eq. 3). Gradients flow through a straight-through estimator. The authors are explicit that this is a local, per-site guarantee: it removes the discrepancy amplification caused by inconsistent rounding, not the underlying activation difference due to policy staleness.

**Mantissa-only communication.** A data-volume analysis shows the full-information variant is infeasible: for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (hidden 2048, KV head dim 256, 2 KV heads, 40 layers, 4.5 bits/value, 256K max response) each generated token produces ~45 KB of activation guidance plus ~5.6 KB of KV guidance — roughly 51 TB per RL step at 4096 trajectories, ≈3 minutes of aggregate transfer even at 300 GB/s. Two empirical observations justify compression: (1) over 95% of mismatched FP4 codeword entries differ by exactly one adjacent code, so mantissa code + rollout scale suffice to identify the desired neighboring codeword; (2) rounding corrections toward lower codewords concentrate in deeper layers (Figure 6), so guidance is needed mainly from the model's second half. TRACE therefore caches 1-bit mantissa + scale for the latter 20 layers only (~2048 × 20 × 1.5 = 61,440 bits ≈ 7.5 KB/token, ~6× less than full activation caching), staged through GPU/CPU caches with async H2D/D2H overlapped with computation; the reconstruction picks the E2M1 magnitude whose low-b code bits match the cached code, using the sign of the current training activation.

**Setup.** Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (35B total / 3B active) trains reasoning RL (LiveCodeBench, AIME24, AIME25, HMMT25) with batches of 256×16 trajectories for 400 steps; Qwen3.5-122B-A10B and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next train coding RL (DeepSWE, Terminal-Bench 2.1) at 128×16 and 64×8 trajectories per step respectively, for 400 steps; Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B trains long-horizon RL (GDPval) for 50 steps. Max response 32K tokens; context up to 256K. Quantization applies NVFP4 (or MXFP4 in one study) to routed experts and KV cache only.

## Results & Highlights

- **Joint FP4 W/A + KV rollout, reasoning RL (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, Table 1):** TRACE averages 75.3 across LiveCodeBench/AIME24/AIME25/HMMT25 vs 68.8 for the strongest baseline QUADS (+6.5) and 59.6 for plain QAT (+15.7), matching BF16 rollout's 74.9. Largest single-task gain on HMMT25: 70.0 vs QUADS 59.0 (+11.0); AIME24 86.3 vs QUADS 80.5 (+5.8). Vanilla QAT collapses on Terminal-Bench 2.1 in the motivating experiment (Figure 1).
- **Larger models, coding and long-horizon RL (Table 2):** Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on Terminal-Bench: TRACE 70.6 vs QUADS 66.4 (+4.2) and BF16 68.8 (+1.8); Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B on GDPval: 90.2 vs QUADS 88.9 (+1.3), within 0.1 of BF16 (90.3); Qwen3.5-122B-A10B on DeepSWE: 33.0 vs QUADS 29.1 (+3.9), vs BF16 33.4 (−0.4).
- **Efficiency:** decoding throughput on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B reaches 5.4× BF16 rollout at 128K output length; TRACE stays close to vanilla FP4 throughput while the full-information variant degrades sharply. End-to-end RL step time rises from 664 s to 713 s — only 7.4% overhead vs vanilla joint FP4. Appendix A.4 (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next): joint FP4 improves output throughput over FP8/BF16 by 1.09×/1.41×/1.77×/2.45× at 8K/16K/32K/64K outputs; the single-run 128K figure (2013 vs 526 tokens/s, 3.83×) includes concurrency effects from KV capacity (peak batch 64 vs 7).
- **Ablations (Tables 3–5):** isolated FP4 W/A with BF16 KV: TRACE 75.4 avg vs QUADS 72.9 (+2.5) and BF16 74.9 (+0.5), with HMMT25 69.4 (+6.6) and AIME25 82.1 (+1.7); isolated FP4 KV: 74.8 vs QAT 73.2 (+1.6). Under MXFP4: W4A8+MXFP4 KV 75.1 vs QAT 69.0 (+6.1); the more aggressive W4A4+MXFP4 73.5 vs 67.2 (+6.3). Modular sensitivity: all-40-layer 1-bit mantissa 75.8, default latter-20-layer 75.3, 10 layers 74.1, 5 layers 73.4 — most guidance value lives in ~20 deeper layers.
- **vs Score Centering and vs post-hoc PTQ (Tables 6–7):** TIS+SC reaches 74.1 average vs TRACE 75.3 (+1.2), with SC's max train–rollout log-prob diff stuck at ~1.8–2.1 while TRACE tracks BF16. Training in BF16 then applying post-hoc FP4 PTQ caps out at 71.4 (H-Scale), 71.0 (4over6), 70.4 (vanilla NVFP4) vs TRACE's 75.3 (+3.9) — RL training with FP4 rollout throughout yields a stronger final FP4 policy.
- **Adaptation dynamics (Figure 7):** TRACE initially scores below BF16 rollout on HMMT25 due to quantization-induced degradation, catches up around step ~120, and stays comparable or higher thereafter — once discrepancy is controlled, the policy progressively adapts to FP4 execution.

## Authors & Credibility

The 12-author team is from Alibaba Token Hub, Alibaba Group, with Ohio State University (first author Xin Wang and Yizhong Cai hold dual affiliations; work done during the Alibaba Research Intern Program). Senior authors Mi Zhang and Jianwei Zhang lead the effort, and the group has a direct track record on exactly this problem: the strongest baseline, QUADS (Zhuge et al., 2026), shares five co-authors with this paper, and H-Scale (Yu et al., 2026) is also from the same lab. The engineering claims are backed by production-scale infrastructure (VeRL/Megatron/SGLang, models up to 2.4T total parameters) that few academic labs could run, which lends strong credibility to the efficiency measurements — but it also means every baseline and every evaluation was run in-house on in-house models, with no external replication and no code release mentioned in the paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **In-house lineage and baselines.** The central comparison, QUADS, is from the same group with overlapping authors, and all four evaluation models are Qwen-family. That is understandable (access to 2.4T-scale training) but it means the "beats QUADS by +6.5" claim was produced by the creators of QUADS re-implementing their own prior method; an independent or external baseline (e.g., on an open MoE like DeepSeek or Qwen3 official releases) is absent.
- **The headline speedup deserves unpacking.** "Up to 5.4×" is measured at 128K output length, where BF16 is weakest; the honest per-length numbers are 1.09× at 8K and 2.45× at 64K (Appendix A.4), and the paper itself concedes the 128K single-run gain (3.83×) includes concurrency effects from KV capacity rather than pure kernel speed. To the authors' credit, they state this explicitly and report the 7.4% end-to-end overhead transparently.
- **Scope of quantization is partial.** Only routed experts and attention-layer KV are FP4; attention activations, dense modules, embeddings, and lm_head remain BF16. The paper does not test full-model FP4 RL, so "FP4 RL" here means a carefully chosen FP4 subset — the train–rollout discrepancy problem may resurface differently at wider coverage.
- **The theoretical guarantee is local and admittedly insufficient.** Eq. 3 bounds per-site discrepancy only; the authors acknowledge it does not imply monotone global discrepancy or policy-level reduction. The mechanism narrative leans on the adaptation phenomenon (initial dip, recovery by ~120 steps, Figure 7), which is empirical: it shows the policy adapts to FP4 rollout, but does not isolate how much of the final gain is rounding alignment versus simply more stable long training under a curated FP4 subset. No ablation disables guidance while keeping the caching infrastructure, beyond the QAT/QaRL/QUADS rows.
- **Layer-heuristic fragility.** The choice of "latter 20 of 40 layers" is justified by an empirical distribution (Figure 6) on one model family; sensitivity is reported only for layer count (75.8 → 73.4 from L40 to L5), not for which layers, and the rule's transfer to non-Qwen architectures is untested.
- **Reproducibility.** Models are 35B–2.4T proprietary checkpoints, the 2.4T run is 50 steps, and no code or model release is mentioned; academic reproduction is essentially impossible at the upper scale.

## Strengths

- The problem formulation is sharp and falsifiable: per-path quantization accuracy ≠ train–rollout agreement, demonstrated with two tiny worked numeric examples (Figure 3) that any reader can verify by hand.
- First framework to show joint FP4 weight/activation/KV rollout during RL can match BF16 rollout performance (75.3 vs 74.9 on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B) rather than merely "degrade less".
- The mantissa-only caching design is grounded in measured statistics (>95% adjacent-codeword mismatches; deeper-layer concentration of downward corrections) and validated end-to-end: ~6× guidance-data reduction with decoding throughput close to vanilla FP4 and only 7.4% step-time overhead.
- Unusually complete ablation coverage: isolated W vs KV, four MXFP4/NVFP4 format combinations, mantissa bits × layer coverage grid (Table 5), a trained-optimizer alternative (Score Centering), and the post-hoc-PTQ pipeline — each table isolates one design axis.
- Honest reporting of unfavorable details, including the concurrency-confounded 128K speedup, the local-only nature of the theoretical guarantee, and the initial performance dip during adaptation.

## Limitations

- Evaluation restricted to one model family (Qwen3.5/Qwen3.8) under one RL algorithm (GRPO with R3); generalization to dense models, PPO, or other pipelines is untested.
- Quantization coverage limited to routed experts + attention KV; full-model FP4 RL remains open, and the latter-half-layer caching heuristic is architecture-specific.
- The local discrepancy guarantee does not compose to a global or policy-level bound, and the paper offers no analysis of when the adaptation phase (≈120 steps) might fail to recover on shorter or weaker training runs.
- Practicality: guidance plumbing (CPU caches, async H2D/D2H, disk staging) adds real systems complexity; the 51 TB full-information figure makes the full variant unusable, so the method lives or dies with the mantissa-only approximation, which is only validated on these models.
- No code, models, or seeds released; the 2.4T model run is only 50 steps, so the long-horizon claim rests on the thinnest evidence in the paper.

## Relevance

与你关注的低精度/高效 LLM 训练方向高度相关：这是把 FP4 量化从"推理加速"推进到"RL 训练闭环"的代表性工作，其"训练-执行路径一致性比单路径量化精度更重要"的核心论断，对量化感知训练、RL 系统设计乃至低精度微调都有直接借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does rollout-guided rounding transfer to dense (non-MoE) models and non-GRPO RL algorithms, where routed-expert quantization — the current guidance substrate — does not exist?
- Can the latter-half-layer caching heuristic be derived from layer-wise statistics automatically, and does it hold for architectures with different depth/width ratios than Qwen?
- What happens under full-model FP4 (attention activations, dense layers included), where the current BF16 "escape hatches" disappear?
- Is the ~120-step adaptation phase guaranteed to recover for short RL runs (e.g., the 50-step 2.4T setting), or is there a regime where the initial dip never closes?
- How does TRACE interact with even lower precision (FP3/FP2 or 1.58-bit) where the "adjacent codeword" argument weakens because the codebook is too sparse to absorb discrepancies?
