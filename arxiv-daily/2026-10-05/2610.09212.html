Methodology

Read the Hessian, skip the rotation

Distilled — the full derivation lives in the paper.

damped Hessian H→LDLᵀ→Dᵢ as Viterbi weights→64-col trellis codes→fused dequant+GEMM
  • Curvature as the branch metric. Error feedback rewrites the loss as Σ Dᵢ·ηᵢ²; CurveTQ sets the Viterbi weight to 1/dᵢ² — reading what QTIP computes but ignores. Proposition 1 bounds excess loss by block anisotropy Γ(H), exactly what rotation flattens: weight ≡ rotation under the high-resolution relaxation.
  • Block-local trellis. Each 64-column block starts in any state; the chosen 14-bit boundary state is stored (0.22 bpw) instead of QTIP's tail-biting, which pins block heads to the wrong residual constraint.
  • Cheap side info. Closed-form 128-entry equiprobable quantile table, Mealy-emitted from the state edge (2¹⁴ survivors vs QTIP's 2¹⁶); factored scale field s_bg = μ + σ_r + β_g + ε_bg at 0.068 bpw.
  • Protocol. Six checkpoints, C4 calibration, lm-eval at 4096 ctx, paired 95% bootstrap CIs, all rates count every bit of side information, zero post-quantization fine-tuning; builds are deterministic.

  • Attribution: a stored start state alone lifts QTIP/Proteus +1.0–3.3 pts — the single biggest source of their gap; the group scale adds far less.
  • MoE first: first trellis-coded result on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (256 experts, top-8); beats Proteus by +2.1–3.3 and strengthened QTIP/Proteus by +0.6–2.8, winning 18–21 of 30 tasks.
  • Speed (RTX 3090, batch 1): 139.0 tok/s vs QTIP 127.0 and Proteus 105.9 on Llama-2-7B — fastest of the three at every tested batch size and bit width, since there is no transform to undo.
  • 3 bits: ahead or level in 12 of 16 model-metric-baseline contrasts; at 4 bits all trellis codecs tie within 0.35 ppl / 1 pt.

Strengths

  • Crisp, testable theory (rotation ≡ weighting) backed by a targeted 2×2 experiment, not just end-to-end scores.
  • Exceptional fairness engineering: strengthened baselines, paired CIs, every side-info bit counted, zero fine-tuning for any method.
  • The start-state finding is independently valuable and transferable to QTIP/Proteus at 0.22 bpw.
  • Thorough build-up/teardown ablation; negative and non-monotone results reported verbatim.
  • Practical payoff: fastest decoder everywhere tested, deterministic builds, first trellis-coded MoE result.

Limitations

  • Substitution test spans three models, one rate, n=4 slices; the native-Euclidean cell collapses on Llama-3-8B, and the rotated search is ahead on 25/34 Qwen3-4B tasks.
  • Lead shrinks with scale: +0.5–1.3 pts on Qwen3-32B vs +2.5–5.2 on 4–8B; no 70B-class dense model tested.
  • Gains concentrate on generative tasks (GSM8K, IFEval); multiple-choice suites barely move.
  • On MoE the win is carried by start state + scale field — the paper's namesake curvature weight is nearly inert.
  • No code or kernel release stated; throughput claims rest on one RTX 3090 and authors' own Proteus kernel.
  • No comparison with non-trellis extreme-compression lines (AQLM, additive quantization) beyond GPTQ/HIGGS.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the substitution thesis survive at 70B+ scale and sub-2-bit rates, where the high-resolution relaxation is least accurate?
  2. If QTIP/Proteus simply absorb the start-state fix, does the contribution become primarily diagnostic rather than a codec win?
  3. On damping-dominated MoE expert Hessians, could a routing-aware (token-count-corrected) weight reactivate the curvature term?
  4. The Euclidean-metric swap lowers WikiText-2 perplexity yet costs 3.3 downstream points — what makes perplexity and generation quality diverge, and can it be predicted before deployment?
  5. Would a learned (rather than closed-form equiprobable) quantile table interact positively with native-basis weighting?
