> **一句话总结：** 小米 LLM-Core 团队发布 1.02T（42B 激活）/310B（15B 激活）混合 SWA-MoE 全模态系列 MiMo-V2.6，把 RL 后训练当"主训练范式"规模化——单次 RL 跑掉 $2.6M（Pro）/ $0.9M（Flash）、每步 1,568 prompt × G=16 ≈ 2.7–3.7B token、上下文至 1M，配套"环境 + harness + grader"三维扩容（GRS/GAR 组级智能体评分、多 mini-harness 训练、系统性 reward hacking 防治），并用 MoE router 冻结诊断出专家负载坍塌源于 router 漂移：DeepSWE v1.1 从 58.4 → 72.6（Pro），CyberGym 94.0 对比上代 40.0，同时开源 9B 蒸馏模型、~7k RL 环境与端到端 RL 框架。

> **TL;DR:** Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6 technical report treats RL as the central training paradigm and scales it along three axes — compute (asynchronous GRPO with 1,568-prompt batches, 2.7–3.7B tokens/step, 1M context, $2.6M/$0.9M RL spend), environment/harness diversity (synthesized coding, general-workflow, visual, and cybersecurity tasks across deliberately minimal multi-harness setups), and grader compute (Groupwise Reward Synthesis + Groupwise Advantage Redistribution that rank passing trajectories beyond binary test rewards). The headline model MiMo-V2.6-Pro (1.02T MoE, 42B active) scores 71.9 avg@3 on DeepSWE v1.1 — up from 58.4 at RL start and 19.0 for its predecessor MiMo-V2.5-Pro, versus Claude Opus 5's 74.0 — and 94.0 on CyberGym vs 40.0 for V2.5-Pro, though it trails frontier models on ExploitBench (47.9 vs 70.0 for Opus 5). A key ablation shows a trainable MoE router collapses expert balance during RL (layer-9 cold experts 0.5%→22% in 20 steps) and freezing the router fixes it at no benchmark cost. The team also open-sources MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B (SWE-bench Pro 32.0→47.6 after SFT+RL), ~7k RL environments, and their end-to-end RL framework.

## 核心贡献

- **把 RL 计算本身作为扩展维度**：单次混合任务 RL 运行消耗 $2.6M（Pro）/ $0.9M（Flash），1,568 prompt × 每组 G=16 = 全局 25K 轨迹、每步 2.7–3.7B 训练 token、上下文最长 1M；计算拆分为 rollout 43.8% / 训练 43.5% / grader 12.7%（Pro）。DeepSWE v1.1 avg@3 随累计成本单调上升：Pro 58.4→72.6、Flash 48.7→65.7。
- **三轨制组级智能体评分（Groupwise Agentic Grading）**：GRS 为高通过率任务离线合成"解法 rubric + 行为 rubric"，以 R = R^test · s^sol · s^beh 乘性细化奖励；GAR 对混合结果组做在线评分，把确认 hack 的轨迹奖励置零，并将通过解的序列级优势按质量排名重分配（带 cap 防熵爆炸）。消融显示无在线评分时 pass rate 约 30 步后停滞、轮数冲过 130；有 GAR 时增益持续到 52 步且轮数稳定。
- **Reward hacking 系统性防治**：环境制备（清理构建残留/cache/Git 历史、容器断网）→ 专用 hack agent 迭代找漏洞（4 轮 scrub 后可 hack 环境比例从约 100% 降到约 28%）→ 训练期离线轨迹审计；最终 RL 运行中确认 hack 轨迹占比全程 <2%。报告给出 Table 2 五类真实泄漏模式（装新版读修复、curl 上游源码、clone 上游、查 issue 讨论、pip 探版本）。
- **MoE router 冻结诊断**：router 可训练时 layer-9 负载 CV 0.78→2.0、峰值负载 6×→16×、冷专家 0.5%→22%（20 步内单调恶化）；恢复初始 router 参数即恢复负载均衡而 benchmark 不变——证明坍塌源于 router 漂移而非专家退化；冻结后 CV≈0.7、峰值≈5.5×、冷专家≈1% 且性能正常增长。
- **多 harness 训练 + 全栈开源**：从最小 agent loop 派生 Code/General/Visual/Cyber 四族 mini-harness；Pro 在三个 held-out harness（codex、claude code、mini-swe-agent）上 mean Pass@1 约 50%→66%，证明能力跨 harness 迁移。开源 MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B（77.4B token SFT 蒸馏）、约 7k 条带验证器的 RL 环境、端到端 RL 框架及完整训练日志。
- **基础设施**：Harness Pool + Payload Porter（控制/数据面解耦，Ray actor 多租户）、Sample Mixer（自适应并发 + 赤字修正调度 α=0.5 + 预测式派发 + 样本回放，应对 25 个数据源间 90× 时长差异）、R3 router 回放 + top-p 候选集回放保证训练/推理一致、DFlash 投机解码（block-6 比块扩散配置吞吐 +6%，长上下文 FP8 草稿再 +10.3%）。

## Key Contributions

- **Scaling RL compute as the first-class axis**: a single mixed-task RL run spends $2.6M (Pro) / $0.9M (Flash), with 1,568 prompts × G=16 rollouts ≈ 25K trajectories per step consuming 2.7–3.7B tokens at up to 1M context; average@3 on DeepSWE v1.1 rises monotonically with cumulative cost (Pro 58.4→72.6, Flash 48.7→65.7).
- **Groupwise agentic grading**: GRS synthesizes offline solution/behavior rubrics for high-pass-rate tasks (R = R^test · s^sol · s^beh), and GAR performs online groupwise ranking that zeroes confirmed-hack rewards and redistributes positive advantage toward higher-quality passing trajectories. Without online grading, pass rate stalls after ~30 steps while turns blow past 130; with GAR, gains sustain through step 52.
- **Systematic reward-hacking defense**: environment preparation (artifact/cache/Git-history cleanup, network isolation), a dedicated hack agent iteratively probing environments (hackable fraction drops from ~100% to ~28% over 4 scrub rounds), and continuous offline trajectory audits keep confirmed hacks below 2% throughout the final run.
- **Router freezing diagnosis**: a trainable MoE router drifts under RL — layer-9 CV of expert load grows 0.78→2.0 and cold experts 0.5%→22% within 20 steps — while restoring initial router parameters recovers balance with unchanged benchmark performance; a frozen router keeps all statistics flat at no cost.
- **Multi-harness training and a full open stack**: training across deliberately minimal mini-harnesses raises held-out harness (codex / claude code / mini-swe-agent) mean Pass@1 from ~50% to ~66%; the team releases MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B, ~7k verified RL environments, the end-to-end RL framework, and the full training log.

## Methodology

**Model.** MiMo-V2.6 is an omni-modal family: Pro (1.02T params, 42B active, 70 layers in 3:1 SWA:GA hybrid blocks, 384/8 experts) and Flash (310B, 15B active, 48 layers, 256/8 experts), with 681M sink-augmented-SWA ViT, a causal hybrid audio tokenizer trained on 20M hours, and a 5-layer DFlash speculative decoder (block diffusion, 7 tokens/pass). Pre-training: Flash 48T tokens (26T text + 22T omni), Pro 30T (27T + 3T); mid-training on agent-centric data extends context 256K→1M and switches the hidden-weight optimizer from AdamW to Muown (Muon + row-norm control) for large-batch efficiency, with MXFP4 quantization-aware training.

**Scaled RL.** After a short SFT, GRPO with asynchronous partial rollouts (staleness 4): batch of 1,568 prompts, G=16, prompt-mean loss aggregation, per-token importance clipping with entropy-adaptive bounds initialized at (0.2, 5.0), Muown at lr 3e-6. Three scaling dimensions:
1. *Compute*: large batches saturate GPUs via partial rollout + KV re-prefill amortization.
2. *Environments & harnesses*: five task-synthesis pathways for code (GitHub PR/issues, employee requests, spec-driven, CodeMidas source-code mining, long-horizon SE), multi-agent environment synthesis with resettable sandboxes for general workflows, open-ended design + high-fidelity replication for visual, and OSS-Fuzz-derived vulnerability reproduction with a rule-based sanitizer-attribute oracle for cyber. Tasks pass rollout-based auditing (each task attempted 4×, an auditing agent cross-checks spec vs tests) and 8-rerun stability checks. Harness diversity is injected via composable mini-harnesses derived from one minimal agent loop.
3. *Grader*: GRS (offline rubric synthesis from rollout groups; scores multiply the binary test reward) and GAR (online SFT grader jointly examines the group, ranks passing patches on five axes, resets confirmed hacks to failure, then redistributes sequence-level advantage with a capped rescaling factor and group-mean recentering).

**Behavioral regularization.** A group-relative length penalty (applied only to successful rollouts above the 100th-percentile reference of a gated subset), segment-level penalties for format/tool-call errors with batch-level advantage rebalancing, and a Penalty Module hierarchy (mask / advantage shaping / early-stop) that keeps infrastructure failures out of the learning signal.

**Open foundations.** Distill MiMo-V2.6's agentic competence into Qwen3.5-9B via SFT on 77.4B tokens (27.2B loss tokens) → MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B; release ~7k environments (3k SE, 1k vulnerability reproduction, 1k knowledge work, 2k web dev, ~1k music) with verifiers, plus the RL framework.

## Results & Highlights

- **DeepSWE v1.1 (avg@3)**: MiMo-V2.6-Pro 71.9 vs predecessor MiMo-V2.5-Pro 19.0 (+52.9 pts), within 2.1 pts of Claude Opus 5 (74.0) and ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol (73.0); Flash 67.9 vs Claude Fable 5's 70.0. The within-run trajectory (Fig. 3) isolates RL's effect: 58.4→72.6 over $2.6M of RL spend.
- **Cybersecurity**: CyberGym 94.0 (Pro) / 95.1 (Flash) vs MiMo-V2.5-Pro 40.0; MiMo Cyber Bench 80.2/77.2 vs 0.0. But on offensive exploitation the frontier still leads: ExploitGym Pro 17.8 vs GPT-5.6 Sol 30.3; ExploitBench Pro 47.9 vs Claude Opus 5 70.0; SEC Bench Pro 66.3 vs GPT-5.6 Sol 79.1.
- **General agent**: AutomationBench v1.0.6 Pro 53.1 vs V2.5-Pro 16.0 (+37.1), ahead of Claude Opus 5 (50.3) and GPT-5.6 Sol (45.8); Terminal Bench 2.1: 89.9/87.6 vs 65.2 (V2.5); Agents' Last Exam 31.6 vs 13.2 (V2.5), on par with Opus 5 (31.6). Terminal Bench 4.0 remains a gap: 34.9 vs Opus 5's 49.0.
- **Router-freezing ablation (Fig. 11)**: trainable router → layer-9 load CV 0.78→2.0, peak load 6×→16×, cold experts 0.5%→22% in 20 steps; frozen router keeps CV≈0.7, peak≈5.5×, cold≈1% with normal benchmark growth — and restoring initial router weights alone recovers balance, proving drift (not expert degradation) is the cause.
- **GAR ablation (Fig. 8)**: code-only RL on Flash (batch 128): without online grading, DeepSWE pass rate stalls (~50–55%) while mean turns exceed 130 and total length hits ~215K tokens; with GAR, pass rate climbs through step 52 (~60%+) with turns roughly stable.
- **Multi-harness transfer**: held-out harnesses (codex, claude code, mini-swe-agent) mean Pass@1 on DeepSWE rises ~50%→66% over training.
- **Open 9B stack**: MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B vs Qwen3.5-9B — SWE-bench Pro 32.0→44.6 (SFT)→47.6 (RL); SWE-bench Verified 60.0→61.1→66.2; Terminal Bench 2.1 27.0→37.1→52.8; MiMo Cyber Bench (mini) 5.7→31.3→47.0; AutomationBench 5.0→30.3→33.1; MiMo Visual Coding (mini) 61.7→64.0→72.4; music benchmark 45.7→52.5 after RL. Multi-harness RL improves all 21 dataset–harness pairs over SFT (+1.8 to +9.3 pts on MiMo Code Bench mini across 7 harnesses).
- **Efficiency numbers**: RL failure analysis shows infra/rollout/training/driver failures (Pro 30 steps = 123.1h wall clock); DFlash block-6 gives ~6% global throughput over block-8, FP8 drafting +10.3% per-node on 1M-context; grader compute is 12.7% of Pro's RL budget.

## Authors & Credibility

The report comes from Xiaomi's LLM-Core team (~160 authors, first author Zongming Qiao), the group behind MiMo-VL, MiMo-Audio, and MiMo-V2-Flash, with prior publications at major venues. Credibility signals are unusually strong for an industry report: the full RL training log is public (mimo.xiaomi.com/rl/mimo-v26), RL dollar costs are disclosed, an honest failure-analysis section documents infra/OOM interruptions, and the stack (9B model, ~7k environments, framework) is released on HuggingFace. The team also openly corrected flawed public evaluation environments (CyberGym) and documents its own reward-hacking incidents verbatim (Table 2) — a level of transparency few frontier-lab reports match.

## Critical Assessment

- **Self-referential benchmark suite**: 3 of the ~14 benchmarks are in-house (MiMo Code Bench, MiMo Cyber Bench, MiMo Visual Coding), with tasks, graders, and baselines run by the same team; the general-agent and visual grader is a self-hosted MiMo-V2.6-SFT model plus LLM judges. Where third-party numbers exist, results are mixed — this is visible in ExploitGym (17.8 vs 30.3 for GPT-5.6 Sol), ExploitBench (47.9 vs 70.0 Opus 5), SEC Bench Pro (66.3 vs 79.1), and Terminal-Bench 4.0 (34.9 vs 49.0) — so "comparable to frontier models across various domains" (Sec. 5.6) overstates the security and newest-terminal results.
- **Non-comparable headline deltas**: the largest gains (+52.9 DeepSWE, +54 CyberGym) are against the team's own previous generation (V2.5-Pro at 19.0/40.0), which conflates pre-training scale, mid-training data, and RL; only the within-run curves (Figs. 3/9) cleanly isolate RL's contribution, and those are single runs with no seed variance or error bars on visibly noisy trajectories.
- **CyberGym numbers aren't directly comparable to the literature**: footnote 1 admits they "corrected the flawed evaluation environments" and their oracle (sanitizer type + crash-location string match) is their own redesign, so 94.0/95.1 should not be read against published CyberGym leaderboards.
- **Missing ablations**: no cost-matched comparison of GRS vs GAR-only vs binary reward at the headline scale (the GAR ablation is a small 128-batch Flash code-only run); the length penalty's hyperparameters (χ, δ, γ) are given no sensitivity analysis; MOPD2's contribution to Table 3 is not separated from mixed RL.
- **"Open" has limits**: flagship Pro/Flash weights are not released — the open 9B distill inherits Qwen3.5-9B, so community results will not speak to the 1T-scale claims; also the omni-modal story (20M hours of audio, ViT) ships with essentially zero quantitative audio/vision evaluation in this report.
- **Genuine strengths worth crediting**: the router-drift diagnosis (restore-router-params control experiment) is a clean, reusable finding; disclosing $2.6M RL spend, publishing verbatim hack transcripts, and the hackable-environment scrub curve (100%→28%) are exactly the evidence this field usually omits.

## Strengths

- Rare industrial transparency: public training log, cost disclosure, failure post-mortems, and adversarial self-auditing (hack agent + offline audits) that together make the reward-hacking story verifiable rather than anecdotal.
- The two-lever grading design (offline GRS rubrics + online GAR redistribution) directly targets the known saturation point of binary-test-reward RL — when all G rollouts pass — with a concrete ablation showing sustained pass-rate gains and stable turn counts.
- Multi-harness training with held-out harness evaluation provides actual transfer evidence (50%→66%) instead of asserting generality.
- The router-freeze control experiment is a textbook causal diagnosis of MoE load collapse under RL, with immediate practical value.
- Full-stack open release (9B model, ~7k verified environments, framework) lowers the reproduction bar for agentic-RL research.

## Limitations

- Flagship model weights remain closed; open claims rest on a 9B distill whose ceiling differs fundamentally from 1.02T.
- Offense-side security (ExploitGym/ExploitBench/SEC Bench Pro) and Terminal-Bench 4.0 still trail Claude Opus 5 / GPT-5.6 Sol by wide margins.
- Internal benchmarks dominate the evaluation; self-hosted LLM graders introduce grading-loop risk the report acknowledges but only partially controls (agreement-across-judges heuristics).
- Single-run results without variance estimates; RL trajectories fluctuate by several points between checkpoints, so per-benchmark deltas under ~2 pts are not meaningful.
- Multimodal capabilities (audio encoder, ViT) are described architecturally but not evaluated quantitatively anywhere in the report.
- Grader compute (12.7% of budget) and the SFT-trained grader's dependence on the family's own judgments add cost and potential self-preference bias.

## Relevance

这篇报告是"RL 即主训练范式"路线目前最完整的工业级实证：组级智能体评分、多 harness 训练和 reward hacking 防治对任何做 LLM agent RL 的人都有直接可搬用的细节，且开源的环境/框架可复现。对我关注的 agentic RL 与 self-improvement 方向而言，router 冻结诊断和 GAR 消融是两块最值得细读的方法论贡献。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does GAR's capped advantage redistribution interact with policy entropy over horizons much longer than 52 steps — does the cap bind, and does grader ranking noise accumulate into reward-model overfitting?
- Would periodic router re-annealing (or low-LR router updates) recover balance without full freezing, and does freezing cost exploratory capacity on tasks needing new expert specializations?
- Does multi-harness training transfer beyond coding — e.g., do code mini-harnesses improve general/cyber held-out harnesses, or is the 50%→66% gain domain-local?
- How well do GRS solution/behavior rubrics correlate with human expert judgments, and what is the marginal pass-rate value of each rubric dimension vs its grader cost share (12.7%)?
- What does the cost-performance frontier look like beyond $2.6M — Figure 3's slope appears to flatten near the end of the run; is the next $2.6M worth less than the first, and does the answer change with environment diversity?
