> **一句话总结：** 该文证明 SAE 特征发现所依赖的观测统计量（FDFI 分数、相关系数、秩检验、探针系数）原则上无法识别干预（steering/ablation）的一阶效应，并给出一个精确的梯度分解：探针隐含变化 − 干预归因 = Stein 残差 + 遗漏变量项 − 编辑梯度协方差；在 GPT-2、Gemma-2-2B/9B、Llama-3.1-8B 四个模型-SAE 对、11 个任务上实测，归因中位数只有探针系数的 2.8%–4.6%，而每 prompt 一次反向传播的归因预测单 latent 消融的实化符号准确率达 91%–97%（探针仅 17%–35%），按归因选出的特征集在 held-out 消融上产生的效应也系统性大于按相关性/秩检验/系数选出的集合。

> **TL;DR:** SAE features are discovered by how activations co-vary with behavior but *used* via interventions (steering, ablation). This paper proves that observational discovery evidence (FDFI scores, correlations, rank tests, probe coefficients) does not in general identify an intervention's first-order effect, and decomposes the probe-vs-attribution gap exactly into a Stein residual, an omitted-variable term, and an edit-gradient covariance — all estimable with one backward pass per prompt. Across 4 model-SAE pairs (GPT-2, Gemma-2-2B/9B, Llama-3.1-8B) and 11 tasks, the median intervention attribution is only 2.8–4.6% of the probe coefficient; on single-latent zero-ablations the gradient prediction matches the realized sign in 91–97% of rows versus 17–35% for the probe; and attribution-selected feature sets produce larger held-out ablation effects than correlation-, rank-test- or coefficient-selected sets in every model-task unit. Bottom line: use attribution, not association, when you intend to intervene.

## 核心贡献

- **不可识别边界（Proposition 1）：** 证明对任意仅依赖观测规律 (H, Y) 的发现统计量（FDFI、相关性、秩检验、类均值差、探针系数），存在同样观测规律的响应函数类使其干预归因可取任意实值——在评测设计中，均值编辑落在观测激活张成空间之外的中位份额高达 99.94%–99.97%，该边界直接适用于实际使用的有限数据。
- **精确分解（Theorem 1）：** 推导出探针隐含变化 ρ(Δ) 与干预归因 α(Δ)=E[Δ(H)ᵀ∇F(H)] 之差的恒等式 ρ−α = s_w + c(Δ) − e(Δ)（Stein 残差 + 遗漏变量项 − 编辑梯度协方差），每一项都可由每 prompt 一次反向传播在层内估计；对消融干预（Corollary 1）进一步分解为均值激活项 M_j 与协方差项 C_j，并解释了为何终位探针会完全错过从不触发于末位的 latent。
- **实证量化：** 在 4 个模型-SAE 对（GPT-2 block 6、Gemma-2-2B layer 12、Gemma-2-9B layer 22、Llama-3.1-8B layer 16 的残差流 SAE）× 11 个任务、8,828 条 prompt（7,063 选择 / 1,755 评测）上验证：间隙巨大、可被采样噪声排除、归因能更好预测实化干预并改变特征选择结果。
- **实践准则：** 白盒模型中每个指定干预只需一次反向传播即可报告带置信区间的归因；发现统计量保留用于筛候选特征，但关于"干预后会发生什么"的论断必须基于针对该干预的证据。

## Key Contributions

- An **identification boundary** (Proposition 1): no functional of the observational law — including FDFI scores, correlations, rank tests, class-mean differences and probe coefficients — identifies the attribution α(Δ); a two-prompt counterexample shows the same observations permit every real attribution for a fixed edit.
- An **exact decomposition** (Theorem 1): ρ(Δ) − α(Δ) = s_w + c(Δ) − e(Δ), splitting the gap into regression (Stein residual), omitted-variable, and edit-gradient terms, each with classical counterparts (Stein identity, omitted-variable bias, Haufe et al. pattern/distinction) and each estimable per stratum from one backward pass per prompt; Corollary 1 specializes it to ablations, showing a final-position probe sees ρ(Δ_j)=0 exactly when the latent never fires there.
- **Intervention consequences** across 4 public model-SAE pairs and eleven tasks: the gap is large (attribution a few percent of the probe coefficient in the median), far above sampling noise, attribution predicts realized steering and single-latent ablations far more accurately than probe-based predictions, and attribution-based selection changes which latents you would pick.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Prompts are partitioned into strata by task, answer and tokenized length; within a stratum the target is the teacher-forced standardized log-probability of the fixed answer F(H), and every estimand is defined within a stratum. An intervention modifies activations as H → H+Δ(H) keeping the SAE error term; its attribution is the first-order effect α(Δ) = E[Δ(H)ᵀ∇F(H)], computed by attribution patching (one backward pass per prompt). The observational benchmark is a linear least-squares probe on the latent's final-position score, whose coefficient β implies a probe-implied change ρ(Δ) = ūᵀAᵀβ_S for the same edit.

**Theory.** Proposition 1 constructs, for any observational law, an off-support affine perturbation F_ζ = F + ζᵀ(x−x₁) with ζ ∈ V⊥ that leaves (H,Y) and every discovery statistic unchanged while shifting the attribution by ūᵀζ. Theorem 1 then decomposes the gap exactly via the normal equations: a Stein residual s_w = wᵀ{cov(H,Y) − Σ_H γ} comparing the probe's fitted slope to the mean gradient γ = E∇F(H); an omitted-variable term c(Δ) = (v_A(ū) − ū)ᵀγ comparing the probe's activation pattern v_A(ū) with the mean edit; and an edit-gradient covariance e(Δ) = E[(Δ(H)−ū)ᵀ(∇F(H)−γ)]. For zero-ablation of latent j, α(Δ_j) = M_j + C_j splits into a mean-activation term and a covariance of the code with the directional derivative; rare firing makes C_j often exceed M_j, and if the latent never fires at the final position the probe implies ρ = 0.

**Experiments.** Four model-SAE pairs: GPT-2 with a 24,576-latent residual SAE at block 6, Gemma-2-2B (Gemma Scope, layer 12, width 16,384, L0 41), Gemma-2-9B (layer 22, width 131,072, L0 32), and Llama-3.1-8B (Llama Scope, layer 16, 32,768 latents). Eleven tasks (factual recall: capitals, languages, birthplaces, occupations; lexical: antonyms, last letter; in-context: few-shot induction, IOI; arithmetic: addition, two-digit subtraction) yield 8,828 prompts split into a selection split (7,063) fixing latents, tests and edits, and an evaluation split (1,755) supplying all estimates. FDFI (Gaussian-transport disentangled feature importance with Benjamini-Hochberg q = 0.05) selects 50/244/481/24 direction-task pairs per model. Analyses distinguish prespecified vs post hoc; inference uses many-strata CLT intervals (Proposition C.1, with a coverage simulation) and percentile bootstrap over strata/tasks. Total compute: at most ~2 GPU-hours per model and analysis on NVIDIA A100 80GB.

## Results & Highlights

- **The gap is tens-to-hundreds of percent of the coefficient.** Across the 756 FDFI-selected direction-task pairs (46 GPT-2 / 230 Gemma-2-2B / 457 Gemma-2-9B / 23 Llama-3.1-8B), the median |α/β| is 4.6% [3.0, 5.8], 3.2% [2.2, 3.6], 2.8% [1.9, 3.5] and 3.2% [2.2, 5.1]; |α/β| < 0.1 for 74–83% of pairs. In a post hoc descriptive check, probe coefficients exceed realized small-step steering quotients (|T/β| median 7.0×10⁻³ to 2.9×10⁻³) — roughly 130–400× the steering effect — and take the opposite sign for 21–40% of pairs.
- **Attribution predicts realized interventions; the probe does not.** On single-latent zero-ablations (1,795 / 10,727 / 29,180 / 1,792 prompt-latent rows), the prompt-specific gradient prediction matches the realized effect's sign in 92%, 91%, 93% and 97% of rows versus only 21%, 22%, 17% and 35% for the probe-based prediction, with MAE gains over the probe of 0.303 [0.037, 0.799] (GPT-2), 0.119 [0.078, 0.167] (Gemma-2-2B), 0.114 [0.064, 0.171] (Gemma-2-9B) and 0.300 [0.114, 0.611] (Llama-3.1-8B). For steering of 380 coefficient-selected directions, the attribution is closer to the realized central-difference quotient than β for 380/380 pairs, median |T/α| = 1.000, and the prespecified contrast Gain_m = 0.345 [0.267, 0.487].
- **The gap is not sampling noise.** Per-pair BH tests at q = 0.05 reject a zero gap for 227/756 FDFI-selected and 177/380 coefficient-selected pairs; a noise-only simulation would place the median |α/β| between 0.067 and 0.226, with all 20,000 simulated medians exceeding the observed 0.028–0.046.
- **Selection changes.** Within FDFI candidates, |α| ranks first the latent with the largest realized steering response in 28 of 28 model-task units at one within-stratum SD (FDFI statistic: 4; |β|: 2; uniform random: 3.31). Held-out set ablations: attribution-selected sets (up to 10 latents per task) produce larger absolute mean effects than correlation-, rank-test- and coefficient-selected sets in every model-task unit with two-stage intervals excluding zero; versus FDFI selection the contrast is positive on three of four models but its interval includes zero.
- **Why the probe fails:** 37.0%/29.6%/28.7% of FDFI pairs on GPT-2/Gemma-2-2B/Gemma-2-9B have non-negligible attribution but ρ ≈ 0 (|ρ| ≤ 10⁻³ < |α|) under zero-ablation — almost all these latents never fire at the final position, the only one the probe reads, so the whole attribution sits at unread positions (omitted-variable term). The covariance term C_j exceeds the mean term M_j for 52%/34%/39%/17% of estimates. Worked example: on Gemma-2-9B ("In Turkey, people commonly speak", p(Turkish) = 0.349), FDFI's top latent 102779 moves p to 0.333 while the 23rd-ranked latent 99439 moves it to 0.403 with a larger edit norm (124.24E vs 97.00E) — a single backward pass anticipates both.

## Authors & Credibility

Yifeng Guo and Xiang Chen are at St. Jude Children's Research Hospital; Yuaran Li is at Columbia University. The team shows strong mathematical statistics craftsmanship (Stein identities, Manski partial identification, many-strata CLT with Lyapunov conditions, split-sample inference à la Cox/Rinaldo et al.) combined with working mechanistic-interpretability practice (Gemma Scope / Llama Scope / FDFI pipelines). Two authors (Guo, Chen) co-authored the FDFI paper (ICLR 2026) whose selection method they now stress-test — a credibility plus, since they are subjecting their own tool to the identification critique. The paper is unusually disciplined about prespecification: every analysis is labeled prespecified or post hoc, and the analysis specifications are stated to be released with the code.

## Critical Assessment

The core identification point is genuinely valuable but should be sized honestly: Proposition 1 is an off-manifold argument in the spirit of Manski partial identification and Anders et al. (2020)'s off-manifold, and Theorem 1 is essentially a careful assembly of classical regression identities (Stein/Brillinger-Stoker, Goldberger omitted-variable bias, Haufe et al. pattern distortion) — the novelty lies in the exact composition and in *measuring* which terms dominate in SAE pipelines, not in new statistical machinery. The empirical scope is narrow: one SAE at one layer per model, models up to only 9B, and eleven small hand-crafted tasks (capitals, antonyms, 2-digit subtraction) with teacher-forced log-prob targets; nothing here tests realistic interpretability workloads such as refusal, sycophancy, or multi-mechanism behaviors. The Llama-3.1-8B results rest on only 23 direction-task pairs (flagged with an asterisk), the weakest leg of Table 2, and the headline "91–97% vs 17–35% sign agreement" is partly mechanical — the probe structurally predicts zero change for the 15/67, 130/131, 130/131 and 1 of 1 rows whose latents never fire at the final position, so the comparison, while real, flatters the gradient predictor by construction of Corollary 1's failure mode. Several key analyses are post hoc and descriptive (steering comparisons, set-selection contrasts, the worked example), because σ_ij is estimated on the evaluation split; the authors admit the FDFI-vs-attribution set contrast is not distinguished under task resampling (interval includes zero). Statistical honesty has a cost: the coverage simulation shows the Stein-residual intervals cover only 0.820–0.835 at nominal 95% under sparse JumpReLU codes at their actual stratum sizes, so some reported intervals understate uncertainty. Finally, attribution itself requires white-box gradient access — the paper's remedy is unavailable for API-only models, and it is strictly first-order: at five maximum activations the approximation breaks down for most pairs (breakdown fractions 0.389–0.600).

## Strengths

- Exact, non-asymptotic theory: the decomposition (5) is an identity, not an approximation, and its three terms map cleanly onto concrete SAE failure modes (probe-blindness to unread positions, pattern-vs-edit mismatch, prompt-varying edits under ablation).
- Rigorous split design: selection/evaluation splits, stratum-level inference with proven many-strata CLT, coverage simulations, and Benjamini-Hochberg control — rare statistical discipline for interpretability work.
- Highly actionable: the fix costs one backward pass per prompt and ~2 GPU-hours per model/analysis; the worked example (FDFI rank 1 vs rank 23) makes the practical stakes vivid.
- Transparent reporting: prespecified vs post hoc labels on every analysis, asterisks for <30 pairs, and frank admission where intervals include zero or coverage is short.
- The second-dictionary replication (alternate SAEs at the same hook, median |α/β| ≤ 9.51×10⁻³ on three eligible models) shows the gap is a property of the probe, not of one dictionary.

## Limitations

- Narrow experimental scope: one layer and one SAE per model, ≤9B models, 11 toy-style tasks with fixed short answers; no naturalistic behaviors or larger frontier models.
- Llama-3.1-8B statistics are thin (23 pairs, marked "*"); several comparisons are post hoc and descriptive rather than inferential.
- Stein-residual intervals undercover at the observed stratum sizes (0.820–0.835 vs nominal 95% under JumpReLU-code simulation), and bootstrap intervals for medians/shares lack coverage guarantees (stated openly).
- First-order attribution only: accuracy degrades at large steps (breakdown at 5× maximum activation for most pairs) and downstream nonlinearities can make local attribution misleading; inference conditions on independence across strata, which fixed-template clusters might violate.
- The remedy needs gradient access to the model and fixed strata; nothing is said about features whose behavior is inherently distributed across many latents or about attribution *errors* of the SAE reconstruction itself (the SAE error term is kept throughout by assumption).

## Relevance

对做 SAE/机制可解释性研究的人这是一篇必读的"方法论纠偏"文章：它量化证明了用相关性/探针分数挑特征后直接做 steering 或 ablation 的常见做法在统计上站不住，并给出每个 prompt 一次反向传播的替代方案，直接影响特征选择、causal 置信声明和解释性实验设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the gap persist for behaviors with distributed, multi-latent mechanisms (refusal, in-context learning) and for models at 70B+ scale, where strata per task may be too small for the many-strata inference?
- Can attribution be replaced by a query-only or second-order correction (e.g., the cited anytime-valid confidence sequences under adaptive intervention sampling) for API-only models where gradients are unavailable?
- How large is the second-order remainder in practice relative to α across realistic steering magnitudes — can the Zhang & Wang-style second-order corrections be integrated with the s_w + c − e decomposition?
- Does selecting features by attribution instead of discovery scores improve downstream applications (unlearning, capability removal, circuit-level edits), and at what compute overhead at scale?
- What fraction of the omitted-variable term survives when probes read all positions (position averaging removes blindness but the paper still finds median |α/ρ| of 0.056–0.298 and 16–54% sign disagreement) — is a multi-position linear probe ever sufficient?
