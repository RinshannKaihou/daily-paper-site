Solver-trained neural PDE surrogates imitate the solver’s discretization error, not the physics — and held-out benchmarks cannot tell the two apart.

The Symbol of the Surrogate: Measuring Numerical Provenance in Neural PDE Solvers
Ridham Patel (IIT Gandhinagar) · AI for Science Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026

>99.8%
of the analytic full-imitation ceiling recovered by twin-scheme differencing, with provenance coefficient α = 1.000 ± 0.000 — the learned operator is its training scheme, almost exactly.
Why this matters

Low error on solver-generated held-out data proves you learned the solver, not the physics — this cheap, gradient-free probe tells which, before you trust a benchmark score.

Critical edge

The clean ceiling recovery lives in a tailor-made regime — linear dynamics, exactly orthogonal scheme errors, σ = ½ — and the motivating PDEBench / PDEArena corpora are never actually probed.

01Methodology — how the probe works

  1. Probe

    Excite each resolved wavenumber with a single mode u = ε·cos(2πkx/L), ε = 10⁻²; one forward pass of the one-step map, read the DFT ratio Ĝθ(k). No gradients, no model internals. Linearity certified over ε ∈ [10⁻⁴,10⁻¹]; analytic solver symbols recovered to 2×10⁻¹⁶.

  2. Project

    In the complex plane rotated by the exact symbol e−iσφ, the learned error is projected onto the training scheme’s von Neumann displacement: α = 1 full imitation, α = 0 exact physics, α < 0 emulator superiority.

  3. Cancel the confound

    Architectural spectral bias B(k) points radially — the same direction as dissipative scheme error — so single-scheme α is inflated. Two identical networks are trained on upwind (pure dissipation, radial) and Crank–Nicolson (pure dispersion, tangential); B(k) cancels in the difference, and the analytic symbols give a computable full-imitation ceiling.

  4. Verify

    FNO capacity control (fits exact evolution to MSE 3.6×10⁻⁸), nonlinear forced-Burgers operator fingerprints with a common-input control, and fine-tuning on 25–4000 exact samples read through α rather than the loss.

Capacity is not the explanation. Trained on exact data the FNO fits the evolution (MSE 3.6×10⁻⁸) while the CNN cannot (3.3×10⁻³) — yet both mimic the scheme when trained on solver data. Same probe returns α ≈ 1 when the target is a scheme, α ≈ 0 when it is exact.

Provenance ≠ accuracy. 25 exact samples (<1% of the corpus) flip α from 1.000 to ≈0 in one step while error vs the original scheme rises from 1.4×10⁻⁷ to 1.0×10⁻³ — a target switch, not a blend; MSE still saturates at an architecture-dependent floor (≈3.5×10⁻³).

Rollout is a separate distortion. Run to steady state, all surrogates collapse onto one over-smoothed spectrum (high-k slopes −7.4..−7.9 vs solver −5.1..−6.7) — the scheme signature lives at the operator level, not in attractor statistics.

Strengths

  • A new measurement question: not “how accurate is the surrogate” but “which reference is the learned operator near” — with a cheap instrument, one forward pass per wavenumber.
  • Elegant identification: orthogonal error geometry of upwind vs Crank–Nicolson plus an analytically computable ceiling turns a qualitative claim into a percentage.
  • Mutually reinforcing controls: FNO capacity check, common-input Burgers control, restricted-capacity runs, rollout separation, fine-tuning trajectories read through α.
  • Instrumentation hygiene: linearity certified over four orders of magnitude, analytic verification to 2×10⁻¹⁶, and two silent numerical bugs candidly documented (Appendix M).

Limitations

  • Narrow scope: 1-D periodic advection + one Burgers system, two architectures, three seeds — no 2-D turbulence, and PDEBench / PDEArena are never actually probed.
  • Engineered headline regime: the >99.8% claim needs exactly orthogonal scheme errors at σ = ½; separation degrades elsewhere and mixed-error schemes are untested.
  • Indirect identification: B(k) scheme-independence is verified only via cross-architecture agreement; direct decomposition is impossible because B(k) is target-dependent.
  • Nonlinear caveats: the probe is a frozen-coefficient linearization; α > 1 readings are extrapolations, and the α < 0 emulator-superiority region is essentially unexplored.
  • No public code release, and the practical de-contamination recipe beyond 1-D fine-tuning is left open.

03Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the twin-scheme diagnostic survive 2-D turbulence and real PDEBench / PDEArena corpora, where scheme errors are neither purely radial nor tangential?
  2. Can the α < 0 emulator-superiority regime be detected reliably at operator level, e.g. in closure-learning settings where solver inheritance is already documented?
  3. Is there a training objective that directly penalizes provenance (projected ‖Ĝθ − Gexact‖) rather than just adding exact data — and does it avoid the architecture-dependent MSE floor?
  4. Can a scheme-independent probe of B(k) be designed, so twin corpora no longer need matched spectra?
  5. Is there a rollout-level statistic that separates scheme signature from over-smoothing instead of collapsing onto the scheme-independent component?
