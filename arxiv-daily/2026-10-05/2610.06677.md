> **一句话总结：** 在只换路由概率映射、其余完全对齐的 300M/1B top-2 MoE 语言模型对照实验中，稀疏映射的稀疏性在训练后仅部分保留：1.5-entmax 比 softmax 少丢弃 30% 概率质量（δ̄=0.462 vs 0.661）且几乎从不去掉已选专家（贡献专家数=2.000），sparsemax 保留最多质量（δ̄=0.153），normmax-2 把 21.3% 的 token 路由给单个专家；四种映射验证损失相差 ≤0.012 nats，但 sparsemax 模型从 K=2 改到 K=8 推理仅损失 0.02 nats（softmax 损失 0.58），证明自适应 MoE 路由必须围绕"映射+路由器学到的分数分布"联合设计，而非只看映射本身。

> **TL;DR:** The authors train matched 300M and 1B top-2 MoE language models on DCLM (10B tokens) that differ only in the router's probability map — softmax vs 1.5-entmax vs sparsemax vs normmax-2 — and ask whether the maps' ability to emit exact zeros survives training. It survives only partially: at 1B, 1.5-entmax discards 30% less probability mass than softmax (δ̄=0.462 vs 0.661) while keeping both selected experts, sparsemax retains the most mass (δ̄=0.153), and normmax-2 routes 21.3% of tokens to a single expert. No sparse map beats softmax on DCLM validation loss (3.240–3.244 vs 3.232), but sparsemax loses only 0.02 nats (vs 0.58 for softmax) when a K=2-trained model is re-run at K=8. The mechanism: each map drops an expert when the top-2 score gap crosses a fixed threshold (2 for 1.5-entmax, 1 for sparsemax/normmax-2), and the routers co-adapt their learned score spreads to that threshold — so participation is set jointly by map and learned scores, not by the map alone.

## 核心贡献

- **受控对照研究**：在 300M 与 1B 两个规模上训练仅路由映射不同（softmax / 1.5-entmax / sparsemax / normmax-2）、数据与超参完全一致的 top-2 MoE 语言模型，系统测量保留质量（retention）、参与专家数（participation）、负载均衡与预测质量，并记录每个训练后路由器的分数分布。
- **分离"保留"与"参与"两个量**：top-2 下，只有当支撑集大小 s≥3 时才会丢质量，只有 s=1 时参与数才会低于 2——训练后的路由器落在不同区域：1.5-entmax 几乎不丢专家（99.9% 的状态 s≥3），normmax-2 有 21.3% 单专家状态，而 sparsemax 两者兼得最少质量损失。
- **机制解释：阈值 + 学到的分数分布**：每种映射在 top-2 分差 g 达到固定阈值时产生单专家路由（1.5-entmax 为 g≥2，sparsemax 与 normmax-2 共享 g≥1）。稀疏路由器学到的分数离散度约为 softmax 的一半（RMS 0.32–0.38 vs 0.86）：entmax 因此把 99% 分位分差压到 1.45（低于阈值 2）；sparsemax 与 normmax-2 阈值相同却学到不同分差分布（中位数 0.40 vs 0.61），参与率因此不同。
- **推理预算鲁棒性**：K=2 训练的模型在 K=8 冻结权重推理时，sparsemax 仅损失 0.02 nats、normmax-2 损失 0.064、1.5-entmax 损失 0.250，softmax 损失 0.582；但所有映射在 K=1 时都损失约 0.10 nats——鲁棒性是单侧的，且损失都在训练预算处最低。
- **设计启示**：保留、参与、预测质量是三个互相独立的量；路由器会把分数尺度共适应到映射上，因此稀疏映射应被"围绕其设计"（如显式控制分数尺度、按支撑集而非固定 top-K 选择专家），而不是"换上去就行"。

## Key Contributions

- A controlled study of sparse maps as MoE routers: matched 300M and 1B top-2 MoE language models that differ only in the probability map (softmax, 1.5-entmax, sparsemax, normmax-2), measuring retention, participation, load balance, and predictive quality, plus the router scores of every trained model.
- Retention and participation are shown to be distinct outcomes under top-2 selection: mass is discarded only when more than two experts have positive probability (s≥3), while participation drops only on singleton supports (s=1). Trained routers land in different regimes — 1.5-entmax discards 30% less mass than softmax yet keeps both experts, normmax-2 uses the fewest contributors.
- A mechanism showing the map's threshold and the learned score distribution jointly set participation: each map drops a selected expert exactly when the top-2 gap reaches a fixed threshold (2 for 1.5-entmax; 1 for sparsemax and normmax-2, which share it yet differ in behavior because their routers learn different gap distributions).
- Evidence that sparse maps buy robustness to the inference-time expert budget rather than quality: all maps are within 0.012 nats of softmax in validation loss, but a sparsemax model trained at K=2 and run at K=8 loses only 0.02 nats where softmax loses 0.58.
- A dense-auxiliary balancing ablation (300M) showing that computing the Switch-style balancing loss on softmax(z) does not improve load balance and moves per-token participation and load balance in opposite directions, supporting the explanation that zero-probability experts receive no direct balancing gradient under a sparse map.

## Methodology

**Models and training.** Llama-3-style backbone where every second feed-forward block is an MoE layer. The 300M model: 8 layers, width 320, 4 MoE layers with 26 experts each; the 1B model: 9 layers, width 768, 4 MoE layers with 27 experts each (302M active parameters). Every token selects K=2 experts (Mixtral-style selection and weighted combination) with no capacity constraints and no token dropping. The four maps share everything except π, including the Switch-style balancing objective (Equation 5, coefficient 0.01, detached selection frequencies), evaluated at local minibatches. A dense reference with a larger active-parameter budget is trained at each scale. All models are trained on the DCLM baseline corpus for 10B tokens (19,073 updates of 524,288 tokens) with AdamW, cosine decay, and bfloat16 mixed precision.

**Probability maps.** All maps are regularized argmax transformations differing only in the entropy H: Shannon entropy gives softmax (dense); Tsallis entropy with α=1.5 gives 1.5-entmax (exact-charge fast algorithm); α=2 gives sparsemax (Euclidean projection onto the simplex); norm entropy with γ=2 gives normmax-2, which is also sparse but favors near-uniform distributions on its support. All are differentiable almost everywhere and trained by ordinary backpropagation. Each map has a fixed singleton threshold on the top-2 score gap g: g≥2 for 1.5-entmax, g≥1 for sparsemax and normmax-2; softmax has no finite threshold.

**Routing measurements.** Discarded mass δ_i = mass of unselected experts (equal to total-variation distance between the routing distribution and its selected mixture); number of contributors K_{+,i} = |T_i| ≤ K (selected experts with positive weight; zero-weight selected experts are "fillers"); coefficient of variation of expert loads under both nominal (every selection) and contributor (positive-weight selections only) conventions.

**Evaluation.** Predictive quality: DCLM validation loss, LAMBADA perplexity/accuracy, and mean accuracy over the eight OLMES tasks (ARC-Easy/Challenge, CommonsenseQA, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, PIQA, SocialIQA, Winogrande), using the higher-accuracy of multiple-choice and completion formulations. Routing diagnostics: 61 documents from the WikiText-103 test subset of Paloma at context 2,048, giving 285,107 targets per MoE layer, averaged with equal weight over the four MoE layers. An inference-budget experiment re-runs each K=2-trained model with frozen weights at K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}.

## Results & Highlights

- **Model quality is statistically flat across maps.** On DCLM validation (1B, 10B tokens), softmax reaches 3.232 nats vs 3.240 (1.5-entmax), 3.242 (sparsemax), and 3.244 (normmax-2) — margins of 0.008–0.012 nats; all four MoEs sit within 0.14 nats of the dense reference (3.103) which has 3.3× the active parameters. Mean OLMES accuracy: softmax 42.8 vs 42.0/42.0/42.1 for the sparse maps and 45.0 dense; individual task leaders vary (normmax-2 leads ARC-Easy and PIQA; sparsemax leads SocialIQA and Winogrande), with no consistent sparse-map advantage.
- **Retention and participation diverge across maps (1B routing diagnostics, 285,107 targets/layer on WikiText-103/Paloma).** 1.5-entmax discards δ̄=0.462 vs softmax's 0.661 (30% less discarded mass) with a contributor count of 2.000 to three decimals; sparsemax retains the most mass (δ̄=0.153 vs softmax 0.661); normmax-2 uses the fewest contributors (K̄₊=1.787) with 21.3% of token-layer states routed to a single expert, versus only 6.9% for sparsemax, which instead has more exactly-two-expert states (14.1% vs 7.6%).
- **The learned scores matter as much as the map.** The three sparse routers learn score spreads with RMS between 0.32 and 0.38 versus 0.86 for the softmax router (1B). The 1.5-entmax router's 99th-percentile gap is 1.45, below its threshold of 2, so it produces singletons in only 0.05% of states even though 7.8% of the softmax router's gaps reach that threshold; softmax gaps reach g≥1 in 44.76% of states. Sparsemax and normmax-2 share threshold 1 yet their routers learn median gaps of 0.40 vs 0.61, so normmax-2's gaps reach 1 three times as often (21.26% vs 6.88%).
- **Robustness to the inference-time budget (Table 3, NLL on the DCLM diagnostic sample, trained at K=2).** At K=8, softmax loses 0.58 nats (3.254→3.835) because it must spread weight over six experts it never learned to combine; sparsemax loses only 0.02 nats (3.263→3.284, Δ=+0.021), normmax-2 +0.064, and 1.5-entmax +0.250 — sparsemax keeps assigning zero weight to most extra slots and uses 4.3 experts on average at K=8. The robustness is one-sided: reducing the budget to K=1 costs every map about 0.10 nats, and every map's loss is lowest at its trained budget.
- **Strong depth dependence in participation.** Normmax-2 routes 54% of tokens to a single expert in the first MoE layer but only 7% in the last; sparsemax shows the same pattern at 19%→4%; 1.5-entmax has essentially no singletons anywhere — early layers learn the sparsest routers.
- **Dense-auxiliary balancing ablation (300M) does not improve balance.** Using softmax(z) only in the auxiliary loss raises 1.5-entmax's nominal/contributor CV from 0.400 to 0.485 and its centered score RMS from 0.517 to 0.603; sparsemax's contributor CV rises from 0.450 to 0.668. Per-token participation and load balance move in opposite directions, supporting the "zero-probability experts get no balancing gradient" account of sparsemax's imbalance.

## Critical Assessment

The controlled design is the paper's main asset, but several specifics temper the headline claims. First, the flagship "30% less discarded mass" is a routing diagnostic, not a quality gain: 1.5-entmax's δ̄=0.462 versus softmax's 0.661 coexists with slightly worse validation loss (3.240 vs 3.232) — the paper is honest that retention, participation, and quality are three distinct quantities, but a reader skimming the abstract could mistake retention for a benefit. Second, the quality comparison is weakly powered: a single training seed per configuration means differences of 0.008–0.012 nats (the entire spread between maps) cannot establish any ranking, which the authors concede in Limitations — so "no sparse map improves validation loss" is really "no detectable difference at one seed." Third, the practical value of the budget-robustness result is under-argued: since every map's loss is lowest at its trained budget and K=8 on a K=2-trained model is an unusual deployment, the sparsemax +0.021 vs softmax +0.582 delta is a clean scientific finding whose engineering payoff (e.g., budget-adaptive serving) is asserted only in the outlook. Fourth, the scale is modest — at most 1B parameters, 10B tokens, 27 experts, and explicitly no capacity constraints or token dropping — so transfer to production MoE regimes (where dispatch implementations may or may not skip zero-weight fillers, which the authors note they do not compute) is untested. Finally, the score-scale story is admittedly descriptive: the paper shows sparse routers learn half the spread of softmax but does not identify the training mechanism that shrinks it (the dense-auxiliary ablation moves RMS only from 0.517 to 0.603 for entmax, leaving most of the gap unexplained). On the positive side, the Limitations section is unusually candid, flagging the single seed, the single balancing objective, unverified disjointness of diagnostic samples from training data, and the descriptive nature of the score-scale analysis.

## Strengths

- Genuinely controlled comparison: identical data, seed, hyperparameters, and balancing objective across all four maps at two scales, isolating the causal effect of the probability map.
- Sharp mechanistic insight — the singleton-threshold analysis (g≥2 for 1.5-entmax, g≥1 for sparsemax/normmax-2) cleanly separates what the map fixes from what the learned scores determine, including the elegant natural experiment that sparsemax and normmax-2 share a threshold yet diverge in behavior.
- Clarifying conceptual contribution: retention, participation, and predictive quality are demonstrated to be three distinct, separately-tunable quantities.
- The depth-dependence finding (54%→7% singletons across layers for normmax-2) is a novel, non-obvious observation relevant to per-layer K design.
- The dense-auxiliary balancing ablation directly tests a mechanistic hypothesis rather than leaving it as speculation.
- Strong reproducibility posture: complete recipe in appendices, code and configs promised for every run, and every table/figure regenerable.

## Limitations

- Single training seed per configuration: quality differences of ≤0.012 nats cannot support any ranking claim between maps.
- Small scale relative to production MoE (≤1B active parameters, 10B tokens, ≤27 experts) and no capacity constraints or token dropping, which real systems rely on.
- Only one balancing objective (Switch-style) in the main grid, varied in a single 300M ablation; only one normmax variant (γ=2) and two entmax variants (α=1.5, sparsemax) are tested.
- Contributor counts measure expert dispatch, not compute — whether zero-weight fillers are actually skipped depends on the dispatch implementation and is not measured.
- Routing diagnostics were not verified to be disjoint from the training data.
- The score-scale analysis is descriptive: it relates singleton rates to learned gap distributions but does not identify the training mechanism producing the smaller spreads.
- The inference-budget robustness result has no demonstrated practical use case; loss is lowest at the trained budget for every map.

## Relevance

该工作与你对 LLM 效率与 MoE 稀疏路由机制的兴趣高度相关：它用严格的对照实验回答了"稀疏概率映射在训练后是否仍然稀疏"这一基础问题，并给出可直接指导自适应路由设计的阈值-分数共适应机制，对评估或设计基于 sparsemax/entmax 的 MoE 路由器是必读的实证参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the score-spread co-adaptation (sparse routers learning ~half the softmax RMS) persist at production scale (tens of billions of parameters) and under capacity constraints with token dropping?
- Can the router's score scale be explicitly controlled (e.g., a learned per-layer temperature as in sparse attention) to decouple participation from the map's fixed threshold — and would that let one map emulate another's participation profile?
- Since sparse maps already assign zero weight to most extra slots at K=8, would selecting experts on the support (rather than a fixed top-K) capture the budget robustness without paying the trained-budget penalty?
- How do alternative balancing objectives (φ-balancing, balanced assignment, loss-free bias adjustment) interact with sparse maps' missing balancing gradients for zero-probability experts?
- What is the training-dynamics mechanism behind the depth dependence — why do early MoE layers learn the sparsest routers, and is coarse early routing functionally necessary?
