Why this matters

A near-free, inference-invisible knob that buys low-resource languages more expert capacity — a practical third route beyond data upsampling and expert scaling for multilingual MoE.

⚠ Critical edge

Every headline number is validation loss at ≤10.6B / 10B-token scale; downstream benchmarks are underpowered and free-running generation degenerate — transfer to real model quality stays unproven.

01 Method COAX

Borrow the per-expert bias from auxiliary-loss-free load balancing (Wang et al., 2024), but condition it on the input category instead of expert underuse — the delta is category-conditioning; the contribution is empirical and analytical.

token · category c→ router scores si→ select: si + σ·1[i∈Pc] → Top-K→ weight: unbiased si

Bias touches selection only — a soft prior; any expert stays reachable if learned scores outweigh σ (default 0.03). Removed at inference → standard top-K MoE, no category labels.

  • Capacity knobs: preferred share qc = Mc/N and relative share rc = qc/pc — Swahili (0.85% of tokens) is biased toward 4/128 experts (rc ≈ 3.7); K fixed, per-token compute unchanged.
  • Subsets Pc are mutually exclusive by default; bias applies at every MoE layer, pretraining only.
  • Testbed: 345M-A147M / 3B-A654M / 10.6B-A1.2B (64/128/256 experts, top-8), 10B tokens each on 64×GH200; multilingual, multimodal, multi-domain mixes; matched seeds with paired-difference 90/95% CIs; baselines: DEMix-style hard routing, EDL.

—

EDL regresses every language; DEMix regresses +251% if its hard constraint is dropped at evaluation. EDL overhead not reported.

  • Multimodal (345M): all six subsets improve (text −0.88%, captioning −0.76%, interleaved −0.65%…); ASR top-5 word accuracy 47.50% → 49.38%.
  • Real localization: removing Swahili’s 4 designated experts raises its loss +107.1% vs +9.3% for the baseline’s most-used experts; they carry 12.15% of Swahili traffic vs 3.12% uniform.
  • Ablations: peak at σ=0.03; σ=0.3 without inference bias → +19.32% (annealed cooldown recovers 76.4%); coarse English/non-English grouping matches per-language at 1.3% overhead; gains persist undecayed to 100B tokens; stacks with upsampling (−3.738% vs −3.579%).

03 Strengths

  • Explicit, interpretable knob (rc) decouples expert-capacity allocation from data prevalence without touching per-token compute.
  • Rigorous evaluation culture: matched seeds, paired-difference CIs, 3–5 seeds per config, a 100B-token run refuting the under-training artifact, A/B/A/B throughput protocol.
  • Ablations actually locate the mechanism — coarse-grouping sufficiency, majority-bias necessity (−0.286% → −0.131% without it), lesion tests, router-entropy analysis.
  • Honest negative space: Khmer regressions, EDL comparisons, the compute confound, and underpowered benchmarks are disclosed, not buried.
  • Deployment-friendly: standard top-K at inference, no category labels, 1.3–2.4% overhead at 10.6B; usable for steering new capacity in continual pretraining.

04 Limitations

  • Primary evidence is held-out validation loss at ≤10.6B / ≤100B-token scale; downstream benchmarks underpowered, free-running generation degenerate — real-quality transfer unproven.
  • Absolute gains are modest (−0.286% mean minority loss; ≈2.7% token-equivalence) and shrink with scale — frontier-scale benefit is extrapolative.
  • Multi-domain breaks the “remove at inference” story: math/legal/medical gains require keeping the bias (and category labels); only code persists without it.
  • Khmer regresses in every 10.6B run with only a post-hoc tokenization explanation — at-risk categories can’t be predicted or protected.
  • Expert subsets and per-category rc are hand-set from data statistics, not learned; no code release; replication needs 64 GH200s × multi-seed × three scales.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Does the benefit survive frontier scale (≥100B params, ≥1T tokens), given gains already shrink from 345M to 10.6B?
  • Can rc be learned or auto-allocated — and can at-risk categories like Khmer be flagged before training?
  • Why do domain gains (math/legal/medical) need the inference-time bias while language/modality gains don’t?
  • Would pairing COAX with the auxiliary-loss-free scheme it borrows from change the picture, given stronger balancing suppresses its gains?
  • Can per-category σ-annealing during cooldown (recovers 76.4% of the with-bias gain) be tuned, and does it compound with expert-parallel topology?
