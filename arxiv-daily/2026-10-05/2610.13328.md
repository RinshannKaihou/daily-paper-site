> **一句话总结：** 针对 MoE 推理中"专家权重精度被计费两次"（驻留存储对所有可路由专家付费 vs 批大小为 1 的解码只对被选中专家付费）导致的 store–read gap（已发布的 BitSMoE 2-bit 检查点存 2.54 bpw 却在解码时读 7.50 bpw），提出 DuoRATE：离线一次性存储可前缀读取（StagQ）的统一 p4 权重流并烘焙失真张量，线上每个工作负载用自身无标签 prefill、以每专家一次点积 + 共享乘子的二分法求解解码端拉格朗日率失真分配（路由频率可证明地从逐专家决策中消去）；在 Qwen3-30B-A3B 上，读取 2.3 bpw 时平均准确率超出 BitSMoE 6.3 分（73.63 vs 67.32），读取 2.9 bpw 时追平多读 1.4–1.6 bpw 的 4-bit 格式（GSM8K 91.00，距 BF16 仅 0.25 分）。

> **TL;DR:** MoE serving pays expert precision twice: as resident footprint (every routable expert) and as batch-one decode traffic (only the selected experts), but published per-expert allocators constrain only the first — the released BitSMoE 2-bit checkpoint stores 2.5425 bpw yet reads 7.5008 bpw at decode. DuoRATE stores one uniform p4 tier (4.2704 bpw) of a prefix-readable weight stream once, then per workload solves a Lagrangian rate-distortion allocation over the decode functional — routing frequency provably cancels from every per-expert decision — costing one dot product per expert plus a CPU bisection, yielding a read table of per-expert byte-prefix tiers. On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, DuoRATE reading 2.3 bpw beats the strongest 2-bit baseline BitSMoE by +6.31 avg points (73.63 vs 67.32 on MMLU-Pro/MMLU/GSM8K, with WikiText-2 perplexity 9.97 vs 14.69) while reading 3.3× less per decoded token, and at 2.9 bpw it matches 4-bit formats that read 1.4–1.6 bpw more (76.16 avg vs HiF4's 76.82), hitting GSM8K 91.00 within 0.25 points of BF16.

## 核心贡献

- **双速率形式化与 store–read gap 的量化：** 将 MoE 权重精度显式拆成两个速率泛函——驻留速率 R_Store（所有专家的无加权均值）与路由加权解码速率 R_Read——并证明只约束存储会把检查点的 6.29× 驻留足迹削减变成仅 2.13× 的解码流量削减；BitSMoE 的 2.54 bpw 存储 vs 7.50 bpw 解码读取（2.95×）就是实证。
- **频率消去定理（Proposition 1/Corollary 1）：** 当同一组权重同时出现在目标函数与预算约束中时，路由频率 f_e 从逐专家分配的 arg min 中完全消去——频率无法给两个专家排序，排序只由失真曲线的交换率 Δd/ΔR 决定；并刻画了频率仅存的两个通道（非成比例的位宽项、跨域校准池化）。
- **store-once / read-per-workload 的 DuoRATE 流水线：** Stage 1 离线一次，存储 StagQ 前缀可读流的统一 p4 层（4.2704 bpw）并烘焙每专家每层的失真张量 S（平方权重量化误差 × 输入激活能量 × 输出"决策翻转"敏感性）；Stage 2 每工作负载在线一次，用无标签 prefill 统计以每专家每层一次点积打分，单一共享乘子 λ 二分至读取预算（误差 <0.001 bpw，CPU 数秒）；部署期单一工件两个速率，换工作负载只需数 KB 的层索引表、无需重新编码权重。
- **在 Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base 上的实证：** 2.3 bpw 读取即全面超越 2-bit 类方法（对 BitSMoE +6.43 MMLU-Pro / +2.25 MMLU / +13.00 GSM8K、困惑度低 32%、解码流量省 3.3×）；2.9 bpw 追平多读 1.4–1.6 bpw 的 4-bit 格式；同格式对照组（随机相邻层混合、插值零假设、统一层）配对符号检验隔离出分配本身值 0.07–1.35% 的困惑度。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes MoE weight precision as two rate functionals — the resident (stored) rate and the routing-weighted decode read rate — and shows on the released BitSMoE checkpoint that constraining storage alone hides a 2.95× store-read gap (2.5425 bpw stored vs 7.5008 bpw read at batch-one decode).
- Proves (Proposition 1) that routing frequency cancels from decode-side per-expert allocation when the same weights appear in objective and budget; frequency cannot reorder experts, deciding only which member of the Lagrangian family meets the budget — and characterizes (Proposition 2) the exactly two channels (non-proportional bit-width term; pooled calibration domains) through which frequency survives.
- Proposes DuoRATE, a store-once, read-per-workload allocator: Stage 1 bakes a distortion tensor (activation-energy × decision-flip-sensitivity weighted squared quantization error) for every tier of a prefix-readable StagQ stream; Stage 2 solves the per-workload read table label-free with one dot product per expert and a bisection on a single shared multiplier, so a new workload costs seconds on a CPU and no new checkpoint.
- Empirically, on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base: outperforms 2-bit-class methods at 2.3 bpw read (by 6.3–47.0 avg-accuracy points), matches/exceeds 4-bit formats at 2.9 bpw on long decoding, and isolates with same-format paired controls that the allocation itself is worth 0.07–1.35% perplexity on WikiText-2, with every rate-matched interval excluding zero.

## Methodology

**Two rate functionals.** With expert e at precision M_e, prefill (batch T large) fetches an expert if any of its tokens routes to it, so prefill traffic amortizes to the unweighted resident footprint: R_Store = (1/E) Σ R(M_e). Batch-one decode (T=1) pays per selected expert, giving the routing-frequency-weighted R_Read = Σ f_e R(M_e) / Σ f_e. A prefix-readable stream (StagQ) makes R_Read ≤ R_Store reachable: tiers p2..p6 cost 2.2704, 3.2704, 4.2704, 5.2704, 6.2704 bpw, each lower tier being a byte prefix of a higher one, so changing an expert's tier needs no re-encoding.

**Frequency cancellation.** For the decode-side problem min Σ f_e d_e(M_e) s.t. Σ f_e R(M_e) ≤ B Σ f_e, every Lagrangian term carries the same positive factor f_e, which cannot move the arg min: the per-expert minimizer M_e(λ) = argmin_M [d_e(M) + λR(M)] depends only on the distortion curves; f_e selects via a single shared λ which family member meets the budget. The authors note the algebra is known (Park et al., 2026a, for Gaussian-mixture sources) — the MoE-specific point is that the mixture weight is precisely the routing frequency that prior allocators treat as an importance prior, which therefore carries no assignment information. Frequency enters only via (i) bit-width terms whose weight is not proportional to f_e (the stored budget), and (ii) pooling of calibration domains (per-domain mixture weights π_d|e).

**Stage 1 (offline, once).** Store the uniform p4 tier (4.2704 bpw) on all 6,144 experts of Qwen3-30B-A3B (48 MoE layers × 128 experts); attention, embeddings and normalization stay BF16. For every expert and tier, bake S_{e,c,M,j} = Σ_i (ΔW^{(M)})² A_{e,x} X_{e,j}: squared byte-prefix weight error scaled on the input side by activation energy X and on the output side by decision-aligned sensitivity A — the expected number of flipped top-two margin decisions, obtained from one offline backward pass pooled over four reasoning/multiple-choice domains. A_j ≡ 1 recovers the activation-weighted squared error of prior allocators.

**Stage 2 (online, per workload, label-free).** Run the model over the workload's own prefill once; record per-channel second moments X_e and routing counts f̂_e. The full distortion table is then one dot product per expert and tier; the read table is the per-expert minimizer of d_e(M) + λR(M) over M ∈ {p2, p3, p4} (capped by the stored p4), with a bisection on the single shared λ meeting the budget to within 0.001 bpw. Ties break toward the lower rate; the Lagrangian sweep is globally optimal because each tier family is convex.

**Serving.** One artifact, two rates: prefill reads the full stored depth (paying R_Store); each decode step reads only the per-expert prefixes of the read table (paying R_Read). Swapping workloads replaces a few kilobytes of tier indices, leaving stored weights untouched; tables can be recalibrated online (no labels, no reference model) when the served domain shifts — Appendix E.3 shows a 150-document code corpus recalibration closing 95.3% of the gap to an offline code-calibrated table (perplexity 4.9849 → 4.8320 vs 4.8245 offline target).

**Evaluation protocol.** Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base; WikiText-2 perplexity (62 docs); MMLU-Pro (100/subj, 5-shot), MMLU (25/subj, 5-shot), GSM8K (first 400, strict-match, 5-shot) as the log-likelihood-scored benchmarks where decode dominates. Baselines: five 4-bit formats on the same matrices (NVFP4, NF4, FP4, MXFP4, HiF4; 4.5 bpw), four 2-bit post-training methods (HQO, GPTQ, MiLo, MoEQuant), and the released BitSMoE 2-bit checkpoint re-scored after verifying the harness reproduces its published numbers. Same-format controls: a random mixture of two adjacent tiers at the same read rate, an interpolated uniform-tier null, and the uniform tiers themselves — compared with exact document sign tests and 20,000-resample percentile bootstraps.

## Results & Highlights

- **Far above the 2-bit class at 2.3 bpw (Table 1).** DuoRATE at 2.3 bpw read reaches 73.63 average accuracy (MMLU-Pro/MMLU/GSM8K) vs 67.32 for BitSMoE — which reads 7.5008 bpw, 3.3× more per decoded token: +6.43 MMLU-Pro (52.64 vs 46.21), +2.25 MMLU (80.00 vs 77.75), +13.00 GSM8K (88.25 vs 78.00), and WikiText-2 perplexity 9.97 vs 14.69 (32% lower). At 2.3 bpw the other 2-bit methods collapse: HQO 37.98, GPTQ 26.62, MiLo 34.93, MoEQuant 57.68 — DuoRATE exceeds them by 6.3 to 47.0 points, though the authors note most of this margin comes from the weight format rather than the allocation.
- **On par with 4-bit formats at 2.9 bpw (Table 1, Figure 3).** DuoRATE at 2.9 bpw scores 76.16 average vs 76.82 for the best 4-bit format HiF4 (−0.66) and vs NF4 76.13 / FP4 75.48, while every 4-bit format reads 4.5 bpw — 1.6 bpw more per activated weight. On GSM8K it scores 91.00, exactly matching HiF4 and within 0.25 of BF16 (91.25); at 2.6 bpw its GSM8K 89.50 still matches or exceeds 4.5-bpw NF4 (89.50) and FP4 (88.25). Under long decoding the realized GSM8K decode traffic is 2.82 bpw at the 2.9 budget and 2.56 at 2.6, vs BitSMoE's 7.50.
- **What the allocation alone is worth (Figure 4, Section 4.2).** Against a random mixture of the two adjacent uniform tiers at the same read rate on WikiText-2, the allocated table wins 1.35% perplexity at 2.6 bpw (48 of 56 documents, p = 4.7×10⁻⁵) and 1.04% at 2.9 bpw (45 of 54, p = 7.3×10⁻⁷); against uniform tiers it wins 1.46%/1.18% at budgeted read and 0.95%/0.92% at the stored rate — all four intervals exclude zero. Against the interpolated null the margin at 2.9 shrinks to 0.07%. Re-solving the table from a disjoint validation split changes perplexity by only 0.20%.
- **Objective ablation vs MC-MoE (Section 4.3, Figure S9).** Substituting MC-MoE's frequency-weighted objective into the same bisection loses 0.42% (2.6 bpw, sign-test p = 0.0032) and 0.61% (2.9 bpw, p = 0.040) WikiText-2 perplexity at matched read rates — but MC-MoE's tables store 0.29 and 0.43 bpw less (2.3729/2.4905 vs 2.6594/2.9167), so neither table dominates.
- **Efficiency accounting (Table 2).** BitSMoE stores 8.58 GiB of expert weights but decodes at 1,698 MB/token; DuoRATE stores 14.41 GiB (uniform p4, 4.2704 bpw) and decodes at 520.9–656.8 MB/token at 2.3–2.9 bpw. Against its own uniform p2 reference (2.2704 bpw stored), DuoRATE moves 3.30× more traffic — the trade the two-rate formulation makes explicit.
- **Negative results reported honestly (Appendix F).** A frequency heuristic promoting the 30% most-routed experts scores 78.87 vs 83.01 for a sensitivity allocator at 3.68 bpw (larger MMLU sample) — worse than not allocating at all, as the shadow-price argument predicts; a rate-structure-only control (allocation histogram with random distortion) loses 6.10% WikiText-2 perplexity at 3.2 bpw, showing the distortion model, not mixed precision per se, drives the gain.

The references show the team's direct lineage: the StagQ prefix-readable stream the allocator runs on is their own concurrent preprint (Wei et al., 2026, arXiv:2610.05977, with an overlapping author set), and they cite their own prior mixed-precision work. ## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The paper is candid that the cancellation algebra of Proposition 1 is not new — it is Park et al. (2026a)'s PrismQuant result transplanted from Gaussian-mixture sources; the MoE-specific contribution is recognizing that the mixture weight *is* the routing frequency prior allocators use as importance. The genuinely new elements are the two-functional framing (store vs read, and the store-read gap audit), the store-once/read-per-workload deployment enabled by the prefix-readable stream, and the decision-aligned distortion functional. This is a systems/operations contribution with a thin but honest theory core.

**Rigor — strong where it matters, but with disclosed quirks.** The same-format control suite (random adjacent-tier mixture, interpolated null, uniform tiers) with paired document sign tests and bootstraps is more careful than typical quantization papers, and the negative-results appendix (frequency heuristic underperforms a sensitivity allocator by 4.14 points; rate-structure-only control loses 6.10% perplexity) demonstrates the claims are falsifiable and were tested. Two quirks: (i) WikiText-2 tables are calibrated on the same 62 documents that are scored — disclosed, and the disjoint-split re-solve shifts perplexity only 0.20%, but it is still calibration-on-test; (ii) GSM8K is scored on the first 400 questions with strict matching and MMLU on 25/subj — small subsamples defended at length in Appendix D (including a finding that the released BitSMoE checkpoint actually stores 2.5425 bpw, not its paper's labeled 2.0, and that their harness-vs-published gaps reduce to few-shot count and split size), yet these remain the authors' harness on their subsample.

**Red flags / overclaim check.** The headline "+6.3 to +47.0 points over 2-bit baselines" is mostly the StagQ format, not the allocator — the paper says so itself ("Most of this margin comes from the weight format rather than from allocation"), and the allocation's isolated effect is at most 1.35% perplexity on one benchmark (WikiText-2), shrinking to 0.07% against the interpolated null at 2.9 bpw. The efficiency numbers (MB/token, bpw read) are derived from safetensors headers and the storage layout — there is no fused prefix-read kernel, so no measured tokens/s, TTFT or TPOT; the serving claim is arithmetic, not benchmarked. The evaluation covers exactly one model (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, 48×128 expert topology); the authors admit extension to other routing schemes is untested. Finally, the deployment doubles resident weight memory vs the 2-bit checkpoint (14.41 vs 8.58 GiB): in memory-bound regimes BitSMoE wins, and DuoRATE's advantage is confined to batch-one/long-decode serving — a trade the paper makes explicit rather than hiding, which is to its credit.

**Significance.** For MoE serving the two-rate framing is a real conceptual cleanup: the demonstration that one artifact can serve many workloads with per-workload CPU-second read tables, and that routing frequency is provably uninformative for decode-side allocation, should change how per-expert quantization papers state their objectives (the Appendix A objective audit of 14 published allocators is a useful contribution in itself). But the practical impact hinges on a kernel that does not yet exist in the paper.

**Reproducibility.** Code promised upon publication; the rate accounting is auditable from released checkpoints (the BitSMoE field-by-field audit is exemplary), the protocol appendices give question counts, seeds and test statistics, but the StagQ stream itself is the authors' concurrent unreleased preprint, so full replication currently requires their artifacts.

## Strengths

- The store/read two-functional decomposition is clean, checkable, and immediately actionable: it explains a 2.95× discrepancy (2.5425 stored vs 7.5008 read bpw) in a released production checkpoint that single-rate papers cannot even express.
- Propositions 1–2 give exact, falsifiable guidance (frequency is irrelevant for decode-side allocation except through two named channels), and the paper then *uses* the theory to design controls and to interpret the negative result of the frequency heuristic.
- Same-format controls with paired sign tests and bootstrap intervals isolate the allocator's causal contribution (0.07–1.35% perplexity) instead of crediting the format — rare honesty in the quantization literature.
- Practical deployment story: a new workload's read table costs one forward pass over its prefill plus CPU seconds; online recalibration closes 95.3% of the domain-shift gap; no re-encoding of weights is ever needed.
- Exceptional diligence toward the external baseline: a field-by-field audit of BitSMoE's released checkpoint, a harness-alignment study separating few-shot/split-size confounds, and a published-objective audit table of 14 prior allocators.
- An explicit negative-results appendix (frequency heuristic, rate-structure-only control, proxy-objective non-effects) that most papers would omit.

## Limitations

- **Single model.** All quantitative results are on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base (48 MoE layers × 128 experts); other routing schemes, granularities and scales are explicitly untested.
- **No end-to-end serving measurement.** Decode traffic and store rates are derived from the storage layout; there is no fused prefix-read kernel, so throughput/latency benefits remain an accounting exercise, and measured prefill ran at the read rate (conservative for quality, but the two-rate serving path itself is unvalidated).
- **Regime-limited benefit.** The read-rate advantage concentrates in batch-one, long-output decoding; as batch grows the routing-read rate saturates to the stored rate, and the resident footprint is 14.41 GiB vs BitSMoE's 8.58 GiB — memory-bound deployments are better served by the 2-bit checkpoint.
- **Thin allocation effect at the top budget.** The allocation-only gain is 1.35%/1.04% perplexity vs the random-mixture null and only 0.07% vs the interpolated null at 2.9 bpw — at the operating point that matches 4-bit formats, the allocator's isolated contribution is near the noise floor.
- **Operational loose ends.** Per-workload calibration must be scheduled when the request mix drifts; the stored-table mixture channel (pooled calibration domains) that could exploit frequency is deferred to future work; code and the StagQ stream are not yet released.

## Relevance

契合"极端性能优化下的可靠性"兴趣区：论文把 MoE 低精度部署从单一存储约束推广为存储/解码双速率泛函，量化误差用决策翻转敏感性（margin 翻转）加权建模并做了配对显著性检验，属于带精度/正确性视角的激进量化优化，而非纯速度收益。对关注推理基础设施（解码带宽、长解码、按工作负载再校准）的读者也有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How large is the store-read gap on other MoE families — e.g. DeepSeek-style models with shared experts, fine-grained experts, or sigmoid routing — and does Proposition 1's cancellation hold unchanged for their surrogate objectives?
- What does a fused prefix-read kernel actually deliver end-to-end (tokens/s, TPOT, TTFT) relative to the derived 520–698 MB/token accounting, and does the 2.95× traffic gap survive real serving with batching and expert caching?
- Does the allocation-only gain (≤1.35% perplexity here) grow on models with more heterogeneous expert sensitivity, or is it bounded by the convex tier family's resolution?
- How should the stored-table mixture channel (cross-domain pooling, π_d|e) be exploited when one artifact serves a mixed request stream, and what recalibration schedule keeps read tables valid under drift?
- Can the decision-aligned distortion (expected flipped decisions) be computed without the offline backward pass, so Stage 1 scales to much larger checkpoints?
