> **一句话总结：** 作者系统追踪了 10–590 个合成样本下激活探针的学习曲线（3 个监控概念 × 14 个评测分布 × 4 个探针模型 × 4 个生成器，共拟合 383–492 条曲线），发现数据需求由"监控什么"而非"数据多难"决定：在 Gemma-3-27B-IT 第 32 层上，high-stakes 与 harmful 概念 110–170 个样本即饱和（中位半增益尺寸仅 22 个样本），而 instruction-following 需要 ≥350 个样本（中位 164），把训练集从 590 砍到 80 时前者 AUROC 只掉百分之几、后者最多掉 0.21；方差分解显示概念+分布解释了半增益尺寸 42–45% 的方差（生成器/探针/提示细节各 <10%），新增的"覆盖度测试"（R/G/T 统计）解释了概念间差距的 79%，并给出无需部署流量即可把预算估准到 2 倍以内的公式 R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T)。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled study of how many synthetic conversations a linear activation probe needs, tracing learning curves over 10–590 samples for three monitoring concepts — *high-stakes situations*, *harmful-to-human replies*, and *instruction-following* — across 14 held-out evaluation distributions (reusing Anthropic HH-RLHF, MTS-Dialog, ToolACE, BBQ, AI-Dilemmas, etc., 6,462 conversations), four probe models (Gemma-3-27B-IT at layer 32 plus Qwen3-8B, Mistral-NeMo-12B, Llama-3.2-1B), and four generators (Llama-3.3-70B, GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B). Against the prior "favor breadth over depth" advice (McKenzie et al. 2025, Liu et al. 2024, Krirch et al. 2026), the paper shows the data need is a property of the monitored concept: median fitted half-gain size is 22 samples for high-stakes and harmful vs 164 for instruction, and cutting training from 590 to 80 samples costs instruction probes up to 0.21 AUROC on Gemma-3-27B-IT while costing high-stakes only a few hundredths. A new coverage test (own-split-kind / mixed / set-minus-own cuts, statistics R, G, T) shows split-kinds substitute almost freely under high-stakes (transfer AUROC 0.98) but not under instruction (0.80 generated, 0.59 real), accounting for 79% of the concept gap; a parameter-free readout R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T) estimates the budget within a factor of two on 12 of 14 real splits, with no deployment traffic needed.

## 核心贡献

- **受控的多因子学习曲线研究**：固定探针架构、生成器、提示结构与评测协议，在 3 个概念（high-stakes / harmful / instruction）× 14 个评测分布（共 6,462 条对话）× 4 个探针模型 × 2 级提示细节 × 4 个生成器上追踪 10–590 样本的学习曲线，并发布评测套件、dev 集与全部生成集。
- **"数据需求由监控对象决定"的证据**：Gemma-3-27B-IT 上 detailed 提示下 high-stakes 在 110–170 个样本饱和、harmful 在 110–350、instruction 需 ≥350；383 条拟合曲线的方差分解显示概念+分布解释 log 半增益尺寸 42–45% 的方差，而生成器、探针模型、提示细节各不足 10%。
- **覆盖度机制（而非单类难度）**：把样本按 split-kind 标注后做三种切分（own-split-kind / mixed / set-minus-own），发现 high-stakes 与 harmful 的各类 split-kind 在激活空间中类间方向近乎正交但可互相替代（去掉本类后仍保留 95% / 90% 的 AUROC 增益），instruction 各类不可替代（只能保留约 2/3 增益、整集需约 5 倍样本）；覆盖度解释了 instruction 与另两个概念半增益差距的 79%（生成样本）/ 66%（真实样本）。
- **其他生成策略只动水平不动排序**：prompt 细节化、用分布样本让 LLM 写提示、按 split 定向生成、对抗性红队（instruction +0.12、high-stakes +0.05、harmful +0.01 AUROC）都只移动 AUROC 水平，不改变概念排序。
- **可操作的预算法则**：先写下概念覆盖的 K 类对话、用 detailed 提示生成 kind 标注集，测迁移 AUROC T，再用 R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T) 估计混合数据需求——在 14 个真实分布中的 12 个、13 个生成分布中的 8 个上与实测 R 相差不到 2 倍，全程无需部署流量。

## Key Contributions

- A controlled factorial study of synthetic-data learning curves for activation probes: 3 concepts × 14 evaluation distributions × 4 probe models × 2 prompt-detail levels × 4 generators, with released evaluation suites, dev sets, and generated sets.
- Evidence that the data need is set by what is monitored: saturation on Gemma-3-27B-IT at 110–170 samples for *high-stakes*, 110–350 for *harmful*, and ≥350 for *instruction*; a sequential ANOVA over 383 non-flat fitted curves attributes 42–45% of the variance of log half-gain size to concept identity and split, with generator, probe model, and prompt detail each under 10%.
- The mechanism: a coverage test that cuts one set into own-split-kind, mixed, and set-minus-own curves. Split-kinds substitute freely under *high-stakes* (median R = 1; set-minus-own keeps 95% of AUROC gain) and *harmful* (90%), but not under *instruction* (median R = 5; only two-thirds of the gain), where transfer AUROC between split-kinds drops to 0.80 (generated) and 0.59 (real).
- Four alternative generation recipes (detailed prompts, LLM-written prompts from distribution samples, split-targeted sets, adversarial red-teaming) move the AUROC level a probe reaches by up to 0.23 but never the ordering of the concepts.
- Practical guidance: a parameter-free budgeting readout, R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T), computed purely from a kind-tagged generated set, lands within a factor of two of the measured need on most splits — letting a practitioner size the budget before seeing deployment traffic.

## Methodology

**Probe.** Unless stated otherwise, every probe is a linear head (`linear_then_softmax` head of Blundfort et al.) on the residual stream of Gemma-3-27B-IT at layer 32, mean-pooled with a softmax-weighted mean per token. Training uses Adam (lr 5e-5, weight decay 1e-3), batch 16 with 4 gradient-accumulation steps, up to 200 epochs with early stopping on a held-out dev set disjoint from the evaluation splits; conversations are capped at 1,024 tokens. Three further probe models (Qwen3-8B at layer 18, Mistral-NeMo-12B-Instruct at layer 20, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct at layer 8) replicate both prompt conditions.

**Concepts and evaluation suites.** Three monitoring targets, each with its own held-out suite: *high-stakes* (a property of the situation; 4 splits, 4,408 eval / 1,988 dev samples; suites reusing Anthropic HH-RLHF, MTS-Dialog, ToolACE), *harmful* (a property of the reply; 4 splits, 866 / 290; AI-Dilemmas, Anti-Dilemmas, Harmful-refusal, BBQ-substitution), *instruction* (reply follows the user's instruction; 6 splits, 1,188 / 404; context drift on HealthGuard, OIG contract, contrived source on HealthContract, moral transposition). All splits are class-balanced; *high-stakes* labels come from GPT-4o, *harmful* and *instruction* labels from two LLM judges that must agree with the construction label, and the authors hand-verified 30–40% of samples. The "ceiling" — the same probe family trained by 5-fold CV on the evaluation distribution itself — is ≈0.98 for all three concepts, so reported gaps are gaps in the synthetic data.

**Generators and prompt levels.** Four generators (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B) each write a 600-sample set per concept, checked for exact and near-duplicate overlap with evaluation and dev splits (none). The *general* prompt gives the generator the concept definition plus one contrastive example; the *detailed* prompt adds a paragraph enumerating the kinds of conversation each evaluation split scores on. Learning curves are fit at n ∈ {10, 30, 80, 110, 170, 350, 590} class-balanced samples with eight resampled draws each (492 per-split curves), scored as AUROC per split with unweighted means.

**Fitting.** Each curve with ≥5 points is fit to a log-logistic A(n) = L + (U−L)/(1+(n/m)^k), where U is the level the curve heads for and m is the *half-gain size* (samples at which half of U−L is in hand); bootstrap-over-draws intervals. Curves still lifting at 590 follow log-logistic bounds. A sequential ANOVA over the 383 non-flat curves decomposes the variance of log m.

**Coverage test.** Every sample carries a *split-kind* (the evaluation split it was written for, or the dev split it came from). For each target split s, three cuts of one set are traced: s's own split-kind, the whole mixed set, and the set minus s's own split-kind. Three statistics: R = m_mixed/m_own (how many mixed samples one own-kind sample is worth), G = share of the mixed-curve gain kept without the own split-kind, and T = transfer AUROC of a probe trained on one split-kind scored on the others' samples (computable from a generated set alone, no deployment knowledge). Replicated on real dev-set samples.

**Other strategies.** LLM-written prompts (an LLM writes the detailed prompt from a split's own samples), split-targeted sets, and an attacker–judge red-teaming scaffold (Blundell & Graham style; ten iterations, four attackers per concept) run on Gemma-3-27B-IT.

## Results & Highlights

- **Concept sets the saturation point.** With detailed prompts on Gemma-3-27B-IT, the *high-stakes* curve saturates at 110–170 samples and three of four *harmful* curves at 110–350, while no *instruction* curve saturates below 350. The fitted median half-gain size is 22 samples for *high-stakes* and 22 for *harmful*, versus 164 for *instruction* — roughly 7.5× more data for the same probe architecture, generators, and protocol.
- **The gap is worth real AUROC.** Cutting the training set from 590 to 80 samples costs *high-stakes* probes a few hundredths of AUROC but costs *instruction* probes up to 0.21 AUROC on Gemma-3-27B-IT; on real dev-set samples at 80 samples, *harmful* and *high-stakes* sit 0.06 below and *instruction* 0.18 below the AUROC at the largest dev size. Ceilings are ≈0.98 for all three concepts (0.981 high-stakes, ≥0.984 harmful [lower bound — its curve was still rising at 590], 0.980 instruction), so the differences are not ceiling artifacts.
- **Coverage, not per-kind difficulty.** Per split-kind the concepts are alike: the own-split-kind half-gain is ~11 samples under *instruction* and ~7 under the other two on generated sets (17, 9, 12 on real samples). The divergence is substitution: under *high-stakes*/*harmful* the whole set has about the same half-gain as the own split-kind alone (R ≈ 1) and the set-minus-own cut still delivers 95% and 90% of the AUROC gain, whereas under *instruction* R's median is 5 and set-minus-own delivers only two-thirds. Transfer AUROC: 0.98 (high-stakes), 0.86 (harmful), 0.80 (instruction) on generated sets; real samples reproduce the same order — R = 1.5, 2.5, 4.7; G = 0.95, 0.76, 0.51; T = 0.87, 0.75, 0.59.
- **Variance decomposition.** Over the 383 non-flat curves, concept identity and split account for 42–45% of the variance of log m; generator, probe model, and prompt detail each contribute < 10%, with draw noise fifth. Coverage accounts for 79% of the *instruction*-vs-others half-gain gap on generated samples and 66% on real samples.
- **Ordering survives models and real data.** On Qwen3-8B, Mistral-NeMo-12B, and Llama-3.2-1B the ordering is unchanged: *high-stakes* is within 0.04 of its 590-sample level from 110 samples on all four models; *harmful* saturates between the two (moving with the model: 110–350 on Gemma-3-27B-IT, 170 on Mistral-NeMo-12B, past 590 on Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.2-1B); *instruction* still gains past 350 on mid-sized models and is not learnable at all on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct.
- **Other recipes move level, not ordering.** Prompt detail moves the level: detailed-over-general leaves the *instruction* gap at 0.14→0.18 on three of four generators (+0.03 to +0.11 on *high-stakes*, ~+0.03 on *harmful*); under general prompts *harmful* is within 0.05 of its 590-sample level from 50 samples and *high-stakes* within 0.06 at 80, while *instruction* reaches only 0.71 at 590. Prompts written from a split's own samples lift the level by up to 0.23 (halving m by ~1.2×); red-teaming lifts *instruction* by +0.12 and *high-stakes* by +0.05 but *harmful* by only +0.01, never beating the detailed-prompt set's ordering.
- **Actionable readout.** Per kind the half-gain size is 7–17 samples on every concept and ~the whole set needs R = 1 when split-kinds substitute (T ≈ 0.98/0.86) and R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T) when they do not; this fixed-endpoint line with no fitted parameter is within a factor of two of the measured R on 12 of 14 real splits and 8 of 13 generated ones.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper has a single author, Ankush Checkerarty, listed as an Independent Researcher (contact via Gmail), so there is no institutional affiliation or lab backing to lean on; it is a v1 preprint with no stated peer review. That said, the work shows unusually careful empirical hygiene for a solo effort: a genuinely factorial design (3 concepts × 14 splits × 4 models × 4 generators × 2 prompt levels), per-split AUROC with bootstrap-over-draws intervals, a sequential ANOVA for the variance decomposition, held-out dev early stopping, duplicate checks between generated and evaluation sets, and manual verification of 30–40% of the LLM-assigned labels. The engagement with the probe-monitoring literature (McKenzie et al. 2025, Krirch et al. 2026, Liu et al. 2024, Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025, MacDermott et al. 2024) is current and specific. The release of evaluation suites, dev sets, and all generated sets materially supports reproducibility.

## Critical Assessment

The framing is the paper's real contribution: prior advice ("spend your budget on breadth, not depth"; "diverse distributions are required") is shown to be concept-dependent, and the coverage test turns that from an observation into a mechanism — *instruction* is hungrier not because its six-way suite needs six times the data (the four-way *harmful* and *high-stakes* suites share one description per split-kind and behave alike) but because its split-kinds do not substitute in activation space. That is a clean, falsifiable decomposition, and the parameter-free R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T) readout is the kind of practitioner-facing artifact the field usually lacks.

On rigor, several caveats temper the headline. First, every label in the pipeline is LLM-produced — *high-stakes* labels come from GPT-4o and *harmful*/*instruction* labels from two LLM judges agreeing with the construction label, with 30–40% hand-verified — and several evaluation distributions are themselves constructed pairs (Harmful-refusal refusals, BBQ-substitution, Anti-Dilemmas); near-ceiling cross-kind transfer under *high-stakes* (T = 0.98) may therefore partly reflect shared construction conventions between generator and evaluator rather than genuine behavioral coverage. The author acknowledges that deployment traffic is only simulated ("the traffic can't be built as drawn"), and real-sample T under *instruction* dropping to 0.59 hints the synthetic numbers are the optimistic end. Second, the statistical support for the concept-level ordering is thinner than the 383-curve machinery suggests: the author concedes the concept-level result rests on three points, and a third of the curve fits have half-gain intervals spanning more than a factor of ten, with the *harmful* ceiling only a lower bound (still rising at 590). Third, the claim that the ordering holds across models is weakened by *instruction* being flat-out unlearnable on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct — an ordering over a concept that cannot be probed is vacuous at that rung, and the mid-model saturation drift for *harmful* (110–350 → past 590) suggests absolute needs scale with probe-model capability in ways the "relative need carries over" hedge only partly covers. Fourth, the ancillary recipes (red-teaming, LLM-written prompts) run on a single model with a single head, so their null effect on ordering is a one-model claim. None of these are disqualifying — the released artifacts make everything checkable — but the practical formula's "within a factor of two" accuracy is coarse for actual budget decisions, and the concept set (situational refusal, reply harm, instruction-following) leaves open whether representational concepts like deception or sycophancy behave the same way.

## Strengths

- A genuinely controlled design: architecture, generators, prompt structure, and protocol held fixed while the concept varies — the cleanest attribution of data need to the monitoring target I have seen in this literature.
- Mechanism over observation: the own-split-kind / mixed / set-minus-own coverage test with R, G, T decomposes *why* *instruction* is hungrier and quantifies that coverage explains 79% (generated) / 66% (real) of the gap.
- Replication on real samples: every ordering claim is re-checked on dev-set draws, where the effect persists (R = 1.5, 2.5, 4.7) with realistic ceilings (0.99–1.00).
- Honest statistics: bootstrap intervals, sequential ANOVA, ceilings computed by cross-validation on the evaluation distribution itself, and explicit saturation criteria (≤0.001 AUROC gain per doubling).
- Practical payoff: a deployment-traffic-free budgeting procedure (kind-tag the set, measure T, apply the fixed formula) validated on 12 of 14 real splits.
- Full release of evaluation suites, dev sets, and generated sets.

## Limitations

- Only three concepts, and the concept-level ordering rests on three points (the split-level result on fourteen is stronger but conditional within concept); extension to other monitoring targets is conjecture.
- All supervision is LLM-labeled (GPT-4o for *high-stakes*, two agreeing LLM judges for the rest, 30–40% hand-verified); concept validity is bounded by judge conventions, and several evaluation suites share construction provenance with the training data's labeling scheme.
- The *harmful* ceiling is a lower bound (curve still rising at 590), and on a third of curves the fitted half-gain interval spans more than a factor of ten — saturation claims for that concept are correspondingly soft.
- *Instruction* is not learnable at all on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, so "the ordering holds on smaller models" glosses over a capability floor; the ancillary generation strategies are run on one model (Gemma-3-27B-IT, one head) only.
- No genuine deployment traffic: real samples are dev-set draws from the same constructed suites, so synthetic-to-deployment gaps (especially for *instruction*, T = 0.59 real) are under-sampled.
- Absolute sample counts are specific to these probes and suites; only the relative ordering and the T-based readout are claimed to transfer, and the readout is only accurate within a factor of two.

## Relevance

与你关注的 LLM 可解释性/安全监控和合成数据效率方向高度相关：这篇论文把"探针需要多少合成数据"从经验玄学变成可测量的量——按监控概念先做 kind 切分、测迁移 AUROC、再用 R ≈ 1+2(K−1)(1−T) 定预算——对任何要在部署模型上挂监控探针的实践者都是直接可用的方法论。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the coverage ordering extend to representational rather than behavioral concepts (deception, sycophancy, power-seeking, situational awareness), where split-kinds may entangle differently in activation space?
- How do the half-gain sizes shift when synthetic data is mixed with even small amounts of real deployment traffic, especially for *instruction* where real-sample transfer collapses to T = 0.59?
- Would non-linear probes (MLP heads, attention pooling) reduce the per-kind half-gain size or change which split-kinds substitute?
- Is *instruction*'s failure on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct a representational absence (the direction does not exist at layer 8) or an optimization issue, and how does the need scale with layer choice?
- How much of the near-perfect *high-stakes* transfer (T = 0.98) survives when evaluation distributions come from genuinely deployed conversations rather than constructed suites?
- The formula R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T) assumes fixed endpoints by definition; can T itself be sharpened (e.g., calibrated per concept) to tighten the factor-of-two accuracy for budget planning?
