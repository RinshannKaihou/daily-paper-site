> **一句话总结：** 提出"逐层误差归因"的混合精度 PTQ 框架：将量化误差分解为"前层传播项 + 本层局部扰动项"，用概率上界（条件单边 Chebyshev）推导层间误差界，再把局部扰动做成可分离、可复用的 (block, bit-width) 敏感度分数，配合一个无求解器的贪心分配算法——在 DRUNet 去噪模型上以 28×–2,570× 的分配速度追平 CLADO/AIMET 最强基线（B=4 时 PSNR 34.401 vs CLADO 34.409 dB），在校准数据被腐蚀时比 CLADO 高 5.5–7.5 dB，并直接迁移到 latent diffusion U-Net（CelebA-HQ 上 FID 20.853，优于 Q-Diffusion 的 22.626）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper recasts mixed-precision post-training quantization (PTQ) as a layerwise error-attribution problem. It separates quantization error propagated from earlier layers from the local perturbation introduced at each layer, derives probabilistic upper bounds (via conditional one-sided Chebyshev/Cantelli) for both a fixed configuration and the layerwise recursion, and turns the local term into per-(block, bit-width) sensitivity scores computable from a single full-precision pass over the calibration set. A greedy, solver-free allocation then meets any memory budget by reusing these fixed scores. On DRUNet denoising on CelebA at a 4-bit average budget, it matches the strongest baseline CLADO (34.401 vs 34.409 dB PSNR) and beats OMPQ-ORM by +1.814 dB, is 28.4× faster than AIMET AMP and 2,570× faster than CLADO at bit-width selection, and uniquely keeps PSNR within 0.03 dB of its clean-calibration result when the calibration set is corrupted (CLADO drops 5.5–7.5 dB). The same score + greedy rule transfers unchanged to a latent-diffusion U-Net on CelebA-HQ, reaching FID 20.853 vs Q-Diffusion's 22.626 and paired-PSNR-to-FP32 of 34.644 dB vs TFMQ-DM's 24.545.

## 核心贡献

- **概率化逐层误差分析**：将混合精度 PTQ 的量化输出误差精确分解为 Δ_ℓ = W_ℓΔ_{ℓ-1} + (W_ℓ − W'_ℓ)S'_{ℓ-1}（前层传播项 vs 本层局部量化误差），并引入随机放大比 X_ℓ、Y_ℓ，用条件单边 Chebyshev（Cantelli 不等式）构造每层的 δ-概率上界（Theorem 1），将 Houache et al. (2026) 的确定性逐层分析推广到概率设定；同时给出固定配置的全局界（Lemma 1）。
- **可分离、无求解器的分配算法**：每个 (block, candidate bit-width) 的局部分数 s^loc_{g,b} = μ̂ + δ_score·σ̂/√n 只需对校准集做一次全精度前向（低误差使 S'≈S 成立），分数与其它 block 的位宽选择无关，可在任意预算间复用；贪心规则"每节省一比特权重带来的分数增量最小者优先降位"即可满足预算，无需整网重评估、无需 Pareto 搜索或整数规划。
- **面向现代架构的误差界扩展**：附录给出计算 DAG 上的传播规则表（量化线性层、冻结/数据依赖归一化、Lipschitz 非线性、残差、拼接、上采样、加性嵌入、self-attention），覆盖 U-Net 与视觉 Transformer。
- **鲁棒性 + 速度的实证优势**：在 DRUNet 去噪任务上，仅腐蚀校准输入（测试集保持干净）时，本方法 PSNR 仍与干净校准相差 <0.03 dB，而 CLADO 最多掉 7.509 dB；位宽选择比 AIMET AMP 快 28×、比 CLADO 快 2,570×。同一分数与贪心规则不加修改地迁移到 latent diffusion U-Net，FID 与 paired-PSNR 均达到 SOTA 水平。

## Key Contributions

- A probabilistic layerwise analysis of mixed-precision quantization error: an exact decomposition separating the error propagated from earlier layers from the local perturbation introduced at the current layer, with random amplification ratios X_ℓ, Y_ℓ and a δ-probabilistic layerwise upper bound (Theorem 1, via conditional one-sided Chebyshev) that generalizes prior deterministic worst-case layerwise bounds; a global bound for a fixed configuration is given as a warm-up (Lemma 1).
- A separable, solver-free allocation algorithm (Algorithm 1): local scores per (block, bit-width), computed in one full-precision pass over the calibration set, are fixed and reusable across memory budgets; a greedy rule lowers bit-widths where the score increase per saved weight bit is smallest — no full-network re-evaluation, no pairwise interactions, no external ILP/Pareto solver.
- Propagation rules extending the bounds to computation DAGs (U-Nets, self-attention), covering the architectures used in the experiments.
- Empirical evidence on DRUNet denoising that the method matches state-of-the-art mixed-precision quality under clean calibration, is far more robust to corrupted calibration inputs (PSNR within 0.03 dB of clean vs >5 dB drops for CLADO), and allocates 28×–2,570× faster; a direct transfer of the unchanged score + greedy rule also improves mixed-precision latent-diffusion results on CelebA-HQ.

## Methodology

**Error decomposition.** For a depth-L quantized network with full-precision state S_ℓ and quantized state S'_ℓ, the error Δ_ℓ = S_ℓ − S'_ℓ satisfies the exact identity Δ_ℓ = W_ℓΔ_{ℓ-1} + (W_ℓ − W'_ℓ)S'_{ℓ-1}: the first term is the propagated error from earlier layers, the second the local quantization error introduced at layer ℓ. Two random amplification ratios, X_ℓ = ‖W_ℓΔ_{ℓ-1}‖₂/‖Δ_{ℓ-1}‖₂ and Y_ℓ = ‖(W_ℓ − W'_ℓ)S'_{ℓ-1}‖₂/‖S'_{ℓ-1}‖₂, capture how the data actually amplifies each term (bounded by the corresponding operator norms, which cover the worst case).

**Probabilistic bounds.** The paper defines a δ-probabilistic upper bound (P(Z ≤ A) ≥ 1−δ) and proves: (i) a global bound via Cantelli's inequality on the accumulated recursion G_ℓ (Lemma 1); (ii) a layerwise bound (Theorem 1) that recursively propagates deterministic error/output bounds A_ℓ, B_ℓ through the network and applies conditional Cantelli per layer, preserving the two-term structure so the source of each error stays visible — the information an allocation algorithm needs. The total tail budgets Σ(δ_ℓ + δ̃_ℓ) < ε. Appendix A extends this to DAGs with a table of propagation rules (frozen and data-dependent normalization, Lipschitz nonlinearities, residual addition, concatenation, upsampling, additive embeddings, self-attention with realized normalization ratios).

**Score + greedy allocation.** Layers are grouped into blocks g (matching baselines' block partitions). The practical local score uses only the local term evaluated on full-precision layer inputs (justified because the algorithm's low-error path keeps S'≈S): P_{g,b}(x) = Σ_{ℓ∈g} ‖(W_ℓ − Q_b(W_ℓ))S_{ℓ-1}(x)‖₂, summarized over the calibration set as s^loc_{g,b} = μ̂_{g,b} + δ_score·σ̂_{g,b}/√n_score (δ_score = 0.05 is a fixed dispersion weight, not a confidence level). Every score depends on a single (g, b) pair — hence separable and budget-independent. Algorithm 1 starts every block at its largest candidate bit-width and greedily lowers the block with the smallest score increase per weight bit saved (Eq. 17), stopping when Cost(b) ≤ Bud. All scores are precomputed, so re-allocation for a new budget requires no new network evaluations and no external solver.

**Experimental protocol.** DRUNet color denoising (32.64M weights, 64 convs grouped into 36 blocks; weight-only quantization, activations FP32) on 256×256 CelebA with noise σ=0.1; three 256-image calibration sets (seeds 0/1/2), 100 clean test images. Calibration inputs are corrupted in a controlled way (x̄ = M⊙x + ε, spatial binary masks at p=0.5 shared-RGB or p=0.9 per-channel) while the test set stays clean. Baselines are matched in quantizer backend and block partition: OMPQ-ORM (per-channel RTN + MILP), CLADO (pairwise ILP), AIMET AMP (TF-Enhanced + Pareto search), plus an adapted output-SQNR greedy control; a corrected control restricts both CLADO and this method to the same W3/W4/W8 candidates. The diffusion experiment reuses the Q-Diffusion/AdaRound backend on a pretrained latent-diffusion U-Net (123 quantized tensors → 48 blocks, W3–W8) without any diffusion-specific score or solver.

## Results & Highlights

- **Clean-calibration quality (DRUNet, RTN backend, budget B=4 bits/weight, PSNR on 100 CelebA test images):** uniform 4-bit loses 5.687 dB vs FP32 (34.664 → 28.978 dB); the proposed method reaches 34.401 dB (3.987 realized bits) vs 32.587 dB for OMPQ-ORM (+1.814 dB) and 34.409 dB for CLADO (−0.008 dB, 95% CI of the mean difference [−0.015, −0.001] dB). With the AIMET TF-Enhanced backend it edges out AIMET AMP at B=5 (+0.010 dB, with slightly lower realized bits 4.932 vs 4.961) and B=6 (+0.017 dB), and ties at B=7 (−0.0003 dB).
- **Robustness to corrupted calibration (only allocation inputs corrupted; clean test set):** at B=4 with W3–W8 candidates, the method's PSNR moves from 34.401 dB (clean) to 34.429 dB (p=0.5 shared-channel) and 34.429 dB (p=0.9 per-channel) — within 0.03 dB — while CLADO collapses from 34.409 dB to 27.824 dB and 28.928 dB (largest observed gap 7.509 dB at B=5 shared corruption). With identical W3/W4/W8 candidates for both methods (Table 8), gains are +6.5845 dB (p=0.5) and +5.4800 dB (p=0.9), ruling out the extra-candidate-bits explanation. Allocation stability diagnostics: Spearman correlation between clean and corrupted bit-width configurations is 0.961/0.987 for this method vs 0.060/0.313 for CLADO.
- **Allocation speed (DRUNet, 256 calibration images, one A100; CLADO uses two A100s, Table 9):** full selection takes 1 min 29 s vs AIMET AMP's 42 min 04 s (28.4×), 15.7 s vs OMPQ-ORM's 8 min 01 s (30.6×; 52.9× vs its δ-search variant), and 8.66 s vs CLADO's 6 h 11 min (2,570×) under the matched W3/W4/W8 candidates. The recorded end-to-end layerwise allocation stage takes a median 24.32 s on one A100.
- **Latent-diffusion transfer (CompVis latent diffusion, CelebA-HQ 256×256, Q-Diffusion/AdaRound backend, 48 blocks, W3–W8, realized 3.9932 bits):** FID over 50,000 generated images (30,000 per method for calibration) is 20.853 vs 22.626 for uniform-W4 Q-Diffusion and 20.928 for TFMQ-DM (FP32: 20.807); mean paired PSNR to the FP32 outputs over 50,000 images is 34.644 dB vs 24.545 dB for TFMQ-DM and 24.060 dB for Q-Diffusion.
- **Honest edge case:** at the smallest budget B=3.5, CLADO wins on clean calibration (34.101 dB vs 33.300 dB) — the greedy rule concedes ~0.8 dB at very tight budgets (Table 6).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The genuine conceptual contribution is the separation of propagated vs local error with a probabilistic (Cantelli-based) treatment, and the observation that a fixed, separable local score suffices for budget-constrained allocation — removing the per-decision score updates of earlier greedy PTQ (Tsujii et al. 2021) and the pairwise/solver machinery of CLADO/OMPQ. That said, "greedy on a cheap layerwise sensitivity score" is an old idea (HAWQ-lineage methods); the novelty is in the robustness argument and the score's budget-independence, not the allocation rule itself.

**Rigor.** The theory is careful (exact decomposition, proper conditional-moment bookkeeping, union-bound tail budgets, DAG extension with self-attention handled via realized ratios), but the bridge from bounds to algorithm is loose: the practical score drops the propagated term entirely, so Theorem 1 motivates rather than guarantees the greedy allocation — there is no approximation-quality or optimality bound for the final configuration. The δ_score=0.05 term is a heuristic dispersion penalty, honestly flagged as "not a confidence level." The bounded-input assumption P(‖x‖₂ ≤ B₀)=1 is an idealization.

**Evaluation concerns.** The scope is narrow: two vision models (a DRUNet denoiser and one latent-diffusion U-Net), weight-only quantization with FP32 activations — the regime where memory savings and the deployment story are least stressed; no LLM/ViT/language results despite mixed-precision PTQ's biggest audience being LLM compression. The headline robustness win is against one baseline's specific failure mode: CLADO's pairwise ILP is demonstrably score-noise-sensitive (Spearman 0.060 under shared corruption), so the "5.5–7.5 dB gain" is real but partially a stress test designed around a competitor's weakness; the paper's own controls (identical candidate sets) do defuse the most obvious confound. The 2,570× speedup compares against a heavyweight pairwise optimizer on 2× A100 while this method uses 1× — fair but flattering; the more conservative comparisons (28× vs AIMET AMP, 30× vs OMPQ-ORM) are already decisive. Clean-quality improvements over the strongest baselines are within noise (−0.008 dB vs CLADO, ±0.01–0.017 dB vs AIMET AMP) — the paper correctly claims parity, not superiority, on quality under clean calibration, and concedes the B=3.5 point. All DRUNet numbers rest on 100 test images; the diffusion paired-PSNR pairing with historical FP32 outputs had to be "reconstructed from the recorded protocol" because exact equality of initial-noise tensors "cannot be verified" — a small but candid data-provenance blemish on the 34.644 vs 24.545 dB comparison.

**Significance.** For embedded/edge deployment where calibration data may be noisy or mis-collected, a fast, corruption-stable allocator is practically valuable, and the diffusion result suggests the score transfers. Whether the robustness conclusion generalizes beyond one denoiser and one U-Net, and beyond mask/noise corruptions to realistic calibration-set drift (domain mismatch, compression artifacts), remains open.

## Strengths

- Separable, budget-independent scores computed in a single full-precision pass make re-allocation for new budgets essentially free (8.66 s–24.32 s on one A100), a real engineering win over Pareto/ILP/Pareto-search pipelines (28×–2,570× measured).
- Strong, well-controlled robustness evidence: corrupted-calibration stability shown within 0.03 dB of clean results, with a corrected control (same W3/W4/W8 candidates for CLADO) and allocation-stability diagnostics (Spearman 0.961–0.987 vs 0.060–0.313) that isolate the mechanism.
- Theory with a purpose: the propagated/local decomposition is exactly the structure the algorithm consumes, and the DAG/self-attention extension makes the analysis match modern architectures rather than sequential toy models.
- Direct, unmodified transfer of the same score + greedy rule to latent diffusion — no diffusion-specific allocation machinery — improving FID (20.853 vs 22.626 for Q-Diffusion) and paired PSNR (34.644 vs 24.545 dB for TFMQ-DM).
- Uncommon transparency: adapted baselines labeled, CIs reported, δ_score caveated, the losing B=3.5 case and the un-verifiable FP32 pairing disclosed.

## Limitations

- Narrow empirical scope: one denoiser (DRUNet, CelebA, σ=0.1) and one latent-diffusion U-Net (CelebA-HQ), weight-only quantization with FP32 activations; no language models, no ViT backbones, no activation quantization, so memory/compute savings in real deployments are not directly demonstrated.
- Clean-calibration quality is parity, not progress: −0.008 dB vs CLADO and ±0.01–0.017 dB vs AIMET AMP are inside reported confidence intervals; at B=3.5 the greedy rule loses ~0.8 dB to CLADO, suggesting very tight budgets remain a weak spot.
- The robustness claim is established mainly against mask/noise corruption patterns chosen by the authors and mainly against CLADO's known sensitivity; OMPQ-ORM is also corruption-stable (though at lower absolute quality), so "robust to corrupted calibration" is not unique to this method — it is robust *and* fast *and* high-quality simultaneously.
- No end-to-end approximation guarantee: Theorem 1 bounds the error of a given configuration, but the greedy's final allocation carries no theoretical quality bound; the probabilistic bounds are also not evaluated numerically (tightness vs realized errors is not measured).
- Two GPUs vs one in the CLADO timing comparison inflates the headline 2,570× figure relative to like-for-like hardware; PSNR rests on 100 test images; the historical FP32 pairing in the diffusion PSNR comparison could not be exactly verified.
- No public code release is mentioned, which limits immediate reproducibility despite the detailed protocol appendix.

## Relevance

对模型压缩/边缘部署方向很有参考价值：它把混合精度 PTQ 的敏感度估计做成"一次前向、可复用、无求解器"，并首次系统研究了校准数据被腐蚀时的分配稳定性，对量化 diffusion 模型（Q-Diffusion/TFMQ-DM 一脉）也有直接可借鉴的分数设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the local score's corruption stability extend to realistic calibration-set mismatch (domain shift, low-light/blur sensors, different datasets) rather than synthetic masks and noise?
- How does the method behave with activation quantization (A8 and below) and on transformer/LLM workloads, where layer interactions and outlier channels dominate — does dropping the propagated term still hold up?
- What is the empirical tightness of Theorem 1's probabilistic bound versus realized output errors, and can a data-dependent bound on the greedy's final configuration be derived?
- Can the propagated-error term be re-introduced cheaply (e.g., one correction pass) to close the ~0.8 dB gap to CLADO at very tight budgets (B=3.5)?
- Is the robustness specific to CLADO's pairwise ILP, or would other interaction-based allocators (e.g., modern Pareto/learned-policy methods like GMPQ-TE) degrade similarly under corrupted calibration?
