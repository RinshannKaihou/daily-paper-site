> **一句话总结：** CARE 用"参考策略成功而加速策略失败"的成对回合级指标（Acceleration-Induced Failure, AIF）刻画 VLA 加速的真实代价，通过 Learn-then-Test 框架 + 任务均衡序贯 e-process 检验 + 失败触发/缓存的参考评估，以有限样本保证（95% 置信下 AIF 风险 ≤ 用户预算 α）选出最快的可认证加速配置：在 LIBERO 四个套件上为 OpenVLA-OFT 认证出 9.02–10.81× 加速并保留 85.8–95.7% 参考可解回合，而延迟/平均成功率等常用选择器在紧预算下最高 75% 的试验超出风险预算；序贯版把认证 rollout 从 25,000 降到 5,263（省 78.9%），并推广到 π₀.₅ 的流步数缩减与 Crafter 中 Qwen3.5-9B / Llama-3.1-8B 语言体。

> **TL;DR:** CARE is a training-free, model-agnostic statistical procedure that decides which VLA inference accelerators (action chunking, visual-token pruning, KV caching, flow-step reduction) may safely replace a full-compute reference policy: it certifies, at confidence 1−δ, that the paired episode-level "acceleration-induced failure" (AIF) risk stays below a user budget α, deploying the fastest certified candidate and falling back to the reference otherwise. On OpenVLA-OFT across the four LIBERO suites (α=5%), CARE certifies 9.02–10.81× speedups while guaranteeing ≥85.8% of reference-solved episodes are preserved — at tighter budgets (α=2.0%) latency-only and average-success selectors deploy over-budget policies in 75% of resampled trials versus 0% for CARE — and its sequential form cuts certification rollouts from 25,000 to 5,263 (78.9% fewer) over a 24-configuration candidate family.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 AIF 风险定义与认证式加速选择问题**：定义加速度致败损失 L_λ(U) = Y₀(U)(1−Y_λ(U))——同一任务、同一初始场景/种子下，参考策略成功而加速策略失败才算失败；并证明边际成功率、单步置信/分歧等局部指标原则上无法恢复该回合级风险（Theorem 2 给出两个单步效应全为零、AIF 风险却分别为 1 和 0 的反例）。
- **CARE：certify-then-select**：在保留校准集上做成对 rollout，对每个候选检验 H: R(λ) ≥ α，用 union bound 做家族级错误率控制（δⱼ = δ/M），固定样本用 Hoeffding–Bentkus p 值，部署最快通过认证的候选，若无认证则回退参考策略，保证 Pr(R(λ̂) ≥ α) ≤ δ。
- **两个面向成对回合评估的效率设计**：(1) 任务均衡序贯认证——按"每轮每任务 m 个单元"轮转取数据，使 e-process 在异质任务风险下仍是超鞅（anytime-valid）；(2) 失败触发参考评估——候选成功即损失为 0，参考策略只在候选失败时执行并缓存共享，Lemma 1 证明其损失与认证决策和穷举成对评估完全一致。
- **实验验证**：LIBERO + OpenVLA-OFT 上所有套件均认证出加速策略（9.02–10.81×，保留 85.8–95.7%），序贯版比穷举省 78.9% rollout；推广到 π₀.₅ 流步数缩减（认证 2/4 步）与 Crafter 中的语言体（collect_wood 在 69.4–74.9% 参考算力下 3/3 种子认证）。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes acceleration-induced failure (AIF): a paired, episode-level risk L_λ(U) = Y₀(U)(1−Y_λ(U)) measured on complete closed-loop rollouts from identical initial conditions, and proves (Theorem 2, via a scalar-error construction) that single-decision effects such as per-step confidence or action disagreement cannot determine AIF risk — it can only be estimated from paired complete episodes.
- Introduces CARE, a certify-then-select procedure in the Learn-then-Test framework: family-wise error control across M candidates (δⱼ = δ/M), Hoeffding–Bentkus p-values for fixed-sample testing, and a PAC-style deployment guarantee Pr(R(λ̂) ≥ α) ≤ δ; the selected policy is the fastest certified candidate, with safe fallback to the reference when evidence is insufficient.
- Two mechanisms that make closed-loop certification tractable without weakening the guarantee: (1) task-balanced sequential rounds that turn the averaged e-process into a non-negative supermartingale under heterogeneous per-task risks (Lemma 2), giving anytime-valid early stopping; (2) failure-triggered, cache-shared reference evaluation that runs the expensive reference only where a candidate fails (Lemma 1: identical losses and decisions to exhaustive paired evaluation).
- Empirically: on OpenVLA-OFT/LIBERO, CARE certifies acceleration on every suite (9.02–10.81× speedup, 85.8–95.7% reference episodes preserved at 95% confidence), stays within budget where common selectors violate it, cuts certification cost by up to 78.9%, and transfers unchanged to π₀.₅ flow-step reduction and to Qwen3.5-9B / Llama-3.1-8B agents in Crafter.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** An evaluation unit U = (τ, s, ξ) fixes a task, an initial scene, and execution randomness. Running the reference π₀ and a candidate accelerator λ (action-chunk length, token-pruning ratio, cache policy, or number of flow-integration steps) on the same unit yields binary outcomes Y₀, Y_λ. The AIF risk R(λ) = (1/T)Σₜ E[L_λ(U) | τ=t] is the equal-weight task-mixture probability that acceleration degrades a reference success into a failure. Given budget α ∈ (0,1) and error δ ∈ (0,1), CARE returns the fastest λ with Pr(R(λ̂) ≥ α) ≤ δ.

**Certify then select.** Following Learn-then-Test, each candidate is tested against Hⱼ: R(λⱼ) ≥ α at level δⱼ = δ/M, so the union bound bounds the family-wise false-certification probability by δ. For fixed-n calibration, CARE uses the Hoeffding–Bentkus p-value p̃ⱼ = p̃(R̄ⱼ; α), which is valid for [0,1]-valued losses without distributional assumptions.

**Task-balanced sequential certification.** Calibration units are drawn in K rounds of m units *per task* (B = mT per round). At each round boundary the evidence is inspected via an e-process Eⱼ(nₖ) that averages likelihood-ratio terms over a grid Q ⊂ (0,α) of safe risks. Because every round contains m units of every task, the average per-round conditional loss mean equals R(λⱼ), making Eⱼ a non-negative supermartingale under Hⱼ (Lemma 2); Ville's inequality then gives Pr(maxₖ Eⱼ(nₖ) ≥ 1/δⱼ) ≤ δⱼ regardless of when inspection stops. Candidates are ordered fastest-first by latency measured on a disjoint profiling set; the first candidate to be certified is deployed, and a candidate that can no longer reach 1/δⱼ even with zero further losses is abandoned early.

**Failure-triggered reference evaluation.** Since L_λ = Y₀(1−Y_λ) is 0 whenever the candidate succeeds, the reference is executed only on units where a candidate fails; its outcome is cached and shared across all candidates. Lemma 1 shows the resulting losses, certification decisions, and λ̂ coincide exactly with exhaustive paired evaluation, while reference executions drop to N_ref = |C| ≤ min(n_K, Σⱼ Fⱼ). Assumptions are pre-specification of all choices (A1), i.i.d. sampling within tasks (A2), and unit-local execution randomness (A3); retention-mode variants (Theorem 3) certify a lower bound on the fraction of reference-solved episodes preserved.

## Results & Highlights

- **Certified acceleration on all four LIBERO suites (OpenVLA-OFT, α=5%, δ=5%, 250 task-balanced calibration episodes per suite):** CARE certifies FastV-25 H8 on Goal (9.02× speedup, 93.5% of reference-solved episodes preserved at 95% confidence), FastV-75 H8 on Spatial (10.08×, 95.7%), Plain H8 on Object (9.50×, 92.2%), and VLA-Cache H8 on Long (10.81×, 85.8%) — a certified accelerated policy on every suite, with the family-wise correction keeping all bounds valid across the 8-configuration family.
- **Budget validity vs. common selectors (10,000 resampling trials per suite):** at α=2.0% and α=2.5%, CARE's observed over-budget deployment rate is 0.0%, while latency-only selection exceeds the budget in 75.0%/25.0% of trials, average-success matching in 75.0%/25.0%, validation-tuned selection in 36.8%/13.5%, and an unpaired marginal test in 67.1%/19.5% — CARE achieves this by safely retaining the reference when evidence is insufficient.
- **Deployment speed and evaluation cost (α=5.0%):** fixed-sample CARE attains the highest mean speedup among risk-controlled selectors (8.88× with 11.7% fallback) versus Pareto Testing (6.16×, 42.2% fallback), cost-order fixed-sequence LIT (6.35×, 45.5%), and safe policy improvement (5.30×, 60.5%); sequential CARE keeps a 7.32× speedup (26.9% fallback) while cutting mean evaluation rollouts from 2,250 to 592 (73.7% fewer).
- **24-configuration candidate family (4 action horizons × 6 inference modes):** CARE is the only method that certifies an accelerated policy on every suite, using 5,263 total rollouts versus 25,000 for full-sample Hoeffding–Bentkus (78.9% fewer; reference rollouts 113 vs 1,000), and 610/1,222/2,043 fewer rollouts than the plug-in e-process, sequential exact, and sequential HB baselines, all of which fail to certify acceleration on at least one suite.
- **Generalization:** for π₀.₅ flow-step reduction, CARE certifies two-step policies on Spatial and Object (1.45×, 20% of reference compute) and four-step on Goal (1.26×), retaining the ten-step reference on Long — 1,276 vs 4,000 rollouts (68.1% fewer); in Crafter, collect_wood schedules certify on 3/3 seeds at 69.4% (Qwen3.5-9B) and 74.9% (Llama-3.1-8B) of reference compute, while collect_drink certifies on 2/3 seeds (85.8% and 98.6% compute).
- **Ablations confirm each design choice:** local single-decision AIF diverges from whole-episode AIF by factors of 1.31 and 3.52 on Object (4.3%→1.0%) and Long (9.5%→2.7%); uncorrected selection deploys an over-budget policy in 46.0% of trials vs ≤0.15% family-corrected; task-by-task acquisition certifies over-budget policies with probability up to 1.00 vs ≤4.77×10⁻³ for balanced rounds; and the two efficiency components together reduce total rollouts from 9,000 to 1,997 (77.8%) and wall-clock from 38.6 to 7.0 hours (81.0%), with sequential fastest-first cutting candidate rollouts 8,000→1,950 and failure-triggered evaluation cutting reference rollouts 1,000→47.

The first author's bibliography shows a consistent prior line of work on risk-sensitive control, distributionally robust optimal control, and adaptive conformal guidance (e.g., Adaptive Conformal Guidance at ICLR 2026, CamL at NeurIPS), so the conformal/sequential-testing machinery here sits squarely within the authors' expertise. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — real framing contribution, assembled statistics.** The problem framing (paired episode-level AIF instead of latency + average success) is genuinely new for VLA acceleration and well-motivated: the paper's own Table 7 shows local and episode-level disagreement differ by 1.31×–3.52× across suites, and Theorem 2's zero-single-step-effect counterexample cleanly justifies whole-episode measurement. However, the statistical toolkit is a careful composition of existing pieces — LTT, Hoeffding–Bentkus bounds, e-processes/Ville, union-bound FWER control — and the efficiency mechanisms, while elegant, are straightforward consequences of the loss's structure (candidate success ⇒ loss 0).
- **Rigor — assumptions stated but deployment-narrow.** The guarantee holds for scenes drawn i.i.d. from the deployment distribution; LIBERO provides only 500 fixed initial scenes per suite, split 250/250 for calibration/test, so both certification power and the "preserved" lower bounds (e.g., 85.8% on Long) rest on small test samples, and behavior under layout/object shift is untested. The mixture risk also pools tasks equally: a policy could pass at α=5% while failing disproportionately on the hardest task suite.
- **Practical cost of honesty.** The certificate frequently says "no" under tight budgets: fallback is 56.3% at α=2.5% with n=500 (Table 11) and 100% at α≤2.5% with n=250, and sequential CARE trades mean speedup (7.32× vs 8.88× fixed-n) for cheaper certification. This is honest behavior, but it means tight budgets largely recover the reference policy in practice.
- **Thin LM-agent evidence.** The Crafter experiments cover only two tasks with three seeds each; collect_drink certifies at 85.8% and 98.6% of reference compute (near-zero actual savings) and fails on 1/3 seeds — this part reads more like a generality check than a substantive result.
- **Simulation-only pairing.** Paired rollouts require resetting to identical initial conditions and seeds — feasible in LIBERO/Crafter simulators, undemonstrated on real robots where exact resets are impossible. Certification cost (250 episodes × candidates of full closed-loop simulation, even after the 78.9% reduction) is only acceptable in cheap simulators.
- **Reproducibility.** Benchmarks and candidate methods are all public and the algorithm is precisely specified, but no code release is mentioned; replication requires re-implementing the e-process scheduling and retention-mode bisection from the appendix.

## Strengths

- Reframes VLA acceleration from a speed-accuracy tradeoff into explicit, finite-sample risk control — a question (what does acceleration *break*?) that average success demonstrably cannot answer.
- Theory-experiment alignment is strong: observed over-budget rates (0.00–0.02% across Table 11 settings) sit far below the nominal δ=5%, and every design choice is isolated by a targeted ablation (Tables 7–10).
- Failure-triggered reference evaluation with caching is a genuinely clever cost cut with an exact-equivalence proof (Lemma 1), not an approximation.
- Model- and mechanism-agnostic: the same black-box procedure covers action chunking, token pruning, caching, flow-step reduction, and language-model agents, requiring only terminal outcomes and measured compute.
- Honest fallback: when evidence is insufficient CARE retains the reference rather than forcing acceleration, which is exactly the behavior the risky selectors lack.

## Limitations

- The guarantee is distribution-bound: it holds for i.i.d. scenes from the calibration distribution and says nothing about deployment shift (new scenes, objects, or instructions).
- Paired evaluation needs exact initial-state reset, confining current evidence to simulators; real-robot certification remains open.
- Tight risk budgets trigger heavy fallback (100% at α≤2.5% with n=250; 56.3% at α=2.5% with n=500), so certified acceleration is only available when the budget is relatively loose.
- The equal-weight task mixture can hide per-task regressions; no per-task certificate is provided.
- Certification requires a pre-specified α, δ, round schedule, latency-ordered candidate list, and e-process grid; the sensitivity of certification power to m, K, and Q is analyzed only via a coarse grid in Table 11.
- LM-agent evaluation (Crafter) is small: two tasks, three seeds, and near-zero compute savings on one of the two tasks.

## Relevance

与你关注的高效推理与 VLA/机器人策略部署直接相关：CARE 把 action chunking、visual-token 剪枝、KV 缓存等 VLA 加速手段的安全性选择问题转化为带有限样本保证的假设检验，提供了比"延迟 + 平均成功率"更严格、可直接复用的评估与选型框架，也可迁移到多模态智能体的算力缩减决策上。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How does certification behave under distribution shift between the 500 fixed LIBERO calibration scenes and novel deployment scenes — is there a way to make the AIF bound robust or continuously re-certified online?
2. Can paired-rollout certification be adapted to real robots where exact initial-state reset is impossible, e.g., via approximate matching, importance weighting, or runtime monitoring with e-processes?
3. How sensitive is certification power to the round size m, the e-process grid Q, and the latency-based candidate ordering when latency measurements on the profiling set are noisy?
4. Would per-task (rather than equal-weight mixture) risk certificates change which accelerators are certifiable on Long, where preservation is lowest at 85.8%?
5. Can failure-triggered reference evaluation be combined with speculative decoding or batched simulation to reduce the remaining 7-hour certification wall-clock, and does CARE extend to certifying acceleration for multi-agent or fine-tuned small-LM controllers?
