Key metric

+6.3pts

avg accuracy over BitSMoE at 2.3 bpw read (73.63 vs 67.32 on MMLU-Pro / MMLU / GSM8K) — while reading 3.3× less per decoded token.

Why this matters

Decouples what MoE serving stores from what it reads: one artifact, many workloads, CPU-second recalibration — directly relevant to decode-bandwidth-bound inference today.

Critical edge

The headline margin over 2-bit baselines comes mostly from the weight format, not the allocator — whose isolated effect is at most 1.35% perplexity, on one benchmark.

01Approach — store once, read per workload

R_Store (resident) (1/E) Σ R(Me)

Unweighted footprint that prefill amortizes to — the only thing prior allocators constrain.

R_Read (decode) Σ feR(Me) / Σ fe

Routing-weighted traffic that batch-one decoding actually pays. BitSMoE ckpt: stores 2.54 bpw, reads 7.50 → 2.95× gap.

↓
Stage 1 · offline, once

Store one StagQ prefix-readable stream, uniform p4 = 4.2704 bpw across all 6,144 experts (48 layers × 128); attention / embeddings stay BF16. Bake a distortion tensor per expert·tier·channel from one offline backward pass.

↓
Stage 2 · per workload, label-free

One forward pass over the workload’s own prefill → per-channel second moments + routing counts; one dot product per expert·tier scores every candidate; bisection on a single shared λ hits the read budget (<0.001 bpw) in CPU seconds → a KB-scale tier table over {p2, p3, p4}.

↓
Serving

Prefill reads full stored depth; decode reads only per-expert prefixes. Swapping workloads swaps kilobytes of tier indices — weights are never re-encoded. Online recalibration closes 95.3% of the domain-shift gap.

Proposition 1 — routing frequency cancels. min Σ fede(Me)  s.t.  Σ feR(Me) ≤ B·Σ fe The same positive fe multiplies objective and budget, so it cannot reorder experts: Me*(λ) = argminM [de(M)+λR(M)] depends only on distortion exchange rates Δd/ΔR. The algebra is Park et al.’s (2026a) — the MoE point is that the frequency prior allocators use as “importance” carries no assignment information.

Distortion baked per tier: S = (ΔW)² × activation energy × decision-flip sensitivity (expected flips of top-two margin decisions). A ≡ 1 recovers prior activation-weighted squared error.

  • Other 2-bit methods at 2.3 bpw collapse: MoEQuant 57.68 · HQO 37.98 · MiLo 34.93 · GPTQ 26.62 avg — though most of that margin is the StagQ format, not allocation.
  • Same-format controls: allocation beats a random adjacent-tier mix by 1.35% ppl at 2.6 bpw (p = 4.7×10⁻&sup5;) and 1.04% at 2.9 (p = 7.3×10⁻⁷); only 0.07% vs the interpolated null. Swapping in MC-MoE’s frequency-weighted objective loses 0.42–0.61%.

03Strengths

  • Two-functional decomposition is clean and checkable — it expresses a 2.95× checkpoint discrepancy that single-rate papers cannot even state.
  • Theory is falsifiable and then used — Propositions 1–2 design the controls and explain the frequency-heuristic negative result.
  • Same-format paired controls (sign tests, bootstraps) isolate the allocator’s causal share instead of crediting the format — rare honesty in quantization literature.
  • Practical deployment story — one artifact, CPU-second per-workload tables, online recalibration, zero weight re-encoding.
  • Exemplary baseline diligence — field-by-field BitSMoE audit, harness-alignment study, objective audit of 14 prior allocators, explicit negative-results appendix.

04Limitations

  • Single model — all results on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base (48×128 experts); other routing schemes and scales are explicitly untested.
  • No end-to-end serving measurement — no fused prefix-read kernel; tokens/s, TTFT and TPOT remain unmeasured accounting.
  • Regime-limited — the gain concentrates in batch-one long decode; resident memory doubles vs 2-bit (14.41 vs 8.58 GiB), so memory-bound deployments still prefer BitSMoE.
  • Thin allocation effect at the top budget — 1.35%/1.04% vs the random null, only 0.07% vs the interpolated null at 2.9 bpw.
  • Calibration quirks — WikiText-2 is calibrated on its own 62 scored docs; benchmark subsamples are small; code and the StagQ stream are not yet released.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Q1Does the store–read gap — and Proposition 1’s cancellation — hold on DeepSeek-style routing (shared experts, fine-grained experts, sigmoid routing)?
  • Q2What does a fused prefix-read kernel actually deliver end-to-end (tokens/s, TPOT, TTFT) vs the 520–657 MB/token accounting?
  • Q3Does the ≤1.35% allocation-only gain grow with more heterogeneous expert sensitivity, or is it bounded by the convex tier family’s resolution?
  • Q4How should the stored-table mixture channel (πd|e pooling) and recalibration scheduling handle drifting request mixes?
  • Q5Can decision-flip sensitivity be computed without the offline backward pass, so Stage 1 scales to much larger checkpoints?
