Why this matters

Harness-engineering gains vanish when backbones upgrade; orchestrating untouched agents through a shared workspace and context reset is the durable, zero-prior alternative.

Critical edge

The +8.0-point SOTA headline compares different backbones and budgets (6 h vs 12–24 h); without a budget-matched best-of-2 control, alternation versus diversity-plus-selection stays unisolated.

The authors flag this themselves — the honest same-model delta is +5.8 (their Table 5).

Methodology

Diagnose two failure modes, prove alternation can beat them, then orchestrate — no new harness, no task prior, no prompt engineering.

Diagnosis

  • Long-horizon deceleration — across six frontier model–harness pairs, mean output tokens per response (a reasoning-intensity proxy) falls 29.8–45.0% from hours 0–2 to hours 3–5 of a run.
  • Harness staleness — ScienceFlow, ML-Master 2.0 and MLEvolve beat the native DeepSeek harness on V4-Flash, yet all fall below it once the backbone upgrades to V4.1.

Theory

  • Alternation modeled as a context-reset semi-MDP: state (workspace W, best result E, context c); option boundaries keep (W, E) and reset c to ∅. Under result retention and cooperative access (α·β > 0), alternating sessions provably exceed every single-agent reference ceiling (Thm 3.3); a fixed k-cap preserves the edge when Δ+A > 0 (Thm 3.5).

  • k=5 set empirically: across 1,065 options in 11 configurations, the first 5 experiments retain 97.83% of observed gains while spending 73.77% of the experiments (simultaneous KL bounds).

Strengths

  • Reframes an active research direction with a well-evidenced negative result: harness gains are not version-stable, so workflow-level coordination of native agents is the more durable lever.
  • Rare theory–measurement–ablation loop: the interruption theorem motivates short caps, a prefix analysis with confidence bounds selects k, and the executed ablation (65.3/66.7/70.7 for k=1/3/5) confirms it.
  • Mechanism-level evidence, not just end scores: 86.4% vs 60.7% token retention ties the intervention to the diagnosed decay; final review audited per configuration.
  • Exceptional transparency for a systems paper: matched-budget own baselines, three repeats with ± values, silent failures documented, code released.

Limitations

  • Gains are pair- and order-dependent: weakest pair +4.9 pts, and α, β are never estimated — no rule predicts which pairs will benefit.
  • All main evidence sits at a 6-hour horizon; 24–48 h persistence is probed only by a single, differently-judged AOPT run.
  • No budget-matched best-of-2 control, so “alternation vs diversity + selection” is not isolated; the cross-paper SOTA claim is confounded by backbone choice.
  • Medal rates are coarse and leaderboard-size dependent; k=5 is optimal only within {1, 3, 5} on one starting order.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Best-of-2, budget-matched: does alternation itself matter, or mostly diversity plus archive-based selection?
  2. Can α and β be estimated online from execution traces — to predict which pairs gain, choose the starting order, and adapt k per task?
  3. Does the advantage survive 24–48 h MLE-bench horizons, where deceleration bites hardest?
  4. Model diversity or harness diversity? Alternating two harness variants of the same weights would cleanly separate the two.
  5. Fixed-k caps bound experiments, not wall-clock time — is an explicit time-allocation policy superior?
