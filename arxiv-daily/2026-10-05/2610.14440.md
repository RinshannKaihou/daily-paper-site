> **一句话总结：** 针对 LLM 异步 RL 后训练中陈旧 rollout 导致重要性权重重尾、有效样本量（ESS）崩塌的问题，LinkedIn 提出 TETPO：为每个 prompt 组自适应地选择最大幂指数 α∈[0,1]，将重要性权重 ρ 平滑地压成 ρ^α，使组内 ESS 始终不低于目标比例 κ（实验取 0.85）——α 由 24 次一维二分法高效求解，且被证明是"采样器—学习者几何桥接策略"的精确重要性权重；在高陈旧度 k=32 的数学推理评测中，TETPO 在 Qwen3-Base-1.7B 五个基准平均分 44.4%，超过最强基线 M2PO 的 42.3%（+2.1 分），并在 Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct 上超过 GEPO +2.3 分，全程无 GEPO 式奖励崩塌。

> **TL;DR:** Asynchronous RL post-training of LLMs trains on stale rollouts, and the sequence-level importance weights are so heavy-tailed that the effective sample size (ESS) collapses. TETPO (LinkedIn, OPT2026 workshop) replaces each weight ρ with the data-adaptively tempered ρ^α, choosing per prompt-group the largest α∈[0,1] whose tempered ESS meets a target fraction κ (0.85 in experiments) via 24-iteration bisection — a smooth knob that interpolates between full importance sampling (α=1) and uniform GRPO-style weights (α=0). The tempered coefficients are exactly the importance weights of a geometric-bridge policy, and the paper proves a deterministic dispersion bound Σ(w̄−1)²/G ≤ 1/κ−1 that is independent of policy drift. On five math benchmarks (AIME24/25, AMC23/24, MATH-500) under high staleness k=32, TETPO averages 44.4% on Qwen3-Base-1.7B vs 42.3% for the strongest baseline M2PO (+2.1 pts) and 18.5% on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct vs 16.2% for GEPO (+2.3 pts), while avoiding the mid-training reward collapses GEPO and M2PO exhibit.

## 核心贡献

- **问题刻画**：异步 LLM RL 的序列级重要性权重是数千个 per-token 比率的乘积，呈重尾分布（"horizon 诅咒"），少数轨迹主导更新，ESS 低于 batch size，导致梯度方差增大、learner-to-sampler KL 尖峰甚至奖励崩塌。
- **方法（TETPO）**：将每个重要性权重 ρ 替换为 ρ^α，α∈[0,1] 逐 prompt 组自适应选择：α*=max{α: ESS(α)≥κG}，健康组几乎不平滑（α*=1），退化组加强平滑；α=1 保留原 IS 权重，α=0 退化为均匀权重（GRPO 式），但始终不产生零权重掩码（区别于 M2PO 的硬掩码）。
- **理论保证**：(Thm 1) 平滑系数是采样器与学习者之间几何桥接策略 π_α ∝ π_θ^α μ^(1−α) 的精确重要性权重；(Thm 2) ESS(α) 连续单调不增，α* 存在唯一、可由二分法求得（无需 α 下限）；(Thm 3) 自归一化权重的离散度 Σ(w̄−1)²/G = G/ESS−1 ≤ 1/κ−1，梯度范数满足 ‖ḡ‖² ≤ (1/κMG)·Σ‖g‖²，均与策略漂移无关；(Thm 4) 固定 α 下桥接目标与 on-policy 目标之差 ≤ R_max·√(χ²(π_α‖π_θ))，且 α* 是最小化该 χ² 偏差的最小平滑指数。
- **实验**：在 k=32 高陈旧度下，Qwen3-Base-1.7B 和 Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct 上对比 GRPO/GSPO/GEPO/M2PO，五基准平均分均为最优，且训练曲线平滑、无 GEPO/M2PO 式的中途崩塌或衰减。
- **开销**：相比 vanilla GRPO 仅增加 O(n_iter·MG)（24 次二分 + O(G) 自归一化），远小于 per-token 前向/反向传播。

## Key Contributions

- Identifies and formalizes the failure mode of existing stabilizers for off-policy LLM RL at high staleness: M2PO's discrete per-token mask collapses under heterogeneous staleness (its trusted-token fraction becomes erratic when mixed-staleness batches arrive), while GEPO has no explicit variance-control knob and passes through highly concentrated weights when a batch is dominated by a few heavy sequences.
- Proposes TETPO, which replaces each importance weight ρ by ρ^α with a per-prompt-group, data-adaptive exponent α* = max{α∈[0,1] : ESS(α) ≥ κG}, computed by 1-D bisection over [0,1] — a smooth, always-positive reweighting with no explicit zero-weight mask.
- Grounds the method in a geometric-bridge importance identity (Theorem 1): the tempered coefficient ρ^α/Z_α is the exact importance ratio of the bridge policy π_α ∝ π_θ^α μ^(1−α) against μ, recovering π_θ at α=1 and μ at α=0.
- Proves four guarantees: exact bridge target (Thm 1), well-posed unique α* via monotonicity of ESS(α) (Thm 2), a deterministic ESS-controlled dispersion bound Σ(w̄−1)²/G ≤ 1/κ−1 and weighted-gradient-norm bound ‖ḡ‖² ≤ (1/κMG)Σ‖g_m,i‖² that hold independent of π_θ-to-μ divergence — unlike ordinary IS whose variance scales as e^{D₂}−1 (Thm 3), and a fixed-α bridge-target discrepancy bound R_max·√(χ²(π_α‖π_θ)) showing α* is the least-tempered exponent minimizing this χ² bias (Thm 4, Prop 1).
- Empirically outperforms GRPO, GSPO, GEPO, and M2PO on five math-reasoning benchmarks under staleness k=32 on two model scales, with stable training trajectories and negligible computational overhead (24-iteration bisection + O(G) normalization).

## Methodology

**Setting.** Following the off-policy LLM-RL setup of M2PO: a minibatch has M prompts with G completions each; for prompt x_m the completions come from a recorded behaviour policy μ_m (a stale sampler snapshot). The sequence-level ratio is ρ_m,i = π_θ(y_m,i|x_m) = exp(Σ_t log π_θ/μ per token), a product of T per-token ratios with T in the thousands, hence extremely heavy-tailed. The realized ESS, ESS(w) = (Σw_i)²/Σw_i² ∈ [1,B], collapses below the batch size B under staleness.

**Groupwise tempering.** Because the bridge normalizer Z_α(x) is prompt-dependent, tempering is normalized and selected within each prompt group (not across the pooled minibatch). For α∈[0,1], the tempered weights are w̄_m,i(α) = ρ_m,i^α self-normalized within the group, giving groupwise ESS_m(α) ∈ [1,G]. The target-ESS exponent is α*_m = max{α : ESS_m(α) ≥ κG} for target fraction κ∈(0,1). Since ESS_m(0)=G ≥ κG and α→ESS_m(α) is continuous and non-increasing (Theorem 2), α*_m always exists and is found by 24-iteration 1-D bisection (~1e-7 precision); no floor α_min is needed.

**Stop-gradient surrogate.** The exponent α*_m and weights are computed from detached (stop-gradient) ratios; a straight-through surrogate ratio r_m,i(θ) = sg[w̄_m,i(α*_m)]·exp(ℓ_m,i(θ) − sg[ℓ_m,i(θ)]) has forward value w̄ and gradient w̄·∇ℓ. At α*=0 this reduces to a uniform-coefficient, GRPO-style score update on the stale samples. The final loss is a clipped PPO-style surrogate (ε_lo = ε_hi = 0.2) with group-relative advantages, all advantages and per-selection quantities treated as constants w.r.t. θ. Note the learner gradient is a bridge-weighted PPO surrogate, not the exact gradient of the bridge objective (which would include a ∇Z_α,θ term that (7) does not reproduce).

**Two regimes.** Healthy groups (ESS ≥ κG) get α*=1, i.e., plain groupwise self-normalized IS inside the clipped surrogate. Degenerate groups get α*∈[0,1), tempering toward the uniform-weights limit; the group always produces strictly positive weights (Prop 2), unlike M2PO whose greedy mask can zero an entire trust region.

**Theory (Appendix A).** (1) The tempered coefficient ρ^α/Z_α(x) is the exact importance ratio of the geometric-bridge π_α ∝ π_θ^α μ^(1−α) w.r.t. μ (Thm 1). (2) α↦ESS is continuous, non-increasing (strictly when ratios are non-constant), proved via an exponentially tilted law argument (Thm 2). (3) Self-normalization gives (1/G)Σ(w̄−1)² = G/ESS−1 ≤ 1/κ−1 independent of any π_θ-to-μ divergence, and Cauchy–Schwarz + Jensen yield ‖ḡ‖² ≤ (1/κMG)Σ‖g_m,i‖² — a deterministic realized-batch bound, contrasted with ordinary IS whose variance ρ grows as e^{D₂(π_θ‖μ)}−1 (Thm 3). (4) |E_{π_α}[R] − E_{π_θ}[R]| ≤ R_max√(χ²(π_α‖π_θ)), and χ² is non-increasing in α (Prop 1), so α* is the least-tempered feasible exponent minimizing this bound (Thm 4).

**Experimental setup.** Qwen3-Base-1.7B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct post-trained with RL on the SimpleRL-Zoo competition-math split (difficulty 3–5, prompts ≤768 tokens); eval on MATH-500, AMC23, AMC24, AIME24, AIME25 (disjoint sources, n-gram decontamination) with binary math-verify rewards. All methods share AdamW (lr 1e-6, cosine + 0.05 warmup), G=8 completions, M=64 prompts (B=512 sequences), 4096-token completions, 4 GPUs, 3 epochs. Staleness: PipelineRL-style uniform lag with maximum k=32 (realized lag ~ Uniform{1,…,32}, pool of up to 256 checkpoints). TETPO: κ=0.85, floor α_min=0, 24-iteration bisection. Metrics are avg@16 accuracy over 3 seeds.

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen3-Base-1.7B, average over 5 benchmarks (avg@16, 3 seeds):** TETPO reaches **44.4±0.4** vs **42.3±0.5** for the strongest baseline M2PO (**+2.1 pts**), with GSPO at 39.0±0.5, GEPO at 36.2±0.9, and GRPO at 34.2±0.6 — TETPO is best on every column (AIME24 26.6±0.6, AIME25 24.6±0.6, AMC23 54.3±0.5, AMC24 32.8±0.7, MATH-500 83.8±0.3).
- **Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, average:** TETPO **18.5±0.3** vs GEPO **16.2±0.6** (**+2.3 pts**), M2PO 15.6±0.4, GSPO 16.1±0.4, GRPO 12.6±0.5. Largest per-benchmark gaps: AMC24 **14.9±0.9** vs GEPO 10.2±1.1 (**+4.7**), AIME24 **8.2±0.7** vs M2PO 6.6±0.6, and AIME25 **1.6±0.6** where GRPO and GEPO score 0.0±0.0.
- **No-collapse guarantee in practice:** on MATH-500 at staleness k=32 (Figure 1), GEPO collapses sharply around steps ~200 and ~450 as low-ESS batches pass through concentrated weights, M2PO peaks early then declines as its mask grows aggressive, while TETPO rises smoothly to the highest reward — consistent with the deterministic dispersion bound (Thm 3).
- **Overhead:** TETPO adds only O(n_iter·MG) over a vanilla GRPO step — 24-iteration per-group bisections plus O(G) self-normalization — negligible next to the per-token forward/backward passes.
- **Qualitative comparison (Table 2):** TETPO is the only listed method that is simultaneously sequence-granular, smooth (no hard mask/truncation), carries a divergence-free dispersion bound (1/κ−1), and provably adds no zero-weight mask (GSPO/M2PO/SNIS-ESS rely on clipping, hard masks, or hard truncation).

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline theory does not strictly apply to the experiments — and the authors admit it.** Appendix B states that because the rollout sampler uses temperature scaling + top-k/top-p truncation, the experiments log importance ratios computed from the *pre-truncation* temperature-scaled distribution (a proxy ρ̃) rather than exact ratios for any well-defined behaviour policy. Consequently the "exact geometric-bridge importance identity" (Thm 1) and the bridge-target discrepancy bound (Thm 4, Prop 1) hold for exact support-restricted ratios that were **not computed**; only Thms 2–3 and Prop 2 (which hold for any strictly positive ratios) transfer. The abstract's "exact importance weights for a geometric bridge" phrasing oversells what the running system actually satisfies.
- **No ablation over the key hyperparameter κ.** All experiments use κ=0.85, α_min=0, and 24 bisection iterations, but the paper reports no sensitivity analysis for κ, leaving open how much performance depends on this single tuning choice versus the adaptive mechanism itself.
- **Narrow empirical scope.** Two models (1.7B and 3B), one domain (competition math from SimpleRL-Zoo), one staleness regime (k=32, realized lag uniform in 1..32). No results at low staleness (where the method should be nearly inert, α*≈1) or at even higher staleness; no code release mentioned.
- **Sequence-level ratios only.** The method deliberately operates at sequence granularity; the paper itself notes combining TETPO with a per-token mask is future work, so it cannot address token-level creditassignment pathologies M2PO targets.
- **Finite-sample and adaptation bias uncharacterized.** Theorem 4 bounds the fixed-α population discrepancy, but selecting α* from the same batch introduces an adaptation bias the paper explicitly does not analyze; the learner gradient is also a clipped PPO surrogate rather than the true bridge-objective gradient.
- **Near-floor cells weaken part of the evidence.** On Llama-3.2-3B, AIME25 scores for all methods are 0.0–1.6%, and AIME24 spans 1.5–8.2% — differences there are noisy; the +2.3 average gain is carried substantially by AMC24/AMC23/MATH-500.
- On the positive side, the +2.1/+2.3 claims check out exactly against Table 1, baselines are recent and strong, three-seed avg@16 evaluation on small benchmarks (AIME has 30 problems) is the right protocol, and the honest Appendix B ratio discussion is commendable.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated interpolation: one scalar knob α per group smoothly spans full IS (α=1) to uniform GRPO-style weights (α=0), replacing the brittle binary choice between masking (M2PO) and no control (GEPO).
- Genuine theoretical content: the dispersion bound Σ(w̄−1)²/G ≤ 1/κ−1 is deterministic on the realized batch and independent of policy drift — a categorically different guarantee from divergence-scaling IS variance bounds.
- Always-positive weights (Prop 2) directly addresses M2PO's documented mixed-staleness mask-collapse failure mode; the no-collapse claim is validated in the training trajectories (Figure 1).
- Essentially free at runtime (24 bisection iterations per group on already-available log-ratios), making adoption trivial on top of GRPO-style pipelines.
- Strong, current baselines (GRPO, GSPO, GEPO, M2PO — two from ICLR 2026), consistent shared hyperparameters across methods, decontaminated eval sets, and avg@16 × 3 seeds protocol.
- Exceptionally transparent Appendix B, including a precise statement of which theorems survive the approximate-ratio implementation.

## Limitations

- The exact bridge identity and target-discrepancy guarantees do not hold for the truncated-decoding ratios actually used in experiments (pre-truncation proxy ρ̃); logging retained support or post-truncation normalizers is left as future work.
- Finite-sample bias of the self-normalized estimator and the bias from data-adaptive α* selection are not characterized theoretically.
- Sequence-level weighting only; per-token integration is future work.
- Empirical validation limited to two small-ish models, math reasoning, and a single staleness setting (k=32); no κ sensitivity ablation, no code release, and the Llama AIME24/25 numbers are near-floor.
- Workshop-level (OPT2026) review; the learning-theory framing (bias of the adapted estimator) would likely face tougher scrutiny at a main conference.

## Relevance

TETPO 直接命中异步/陈旧 rollout 下 LLM RL 后训练的核心痛点——重尾重要性权重与 ESS 崩塌，其"目标 ESS + 幂平滑"思路可无缝叠加到 GRPO/GSPO 类训练栈上，对我们关注的高效 off-policy RL 训练与方差控制极具参考价值；但需记住其实验只在数学推理小模型上验证，且桥接恒等式在截断解码下只是近似成立。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How sensitive is TETPO to the target ESS fraction κ? Is there a principled way to schedule κ (e.g., anneal from 1 downward) rather than fixing it at 0.85?
- Can the exact support-restricted ratios under top-k/top-p truncation be logged cheaply at rollout time, so Theorems 1 and 4 apply verbatim — and does that change empirical performance?
- How does TETPO behave at low staleness (k≈4–8) and extreme staleness (k≥64), and does the adaptive α* gracefully reduce to vanilla GSPO/SNIS when samples are fresh?
- What is the bias of the data-adaptively selected α* estimator, and can a double-robust correction recover unbiasedness while keeping the dispersion bound?
- Can tempering be composed with per-token mechanisms (à la M2PO) to get both token-level credit assignment and groupwise ESS control, at what additional variance cost?
- Does the approach transfer beyond verifiable math rewards — e.g., to RLHF with learned reward models where R is not θ-independent?
