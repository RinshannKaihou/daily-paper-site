> **一句话总结：** 本文首次大规模刻画"沉默失败"——即测试分数漂亮却违反数据科学方法论（如数据泄漏、不设验证集）的失败模式：对 220 个任务上 9 种 agent×LLM 配置（ReAct 基线、AIDE、MLE evolve × Qwen/Gemini 模型）的 LLM-as-judge 分析发现每种配置至少有一种失败模式出现在 ≥43% 的流水线中，且数据泄漏类失败反而与更好的测试排名正相关，仅看准确率会"奖励"泄漏。

> **TL;DR:** The first large-scale audit of *silent* methodological failures in LLM data-science (DS) agents. Across 220 DS tasks and 9 agent-model configurations (ReAct baseline vs the DS agents AIDE and MLE evolve, on qwen-3.5-4b, qwen-3.8-27b, gemini-3.7-flash), every configuration commits at least one failure mode in ≥43% of its pipelines, judged with 89.5% human agreement (Cohen's κ=0.62). Strikingly, all data-leakage failure modes correlate with *better* test-set rank, while skipping the validation split (FM06) costs more than a full rank position. A controlled 6-test suite and Kaggle comparisons with 1st-place human experts (e.g., agents hit local QWK ≈1.0 on the Problematic Internet Use challenge via a leaked feature vs the human winner's 0.482/0.501) confirm that downstream accuracy alone rewards leakage.

## 核心贡献

- 提出"沉默失败"（silent failures）概念：不违反可执行性、甚至伴随高预测分数、但损害方法论效度的失败（数据泄漏、缺失验证集、不恰当评估等），并论证现有 agentic DS 评测只看下游准确率会掩盖这些问题。
- 构建了包含 15+ 种失败模式（FM01–FM15，分为数据泄漏类与非泄漏类，覆盖任务表述、预处理、数据划分、特征工程、模型选择、评估六个阶段）的分类体系，并用两阶段 LLM-as-judge（先判断失败模式是否适用于该流水线结构，再做二值判定并定位代码行）在 **220 个任务 × 9 种配置**上做大规模分析。
- 设计了一个 **unit-test 式可控测试套件**（6 个精心构造的"埋雷"测试 × 3 个底层数据集，共 17 个任务，每种 agent-task 组合跑 5 次），每个测试针对单一失败模式注入受控扰动，并提供多级提示（hint）以区分"没意识到"与"做不到"。
- 与 Kaggle 竞赛人类专家（各挑战第一名方案）和 AutoGluon 做对比及案例研究，揭示 agent 本地分数虚高（泄漏导致 QWK≈1.0）与专家级策略差距（问题表述、目标函数、验证设计等高层选择）。
- 关键实证发现：数据泄漏失败模式与更好的测试排名一致相关——直接证明 agentic DS 评估必须"超越准确率"（beyond accuracy）；并验证结果不受 LLM 对表格数据记忆的影响（列名随机化 + Wilcoxon 检验，p 值均不显著）。

## Key Contributions

- Defines **silent failures**: task-relevant methodological violations (e.g., data leakage, no validation split) that leave executable code and strong predictive scores intact — a failure class missed by output-based agentic DS evaluation.
- A 15-mode failure taxonomy spanning task formulation, preprocessing, splitting, feature engineering, model selection, and evaluation, applied at scale via a two-stage LLM-as-judge over **220 tasks × 9 agent-LLM configurations** (ReAct, AIDE, MLE evolve × 3 models).
- A reusable **controlled test suite**: 6 "unit-test-style" tasks (17 tasks over 3 datasets, 5 runs each) that each embed one targeted pitfall with multi-level hints, separating failure-to-recognize from failure-to-execute.
- Expert-grounded comparison on 5 Kaggle challenges against 1st-place solutions and AutoGluon, including a case study showing agents achieve inflated local QWK ≈1.0 from the leaked PCIAT_Total feature.
- The central empirical result: **every data-leakage mode correlates with better test-set rank**, proving accuracy-only evaluation can actively reward methodologically invalid pipelines; memorization-robustness checks (column renaming, Wilcoxon tests, all p non-significant) rule out LLM having seen the tabular data.

## Methodology

The paper triangulates three evaluation angles (CWI Amsterdam; project resources public at tri-lab.github.io/ds-agent-failures).

1. **Large-scale failure-mode analysis (LLM-as-judge).** 220 tasks drawn from 5 sources (108 Stable, 44 OpenML, 36 Kaggle, 24 UCI, 8 MLE-bench; 123 regression, 97 classification; 80:20 split). Nine configurations: ReAct (baseline), AIDE (iterative code refinement), MLE evolve (self-evolving multi-agent with progressive Monte Carlo Graph Search; both DS agents capped at 10 steps), each with qwen-3.5-4b, qwen-3.8-27b (locally hosted), or gemini-3.7-flash. A two-stage judge (gemini-3.1-pro) first extracts pipeline structure (which components exist: cleaning, feature engineering, validation, tuning, ensembling; deep-learning/stochastic usage) to filter inapplicable failure modes, then outputs binary per-mode decisions plus offending code lines, with an "other" escape category. Judge reliability: 89.5% agreement with a human annotator over 285 failure-mode ratings on 19 pipelines (κ=0.62, 95% CI [0.50,0.73]; precision 0.71, recall 0.65). Confound controls: leaderboard solutions from Kaggle, and a memorization-robustness test (column-header completion to estimate memorization; randomizing column names and metadata; Wilcoxon tests on high vs low memorization subsets).

2. **Curated test suite.** Six designed tests (Table 2), each instantiating one failure mode through a controlled perturbation: TFC1 class imbalance, TFC2 inconsistent formatting, TFC3 spurious correlation vs causation, TFC4 missing domain-specific feature transformation, TFC5 leakage in feature selection, TFC6 wrong metric under imbalance. Each agent-task-hint combination runs 5 times; the judge checks for a predeclared set of acceptable treatments. Multi-level hints (from none to explicit) probe whether failures stem from recognizing the issue or executing the practice.

3. **Expert comparison on Kaggle.** Agent pipelines are submitted to 5 Kaggle challenges with leaderboards (Single-Cell Perturbation, Heart Disease, Obesity Risk, Problematic Internet Use, Survival Predictions); performance is compared to AutoGluon and the 1st-place human solution, plus a detailed case study on the Problematic Internet Use challenge (test split contains PCIAT_Total, derived from the target sii).

## Results & Highlights

- **Failures are ubiquitous across all configurations.** Every one of the 9 agent-model configurations exhibits at least one failure mode in ≥43% of its pipelines. Frameworks shift failures rather than remove them: with gemini-3.7-flash, AIDE and MLE evolve cut ReAct's "No val split" (FM06) rate from 0.40 to 0.00, and MLE evolve slashes "No feature engineering" from 0.90 to 0.04 — yet AIDE uses a single evaluation metric (FM15) in 85–88% of its pipelines vs only 15% (ReAct, qwen-3.5-4b) and 14% (ReAct, gemini-3.7-flash), and AIDE *raises* "No feature engineering" from 0.50 to 0.71 with qwen-3.8-27b.
- **Leakage correlates with better test rank.** In the rank-residual analysis (Figure 2, per-configuration leaderboard ranking on the held-out set), all data-leakage modes push residual rank positive (worse-rank→"better score" direction), while non-leakage modes push negative; skipping the validation split (FM06) costs on average **more than one full rank position**, whereas using a single eval metric (FM15) is paradoxically associated with a *better* rank.
- **Capability covers the stages, not the practices.** Stronger models/bigger frameworks commit more leakage-type errors: gemini-3.7-flash ignores temporal order in >⅓ of applicable pipelines for *all three* agents; imputation-before-split rises from 0.00 (qwen-3.5-4b ReAct) to 0.08 (qwen-3.8-27b ReAct); and 50% of MLE evolve + gemini-3.7-flash pipelines — the configuration with the *fewest* in-taxonomy failures, despite MLE evolve shipping dedicated leakage checks — leak between validation and train data. 37% of pipelines carry judge-flagged failures outside the fixed taxonomy (most commonly leakage from validation into train).
- **Controlled suite confirms persistent failure patterns.** On the 6-test suite (17 tasks, 5 runs each), correlation-vs-causation (TFC3) and feature-selection leakage (TFC5) reach a failure rate of **1.0 across all six agent-model configurations**; messy-data normalization (TFC2) fails every run in five configurations (ReAct + gemini-3.7-flash: 0.8). Explicit hints drive ReAct and AIDE toward ~0 failure, but MLE evolve plateaus far higher — elaborate search pipelines fail to carry explicit constraints through optimization. gpt-5.6-sol beats gemini-3.7-flash only on some tests (ReAct TFC1, TFC4), so model capability gains do not uniformly transfer to methodological decisions.
- **Agents fall short of experts and inflate their own scores.** On the Kaggle Problematic Internet Use challenge, ReAct and AIDE select the leaked PCIAT_Total feature and reach local QWK ≈1.0 — far above the 1st-place human score of 0.4820/0.5010 (private/public) — yet would fail under competition conditions; agents' actual leaderboard QWK is 0.4480/0.3680 (ReAct) vs 1st place 0.4820/0.5010. On Obesity Risk, agents (0.9065–0.9071 public accuracy) approach AutoGluon (0.9074/0.9075) and 1st place (0.9116/0.9234) on playground tasks, but on Survival Predictions MLE evolve scores C-index 0.5155/0.5233 vs AIDE's 0.6536/0.6488 and 1st place's 0.7913/0.6981 — MLE evolve optimized on the inverted ranking, a simple error that dominates downstream score. No agent dominates across the five challenges.
- **Judge reliability & confound checks.** 89.5% human agreement over 285 ratings (κ=0.62); memorization tests show no significant performance differences between high- and low-memorization task subsets (all Wilcoxon p non-significant on three DS agent frameworks with gemini-3.7-flash).

## Authors & Credibility

The team is from Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica (CWI), Amsterdam — the Dutch national research center for mathematics and computer science, with a strong track record in data systems and AutoML. Madelon Hulsebos (previously works on table-understanding corpora such as GitTables) and Cornelius Wolff lead the effort; the project maintains public resources (tri-lab.github.io/ds-agent-failures/). Funding: NWO AiNed Fellowship, SAP, plus Google/OpenAI compute grants; work used the Dutch national e-infrastructure (SURF). The paper includes an explicit AI-use statement and reports that a human author validated judge outputs, figures, and generated code. The evaluation is notably careful about confounds (memorization controls, judge-human agreement, budget-matched agent steps), which speaks to methodological maturity, though the group is not a veteran AutoML benchmarking group like the TPOT/AutoGluon teams.

## Critical Assessment

- **The judge's own noise is material to the headline claims.** Precision 0.71 / recall 0.65 over 19 pipelines and 285 ratings means roughly a third of present failures are missed and ~29% of flags are false positives — and the two headline results (≥43% prevalence; leakage modes correlating with better rank in Figure 2) are computed from these judge labels, with no larger human re-annotation. The two-stage filtering mitigates but does not remove this.
- **The leakage-rank correlation is observational, not causal.** Figure 2 compares each pipeline's rank to its configuration-expected rank conditioned on FM presence; pipelines that leak are not randomly assigned, so confounding (e.g., which pipelines attempt feature engineering at all) is not ruled out. The direction is plausible and consistent with the Kaggle case study, but the paper's framing "leakage leads to stronger performance" overreaches the design.
- **The ≥43% headline mixes stakes.** The aggregate counts benign modes such as FM15 (single eval metric — the paper itself shows it correlates with *better* rank) alongside serious leakage modes; a prevalence number restricted to leakage-class modes would be a fairer alarm, and is not reported as a single headline figure.
- **Narrow model coverage at scale.** The 220-task analysis covers two Qwen open-weights models and one Gemini model; gpt-5.6-sol appears only on the 17-task curated suite. Whether frontier closed models (GPT-5-class, Claude) exhibit the same patterns on the large suite is untested, even though the paper's recommendations target frontier-model-driven agent design.
- **Small curated suite.** Six tests over three underlying datasets (17 tasks) is thin ground for the strong claim that failures persist "across configurations"; TFC3/TFC5's 1.0 failure rate is striking but n=3 datasets.
- **Budget and design choices could move results.** AIDE and MLE evolve are capped at 10 steps; judge-applicability filters define failure applicability from the pipeline itself (a pipeline that never does feature engineering cannot "fail" at scaling-before-split), which shapes per-mode denominators. Neither choice is ablated.
- Genuine strengths: the taxonomy is grounded in DS best practice rather than generic agent-trace taxonomies (Cemri et al. 2025 focus on execution/planning, not statistical validity), the memorization and hint-level controls are thoughtful, and the Kaggle case study concretely demonstrates the local-score inflation mechanism. This is a real contribution, not a rubber-stamp benchmark.

## Strengths

- First large-scale, methodology-level (not execution-level) failure analysis of agentic DS: 220 tasks × 9 configurations with a principled two-stage judge and published reliability numbers.
- The central finding — leakage modes correlate with better test rank while skill modes correlate with worse rank — is quantitatively demonstrated and directly motivates "beyond accuracy" evaluation.
- Triangulation across naturalistic (220 tasks), controlled (unit-test suite with hint levels), and expert-referenced (Kaggle leaderboards) evidence, plus confound controls for LLM memorization.
- Actionable design guidance distilled from the expert case study (problem formulation, task-specific objectives, late threshold optimization, multi-seed 10-fold CV, model diversity) rather than vague "agents should be careful."
- Reproducibility posture: public project resources, budget-matched agent configurations, AI-use statement, human validation of artifacts.

## Limitations

- Judge precision/recall (0.71/0.65) bounds the accuracy of every downstream prevalence and correlation claim; no large-scale human audit.
- Only 3 agent frameworks, 3 LLMs at scale (one closed); frontier models under-tested; AIDE/MLE evolve capped at 10 steps.
- Curated suite is small (6 tests, 3 datasets); results may not generalize to failure modes outside the 6 instantiated ones.
- "Other"-category failures (37% of pipelines) are analyzed only qualitatively in the appendix, so the fixed-taxonomy picture is admittedly incomplete.
- No causal identification for the leakage-performance association; no intervention study (e.g., adding leakage checkers and measuring failure-rate change) beyond observing that MLE evolve's built-in checks fail 50% of the time.
- Metrics-side blind spot: the evaluation set's own labels and splits are taken as given; agent behavior on truly novel task types (time series, text) is not covered (tabular regression/classification only).

## Relevance

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the leakage-vs-rank association be established causally via controlled injection (same pipeline, with/without the leaked feature), rather than observational rank residuals?
- Would a verifier-in-the-loop agent (dedicated methodological checker with veto power) eliminate the failure modes that MLE evolve's built-in leakage checks miss (50% of its gemini-3.7-flash pipelines)?
- Do frontier closed models (GPT-5-class, Claude) on the full 220-task suite show the same "capacity covers stages but not practices" pattern, or is it an artifact of the Qwen/Gemini-flash class?
- Can the two-stage judge be pushed to precision/recall >0.9 (e.g., with code-grounded unit tests instead of LLM judgment), making silent-failure detection cheap enough to run inside every evaluation?
- The curated suite shows hints fix ReAct/AIDE but not MLE evolve — is the failure "recognition vs propagation" hypothesis testable by instrumenting the search loop's constraint memory?
