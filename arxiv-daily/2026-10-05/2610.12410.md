> **一句话总结：** 作者从 Anthropic Constitution 提炼 66 个值，在 Olmo-3-7B/32B、Qwen3-8B、Qwen3-30B-A3B 四个模型上用单值 DPO 和 SFT 各微调一遍，测出 49,666 次测量的"对齐泛化矩阵"（训一个值对其余值 adherence 的迁移效应），发现基于激活的表征相似度远比文本描述嵌入更能预测这种泛化：persona vectors 达 ρ=0.45 而 description embedding 仅 ρ=0.05（可预测上限 0.89）；同一表征定义的"目标一致性"还能预测 64 个六值 DPO 微调模型的对抗 prefill 鲁棒性（ρ=0.43, p=5×10⁻⁴，而描述嵌入 ρ=0.12 不显著），并据此提出 VALUEMAP，把 266 个真实用户-LLM 交互中的值聚成 attunement/rigor/stewardship/integrity 四类，调整轮廓系数 2.61 显著优于 Values in the Wild（0.95）和 LimnusValues（1.62）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper formalizes *alignment generalization prediction*: given that a model is fine-tuned to follow one value, predict how its adherence to all other values shifts. Training single-value DPO (~1,000 preference pairs/value) and SFT (5,000 synthetic prompts/value) checkpoints for 4 open models (Olmo-3-7B/32B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B) on 66 values extracted from Anthropic's Constitution, they build a 49,666-measurement generalization matrix over 11,188 LLM-generated ConflictScope value-conflict scenarios. Among five representation methods, similarity of activation-based embeddings predicts the ground-truth matrix far better than the description-embedding baseline used by prior taxonomies: PERSONA vectors reach Spearman ρ=0.45 (GRADIENT 0.42) vs ρ=0.05 for DESCRIPTION-EMBD, against a predictability ceiling of 0.89. Applied downstream, PERSONA-derived coherence predicts prefill (adversarial anti-target) robustness of 64 six-value Qwen3-8B DPO fine-tunes at ρ=0.43 vs ρ=0.12 (n.s.) for descriptions, and the VALUEMAP taxonomy built with k-medoids on persona vectors recovers generalization structure better than Values in the Wild (adjusted silhouette 2.61 vs 0.95).

## 核心贡献

- **新任务定义**：首次大规模实证研究"对齐泛化预测"（alignment generalization prediction）——在训练前预测「对模型做单值 v₁ 微调会如何改变其对保持值 v₂ 的遵循程度」，为对齐目标（alignment target）设计提供经验依据。
- **大规模泛化矩阵**：66 个 CONSTITUTION 值 × 4 个开源基座模型（Olmo-3-7B、Olmo-3-32B、Qwen3-8B、Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base）× 2 种训练方式（单值 DPO、单值 SFT），共 49,666 条测量，基于 ConflictScope 生成的 11,188 个值冲突场景。
- **表征方法基准**：系统对比 5 种值表征（DESCRIPTION-EMBD、BEHAVIOR-EMBD、WEIGHT task vectors、PERSONA vectors、GRADIENT 更新方向），发现基于"模型在上下文中应用该值时的激活"的表征显著优于基于文本描述的表征（ρ=0.45 vs 0.05）。
- **共享值空间的初步证据**：8 个「模型×训练方式」组合的 66×66 persona-vector 相似度矩阵之间平均 Spearman ρ=0.54（两两相关 0.75–0.93），提示不同模型以相似方式编码值空间。
- **两个下游应用**：(1) PERSONA 一致性可预测多值对齐目标微调后模型的对抗 prefill 鲁棒性；(2) VALUEMAP——一种以泛化动力学为落地的无监督值分类法，在 266 个 Values in the Wild 值上聚出 attunement/rigor/stewardship/integrity 四类，优于既有文本驱动分类。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes and releases the first large-scale empirical study of *alignment generalization prediction*: a 49,666-measurement generalization matrix G over 66 Constitution values, computed across 4 base model families and both single-value DPO and SFT, using a bounded variant of the ConflictScope steerability metric (G = fraction of the remaining alignment gap toward v₂ closed by fine-tuning on v₁, clipped to [0,1]).
- Benchmarks five candidate value representations on this prediction task and shows activations-based/behavioral methods dominate textual-description embeddings: aggregate Spearman ρ of 0.45 (PERSONA), 0.42 (GRADIENT), 0.35 (BEHAVIOR-EMBD), 0.23 (WEIGHT) vs 0.05 (DESCRIPTION-EMBD), with a 0.89 ceiling; GRADIENT is strongest on DPO settings (0.54 on Olmo-3-7B) and PERSONA on SFT, and prediction works with generic elicitation data, not value-specific curated data.
- Provides preliminary evidence for a model-independent latent value space: the eight 66×66 PERSONA similarity matrices correlate at mean ρ=0.54 (0.75–0.93 pairwise).
- Demonstrates two applications: (1) coherence of a multi-value alignment target measured with PERSONA embeddings significantly predicts the adversarial-prefill robustness of 64 Qwen3-8B six-value DPO fine-tunes (ρ=0.43, p=5×10⁻⁴), while description-based coherence does not (ρ=0.12, p=0.37); (2) VALUEMAP, an unsupervised taxonomy pipeline (k-medoids, k=4, on persona vectors of Olmo-3.1-32B-SFT), clusters the 266 Values-in-the-Wild values into attunement (n=70), rigor (n=55), stewardship (n=89), and integrity (n=52), achieving the best adjusted silhouette (2.61 vs 0.95 VITW, 1.62 LimnusValues).

## Methodology

**Value sets.** CONSTITUTION: starting from a public decomposition of Anthropic's Constitution into 211 tenets, an LLM-as-a-judge filter (gpt-5.6-i) keeps tenets that are well-defined behavioral traits, identifiable from a single prompt–response, and whose negative pole is generatable; after manual deduplication this yields 66 values with standardized gerund-style definitions. VITW: 266 L1 values from Huang et al. (2025)'s Values in the Wild, used only for taxonomization.

**Training.** Two post-training regimes per value. Single-value DPO: a 19,748-example value-neutral SFT subset of Tulu 3 (cleaned of Constitution-relevant examples) first makes base models instruction followers; value-specific preference pairs are harvested from public preference datasets (HH-RLHF, PKU-SafeRLHF, HelpSteer2, UltraFeedback, WildFeedback, Community-Alignment) via preference-pair extraction with clear value gaps, assembling ~1,000 non-overlapping pairs per value. Single-value SFT: 5,000 value-relevant synthetic prompts per value; completions are generated toward the target value and filtered so only value-aligned responses remain, and each base model is fine-tuned on all 66 datasets. Four base models are used: Olmo-3-7B, Olmo-3-32B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base.

**Ground-truth generalization effects.** ConflictScope generates value-conflict scenarios ⟨d, A, v₁, v₂⟩; after embedding-based filtering of highly similar value pairs (cosine > 0.5 via all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and quality filtering, 11,188 scenarios remain. An LLM judge scores how aligned each model's actions are to v₂ in every scenario involving v₂, yielding per-model alignment scores. The generalization metric G(v₁,v₂) = (a′₂ − a₂)/(a*₂ − a₂), clipped to [0,1], measures the fraction of the alignment gap toward v₂ closed by fine-tuning on v₁ (1 = full transfer, 0 = no effect, −1 = misalignment); clipping yields the 49,666-measurement matrix.

**Candidate representations.** All non-baseline methods use base-model responses to a generic value elicitation dataset (value-supporting vs value-opposing responses; no privileged training/evaluation data). DESCRIPTION-EMBD embeds the value definition with all-mpnet-base-v2 (mimicking Values in the Wild). BEHAVIOR-EMBD averages the embedder differences between pro- and anti-value responses. WEIGHT takes the difference of the two fine-tunes' weights (task vectors), layer-selected by held-out steering. PERSONA computes per-layer mean-activation differences and selects the layer steering held-out prompts most (Chen et al. 2025). GRADIENT averages the DPO-loss gradient w.r.t. residual-stream activations on the preference pairs (Sim et al. 2026), layer-matched to the persona sweep. Quality = Spearman ρ between the method's pairwise cosine-similarity grid and G (off-diagonal), with 95% CIs via bootstrapping; a ceiling (0.89) is set by correlating G with its symmetrized self.

**Applications.** Prefill robustness: define a target's coherence as the average pairwise cosine similarity of its value embeddings; train Qwen3-8B via DPO on each of 64 six-value targets (stratified over a wide coherence range), then measure the normalized rate at which target-aligned responses survive an adversarial anti-target prefill. Taxonomy: select embedding+clustering (PERSONA + k-medoids, k=4) by adjusted silhouette S* (chance-corrected over 2,000 random partitions) on CONSTITUTION, then apply unchanged to the 266 VITW values, comparing recovered cluster structure against Values in the Wild and LimnusValues on the 49×66 generalization matrices.

## Results & Highlights

- **Activations beat descriptions at predicting generalization.** Aggregate over 8 model×method combinations, PERSONA similarity correlates with the ground-truth 49,666-measurement matrix at Spearman ρ = 0.45 ± 0.07 and GRADIENT at 0.42 ± 0.05, versus 0.35 ± 0.06 for BEHAVIOR-EMBD, 0.23 ± 0.05 for WEIGHT, and only 0.05 ± 0.03 for the DESCRIPTION-EMBD baseline — against a predictability ceiling of 0.89.
- **Method–regime match.** GRADIENT (a DPO-loss derivative) is strongest exactly where it is trained: ρ = 0.54 on Olmo-3-7B DPO and 0.50 on Qwen3-8B DPO (vs WEIGHT's 0.19 and 0.29 in the same settings), but drops to 0.24 on Olmo-3-32B SFT; PERSONA is the most uniform (0.39–0.51 across all eight settings), while the description baseline never exceeds 0.10 in any setting.
- **Shared value space across models.** The 66×66 PERSONA similarity matrices of the eight independently trained model×method pairs correlate pairwise at 0.75–0.93 (mean Spearman ρ = 0.54; e.g., Olmo-3-7B SFT vs Qwen3-8B SFT = 0.81, Qwen3-8B DPO vs Qwen3-8B SFT = 0.83), supporting a largely model-independent encoding of value relationships.
- **Coherence predicts adversarial robustness.** Across 64 six-value Qwen3-8B DPO fine-tunes, alignment-target coherence computed with PERSONA embeddings correlates with prefill robustness at ρ = 0.43 (p = 5×10⁻⁴), and the correlation also holds under the evaluation metrics of Maya et al. (2025) and Surgeon et al. (2026); DESCRIPTION-EMBD-derived coherence is not predictive (ρ = 0.12, p = 0.37).
- **VALUEMAP out-taxonomizes text-based taxonomies.** Clustering the 266 VITW values with persona vectors + k-medoids (k=4 → attunement n=70, rigor n=55, stewardship n=89, integrity n=52), VALUEMAP-Olmo-32B achieves the highest aggregate adjusted silhouette at 2.61 ± 0.13, versus 1.62 ± 0.17 for LimnusValues and 0.95 ± 0.09 for Values in the Wild (per-setting, e.g., Qwen3-8B DPO: 3.02 vs 1.94 vs 0.99), and it also transfers best to generalization matrices computed on models/value sets not used to build it.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline number is modest, not just the baseline.** Even the best representation reaches ρ = 0.45 against a 0.89 ceiling, so the activations-based methods explain roughly half of the predictable structure of G; calling these "general-purpose value embeddings" (as the intro implies) is ahead of the evidence, and the authors' own conclusion concedes better predictors are needed.
- **Part of the gap over DESCRIPTION-EMBD is elicitation data, not internals.** Swapping the same all-mpnet-base-v2 embedder from a one-line definition to pro/anti behavioral responses lifts ρ from 0.05 to 0.35 (BEHAVIOR-EMBD) — the paper honestly notes this, but the abstract's "0.45 vs 0.05" framing still conflates "model internals matter" with "behavioral examples matter."
- **The ground truth is a synthetic, LLM-judged construct.** G is measured on 11,188 ConflictScope scenarios generated by an LLM, filtered by MiniLM embeddings and an LLM judge's adherence scores; the 0.89 ceiling itself (correlating G with its symmetrized self) shows how much asymmetric noise the matrix carries. Whether these single-turn synthetic conflicts capture real-world value tension is untested.
- **Narrow robustness experiment.** The prefill-robustness application uses exactly one model (Qwen3-8B), one post-training method (DPO), and 64 six-value targets; no RLHF/RLAIF or frontier-scale replication is attempted, and the result rides on stratified target selection chosen by the authors.
- **Internal validation for the taxonomy.** VALUEMAP's superiority is scored by adjusted silhouette on the same style of generalization matrices that motivated the method; there is no human or downstream behavioral validation of the four categories, only a qualitative remark that they "partially resemble" the original Wild taxonomy.
- **Value-set curation narrows scope.** Filtering requires a generatable negative pole and single-turn identifiability, which excludes hard constraints and catastrophic-risk tenets by construction; conclusions may not transfer to safety-critical values, and the pipeline depends on specific GPT-5.6 variants for filtering and normalization.
- On the positive side, reproducibility is genuinely strong: open-weight models, released code/data, appendix prompts, bootstrapped CIs, and a ceiling-calibrated evaluation metric — far above the norm for this genre.

## Strengths

- A genuinely new, well-posed prediction task with an unusually large controlled artifact: 49,666 ground-truth measurements across 4 model families and 2 post-training regimes, all from open-weight checkpoints.
- Fair, ceiling-calibrated comparison of five representation families under identical elicitation data, with the GRADIENT-vs-PERSONA split across DPO/SFT settings giving mechanistic insight rather than a single winner.
- Practical payoff shown twice: coherence as a cheap pre-training predictor of adversarial robustness, and a taxonomy that demonstrably tracks generalization dynamics better than the text-based taxonomies the field currently uses.
- Honest reporting throughout — confidence intervals, a predictability ceiling, an explicit bounded variant of the steerability metric, and acknowledgment that generic elicitation data suffices (no privileged data advantage).

## Limitations

- Best predictability is ρ = 0.45 (ceiling 0.89); none of the five methods is yet reliable enough to replace a real training run for target design.
- All models are open-weight 7B–32B dense/MoE (Olmo-3, Qwen3); nothing is shown about frontier or heavily RLHF-ed models, where value encoding may differ.
- Ground truth rests on synthetic ConflictScope scenarios and LLM-judge adherence scores; conclusions inherit those construct-validity limits.
- The robustness and taxonomy applications are each demonstrated on a single model and a single configuration (Qwen3-8B DPO; Olmo-3.1-32B-SFT embeddings), without ablations over those choices.
- The value set excludes extreme-risk/hard-constraint tenets by design, and the taxonomy metric (adjusted silhouette) is internal rather than externally validated.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do activations-based predictors keep their edge for RL-based post-training (RLHF/GRPO) rather than DPO and SFT, or does the GRADIENT-method advantage shift with on-policy updates?
- Can value embeddings predict *negative* generalization (emergent misalignment, sycophancy side effects documented in prior work) rather than only positive transfer to held-out values?
- Would a frontier-scale, instruction-tuned model close the gap between description and activation embeddings, or does the 0.45-vs-ceiling gap indicate missing structure in how traits are encoded in fine-tuning data?
- Can VALUEMAP's four categories be validated behaviorally — e.g., do cluster-level conflict rates or robustness profiles differ — instead of relying on adjusted silhouette on the same matrices used to design the method?
- How much of the remaining headroom to the 0.89 ceiling is label noise in the LLM-judged generalization matrix versus genuinely uninformative representations?
