# The Winner's Curse in LLM Self-Improvement Loops: Selection Noise, Lock-in, and Acceptance Rules

> **一句话总结：** 把 LLM 自改进循环中的"比基线好就保留"步骤建模为测量噪声下的选择问题，作者推导出胜者诅咒的解析规模，并在 Qwen 循环和 GEPA/MIPROv2 的验证分数上实证确认：16 项选择集上报的增益比 held-out 增益虚高 13–20 个点（256 项时缩到 1–5 点），而测过的各种验收规则（McNemar、PACE、select-then-confirm、校准贝叶斯门）在完整循环上都不如朴素的贪心接受。

> **TL;DR:** This paper models the "keep-if-better" step of LLM self-improvement loops (prompt/program optimizers rewriting their own instructions) as best-of-K selection under correlated measurement noise, and quantifies the resulting winner's curse. In oracle-instrumented runs where Qwen2.5 models rewrite their own instructions on TREC-50, Banking77, and GSM8K with every candidate also scored on a 600-item held-out gold set, at a 16-item selection set the winner's held-out differential is only 1–51% of its measured one, and the noise model's implied ratio matches the observed ratio across nine settings with MAE 0.034. A pre-registered confirmatory study shows greedy loops' final selection-set scores exceed held-out accuracy by 13–20 points at n=16 (e.g., +15.9 ± 2.7 points on 1.5B TREC, Holm p=0.002) versus 1–5 points at n=256, while none of the tested acceptance rules — McNemar at α=0.05, PACE, select-then-confirm, pilot-calibrated Bayes gates — beats plain greedy acceptance over whole runs (the Bayes gate is 2.1 ± 1.1 points *below* greedy pooled over 32 seed pairs). The same inflation appears in the validation scores of GEPA (38.8 points implied vs 19.2 real on TREC) and MIPROv2. The recommended remedy is cheap: score the seed and incumbent on 64 never-used items to remove the bias of the reported gain, and always report held-out gains with uncertainty.

## 核心贡献

- **理论模型：** 把 keep-if-better 循环的每代决策写成高斯选择模型（候选共享与当前指令和评测项相关的共同误差 c + 各自特异误差 η），区分两个量：程序的 *inflation*（选择集分数 − held-out 准确率）和增益的 *overstatement*（选择集上测得的增益 − held-out 增益）。推导出三个命题：胜者诅咒的解析规模（优化者/育种者方程式 E|Δ̂_k − Δ_k| = (1−h²_w)σ_w κ_K + E[c]）、最优短视接受规则是一个校准阈值（贪心 τ=0 仅在 μ=0 时最优）、以及纯噪声下的 Goodhart 退化区（每代 held-out 漂移 −|μ|）。
- **Oracle 实证：** 在每个候选都被 600 项 gold 集打分的 instrumented 运行中（55–152 候选/设置），首次确认：(a) 种子指令之后的大多数改写是有害的（首代 TREC 上 75–100% 的候选有改进，之后仅 4–39%）；(b) 胜者诅咒的实测规模与噪声模型预测一致（200 次划分、九个设置、MAE 0.034/0.064）；(c) 在 n=16 时胜者的 held-out 选择差仅为测量值的 1–51%。
- **预注册确认研究：** 分离了"改评估集大小"与"给 proposer 的失败样本"两个混淆因素（新 gold 集 + 独立 256 项失败池），五假设事先写定并在开跑 10 分钟时归档。核心发现 H2：n=16 时循环自我报告的最终 proxy 比 held-out 高 13–20 点，n=256 时仅 1–5 点（配对差约 15 点，三个设置 Holm p ≤ 0.04）；held-out 增益随 n 在 TREC 上增长（1.5B：13.7±2.9 → 24.2±0.7）但在 GSM8K 上不增长（+1.6±2.2，p=0.483）。
- **验收规则的否定结果：** McNemar(α=0.05)、PACE（anytime-valid 测试）、select-then-confirm、先导校准贝叶斯门（含 Equation (3) 的后验均值阈值）在完整循环上都不优于贪心：贝叶斯门在 n=16 合并后比贪心低 2.1±1.1 点（GSM8K 上 5.7±1.6 点显著更差），固定 McNemar 在 TREC 上放弃 −7.4/−12.3 点。复用选择集时现任者的正选择误差进入每次比较（"lock-in"），使贪心反而更保守。
- **当前模型与真实优化器的退化：** Qwen3.5-4B 自改时第一代改写就交付最终增益的 90–112%；从"有能力的起点"重启时仅 5% 的 TREC 改写有改进（均值效应 −14 点），但 16 项循环仍报告 12.5 和 14.1 点增益而 held-out 实际变化 −2.2 和 0.0。GEPA 在 1000 次调用后 TREC 真实增益约 19 点，其 16 项验证分数却暗示 38.8 点；MIPROv2 把验证集从 16 扩到 256 项使 TREC held-out 增益从 5.8 升到 11.4 点（配对差 5.6±0.7，p=0.001，6/6 种子）。
- **可操作建议：** 在 64 个从未用于选择的项上给种子和现任者各打一次分（19 代后仅需 128 次评估，约为 n=16 名义预算 1,280 的十分之一）即可消除报告增益的偏差并把 RMSE 从 15.5 降到 5.7 点；同时建议所有自改进研究随选择分一起报告 held-out 分数及其标准误。

## Key Contributions

- **A selection model of keep-if-better loops:** Under a Gaussian model where all candidates in a generation share a common error (same incumbent, same items) plus idiosyncratic noise, Proposition 1 gives the winner's-curse size of a generation's best candidate, E|Δ̂_k − Δ_k| = (1−h²_w)σ_w κ_K + E[c] — the breeder's equation plus regression to the mean plus shared item/incumbent error; Proposition 2 derives the myopic Bayes-optimal threshold (greedy τ=0 is optimal only when the proposal bias μ = 0); Proposition 3 characterizes a Goodhart regime where greedy commits with probability increasing in K and decreasing in n while held-out performance drifts down by |μ| per generation.
- **Oracle-instrumented empirical validation:** In runs where every candidate is additionally scored on a 600-item gold set (55–152 candidates per setting, three models, two inference stacks, n ∈ 8..256), most proposals after the first rewrite are harmful (only 4–39% improve over the incumbent after generation 1), the noise model predicts the observed true-to-measured selection-differential ratio with MAE 0.034 (empirical) / 0.064 (Gaussian), and at n=16 the winner's held-out differential is only 1–51% of its measured value. A causal, log-based estimate of h²_w shrinks winners' measured advantages to within 0.6 points of the held-out mean (vs 5.0 points unshrunk at n=16).
- **Pre-registered confirmatory study (the paper's core evidence):** With 16 selection items, greedy loops' final self-reported scores exceed held-out accuracy by 13–20 points across four settings (e.g., +15.9 ± 2.7 on 1.5B TREC, Holm p=0.002); with 256 items the gap is only 1–5 points. Held-out gains grow with selection-set size on TREC (1.5B: 13.7 ± 2.9 → 24.2 ± 0.7 points; 7B: 14.0 ± 0.7 → 20.4 ± 0.6) but not on GSM8K (+1.6 ± 2.2, p=0.483), and the exploratory Goodhart loss on Banking77 did not replicate.
- **A negative result on acceptance rules:** Over whole runs on a reused selection set, none of McNemar (α=0.05), PACE's anytime-valid test, select-then-confirm, or pilot-calibrated Bayes gates beats greedy acceptance — the calibrated gate ends 2.1 ± 1.1 points below greedy pooled at n=16 (significantly worse on GSM8K, −5.7 ± 1.6) — because incumbent inflation acts as an unmodeled implicit threshold ("lock-in") that makes greedy conservative in exactly the regime where calibrated gates pay their evaluation cost.
- **Inflation in current models and real optimizers:** Qwen3.5-4B refining a competent instruction gains ~0 held-out while 16-item loops report 12.5 and 14.1 points (overstatement 16.3 → 1.4 points as n goes 16 → 256); GEPA's 16-item validation score implies 38.8 points on TREC where the real gain is 19.2 (26.5 vs −1.9 overstatement vs start, p=0.007), and MIPROv2 gains 11.4 held-out points on TREC with 256 validation items vs 5.8 with 16 (paired 5.6 ± 0.7, p=0.001).
- **A cheap bias-removal audit:** Scoring the seed and current incumbent after 19 generations on 64 never-used items costs 128 evaluations (~10% of the 1,280-evaluation nominal budget at n=16), removes the average bias of the loop's reported gain, and cuts its root-mean-square error from 15.5 to 5.7 points.

## Methodology

**Model.** An artifact c (instruction, program, memory, scaffold) conditions a fixed solver. At each generation a proposer draws K = 4 rewrites of the incumbent; each candidate is scored on a selection set D of n items, yielding paired mean-difference estimates Δ̂_k. Greedy keep-if-better commits k = argmax Δ̂_k iff Δ̂_k > 0. Assumption 1 (Gaussian selection model): true effects Δ_k ~ N(μ, s²) with μ the proposal bias and s the proposal spread; measurements are Δ̂_k = Δ_k + c + η_k where the shared error c ~ N(0, e_c²) (all candidates face the same incumbent and the same items) and idiosyncratic errors η_k ~ N(0, e_η²) are independent. Noise variances are identified from item vectors: e_c² + e_η² = v/n and e_c² = v_cc/n with v_cc the per-item co-disagreement between two candidates. Two target quantities are defined throughout: *inflation* (selection-set score minus held-out accuracy) and *overstatement* (measured gain minus held-out gain).

**Theory.** Proposition 1 gives the selection differential and overstatement via the breeder's-equation factor h²_w = s²/(s² + e²) and the expected maximum of K normals κ_K; conditioning on acceptance (Δ̂_k > 0) raises the bias — a single candidate with true effect −ε and noise e has accepted-candidate mean error ≈ 1.53e. Proposition 2 derives the myopic Bayes commit: commit the candidate with the largest posterior mean iff positive, which with independent noise reduces to a threshold τ* = −μe²/s² on the measured gain, so greedy (τ=0) is optimal only when most proposals are harmless (μ=0). Proposition 3 shows a Goodhart regime (s=0, μ<0, fresh sets): commit probability rises with K and falls with n while held-out drifts down |μ| per generation. Reused sets change the picture: the incumbent's positive selection error enters every later comparison with a negative sign (an implicit threshold the fresh-set model cannot identify) — the "lock-in" mechanism.

**Experiments.** Solver = proposer = Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct (exploratory + pre-registered confirmatory studies; Qwen2.5-14B as stronger proposer; Qwen3.5-4B self-loop in §5.4), served with vLLM. Each generation the proposer sees the current instruction, the task description, and 4 failures, and writes 4 rewrites. Tasks: TREC-50, Banking77, GSM8K with one-sentence seed instructions; each task has a 600-item *gold* set excluded from selection by item ID (gold accuracy = held-out; selection-set score = proxy; proxy − held-out = inflation). Candidate held-out effects are estimated with 1.5–2 point standard errors. Three study types: (1) exploratory runs (T=20 generations, 3 seeds, oracle-instrumented in Appendix D); (2) a post hoc native-loop analysis comparing model-predicted to observed overstatement of commits on the loop's own reused set; (3) a pre-registered confirmatory study — fresh 600-item gold set disjoint from exploratory data, proposer failures from a separate 256-item pool (so n changes only the evaluation), four settings × nine arms × eight seeds, design/hypotheses/amendment archived ten minutes into the runs, seed-paired two-sided Holm-corrected tests. Acceptance rules compared: greedy, exact one-sided McNemar at α=0.05, PACE (Shawn 2026's anytime-valid wealth test), select-then-confirm (hold out half of D), tuned baselines, and Bayes gates implementing Equation (3) with noise terms estimated from the generation's item vectors (pilot-calibrated and online variants). §5.4 repeats the loop with Qwen3.5-4B and instruments two DSPy prompt optimizers: GEPA (reflective prompt evolution, 1000–2250 metric calls, task model Qwen3.5-4B, proposer Qwen3.8-27B) and MIPROv2 (Bayesian search over a fixed candidate pool, 24 trials), each with n_val ∈ {16, 256} validation items and five seeds; every GEPA metric call was reconstructed exactly from saved run state and checked against logs.

## Results & Highlights

- **Self-reported progress is inflated, and the inflation scales as predicted by set size (H2):** With n=16 selection items, the final proxy score of greedy loops exceeded held-out accuracy by 13 to 20 points across the four confirmatory settings (e.g., +15.9 ± 2.7 points on 1.5B TREC, Holm p=0.002; +14.7 ± 4.4 on 1.5B GSM8K, p=0.037); with n=256 the gap shrinks to 1 to 5 points, a paired reduction of about 15 points (significant in three of four settings, Holm p ≤ 0.04).
- **The winner's curse has exactly the implied size:** Across nine oracle settings (three models, two Qwen generations, two inference stacks, n = 8..256, 200 gold-set splits), the true-to-measured selection-differential ratio of the best-of-4 candidate matches the noise model's prediction with mean absolute error 0.034 (empirical effect distribution) and 0.064 (Gaussian h²_w); at n=16 the winner's held-out differential is only 1–51% of its measured one. The causal log-based h²_w estimate has MAE 0.10 and shrinks winners' measured advantages to within 0.6 points of the held-out mean (5.0 points unshrunk at n=16).
- **The model matches native-loop commits on average, not per-setting:** For the 72 confirmatory commits whose incumbent was the seed, predicted vs observed average overstatement is 9.1 vs 9.0 points at n=16, 3.2 vs 3.8 at n=64, and 0.1 vs 1.3 at n=256 — but individual settings hide errors of several points (1.9 predicted vs 5.9 observed on 1.5B TREC; 12.9 vs 7.0 on Banking77, with only four to six commits each). Incumbent inflation relative to the seed grows across generations: 13.6 ± 1.7 points at n=16, 7.2 ± 1.1 at n=64, 1.7 ± 0.6 at n=256; of late candidates only 14% have held-out effects within one gold-set standard error of zero, and almost none clears the incumbent.
- **Larger selection sets buy real held-out gains on TREC but not everywhere (H1):** 1.5B greedy loops improve from 13.7 ± 2.9 (n=16) to 24.2 ± 0.7 points (n=256) over the seed on TREC (paired +10.5 ± 2.6, p=0.010, 7/8 seeds); the 7B model goes from 14.0 ± 0.7 to 20.4 ± 0.6 (+6.4 ± 0.7, p<0.001, 8/8 seeds). On GSM8K no arm gained more than 3.1 points (+1.6 ± 2.2 at n=256, p=0.483), and the exploratory Goodhart loss on Banking77 did not replicate (greedy at n=16 ends 0.6–0.7 points below seed, p=0.20).
- **Tested acceptance rules do not beat greedy over whole runs (H4, H5):** The pilot-calibrated Bayes gate ends 2.1 ± 1.1 points below greedy pooled over 32 seed pairs at n=16 (p=0.06; significantly worse on GSM8K at −5.7 ± 1.6, p=0.032); select-then-confirm is 1.6 ± 1.2 below greedy (7B TREC −6.4 ± 1.9, p=0.045); fixed-level McNemar gives up most of the available TREC gain (−7.4 and −12.3 points for the 1.5B and 7B models). Calibrated thresholds do improve *isolated* decisions on fresh evaluations, mainly by committing less often — but not complete runs on reused sets.
- **Strong starts collapse the room for self-improvement while reports stay inflated:** Qwen3.5-4B improving its own one-sentence seed gains 37–39 points on TREC and 13–15 on Banking77, with the first rewrite already delivering 90–112% of the final gain; restarted from a competent instruction, only 5% of TREC rewrites improve (mean effect −14 points), yet planned 16-item loops report gains of 12.5 and 14.1 points on TREC/Banking77 while held-out accuracy changes by −2.2 and 0.0 (overstatement falls from 16.3 to 1.4 and 11.5 to 1.5 points at n=256, 8 seeds).
- **The same inflation contaminates GEPA and MIPROv2 validation scores:** From 1000 metric calls GEPA's returned program gains ~19 held-out points on TREC, 1–2 on Banking77, and ~0 on GSM8K (task model already ~95%); its 16-item validation score implies 38.8 points on TREC (real 19.2) and 18.8 on Banking77 (real 1.2 ± 1.7), an overstatement of 26.5 vs −1.9 points on TREC (p=0.007) that shrinks to at most 3 points with 256 items — though GEPA's held-out gain never grows with n_val even at 2250 calls (TREC 18.5/20.4/19.0 for n=16/64/256), because the small set allowed 10× more search. MIPROv2 with 256 validation items gains 11.4 held-out points on TREC vs 5.8 with 16 (paired 5.6 ± 0.7, p=0.001, 6/6 seeds); its 16-item Banking77 arm returned programs 1.6 points worse than seed while validation scores implied a 5-point gain.
- **The proposed audit is cheap and removes the bias:** Scoring seed and incumbent after 19 generations on 64 never-used items costs 128 evaluations (~1/10 of the 1,280-evaluation nominal n=16 budget), removes the average bias of the reported gain, and reduces its RMSE from 15.5 to 5.7 points — though 5.7 points is still as large as a typical true gain.

## Authors & Credibility

Litao Hu (Meta) and Yutong Tang (Microsoft) are the two authors; the citations suggest active engagement with both the self-improvement literature (prompt breeders, GEPA, agent scaffold evolution) and the decision-theory/statistics literature (breeder's equation, optimizer's curse, inference on winners, selection-adjusted Bayesian inference). The methodological hygiene is well above field norms: a genuinely pre-registered confirmatory study with the design, five hypotheses, an amendment, and the archived analysis script (archived ten minutes into the runs), explicit deviation lists (Appendix B), Holm correction within test families, seed-paired tests, and a fresh gold set disjoint from exploratory data. The paper reports its own failed replication (the exploratory Goodhart loss on Banking77) and its own deviations, and releases code, saved run records, and reproduction instructions as ancillary files. Two caveats on credibility: the author list is small and affiliations (Meta, Microsoft) are stated without further group context, and the strongest quantitative checks necessarily rely on oracle quantities that practicing loops do not have.

## Critical Assessment

The core contribution — recasting keep-if-better as best-of-K selection under correlated noise and measuring the resulting curse — is solid, timely, and unusually well-executed for this area; the pre-registration discipline and the negative results on acceptance rules are genuinely valuable. That said, several paper-specific limits deserve scrutiny. First, **scope of tasks and models**: everything is instruction rewriting on three classification/math tasks (TREC-50, Banking77, GSM8K) with deterministic scoring and proposers up to Qwen3.8-27B; the authors themselves exclude agentic and code tasks, and Proposition 1's prediction "larger winner's curse" under stochastic rollouts is left untested — the PACE-style setting of noisy rollouts, arguably the practically dominant case, is out of reach of the current evidence. Second, **the acceptance-rule comparison has a structural asymmetry the paper acknowledges but does not fully resolve**: the pilot-calibrated Bayes gate requires a prior (μ, s²) from a separate pilot in which candidates are scored on a *large* evaluation set — an oracle-flavored input that a native loop does not possess — so H4/H5 partly measure the cost of calibration rather than the value of calibrated thresholds; the rules were also tested at a single α and a fixed PACE bet taken "as published", leaving tuned McNemar and PACE variants under-explored. Third, **the theory is only average-accurate in the regime that matters**: on native reused-set commits the model matches the mean (9.1 vs 9.0 points at n=16) while individual settings deviate by several points (1.9 vs 5.9 on 1.5B TREC), the lock-in mechanism is measured only associationally ("does not isolate the mechanism causally"), and Proposition 1 overpredicts MIPROv2's 16-item TREC cell (9.0 vs 5.5) and undershoots GEPA's cells by 1.1–1.9× because GEPA's pool is itself selected on the validation set. Fourth, **the headline remedy is weaker than the abstract implies**: the 64-item audit removes average bias but leaves per-setting errors of −8.5 to +5.8 points, and RMSE of the reported gain is still 5.7 points — as large as a typical true gain — so "report held-out gains with uncertainty" remains necessary but not sufficient. Finally, the claim that larger selection sets help is benchmark-dependent by the paper's own data (it fails on GSM8K and is not detectable in GEPA where search budget is confounded with n), so readers should not generalize "bigger n helps" beyond TREC-like settings.

## Strengths

- Rare methodological rigor for the self-improvement literature: pre-registered hypotheses with archived analysis scripts, Holm-corrected seed-paired tests, disjoint fresh gold sets, and explicit deviation reporting.
- Clean conceptual toolkit (inflation vs overstatement; shared vs idiosyncratic error; lock-in) that maps directly onto decisions practitioners make, with all noise terms identifiable from item-level logs.
- Honest, useful negative results: none of the five tested acceptance rules improves whole runs; the exploratory Goodhart effect failed to replicate and the authors say so.
- The findings extend beyond toy self-loops to two widely used prompt optimizers (GEPA, MIPROv2), with exact reconstruction of every GEPA metric call from saved run state.
- Actionable, quantified guidance: the 64-item audit removes reported-gain bias at ~10% of the nominal evaluation budget, and the recommended reporting practice (held-out score + SE alongside the selection score) costs almost nothing.

## Limitations

- Instruction rewriting only: no agentic tasks, no code optimization, largest proposer 27B; stochastic-rollout noise (the practically common case) is analyzed only as a prediction, not measured.
- All model-validation checks that use held-out truth require oracle instrumentation unavailable in native loops; the native-loop check covers only four Qwen2.5 settings and is post hoc.
- The Bayes gates need a pilot with a large evaluation set; PACE ran with its published fixed bet; rules were compared at one error level and one budget, so "no rule beats greedy" is a bounded claim for reused-set loops, not a law.
- The lock-in mechanism is measured descriptively (incumbent inflation and subsequent commit errors), not isolated causally; the dynamics of lock-in over long recursions are explicitly not modeled, and no claims about recursive self-improvement are made.
- Per-setting prediction error of the model is several points even where the average matches, and the audit-set remedy still leaves RMSE ≈ 5.7 points — the paper's own fixes reduce, but do not eliminate, the measurement problem.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击当前 LLM 自改进与 prompt 优化（GEPA、MIPROv2、agent scaffold 演化）工作流中最容易被忽视的统计陷阱：在小验证集上"好一点就保留"会系统性虚报收益，且常用验收规则救不回来。对你关注的 LLM agents 与评测方法学方向而言，这是一篇方法论守则级别的必读工作，其"held-out 分数 + 标准误随选择分数一起报告"的建议应直接纳入自己的实验规范。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the winner's-curse size transfer to stochastic rollouts (agentic tasks, code execution), where per-candidate measurement noise is much larger and Proposition 1 predicts a bigger curse?
- Can acceptance thresholds be calibrated from the loop's own accumulated item-level logs alone (the online gate's h²_w approach) without any oracle pilot, and at what sample size does that become competitive with greedy?
- Is lock-in net beneficial or harmful? The reused incumbent's error acts as an implicit threshold that makes greedy conservative — can this be turned into a principled, free acceptance rule rather than an accident?
- How does the trade-off between selection-set size and search budget behave in optimizers like GEPA, where the paper found more validation items bought no held-out gain because the small set enabled 10× more search?
- Do the conclusions hold for population-based search (the paper applies its model only to the single program a population method returns) and for multi-incumbent loops where the shared-error structure breaks?
