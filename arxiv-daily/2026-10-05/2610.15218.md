> **一句话总结：** USC 团队发现循环深度语言模型（Ouro、Huginn-0125）在推理时减少循环次数（loops）会优先摧毁安全拒答行为而非能力：Ouro-1.4B-Thinking 从 4 循环砍到 2 循环，GSM8K 仍保留 89% 精度（0.775→0.692），但有害请求配合率从 0.038 升到 0.205（5.3 倍）；机理上"有害性"信息在第一循环后即可被线性探针读出（AUC 0.967），缺的是尚未收敛的拒答方向（余弦仅 0.486）；运行时修复（早退规则、按请求调深度）均无效，而在随机深度上施加安全训练损失可使 1 循环拒答间隔从 +0.118 提升到 +0.399，且满深度精度零损失。

> **TL;DR:** In recurrent-depth LMs (Ouro-1.4B/2.6B base+Thinking, Huginn-0125), lowering the inference loop count destroys refusal far faster than capability: on Ouro-1.4B-Thinking, halving loops keeps 89% of GSM8K accuracy (0.775→0.692) but raises harmful compliance on JailbreakBench/AdvBench/XSTest prompts from 0.038 to 0.205 (5.3×), while a linear probe still reads harmfulness from the state at AUC 0.967 vs 0.986 full-depth. Two run-time fixes (the model's early-exit gate, per-request depth routing) fail, but supervising safety training (LoRA) at randomly chosen loops instead of only the final loop lifts the 1-loop refusal gap from +0.118 to +0.399 with zero full-depth accuracy loss (0.783 both), and holds Huginn-0125's compliance ≤0.040 across all 1–32 loops.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统量化"循环数-安全性"关系**：在 4 个已发布的 Ouro 检查点（1.4B/2.6B × base/Thinking）和 Huginn-0125（1–32 循环）上证明：减少推理循环时，拒答行为比"有害性内部识别"和"任务能力"下降得都快——单循环时 Ouro-1.4B-Thinking 保留 96.1% 的危害检测能力（探针 AUC）却只剩 16.1% 的拒答间隔。
- **给出一套严谨的深度相关安全测量方法**：260 条有害 + 200 条无害 prompt（JailbreakBench/AdvBench/XSTest 按主题配对），用 rep≤0.25 三元重复率 + dup≤0.02 相邻重复词率的文本质量过滤器剔除浅深度产生的"看似合规实则乱码"文本，并用 4 种互相独立失败的测量方式（直接生成、强制作答、等长截断、单次前向 forced-choice 无生成打分）交叉验证同一结论。
- **定位缺失的组件**：危害信息第一循环后已就位（1 循环探针 AUC 0.960–0.982），但把信息转化为拒答的方向尚未收敛（与满深度方向余弦 0.412–0.842）；满深度移除该方向使 Ouro-1.4B 有害拒答率从 0.950 跌至 0.130，单循环加回该方向使 Ouro-1.4B-Thinking 拒答间隔从 -0.170 回升到 +0.220。
- **排除两类运行时修复**：模型自带早退门在有害/无害 prompt 上的平均退出循环差从未超过 0.058/4；即使被告知真实标签的"每类一个深度"路由也无法逼近逐 prompt 神谕（0.896 @ 平均 1.52 循环）——正确深度取决于具体 prompt 而非有害标签。
- **给出有效训练修复**：LoRA 安全训练中把损失从"只监督最终循环"改为"每样本随机选一个循环监督"，1 循环拒答间隔 +0.118→+0.399（3.4 倍），无害误拒率 0.865→0.585（对照组 exit-gate mixture 高达 0.926），满深度 GSM8K 精度 0.783→0.783 零损失，3 个随机种子结论一致（+0.371~+0.520 vs +0.024~+0.118，区间不相交）。

## Key Contributions

- **Safety falls faster than anything else when loop count drops.** Across four released Ouro checkpoints and Huginn-0125, the refusal gap collapses before harm detection or task ability: at one loop, Ouro-1.4B-Thinking retains 0.961 of its harm-probe AUC but only 0.161 of its refusal gap — a separation of 0.800. The dense-layer controls (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) show the *opposite* ordering (+0.269 and +0.297 retention gaps, CIs above zero), isolating the effect to weight-tied recurrence rather than reduced compute in general.
- **A measurement protocol built for the confound.** Shallow loops produce fluent-looking but useless text, which inflates naive attack-success scores; the paper filters on trigram repetition and duplicate-word rates, and replicates the effect with four instruments — including a generation-free forced-choice test (harmful-specific shift +0.303, 95% CI [+0.214, +0.391], on Ouro-1.4B-Thinking) — plus a second judge (Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B) and sampled decoding at three seeds.
- **The missing piece is a direction, not information.** A one-loop linear probe already separates harmful from harmless at AUC 0.960–0.982, but the refusal direction (difference-of-means) has cosine only 0.412–0.842 to its full-depth version; ablating it at full depth drops Ouro-1.4B's refusal rate on harmful prompts from 0.950 to 0.130, and adding it at one loop moves the refusal gap from -0.170 to +0.220 (random-direction control: -0.050).
- **Run-time repairs fail; training repairs work.** Early-exit gates allocate loops ignoring harmfulness (max gap 0.058 loops); label-oracle depth routing stays within [-0.060, +0.111] of a fixed-depth policy. But a random-loop LoRA loss triples the 1-loop refusal gap (+0.118 → +0.399) while refusing far fewer harmless prompts (0.585 vs 0.865) and matching full-depth GSM8K exactly (0.783 = 0.783); on Huginn-0125 it holds harmful compliance ≤0.040 at every depth from 1 to 32 loops (final-loop training reaches 0.194 at 4/32 loops, 6.2× full depth).
- **A deployment-relevant threat model.** No weight tampering is assumed: a serving stack cuts loops for cost/latency and re-tests quality but not safety — exactly the configuration where compliance rises 5.3× (Ouro-1.4B-Thinking at 2 loops) to 9.9× (Ouro-2.6B-Thinking at 1 loop, 0.035 → 0.344, with 0.962 of answers still coherent), and where jailbreak templates compose multiplicatively (0.080 full depth → 0.447 one loop → 0.830 both).

## Methodology

**Models.** Four released Ouro checkpoints (1.4B and 2.6B parameters, each in base and Thinking variants; trained with r=4 whole-stack loops) plus Huginn-0125 (prelude–core–coda architecture where only the middle block repeats, trained at an average of 32 repetitions). All runs use bfloat16 on one H100; weights are never modified — only the loop count changes (truncation; gated exit is verified to perform the same computation at equal stopping depth).

**Prompts and metrics.** 260 harmful prompts (100 JailbreakBench behaviours, 100 AdvBench, 60 unsafe XSTest) and 200 topic-matched harmless ones (100 JailbreakBench harmless counterparts, 100 safe XSTest). Two headline quantities: the **refusal gap** D = Pr[refuse|harmful] − Pr[refuse|harmless] (so a model refusing everything scores 0, not high), and **harmful compliance** (fraction of harmful prompts receiving real, on-topic help), judged by Qwen3Guard-Gen-4B combined with a refusal-phrase check. Task ability is GSM8K exact match on 120 problems under the same greedy decoding.

**Text-quality filter.** An answer is coherent iff its repeated-trigram rate rep3 ≤ 0.25 and adjacent duplicate-word rate dup ≤ 0.02 (thresholds set on full-depth output, which the filter keeps at 0.920–1.000). Filtering is conservative: in cells keeping ≥0.850 of answers, filtered vs unfiltered compliance differs by ≤0.056, and the headline refusal-gap drop shrinks (0.863 unfiltered vs 0.694 filtered on Ouro-1.4B-Thinking). The fraction kept is reported next to every number.

**Four cross-checking instruments.** (1) plain greedy generation; (2) forced answer — cap the thinking phase, insert the end-of-thinking token, score only the answer; (3) equal-length — truncate every depth's answer for a prompt to the shortest that prompt produced at any depth (length held constant across all 460 prompts); (4) generation-free forced choice — compare the model's probability of a benchmark-supplied complying sentence ("Sure, here is how to...") vs a fixed refusal sentence in one forward pass, harmful minus harmless to remove generic refusal shifts.

**Internal-state analysis.** Logistic probes on the post-loop state (5-fold CV over 300 held-out prompts, ROC AUC); a refusal direction u = mean(refused states) − mean(complied states); ablation and addition of u via a hook on the shared normalization layer, with random-direction controls of matched norm.

**Training comparison.** LoRA (rank 16, 2 epochs, LR 1e-4) on 1200 harmful/refusal pairs + 600 general instruction examples + 600 GSM8K solutions (the last keeps answer length normal); three objectives differing only in which readout the loss supervises — final-loop (standard), exit-gate weighted mixture, and one uniformly random loop per example — plus the untrained released model; the two key arms re-run at three seeds, and tested against jailbreak templates (refusal suppression, forced "Sure, here is", persona) never seen in training.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline asymmetry (Table 1):** Ouro-1.4B-Thinking at 2 of 4 loops scores 0.692 on GSM8K vs 0.775 at full depth (89% kept, 0.992 of answers coherent), while harmful compliance rises 0.038 → 0.205 (5.3×) and the refusal gap falls +0.827 → +0.644. Ouro-2.6B-Thinking at 1 loop: compliance 0.035 → 0.344 (9.9×) with 0.962 coherence; its 1-loop probe AUC is 0.982 vs 0.989 full depth — detection survives, refusal does not.
- **Three abilities fall in different orders (Fig. 3):** at one loop, retained harm detection / task ability / refusal gap = 0.954/0.553/0.359 (Ouro-1.4B), 0.961/0.398/0.161 (Ouro-1.4B-Thinking), 0.985/0.646/0.555 (Ouro-2.6B-Thinking). The safety-minus-task retention difference is -0.237 [-0.430, -0.046] on Ouro-1.4B-Thinking; the detection-minus-refusal distance exceeds 0.75 in all three.
- **Dense controls invert the order (Sec. 4.4):** removing layers from Qwen3-1.7B makes compliance *fall* (0.089 → 0.047); at a gentle 2-of-28-layer removal it keeps 0.625 of task ability and 0.894 of its refusal gap (+0.269, 95% CI [+0.075, +0.468]); Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct gives +0.297 [+0.062, +0.568] with one layer removed — refusal is the *last* thing to go in dense models, the *first* in recurrent-depth ones.
- **Direction ablation/addition (Sec. 5):** removing u at full depth drops Ouro-1.4B's refusal on harmful prompts 0.950 → 0.130 and raises compliance 0.050 → 0.850 (gap +0.800 → +0.080); adding u (coefficient 0.2) at one loop on Ouro-1.4B-Thinking moves harmful refusal 0.320 → 0.910, compliance 0.680 → 0.090, gap -0.170 → +0.220, vs -0.050 for a random same-norm direction. Direction maturity after one loop (cosine 0.412 / 0.486 / 0.842) tracks which model loses the least refusal.
- **Run-time repairs fail (Sec. 6):** early-exit mean loop differs by at most 0.058 of one loop between harmful and harmless prompts (2.439 vs 2.397 at the earliest-exiting setting); a label-told depth router stays within [-0.060, +0.111] of a fixed depth while the per-prompt oracle reaches D = 0.896 at a mean of 1.52 loops — the needed depth is per-prompt, not per-label.
- **Random-loop training wins (Table 2, Ouro-1.4B):** at one loop all three objectives hold compliance at 0.008–0.009, but final-loop and exit-gate mixture achieve this by refusing 0.865 and 0.926 of *harmless* prompts; random-loop refuses 0.585 with refusal gap +0.399 vs +0.118 and +0.066 (3.4×). Full-depth GSM8K: 0.783 (random) = 0.783 (untrained); full-depth gap improves +0.737 → +0.797; 1-loop coherence 0.585 → 0.992. Three seeds: random-loop 1-loop gap +0.371..+0.520 vs final-loop +0.024..+0.118 — ranges do not overlap.
- **Second family (Sec. 4.6, 7):** on Huginn-0125 retrained with standard final-loop supervision, compliance at 4/32 loops is 0.194 vs 0.031 full depth (6.2×); random-depth training holds it ≤0.040 at every depth 1–32 (vs 0.019 full depth) and raises 1-loop filter pass rate 0.119 → 0.962. Robustness: the depth trend holds under Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B re-scoring (agreement ≥0.827, κ ≥0.637) and sampled decoding at temp 0.7 (greedy numbers are the conservative ones); trained adapters also cut worst-case compliance under unseen jailbreak templates (refusal suppression 0.354 → 0.064/0.082; persona 0.721 → 0.155/0.233).

## Authors & Credibility

Seyedarmin Azizi (corresponding author, USC), Erfan Baghaei Potraghloo, Arya Fayyazi, Saeid Shokoufa, and Massoud Pedram (USC; a senior, well-established researcher in efficient computing systems). The team is from the University of Southern California. Credibility is high: the paper studies *released third-party checkpoints* (Ouro, Huginn-0125) rather than only models it trained itself, invests heavily in measurement hygiene (four instruments, a text-quality filter with reported keep-rates, a second judge, sampled-decoding checks, multi-seed retrains, dense-model controls, random-direction controls), and is unusually transparent about weak cells — it flags exactly where the filter leaves thin samples and prefers the conservative (filtered) number even when unfiltered numbers would strengthen the claim.

## Critical Assessment

- **Genuinely novel and well-targeted question.** No prior work measures safety as a function of loop count in recurrent-depth LMs (related work is position-based alignment fragility, quantization/pruning safety loss, and adaptive-compute accuracy work — the axis "how much computation per token" with prompt, positions, and decoding held fixed is new). The dense-control inversion (refusal last-to-go in dense models, first-to-go in looped ones) is the paper's strongest single result because it rules out "less compute = less safe" as an explanation.
- **The headline statistical claim is softer than the abstract suggests.** The paper itself reports that "refusal falls faster than task ability" is only significant in one of three checkpoints at one loop (bootstrap intervals for -0.194 [-0.406, +0.017] and -0.090 [-0.250, +0.072] include zero); the robust claim is refusal vs *harm detection*, not vs task ability. The abstract's framing leans on the comparison that is clean (detection vs refusal) but a hurried reader may over-generalize to capability.
- **Thin cells at the shallowest settings.** For the 1.4B models at one loop the text-quality filter keeps only 0.377–0.585 of answers (~98 harmful / 75 harmless prompts on Ouro-1.4B-Thinking), so the most dramatic cells rest on a fraction of the 260/200 prompt set. To the authors' credit, they anchor the deployment-relevant story on 2-loop cells (0.992 kept) and the 2.6B 1-loop cell (0.962 kept), but this is still 120 GSM8K problems per accuracy cell.
- **The Huginn repair result is on their own adapter, not the released artifact.** Released Huginn-0125 has almost no refusal to lose (gap +0.333, compliance 0.455 at full depth), so the "works on a second family" claim concerns models *they* safety-trained with LoRA on 1,200 refusal pairs — a small, idiosyncratic alignment recipe. The random-loop objective is likewise demonstrated on Ouro-1.4B only, not across all four Ouro checkpoints.
- **Single-direction mechanism is suggestive, not complete.** The difference-of-means refusal direction restores separation on only one of three checkpoints (gaps -0.050 and +0.370 on the other two — refusal rises but indiscriminately); "the direction has not settled" (cosine 0.412–0.842) is a correlation with fragility across just three models. Judge is Qwen3Guard-Gen-4B — conveniently from the same family as the Qwen3 dense control — mitigated by the 8B re-score, but guard models remain noisy labelers on degraded text, which the authors themselves show (agreement lowest at one loop).
- **No red-team-grade attacks.** The composition study uses three fixed template jailbreaks; no GCG/adaptive-attack sweeps, and the threat model legitimately excludes an attacker who controls depth — the risk is a *deployer's* cost knob, which some may argue is easier to govern than the paper's framing implies. Overall: rigorous, honest about its weaknesses, and the deployment argument survives the caveats.

## Strengths

- First-mover result on a real deployment knob (loop count) that existing single-number safety evaluations do not capture, with a threat model that requires no attacker and no weight modification.
- Methodological care rare in safety evaluations: four instruments that fail differently, an explicit text-quality filter with per-cell keep rates, refusal-gap (two-sided) rather than one-sided attack-success metrics, second judge, seed retrains, and conservative-number preference throughout.
- Mechanistic story with intervention, not just probing: ablating and re-adding one direction causally moves refusal, with random-direction and per-model controls, and direction maturity predicts per-model fragility.
- Actionable, cheap fix (randomize the supervised loop during safety training) validated at zero full-depth accuracy cost, across seeds, on unseen jailbreak templates, and on a second architecture with an 8× depth range.
- Clear dense-model controls at two granularities (quarter-stack and fine 1–10-layer grids, two dense models) establishing the opposite ordering.

## Limitations

- Refusal-vs-task-ability ordering is statistically established in only one of three Ouro checkpoints at one loop (the other two intervals include zero); the unambiguous gap is versus harm detection.
- Small evaluation scale: 260 harmful / 200 harmless prompts, 120 GSM8K problems per cell, three template jailbreaks; single-loop cells for the 1.4B models survive filtering at only 0.377–0.585 keep rates.
- The random-loop training fix is shown on one Ouro size and on a self-trained Huginn adapter (the released Huginn checkpoint has negligible refusal to begin with); not yet validated on the full 2.6B family or production-scale alignment pipelines (RLHF/DPO rather than 1,200-pair LoRA SFT).
- Adding the refusal direction back restores harmful/harmless *separation* only on one checkpoint, so the mechanistic repair is incomplete; the causal account of *why* the direction settles late is not given.
- Concurrency with model scale is unexplored (only 1.4B/2.6B/1.7B-class models), and the oracle depth-routing analysis, while decisive against label-based routing, does not test learned per-prompt routers with richer features.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 安全对齐与高效推理（test-time compute、自适应深度）的读者这是一篇必读：它证明了"推理计算量"本身就是安全参数，循环深度模型在省算力设置下会静默丢失拒答能力，而修法只需把安全训练的监督深度随机化。其"四 instruments + 文本质量过滤"的评估方法论对任何涉及深度/早退/剪枝的部署与评测都有直接借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the random-loop objective survive full-scale preference-based alignment (RLHF/DPO) and larger models (2.6B and beyond), or is the LoRA-SFT result an artifact of a small recipe?
- Why does the refusal direction settle late in weight-tied recurrence — is it a function of the pretraining loss profile across loops, and can it be predicted from direction cosine alone for a new checkpoint?
- Would a learned per-prompt depth router with features beyond the harm label (e.g., prompt difficulty embedding) close the gap to the per-prompt oracle (D = 0.896 at 1.52 mean loops), and is that safe or itself a new attack surface?
- Do the dense-model controls extend to mixture-of-depths and early-exit transformers used in production, i.e., is "refusal first-to-go" specific to weight tying or general to any learned-halting system?
- Does the composition of low depth with stronger attacks (GCG, multi-turn) exceed the 0.830 observed with templates, and can depth-aware training close that too?
