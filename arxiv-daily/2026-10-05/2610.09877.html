Layerwise Error Attribution for Fast and Robust Mixed-Precision Post-Training Quantization

Key metric
2,570×

faster bit-width selection than CLADO — 8.66 s vs 6 h 11 min. Like-for-like: 28.4× vs AIMET AMP, 30.6× vs OMPQ-ORM.

CLADO was timed on 2× A100, this method on 1× — the headline is fair but flattering; the 28×–30× comparisons are the conservative ones.

Why this matters

Bit-mixing decisions that take seconds instead of hours — and survive noisy calibration data — directly useful for edge deployment and quantizing diffusion models.

Critical edge

Clean-quality gains over the strongest baselines sit inside noise (−0.008 dB vs CLADO), and the headline robustness win is a stress test built around CLADO’s known weakness.

01Method

  1. Decompose the error Exact per-layer identity splits propagated error from the local quantization perturbation; random amplification ratios Xℓ, Yℓ track how data scales each term. Δℓ = WℓΔℓ−1 + (Wℓ − W′ℓ)S′ℓ−1
  2. Bound it probabilistically Conditional one-sided Chebyshev (Cantelli) gives a δ-probabilistic upper bound per layer (Thm. 1) while keeping the two-term structure visible; Lemma 1 bounds fixed configurations; appendix DAG rules cover residual, normalization, concatenation, self-attention.
  3. Score once, reuse forever Each (block, bit-width) gets a local score s = μ̂ + 0.05·σ̂/√n from a single full-precision pass over calibration data. Scores are separable — independent of every other block’s choice — hence reusable across any memory budget.
  4. Allocate greedily, no solver Start all blocks at max bit-width; greedily lower the block with the smallest score increase per saved weight bit until the budget is met. No ILP, no Pareto search, no network re-evaluation.

The practical score drops the propagated term (valid on the method’s low-error path, where S′ ≈ S), so Theorem 1 motivates rather than guarantees the greedy — and δscore = 0.05 is a heuristic dispersion weight, “not a confidence level” by the authors’ own caveat.

03Strengths

  • Re-allocation for a new budget is essentially free — scores are fixed after one full-precision pass (8.66–24.32 s on one A100), a real engineering win over ILP/Pareto pipelines.
  • Well-controlled robustness evidence: identical-candidate control plus allocation-stability diagnostics isolate the mechanism behind the 5.5–7.5 dB gaps.
  • Theory with a purpose: the propagated/local split is exactly the structure the algorithm consumes, and DAG rules make the analysis match modern architectures.
  • Unmodified transfer to latent diffusion improves both FID and paired PSNR — no diffusion-specific allocation machinery.
  • Uncommon transparency: 95% CIs on differences, adapted baselines labeled, the losing B=3.5 case and the unverifiable FP32 pairing all disclosed.

04Limitations

  • Narrow scope: one denoiser and one diffusion U-Net, weight-only quantization with FP32 activations — no LLMs, no ViTs, no activation quantization, so real deployment savings are not directly demonstrated.
  • Clean-calibration quality is parity, not progress: −0.008 dB vs CLADO and ±0.01–0.017 dB vs AIMET AMP sit inside reported CIs; ~0.8 dB lost to CLADO at B=3.5.
  • The robustness claim leans on synthetic mask/noise corruptions and on CLADO’s known sensitivity; OMPQ-ORM is also corruption-stable (at lower absolute quality).
  • No end-to-end guarantee on the greedy’s final allocation, and the probabilistic bounds are never evaluated numerically for tightness.
  • 2,570× headline flattered by 2-vs-1 GPU timing; PSNR rests on 100 test images; the diffusion FP32 pairing was reconstructed, not exactly verified; no public code release mentioned.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Does corruption stability extend to realistic calibration drift — domain shift, low-light/blur sensors — rather than synthetic masks and noise?
  • With activation quantization (A8 and below) and on transformer/LLM workloads where layer interactions and outlier channels dominate, does dropping the propagated term still hold up?
  • How tight is Theorem 1’s bound versus realized errors, and can a data-dependent quality bound on the greedy’s final configuration be derived?
  • Can the propagated-error term be re-introduced cheaply (one correction pass) to close the ~0.8 dB gap to CLADO at very tight budgets?
  • Is the observed robustness specific to CLADO’s pairwise ILP, or would other interaction-based allocators (modern Pareto / learned-policy methods) degrade similarly?
