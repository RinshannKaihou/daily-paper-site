> **一句话总结：** NUS/SJTU 团队发现细粒度 MoE upcycling 的结构性病理——从单一稠密模型切出的细粒度专家会路由坍缩（Qwen3-1.7B 上 Drop-Upcycling-fine 仅 23.2%，接近从零训练的 22.2%），并提出 DivMoE：先在 4 个公开领域语料（数学/代码/科学/常识）上对基座做领域自适应续训得到 4 个专家稠密模型，再将每个 FFN 纵切成 2 个细粒度专家（共 8 个），配合"每个 token 至多激活每个领域组 1 个专家"的硬性多样性约束路由，在 500B token 联合续训后以 54.3% 的 11 基准平均分超越最强基线 BTX（50.8%）+3.5 分，并在每个基准上都反超稠密基座。

> **TL;DR:** DivMoE is the first framework to make *fine-grained* MoE upcycling work. It shows that slicing experts from a single dense source collapses routing (on Qwen3-1.7B-Base, fine-grained Drop-Upcycling hits 23.2% avg vs 22.2% from-scratch over 15 benchmarks), and fixes it with two ideas: (1) derive experts from 4 domain-specialized dense models (math/code/science/commonsense CPT on public Dolmino-Mix partitions, 40B tokens), each vertically sliced into 2 shards, and (2) a hard structural routing constraint forcing each token to pick at most one expert per domain group. After 500B tokens of joint CPT, DivMoE reaches 54.3% on the 11-benchmark suite — +3.5 pts over Branch-Train-Mix (BTX, 50.8%) and +3.8 over NVIDIA Upcycling (50.5%) — beats the dense base on every benchmark (e.g., MMLU 64.5 vs 62.6), and a 12B version (3.6B active) matches Moonlight-MoE (16B) at 64.5% on 8 benchmarks.

## 核心贡献

- **识别并命名"细粒度 upcycling 坍缩"病理**：当细粒度专家全部源自同一个稠密模型时，专家相似度极高，top-k 路由会坍缩到少数专家（Sparse Upcycling 在第 36 层有超过 60% 的 token 集中到 2 个专家），下游精度跌至近随机水平——此前该失效模式未被系统刻画。
- **领域专用细粒度专家初始化**：先用公开语料把基座分别续训成 n=4 个领域专家（数学、代码、学术/科学、web 常识，各 10B token），再纵向切分每个专家的 FFN 成 m=2 个分片，得到 N=8 个细粒度专家，按"virtual group"设计保证初始化时任意组内取一个分片即可重构完整稠密 FFN 输出，配合幅度校正因子 γ。
- **多样性约束路由（硬约束而非软损失）**：强制每个 token 在每个领域组内至多激活 1 个专家（|S ∩ D_i| ≤ 1），从而 k=2 个激活专家必然来自 2 个不同领域；与辅助负载均衡损失等"软"机制不同，该保证与梯度大小无关，且不引入任何新参数或超参。
- **受控实验与消融**：两个基座（Qwen3-1.7B、Llama3.2-1B）× 三种规模（1B→3.2B、1.7B→5.4B、4B→12B）、15 个基准、6 个 upcycling 基线，所有方法共享 Stage-2 语料/优化器/步数；消融证明三个设计轴缺一不可，去掉多样性约束平均分从 55.6 降到 52.3。
- **反直觉发现**：强制跨领域专家共激活并未损害领域内任务——约束在 HumanEval +4.8、MBPP +2.3、GSM8K +2.1、MATH +1.4 上反而全面提升，说明编码 token 内部也需要数学/常识等异质功能。

## Key Contributions

- **A new pathology**: fine-grained expert partitioning combined with single-source initialization causes routing collapse and near-random downstream accuracy (23.2% for Drop-Upcycling-fine on Qwen3-1.7B-Base, statistically indistinguishable from the 22.2% from-scratch floor), isolating a failure mode prior upcycling work had not characterized.
- **Domain-specialized fine-grained expert initialization**: 4 domain-adaptively continual-pre-trained dense specialists (public corpora only) are each vertically sliced into m=2 shards, yielding 8 fine-grained experts organized in 4 domain groups, with a dense-equivalent "virtual group" initialization and magnitude correction that preserve the base model's function at init.
- **Diversity-constrained routing**: a hard structural constraint (at most one active expert per domain group per token) that guarantees cross-domain expert composition without auxiliary-loss tuning; adds no parameters or hyperparameters and integrates with standard MoE kernels.
- **Controlled evidence at three scales**: across Qwen3-1.7B→5.4B, Llama3.2-1B→3.2B, and Qwen3-4B→12B, DivMoE beats all six upcycling baselines (54.3% vs BTX 50.8% on Qwen3-1.7B-Base, 11 benchmarks), surpasses the dense base on every benchmark (closing the post-CPT regression that plagued prior pipelines), and the 12B model matches Moonlight-MoE (16B, trained from scratch) at 64.5% on the 8-benchmark set.
- **A refuted intuition**: obligating cross-domain co-activation *improves* every domain-specific benchmark tested (HumanEval +4.8, GSM8K +2.1 vs the unconstrained variant), evidence that individual tokens carry heterogeneous functional roles.

## Methodology

**Setup.** A dense transformer with SwiGLU FFNs is converted per-layer into an MoE. DivMoE uses n=4 domain groups, m=2 experts per group (N=8 total), and k=2 active experts per token throughout, doubling total capacity while activating half the dense FFN width per token.

**Stage 1 — Domain specialists (40B tokens).** Four copies of the base model are continually pre-trained (next-token prediction on full sequences, not SFT) on disjoint per-domain partitions of the public Dolmino-Mix-1124 corpus: math, code, academic (science), and web (commonsense), 10B tokens each. Each specialist M_i = W_base + Δ_i captures domain adaptation while staying anchored to the base weights.

**Expert construction.** Each specialist's FFN is vertically sliced into m shards: W_gate → {W_gate^(i,1..m)}, likewise W_up and W_down, so expert (i,j) has d/m hidden columns. The "virtual group" identity Σ_j Expert_(i,j)(x) ≈ FFN_base(x) (Eq. 4) guarantees the assembled MoE reproduces the dense function at initialization, and expert weights are rescaled by γ = sqrt(E·G²/k) to compensate for softmax attenuation of router scores. Embeddings, attention, and norms are initialized as the mean of the base model and all four specialists (an ablated choice: mean init 58.3 vs base-only 57.9 vs random selection 57.0 on 9 benchmarks — the FFN init is the primary driver).

**Stage 2 — Diversity-constrained routing + joint CPT (500B tokens).** Routing is two-stage and deterministic: (1) intra-group selection takes each group's argmax expert; (2) inter-group top-k picks the k groups by representative scores, with softmax weights over representatives. This enforces |S ∩ D_i| ≤ 1 by construction — a *hard* guarantee outside the loss landscape, unlike auxiliary load-balancing losses or router z-losses, which only bias gradient dynamics. A small standard auxiliary loss (α=0.01) is retained only for within-group utilization. The assembled MoE is then continually pre-trained on allenai/dolma3_dolmino-mix-100B for 500B tokens with the identical corpus, optimizer, and step count as every baseline.

**Stage 3 — SFT (only for the open-source MoE comparison).** Tulu-3-SFTMixture fine-tuning, applied identically to DivMoE, NVIDIA-Upcycling, and OLMoE to isolate architecture from data effects. Everything uses public corpora; Stage 1 adds ~8% token overhead vs from-scratch training (~28,800 vs ~26,500 H20 GPU-hours).

## Results & Highlights

- **Upcycling comparison, Qwen3-1.7B-Base (11-benchmark avg, Table 2):** DivMoE 54.3% vs Branch-Train-Mix 50.8% (+3.5), NVIDIA Upcycling 50.5% (+3.8), Sparse Upcycling / Drop-Upcycling 49.1% (+5.2), Drop-Upcycling-fine 28.8%, From-Scratch 20.7%. Largest gains on reasoning and code: GSM8K +4.0, MATH +4.5, HumanEval +5.0 over BTX. The pattern replicates on Llama3.2-1B: 37.4% vs BTX 35.5% (+1.9); fine-grained Drop-Upcycling again collapses there (22.3% vs 18.5% from-scratch).
- **Beats the dense base everywhere (Qwen3-1.7B-Base reference):** MMLU 64.5 vs 62.6, MATH 40.5 vs 38.4, BBH 55.5 vs 53.5 — eliminating the post-CPT regression that prior upcycling pipelines exhibit — and outperforms a data-matched dense baseline given the same 540B CPT tokens by ~2 points while activating only ~2.4B of 5.4B parameters per token.
- **Component ablation (15-benchmark avg, Qwen3-1.7B-Base, Table 5):** full DivMoE 55.6; removing only the diversity constraint (DivMoE −DC) drops to 52.3; BTX 51.6; NVIDIA Upcycling 51.1; fine-grained Drop-Upcycling 22.2. Removing any single design axis hurts; removing two collapses fine-grained variants to near-random.
- **Controlled open-source MoE comparison (Qwen3-4B→12B DivMoE, 3.6B active, Table 4):** with the same Tulu-3 SFT, DivMoE averages 63.0 over 7 benchmarks vs 56.2 for NVIDIA Upcycling (+6.8) and 45.1 for OLMoE (+9.3); biggest gaps on GSM8K (73.4 vs 66.2, +7.2) and MATH (24.4 vs 15.7, +8.7). Against from-scratch Moonlight-MoE (16B), DivMoE ties at 64.5% on the full 8-benchmark set while leading MMLU +1.5 (71.3 vs 69.8), TriviaQA +7.0, and MATH +2.2.
- **The constraint helps domain tasks (Table 6):** DivMoE w/o DC vs with DC — HumanEval 23.8→28.6, MBPP 31.5→33.8, GSM8K 43.2→45.3, MATH 18.4→19.4; the hard constraint improves all four math/code benchmarks evaluated.
- **Sustained advantage (Table 15):** DivMoE leads at every Stage-2 checkpoint — 52.0 vs 47.5 for NVIDIA Upcycling at 150B tokens and 55.6 vs 51.1 at 500B — so the gain is architectural, not an initialization-only transient. Routing analysis shows Sparse Upcycling routes >60% of tokens to just two experts at layer 36, while DivMoE stays balanced across groups yet preserves within-group specialization (GSM8K activates more math experts, HumanEval more code experts).

## Authors & Credibility

Yuxuan Lou and Yang You are with the School of Computing, National University of Singapore (You leads the NUS Presidential Young Professor group behind "Littling"/efficient-training work and is sponsored by NSF, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Singapore MOE grants); Kai Yang, Geng Zhang, and Yong Liu are with Shanghai Jiao Tong University. The team has direct prior exposure to MoE upcycling literature (they engage closely with NVIDIA's upcycling paper, Drop-Upcycling, and Branch-Train-Mix). The paper is a NeurIPS 2026 submission with unusually disciplined experimental hygiene: all baselines share the Stage-2 corpus, optimizer, and step count; per-method GPU-hour accounting is disclosed (Table 9); every corpus is public and the slicing/routing code plus data splits are promised for release. Credibility is high; the corresponding-author lab's scale of compute (~26-29k H20 GPU-hours per run) is plausible for the model sizes used.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline "+3.5 over BTX" is computed on an unequal-compute footing that the paper acknowledges but downplays in the abstract.** DivMoE and BTX both use 540B total tokens, but Table 2's caption shows all other baselines get 500B; the ~8% Stage-1 overhead is claimed to be "dense-model compute, substantially cheaper per-token" — fair, yet the 11-benchmark margins (+3.5, +3.8) are of the same magnitude as this budget asymmetry, so the cleanest claim is the Table-15 checkpoint story (4.5-pt lead at both 150B and 500B), which the paper does provide.
- **A visible weak spot the narrative glosses over:** in the controlled SFT comparison (Table 4), NVIDIA-Upcycling + Tulu-3 reaches 63.8 on HumanEval versus DivMoE's 51.4 — a 12.4-point deficit on the flagship code benchmark, and DivMoE also trails Moonlight-MoE on MBPP (60.0 vs 61.2) and ARC-c (63.1 vs 65.5). The "+6.8 controlled" average is carried by MATH/GSM8K; code generation is where forced cross-domain composition costs the most, which the "constraint helps every domain-specific benchmark" claim (based on +4.8 HumanEval over its *own* ablation) partially contradicts when the comparator is a different method.
- **The domain taxonomy is hand-fixed at 4 broad clusters (math/code/academic/web)**, and the authors admit learning the partition end-to-end is future work. All conclusions therefore hold for one hand-picked partition; sensitivity to the number of domains n (only n=4 is tested for the main results) and to domain boundaries is not ablated.
- **Scale ceiling:** all evidence is at 1B–4B dense bases (up to 12B MoE). Whether the collapse pathology and the fix persist at 7B–13B bases — where upcycling is actually most economically interesting — is untested, as the limitations section concedes.
- **On rigor:** the controlled design (identical Stage-2 corpus/optimizer/steps, identical SFT applied to the strongest baseline and OLMoE, dense data-matched reference rows) is genuinely better than typical upcycling papers, and the from-scratch floor of 20.7% makes the collapse diagnosis credible. Reproducibility is strong on paper: public corpora only, hyperparameters tabulated, code promised. One quirk: evaluation uses few-shot counts varying 0–25 per benchmark (e.g., ARC 25-shot, BBH 3-shot CoT), so cross-paper number comparisons require care.

## Strengths

- First clear characterization of the fine-grained upcycling collapse, with a from-scratch floor (22.2%) that makes "near-random" quantitatively defined rather than rhetorical.
- The diversity constraint is a genuinely *hard* guarantee — no auxiliary-loss tuning, no new hyperparameters — a clean contrast to the soft remedies (load-balancing loss, z-loss) it replaces, and it plugs into standard MoE kernels.
- Controlled comparisons across two base families and three scales, with data-matched dense baselines that isolate the MoE architecture from the extra CPT data — a control most upcycling papers skip.
- The counter-intuitive finding that forced cross-domain co-activation helps code and math tasks is well-motivated (token-level functional heterogeneity) and directly ablated (Table 6).
- Fully public data pipeline and disclosed compute costs make independent reproduction realistic.

## Limitations

- Base models capped at 4B (MoE at 12B); scaling to 7B–13B bases is explicitly left as future work, leaving the practical economics of the method at production scale unverified.
- The four-domain partition is fixed by hand; learning it end-to-end, and testing sensitivity to n or to alternative domain taxonomies, is unaddressed.
- Stage 1 adds 40B dense tokens (~8% overhead vs from-scratch token budget) — small in absolute terms but non-trivial at very large scale, and it means DivMoE is never a strictly equal-token comparison against the 500B baselines.
- Code-generation results are mixed against the strongest controlled baseline (HumanEval 51.4 vs 63.8 for NVIDIA-Upcycling+SFT), suggesting the constraint trades single-domain peak performance for cross-domain robustness.
- The granularity sweep (m=k ∈ {1,2,4}) is shallow — only one base model, and the m=k=4 overfitting observation on MMLU/HellaSwag is not analyzed mechanistically.

## Relevance

DivMoE 给出了 MoE upcycling 这条"省钱路线"上第一个被清晰定义的失效模式及其结构化解法，对任何考虑把现有稠密基座升级为细粒度 MoE 的训练项目都有直接参考价值；其"领域专家初始化 + 硬路由约束"的组合也呼应了当前关于专家多样性与路由稳定性的研究热点。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the collapse pathology and DivMoE's fix persist at 7B–13B dense bases, and does the ~8% Stage-1 token overhead stay acceptable when pre-training budgets reach trillions of tokens?
- Can the domain partition (number of groups n and their boundaries) be learned end-to-end — e.g., via clustering of router gradients or data geometry — instead of being hand-fixed to math/code/academic/web?
- Why does forced cross-domain composition hurt HumanEval against NVIDIA-Upcycling+SFT (51.4 vs 63.8) while helping it against the method's own ablation (+4.8)? Is there a code-heavy domain configuration (e.g., a larger code corpus share) that recovers single-domain peak performance?
- How does DivMoE interact with shared-expert designs (DeepSeek-V2 style) and with expert-choice routing, given the constraint currently requires fixed top-k group selection?
- The granularity study suggests m=k=4 overfits MMLU/HellaSwag — is this a routing-capacity effect or an initialization artifact of smaller shards, and does it vanish at larger token budgets?
