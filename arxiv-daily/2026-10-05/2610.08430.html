Critical edge

The 12–40× speedups are bandwidth-shaped — measured only over a slow ≤5 Gbps WAN, with no fast-interconnect comparison where the delta advantage would shrink.

Stress rates (3%/5%) come from a single 1e-6-LR recipe, and latency runs inject synthetic uniform-random changes rather than replayed optimizer deltas.

01Methodology — how it works

  1. Measure. Only 0.6–1.2% of BF16 elements flip stored bits per GRPO step, so a full transfer spends 98–99% of its bytes on unchanged values.
  2. Detect. One owner per shard diffs stored bit patterns (not float equality — catches ±0.0 sign flips) against a committed baseline.
  3. Project. Fixed affine maps push changes straight into canonical HuggingFace-checkpoint coordinates — 96.3–97.0% of weight bytes; a distributed residual baseline covers stacking, padding, tied weights and quantization scales.
  4. Encode. XOR masks reproduce exact bits and compress 1.7–2.2× smaller than overwrites (zstd-1); overwrites are reserved for representation-changing updates and retries.
  5. Apply. Deltas go in place through vLLM’s native loader; failed attempts retry as full overwrites, then a versioned joint commit. Proposition 1: receiver bits equal a dense refit.
  6. Deliver. Object storage or relay tree — exactly one cross-cluster crossing per attempt, no cross-cluster collective; construction, transfer and application run pipelined.

At 5% change: 41.6 s (18.0×) and 49.7 s (15.1×). Reference is transport-only — checkpoint save/load excluded, to the baseline’s advantage.

  • Relay tree wins at every size, its edge growing from 1.13× at 30B to 1.36× at 1T.
  • Transport is the bottleneck by design: 77–94% of refit latency vs 18–45% for construction; finalization adds just 0.2–1.7 s.
  • Mixed XOR/overwrite encoding cuts Qwen3 payload bytes 38–40% vs overwrites alone (30B @ 3%: 1.40 GB vs 2.34 GB).
  • Kill-and-recover: a random vLLM instance dies every 5 steps mid-refit; 50-step GRPO reward (0.41) and KL (≈0.002–0.03) track dense NCCL, with every post-apply check passing.

03Strengths

  • Only system meeting all five requirements (bit-exact, delta, native placement, decoupled, recoverable); every cited prior system meets at most two (Table 1).
  • Bit-exactness is proven (Proposition 1) and verified bitwise on every measured refit, with post-apply checks over all written bits.
  • Production-scale evidence: real 30B–1T MoE checkpoints, GB300 + H100 clusters across two AWS regions, and a kill-and-recover training run.
  • Canonical-coordinates abstraction is model-agnostic — Qwen3 and hybrid Nemotron/NeMo checkpoints handled by the same code.
  • Fully open-sourced in NeMo RL (PR #2444, ~7K LoC), so results are reproducible with comparable hardware.

04Limitations

  • Demonstrated only over a ≤5 Gbps cross-cluster WAN; the InfiniBand-class regime where the gap to dense NCCL narrows is never measured.
  • Change-rate evidence comes from one 1e-6-LR GRPO recipe; behavior at 10–20% change rates — where the delta advantage decays — is unquantified.
  • Latency benchmarks inject synthetic uniform-random changes instead of replaying real optimizer deltas at the largest scales.
  • Tightly coupled to vLLM’s native loader and Megatron Bridge mappings; SGLang / TensorRT-LLM support is asserted by design, not shown.
  • Receiver baselines cost ~1.03–1.04× one checkpoint in host memory (~36 GB/rank at 550B); the XOR/joint-commit protocol adds real operational complexity.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Where is the crossover change rate at which delta refit loses to dense transfer on a given link — and do realistic high-LR or long-horizon RL runs ever approach it?
  • On 100–400 Gbps interconnects, does delta construction (150–300 s at 1T) become the dominant cost?
  • Do the Table A.3 loader conditions port to SGLang or TensorRT-LLM — and what is the minimal native-loader API for bit-exact in-place updates?
  • Does bitwise-identical refit actually matter for RL stability — what empirically breaks with approximate reconstruction approaches (ROSE, ARoL-DTE)?
