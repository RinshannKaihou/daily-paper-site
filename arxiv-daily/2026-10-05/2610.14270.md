> **一句话总结：** 来自 KAUST 的团队证明：对 Qwen3 混合推理模型在 no-think 模式下做 GRPO 训练所获得的 AIME 准确率提升（8B：0.189→0.411），大部分并非学到了新推理能力，而是"思维泄漏"——把原有 think 模式的能力重新激活；把一个 8 维 think 子空间的激活替换回原始模型的取值即可抹掉 75% 的 RL 增益，且该增益经由少数几个注视空 think 块的注意力头（如 L18H19 单头占 44%）传递。

> **TL;DR:** Training Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B with GRPO in no-think mode raises AIME accuracy (8B: 0.189 → 0.411, +0.222), but much of this gain is "thinking leakage": the model reuses reasoning behavior already present in its own think mode (whose accuracy is 0.74) rather than learning new problem-solving. Scaling the RL weight update makes the no-think model emit `</think>` at the same position as think mode and approach — but never exceed — the original think-mode accuracy (0.72 vs 0.74); patching an 8-dimensional "think subspace" back to original-model values removes 75% of the RL gain (vs 22% for a matched PCA control), and the RL-induced shift passes through the few attention heads that read the empty think block (single head L18H19 carries 44% of the shift). A final analysis shows the GRPO advantage itself favors think-like responses: one gradient step on the full signal moves the model along the think direction (Δα = +0.0075) while shuffled advantages do not (−0.0022).

## 核心贡献

- **提出并量化"思维泄漏"现象**：在 no-think 模式下用 GRPO（DAPO-Math-17k，可验证奖励）训练 Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B 后，AIME 准确率提升的同时，回答变长（8B：2.7k→7.2k tokens）、反思词增多（1.3→13.0/条）、think 胜率（用原始模型做固定裁判）从 50% 升至 94%（8B），内部激活沿原始模型 think 方向的偏移 α 达 0.56（8B）/0.40（4B）/0.21（1.7B）——行为与内部状态整体向 think 模式漂移。
- **权重层面的因果证据**：将 RL 更新放大为 W₀+cΔW，缩放后的模型在 no-think 模式中生成 `</think>`（step-67 更新在 c=3 时 51% 的回答出现），且位置与 think 模式中几乎一致（中位数 0.91 vs 0.92）；准确率最高升至 0.72，但在任何被测 c 下都**不超过**原始模型 think 模式的 0.74。
- **激活层面的因果证据**：仅用原始模型和训练 prompt 构造 8 维 think 子空间，做跨模型激活修补：original→RL 修补把 8B 准确率从 0.411 压回 0.244，**抹掉 75% 的 RL 增益**（匹配维度 PCA 对照只抹掉 22%，随机子空间 −3%）；反向 RL→original 修补把原始模型从 0.189 提到 0.300，转移 50% 的增益。
- **注意力头层面的证据**：空 think 块（`<think>\n\n</think>\n\n`）的信息只经注意力进入回答；knockout 掉注意力最集中的 5% 头即可使 α=0.78、KL 相对移动 R_KL=72%（8B），而随机 5% 头 α≤0.04、R_KL≤6%；单头修补实验中 L18H19（8B/4B）与 L19H13（1.7B）分别承载 RL 偏移的 44%、37%、54%，且这些头都属于注视空 think 块最多的前 5% 头。
- **溯源到训练信号**：GRPO 组内优势结构性偏向 think 风格回答——组内仅 1 个正确回答时其优势为 +1.79，4 个正确时降为 +0.45，而正确回答的 think 胜率随 k 增大从 57.8% 降至 40.5%；在新鲜 LoRA 上走一步完整 GRPO 梯度使 α 增加 +0.0075，组内打乱优势后变为 −0.0022，均匀加权仅 +0.0006，说明漂移主要来自"正/负优势分配给哪些回答"。

## Key Contributions

- Identifies and names **thinking leakage**: RLVR in no-think mode of hybrid models (Qwen3) largely reactivates the think mode's existing reasoning rather than adding new capability, shown consistently across output behavior (length, reflection words, think win rate: 50%→94% at 8B) and internal activations (shift α = 0.56 of the full think/no-think activation difference at 8B, cosine 0.33→0.70 with the think direction).
- Provides **three levels of causal interventions** with controls: scaling the RL update (W₀+cΔW) approaches but never exceeds the original think-mode accuracy (peak 0.72 vs 0.74); cross-model activation patching of an 8-dim think subspace removes 75% of the gain (PCA control 22%, random −3%) and transfers 50% in the reverse direction; attention knockout and single-head patching localize the shift to the ~5% of heads attending to the empty think block (top head L18H19: 44% of the shift, held-out confirmation 44/37/56%).
- **Traces the cause to the GRPO training signal**: advantages depend only on the number of correct responses k in a group of 5 (+1.79 at k=1 vs +0.45 at k=4), and correct responses with larger advantages are more think-like (TWR 57.8%→40.5% as k goes 1→4); a single gradient step on the full GRPO signal moves the model toward think mode (Δα=+0.0075), while shuffling advantages within groups (−0.0022) or weighting all responses equally (+0.0006) does not.
- Methodological care: the 8-dim subspace, patched layers, and primary comparisons were fixed before any AIME result was seen; α estimates are cross-fitted (effect ≤0.01); head rankings are validated on 60 held-out responses.

## Methodology

**Models and training.** Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B ("original models") are trained with a GRPO-style recipe on DAPO-Math-17k entirely in no-think mode (the chat template appends an empty think block `<think>\n\n</think>\n\n`). Each step samples 256 prompts × 5 responses at temperature 1, with a PPO-style clipped objective (clip 0.2, token-mean loss), a KL penalty of 1e-3 to the original model, and LoRA rank 64 (scale 64) on all linear layers; AdamW, lr 1e-5, ~67 steps.

**Evaluation.** AIME 2022–2024 (90 problems, both I and II), 2 samples per problem at temperature 1, 32,768-token budget, graded by the last `\boxed` expression.

**Measures of think-likeness.** (1) Reflection-word counts (wait, hmm, alternatively, ...); (2) **think win rate (TWR)**: the original model as a fixed judge scores each response under think vs no-think prefixes; the Mann-Whitney statistic against the original model's own no-think responses gives 50% by construction; (3) **thinking activation shift α**: the projection of a checkpoint's mean activation change onto the "think direction" v (the original model's activation difference between modes on the same fixed tokens), at the middle layer (18/36 for 8B, 14/28 for 1.7B), computed with cross-fitting to avoid upward bias.

**Three interventions.**
1. *Weight scaling*: evaluate W₀+cΔW for c>1 (task arithmetic style) on the step-15 and step-67 updates, exploring c on the same 90 AIME problems.
2. *Activation patching*: an 8-dimensional think subspace per layer is estimated from 128 DAPO-Math-17k prompts using only original-model activations (mean think/no-think difference + 7 orthogonal directions). Cross-model patching replaces, at every layer 1–35 and every position, only the target's component inside the subspace with the source model's (Eq. 6), preserving token alignment. Controls: a matched PCA subspace and a random subspace of the same size.
3. *Attention heads*: heads are ranked by attention weight on the empty think block; attention knockout zeroes and renormalizes attention to the block for selected head sets (measuring α and the KL-based R_KL toward the think-mode distribution); single-head cross-model patching measures each head's share of the full RL-induced shift.

**Cause analysis.** 20 responses per training question are sampled from the original model; GRPO advantages are computed in groups of 5. TWR of correct vs wrong responses is compared across and within groups; a fresh LoRA adapter receives one AdamW step of the GRPO gradient under different weightings (full / positive-only / negative-only / shuffled / uniform), and Δα is measured on the fixed responses.

## Results & Highlights

- **RL in no-think mode shifts the model toward think mode.** Qwen3-8B AIME accuracy rises from 0.17 to 0.43 over ~67 steps (final unpatched comparison: 0.411 vs 0.189 original, G=0.222); response length grows 2.7k→7.2k tokens (8B) and reflection words 1.3→13.0 per response (8B), against think-mode references of 16k–18k tokens and ~120–130 reflection words with accuracies 0.74/0.69/0.43 (8B/4B/1.7B). Think win rate rises from 50% to 94% (8B), 86% (4B), 84% (1.7B), and the activation shift α reaches 0.56/0.40/0.21 respectively, with shift-vs-think-direction cosine rising 0.33→0.70 at 8B.
- **Scaling the RL update approaches but never exceeds think-mode accuracy.** The step-67 update at c=3 reaches 0.72 accuracy and emits `</think>` in 51% of responses (6% at c=1.5); the step-15 update at c=7 reaches 0.683 with 19.4% `</think>`; no tested c exceeds the original model's think-mode 0.74. All 443 `</think>` occurrences (of 2,520 scaled responses) appear exactly once at median position 0.91 vs 0.92 in think mode, and none contains an opening `<think>`.
- **Patching the 8-dim think subspace removes or transfers most of the gain.** Original→RL patching drops 8B accuracy from 0.411 to 0.244, removing **75%** of G=0.222 (length falls 7.0k→4.2k, reflection words 13.0→3.9); the matched PCA subspace removes 22% (0.361) and the random subspace −3% (0.417) — the pre-specified think-vs-PCA difference is 0.117 accuracy. RL→original patching lifts the original model from 0.189 to 0.300, transferring **50%** of G (PCA 25%, random 10%); a 16-dim subspace transfers 60% (accuracy 0.322), while the single 1-dim think direction removes only 22%.
- **A small, consistent set of attention heads carries the shift.** ~90% of heads place <1% of attention on the empty think block; knocking out attention in the top-5% heads gives α=0.78/0.88/0.66 and R_KL=72%/74%/77% (8B/4B/1.7B) vs α≤0.04 and R_KL≤6% for random 5% sets; knockout in all heads (α=1.11/1.17/1.13, R_KL=89%/85%/90%) nearly matches deleting the block itself (α=1.03/1.11/1.08, R_KL=93%/88%/92%). Single-head patching: L18H19 carries 44% (8B) and 37% (4B) of the RL shift, L19H13 54% (1.7B), confirmed on 60 held-out responses (44%/37%/56%); all three top heads per model rank in the top 0.2–0.9% by block attention and belong to the top-5% set.
- **The GRPO signal itself favors think-like responses.** Correct-response advantages fall from +1.79 (k=1) to +0.45 (k=4) while their TWR falls 57.8%→40.5%; within groups, correct responses beat wrong ones by 3.0 percentile points of judge percentile on average. One gradient step: full GRPO signal Δα=+0.0075, positive-only +0.0072, negative-only +0.0057, shuffled-within-group −0.0022, uniform +1 weighting +0.0006 (2,990 responses, Qwen3-8B).
- **Appendix steering confirms the direction is sufficient for behavior**: adding a·v^think at layer 18 of the original 8B raises accuracy from 0.189 (a=0) to 0.60 (a=1), with reflection words 1.9→81.2, though 24% of responses then hit the 32k budget.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from the Image and Video Understanding Lab at KAUST (Wenxuan Zhu, Shuming Liu, Bang An, Aznaur Aliev, Bing Li, Bernard Ghanem) and Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Yibo Yang). Ghanem's group is a well-established computer/ML lab with a strong publication record; the paper follows an unusually careful empirical protocol (pre-registered subspace dimension and patched layers, cross-fitted activation metrics, held-out validation of head rankings, matched PCA/random controls), and code is released on GitHub. The team cites and correctly positions itself within the fast-moving 2025 literature on whether RLVR adds capability beyond the base model (Yue et al., Zhao et al., Zhu et al.) and on activation/attention interventions (Arditi et al., Geiger et al., Zhang & Nanda). No red flags regarding authorship or venue; this is a preprint.

## Critical Assessment

- **Single model family, single domain.** All conclusions rest on Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B and math (AIME + DAPO-Math-17k). The paper motivates the study with Llama-Nemotron as another hybrid model but never tests it; whether leakage generalizes to other hybrid architectures, other RL algorithms (this is a PPO/GRPO-specific mechanism — the cause analysis in Section 6 is tied to group-normalized advantages), or non-math verifiable tasks is untested.
- **Small and noisy evaluation.** The headline patching numbers come from 90 AIME problems × 2 samples (180 responses per condition). The authors themselves show the two independent samplings of the unpatched models differ by 0.022/0.017 accuracy (0.411 vs 0.433; 0.189 vs 0.172) — the same order as some reported contrasts (e.g., the −3% random-subspace effect). No error bars are reported anywhere.
- **The cause analysis is correlational, and the authors say so.** GRPO advantages are determined by k (number correct in the group), so the association "larger advantage ↔ more think-like" is confounded by question difficulty (small-k groups are harder questions whose correct responses are more think-like). The one-gradient-step LoRA probe (Δα=+0.0075) is an initial-gradient proxy, not a demonstration that full training amplifies leakage through this channel; the within-group percentile contrast (3.0 points) is the cleanest evidence but is modest in magnitude.
- **The scaling experiment is exploratory on the eval set.** Scaling factors were explored on the same 90 AIME problems; the peaks (c=7 for step 15, c=3 for step 67) are selected, not held-out values, and the appendix concedes this. The "never exceeds think-mode accuracy" claim is therefore bounded by the tested grid.
- **Leakage explains most, not all, of the gain.** The 8-dim subspace removes 75% but the reverse patch transfers only 50% (60% at 16 dims); a quarter of the gain lives outside the analyzed component, and the paper does not characterize what that residual is (the appendix's Table 8 shows removing the update's top-8 singular directions hurts more than removing its think-subspace component, 0.431→0.315 vs 0.435 — suggesting the update contains important non-leak structure the framing undersells).
- **The judge metric has a mild circularity.** TWR uses the original model as judge of its own modes; the ~100% mode separation and 0.08 nats/token gap mitigate this, but "the model prefers its own think-mode text" is not the same as "the response is doing think-mode reasoning."
- These caveats do not undermine the central result — the 75%/50% patching numbers against matched controls are striking — but the paper's framing ("much of the gain") is sometimes stronger in the abstract than the 50% reverse-transfer figure supports.

## Strengths

- **Triangulated causal evidence**: three independent intervention levels (weights, activations, attention heads) all point to the same mechanism, each with matched negative controls (PCA/random subspaces, random head sets, shuffled advantages) — this is far above the standard for RLVR analysis papers.
- **Pre-registration discipline within a paper**: subspace dimensionality, patched layers, and the primary think-vs-PCA comparison were fixed before any AIME result was seen; head effects validated on held-out responses; cross-fitting removes the known inner-product bias in α.
- **A clean experimental design unique to hybrid models**: the two modes share all weights and differ only in a few prompt tokens, making the think direction, subspace, and empty-block attention well-defined targets for intervention.
- **Honest self-limitation**: the paper explicitly flags which analyses are exploratory (scaling factors), which are correlational (Section 6 associations), and that the frozen-weight-term dominance (Appendix F) is not itself evidence of leakage.
- Practically important implication: no-think-mode RLVR benchmarks may conflate "new capability" with "mode control," which changes how hybrid-model post-training should be evaluated.

## Limitations

- Findings are demonstrated only on Qwen3 at ≤8B scale and on AIME-style math; no second model family, no other RL algorithm, no out-of-math transfer task.
- Evaluation is 90 problems × 2 samples per condition without confidence intervals; several reported deltas are comparable in size to the observed sampling noise.
- The GRPO-signal explanation remains an association plus an initial-gradient probe; no training run is re-run with a leakage-debiasing objective to test the cause interventional.
- The 8-dim subspace accounts for 75% (removal) / 50–60% (transfer) of the gain; the remaining share and the role of the update's top singular directions are left uncharacterized.
- All activation analyses use fixed pre-generated responses with teacher forcing at 2,048 tokens (α values differ slightly from free-generation settings), and the TWR judge is the original model itself.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击当前 RLVR/推理模型研究的核心争议——"RL 到底是新增能力还是唤起已有能力"，并给出了少见的权重-激活-注意力头三层因果证据，与用户对 LLM 推理、RL 后训练和机制可解释性的兴趣高度契合。其结论对任何在混合推理模型上做 no-think 模式 RL 训练与评测的人都是必读的警示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does thinking leakage appear in other hybrid reasoning models (e.g., Llama-Nemotron) and under non-group-normalized RL algorithms (PPO with a value baseline, REINFORCE), where the advantage-structure mechanism of Section 6 would not apply?
- What is the residual 25–40% of the RL gain that the think subspace does not explain — new problem-solving behavior, better formatting/answer extraction, or length/budget effects?
- Can a leakage-aware training objective (e.g., penalizing the activation shift α during RL) preserve the no-think accuracy gain while preventing mode drift, and would that gain then shrink to zero?
- Does leakage work in the opposite direction — does RL in think mode degrade no-think behavior through the same heads — and is the think/no-think boundary reversible by targeted head-level edits alone?
- Does the empty-think-block head circuit (e.g., L18H19) exist in models trained with different templates or with latent/continuous reasoning, or is it an artifact of the Qwen3 template's four sentinel tokens?
