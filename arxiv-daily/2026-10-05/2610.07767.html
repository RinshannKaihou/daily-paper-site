Why this matters

FP4 quantization moves from inference-only acceleration into the RL training loop — align execution paths, don’t just minimize per-path error.

把 FP4 量化从「推理加速」推进到「RL 训练闭环」：路径一致性比单路径量化精度更重要。

Critical edge

The headline +6.5 came from QUADS’s own authors re-running their own baseline, on Qwen-family models only — no external replication, no code release.

Methodology // rollout-guided QAT

1 · Rollout (FP4)SGLang engine generates; actual E2M1 codewords recorded at routed-expert activations + FP4 KV.
→
2 · Cache1-bit mantissa + scale, latter 20/40 layers only ≈ 7.5 KB/token (~6× less), async GPU/CPU staging.
→
3 · QAT forwardPick the adjacent E2M1 codeword nearest the rollout’s: q = argmin |q − q_rollout|.
→
4 · TrainStraight-through gradients; local guarantee ≤ round-to-nearest, per site.

The wrong objective. Existing FP4 RL methods (QAT, QaRL, QUADS, Rollout-ResQ) minimize quantization error on each execution path independently — which does not minimize the train–rollout discrepancy. QUADS cuts per-path error yet leaves discrepancies of 0.10/0.99; inconsistent rounding can amplify a 0.48 BF16 activation gap (60.24 vs 59.76) into a 24-point quantized gap (72 vs 48).

Rollout-guided rounding. At each quantization site, the training activation is normalized with the rollout scale, bracketed by its two adjacent E2M1 codewords, and assigned the one closest to the recorded rollout codeword — by construction never locally worse than RTN. The authors are explicit: the guarantee is per-site and local; it removes rounding inconsistency, not the underlying activation difference from policy staleness.

Why mantissa-only. Full rollout records would be ~50 KB/token — ≈51 TB per RL step at 4096 trajectories. Two measured facts shrink this ~6×: over 95% of mismatched codewords differ by exactly one adjacent code, and downward corrections concentrate in deeper layers — so only the latter 20 of 40 layers need guidance.

Setup. GRPO + R3 on VeRL/Megatron, async SGLang rollout. NVFP4 (one MXFP4 study) on routed experts + KV cache only; attention activations, dense modules, embeddings, lm_head stay BF16. Four MoE models, up to 256K context, batches of 64–256 trajectories × 16.

Strengths //

  • Sharp, falsifiable problem statement — per-path quantization accuracy ≠ train–rollout agreement — demonstrated with hand-checkable numeric examples (Figure 3).
  • First framework to show joint FP4 weight/activation/KV RL rollout matching BF16 (75.3 vs 74.9) rather than merely degrading less.
  • Caching design grounded in measured statistics (>95% adjacent-codeword mismatches, deeper-layer concentration) and validated end-to-end: ~6× guidance cut, near-vanilla FP4 throughput, 7.4% overhead.
  • Unusually complete ablations (W vs KV, MXFP4/NVFP4 grid, bits × layers, Score Centering, post-hoc PTQ) and honest reporting of unfavorable details — the confounded 128K speedup, the initial dip, the local-only guarantee.

Limitations //

  • One model family (Qwen3.5/3.8), one algorithm (GRPO+R3), all in-house; the headline baseline QUADS shares five co-authors with this paper.
  • FP4 covers only routed experts + attention KV — attention activations and dense modules stay BF16; full-model FP4 RL is untested, so the discrepancy problem may resurface at wider coverage.
  • The guarantee is local and per-site; the ~120-step adaptation recovery is empirical and unanalyzed — the 2.4T long-horizon run is only 50 steps.
  • The “latter 20 of 40 layers” heuristic is validated for layer count but not layer choice, and only on one architecture; guidance plumbing adds real systems complexity.
  • No code, models, or seeds released — academic reproduction is essentially impossible at the upper scale.

Questions for further exploration //

  • Does rollout-guided rounding transfer to dense (non-MoE) models and non-GRPO RL, where routed-expert guidance doesn’t exist?
  • Can the latter-half-layer caching heuristic be derived automatically from layer statistics, and hold across depth/width ratios?
  • What happens under full-model FP4 — attention activations and dense layers included, BF16 “escape hatches” gone?
  • Is the ~120-step adaptation recovery guaranteed for short RL runs, or is there a regime where the initial dip never closes?
  • How does TRACE behave at FP3/FP2 or 1.58-bit, where the adjacent-codeword argument weakens because the codebook is too sparse?
