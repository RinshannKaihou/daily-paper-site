> **一句话总结：** RECAP 针对"低比特量化破坏推理模型长思维链"的问题，把校准目标从"重建当前层输出"改为"盯住模型实际要读的历史 KV"：动态前缀校准每步只重建 4K 活跃块、其前最长 12K 前缀的 K/V 用当前量化器重生成并沿轨迹轮转（证明是全序列重建目标的无偏划分，注意力对计算量约省 3/4），前缀读取几何再按注意输出的一阶敏感度给历史缓存误差加权（键误差按查询方向投影、值误差按所受注意力强度，不对称处理）；W4A4KV4 下 MATH500 上 R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B 达 79.4% vs FlatQuant 66.1%（+13.3 pts），Qwen3-4B 的 AIME-120 从 32.8% 提到 48.6%（+15.8 pts），且全部开销只在校准期、推理零额外成本。

> **TL;DR:** RECAP (SpinEntropy + ETH Zurich + PolyU, under review at ICLR 2027) is a post-training quantization method that calibrates low-bit reasoning models on the KV history they actually read. It diagnoses three failures of standard calibration: cache-importance scores transfer across reasoning trajectories at near-chance AUC (0.43–0.50), short calibration windows and fixed long prefixes each leave opposite regions of the sequence unsupervised (teacher-forced KL diverges to 7.33 nats/token late in an 8K context for a 4K window, and to 6.58 in the first 1K for a fixed 4K-prefix/late-block split), and equal-size cache errors distort attention unequally. Its fix has two calibration-only components: (1) dynamic-prefix calibration reconstructs only a rotating 4K active block per update while regenerating up to 12K prefix K/V under the current quantizer — a provably unbiased partition of full-sequence reconstruction at ~1/4 the attention pairs; (2) prefix read geometry adds an auxiliary loss weighting historical key errors by their projection onto query directions (shared rank-8 PSD metric) and value errors by the attention they receive, with statistics collected once from the FP model and discarded after calibration. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B under W4A4KV4 it reaches 79.4% on MATH500 vs 66.1% for the FlatQuant baseline (+13.3 pts; BF16 84.7%), and on Qwen3-4B it lifts AIME-120 from 32.8% to 48.6% (+15.8 pts), recovering 60.5% of FlatQuant's deficit to BF16 — with no inference-time token selection or precision allocation.

## 核心贡献

- **动机分析（三个诊断性发现）**：推理模型量化中，历史缓存的重要性得分几乎不跨轨迹迁移（5 种打分规则在 held-out 轨迹上识别 top-10% 有益缓存修复的 AUC 仅 0.43–0.50，接近随机）；短窗口校准与"长前缀+只重建末段"校准分别在序列两端失察（8K 上下文、4K 窗口的 teacher-forced KL 从前 1K 位置的 0.14 nats/token 涨到末 1K 的 7.33，而只重建 4K–8K 的模型在前 1K 达 6.58）；等大小的缓存量化误差因注意力权重不同而对输出的影响不等（如规则步骤 r²=9 比"so"被读得更重）。
- **动态前缀校准**：把轨迹按 B=4K 分块，每步只重建一个活跃块，但活跃块之前的全部历史 K/V 用"当前"量化参数从前缀 FP 层输入重新生成，并按 j=(c+i) mod J 轮转活跃块——每个位置每 J 个 epoch 被监督一次；证明了固定参数下各块损失恰为全序列重建目标的划分（Eq. 7），因此是无偏估计，每次更新的 query-key 对数约为全序列重建的 1/4（T=4B 时）。
- **前缀读取几何**：从注意输出对前缀 K/V 扰动的一阶展开（GGN/Jacobian Gram 矩阵）导出辅助目标——键误差经"与查询的对齐 + 值对比 (v_t−y_s)"起作用，按 tr(G^K)（对位置桶×KV 头池化、秩 r=8 的 PSD 共享近似）加权；值误差按 E[a_s²]（所受注意力）加权。该度量只从 FP 模型收集一次并在校准前冻结，校准结束后推理完全不需要。
- **一致的实验收益**：3 个推理模型（R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B、Qwen3-4B）× 2 种位宽（W4A4KV4、W4A8KV4）× 5 个基准上，RECAP 在几乎所有格子持平或超过 FlatQuant（唯一平局是 R1-7B GSM8K 90.9%），且收益集中于长链推理任务（AIME-120、MATH500、LiveCodeBench），与动机预测一致。

## Key Contributions

- **Motivation analysis with quantitative diagnostics**: cache-importance scores tested (codeword shrinkage, residual-direction cluster, position-bin shrinkage, future-read count, local V error) rank the top-10% most beneficial cache repairs at AUC 0.429–0.504 on a held-out trajectory — none exceeds chance (0.51); 4K-window and fixed-prefix calibrations fail in complementary sequence regions (KL 0.14→7.33 vs 6.58→0.17 nats/token), while rotating-prefix coverage leaves no 1K bucket above 0.191.
- **Dynamic-prefix calibration**: decouples *what* the calibrator sees (long quantized history, up to 12K) from *what* it reconstructs (one 4K block per update); the prefix K/V is regenerated at every microstep under the current quantizer, and block rotation gives an unbiased estimator of the full-sequence objective (Eq. 7) at roughly a quarter of the query-key pair computation.
- **Prefix read geometry**: an asymmetric first-order cache penalty — key errors weighted by per-entry key strength tr(G^K) via a shared rank-8 PSD approximation pooled per position bucket and GQA head, value errors by mean squared attention E[a_s²] — supervising history by its effect on attention outputs, which raw cache reconstruction error does not capture. Calibration-only: reference statistics and the auxiliary loss are discarded for inference.
- **Consistent empirical gains**: under W4A4KV4, RECAP beats FlatQuant by 6.3 / 4.8 / 6.1 average points on R1-1.5B / R1-7B / Qwen3-4B and is the best quantized method in all six model-precision combinations; the released code is at github.com/AI-Efficiency/RECAP.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Calibration uses self-generated full-precision trajectories (NummaMath-1.5 traces; pool of 1,000 for R1-1.5B, filtered to remove exact-match overlaps with MATH500/GSM8K; default 64 fit trajectories). RECAP optimizes FlatQuant's learnable transformations, diagonal scales, and clipping parameters with pretrained weights frozen, under W4A4KV4 / W4A8KV4 (symmetric per-channel weights, asymmetric per-token activations, asymmetric KV in groups of 128, 16-bit queries). The full-sequence reference objective is layerwise MSE between quantized and FP outputs under teacher forcing.

**Dynamic prefixes.** Each trajectory is partitioned into J = T/B blocks of B = 4K tokens. Per update, the active block starting at P = jB is reconstructed with loss L^block (Eq. 6): queries in the block attend to the prefix and to causally available K/V inside the block, and only the B active outputs are penalized. The prefix K/V, C_{<P}(θ_l) = KV_θ(H^FP_{<P}), is rebuilt from fixed FP layer inputs with the *current* layer normalization, projection, activation, and cache-quantization rules at every microstep, keeping it consistent with changing parameters while gradients flow through it. The active block rotates (j = (c+i) mod J) so every position is supervised once per J epochs; the first block uses an ordinary causal forward. When parameters are fixed, the block losses exactly partition L^full (Eq. 7), making a uniformly sampled block an unbiased estimate of full-sequence reconstruction. Cost: BP + B(B+1)/2 query-key pairs per update versus T(T+1)/2 for full-sequence reconstruction — with T = 4B, about one quarter.

**Prefix read geometry.** From the first-order change in attention head output (Eq. 8), a key perturbation at t < P acts through its alignment with the query and the value contrast (v_t − y_s), while a value error enters scaled by the attention weight a_st. Taking the GGN-style Jacobian Gram matrix and keeping per-entry diagonal blocks gives key strength G^K = E[a_st²‖v_t−y_s‖²/d_h · q_s q_sᵀ] and value strength g^V = E[a_st²]. Per-entry key strengths are pooled into position buckets per KV head, replaced by a rank-8 PSD shared approximation (trailing spectrum by isotropic residual); value strengths are scalars. The penalty R_k (Eq. 10) compares quantized vs FP K/V in the current learned basis (true right inverses retained in gradients) and is added to the block loss with a fixed per-layer coefficient α_l set by a gradient-ratio heuristic (median block-to-auxiliary ratio at initialization). Metrics are collected once from the FP target model and frozen; after calibration nothing extra is needed at inference.

**Evaluation protocol.** Temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, 32,768-token output limit; AIME-120 = 90 problems from 2022–2024 + 30 from 2025, averaged over 3 runs (MATH500 ablations likewise over 3 runs).

## Results & Highlights

- **MATH500, W4A4KV4, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B**: RECAP 79.4% vs FlatQuant 66.1% (+13.3 pts) and QuaRot 1.4% (released checkpoints degenerate); BF16 reference 84.7%. Under W4A8KV4 the same model reaches 82.1% vs 67.1% for FlatQuant (+15.0 pts). Corresponding attention readout error (Fig. 1a): cumulative NMSE at a ~14.8K reader position with a 12K INT4 KV prefix is 2.99% for RECAP vs 36.51% for the baseline.
- **AIME-120, W4A4KV4**: +9.7 / +8.6 / +15.8 points over FlatQuant on R1-1.5B (18.3 vs 8.6), R1-7B (34.7 vs 26.1), and Qwen3-4B (48.6 vs 32.8; BF16 59.9) — on Qwen3-4B, RECAP recovers 60.5% of FlatQuant's deficit to BF16. LiveCodeBench improves by 4.6 / 6.4 / 8.3 points (e.g., Qwen3-4B 37.4 vs 29.1).
- **Five-benchmark averages**: RECAP exceeds FlatQuant by 6.3 / 4.8 / 6.1 points under W4A4KV4 and 6.8 / 3.6 / 1.1 under W4A8KV4; relative to BF16 it retains 90.9–91.2% of the average score under W4A4KV4 vs 78.0–82.6% for FlatQuant, closing 48–58% of FlatQuant's remaining gap. Gains on short-history tasks stay small (GSM8K and GPQA-Diamond never exceed +3.1 and +2.1).
- **Ablations (R1-1.5B, W4A4KV4, Table 2)**: extending the prefix 8K→12K adds +1.2 MATH500 (78.2→79.4) and +1.6 AIME-120 (16.6→18.3) while cutting mean generation length 14.3% / 10.8%; read geometry over block-MSE-only adds +1.0–1.6 on MATH500 and +2.5–3.3 on AIME-120 with 4.8–11.8% fewer generated tokens (e.g., AIME-120: 18.3%/19,865 tokens vs 15.0%/20,866). Monotone prefix sweep (Fig. 3c): 69.4 (0K) → 75.0 (4K) → 78.0 (8K) → 79.4 (12K) MATH500.
- **Generation-length forensics**: of the 101 MATH500 questions that flip correct→incorrect between BF16 and FlatQuant, 96 produce longer quantized outputs approaching the 32K limit — quantization errors corrupt history and cause runaway generations, which explains why RECAP's accuracy gains come together with shorter outputs.
- **Baseline collapse**: QuaRot collapses on both R1 models (averages ≤5.3% under W4A4KV4; the released 7B K4 checkpoints degenerate during generation) but stays competitive on Qwen3-4B (55.9 avg) — quantization difficulty differs markedly across model families.

## Authors & Credibility

Chi Ruan (SpinEntropy), Yujie Chen and Zixiang Zhao (ETH Zurich), Jingcai Guo and Haotong Qin (PolyU; Qin corresponding, a recognized researcher in efficient/quantized deep learning). The paper is under review at ICLR 2027 (not yet peer-reviewed), with a public GitHub repository (AI-Efficiency/RECAP), a reproducibility statement (experiment configurations, evaluation outputs, and analysis scripts retained), and an AI-use statement disclosing AI-assisted manuscript revision and FP-model-generated calibration trajectories. The baselines are the official FlatQuant method and published R1/Qwen3 numbers where available, and AIME-120/MATH500 results are averaged over three runs — above-average protocol hygiene for this area.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but incremental at the mechanism level.** The reframing — calibrate on "the history the model reads" — is well-earned by the diagnostics (near-chance AUC transfer in Table 4; the complementary 7.33-vs-6.58 nats/token failure regions). But dynamic-prefix calibration is essentially a sequence-dimension extension of FlatQuant's own block reconstruction with a rotation schedule, and read geometry is a first-order (diagonal GGN, rank-8) heuristic that deliberately drops cross-position K–V cross terms — the method adds machinery on top of one PTQ framework rather than a new quantization principle.
- **Effectively a one-baseline comparison.** QuaRot's released R1 checkpoints degenerate during generation (avg ≤5.3%), so the quantitative story rests almost entirely on FlatQuant; reasoning-aware self-calibration methods the paper itself cites (Self-calibration, AYOT) and KV-cache quantizers (KIVI, RotateKV, OSCAR) are discussed but not compared. No GPTQ-style framework is tested, so it is unknown whether dynamic prefixes help non-FlatQuant calibrators.
- **Attribution is weaker than the headline.** The authors concede the prefix-length ablation "cohorts differ, so the prefix comparison also changes data exposure," and that read-geometry point estimates "do not establish statistical superiority" with decoding differences further limiting causal attribution. Read geometry's own gain is modest (+1.0–1.6 MATH500, +2.5–3.3 AIME-120 on the single ablated model, R1-1.5B) — most of the headline comes from simply supervising history at all.
- **Data/domain quirk**: calibration trajectories are math-only (NummaMath-1.5), yet the largest claimed wins include code (LiveCodeBench +8.3 on Qwen3-4B) and science QA; no ablation isolates whether code/mixed calibration data would change the picture, and the FP-model dependency inherits self-calibration biases.
- **Statistical thinness on close margins**: AIME-120 is 120 problems; the 15.8-point gain is robust, but the W4A8KV4 Qwen3-4B margin (69.1 vs 68.0, with BF16 at 70.8) is within plausible run-to-run noise even with 3 evaluations. The +13.3 headline also sits on the smallest, most degradation-prone model where FlatQuant is weakest.
- **No serving metrics**: despite the motivation being deployment cost, there is no throughput/latency/memory measurement, only the ~1/4 attention-pair calibration cost argument.

## Strengths

- The motivation section is unusually rigorous: five tested importance scores, four-fold cross-trajectory AUC evaluation, position-bucket KL coverage diagnostics — the three failures are demonstrated, not asserted.
- Clean theoretical property (block losses partition the full-sequence objective, Eq. 7) backing an unbiased estimator, plus explicit cost accounting (~1/4 of attention pairs).
- Both components are calibration-only: no inference-time token selection, precision allocation, or retained statistics — deployment behavior is unchanged.
- Gains land exactly where the motivation predicts (long-history AIME/LCB/MATH vs near-flat GSM8K/GPQA), an internal consistency check many quantization papers lack.
- Honest limitations: the authors flag their own cohort confounds, statistical weakness of point estimates, and the decoding-difference confound, and the length/correctness analysis (96/101 flips produce longer outputs) is a genuinely useful forensic result.

## Limitations

- Diminishing returns when the baseline is already strong: under W4A8KV4 on Qwen3-4B the gain over FlatQuant narrows to 1.1 point (69.1 vs 68.0), so the method mainly rescues badly-degraded model/settings.
- Validated only on ≤7B dense distilled reasoning models with one PTQ framework (FlatQuant) and one calibration domain (math); larger, MoE, or non-math settings are untested.
- Ablations are confined to R1-1.5B; read-geometry adds tuning surface (rank r = 8, per-layer α_l via a gradient-ratio heuristic, position buckets) whose sensitivity is unexplored.
- Remaining gap to full precision is still large on the weakest setting (44.5 vs 49.0 BF16 average on R1-1.5B W4A4KV4), and the paper does not claim otherwise.
- Under review, not yet peer-reviewed; the near-chance importance-transfer conclusion is drawn from five scores on one model family (the authors themselves note it is "not a general impossibility claim").

## Relevance

这篇论文正对着用户「极端性能优化下的可靠性」核心关注：它量化了低比特 KV cache 误差如何沿推理轨迹累积并破坏长链推理，并给出"按读历史的影响加权校准"这一可复用的精度修复手段。同时其"重要性得分不跨轨迹迁移、等大小误差影响不等"的诊断，对推理栈静默退化（silent degradation）检测也有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does prefix read geometry transfer to other PTQ frameworks (e.g., GPTQ-style Hessian objectives) and to larger or MoE reasoning models, or is the gain tied to FlatQuant's transformation structure?
- Would a mixed math + code calibration corpus close the LiveCodeBench gap further, and does the read-geometry delta persist out of the calibration domain?
- Can the near-chance transfer of cache-importance scores (AUC ≤ 0.51) be fixed with trajectory-conditioned scores, enabling *inference-time* selective KV protection as a complement to calibration-only supervision?
- Is RECAP's shorter-generation effect caused by restored history fidelity or by changed logit calibration, and how does it interact with sampling temperature and the 32K budget (the 96/101 flip analysis suggests a mechanistic study)?
- How does RECAP compose with sub-4-bit KV cache quantization (KIVI/RotateKV-style), given RECAP quantizes KV to 4-bit groups of 128 but leaves queries at 16 bits?
