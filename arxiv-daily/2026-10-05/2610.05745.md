> **一句话总结：** 论文发现量化 VLA 在环境扰动下泛化能力大幅退化（LIBERO-Plus 上 GR00T-N1.5 + QuantVLA 平均成功率从 81.4% 跌至 66.6%），且量化导致的动作偏差高度集中在少数"高脆弱"状态上；据此提出 PIVOT-Q，每条轨迹只挑选 16 个高脆弱状态做脆弱度加权蒸馏 + 行为锚定，就把成功率恢复到 81.3%（接近全精度参考），超过全状态蒸馏 FSD 的 76.9%，而状态级蒸馏预算只用后者的约 7.4%。

> **TL;DR:** Post-training quantization preserves VLA accuracy on standard LIBERO but causes a hidden generalization collapse on LIBERO-Plus (GR00T-N1.5 + W4A8 QuantVLA: 81.4% → 66.6% across seven distribution shifts). The authors show teacher–student action discrepancies are log-normal with a heavy, behaviorally-consequential tail, and propose PIVOT-Q, an on-policy distillation method that distills only 16 vulnerability-selected states per trajectory (α=β-ranked current + discounted future discrepancy, phase-balanced top-k) plus a Behavioral Anchor replay loss. Across four model–quantizer pairs it beats full-state distillation (FSD) everywhere — e.g., 81.3% vs FSD's 76.9% (original 81.4%) on GR00T-N1.5/QuantVLA, and 60.8% vs 60.4% (original 63.5%) for OpenVLA-OFT/QVLA — using only ~7.4% of FSD's state-level distillation budget.

## 核心贡献

- **量化泛化鸿沟的系统性实证**：在 4 个「模型 × 量化器」组合（GR00T-N1.5、π0.5 配 W4A8 QuantVLA / W4A4 HoloQ-VLA，OpenVLA-OFT 配 QVLA）上证明：量化后在原始 LIBERO 分布内几乎不掉点（约保留 97%），但在 LIBERO-Plus 七类扰动（相机视角、初始位姿、语言、光照、背景、传感噪声、物体布局）下平均掉 6.0–15.4 个百分点。
- **动作偏差的非均匀性分析**：量化引起的师生动作偏差近似服从对数正态分布、右尾厚重——最大的 5% 偏差状态承载了总偏差的 72.3%（GR00T-N1.5/QuantVLA）与 78.5%（GR00T-N1.5/HoloQ-VLA）；干预实验表明只修正高偏差状态可 +7.4 分，随机干预仅 +1.8，修正低偏差状态反而 −0.2。
- **PIVOT-Q 框架**：学生自控的 on-policy 采样（固定扩散噪声下对比师生动作）→ 用「当前偏差 + 折扣未来偏差」的脆弱度分数做相位平衡的稀疏状态选择（K=4 相位 × M=4 状态，强制时间最小间隔）→ 脆弱度加权（clip 到 [0.5, 2.0]）蒸馏 + 基于原始数据回放库的行为锚定损失（λ=0.1）。
- **效率与一致性**：在全部 4 个设置上超过全状态蒸馏 FSD，且每条轨迹只蒸馏 16 个状态，仅为 FSD（每回合 199.4–242.0 个状态）的 6.9%–8.0%（平均 7.4%）。

## Key Contributions

- A systematic empirical study revealing a consistent post-quantization generalization gap on LIBERO-Plus across four model–quantizer settings, despite near-perfect in-distribution retention (e.g., 97% preserved on original LIBERO).
- Evidence that quantization-induced action discrepancies are approximately log-normal and that their heavy tail is behaviorally consequential: intervening at high-discrepancy states raises success by +7.4 points (66.4% → 73.8%) while random intervention gains only +1.8 and low-discrepancy intervention loses 0.2.
- PIVOT-Q, a vulnerability-aware on-policy distillation framework combining (i) student-controlled rollouts with matched diffusion noise, (ii) phase-balanced sparse state selection from a vulnerability score mixing current and discounted future discrepancy, (iii) clipped vulnerability-weighted distillation, and (iv) a Behavioral Anchor that regularizes the student toward the original full-precision policy on reference states.
- Demonstrated consistency: PIVOT-Q outperforms full-state distillation (FSD) in all four model-quantizer settings while consuming only ~7.4% of the state-level distillation budget (16 states vs ~199–243 per episode).

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** Let π^F be a full-precision VLA and π^Q its post-training-quantized copy. At each visited state, both policies predict an action chunk under the *same* observation, language instruction, and diffusion noise ξ_t; only the student's action is executed. The instantaneous discrepancy is q_t = (1/D_a)·‖a_t^Q − a_t^F‖²₂. Recovery must follow the student-induced state distribution, so rollouts are collected by executing π^Q.

**Motivation study.** Policies trained on LIBERO are evaluated on LIBERO-Plus, which keeps the task objective but applies seven controlled shifts (camera viewpoint, robot initialization, language, lighting, background, sensor noise, object layout) over 560 episodes. Logged discrepancies are fit to log-normal/Gamma/Weibull families; log-normal gives the lowest Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance (pooled KS ≈ 0.016–0.037). Tail statistics show the largest 5% of states contribute 72.3%/78.5% of total discrepancy for the two GR00T-N1.5 cohorts, versus 43.8% for π0.5 and 34.2% for OpenVLA-OFT (Spatial). A direct intervention experiment replaces the quantized action with the teacher action at random / high-discrepancy / low-discrepancy states, showing only high-discrepancy intervention is reliably beneficial.

**PIVOT-Q pipeline.**
1. *Student-controlled on-policy rollouts:* the quantized student drives the environment; teacher and student actions are compared at every visited state under identical noise, giving consistent discrepancy estimates.
2. *Vulnerability-aware state selection:* a discounted accumulated future discrepancy r_t⁺ = Σ_{h=1..H} γ^{h−1} q_{t+h} / Σ γ^{h−1} (horizon H=4, γ=0.9) captures deviations that persist or compound in subsequent steps. Each trajectory is split into K=4 contiguous phases; within-phase percentile ranks of current and future discrepancy are combined as P_t = α·q̃_t + β·r̃_t⁺ (α=β=0.5). Greedy selection enforces a minimum temporal separation and retains M=4 states per phase, yielding a fixed supervision set of |I| = K·M = 16 states per trajectory.
3. *Vulnerability-weighted distillation:* per-state weights w_i = clip(P_i / max_j mean_j P_j, c_min=0.5, c_max=2.0), mean-normalized over the selected set, then a weighted MSE to the teacher actions: L_distill = Σ w_i (1/D_a)‖a_i^Q − a_i^F‖²₂.
4. *Behavioral Anchor:* a replay bank B of up to 256 clean states from the original training data; at each update a subset S (≤4 states) is sampled and the frozen teacher's predictions there serve as anchors: L_anchor = (1/|S|)Σ (1/D_a)‖π^Q(s) − π^F(s)‖²₂, with λ=0.1. Total objective L = L_distill + λ·L_anchor.
5. *Training details:* LoRA rank 16 (action head for GR00T-N1.5 + QuantVLA; QKV projections of the action expert for π0.5 and HoloQ-VLA), five optimizer updates per recovery rollout.

**Baselines.** Original full-precision policy, quantized policy, and Full-State Distillation (FSD), which distills the student on *all* recorded rollout states (210.0, 232.7, 199.4, and 242.0 states/episode across the four settings). The ablation adds equal-budget selection baselines (Per-Phase Random, Per-Phase Uniform, Globally Greedy top-16). All results: 560-episode LIBERO-Plus protocol, mean ± std over three seeds.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main recovery (GR00T-N1.5 + W4A8 QuantVLA, LIBERO-Plus):** quantization collapses average success from 81.4% to 66.6% (−14.8 pts). PIVOT-Q lifts it to 81.3% (Table 3 protocol; Table 2 reports 78.3±1.0 with Δ+11.7), recovering nearly the whole gap, while FSD reaches only 76.9% (78.0 in Table 2, Δ+11.4) with ~13× more distilled states. Under an equal 16-state budget, Per-Phase Random / Uniform / Globally Greedy selection obtain only 78.5±0.5 / 78.4±1.0 / 78.5±0.7 — PIVOT-Q is +2.8 / +1.9 / +2.5 pts over them, isolating vulnerability-oriented selection (not sparsity) as the source of the gain.
- **Weaker quantizers still benefit (OpenVLA-OFT + QVLA):** quantized 52.7% → PIVOT-Q 60.8% (+8.1 pts), exceeding FSD's 60.4% and approaching the 63.5% full-precision reference; gains concentrate in initialization and object-layout shifts. For GR00T-N1.5 + W4A4 HoloQ-VLA: 82.0% → 68.2% quantized, PIVOT-Q 78.3% vs FSD 78.0%.
- **Where the gap is small, recovery is marginal (π0.5 + W4A8 QuantVLA):** original 78.9%, quantized 71.9%, FSD 72.7%, PIVOT-Q 72.8% — only +0.9 over quantized and +0.1 over FSD; a 6.1-pt deficit to the original remains.
- **Intervention study (Table 1, GR00T-N1.5/QuantVLA):** replacing quantized actions with teacher actions at high-discrepancy states yields 73.8% mean success (+7.4 over the 66.4% quantized policy; sensor-noise shift jumps from 61.2% to 87.5%, +26.3), versus 68.2% (+1.8) for random states and 66.2% (−0.2) for low-discrepancy states — direct causal evidence that the heavy tail is the consequential part.
- **Ablations (Table 3):** removing the discounted future-discrepancy term drops PIVOT-Q from 81.3±0.4% to 79.6±1.2% (−1.7); removing the Behavioral Anchor drops it to 78.1% (−2.5 pts, with visibly larger cross-seed variance), supporting its role against policy drift. Appendix analyses show current-vs-future discrepancy Spearman correlations of only 0.401–0.632 across phases, i.e., the future term contributes complementary, not redundant, signal.

## Critical Assessment

- **Two protocols, unexplained ~3-point gap.** For the flagship setting (GR00T-N1.5 + QuantVLA), Table 2 reports PIVOT-Q 78.3±1.0 (original 82.0, FSD 78.0), while Section 6.1's prose, the conclusion, and Table 3 report PIVOT-Q 81.3±0.4 (original 81.4, FSD 76.9) for what is described as the same 16-state method. The main text never explains the discrepancy between the two experimental protocols — a reader cannot tell which number corresponds to the shipped configuration, and the abstract-adjacent claim "recovering nearly the entire quantization gap" rests on the more favorable one.
- **Marginal wins are dressed as consistency.** "Outperforms FSD in all four settings" is technically true but the margins are +0.3 (GR00T/QuantVLA, Table 2), +0.1 (π0.5), +0.3 (HoloQ), and +0.4 (OpenVLA) — all within or barely beyond one standard deviation (±0.2–1.0). The genuinely convincing evidence is the equal-budget ablation (Table 3), not the FSD comparison.
- **Simulation-only, one benchmark suite.** Every experiment runs on LIBERO/LIBERO-Plus in simulation; there are no real-robot results, no compositional or out-of-suite shifts, and no wall-clock/latency measurements showing the quantized policy's deployment benefit that motivates the whole exercise.
- **Oracle-flavored motivation.** The intervention study (Table 1) replaces student actions with *teacher* actions at selected states — an upper bound unavailable at deployment — so it motivates, but does not validate, the learnable version; the learned PIVOT-Q recovers +14.7 of the gap where the oracle intervention gains +7.4 at higher-discrepancy states, making the mapping between the two analyses loose.
- **Recovery is inherently bounded by a full-precision teacher** that must be rolled out on every state of every student trajectory, so the method targets accuracy recovery, not distillation compute; the "7.4% budget" counts distilled states, not teacher inference cost.
- Minor: hyperparameters (H=4, γ=0.9, K=4, M=4, λ=0.1, clip [0.5, 2.0]) are fixed with no sensitivity sweep in the main text; the disclosed use of generative AI across ideation, execution, and writing is transparently stated, with authors claiming full verification.

## Strengths

- A clean, decision-relevant empirical finding: in-distribution retention after quantization is a misleading signal of deployment quality, quantified across four model-quantizer pairs and seven shift axes.
- The causal intervention experiment (Table 1) elevates the work above correlational analysis — high- vs low-discrepancy interventions give +7.4 vs −0.2, a crisp dissociation.
- The equal-budget ablation (random/uniform/greedy selection at the same 16 states) correctly attributes the gain to *which* states are supervised, not to sparsity itself.
- Consistent evaluation hygiene: fixed 560-episode protocol, three seeds with std, full hyperparameters and LoRA configuration in Appendix A, plus promised code release.
- Practical framing: 16 distilled states per trajectory is a realistic compute envelope for post-deployment repair of quantized VLAs.

## Limitations

- No real-robot or cross-embodiment evidence; generalization claims are confined to LIBERO-Plus's seven pre-defined shift axes.
- Recovery is incomplete in every setting (final results sit 2.7–6.1 pts below the full-precision references), and gains shrink to +0.9 pts when the quantization gap itself is small (π0.5).
- The FSD comparison is budget-asymmetric in Table 2 (16 vs ~199–243 states), and the one setting where FSD wins a category (camera viewpoint) is not dissected.
- The vulnerability score requires a frozen full-precision teacher at both selection and training time; teacher-free or self-distillation variants are unexplored.
- No sensitivity analysis for the horizon H, discount γ, phase count K, states-per-phase M, or anchor weight λ; the log-normal discrepancy fitting is descriptive (no predictive model of which states will be vulnerable before rollout).

## Relevance

对"量化后 VLA/大模型在分布外扰动下退化"这一问题给出了少见的系统性实证，并展示了"挑状态蒸馏"比"全量蒸馏"更省且更好，与高效推理、模型量化、机器人策略鲁棒性方向高度相关；其折扣未来偏差 + 相位平衡选择的思路也可迁移到其他 on-policy 蒸馏场景。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does PIVOT-Q transfer to real robots or other simulation suites (e.g., SimplerEnv, RoboCasa), and does the log-normal tail structure persist outside LIBERO?
- Can the vulnerability score be estimated without a full-precision teacher — e.g., from quantization sensitivity (Hessian/activation statistics) or the student's own ensembles — enabling teacher-free recovery?
- How does targeted post-quantization recovery interact with quantization-aware training (QAT) methods like QVLA: is PIVOT-Q complementary, or does QAT already suppress the vulnerable tail?
- What explains the ~3-point difference between Table 2 and Table 3 protocols for the identical GR00T-N1.5/QuantVLA setting, and which configuration ships with the released code?
- Is the heavy-tail concentration (top 5% of states carrying 72–79% of discrepancy) predictive of recoverability? π0.5 (43.8%) and OpenVLA (34.2%) show weaker concentration and markedly smaller FSD-relative gains — is tail concentration a usable a-priori diagnostic?
