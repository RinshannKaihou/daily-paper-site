Hardware RDMA alone beats TCP loses to TCP — a software send window, not the NIC, delivers the −74% win.

Deploying RDMA for Distributed Inference and Training at Scale · ByteExpress (ByteDance + Fudan)

Yibo Huang, Yi Cui, Yukai Huang, Huaping Zhou, et al. — senior: Yibo Zhu, Chuanxiong Guo, Jie Wu ·2026-10-05 RDMA ML-Infrastructure Distributed-Training Inference-Serving Datacenter-Networking

300 PS + 124 rankers| 25 Gbps ConnectX-5| 2+ yrs in production| tens of data centers (Toutiao / TikTok / Xigua)

−74.0%

P99 parameter-push latency on the production training cluster (124 PS + 346 trainers): 1.03 s under Linux TCP → 268 ms with RDMA + software send window.

Rare production-grade evidence of what deploying RDMA at scale actually takes — its layered-control and zero-copy lessons transfer to any GPU cluster or serving stack.

生产一线实战经验：RDMA 上生产到底难在哪、收益有多大。

The headline win rests on one statically tuned knob — without it, hardware-only RDMA was worse than TCP (P99 push 1.12 s vs 1.03 s), and no adaptive policy is offered.

Methodology

方法与系统构件
Archon RPC — apps keep socket-like channels
↓
ByteExpress engines — one QP per engine, many connections multiplexed; receiver credits k = d/2 · per-engine send window Ws
↓
ibverbs on RC — SEND metadata, then one-sided WRITE fragments; final WRITE_WITH_IMM doubles as completion flag
↓
RNIC — 25–200 Gbps; hardware DCQCN + PFC stay enabled
  • Zero-copy as an interface problem: bemalloc wraps rpmalloc (~3,000 lines C), registers 4 MB blocks, and passes received buffers to app threads by ownership transfer — registration amortized; footprint stabilizes near 2.2 GB per inference node.
  • Scaling = distinct resources at distinct layers: QP multiplexing avoids QP-context cache misses; credits protect posted receive capacity; the send window bounds outstanding bytes against incast bursts DCQCN reacts to too slowly.
  • Availability lives above transport: keepalive sequence numbers, RNIC port affinity, cross-mini-pod replicas, quarantine (30 conn / 50 req failures in 5 min), and TCP fallback that restores throughput within ~1 min.

  • In synthetic worker/PS incast, the send window raises the server count before PFC stalls appear from 63 (no software control) to 1,218 at a 4 KB window — an out-of-band-data limit delays lossless-fabric collapse.
  • Transport choice grounded in measurement: RC reaches 191.36 Gbps vs UD’s 114.70 at 200 Gbps / 4 KB MTU; 2 KB fragments saturate 25 Gbps with one QP.

Strengths

長处

✓ Worth reading for

  • Production-scale evidence, not a testbed: tens of data centers, 2+ years serving Toutiao/TikTok/Xigua; inference measured at the daily peak on 300 PS + 124 rankers.
  • Intellectually honest: publishes the unflattering hardware-only result (RDMA losing to TCP, Table 7) and marks the limits of its own measurements.
  • Transferable lessons: zero-copy is an interface/memory-management problem; receive capacity, outstanding data, and link-level flow control are separate controls at separate layers.
  • Solid component engineering: bemalloc matches rpmalloc while adding transparent registration; thread domains fix a measured provider lock-contention collapse beyond 64 QPs.

Limitations

局限

⚠ Read with care

  • PS Fetch/Push scope only: no all-reduce / collective patterns dominant in LLM/GPU training — lessons may not transfer directly there.
  • Single baseline: only kernel TCP in production; no tuned user-space stack or eRPC comparison, so some of the headline gap may be recoverable by TCP tuning.
  • Static, manually tuned window: no adaptive policy; stability and fairness of the combined controls “not established across arbitrary workloads.”
  • Not reproducible: internal ByteDance jobs on proprietary topologies; no artifacts released; availability characterized qualitatively (no recovery-time distributions, “no PFC pauses” holds only for the reported interval).

Questions for Further Exploration

待探讨问题
  1. Could an adaptive per-engine send window (reacting to PFC/RTT signals) remove per-workload tuning while preserving the −74.0% training win?
  2. How much of the 35.7% / 43.7% inference gap would a carefully tuned user-space TCP stack (or eRPC-style transport) recover?
  3. Do the layered-control lessons (credits vs send window vs DCQCN/PFC) hold for all-reduce on GPU clusters, or is PS fan-in/out structure essential?
  4. Can recovery be characterized systematically — tail distributions of time-to-recovery by failure mode — to turn the architecture into an SLO-backed guarantee?
  5. How does bemalloc’s ~50% higher peak memory trade off at extreme scale, and could on-the-fly MR registration APIs eliminate the allocator workaround?
