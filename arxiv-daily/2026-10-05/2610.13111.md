> **一句话总结：** 该文在 Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct 上用 2×3×2 全因子设计（查询类型 × 注意力来源 × 语料）审计"视觉头"发现分数，发现注意力来源与查询类型（而非语料）几乎完全决定找到哪些头——换注意力来源后 top-100 头仅保留中位数 49 个（同数据随机对半分却能共享 96–99 个）；query-span 头集的必要性 NCS（0.116–0.126，比 last-token 高 0.083，p=6×10⁻¹⁰）超过 250 个层匹配随机头集 17.5–19.2 个标准差（Holm 校正后 p=0.048），但在默认强度 β=10⁴ 下没有任何发现头集的可操控性（CERS）优于层匹配随机集（rank p ≥ 0.14）。

> **TL;DR:** This ICLR 2027 paper dissects what attention-based "visual head" discovery scores actually find in VLMs. Crossing query type × attention source × corpus (12 settings) on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct over comics (Gaze Heads' corpus) and COCO strips, then scoring every head-set with two causal tests — CERS (contrastive steering on 2×2 grids) and NCS (matched-vs-mismatched masking contrast on a 4-way ImageNet MCQ) — against 20–250 layer-matched random head-sets, it shows: (1) the attention source reshapes the top-100 far more than the corpus (median 49 vs 84 shared heads, noise ceiling 96–99); (2) only referring+query-span head-sets are genuinely necessary, beating all 250 random sets by 17.5–19.2 control SDs on NCS (0.116/0.126 vs control mean ≈ 0.002), while Gaze Heads' own ordinal+last-token setting is null on both corpora; (3) at the default steering strength, no discovered set steers better than layer-matched randoms (rank p ≥ 0.14) — single random draws span CERS −0.34 to +0.84, so prior single-draw validations cannot resolve specificity.

## 核心贡献

- **发现分数的内部选择比数据更重要**：在同一模型上把查询类型、注意力来源（last token / query-span / 生成答案 token）和语料（漫画条 vs COCO 照片）做全 2×3×2 交叉，得到 12 个发现分数；top-100 头的重叠显示——同数据随机对半 96–99（噪声天花板 1.00），换语料保留中位 84（78–91），换查询类型 55（35–79），换注意力来源仅 49（36–79）。已发表的 Gaze Heads 与 visual retrieval heads 两族头集在 top-20 上仅共享 2/20，近乎不相交。
- **必要性（NCS）跟随注意力来源**：4 个 query-span 头集的必要性全部为正且优于 ≥20 个随机集；所有 last-token 头集均无效，包括 Gaze Heads 自己的 ord-last 设置（两个语料上均为零）。query-span 比 last-token 高 0.083（p=6×10⁻¹⁰），referring+query-span 两组头集超过 250 个层匹配随机头集 17.5/19.2 个标准差（NCS 0.116/0.126 vs 对照均值 ±0.007，Holm 校正 p=0.048）；通用损伤头集（attention sink、text-heavy、整层、200/400 随机大头集）NCS 仅 −0.001 至 0.025，证明对照臂设计有效。
- **默认强度下的可操控性不特异**：没有任何发现头集的 CERS 优于其层匹配随机集（rank p ≥ 0.14）；单次随机抽取的 CERS 范围 −0.34 至 +0.84（层匹配）、−0.36 至 +0.77（任意层），同一设置的 20 个随机集分数跨度可达 1.1——此前用单次随机对照的验证（Gaze Heads 的 LLM judge、visual retrieval heads 的单次 20 头对照）在统计上无法区分特异性。
- **泄漏审计与报告协议**：审计 8 个地面对照基准发现多处泄漏（SpatialRGPT oracle 仅 0.28、CVBench/Spatial457 mismatched 臂 0.611/0.537、Spatial457 中 53% 的题目答案 "1" 与 "False" 等价）；提出 4 条报告建议（层+图像注意力双重匹配的多随机对照、通用损伤对照、泄漏审计、头集规模扫描）。

## Key Contributions

- A full 2×3×2 (query type × attention source × corpus) factorization of the discovery score, evaluated identically for all 12 head-sets on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct with deterministic forced-choice readouts (no generation, no LLM judge) and per-level levels borrowed verbatim from the two published methods (Gaze Heads, visual retrieval heads).
- Two causal metrics applied to every head-set: CERS (steer toward the correct grid cell minus steer toward a distractor cell) and NCS (probability drop on a matched ImageNet MCQ image minus the drop on a mismatched image, so generic masking damage cancels), each scored against many layer-matched (and image-attention-matched) random head-set controls with rank statistics and Holm correction.
- Three findings: discovery choices — not the corpus — decide which heads are found (source overlap 49/100 vs split-half ceiling 96–99); necessity follows the attention source (referring+query-span sets clear all 250 layer-matched controls by 17–19 SDs, last-token sets including Gaze Heads' own are null); steerability at the default strength is not specific to discovered heads (no set beats its layer-matched randoms, rank p ≥ 0.14, while single random draws span −0.34 to +0.84).
- A reporting protocol: (i) compare every head-set against many layer- and attention-matched random draws and report where it falls among them; (ii) check necessity against head-sets built to cause generic damage; (iii) audit converted benchmarks with oracle, floor, blank-image and duplicate-option checks; (iv) sweep head-set size.

## Methodology

- **Discovery score factorization.** The attention-based discovery score averages each head's attention to a target region T over "source" token positions. Three bundled choices are crossed: query type (ORDinal "What is happening in the N-th panel from the left?" vs REFerring "Find the truck…"), attention source Q_S (LAST = last prompt token, QSPAN = query-span tokens, OUT = generated answer tokens — the native choices of Gaze Heads and visual retrieval heads respectively), and corpus (1×6 comic strips from Gandikota & Bau 2026 vs COCO photos with object boxes), giving 12 settings (e.g., cm-ref-qsp). N=400 strips per corpus; head-sets are flat top-100 (plus top-20, top-3-per-layer and size sweeps k ∈ {20,…,160}).
- **Random controls.** Per setting, R=20 layer-matched random head-sets with identical per-layer head counts (Control A), plus attention-matched controls additionally binned by each head's image-attention mass quintile (Control B); for the two referring+query-span sets R=250. Rank p = (b+1)/(R+1) (floor 0.048 at R=20, 0.004 at R=250), Holm-corrected across the 12 settings. A noise ceiling is estimated by split-half discovery (200+200 strips).
- **CERS (steerability).** Each test item is a 2×2 grid of images with four letter options. Steering adds +β (β=10⁴, matching the released Gaze Heads code) to the head-set's attention logits toward the image tokens of one region. CERS(S) = P(c | do(S, p(c))) − P(c | do(S, p(d))): steer into the cell holding the correct answer minus steer into a distractor cell, read as forced-choice probability over the four letter tokens in a single forward pass.
- **NCS (necessity).** Masking adds −β to S's attention toward all image tokens. On a 4-way ImageNet MCQ, NCS = matched drop − mismatched drop, where the matched image shows the target class and the mismatched image shows a class excluded from the options; generic masking effects cancel in the contrast. Stress arms include attention-sink, text-heavy, whole-layer and 200/400-head random sets, and a text-solvable variant of the control question. A separate leak-audited 2×2 ImageNet grid (120 items, oracle 0.98, chance 0.23) repeats NCS as a region contrast with 100 layer-matched controls.
- **Out-of-domain and cross-model.** Eight grounding benchmarks (MathVista, CountBenchQA, CVBench, MMSStar, SpatialRGPT, Spatial457, VAW, ImageNet) converted to the same 2×2 CERS / paired-NCS format after a baseline leak audit; a 14-task pointing suite (7 converted + 7 composite, 3 converted tasks flagged for leak shortcuts and reported separately); five further models (Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma-4-E4B, Ovis1.5-Llama3-8B, InternVL3.5-4B 4-bit) with discovery redone per model.

## Results & Highlights

- **Discovery choices dominate the corpus (Table 1/20).** On Qwen3-VL-8B, top-100 head overlap between two random halves of the same discovery data is 96–99 of 100 (noise ceiling 1.00), but changing only the attention source keeps a median 49 (95% range 36–79), the query type 55 (35–79), and the corpus 84 (78–91) — i.e., corpus differences are ~56% of the ceiling while source/query differences are ~50–56%, far below the 1.00 ceiling. The published Gaze Heads (ORD-LAST) and visual-retrieval (REF-QSPAN) native sets share only 2/20 top heads; at top-100 the ord-last/ref-qspan overlap is 25–86/100 across seven models.
- **Necessity follows the attention source (Tables 3, 4, 10).** All four query-span head-sets score NCS above zero on both corpora and beat ≥20 random sets; no last-token set does, and Gaze Heads' own ORD-LAST setting is null on both corpora (CERS −0.003). Query-span sets exceed last-token sets by 0.083 NCS (p = 6×10⁻¹⁰; ordinal +0.045, p = 0.003; referring +0.121, p = 1×10⁻¹⁰). Against R=250 layer-matched controls (mean ≈ 0.002 ± 0.007), cm-ref-qsp reaches NCS 0.116 and co-ref-qsp 0.126 — 17.5 and 19.2 control SDs, Holm-adjusted p = 0.048. Generic-damage head-sets score −0.001 to 0.025 (vs 0.125 discovered) and the 100 most image-heavy heads score −0.002 (p = 0.71), so the effect is not generic masking damage; masking only the question/option rows preserves the full effect (0.140 flat / 0.049 top-3-per-layer, vs 0.126 masking everywhere; last row only −0.001).
- **Steerability is not specific at the default strength (Tables 3, 9).** No discovered head-set steers better than its layer-matched random sets (rank p ≥ 0.14–1.0 across all 12 settings); individual random draws range CERS −0.34 to +0.84 (layer-matched) and −0.36 to +0.77 (any layer), and the 20 layer-matched sets of a single setting span up to 1.1 — wider than the single-draw controls used in prior validations (100 heads vs 20–35 and 20 draws). Image-attention mass does not explain the outcomes (pooled Spearman |ρ| ≤ 0.09, R² = 0.03): the most necessary set (co-ref-qsp) steers at 0.148 vs 0.147, and a strength pilot shows the two referring+query-span sets DO separate at weaker β=3 (CERS 0.689 and 0.789 vs random means −0.103 and 0.204), i.e., specificity is strength-dependent.
- **Out-of-domain and cross-model replication (Tables 7, 8, 23, 26).** On eight grounding benchmarks sharing no images or prompts with discovery, cm-ref-qsp scores CERS 0.288 (random draw −0.117) and NCS 0.225 (random 0.020); across five further models and three architectures, all 25 query-span/output-token sets exceed their random draws on NCS (0.07–0.40 vs 0.00–0.10) while last-token sets stay ≤ 0.024 (except Qwen3.5-9B at 0.223); steering is model-dependent (near zero on Gemma-4-E4B, Ovis1.5, Qwen3.5-9B). The referring+query-span NCS effect replicates on Qwen3-VL-4B (0.054, above all 20 controls) and Qwen2-VL-7B (0.029, above all 20 controls), i.e., ~40% and ~23% of the 8B effect. On the 14-task pointing suite, ORD-LAST steers 0.139 vs REF-QSPAN 0.081 (11 unflagged tasks), but a layer-matched random set reaches 73% of ORD-LAST's 0.102 (paired difference 0.037 [0.025, 0.049], p = 3.2×10⁻¹⁰, N = 880).
- **Benchmark leak audit (Table 22).** Oracle checks expect ~1 but SpatialRGPT gives 0.28 and CVBench/Spatial457 0.63/0.57; the NCS mismatched arm should sit near 0.25 but reads 0.738 on MathVista, 0.611 on CVBench, 0.537 on Spatial457, 0.467 on VAW; Spatial457 contains ambiguous answers ("1" vs "False") for 53% of items. After calibration fixes, four of five pass — demonstrating that converted benchmarks need oracle/floor/blank-image/duplicate-option audits before any head-set comparison.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline "steerability is not specific" is strength-dependent, and the authors say so — but the framing still leads with the null.** Their own pilot (Table 18) shows the two referring+query-span sets steer well above all 5 random sets at β=3 (0.689/0.789 vs −0.103/0.204 means), while at the inherited default β=10⁴ everything is inside the random range. The claim that prior work's single-draw validations are uninformative is solid; the implied stronger claim that discovered heads do not steer at all is not what the paper establishes.
- **"Gaze Heads' own setting is null" is a statement about one score under this paper's readout, not about the original method end-to-end.** Gaze Heads validated by steering + generation + an LLM judge on comic-panel tracking; this paper replaces that with forced-choice letter probabilities on 2×2 grids. The replication reproduces their discovery score and boost/suppress form (β=10⁴), but not their task or metric, so readers should not conclude Gaze Heads' published 83.1% steered-panel rate is refuted — only that its discovery score carries no necessity signal under NCS.
- **Absolute effect sizes are small and sit on near-ceiling baselines.** The matched MCQ baseline is P(correct) = 0.995, and the discovered sets' NCS of ~0.12 means masking removes roughly 12 points of forced-choice probability from a 99.5% baseline on a 4-way ImageNet question; the mismatched-arm distribution is bimodal (62% of examples below 0.05), so a meaningful share of items contributes nothing to the contrast. The necessity claim would be stronger with an accuracy-level task where the model actually errs.
- **Asymmetric statistical depth across models.** Qwen3-VL-8B gets 20–250 random draws per head-set, but every out-of-domain and cross-model comparison uses a single layer-matched random draw — precisely the practice the paper criticizes in prior work. The cross-model CERS claims ("near zero on Gemma-4, Ovis1.5, Qwen3.5-9B") therefore rest on the paper's own weakest evidence class, acknowledged in the limitations.
- **Reproducibility is promised, not yet delivered**: code, scores and corrected benchmark conversions ship "with the camera-ready", so the leak-audited benchmark conversions — arguably a community asset — cannot be checked today. No activation-patching or resample-ablation cross-check of the masking-based necessity measure is included (acknowledged as future work), leaving NCS tied to one intervention family.

## Strengths

- The cleanest available treatment of a real methodological problem: prior visual-head papers validated with one intervention and one random draw; this paper shows quantitatively why that cannot work (random draws span CERS −0.34 to +0.84 while discovered sets sit inside that band).
- The two-test design (CERS + NCS) applied identically to all 12 head-sets cleanly separates two properties that prior work conflated: a lever the model does not rely on vs a route the model needs.
- Control design is exemplary: layer-matched AND image-attention-matched random sets, generic-damage stress sets (sinks, text-heavy, whole layers, 200/400-head randoms) that all score ≤ 0.025 NCS, split-half noise ceilings, Holm correction, and per-layer selection robustness (top-3-per-layer keeps NCS 0.146/0.056).
- The question/option-row localization (masking only question+option rows preserves the full 0.140 effect; last row −0.001) is a genuinely mechanistic insight about where necessity acts.
- The leak audit of converted grounding benchmarks (oracle 0.28 on SpatialRGPT, mismatched arms up to 0.738, 53% ambiguous answers) is a service to the whole steering-evaluation subfield and is honestly reported with fixes.
- Honest limitations section and a per-result protocol table; every level of the 12-setting design is borrowed from the two published methods rather than invented, making the audit fair to the audited papers.

## Limitations

- Main-model depth is confined to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct; Qwen3-VL-4B and Qwen2-VL-7B get partial replication (the referring+qspan NCS effect shrinks to ~40% and ~23% of the 8B size), and five further models use single random draws for out-of-domain comparisons.
- Steering is evaluated at a single inherited strength (β=10⁴); the weak-strength pilot suggesting real specificity uses only 5 random sets (rank-p floor 1/6), so it cannot itself establish specificity.
- Necessity is measured only as attention-knockout (masking) on forced-choice probability; no mean/resample ablation, no activation patching, and no generation-based tasks — necessity in free-form answering is untested.
- The image-attention mass used for attention-matched controls is measured on a generic image prompt, and random sets span a narrower range of it than discovered sets (3–6× mass gap), so the attention-matching control is only approximate.
- All experiments are forced-choice readouts on grid-based conversions of real benchmarks; the three flagged converted tasks and the calibration failures show how sensitive conclusions are to conversion choices, which limits how far the out-of-domain numbers generalize.

## Relevance

对于关注 VLM 内部机制与可解释性评测的研究者，这篇论文是"注意力头发现 + 因果验证"这条线的必读方法学审计：它直接质疑了 Gaze Heads、visual retrieval heads 等 2026 年热门工作的验证协议，并给出了一套可直接复用的随机对照与泄漏审计清单。任何打算用 steering/masking 解释或抑制 VLM 幻觉的工作都应先按其四条报告协议自检。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- At which steering strength does specificity emerge, and is there a principled criterion for choosing β rather than inheriting β=10⁴ from the released Gaze Heads code (the pilot shows separation at β=3)?
- Do the referring+query-span heads remain necessary when the model must generate free-form answers or when activation patching (rather than attention masking) is the intervention?
- Why does the necessity effect shrink to ~23–40% on Qwen2-VL-7B/Qwen3-VL-4B, and is the last-token anomaly on Qwen3.5-9B (NCS 0.223) a reasoning-trace artifact or a genuine architectural difference?
- Can the necessity of question/option-row attention be tied to a concrete circuit (e.g., cross-attention into the answer letter pathway), and do the null corpus-specific heads have any function at all?
- Will the released leak-audited conversions of the eight grounding benchmarks become a standard evaluation suite, and do conclusions change on tasks where the model's baseline accuracy is far from ceiling (e.g., hard MathVista items rather than near-oracle grids)?
