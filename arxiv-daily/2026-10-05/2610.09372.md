> **一句话总结：** Zyphra 实测发现 MoE 预训练路由存在稳定的"专家耦合"结构——top-2 下 42% 的 token 集中选自仅 0.8% 的专家对（独立路由下应为 1.6%），且第 ℓ 层的专家选择能以中位数 48% 的份额预测第 ℓ+1 层；据此提出两个纯通信优化——把常被共同选中的专家放上同一 GPU 的"相关专家放置"（配合去重 dispatcher，每 token-目的地只发一行，最多去除 58% 的 dispatch 行）与在 attention 的 reduce-scatter 内部把 token 预先搬到其下一层专家所在 GPU 的"token 洗牌"——在 8×MI300X 节点集群、12 层 128 专家的 13.6B MoE（FineWeb-Edu，2.1B token）上把 all-to-all 时间降低 1.16–2.63×、端到端步时最高提升 1.41×，全程不改动路由决策、训练损失与参数显存。

> **TL;DR:** Zyphra quantifies a stable "expert coupling" structure in MoE pretraining and turns it into communication savings. On a 12-layer, 128-expert, 13.6B-param MoE (0.31B/0.71B active at top-2/top-6) trained 2.1B tokens on FineWeb-Edu, 42% of top-2 tokens select one of just 0.8% of the 8,128 possible expert pairs (vs 1.6% under independent routing with matched loads), and layer-ℓ experts predict layer-ℓ+1 experts with a median 48% share. Two Megatron-LM modifications exploit this with zero change to routing, loss, or memory: (1) correlated expert placement — a Kernighan-Lin-style partition of the per-layer co-selection graph, plus a deduplicating dispatcher that sends one row per (token, destination rank) instead of one per selected expert; (2) token shuffling for TP=EP layouts — each token is moved, inside attention's fused reduce-scatter, to the GPU predicted (from layer-(ℓ−1) routes) to host its next-layer experts. Against Megatron-LM's contiguous-placement baseline on an 8×AMD MI300X cluster (xGMI intra-node, 100 Gb/s RoCE inter-node), correlated placement+dedup cuts all-to-all time by 1.16–1.83× (top-2) and 1.35–1.95× (top-6) across EP8–EP64, and token shuffling extends this to 1.40–1.74× / 1.46–2.63×, with end-to-end step-time speedups up to 1.41×.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统刻画 MoE 预训练中的专家耦合结构**：同层内，top-2 训练约 2.1B token 后，8,128 个可能专家对中仅 0.8% 被高频共同选择，却承载 42% 的 token（16 个置信度 ≥50% 的对承载 16%；128 个专家中有 73 个存在一个 ≥30% 出现率的"固定搭档"）；跨层上，112/128 个第 8 层专家把 ≥30% 的 token 送往某个特定第 9 层专家，中位份额 48%。该结构约 1B token 即形成且全程稳定，并在三种负载均衡路由器下均存在。
- **相关专家放置 + 去重 dispatcher**：按层统计共选图 W[e,e′]，用 Kernighan-Lin 式局部搜索做等分划分（每 GPU 恰好 E/EP 个专家，仅用交换保证平衡，不增显存；多节点时先跨节点再节点内分层划分），把高频共选专家放上同一 GPU；配合每 (token, 目的地 rank) 只发一行的去重 dispatcher。放置只是全局专家 ID 的逐层置换，checkpoint 完全兼容。
- **Token 洗牌（TP=EP 布局）**：利用跨层相关性，在 attention 输出投影的 reduce-scatter 中以融合置换把每个 token 搬到预测的下一层归属 GPU，planner 跑在侧流上、隐藏在 attention 之后（每步仅 ~1 ms 等待，占 0.02%），预测表只需一个 microbatch 的路由记录、每 ~1000 步重拟合一次。
- **多布局、多路由器的实证评测**：EP8–EP64（TP1）、TP8 EP8、TP16 EP16 共 6 种布局 × top-2/top-6，外加三种负载均衡方法（主配方 / 10× 辅助损失 / DeepSeek-V3 无辅助损失）的消融，证实方法收益与路由器无关、只取决于路由器产生的共选结构。

## Key Contributions

- **Characterization of expert coupling in pretraining from scratch**: after ~2.1B tokens of top-2 training, 42% of tokens select within 0.8% of expert pairs (chance: 1.6% with matched loads); cross-layer prediction hits a median 48% share (112/128 experts send ≥30% of their tokens to one specific next-layer expert). The structure forms within ~1B tokens, stays stable across training, and appears under all three tested load balancers — it is a property of the trained router, not of particular tokens.
- **Correlated expert placement + deduplicating dispatcher (no architectural change)**: a per-layer permutation of global expert IDs computed offline via best-improving pairwise swaps (each GPU keeps exactly E/EP experts; hierarchical node-then-GPU partition for multi-node EP), targeting total co-selection weight W[e,e′]. The deduplicating dispatcher sends one row per (token, destination rank) pair, carrying expert IDs and gate weights as metadata — this alone removes up to 58% of dispatched rows under correlated placement.
- **Token shuffling for TP=EP layouts**: a side-stream planner predicts each token's next owner rank from first/second-order layer-(ℓ−1) tables with back-off, assigns owners most-confident-first under a per-rank T/P cap, and the fused permute rides inside the existing reduce-scatter (main stream waits ~1 ms/step, 0.02%); the shuffle-aware all-gather restores canonical order with no extra collective. Tables are refit every ~1000 steps from a single microbatch of recorded routes.
- **Multi-layout, multi-router empirical validation**: 6 layouts (EP8–EP64, TP8 EP8, TP16 EP16) × top-2/top-6 on AMD MI300X nodes, plus a router ablation (main recipe, 10× stronger aux loss, DeepSeek-V3 aux-loss-free), showing the benefit tracks the co-selection structure each router produces rather than any particular balancing method.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Two 12-layer all-MoE models (d_model=4096, 128 experts/layer, FFN width 1024, Qwen3 tokenizer): top-2 (13.6B total / 0.31B active) and top-6 (0.71B active), sequence-level aux loss 1e-2, trained 2000 steps = 2.1B tokens on FineWeb-Edu (seq 8192, global batch 128 sequences ≈ 1.05M tokens/step) in BF16 with FP32 router, on clusters of nodes with 8× AMD Instinct MI300X each (xGMI intra-node, 8× 100 Gb/s RoCE NICs inter-node). Baseline is the Megatron-LM top-k all-to-all dispatcher with contiguous expert placement (expert e on GPU ⌊e/(E/EP)⌋). Measurement protocol: load the step-2000 checkpoint, run on held-out data, two runs of 14 steps, report the median of the last 10 (4 warm-up discarded); expert-placement variants are evaluated via load-step (weights frozen, routes recorded on held-out data).

**Characterization (offline).** Routing tables are fit on routes recorded at a few late steps (524k tokens; fit on iterations 1991–1999, evaluated on held-out 1983–1987). A placement table fit on only 1,024 tokens removes 23.6%/57.9% of dispatched rows (top-2/top-6, EP8); 4,096 tokens give Pearson correlation 0.93/0.98 between fitted and held-out co-selection matrices — one microbatch suffices. Tables fit at step 199 of 2000 already deliver ~half the final benefit (14.4% vs 26.5% rows removed at top-2 EP8), and step-1000 tables come within 2–3 points of final values.

**Correlated placement (Section V-A).** For each MoE layer, build W[e,e′] = number of tokens selecting both experts; find a balanced partition of experts into E/EP-sized groups maximizing intra-group W via Kernighan-Lin-style local search starting from contiguous groups, accepting only positive-gain swaps (balance preserved by construction). For EP groups spanning multiple nodes, partition hierarchically — nodes first (targeting inter-node traffic), then GPUs within each node. Multi-node placement plus the deduplicating dispatcher: assignments sharing the same (token, destination rank) are merged into one row carrying metadata; receiving ranks expand rows to expert-major order; combine works symmetrically.

**Token shuffling (Section V-B, TP=EP only).** The planner predicts, per token, the distribution of its next-layer expert slots over ranks by mixing first- and second-order tables (fraction of position-m expert slots per rank conditioned on the two preceding MoE layers), with a back-off toward uniform when trace tokens are scarce. Owners are assigned greedily, most-confident token first, under a per-rank cap of T/P (sequence parallelism requires each rank to own exactly T/P tokens). The fused permute-plus-residual kernel lives inside attention's reduce-scatter; a shuffle-aware all-gather restores canonical order. The planner runs on a side stream concurrently with expert computation and the next attention block — main-stream cost is ~1 ms/step (0.02%) plus two fused permutations costing a few ms, against hundreds of ms of removed all-to-all. Refitting every ~1000 steps is expected to suffice, with each refit needing one microbatch of recorded routes.

**Router ablation (Appendix B).** Two additional top-6 models differing only in load balancing: a 10× stronger sequence aux loss (1e-1; max/mean load ratio 1.20, most balanced) and the DeepSeek-V3 aux-loss-free sigmoid router with per-expert bias (load ratio 1.37; lowest peak load 4.39 vs 4.46/4.64). All configurations are re-run end-to-end on all three.

## Results & Highlights

1. **All-to-all dominates MoE steps under contiguous placement.** On the MI300X cluster with the Megatron-LM dispatcher, A2A (dispatch+combine, fwd+bwd) consumes 13% of step time at EP8 top-2, 45% at EP32 top-2, and 60.4% at EP32 top-6 (24% at EP8 top-6) — moving from one node (EP8) to four nodes (EP32) raises the A2A share from 13.5% to 44.8% under top-2.
2. **Coupling is far above chance.** At top-2 after 2.1B tokens, 42% of tokens select one of 0.8% of possible expert pairs vs 1.6% expected under independent routing with identical loads (~26× concentration); cross-layer, 112/128 layer-8 experts send ≥30% of tokens to a single layer-9 expert (median 48%).
3. **Correlated placement + dedup cuts communication.** Realized token-expert locality: top-2 tokens whose experts share a GPU rise from 12% (contiguous) to 53% (correlated, EP8), and rows removable by dedup rise from 6% to 26% at top-2 and from 25% to 58% at top-6 (even at EP32 where a top-6 token's experts never all fit on one GPU, removals rise 5.5% → 35.1%). A2A volume falls 1.36× (top-2 EP8) and 2.36× (top-6 EP8) vs baseline; A2A time falls 1.34×/1.38×/1.34× (top-2) and 1.95×/1.48×/1.35× (top-6) at EP16/EP32/EP64, and 1.16×/1.58× within a node (EP8, over xGMI). End-to-end step speedups: 1.05×/1.13×/1.14×/1.11× at EP8/16/32/64 for top-2 and 1.41×/1.25×/1.23× at EP16/32/64 for top-6 — largest when A2A crosses node boundaries.
4. **Token shuffling adds the most on multi-node TP=EP.** At TP16 EP16, shuffling raises the A2A reduction from 1.55× (placement alone) to 1.74× at top-2 and from 1.95× to 2.63× at top-6; realized locality jumps to 59%/53% (top-2, TP8/TP16). End-to-end: 1.34× at TP16 EP16 top-6 (vs 1.28× with placement alone) — but only 1.06× at top-2, and on a single node at top-6 shuffling (1.48×) lands slightly below placement alone, since concentrating rows on one owner loads the busiest xGMI link even as total bytes drop.
5. **Cheap to operate.** Tables fit at step 199/2000 already give half the final benefit (14.4% vs 26.5% rows removed); 4,096 recorded tokens suffice (Pearson 0.93/0.98 vs held-out), i.e., a single microbatch per refit; planner overhead ~1 ms/step (0.02%).
6. **Router-agnostic.** Dedup under correlated placement removes 58%/51%/47% of dispatched rows at EP8 for the main / 10×-aux / DeepSeek-V3 aux-loss-free routers (contiguous placement: only ~25% at EP8, 13% at EP16 for every router), translating into step speedups of 1.41×/1.30×/1.26× at TP1 EP16 and ≥1.20× on every EP16 layout.

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors are at **Zyphra**, an independent LLM lab that pretrains large MoE models in production, which both motivates and grounds the work — the A2A bottleneck numbers (up to 60% of step time) come from real training runs, not simulations. Per the author-contribution appendix, **Radha Gulhane** (corresponding) implemented both methods in Megatron-LM, ran all experiments, and led the initial ideation; **Quentin Anthony** contributed to ideation, guidance, and writing; **Beren Millidge** contributed technical discussion and writing. The implementation sits on Megatron-LM, the de-facto standard open pretraining stack, and every reported speedup is backed by per-step measurement traces (Appendix C: 10 steps per configuration, medians, warm-up discarded) with honest negative results disclosed (e.g., single-node top-6 shuffling underperforming placement alone). The measured, systems-first methodology and the candor about where the methods do not help lend high credibility.

## Critical Assessment

- **Moderate conceptual novelty.** The deduplicating dispatcher is explicitly credited to prior work (SmartMoE, Zhou et al. 2025; DeepEP), and affinity-based expert grouping already exists for inference (Occult, GRACE-MoE, SmartMoE place co-selected or related experts together). The genuinely new pieces are (a) showing the co-selection structure exists *in pretraining from scratch*, forms within ~1B tokens, and is stable, and (b) the cross-layer token shuffle fused into an existing collective. The paper is careful to differentiate itself, but the headline result is more a rigorous measurement + engineering contribution than a new algorithmic idea.
- **Scale is the biggest caveat, and the authors say so.** Everything is measured on one 12-layer, 13.6B-total-parameter model trained only 2.1B tokens. The conclusion "we believe the correlation holds across model families and scales" is stated as belief, with zero evidence at larger scale or longer horizons — production MoE routers with sigmoid/aux-free gating or hundreds of experts could behave differently (though the DeepSeek-V3-router ablation at this small scale is reassuring).
- **Absolute end-to-end wins are thin where A2A is cheap.** At EP8 top-2 (13% A2A share) the step speedup is only 1.05×; at TP16 EP16 top-2 it is 1.01–1.06×. The headline 1.41× requires either top-6 routing or a multi-node EP group — precisely the configurations where A2A is 24–60% of the step. Readers running top-2 within a single 8-GPU node gain almost nothing.
- **Baseline and competitive comparisons are narrow.** The only baseline is stock Megatron-LM with contiguous placement; there is no head-to-head against optimized A2A libraries (e.g., DeepEP kernels) or communication-compute overlap scheduling, which are the rival systems approaches to the same cost. Expert replication/relocation methods (MoonEP, load-driven relocation) are argued to be complementary but never compared.
- **Shuffling's traffic-concentration side effect is real.** On xGMI the collective time is set by the busiest GPU pair, so concentrating a token's rows on one owner can negate the byte savings — visible at top-6 single-node (1.48× with placement alone vs slightly less with shuffling). This limits shuffling's value on high-bandwidth intra-node fabrics.
- **Operation cadence is validated only on a short run.** The ~1000-step refit interval and the stability claim are derived from a 2000-step run; drift over 100k+-step pretrains is untested. Measurements are 10-step medians (per-step traces in Appendix C mitigate this).

## Strengths

- **Zero-risk optimization**: neither method touches routing decisions, the training loss, model quality, or memory (placement is a per-layer permutation of global expert IDs; checkpoints load unchanged) — the same model and loss across all variants.
- **Quantified, held-out characterization**: within-layer (42% vs 1.6%) and cross-layer (median 48%) coupling measured against matched-load chance baselines, with train/held-out table validation and a small-sample sufficiency analysis (1,024–4,096 tokens; Pearson 0.93–0.98).
- **Extremely cheap to operate**: one microbatch of recorded routes per refit (~every 1000 steps), planner hidden behind attention on a side stream at ~1 ms/step (0.02% of the step).
- **Broad, honest evaluation**: 6 parallelism layouts × top-2/top-6, three load-balancing routers (≥1.20× on every EP16 layout), per-step measurement traces published, and explicit disclosure of configurations where the methods add little (EP64 top-2 volume: only 1.12×; single-node top-6 shuffling).
- **Practical scope split**: dedup applies to any expert-parallel layout; shuffling applies to any TP=EP layout with sequence parallelism — together covering most modern MoE pretraining configurations.

## Limitations

- **Token shuffling requires TP = EP** with sequence parallelism; it does not apply to layouts without tensor parallelism or with tensor-parallel experts, and the paper does not evaluate context parallelism or inference (where affinity grouping has already been studied).
- **Small scale, short run**: one 12-layer / 13.6B model, 2.1B tokens, 2000 steps; refit cadence and correlation stability are validated only within this window, and cross-scale/cross-family generalization is asserted, not shown.
- **Gains are gated by the A2A share**: within-node top-2 speedups are marginal (1.05× at EP8; 1.01–1.06× at TP16 EP16 top-2), so the practical payoff is concentrated in top-k-heavy or multi-node EP regimes.
- **Single hardware platform**: AMD MI300X with xGMI intra-node and 100 Gb/s RoCE inter-node; NVLink/NVSwitch or InfiniBand trade-offs (especially shuffling's busiest-link effect) are untested.
- **Addresses volume, not skew**: correlated placement reduces bytes but not load imbalance (main model max/mean load ratio 1.52 vs 1.20 for the stronger-aux router), and an all-to-all still waits for the most heavily loaded rank; relocating/replicating hot experts remains a separate, complementary problem.

## Relevance

该论文正中"大规模 ML 计算基础设施"兴趣点：它系统测量了 MoE 预训练中 all-to-all 集体通信的开销（EP32 top-6 下占步时 60.4%），并把实测到的路由耦合结构转化为不改变训练语义的通信削减方案，对关心分布式训练互连效率与 MoE 路由行为稳定性的读者有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does expert coupling persist at production scale — 100B+ active parameters, hundreds of experts, trillions of tokens, deeper stacks — and under sigmoid/aux-free routers at that scale, or does the 42%/48% concentration degrade?
2. Can the ~1000-step refit cadence be validated or made adaptive over 100k+-step pretrains, and does the co-selection structure ever shift abruptly (e.g., after loss spikes or data-mixture changes)?
3. How do these methods compose with communication-compute overlap schedulers and optimized A2A libraries (DeepEP), given that shuffling concentrates traffic on the busiest link while overlap hides latency?
4. Could cross-layer expert-prediction tables double as a routing-health signal — e.g., detecting routing collapse, expert under-use, or training anomalies via deviations from the predicted co-selection structure?
5. Do the same tables transfer to inference-time affinity grouping (GRACE-MoE-style), avoiding a separate profiling pass, and how does correlated placement interact with expert replication under tighter memory budgets?
