> **一句话总结：** 针对极低比特(2-bit)量化 LLM 的低秩量化误差补偿(LQEC),作者诊断出现有闭式方法(EoRA/QERA/ProQ/LQ-LoRA)共有的两个简化——对称校准(在对称目标 (W−Ŵ)X̂ 上分配秩预算,浪费了非对称目标 WX−ŴX̂ 集中在低维方向的能量)和只优化损失展开的二阶项(忽略补偿后模型已非驻点所残留的一阶信号,而实测该自然梯度步的范数在每个解码器层都大二阶项一个数量级以上)——并提出全闭式两阶段框架:Stage 1 用完整输出经验 Fisher 加权的非对称目标对齐各层的 rank-64 适配器(截断 SVD 闭式解),Stage 2 基于补偿后模型重新测量统计量,用秩约束自然梯度步(K-FAC + 广义秩约束逼近,按构造保持秩 r)吸收残余一阶项;在 Qwen3-8B + 2-bit QuIP# 上将 WikiText-2 困惑度从最强基线 LQ-LoRA 的 11.62 降到 10.26(QuIP# 本身 12.43,FP16 为 9.73),且只用 8K 校准 token 完成第二步。

> **TL;DR:** This paper upgrades training-free low-rank quantization error compensation (LQEC) for ~2-bit LLMs by removing two simplifications shared by all prior closed-form compensators (EoRA, QERA, ProQ, LQ-LoRA): symmetric calibration (target the asymmetric perturbation WX − ŴX̂ on full-precision activations, weighted by the full output empirical Fisher) and the quadratic-only objective (Stage 2 takes one rank-constrained natural-gradient step against the task loss to absorb the neglected first-order term). Both stages reduce to a single truncated SVD per layer. On Qwen3-8B under 2-bit QuIP#, WikiText-2 perplexity falls to 10.26 vs 11.62 for the best baseline LQ-LoRA and 12.43 for QuIP# alone (FP16: 9.73); on the held-out C4 corpus it closes 51% (Qwen3-8B) and 84% (Qwen3-4B) of the FP16 gap vs 31%/63% for the strongest baselines. Gains persist at 3/4 bits (where it even beats the FP16 perplexity) and transfer to a different quantizer (3-bit RTN on LLaMA-3.1-8B: 62.50% CSQA avg vs 62.29% for QERA).

## 核心贡献

- **分析:** 首次指出所有训练免费(training-free)LQEC 补偿器共有的两个结构性简化,并给出直接测量证据:(1) 对称校准使秩-r 目标本身是高秩的——在操作秩 r=64 下,非对称目标 ΔT_asym = WX − ŴX̂ 比对称目标 (W−Ŵ)X̂ 在前 64 个奇异方向集中显著更多能量(Fig. 1);(2) 只最小化二阶项,而量化位移后模型上的自然梯度步范数 ‖M‖_F 在每一层都比二阶残余 ‖Y_0‖_F 大一个数量级以上(Fig. 2),且无任何重建目标能吸收它。
- **Stage 1(Fisher 加权输出对齐):** 首个使用完整输出侧经验 Fisher H_s 和非对称校准(x̂ 为量化激活、目标为全精度输出)的闭式 LQEC 目标;最优秩-r 解是单个截断 SVD:USV^T = SVD(H_s^{1/2} C H_x^{1/2}),辅以仅作用于被选奇异值的收缩(shrinkage)因子 η,按块从前往后顺序校准。
- **Stage 2(秩约束自然梯度校正):** 相对补偿后模型本身(而非量化模型)重新测量统计量,在 K-FAC Kronecker Fisher 下对适配器增量做一步自然梯度;通过广义秩约束逼近(Friedland & Torokhti 2007)将截断"适配器+步"整体作 SVD(Y_0 − αM),按构造保持秩 r 而无需重投影;步长由 LARS 式信任比 α = min(1, τ‖Y_0‖_F/‖M‖_F) 缩放。
- **结果:** 在 QuIP#(2/3/4 bit)与 3-bit 分组 RTN、Qwen3-8B/4B 与 LLaMA-3.1-8B 上均取得最佳困惑度与七任务零样本平均;Stage 2 仅用 8K 校准 token(Stage 1 与所有基线用 256K),单张 A100 40GB 即可构建。

## Key Contributions

- **Analysis:** Identifies two simplifications shared by all existing training-free LQEC methods — symmetric calibration (which makes the rank-r target inherently high-rank: at r=64 the asymmetric target concentrates substantially more energy in the leading directions, Fig. 1) and quadratic-only objectives (which leave a first-order signal whose natural-gradient norm exceeds the second-order residual by more than an order of magnitude in every decoder layer, Fig. 2) — each verified by direct measurement rather than assertion.
- **Stage 1 (Fisher-weighted output alignment):** The first LQEC objective using the full output-side empirical Fisher H_s with asymmetric calibration (adapter acts on the quantized activation it actually receives, matched to the full-precision output); the optimal rank-r solution is a single truncated SVD of H_s^{1/2} C H_x^{1/2}, with singular-value-only shrinkage η and front-to-back block-wise calibration.
- **Stage 2 (rank-constrained natural-gradient correction):** Re-measures statistics on the *compensated* model and applies one natural-gradient step to the adapter increment under a K-FAC Fisher; formulating it as a generalized rank-constrained approximation (SVD of Y_0 − αM) keeps the final adapter exactly rank-r with no re-projection, scaled by a LARS-style trust ratio α.
- **Results:** Best perplexity and seven-task zero-shot accuracy across bit-widths (2/3/4) and quantizers (QuIP#, groupwise RTN); Stage 2 needs only 16×512 = 8K calibration tokens vs 128×2048 = 262K for Stage 1 and every baseline, and the whole pipeline runs on one A100 40GB.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** LQEC attaches a rank-r adapter AB^T to each frozen quantized weight Ŵ, so the layer output perturbation is Δz = Wx̄ − (Ŵ + AB^T)x̂. A second-order Taylor expansion gives ΔL ≈ g^TΔw + ½Δw^T H̄ Δw; all prior LQEC methods (EoRA, QERA, ProQ, LQ-LoRA, LOER, CLoQ) differ only in which approximations of this expansion they solve, but share (a) symmetric calibration — evaluating everything on the same activation x̂, collapsing the target to (W−Ŵ)X̂ — and (b) dropping the first-order term via the convergence assumption g ≈ 0, which is violated once quantization has displaced the model and compensation partially restores it.

**Stage 1.** Optimize the token-level asymmetric objective min_{A,B} ½E[(e_i − A B^T x̂_i)^T H_s (e_i − A B^T x̂_i)], where e_i = W x_i − Ŵ x̂_i is the full target and H_s = E[γ_0 γ_0^T] (γ_0 = ∂ℓ/∂z_0) is the full output-side empirical Fisher, estimated once on the original quantized model and reused. With C = E[x̂ x̄^T] − H_x̂ W, this is a Fisher-weighted reduced-rank regression whose optimal rank-r solution is one truncated SVD (Theorem 1); factors are recovered with left whitening by H_s^{1/2}, and a shrinkage factor η (0.1) applies only to selected singular values, motivated by their systematic out-of-sample inflation on finite calibration sets. Decoder blocks are solved front-to-back, re-measuring input moments at each block to prevent stale input distributions.

**Stage 2.** Replace the Stage-1 adapter by optimizing the increment ΔW_2 = A_2B_2^T − A_1B_1^T against the task loss on the compensated model, where g ≁ 0. Under a K-FAC Fisher F_W ≈ H_x̂ ⊗ H_s, the quadratic completes the square about the natural-gradient direction −H^{-1}g; the rank-constrained solution is SVD(Y_0 − M) with Y_0 = H_s^{-1/2}A_1B_1^T H_x̂^{1/2} and M = H_s^{-1/2}g H_x̂^{1/2}, again rank-r by construction (Theorem 2). The step is scaled by trust ratio α = min(1, τ‖Y_0‖_F/‖M‖_F); a sweep shows τ = 0 exactly reproduces Stage 1, a stable optimum sits at τ ≈ 0.2–0.3, and τ = 1 (unscaled step) degrades badly (perplexity 83.38). Because Stage 2 changes both inputs and gradients of downstream blocks, statistics g, H_x̂, H_s are re-measured block-by-block with one forward and one backward pass per block, on only 16×512 = 8K tokens.

**Cost.** Rank-64 per linear layer; Qwen3-8B 2-bit construction takes ≈53 min with a precomputed H_s cache (1h03m first run) vs 16–37 min for baselines; adapters are 264–349 MB and peak memory stays within A100 40GB (a 47.5 GiB reusable H_s cache for Qwen3-8B lives off-device).

## Results & Highlights

- **2-bit QuIP#, Qwen3-8B (Table 1):** WikiText-2 perplexity 10.26 vs 11.62 for the best baseline (LQ-LoRA), 12.15/12.20/12.22 for ProQ/EoRA/QERA, and 12.43 for QuIP# alone (FP16: 9.73) — closing ~80% of the FP16 gap vs ~30% for the strongest baseline. On the held-out C4 corpus (calibration uses only WikiText-2): 17.17 vs 17.95 (LQ-LoRA) and 19.19 (QuIP#), i.e., 51% of the FP16 gap recovered vs 31%.
- **2-bit QuIP#, Qwen3-4B (Table 1):** WikiText-2 13.22 vs 16.75 (LQ-LoRA) and 21.11 (QuIP#; FP16 13.64); held-out C4 21.36 vs 23.34 (LQ-LoRA) and 29.21 (QuIP#; FP16 19.84) — 84% of the C4 gap recovered vs 63% for the strongest baseline. Seven-task CSQA average rises from 57.47 (QuIP#) to 60.05 vs 59.66 (LQ-LoRA), still ~6.7 pts below FP16 (66.73).
- **Higher bit-widths beat FP16 perplexity (Table 2, Qwen3-8B):** at 3 bits, WikiText-2 8.54 vs 9.73 FP16 (all baselines ≥ 10.24); at 4 bits, 8.22 vs 9.73; held-out C4 also drops below FP16 (14.56 and 14.15 vs 15.24), and 4-bit CSQA reaches 69.41 vs 69.38 FP16.
- **Quantizer transfer (Table 3, LLaMA-3.1-8B, 3-bit groupwise RTN):** CSQA average 56.26 → 62.50, best among all methods (EoRA 62.26, QERA 62.29, ProQ 62.16, LQ-LoRA 61.45) — but the margin over the closed-form baselines is only +0.21.
- **Ablations isolate each claim (Tables 4–5, Qwen3-8B 2-bit, rank 64):** asymmetric target alone does *not* help (12.26 WT2 vs 12.34 symmetric without output Fisher); the combination "asymmetric + full output Fisher" is what drops perplexity to 10.26 (symmetric + full Fisher: 12.80). Stage order matters: S1-only 12.48, S2-only 12.81, reversed order S2→S1 12.55, vs S1→S2 10.26 — Stage 2 needs Stage 1's aligned initialization and cannot compensate on its own.

## Authors & Credibility

Seobin Song, Geonho Lee, and corresponding author Jungwook Choi are at Hanyang University (Seoul); Janghwan Lee is at Qualcomm AI Research (work done while Song was at Hanyang). The group has a visible track record in exactly this niche — their own RIliQ (Lee et al., AAAI 2025) on rank-insensitive LoRA-based quantization compensation is cited, and Choi's group works on efficient inference hardware/software co-design. The paper is technically deep and careful: it correctly grounds the method in natural gradient (Amari 1988), K-FAC (Martens & Grosse 2015), Fisher-based compression (Sood & Lewis 2020), and generalized rank-constrained matrix approximation (Friedland & Torokhti 2007), with proofs in appendices and unusually honest cost accounting (construction time, adapter sizes, a 47.5 GiB cache, and the failure mode at τ = 1 all reported). The Qualcomm affiliation suggests the work targets realistic on-device deployment.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but scoped.** The diagnosis of the two shared simplifications is the paper's best contribution and is backed by direct measurements (Figs. 1–2) rather than hand-waving. However, the improvement is an increment *on top of* an established closed-form pipeline (EoRA/QERA/ProQ already solve Fisher-weighted reconstruction objectives); the delta is the asymmetric target plus one extra correction stage, not a new paradigm.
- **The headline gap over baselines is setting-dependent.** On the single non-Qwen model tested (LLaMA-3.1-8B, 3-bit RTN), the CSQA margin over QERA/EoRA is +0.21/+0.24 — within plausible run-to-run and eval noise — while the dramatic gains (−1.36 WT2 perplexity over LQ-LoRA) all come from 2-bit QuIP# on Qwen3 models. LLaMA-3.2-1B appears in the setup but its results live in the appendix; the main tables are Qwen3-heavy, and the weakest quantization regime where the method claims most value (2-bit) is exactly where Qwen3-4B/8B are most degraded (QuIP# 4B at 2 bits is catastrophically broken at 21.11 WT2, giving the compensator unusually much room).
- **"Training-free" hides two sensitive hyperparameters.** The closed-form story requires η (singular-value shrinkage, 0.1 for QuIP# but 0.3 in the RTN setting) and τ (trust ratio, ~0.2–0.3 for QuIP#, 0.1 for RTN) tuned per quantizer; the τ sweep in Appendix G shows perplexity climbing steeply outside a narrow window (14.07 at τ = 0.4, 83.38 at τ = 1). The method is closed-form, not parameter-free.
- **Beating FP16 at 3/4 bits (8.54 vs 9.73 WT2) is a double-edged result.** It shows the objective does more than recover quantization loss — it partially adapts the model to the WikiText-2 evaluation distribution (C4 is the held-out control, and to their credit it also improves). Still, presenting perplexity below the FP16 reference as pure "recovery" should be read cautiously.
- **Cost honesty cuts both ways:** 53 min vs 16–37 min for baselines on the same GPU, plus a 47.5 GiB reusable Fisher cache for Qwen3-8B. Fine offline, but the pipeline is materially heavier than the baselines it beats.
- **Missing evaluations:** no generative quality, math, code, or long-context benchmarks; uniform rank-64 budget with no adaptive allocation; the K-FAC Kronecker approximation of the Fisher is never validated against true curvature at these bit-widths; no test above 8B, where first-order signal magnitudes (Fig. 2) might scale differently.

## Strengths

- Rare quality of diagnosis: each claimed source of prior-method error is measured directly (rank-k energy spectra; per-layer natural-gradient vs second-order-residual norms) before being exploited.
- Fully closed-form: two truncated SVDs total per layer, rank-exact by construction, no iterative optimization, sequential block-wise execution within A100 40GB.
- Ablations that genuinely isolate the claims — Fisher vs objective (Table 4), stage order (Table 5), τ sweep, shrinkage diagnostics with an out-of-sample singular-value check — rather than one-offs.
- Honest reporting: construction time and memory included, calibration on WikiText-2 with C4 held out, failure at τ = 1 disclosed, FP16 perplexity framed as non-comparable across model families.
- Consistency across three bit-widths, two quantizers, and three model families (including the weaker Qwen3-4B, where the absolute gains are largest).

## Limitations

- Scale: validated only at 1B–8B; behavior at 13B–70B, where quantization error structure and Fisher estimation differ, is untested.
- Evaluation surface: perplexity plus seven zero-shot common-sense QA tasks only — no generative, reasoning-heavy (math/code), or long-context evidence that 2-bit compensation translates to real use.
- At the flagship 2-bit setting on Qwen3-4B, CSQA (60.05) remains ~6.7 pts below FP16 (66.73) — compensation substantially narrows but does not close the low-bit gap on smaller models.
- Two hyperparameters (η, τ) must be retuned per quantizer setting, and stability degrades sharply outside the trust window (τ = 1 → perplexity 83.38).
- Construction is 1.4–3.3x slower than baselines (53m vs 16–37m for Qwen3-8B) and depends on a 30–47 GiB reusable H_s cache.
- The uniform rank-64 budget ignores that the asymmetric target's energy is layer-dependent; no per-layer adaptive rank allocation is explored.

## Relevance

与"量化 + 低秩补偿"(EoRA/QERA/ProQ/LQ-LoRA)这一极低比特部署主线直接相关,也与你关注的 training-free 后处理压缩路线吻合:其"先 Fisher 加权非对称对齐、再秩约束自然梯度校正"的两阶段闭式框架,以及把一阶项从损失展开里救回来的论证,对任何在量化后模型上做闭式适配器设计的工作都有可迁移的洞见。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the first-order signal's dominance (Fig. 2) persist at 13B–70B scale, or does the relative benefit of Stage 2 shrink as models grow?
- Can Stage 2 iterate the natural-gradient step with re-measurement, and at which iteration does the local quadratic model break down (the τ = 1 failure suggests a hard boundary)?
- How does the asymmetric objective behave under joint weight-activation quantization (e.g., W4A8), where the quantized x̂ itself carries error that currently only enters through C and H_x̂?
- Is there a principled per-layer rule for choosing η and τ (from the singular-value spectrum or ‖M‖_F/‖Y_0‖_F ratio) instead of a global sweep per quantizer?
- Would allocating rank non-uniformly — spending budget where ΔT_asym retains the most high-rank energy — outperform the uniform rank-64 setting at the same total adapter size?
