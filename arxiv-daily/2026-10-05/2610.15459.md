# When Messages Go Wrong: Understanding Communication Faults in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems

> **一句话总结：** 首个针对 LLM 多智能体系统（LLM-MAS）通信故障的大规模实证研究：作者构建跨层故障分类法与故障注入/追踪流水线，在 100 个任务 × 5 个领域 × 3 种 AutoGen 拓扑 × 3 种模型配置上注入 15,882 个应用层故障，发现 89.4% 的故障会传播为 MAS 级后果、80.2% 导致任务失败，且 20.7% 的失败是"静默失败"（无任何即时错误信号，部分内容缺失型故障静默率高达 76.2%）；Flat 拓扑凭借冗余证据通路把故障下任务成功率从 3.2–3.3% 提到 53.0%，而换模型只能挽回 0.2–4.5% 的基线失败。

> **TL;DR:** This is the first systematic empirical study of communication faults (message loss, partial delivery, semantic corruption, stale replay, timeouts) in LLM-based multi-agent systems. The authors build a cross-layer fault taxonomy and an injection/tracing pipeline, run 18,900+ executions over 100 tasks from WebArena (Shopping/Admin/Reddit), SWE-bench, and TheAgentCompany on three AutoGen topologies (Sequential, Flat, Hierarchical) with DeepSeek V4 Flash, Qwen3.8-27B, and Qwen3.5-9B. Key numbers: 89.4% of 15,882 applied faults propagate to a MAS-level consequence, 80.2% end in task failure, and 20.7% of detection-complete failures are silent (76.2% for partial-content faults vs 0% for I4 non-delivery). Topology redundancy dominates model choice: Flat retains 53.0% success under faults vs 3.3%/3.2% for Sequential/Hierarchical (+66.5 pp at evidence-handoff non-delivery), while swapping the LLM recovers only 0.2–4.5% of baseline failures vs 19.3–27.0% for switching to Flat. Pairing execution-side resubmission with evidence-contract recovery covers complementary failure sets (67.9% + 32.1% of 106 failures).

## 核心贡献

- **跨层通信故障分类法：** 从 31 份通信标准/协议规范与既有研究中抽取 57 条故障-效应描述，经双编码者独立标注（Cohen's κ 0.78–0.86）+ 第三人仲裁，收敛为 29 个类别；将底层故障源（物理/数据链路/网络/传输层 P·D·N·T）、应用层可见故障（A，如时序、可用性、语义完整性、契约有效性）、下游 MAS 后果（M，系统级/语义级）与任务结局（O）区分开——故障的"起源"与"接收方所见"被显式解耦。
- **故障注入与追踪流水线：** 在 AutoGen 工作流的 4 个通信边界（I2 请求、I3 观测、I4 证据交接；I1 为初始任务分发故排除）部署拦截器，以身份操作（identity operation）作为对照组，逐条验证故障是否真正到达目标边界（未到达的调度故障被剔除，不计入分析），并沿 trace 追踪传播链路。
- **大规模实证刻画（4 个研究问题）：** 覆盖 18,900 次执行（其中 15,882 次成功注入），量化故障的显现/传播、阶段与任务上下文敏感性、拓扑与模型的容错差异、以及保护机制的有效性与恢复边界。
- **可操作的保护设计结论：** 执行侧保护（重提交+去重）与证据保护（契约校验+按需重采集）修复互补的故障集；恢复仅在"未受故障影响的证据通路仍存在"时有效；守卫式重采集可消除干净执行中 13.3–19.4% 的多余通信而不损失任务成功率。
- **开放工件：** 代码、实验结果、分析配置与脚本开源于 GitHub（SonGoku21/mas_communication），并配有交互式配套网站。

## Key Contributions

- A **cross-layer communication-fault taxonomy** for LLM-MAS that separates fault origin (lower-stack P/D/N/T sources) from receiver-visible application faults (A) and downstream MAS consequences (M), consolidated from 31 communication standards and prior studies into 29 categories with inter-coder reliability of Cohen's κ 0.78–0.86.
- A **fault-injection and tracing pipeline** that perturbs concrete communication boundaries (I2 request, I3 observation, I4 evidence handoff), verifies each scheduled intervention by comparing original vs. delivered carriers, and excludes the 318/16,200 scheduled faults that never reach the target boundary — preventing phantom exposures from contaminating the analysis.
- A **large-scale empirical characterization** (18,900 executions; 15,882 applied faults; 100 tasks across WebArena Shopping/Admin/Reddit, SWE-bench, TheAgentCompany; 3 AutoGen topologies × 3 model configurations × 3 repetitions) yielding seven boxed findings on manifestation, silent failures, stage/context sensitivity, and recovery boundaries.
- **Actionable protection guidance** from a 1,098-execution study: execution-side and evidence protection recover disjoint failure sets (67.9% vs 32.1% of 106 baseline failures), recovery fails when the same fault reaches every available evidence path, and guarded reacquisition cuts two workflow HTTP requests per clean execution (13.3–19.4% communication reduction) with no task-success change.
- An **open artifact** (scripts, results, trace adjudication definitions, figure-reproduction code) with a companion website for interactive exploration.

## Methodology

**Taxonomy construction.** The authors extract 57 fault/effect descriptions from 31 sources (RFCs, protocol specs, dependability literature, agent-fault studies), consolidate to 43 candidate concepts, and have two researchers (≥5 years communication-systems / ≥2 years LLM-agent experience) independently code source→manifestation→consequence triples on a 10-candidate pilot; disagreements go to a third researcher. Final yield: 29 categories spanning four layers — P (bit corruption, link interruption, …), D (frame loss, duplication, …), N (packet loss, routing failure, …), T (connection reset, deadline expiry, retry anomaly, …), A (timing, availability, semantic integrity, parseability/completeness, multiplicity/idempotency, ordering/freshness, contract validity), and M (system-level vs semantic-level consequences). Errors originating purely from local model reasoning or activations are explicitly out of scope.

**Formal model.** An LLM-MAS is a tuple Σ = (G, B, T, S, C, R, E) over a communication graph, deployed agents, tools, workflow states, receiver contracts, message-handling policies, and task environment; a logical carrier m_i = (id, src, dst, task, session, round, payload, schema, version, time).

**Injection pipeline (two phases).** *Phase 1 — fault generation:* per task/topology/model/repetition, the pipeline selects a fault operator, parameters, and one of four boundaries (I2 action requests, I3 observations, I4 evidence handoffs; I1 initial dispatch is excluded since disrupting it prevents the collaborative workflow from starting). An interceptor transforms the carrier between sender-side production and receiver-side consumption (φ_{f,θ}(m_i, S_i) → m'_i, S'_i): delivery interception implements omission/delay/duplication/queue-replay/ordering/timeout faults; message-assembly faults alter semantic integrity; structured key/type processing implements contract-validity faults. Local model reasoning and tool semantics are untouched; clean runs traverse the same instrumentation with an identity operator. *Phase 2 — propagation evaluation:* each intervention is verified against original vs delivered carrier traces; per applied fault the pipeline separately labels application exposure (A), downstream MAS consequence (M — assigned only when the trace shows a state/behavior change tied to the affected dependency, with semantic consequences requiring the altered information to be accepted or used), independent task outcome (O), and an auxiliary handling-response flag B (explicitly not treated as detection). Clean-conditional loss L_clean = (# applied-fault fails where the matched clean run succeeds) / (# applied faults with a successful matched clean) isolates fault-induced degradation.

**Experimental setup.** 100 tasks from five domains (WebArena Shopping / Admin / Reddit, SWE-bench, TheAgentCompany) with task-specific success endpoints — for evidence-based workflows the endpoint additionally requires that the final result is supported by the expected delivered evidence rather than merely matching the native benchmark answer. Three AutoGen topologies: Sequential (Planner→Worker→Verifier→Decision), Flat (adds a direct Worker-evidence→Decision branch), Hierarchical (Supervisor delegates and verifies). Deployed models: DeepSeek V4 Flash, Qwen3.8-27B, Qwen3.5-9B. The primary confirmation matrix uses one clean + five I4 faults (valid partial delivery, semantic corruption, stale replay, deadline timeout, non-delivery) plus I2 non-delivery; 18,900 total executions, of which 15,882 scheduled faulted runs reach the boundary and receive the fault. Statistics: 10,000 task-clustered bootstrap 95% CIs, two-sided sign-flip permutation tests with Holm adjustment, paired effect sizes on matched execution cells.

## Results & Highlights

- **Propagation is the norm, and much of it is silent.** Of 15,882 applied faults, 89.4% propagate to a recorded MAS consequence and 80.2% end in task failure. Among 8,335 failures with complete detection records, 20.7% (1,722) are silent — no rejection or error signal — and silent rate is fault-type-dependent: 76.2% for partial-content faults vs 26.3% for semantic corruption, 2.3% for I2 non-delivery, and 0% for I4 non-delivery and deadline faults; partial-content faults alone account for 69.6% of all silent failures. Across canonical categories, task failure rates range from 73.4% (message timeout) to 85.5% (partial content).
- **Topology redundancy beats model choice by an order of magnitude.** Under applied faults, Flat retains 53.0% task success versus 3.3% (Sequential) and 3.2% (Hierarchical) — a >16× gap; clean-conditional loss is 36.8–61.6% for Flat vs 96.5–97.2% for the other two. The Flat advantage concentrates at the evidence-handoff stage: +66.5/+66.6 pp absolute success under I4 non-delivery and +41.3 pp under I4 corruption, but ≤0.7 pp when the fault occurs before Flat's branch receives the evidence. On 1,408 common faulted units, changing the LLM recovers only 0.2–4.5% of baseline failures (recovered sets overlap with Jaccard 0–0.047) while changing topology to Flat recovers 19.3–27.0%.
- **Fault severity has no fixed ordering — it depends on stage and task context.** In WebArena Admin under Flat, I4 non-delivery retains 94.4–100.0% success across the three models while Sequential/Hierarchical fall to 0.0% at I4 (but 34.7–75.9% at I3, where the iterative tool loop can re-acquire the information: 82/110 matched DeepSeek I3 executions receive a later table carrier and 74 eventually succeed, vs 0/110 at I4). Under Flat, semantic corruption causes 99.6% failure in Shopping but only 3.1% in SWE-bench; partial-content shows the inverse pattern (53.0% in Shopping vs 100.0% in SWE-bench; 33.3% vs 100.0% in TAC; 97.7% vs 66.7% in Reddit).
- **Protection mechanisms cover complementary failure sets.** In the 1,098-execution protection study, execution-side protection (resubmission + duplicate suppression) uniquely recovers 67.9% of 106 baseline failures and evidence protection the remaining 32.1%, with no overlap. Evidence checking raises success from 55.6% to 100% for valid partial content and 50.0% to 100% for identity corruption; on Reddit it lifts success from 44.4% to 77.8% (partial content) and 33.3% to 77.8% (semantic corruption), matching contemporaneous clean success. Recovery has a hard boundary: combined protection recovers all 18 Sequential failures when corruption is limited to the initial handoff, but 0 when the same fault also reaches recovery deliveries (Flat likewise 18→0 when all evidence paths are hit). Guarded (conditional) reacquisition removes two workflow HTTP requests per clean execution — a 19.4% communication reduction under Sequential and 13.3% under Flat — with no task-success change.
- **Redundancy changes the failure mix, not a cure.** Under Flat the detection-complete matched cohort fails only 32.1% of executions (vs 94.6%/94.7% for Sequential/Hierarchical), yet per-execution silent-failure rate barely moves (17.6% → 16.6%), so silent faults make up 51.6% of Flat's residual failures vs 18.6%/18.1% under Sequential/Hierarchical; semantic corruption and partial content contribute 94.8% of the residual silent failures.

## Authors & Credibility

The five authors span Waseda University (Jinhao Zhou, corresponding author), HKUST (Xiaoyu Zhang), Nanyang Technological University (Jongchul Yu), Shandong University (Fahao Chen), and Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Jun Wu). The paper shows strong systems-reliability craftsmanship: the taxonomy coding protocol reports inter-coder reliability (κ 0.78–0.86) with a third-party adjudicator, the pipeline verifies that every scheduled fault actually reached its target boundary (excluding 318 non-applied interventions rather than silently counting them), statistics are task-clustered bootstrap CIs with Holm-corrected permutation tests, and a dedicated Threats to Validity section addresses construct/internal/external/conclusion validity. The team is also conversant with classical dependability literature (FATE, Lineage-driven fault injection, Gremlin, end-to-end arguments, TCP/HTTP RFCs), which lends the cross-layer taxonomy real grounding rather than ad-hoc category invention. The full artifact (scripts, processed results, trace adjudication definitions) is public with a companion website, supporting reproducibility.

## Critical Assessment

This is an unusually careful empirical paper, but several choices deserve scrutiny. First, the external-validity surface is narrow relative to the headline framing: all results come from **three AutoGen workflow topologies** — the "topology effect" (Flat's dominance) is partly an artifact of AutoGen's graph shapes, and the authors themselves restrict conclusions to the evaluated settings. Flat's +66.5 pp gain at evidence handoff is essentially a redundancy story; whether it transfers to fundamentally different MAS frameworks (message-passing or marketplace-style orchestration) is untested. Second, the deployed model set (DeepSeek V4 Flash, Qwen3.8-27B, Qwen3.5-9B) is treated as configuration-level rather than model-size comparisons — reasonable, but it means the "model changes recover little" finding (0.2–4.5%) rests on a convenient within-family (Qwen×2 + one DeepSeek) selection without a capability-matched stronger model like a frontier-tier LLM; a more capable reasoner might self-repair from partial evidence where these models cannot, which would weaken the "topology > model" headline. Third, the fault operators are synthetic and stylized (fixed partial-content removals, deterministic corruption); the paper admits it "does not claim direct experimental reproduction of every underlying mechanism," so the mapping from lower-stack reality to A-layer symptoms (Table 1) is partly asserted rather than measured. Fourth, the silent-failure rate depends on the authors' own adjudication definitions of A/M/B labels — inter-coder reliability is reported for the taxonomy, but per-execution trace adjudication is script-defined, and mislabeling a "silent" run would directly move the 20.7% headline. Finally, while the evidence-contract endpoint (final answer must be supported by expected evidence) is a strength, it also makes partial-content faults almost tautologically damaging in contract-checked domains like SWE-bench (100.0% failure), so the cross-domain severity inversion (Finding 4) partly reflects the evaluation endpoints the authors chose.

## Strengths

- First-mover scope with genuine scale: 18,900+ executions, 15,882 verified applied faults, four research questions, and 1,098-execution protection study — an order of magnitude beyond prior agent-fault work that mostly examined adversarial prompts or single-agent failures.
- Methodological hygiene rarely seen in agent papers: verified injection (318 non-reaching faults excluded), identity-operation clean controls on the same instrumented path, matched paired analyses, task-clustered bootstrap CIs, Holm-adjusted permutation tests, and explicit per-repetition variability reporting.
- The taxonomy work is grounded in 31 communication standards with formal coding protocol and reliability statistics, bridging decades of dependability research to a new LLM-MAS setting rather than reinventing vocabulary.
- The findings are actionable and specific: protection must match the failed dependency (delivery vs information), recovery is bounded by the availability of unaffected evidence paths, and guarded reacquisition gives a measurable overhead win (13.3–19.4% fewer clean-run requests) — directly usable by MAS framework designers.
- Complete open artifact with a companion website, plus a candid Threats-to-Validity section that correctly self-identifies the AutoGen/model-set generalization limits.

## Limitations

- Evaluated only on AutoGen with three fixed topologies and three deployed model configurations; conclusions about topology and model effects may not generalize to other MAS frameworks, communication protocols (e.g., A2A/MCP deployments at scale), or frontier-tier models.
- Only 100 tasks across five domains; cross-domain comparisons are explicitly descriptive because tasks are not paired across datasets.
- Fault operators are idealized (deterministic partial removal, fixed corruption, single-boundary injections); correlated, persistent, or adversarially timed faults and multi-fault interactions are out of scope, and the same-fault-on-all-paths condition — where recovery fails — is only probed in a small repeated-exposure study (18 runs per condition).
- Silent-failure and consequence labels derive from the authors' trace-adjudication scripts; per-execution label noise is not quantified.
- No adaptive or learned recovery policies are evaluated — protection is limited to resubmission, duplicate suppression, and evidence checking/reacquisition; detection of silent semantic corruption still depends on having an explicit evidence contract to check against.
- Real physical/network-layer faults are simulated at application boundaries; hardware- or middleware-dependent mechanisms require conditions beyond the testbed.

## Relevance

这项工作直接切中 LLM 多智能体系统的可靠性短板：它证明"消息仍然可解析但信息已损坏"的静默故障（占失败 20.7%，部分内容型高达 76.2%）是当前 MAS 部署最危险的情形，且拓扑冗余比换模型更有效。对于关注 Agent 系统工程、MCP/A2A 通信协议健壮性或 LLM-MAS 容错设计的读者，这是一份必读的实证基线与保护机制设计指南。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the topology-vs-model conclusions hold for frontier-tier or significantly stronger models that might reason their way around partial evidence without any workflow redundancy?
- How do the findings transfer beyond AutoGen to other orchestration frameworks and to physically distributed deployments over MCP/A2A, where real network faults (not interceptors) dominate?
- Can silent semantic corruption be detected without a hand-written evidence contract — e.g., via cross-agent verification, consistency probing, or learned validity models?
- What happens under correlated or adversarially timed multi-fault injections, where every redundancy path is hit simultaneously (the paper's repeated-exposure study only scales to 18 runs per condition)?
- Can protection be made adaptive — selecting resubmission, rerouting, evidence checking, or fresh reacquisition based on the inferred failed dependency — and at what communication-overhead cost?
