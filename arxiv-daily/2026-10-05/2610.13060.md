> **一句话总结：** 提出 KV-Repurpose——一个基于 vLLM 的开源测试床，实现超越精确前缀匹配的段级 KV-cache 复用：在 Zipfian RAG 负载下，30 GB 缓存的 token 级命中率从前缀缓存的 3.3% 提升到 63.9%，单文档任务质量基本无损（QASPER F1 0.42 vs 重算 0.42），但暴露出多文档推理的严重退化（2WikiMultihopQA F1 0.80→0.50）。

> **TL;DR:** KV-Repurpose is an open-source vLLM-based testbed for segment-wise, non-prefix KV-cache reuse in LLM serving. On GPT-OSS-120B (single H200), under a Zipfian RAG-style workload of 2,500 requests built from LongBench Pro, it raises the token-level cache hit rate to 63.9% at a 30 GB cache versus 3.3% for exact prefix caching (an LMCache-style connector that recomputes on prefix mismatch), and even a 1 TB cache only gets prefix caching to 17.9%. Quality is preserved on single-document tasks (QASPER token-level F1 0.42 = recompute 0.42; GovReport ROUGE-L 0.26 = 0.26) but degrades sharply on multi-document QA (2WikiMultihopQA 0.80→0.50, MuSiQue 0.61→0.40), and TTFT improves over prefix caching for contexts beyond ~16K tokens.

## 核心贡献

- 将段级、非前缀 KV-cache 复用作为研究基线集成进 vLLM（v0.20.0）：按分隔符切分输入逐段调度，命中段跳过 prefill 直接复用；KV 以未旋转形式存储、复用时按当前位置施加 RoPE（轻量位置校正），整个扩展少于 900 行 Python。
- 构建端到端评测系统：代理服务器负责伪检索（pseudo-retrieval）、prompt 构建、KV 容量管理与 LRU 淘汰，配合 Guidellm 负载模拟与 Prometheus/Grafana 运行时监控，覆盖缓存生成、prompt 组装、驱逐与监控全链路。
- 系统刻画了朴素复用保持/损害模型质量的边界条件：单文档 QA 与摘要几乎无损，跨文档多跳推理显著退化；并用 oracle 文档筛选实验部分定位退化来源。
- 发现并量化了一个跨段信息泄漏现象：前缀中出现的姓名有 35.0%（100 个名字中泄漏 35 个）会被隐式编码进后续文档段的 KV cache 并在复用时复现——据此确立"可复用 KV 只从纯文档上下文生成"的设计原则。
- 在超过 1000 亿参数的模型（GPT-OSS-120B）上验证：此前的位置无关缓存（PIC）研究均未在该规模上评测。

## Key Contributions

- Integration of segment-wise, non-prefix KV-cache reuse into vLLM as a research baseline: delimiter-based segment scheduling that skips prefill for cached segments, KV stored unrotated with RoPE applied at reuse positions (a lightweight positional correction), implemented in fewer than 900 lines of Python on top of vLLM 0.20.0.
- An end-to-end evaluation system covering cache generation, prompt construction, capacity management (LRU eviction), workload generation (Guidellm), and runtime monitoring (Prometheus/Grafana), with a proxy server providing pseudo-retrieval to emulate RAG serving in a controlled, reproducible way.
- An empirical characterization of when naive reuse preserves quality: single-document QA and summarization are largely intact, while cross-document multi-hop reasoning degrades substantially; an oracle document-selection diagnostic partially narrows the gap without eliminating it.
- Identification and quantification of cross-segment information leakage: names appearing in a prompt segment preceding the cached context leaked into outputs in 35 of 100 trials (35.0%), motivating the design choice that reusable KV caches are generated from document-only prompts via a dedicated preprocessing request.
- Evaluation at >100B-parameter scale (GPT-OSS-120B on a single H200 with NVMe-backed KV storage), which no prior position-independent-caching study had attempted.

## Methodology

**System architecture.** Two components: (1) an extended vLLM server and (2) a proxy server. The input is split by special delimiter tokens (`reserved_20016`/`20017`) into segments; the server checks cache availability per segment independently — a miss in one segment does not prevent reuse in later segments, unlike LMCache-style chunked prefix caching that stops at the first miss. Cached keys are stored unrotated and RoPE is re-applied according to current positions upon reuse; attention with the preceding prefix is not recomputed. Cache reuse is triggered purely by delimiter structure, so a document cache can be reused under any system prompt, conversation history, or dynamic metadata (e.g., timestamps).

**Proxy server.** Emulates a RAG front end: pseudo-retrieval from a preconstructed question-context database (gold contexts from LongBench Pro, bypassing retrieval quality as a variable), prompt construction of the form `{prefix}{perturbation}<delim>{doc}<delim>{q}`, and capacity management with LRU eviction. To avoid cross-segment leakage, reusable document caches are generated via a dedicated context-only request (`{system}<delim>{doc}<delim>`) as a preprocessing step; user-specific prefixes are excluded from cache generation.

**Evaluation setup.** GPT-OSS-120B served by vLLM on a single NVIDIA H200; KV cache on NVMe SSD. Quality: LongBench Pro (25 tasks) plus seven LongBench datasets (QASPER, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue for QA with token-level F1; QMSum, GovReport, Multi-News for summarization with ROUGE-L). Baselines: full recompute (an LMCache connector that cannot reuse due to prefix mismatch — the quality-preserving reference) and a without-context condition (lower bound). Scores averaged over three runs; system prompts for reuse differ from the 80-token cache-generation prompt (Claude 3 Opus: 496 tokens, Haiku 3.5: 2,016 tokens). Serving: a Zipfian workload of 2,500 requests from LongBench Pro T5.1 (English documents, 8K–128K tokens) where 20% or 50% of requests share a fixed datetime prefix; plus a controlled context-length microbenchmark (fixed context requested 100 times, Poisson arrivals, request rates 0.2–0.4 req/s in the Zipfian experiment).

## Results & Highlights

- Cache hit rate under the Zipfian RAG workload (20% fixed-prefix share): KV-Repurpose reaches **63.9%** token-level hit rate at a 30 GB cache versus **3.3%** for prefix caching (16.2% in the 50% configuration); even at 1 TB, prefix caching attains only 17.9% (47.3% at 50%) — a ~19x hit-rate advantage at equal 30 GB capacity, translating into consistently lower p50/p95 TTFT (Figure 4).
- Single-document quality is preserved: on QASPER (LongBench), KV-Repurpose scores token-level F1 **0.42**, identical to the recompute baseline's **0.42**, and on GovReport summarization ROUGE-L **0.26** equals recompute's **0.26**; on LongBench Pro averaged over 25 tasks it reaches **0.43** vs recompute's **0.45** (closing to 0.45 with the Opus-3 prompt variant).
- Multi-document reasoning degrades badly: on LongBench multi-doc QA, F1 drops from recompute's 0.80 to **0.50** on 2WikiMultihopQA (−0.30), 0.61 to **0.40** on MuSiQue (−0.21), and 0.71 to **0.60** on HotpotQA (−0.11); Multi-News ROUGE-L falls 0.22→0.17. Oracle-assisted document selection (Appendix A.5) only partially recovers (2Wiki 0.78→0.57 vs recompute's 0.78), so the gap persists.
- Context-length microbenchmark (fixed context × 100 requests, Poisson arrivals): KV-Repurpose achieves lower TTFT once context length exceeds roughly **16K tokens**, and sustains higher throughput at high request rates where prefix caching saturates (Figure 5, contexts up to 128K tokens).
- Illustrative Wikipedia extrapolation (Appendix A.4): a cache holding 0.2% of total KV capacity yields a 14.55% hit rate and an estimated **23.74%** prefill saving; 1% capacity yields 24.2% hit rate and **42.94%** saving — while prefix caching is capped at ~20% savings even under generous assumptions (and would need ~2.8 PB to cover all 10-turn reuse).
- Cross-segment leakage probe (Appendix A.2): names placed before the cached segment were reproduced in **35 of 100** trials (35.0%); zero leakage when the cache is generated from the context-only prompt.

## Authors & Credibility

Taiga Ikeda, Daisuke Miyashita, and Jun Deguchi are with Kioxia Corporation, Japan — a NAND flash/storage manufacturer, which explains the focus on NVMe-backed KV-cache storage and tiered memory hierarchies. The authors have related prior work on long-term memory and high-speed storage for AI (cited as Yamanaka et al., 2026, with Ikeda and Deguchi among the authors). The system is released as open source under Apache-2.0 (github.com/taiga333/eval-kv-repurpose), and the paper is unusually candid: it explicitly disclaims being the first to propose KV repurposing, reports its own multi-document failures prominently in Table 1, and labels the Wikipedia extrapolation as illustrative. Industry-lab systems work with a reproducible artifact; credibility is solid for the systems claims, more limited for ML generalization claims (single model).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Modest by design. The paper states plainly that neither repurposing nor RoPE-based positional correction is new — both are "commonly adopted in prior work" (CacheBlend, EPIC, MiniPIC et al.) — and the contribution is a *testbed and baseline*, not a technique. That framing is honest but lowers the technical bar: the reuse mechanism is delimiter-based segment matching plus key re-rotation, with no attention-fusion or selective recompute as in CacheBlend.

**Rigor.** Mixed. On the positive side, quality results are averaged over three runs, the recompute baseline is a sensible quality-preserving reference, and the without-context condition usefully shows the model actually reads the provided context (0.45 vs 0.11 on LongBench Pro). On the negative side: (1) the headline 63.9%-vs-3.3% hit-rate gap is partly definitional — the workload gives exactly 20% of requests a shared fixed prefix, so prefix caching is capped near 20% by construction, and the comparison measures the workload's fit to each mechanism as much as mechanism quality; (2) there is **no direct comparison with the PIC techniques** (CacheBlend, EPIC) that were designed to fix exactly the multi-document quality degradation the paper documents — the "naive baseline exposes limitations" claim would be far stronger if it showed sophisticated corrections still fail or cost too much; (3) the Wikipedia extrapolation rests on stacked assumptions (15 KB of KV cache per byte of content, 20% same-page revisit probability, 10-turn conversations) the authors themselves flag as illustrative.

**Reproducibility.** Good: Apache-2.0 code, <900 lines of Python over vLLM 0.20.0, explicit prompts (80/496/2,016 tokens) and workload recipes (2,500 requests, Poisson arrivals, Zipfian sampling) in the appendix. Single H200 + NVMe is a specific but reproducible hardware point.

**Red flags / significance.** The multi-document collapse (2Wiki 0.80→0.50) is both the paper's most important finding and its unresolved weakness: the oracle diagnostic (A.5) recovers only part of the gap and the authors cannot attribute the remainder to a single factor. Also, GPT-OSS-120B is a single, non-standard architecture; hit-rate and quality conclusions may not transfer to dense models or other serving stacks. Finally, the A.2 leakage experiment is a 100-trial probe with family names, not a systematic privacy audit — important observation, thin evidence.

## Strengths

- Complete, usable open-source testbed: scheduling, proxy, pseudo-retrieval, LRU eviction, workload emulation, and Prometheus/Grafana monitoring in one artifact, small enough (<900 LoC) for researchers to extend.
- Honest, well-designed evaluation that reports where the approach *fails* (multi-doc QA) as prominently as where it wins.
- The cross-segment information-leakage finding (35% name leakage) is a genuinely useful negative result for anyone building shared KV-cache repositories, and it directly motivated a concrete design rule (context-only cache generation).
- >100B-model evaluation with realistic serving metrics (p50/p95 TTFT, throughput under Poisson load, NVMe-tiered cache) — a scale no prior PIC study covered.
- Clean system-level result: TTFT/throughput advantages emerge exactly where expected (contexts >16K tokens, high request rates), consistent with the reuse mechanism.

## Limitations

- Only a simple positional correction is implemented; KV-Repurpose is neither combined with nor directly compared against prior PIC quality-preservation techniques, so the reported multi-document degradation is a property of the *naive* baseline, not of non-prefix reuse in general.
- Experiments use a single LLM (GPT-OSS-120B) on a single GPU; the authors themselves state results may not generalize to other architectures, sizes, or training settings.
- The multi-document quality gap remains unexplained and unresolved; the oracle diagnostic conflates reduced concatenation with removal of irrelevant documents.
- Serving evaluation relies on a synthetic Zipfian workload with an engineered fixed-prefix share; no production traces.
- The Wikipedia capacity extrapolation depends on unverifiable numerical assumptions and bounds prefix caching at 20% by assumption.
- Delimiter-based segmentation requires control over prompt construction — adoption in arbitrary existing serving deployments is not demonstrated.

## Relevance

这篇工作直接命中 LLM 推理服务效率的核心痛点：RAG 场景下检索文档因前缀不匹配而无法命中 KV cache，导致 prefill 浪费与 TTFT 恶化。其开源测试床、段级复用基线，以及"单文档无损、多文档退化、跨段泄漏"三个实证结论，对任何做 KV cache 管理、长上下文推理或 RAG 服务的研发都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can CacheBlend/EPIC-style selective attention recomputation close the multi-document gap (2WMQA 0.50 → 0.80) at acceptable TTFT cost within this testbed?
- Does the quality degradation stem from RoPE misalignment, attention-sink displacement, or normalization statistics — and does it vanish for models without attention sinks?
- How does segment-wise reuse interact with prefix caching in a single system (e.g., reuse the shared prefix exactly, repurpose only the document segment)?
- Would a learned segment-boundary detector (instead of hardcoded delimiters) enable reuse on uncontrolled production prompt formats?
- Is the cross-segment leakage phenomenon measurable at scale, and can it be certified away (e.g., by bounds on cross-attention mass into cached segments) for multi-tenant cache repositories?
