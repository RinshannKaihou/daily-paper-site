Why this matters

Validating analog and optical neural hardware by random device sampling can silently miss failures that breach nuclear-safety tolerance limits — worst-case search is required.

Worst-case-search methodology, transferred to wave-based PNN validation

Critical edge

No device was ever built or measured — every number comes from an idealized, uncalibrated simulator (“representative rather than calibrated” tolerances), and code is available only on request.

from Critical Assessment · the abstract’s framing invites a hardware-level reading the evidence cannot yet support

Methodology

A differentiable optics simulator + adversarial search, run as a pre-registered stress test.

32-D tolerance box→ DE + 30-step signed-gradient PGD→ worst in-spec device→ held-out error vs max of N random devices→ RN
  • Simulator. PyTorch model of a 4f coherent optical processor with a digital tail (HybridFNO2d: 4 layers, 24 channels, Fourier modes −12…12); 8-bit amplitude/phase SLM, 25×25 pupil, 10-bit camera. Primary endpoint DET (noise-free) vs secondary REAL (DAC/ADC + detection noise).
  • Tolerance box. 8 systematic knobs × 4 layers = 32-D (registration, defocus, tilt, SLM crosstalk, extinction ratio, reference phase), each device adding one frozen static-error draw.
  • Models. 4 tasks — Darcy, Navier–Stokes, lid-driven cavity, heat exchanger — × 6 training arms × 5 seeds = 120; a same-size digital FNO is the accuracy reference.
  • Search & stats. 16,256 dev-set evaluations per model; unseen attack families (corners, coordinate ascent, CMA-ES); Clopper–Pearson bounds, order-statistic quantile bounds, Wilks 95/95 limits.

  • Sampling more doesn’t help. Budget-matched uniform (Rmatched 1.21–2.26) and Sobol’ (1.19–2.16) stay far behind; the searched device is still 1.45–2.12× the best of 100,000 uniform devices.
  • Random-error training hides fragility. Those models degrade 3.7–39.9× their nominal error at the searched device; widening the box k=0.25→2 inflates searched error 2.6–8.1×.
  • Wilks 95/95 exceeded in all 39 HX models; 11 box-trained models passed Wilks at a 1%-of-mean threshold — random testing shows no alarm, searched devices fail.
  • Certification fails at full box. Taylor-model enclosure is 6×10³–8.5×10⁸× the true worst error; under the REAL configuration the ratio narrows to 0.67–2.70 (≥1 in 116/120).

Strengths

  • Unusually systematic for a case study: 120 models, 4 attack families (2 unseen during adaptation), budget-matched samplers, a 100,000-device run, a pixel-level device model — the “just sample more / better” escapes are sealed.
  • Pre-registered endpoint and protocol, plus an honest secondary endpoint (REAL) where the gap shrinks and four models flip below 1.
  • Consequences on an engineering quantity, not just an error metric: the nuclear-safety Wilks 95/95 limit is exceeded in every HX model tested.
  • Actionable guidance: random box corners as a cheap first screen (1,024 gradient-free evals); Mixed-PGD FT as the best defense; nominal accuracy is a poor robustness proxy.
  • Careful statistics and self-criticism — the authors flag their own LDC metric artifact and PGD start-point artifact.

Limitations

  • Purely simulated and stylised — scalar optics, idealized lenses, representative (not calibrated) half-widths; transfer to real hardware is explicitly untested.
  • Code and data only “upon reasonable request” — no public repository despite the reproducibility rhetoric.
  • Searched error is only a lower bound: DE and CMA-ES were still improving at budget end; the supremum over ≈4.3×10¹⁵ corners is unknown.
  • Endpoint choices shape the numbers: DET omits converters and noise; under REAL, four LDC models fall below 1 and the LDC metric is pressure-dominated.
  • Statistical caveats: Mixed-PGD FT is not compute-matched against other fine-tunes; per-task sign tests (p=0.0625) lack multiple-comparison correction.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • How large is the gap on a calibrated physical 4f platform with measured tolerances and fabrication data?
  • Would interval bound propagation through the spectral layers beat the Taylor-model enclosure at the full 32-D box?
  • Does Mixed-PGD FT keep its advantage under in-situ training, with hardware in the forward pass?
  • Which real fabrication-error correlation structure shrinks the gap most — can extreme-value extrapolation from the 100,000-device tail ever certify the supremum?
  • How do results change under worst-input metrics or local PDE failure criteria that relative L2 may hide?
