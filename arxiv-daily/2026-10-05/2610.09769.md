> **一句话总结：** 提出 Bolzano——由并行 prover、LLM verifier 和 summarizer 组成、维护人类可读研究状态的多智能体系统，在无题目级人工指导下从四组论文（含 STOC 2026 正式录用论文）中抽取的约 3,800 个开放问题上自主运行，标记 485 个有希望输出、报告解决约 214 个，其中 4 个针对 STOC 2026 论文的结果获原文作者私人通信确认。

> **TL;DR:** Bolzano is a multi-agent LLM system (parallel provers + an LLM verifier + a summarizer over a persistent research state) evaluated autonomously on 3,800 open problems extracted from four paper collections — new arXiv math.CO/cs.DS papers, STOC 2026 accepted papers, earlier FOCS/SODA/STOC papers, and the Midsummer Combinatorial Workshop — using GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol. It flagged 485 outputs as promising and reports 214 solved, a ~27x scale-up over the 8 problems of its earlier expert-guided study; on the strictest benchmark, STOC 2026 (420 problems), only 4 solutions survived author verification. The confirmed STOC results include an O(log n)-memory one-pass planted-biclique detector matching the known lower bound up to log factors, an exponential monotone-rank-vs-span-program separation (2m vs m·2^(2m) rows), a strongly convex quartic with no rational witness, and an Ω(log n) circuit-depth lower bound for biased CAT states.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 Bolzano 开源多智能体研究循环：每轮由多个并行 prover（互不可见对方输出）、一个唯一有权更新共享研究状态（problem / notes / proofs / output 四个文档）的 verifier、以及一个 summarizer 组成，支持专家引导与无人值守两种模式。
- 完成从"专家选题"到"全自动开放问题求解"的规模化实验：用 LLM 流水线从四组论文中抽取约 3,800 个自包含的开放问题（抽取时做文献检索排除已解决问题），自主运行后标记 485 个候选、报告 214 个被解决。
- 报告 4 个经 STOC 2026 论文原作者确认的实质性新结果（种植二团检测算法、单调 rank/span 程序指数分离、无有理见证的凸四次多项式、有偏 CAT 态的电路深度下界），其中两个结果原作者仅审阅了核心思路而非完整证明。
- 给出诚实的验证分级方法论：LLM 初筛 + 重复结果注册表 + 结构化审计（要求声称的问题与原文意图一致且证明实质正确）+ 最终人工/原作者复核，并明确区分"LLM 估计已解决"与"作者验证已解决"。

## Key Contributions

- **Bolzano research loop:** each round runs concurrent prover agents (isolated from each other), an LLM verifier that is the *only* writer of the shared research state (four markdown documents: problem, notes, proofs, output), and a summarizer; schema-validated verifier updates keep the loop resumable and human-auditable.
- **Scale-up experiment:** moving from the initial expert-guided study (8 problems, proofs checked by domain experts, 6 classified as publishable research) to fully automated runs on ~3,800 extracted open problems across four paper collections, with no problem-specific human instructions during solving.
- **Four author-confirmed results on STOC 2026 papers:** (1) a one-pass O(log n)-bit algorithm detecting semi-random planted bicliques with both sides of size ≥ C·n^(2/3), matching the known lower bound up to logarithmic factors; (2) an exponential separation between monotone rank programs and monotone span programs (2m rows vs exactly m·2^(2m) rows for the same function over the OR-of-ANDs); (3) a strongly convex quartic in two variables with rational coefficients whose zero sublevel set is a single irrational point, yielding a feasible convex quartic problem with no rational witness; (4) an Ω(log n) circuit-depth lower bound for joining/splitting biased CAT states, resolving the entropy-balanced case H(α)=2H(β) left open in the source paper.
- **Honest verification ladder:** LLM screening with a persistent duplicate registry, source-checking against the actual question (hypotheses, quantifiers, conventions), a structured second audit, and author confirmation — with solved counts explicitly labeled as LLM-based estimates everywhere except STOC 2026.

## Methodology

**Research loop.** Bolzano is an open-source orchestration layer around LLMs. A standard pipeline runs sequential *research rounds*, each consisting of parallel provers, a verifier, and a summarizer. The entering state contains four explicit documents (problem, notes, proofs, output) plus short records of earlier rounds, so agents build on prior work without re-reading every previous response. Provers receive the task and current documents and are asked to explore proof strategies, construct examples/counterexamples, or prove special cases; they run concurrently and never see each other's current-round responses. The verifier receives all prover responses, assesses arguments, identifies unjustified steps and incompatible ideas, and proposes structured (schema-validated) updates — only the verifier writes to the shared documents. A separate summarizer produces a concise round account for display and later rounds. Notably, the agents use **no tools**: no web search, no code execution, no filesystem access — the design deliberately evaluates pure reasoning over supplied mathematical text.

**Open-problem extraction.** A general-purpose LLM agent harness (with local file and web search access) reads papers, finds explicitly stated open problems/questions/conjectures, checks whether the source paper itself resolves them, performs a literature search to confirm the problem is still open, excludes resolved candidates, and assembles a self-contained task card with necessary definitions, assumptions, and related work.

**Screening and audit.** LLM postprocessing identifies substantive claims (proofs, counterexamples, algorithms, improved bounds) and consults a persistent registry of duplicate packages; promising claims are compared against the actual source question (hypotheses, quantifiers, conventions) and assessed for proof gaps, restricted scope, and overlap with known results. Selected candidates are rewritten as mathematical notes and re-audited by another LLM under two conditions: the claimed problem is supported by the source under its intended reading, and the proof is substantially correct; audits also grade resolution completeness, scope limitations, severity, fixability, and priority.

**Run configurations (Table 1).** arXiv math.CO/cs.DS: 1,600 problems, GPT-5.5, 4 rounds. STOC 2026: 420 problems, GPT-5.5, 4 rounds. Earlier FOCS/SODA/STOC: 900 problems, GPT-5.6 Sol, 2 rounds. Midsummer Combinatorial Workshop: 880 problems, GPT-5.6 Sol, 2 rounds. All runs used a single prover set at maximum reasoning effort. The four STOC 2026 results were sent to source-paper authors and confirmed in private correspondence in June–July 2026.

## Results & Highlights

- **End-to-end yield:** across 3,800 extracted open problems, Bolzano flagged 485 runs as promising and reports 214 solved (~5.6% of all attempts) — versus 8 expert-selected problems solved in the initial guided study, a ~27x increase in throughput without per-problem human guidance.
- **Collection-level breakdown (Table 1):** the arXiv math.CO/cs.DS run (1,600 problems, GPT-5.5, 4 rounds) produced 250 promising / 90 solved (5.6%), while the Midsummer Combinatorial Workshop run reached 80 of 880 solved (9.1%) and the earlier-FOCS/SODA/STOC run 40 of 900 (4.4%) with only 2 rounds of GPT-5.6 Sol.
- **Strictest accounting (STOC 2026):** of 420 problems from accepted papers, 41 outputs were flagged promising, but only 4 solutions survived author verification — all 214 headline "solved" counts outside STOC are LLM-based estimates, not human-checked.
- **Planted bicliques (Garg et al., STOC 2026):** a one-pass algorithm using O(log n) bits of memory detects a semi-random planted biclique when both sides have size ≥ C·n^(2/3), matching the known lower bound up to logarithmic factors.
- **Monotone rank programs (Cavallo et al.):** for the OR-of-ANDs function there is a monotone span program with 2m rows over every field, while every column-full-rank monotone rank program needs exactly m·2^(2m) rows — an exponential separation.
- **Biased CAT states (Parham):** an Ω(log n) circuit-depth lower bound for both joining two biased CAT states and splitting one into two, including the entropy-balanced case H(α)=2H(β) explicitly left open in the source paper.

## Authors & Credibility

Eight authors spanning Charles University (Computer Science Institute, Institute of Formal and Applied Linguistics, Department of Applied Mathematics) and the Institute of Mathematics of the Czech Academy of Sciences. The team deliberately mixes LLM/systems researchers (e.g., Václav Rozhoň, Jan Grebík) with established theoretical computer scientists — Martin Balko (extremal combinatorics), Robert Šámal (combinatorics), Pavel Hubáček (computational complexity), Martin Koutecký — which lends real domain credibility to the expert-checking stage; the group also produced the predecessor case-study report (arXiv:2604.16899) and maintains the system at bolzano.app. The paper is accepted at the 6th MATH-AI workshop (NeurIPS 2026), with funding via GA CR grants including a JUNIOR STAR project.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline "214 solved" is partially self-certified.** Table 1's own footnote states that except for STOC 2026, solved counts are *estimates* informed by LLM-based assessments of whether the output addresses the question and whether the argument is "substantially correct." Only 4 results were verified by source-paper authors — and for two of those (convex quartics, biased CAT states) the authors reviewed only the main idea, not the full proof. The abstract's "solving about 200 open problems" therefore rests on the same class of LLM judgment the system is being evaluated.
- **The key ablation is missing.** The Discussion concedes the experiments "include no comparison with direct use of the underlying models" — there is no control arm of one-shot or simple-prompt GPT-5.5 on the same 3,800 problems, so the central implicit claim that the multi-agent loop (rather than raw model capability) drives the yield is untested. No quantitative comparison to Aletheia, AlphaEvolve, or Feng et al.'s Gemini study is offered either.
- **LLM verifier, no formal checking.** The paper itself warns that an accepted-but-incorrect lemma may propagate through the persistent notes with increasing confidence, and that unflagged runs cannot be declared unsolved — both of which make the promising/solved rates non-rigorous denominators.
- **Confounded cross-collection comparisons.** The four runs differ simultaneously in model (GPT-5.5 vs GPT-5.6 Sol), rounds (2 vs 4), and source corpus, so the wide spread in solve rates (4.4% vs 9.1%) cannot be attributed to any factor; token cost per solved problem is unreported.
- **Extraction risk.** The authors note a correctly-solved but inaccurately-extracted problem statement may not advance the original question — a failure mode their own audit can only partially catch, and their expert audit "does not cover all relevant fields."

## Strengths

- Unusually honest accounting for an LLM-for-mathematics paper: explicit separation of LLM-estimated vs author-verified counts, a failure taxonomy (missing assumptions, proofs addressing weaker versions, invalid arguments, rediscoveries of known results), and a duplicate-output registry.
- Credible, hard target domain: STOC 2026 accepted papers represent the current frontier of theoretical computer science — the four confirmed results are genuine research contributions, not re-solved competition problems.
- The tool-free agent design (no web, no code execution) isolates pure mathematical reasoning, making the qualitative evaluation interpretable.
- Persistent human-readable research state enables post-hoc auditing, expert steering mid-run, and reproducibility artifacts (extracted task, proof record, notes, source paper).
- Real domain experts on the team performing the human-review stage, with author confirmation sought on the flagship claims.

## Limitations

- Only 4 of ~214 claimed solutions are author-verified, two of those only at the idea level; no formal (proof-assistant) verification anywhere in the pipeline.
- No baseline comparison — neither against direct use of GPT-5.5/GPT-5.6 without the orchestration, nor against prior systems (Aletheia, AlphaEvolve) — so the scaffolding's marginal value is unknown.
- Cross-collection configurations (model, rounds, budget) are not held constant, precluding controlled comparisons; compute cost per solved problem is not reported.
- Domain coverage is limited to combinatorics and theoretical CS (math.CO, cs.DS, STOC/FOCS/SODA); generalization to analysis, algebraic geometry, or probability is untested.
- "Solved" is fuzzy by construction: unflagged runs cannot be declared unsolved, and screening can itself mis-process outputs.

## Relevance

该工作展示了多智能体 LLM 系统在真实科研级开放问题（而非竞赛基准）上的端到端流程——问题抽取、并行证明搜索、LLM 审校与分级人工验证——对关注 LLM 推理能力边界与 agent 编排的研究者直接相关，其"哪些结果算被解决"的诚实分级方式也值得其他 AI-for-Science 工作借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What fraction of the ~210 estimated solutions would survive full expert review or formalization in a proof assistant (e.g., Lean)?
- Does the prover-verifier loop beat single-shot prompting of the same model at an equal token budget? The paper leaves this ablation explicitly open.
- How do yield and quality scale with research rounds (2 vs 4) and model capability (GPT-5.5 vs GPT-5.6 Sol) when corpus and budget are held fixed?
- Can the persistent research state feed a proof assistant for automated gap-closing, converting candidate proofs into formally checked ones?
- What is the empirical rate of incorrect-lemma propagation through the verifier-maintained notes, and can round summaries be used to detect it?
