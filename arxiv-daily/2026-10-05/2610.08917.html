Key metric
9.02–10.81×

Certified speedup for OpenVLA-OFT across all four LIBERO suites at 95% confidence (α=5%), preserving 85.8–95.7% of reference-solved episodes.

Budget validity: 0.0% over-budget deployments for CARE vs 75.0% for latency-only selection (α=2.0%, 10,000 resampling trials).

Why this matters

Turns VLA accelerator safety — chunking, pruning, caching, flow steps — into hypothesis testing with finite-sample guarantees, stricter than latency plus average success.

Critical edge ⚠

Under tight budgets the certificate mostly says no — fallback reaches 100% at α≤2.5% (n=250) — so certified acceleration exists only when budgets are loose.

Also: the guarantee is i.i.d.-bound and needs exact scene resets — simulator-only evidence; no code release stated.

Methodology — certify, then select

Setup

An evaluation unit fixes a task, an initial scene, and execution randomness; the reference and an accelerated candidate run on identical units. The paired loss marks failure only when the reference succeeds and the candidate fails — acceleration-induced failure (AIF); R(λ) is the equal-weight task-mixture probability of that failure.

Theorem 2: per-step confidence or action disagreement provably cannot recover episode-level AIF risk — two constructed policies with all-zero single-step effects have AIF risk 1 and 0.

Certify

Each of M candidates is tested against H: R(λ) ≥ α at level δ/M (union bound → family-wise error ≤ δ); fixed-sample tests use distribution-free Hoeffding–Bentkus p-values, valid for [0,1] losses without distributional assumptions.

Select

Candidates are ordered fastest-first on a disjoint profiling set; the first certified candidate deploys, otherwise CARE falls back to the reference: Pr(R(λ̂) ≥ α) ≤ δ.

Two efficiency designs

Task-balanced sequential rounds keep the averaged e-process a non-negative supermartingale (anytime-valid early stopping via Ville’s inequality); failure-triggered, cache-shared reference evaluation runs the expensive reference only where candidates fail — Lemma 1 proves losses and decisions identical to exhaustive pairing.

  • Certification cost: sequential form uses 5,263 rollouts vs 25,000 full-sample Hoeffding–Bentkus over the 24-configuration family (−78.9%); reference rollouts 113 vs 1,000.
  • Deployment speed (α=5%): fixed-n CARE attains the best mean speedup among risk-controlled selectors (8.88×, 11.7% fallback) vs Pareto Testing 6.16×, fixed-sequence LIT 6.35×, safe policy improvement 5.30×; sequential CARE keeps 7.32× with 592 vs 2,250 evaluation rollouts (−73.7%).
  • Generalization: π₀ₓ₅ flow-step reduction certifies 2-step on Spatial/Object (1.45×, 20% of reference compute) and 4-step on Goal (1.26×), 1,276 vs 4,000 rollouts (−68.1%); Crafter collect_wood certifies 3/3 seeds at 69.4% (Qwen3.5-9B) and 74.9% (Llama-3.1-8B) of reference compute.
  • Ablations: local single-decision AIF diverges from episode-level AIF by 1.31–3.52×; uncorrected selection deploys over-budget in 46.0% of trials vs ≤0.15% family-corrected; both efficiency designs cut wall-clock 38.6 → 7.0 hours (−81.0%).
Strengths
  • Reframes acceleration as explicit finite-sample risk control — answers “what does acceleration break?” that average success demonstrably cannot.
  • Strong theory–experiment alignment: observed over-budget rates 0.00–0.02% sit far below nominal δ=5%; every design choice isolated by a targeted ablation.
  • Failure-triggered, cache-shared reference evaluation is an exact-equivalence cost cut (Lemma 1), not an approximation.
  • Model- and mechanism-agnostic: chunking, token pruning, caching, flow steps, and LM agents — needs only terminal outcomes and measured compute.
  • Honest fallback: retains the reference when evidence is insufficient — exactly what risky selectors lack.
Limitations
  • Distribution-bound guarantee: holds for i.i.d. scenes from calibration; silent on deployment shift (new scenes, objects, instructions).
  • Paired evaluation needs exact initial-state reset — simulator-only so far; real-robot certification remains open.
  • Tight budgets trigger heavy fallback (100% at α≤2.5% with n=250; 56.3% with n=500).
  • Equal-weight task mixture can hide per-task regressions; no per-task certificate.
  • Many pre-specified choices (α, δ, round schedule, latency ordering, e-process grid); sensitivity analyzed only on a coarse grid.
  • LM-agent evidence is thin: two Crafter tasks, three seeds, near-zero savings on one task.
Questions for further exploration
  1. How does certification behave under distribution shift between the 500 fixed LIBERO calibration scenes and novel deployments — can the AIF bound be made robust or continuously re-certified online?
  2. Can paired-rollout certification adapt to real robots without exact resets — approximate matching, importance weighting, or runtime e-process monitoring?
  3. How sensitive is certification power to round size m, the e-process grid Q, and noisy latency measurements used for candidate ordering?
  4. Would per-task (rather than equal-weight mixture) certificates change what is certifiable on Long, where preservation is lowest at 85.8%?
  5. Can failure-triggered reference evaluation combine with speculative decoding or batched simulation to cut the remaining ~7-hour wall-clock — and does CARE extend to multi-agent or fine-tuned small-LM controllers?
