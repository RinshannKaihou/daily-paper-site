What Do Sparse Autoencoder Feature Discovery Scores Identify? From Activations to Interventions

Yifeng Guo, Yuaran Li, Xiang Chen St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital · Columbia University sparse-autoencoders mech-interp causal-inference attribution-patching
Key metric 2.8–4.6%

Median intervention attribution as a share of the probe coefficient (|α/β|) across 756 FDFI-selected direction–task pairs; probe coefficients overstate realized steering effects by roughly 130–400×.

Why this matters

Picking SAE features by association, then intervening on them, doesn’t survive statistics — the fix costs one backward pass per prompt.

用相关性/探针分数挑特征后直接做 steering 或 ablation 的常见做法，在统计上站不住。

Critical edge

The headline “91–97% vs 17–35%” sign-match win is partly mechanical — the probe structurally predicts zero when latents never fire at the final position, flattering the gradient predictor by construction.

ρ(Δ) − α(Δ) = sₜ + c(Δ) − e(Δ)

sₜ Stein residual  ·  c(Δ) omitted variable  ·  e(Δ) edit-gradient covariance — an exact identity, estimable per stratum

SetupObservational evidence vs interventional truth

An edit H → H+Δ(H) keeps the SAE error term; its attribution is the first-order effect via attribution patching. The observational benchmark is a linear probe on the latent’s final-position score — the practice under audit.

TheoryTwo results, no approximation

Proposition 1 (boundary): an off-support affine perturbation leaves (H, Y) and every discovery statistic unchanged while shifting the attribution arbitrarily — no observational functional identifies α. A two-prompt counterexample makes it concrete. Theorem 1 (decomposition): the probe–attribution gap splits exactly into the three terms above; Corollary 1 specializes to zero-ablations, α = Mₖ + Cₖ, and explains why a final-position probe sees ρ = 0 when the latent never fires there.

ExperimentsScale of the audit

GPT-2 (block 6, 24,576 latents) · Gemma-2-2B (layer 12, L0 41) · Gemma-2-9B (layer 22, 131,072 latents) · Llama-3.1-8B (layer 16). 11 tasks, 8,828 prompts split 7,063 selection / 1,755 evaluation; many-strata CLT + bootstrap inference; ≤ ~2 A100-hours per model.

  • Not sampling noise. BH tests at q=0.05 reject a zero gap for 227/756 FDFI pairs; all 20,000 noise-simulated medians exceed the observed 0.028–0.046.
  • Steering. Attribution is closer to the realized effect than β for 380/380 coefficient-selected directions; median |T/α| = 1.000.
  • Why probes fail. 28.7–37.0% of pairs show real attribution with ρ≈0 — the latent never fires at the only position the probe reads; the covariance term Cₖ beats the mean term Mₖ for 17–52% of estimates.
  • Selection flips. |α| ranks the largest-realized-response latent first in 28/28 model-task units; attribution-selected sets beat correlation/rank/coefficient sets on held-out ablations in every unit.
  • Worked example (Gemma-2-9B, p(Turkish)=0.349). FDFI’s #1 latent 102779 moves p to 0.333; the #23 latent 99439 moves it to 0.403 — one backward pass anticipates both.

03 Strengths

  • Exact, non-asymptotic theory — the decomposition is an identity, and its three terms map onto concrete SAE failure modes (probe-blindness, pattern–edit mismatch, prompt-varying edits).
  • Rare statistical discipline — selection/evaluation splits, proven many-strata CLT, coverage simulations, Benjamini–Hochberg control.
  • Highly actionable — the fix is one backward pass per prompt, ~2 GPU-hours per model/analysis; the rank-1-vs-rank-23 example makes the stakes vivid.
  • Transparent reporting — every analysis labeled prespecified or post hoc, asterisks for thin legs, frank admissions where intervals include zero.
  • Second-dictionary replication — the gap persists on alternate SAEs (median |α/β| ≤ 9.51×10⁻³), so it is a property of the probe, not one dictionary.

04 Limitations

  • Narrow scope — one SAE at one layer per model, ≤9B models, 11 toy-style tasks with teacher-forced targets; no refusal, sycophancy or naturalistic behaviors.
  • Thin Llama leg — 23 direction-task pairs (flagged *); several key comparisons are post hoc and descriptive, and the FDFI-vs-attribution set contrast is not distinguished under task resampling.
  • Undercoverage — Stein-residual intervals cover only 0.820–0.835 at nominal 95% under sparse JumpReLU simulation.
  • First-order only, white-box only — accuracy degrades at large steps (breakdown at 5× max activation for most pairs); the remedy is unavailable for API-only models.
  • SAE error kept by assumption — nothing about attribution errors of the reconstruction itself or inherently distributed, multi-latent features.

Theory novelty is assembly, not machinery: Stein / omitted-variable bias / Haufe pattern identities, composed exactly.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Does the gap persist for distributed, multi-latent behaviors (refusal, in-context learning) and at 70B+ scale, where strata per task may be too small for many-strata inference?
  • Can attribution be replaced by a query-only or second-order correction for API-only models where gradients are unavailable?
  • How large is the second-order remainder relative to α at realistic steering magnitudes — can second-order corrections integrate with the sₜ + c − e decomposition?
  • Does attribution-selected feature selection improve downstream applications (unlearning, capability removal, circuit edits), and at what compute overhead at scale?
  • Is a multi-position probe ever sufficient? Position averaging removes blindness but still leaves median |α/ρ| of 0.056–0.298 and 16–54% sign disagreement.
