Critical edge

The headline conflates algorithm with budget: FreeEvolve always consumes its full 24 h while baselines stop after 6.9–36 h — and no cost-matched comparison exists.

01 Methodology — how the loop disappears

x* = Evolve(s, E[x₀])  ·  E[x₀] = (g, D, x₀, Rₖ, Cₖ)

  1. a.
    Bounded environment

    The interface fixes what interactions exist — goal, data, editable target, execution function, resource caps — but never prescribes when, or in what order, to use them.

  2. b.
    Campaign autonomy

    Claude Code, equipped only with a markdown evolution skill, is invoked once. It alone chooses tasks, repetitions, concurrency, timeouts, branching, acceptance and stopping as evidence arrives. No external loop remains.

  3. c.
    Meta-evolution

    The same Evolve interface runs one level up: the editable target is the skill itself, and candidate skills are judged only through the complete worker campaigns they organize. Nesting is capped at two levels.

  4. d.
    Protocol

    Pre-registered evolution/held-out splits shared by every method; held-out scored 3× (mean ± s.d.) plus pass@3; all-Claude models; 24 h × 5 Slurm nodes for the outer campaign, 12 h per worker campaign.

03 Strengths

  • +Attacks a real, previously unaddressed layer — the search-loop policy itself — cleanly formalized as an environment–evolver boundary that also yields practical Slurm scaling.
  • +Meta-evolution is elegant and cheap: the policy is just text — frozen, inspectable, portable (Appendix G shows the learned task-selection and regression-control rules).
  • +Above-average evaluation discipline: pre-registered splits shared by all methods, 3-fold repeats with s.d., pass@3, leakage scans, wall-clock and token accounting.
  • +Transparent negative results — Haiku 4.5 cannot exercise campaign autonomy — mapped onto five explicit experimental questions (Q1–Q5).

04 Limitations

  • −Budget-favoring: 24 h × 5 nodes always fully consumed vs 6.9–36 h stall-triggered baselines; no cost-matched sweep.
  • −Claude-only ecosystem — every worker, judge and the scaffold itself; “cross-model” transfer stays inside Anthropic tiers.
  • −Small held-out partitions (8 ARC-AGI-3 games, 29 τ³ tasks), ±5–8 pt s.d., and no statistical significance tests.
  • −TB2.1 mean trails GEPA (82.5 vs 84.7) — “matches or exceeds” holds only on pass@3 there.
  • −Steep, non-monotone capability threshold: below Sonnet 4.6 nothing works; the Opus 4.6 inversion is unexplained.
  • −Transfer boundary is real: single-source skills reduce TB2.1 mean (74.3–76.5 vs 81.4 seed) until a joint second meta-evolution run fixes it.

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Does the meta-evolved skill survive a non-Anthropic model family or a non-Claude-Code scaffold, or does it encode Claude-Code-specific tool-use habits?
  2. Q2What explains the Opus 4.6 < Sonnet 4.6 evolver inversion — a capability sweet spot, or one-campaign noise (no cross-seed error bars)?
  3. Q3Can candidate skills be judged on truncated or replayed campaigns instead of full 24-hour runs — and how correlated is such a proxy score?
  4. Q4Do ARC-AGI-3 results hold on the full game suite, and is RHAE stable at n = 8?
  5. Q5If the two-level recursion cap were lifted, would the learned skill exploit deeper nesting — and does the cap cost performance?
  6. Q6The skill is plain markdown: does paraphrasing it degrade performance — is the “process knowledge” robust or brittle?
