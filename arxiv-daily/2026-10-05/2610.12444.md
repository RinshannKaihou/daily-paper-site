> **一句话总结：** UC Berkeley+Nubank 重设计了 4-bit AdamW 优化器状态量化——核心洞察是"舍入应该定义在预条件子（preconditioner）空间而非状态空间"：局部零胞元分析证明 State-SR 虽在状态空间无偏，但其以 Θ(1) 概率舍到零会使下一步预条件子误差发散为 Θ(ε⁻¹)，而 Update-SR 把该误差控制在 Θ(ε)。由此提出两个 4-bit 方案：ZIP-SR（含零 Dyn4 码本+Update-SR）与 ZE-EDEN（去零码本+EDEN 块校准），均用 NF4-RTN 量化一阶矩并在训练最后 10% 仅对 LM-head 一阶矩切换 SR。在 GPT-style 130M–2.7B 和 Llama-style 130M–1.1B 预训练（3 个配对种子）上，两者在所有规模上都缩小 TorchAO 4-bit 相对 32-bit AdamW 的验证损失差距，最大缩减 70.1%（GPT 1.4B：+0.0521→+0.0156），且持久存储字节与 TorchAO 完全相同（4.0625 B/参数，7.53× 压缩）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper (UC Berkeley + Nubank) rethinks 4-bit AdamW optimizer-state quantization by asking where rounding decisions live: a zero-cell analysis shows that rounding which is unbiased in state space (State-SR) still rounds near-zero second moments to zero with Θ(1) probability, and the resulting next-step preconditioner error diverges as Θ(ε⁻¹), whereas rounding in preconditioner space (Update-SR) bounds it at Θ(ε). This yields two 4-bit recipes with the same persistent footprint as TorchAO 4-bit AdamW (4.0625 bytes/param, 7.53× compression): ZIP-SR (zero-inclusive Dyn4 codebook + Update-SR) and ZE-EDEN (zero-excluding codebook + EDEN block calibration), both using NF4-RTN for the first moment with LM-head stochastic rounding only in the final 10% of training. Across GPT-style (162M–2.7B) and Llama-style (130M–1.1B) pretraining with 3 paired seeds, both cut TorchAO's validation-loss gap to 32-bit AdamW at every size — the largest reduction is 70.1% at GPT-style 1.4B (+0.0521 for TorchAO vs +0.0156 for ZIP-SR) — and at 2.7B, where TorchAO diverges late in LR cooldown (marked gap +1.5435), ZIP-SR holds +0.3433 and ZE-EDEN +0.0535. In Tulu 3 SFT of Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.2-3B (5 paired seeds), ZE-EDEN's gap is essentially zero (+0.00003 / −0.00035 vs TorchAO's +0.00060 / +0.00175).

## 核心贡献

- **提出"舍入空间"这一新视角**：首次系统论证优化器状态量化的舍入规则应在预条件子空间（preconditioner space，即 h(v)=1/√(v/(1−β₂))+ε）中定义，而非解码值所在的状态空间——因为存储的二阶矩会经由 AdamW 递推和 ŷ_{t+1} 反馈进下一步更新，仅使状态重构误差最小并不等于使下一步预条件子误差最小。
- **零胞元局部理论（Proposition 1 & 2）**：证明在固定胞元 [0,b) 内，State-SR 以 1−ṽₜ/b = Θ(1) 的概率舍到零，下一步预条件子误差 Δ_r 发散为 Θ(ε⁻¹)；Update-SR 把舍零概率压到 Θ(ε)，误差有界 Θ(ε)。在标量二次损失（β₁=0、码本 {0,b}）上进一步证明 State-RTN 收敛到非零二周期而 Update-RTN 几何收敛到极小值，且近极值区 State-SR 局部扩张、Update-SR 局部收缩——即"保留零且换舍入空间"优于"直接删零"。
- **两个 4-bit AdamW 方案，存储与 TorchAO 完全持平**：ZIP-SR = 含零 Dyn4 二阶矩码本 + Update-SR；ZE-EDEN = 去零码本 Dyn4-NZ + 受守护 EDEN 块校准（重缩放被量化的二阶矩块，缓解正下界对预条件子分布的畸变）。两者均默认 NF4-RTN 量化一阶矩、仅在最后 10% 训练把 LM-head 一阶矩切换为 NF4-SR（针对 2.7B 上发现的 LM-head 径向偏置导致的后期发散）。持久存储均为 4.0625 B/参数（7.53× 压缩），2.7B 模型省约 19.2 GB。
- **多规模多种子端到端验证**：GPT-style 162M/405M/1.4B/2.7B + Llama-style 130M/350M/1.1B（各 3 个配对种子）预训练，及 Tulu 3 上 Qwen3-8B-Base、Llama-3.2-3B 全参数 SFT（5 个配对种子），全部配置下差距均小于 TorchAO；834M 单因子归因实验把增益分解到码本、舍入规则与校准三个组件。
- **开源**：PyTorch 实现与训练脚本公开于 github.com/nubank/adamw4bit。

## Key Contributions

- **A new design axis — rounding space**: the paper argues optimizer-state quantizers should account for how stored states affect optimization, not only state-space reconstruction error. A local zero-cell analysis shows small mean state error can coexist with diverging next-step preconditioner error, motivating rounding rules defined in the coordinate the update actually consumes.
- **Two propositions with matching practice**: Proposition 1 bounds next-step preconditioner bias — State-SR rounds to zero with probability Θ(1) giving Θ(ε⁻¹) error while Update-SR keeps Θ(ε); Proposition 2 shows on a scalar quadratic that State-SR becomes locally expansive near optima while Update-SR stays contractive. These directly motivate Update-SR over both State-SR and zero-exclusion alone.
- **Two deployable 4-bit AdamW configurations at TorchAO's storage cost**: ZIP-SR (zero-inclusive Dyn4 codebook + Update-SR) and ZE-EDEN (zero-excluding codebook + EDEN calibration that rescales the quantized block to shrink the positive floor's preconditioner distortion). Both share NF4-RTN first moments with a phase-dependent switch to NF4-SR on the LM-head first moment for the final 10% of training.
- **Matched-checkpoint intervention diagnostics**: on GPT-style 2.7B the paper localizes the late-cooldown failure of NF4-RTN to the LM-head first moment via interventions that quantize head vs non-head moments independently, and exposes a persistently negative LM-head radial bias b_rad under RTN (≈0 under SR) as the mechanism.
- **End-to-end evidence across scales**: 3-seed paired pretraining at 7 model sizes and 5-seed paired SFT on two models, plus single-component attribution at 834M showing NF4 codebook choice (−33.7% step gain) and the rounding/calibration switch contribute comparably large reductions.

## Methodology

**Setup.** AdamW stores a first moment m_t and second moment v_t per parameter; 4-bit quantization is applied when moments are written back between optimizer steps (working moments are used for the update before storage). A quantizer is decomposed into a decoding codebook C, a rounding coordinate φ, and a rounding rule ρ (RTN or stochastic rounding, SR). Block size 128 with one FP32 absmax scale per block; storage is packed 4-bit indices, 4.0625 bytes/param on eligible tensors vs 8 bytes for FP32 — identical across all three 4-bit configurations, with only normalization-layer moments left in FP32 by the same eligibility criterion for all methods.

**Preconditioner-space rounding theory.** Under the update-before-storage convention, quantizing the working second moment ṽ_t affects the next update and the next-step preconditioner r_{t+1}(v) = 1/√(h_{t+1}(v)+ε) via the recurrence. The paper compares State-p (φ = v) vs Update-p (φ = h_t(v)) for RTN and SR. Proposition 1: (a) for any ε>0 and gradient, Update-SR's conditional mean next-step preconditioner error never exceeds State-SR's; (b) in the zero-cell [0,b), any quantizer with Q(ṽ)=0 with probability Θ(1) has error diverging Θ(ε⁻¹), and Update-SR's zero probability is Θ(ε), so its error stays Θ(ε). Proposition 2 on L(w)=μw²/2 with codebook {0,b}: State-RTN converges to a nonzero two-cycle while Update-RTN converges geometrically to the minimizer; near optima, E[log|w_{t+1}|] is −Θ(√ṽ_t) under Update-SR (contractive) vs log(α−1)+o(1) under State-SR (locally expansive). A controlled 834M GPT-style run with FP32 first moments confirms: State-space RTN/SR destabilize training while Update-space rules track 32-bit AdamW over the first 4B tokens.

**ZE-EDEN.** The zero-excluding codebook Dyn4-NZ (TorchAO's choice) prevents the zero-point failure by construction but imposes a positive decoded floor that inflates small moments and suppresses their large preconditioner entries. EDEN-inspired guarded calibration rescales the quantized block: x̃ = c_x·Q(x) with c_x = ‖x‖²₂/max(‖x‖²₂, ⟨Q(x),x⟩+τs²), τ=10⁻¹². When the floor makes Q(x) overestimate, c_x<1 lowers the decoded block scale, restoring part of the suppressed preconditioner tail (verified distributionally on embedding and LM-head states, Log-RMSE 0.252→0.028 and 0.144→0.118 at 834M).

**First-moment design.** Block-normalized first-moment distributions fit NF4 best (MSE 1.056×10⁻⁵ vs SDyn4 1.739×10⁻⁵ and FP4 2.495×10⁻⁵), and full 834M runs confirm the ordering (gaps 0.0183/0.0239 for NF4 vs 0.0289/0.0272 SDyn4/0.0272... per configuration). At 2.7B, NF4-RTN departs from 32-bit AdamW during LR cooldown while NF4-SR remains stable; matched-checkpoint interventions isolate the failure to the LM-head first moment, and a radial-bias diagnostic shows RTN produces a persistently negative bias (contraction along the realized direction of m̂_t) that feeds back through the recurrence. Hence both final methods use NF4-RTN everywhere except the LM-head first moment, which switches to NF4-SR for the final 10% of training.

**Experiments.** GPT-style (162M/405M/1.4B/2.7B) and Llama-style (130M/350M/1.1B) pretraining on FineWeb-Edu with GPT-2/Llama-2 tokenizers, 3 paired seeds, mean paired gaps to matched 32-bit AdamW; component attribution at 834M; SFT on Tulu 3 for Qwen3-8B-Base and Llama-3.2-3B, 5 paired seeds, evaluating MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, IFEval. LR sweeps select per-model peak rates; weight decay 0.1; warmup-stable-decay schedule.

## Results & Highlights

- **Pretraining validation-loss gap (Table 4, 3 paired seeds, GPT-style & Llama-style 130M–2.7B, FineWeb-Edu):** both recipes beat TorchAO 4-bit at every size. Largest reduction: **70.1% at GPT-style 1.4B** — TorchAO +0.0521 (±0.0015) vs **ZIP-SR +0.0156** (±0.0026) and ZE-EDEN +0.0184 (±0.0007) relative to matched 32-bit AdamW. Llama-style 1.1B: TorchAO +0.0486 vs ZIP-SR +0.0198 (59.3% reduction); Llama 130M: +0.0305 vs +0.0171 (16.6%).
- **GPT-style 2.7B — TorchAO diverges late in cooldown:** its marked gap is **+1.5435 (±1.4473)** (the †-marked endpoint is excluded from percentage reductions), while **ZIP-SR holds +0.3433 (±0.0027)** and **ZE-EDEN +0.0535 (±0.0024)**. At the 95%-token checkpoint before TorchAO's terminal regression, the gaps are TorchAO +0.0754 (±0.0034) → ZE-EDEN +0.0471 (±0.0018, **37.6% reduction**) → ZIP-SR +0.0286 (±0.0024, **62.1% reduction**).
- **Component attribution at GPT-style 834M (Table 3):** TorchAO's gap 0.0246; replacing LN4-NZ with Dyn4-NZ for v alone *worsens* it to 0.0312 (+0.0066, −26.9% vs parent); switching m to NF4 gives the largest single-step gain (0.0207, −0.0039, +33.7%); final configurations reach **0.0177 for ZE-EDEN (28.2% below TorchAO)** and **0.0140 for ZIP-SR (43.2% below TorchAO)**. Steps 1, 2, 3a are 3-seed means (SDs 8×10⁻⁴–1.5×10⁻³); step 3b are single runs.
- **Codebook fit (834M, layer-12 MLP up-projection, RTN):** second-moment MSE per format — NF4's first-moment fit 1.056×10⁻⁵ < SDyn4 1.739×10⁻⁵ < FP4 2.495×10⁻⁵; EDEN calibration cuts the Dyn4-NZ preconditioner Log-RMSE from 0.252 to 0.028 (embedding) and 0.144 to 0.118 (LM-head) with FP32 first moments.
- **Tulu 3 SFT (5 paired seeds, Table 5):** ZE-EDEN attains a mean paired val-loss gap of **+0.00003 on Qwen3-8B-Base and −0.00035 on Llama-3.2-3B** vs TorchAO's +0.00060 and +0.00175; ZIP-SR +0.00042 / +0.00089. Downstream MMLU/GSM8K/HumanEval/IFEval deltas vs 32-bit stay within ~±1.1 pts for all 4-bit runs with no consistent winner (e.g., Qwen3-8B 32-bit MMLU 78.5 vs ZIP-SR +0.1, ZE-EDEN +0.1).
- **Storage:** all three 4-bit configs store 4.0625 bytes/param on eligible tensors vs 8 bytes FP32 (**7.53× compression**), ≈19.2 GB persistent optimizer-state saved on the 2.7B pretraining model and ≈21–56 GB on 3B–8B SFT — identical footprint to TorchAO, so the loss-gap gains come purely from codebook/rounding/calibration design.

## Critical Assessment

- **Theory-to-practice gap:** the two propositions analyze conditional one-step preconditioner bias on a toy scalar quadratic (β₁=0, two-level codebook {0,b}) with bias correction omitted; the authors honestly state this "does not establish general end-to-end convergence guarantees." The bridge from the Θ(ε) bounds to real GPT-style training is empirical (Figure 1 controlled runs), not proven — the theory is motivating, not predictive of the magnitude of end-to-end gains.
- **Headline 70% is a favorable pick:** the abstract's "gap reduction reaching 70%" is the largest across 7 size×family cells (GPT 1.4B). Llama-style reductions are much smaller (16.6% at 130M), and the 2.7B percentage is incomputable because the baseline diverged — the paper handles this honestly (95%-checkpoint numbers: 37.6%/62.1%), but the abstract leads with the best cell.
- **Attribution step rests on single runs:** in Table 3, ZIP-SR's 43.2% step-3b gain is a single-run comparison (steps 1, 2, 3a are 3-seed means), so the relative credit assigned to Update-SR vs EDEN calibration carries no variance estimate; the paper acknowledges the step-3b comparison does not isolate Update-SR's effect.
- **Baseline coverage is narrow:** the only external same-structure baseline is TorchAO 4-bit AdamW. Structurally different low-memory optimizers (Q-Adam-mini, GaLore, Adam-mini, COAT) are discussed but not compared — defensible since they change the update rule, yet it means the claim "best 4-bit quantization of standard AdamW" rests on one baseline implementation. There is also no comparison against the "paired geometric encodings" or "logarithmic-coordinate SR" prior work they cite as concurrent alternatives.
- **The LM-head SR patch is a mitigation, not a cure:** the 2.7B late-cooldown instability under NF4-RTN is diagnosed via radial-bias correlation, but the paper concedes "the cause of the directional first-moment bias and its possible relationship to learning-rate decay remain unresolved"; the final-10%-of-training SR switch is a targeted heuristic whose phase boundary was not ablated.
- **Downstream-task evidence is thin:** SFT gains are validation-loss deltas; the four downstream benchmarks show no consistent advantage (e.g., Qwen3-8B HumanEval −1.1 for ZE-EDEN vs +0.5 for ZIP-SR), so the practical significance at 8B rests on loss, not task metrics.
- **Reproducibility is a strength:** open-source code + scripts, matched seeds, published hyperparameter sweeps, and per-table SD reporting make the empirical claims unusually checkable for this subfield.

## Strengths

- Reframes a design choice (rounding coordinate) that prior work treated as fixed, and supports it with clean, checkable propositions whose predictions (State-SR's Θ(1) zero-rounding hazard) are confirmed in controlled training runs.
- Both proposed methods deliver strictly better accuracy at *identical* storage to the deployed TorchAO baseline — no memory/quality tradeoff to argue about.
- Excellent diagnostics: distribution-level preconditioner comparisons, matched-checkpoint interventions that localize the 2.7B failure to the LM-head first moment, and a radial-bias metric that gives a mechanistic story for the RTN instability.
- Multi-scale (130M–2.7B pretraining; 8B SFT), multi-seed, paired-difference evaluation with SDs; honest treatment of the divergent 2.7B TorchAO endpoint.
- Open-source implementation with training scripts.

## Limitations

- Theory covers only one-step conditional bias on a toy problem; no end-to-end convergence result for the quantized optimizer, and the Θ(ε) analysis assumes regimes (|g_{t+1}| = O(ε)) whose realism in trained networks is argued empirically.
- Gains are model-family dependent: 16.6%–70.1% across sizes, with Llama-style benefits consistently smaller; scaling beyond 2.7B pretraining / 8B SFT is untested.
- The NF4-RTN late-training instability fix (LM-head SR for final 10%) is a patch whose mechanism is only correlated, not causally established, and whose 10% threshold is not ablated.
- Only validation loss and four SFT benchmarks; no long-horizon RL or multimodal settings where optimizer-state fidelity might differ.
- Quantization is evaluated only for AdamW's two moments; applicability to other adaptive optimizers (Shampoo, Muon) is named as future work.

## Relevance

该论文属于"极端性能优化下的可靠性"与"LLM 训练稳定性"的交叉点：它证明了一个在状态空间无偏的量化/舍入方案仍会通过优化器矩递推反馈放大预条件子误差并在 LR 冷却期引发发散，给出了从局部数值分析定位并修复训练不稳定性的完整示范。对于关注低精度数值误差如何静默破坏大规模训练运行的读者，这是一篇方法论上可直接借鉴的工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the Update-SR advantage hold or shrink when optimizer-state quantization is combined with other low-precision components (BF16 weights, FP8 activations), where error budgets are already tight?
- Is the LM-head radial-bias instability specific to tied output embeddings / softmax-heavy layers, or does it appear in other gradient-norm-heavy tensors (e.g., MoE router moments) at larger scale?
- Can Proposition 1's one-step bounds be lifted to a multi-step drift bound that predicts the observed seed-level gap magnitudes, rather than just their ordering?
- How does ZIP-SR/ZE-EDEN interact with learning-rate schedules other than WSD (e.g., cosine), given the instability appears precisely during cooldown?
- Would the same preconditioner-space analysis transfer to Muon/Shampoo-style orthogonalized updates, where the "preconditioner" is a matrix rather than a diagonal?
