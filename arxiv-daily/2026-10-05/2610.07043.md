> **一句话总结：** Infx.ai + NVIDIA 的 TRIAGE 把 RL 训练中 learner–sampler 不匹配的治理从"控制幅度"改为"控制方向"：一阶能量分解证明只有优势与 log-prob 差同号（A·δ>0）的更新会自我放大 mismatch，据此在 64-token 片段级做方向选择性门控（只削弱负优势响应中的负 δ token 并重分配更新质量），再对正优势响应加有界 pseudo-Huber 修复项——全程保持 sampler 与 learner 的原生 NVFP4 W4A4 前向。Qwen3-4B/30B-A3B 上 GRPO 训练稳定不崩，4B 五基准平均 58.49% 反超 BF16 的 58.26%，30B 达 BF16 的 70.96%（naive NVFP4 仅 65.87%、TIS 仅 63.18%），rollout 吞吐 2.27–2.30×、端到端 1.21–1.30× 于 BF16，learner 开销中位数仅 0.84%。

> **TL;DR:** TRIAGE (Infx.ai + NVIDIA) stabilizes native NVFP4 (W4A4) RL training by acting on the *direction* of learner–sampler mismatch rather than its magnitude: a first-order expansion of the mismatch energy shows updates amplify mismatch exactly when advantage and log-prob gap share a sign (A·δ>0), and in native-NVFP4 GRPO runs the advantage-weighted asymmetry ratio between the two amplifying regions rises from 1.34 to 1.85 on Qwen3-30B-A3B *before* the marginal gap distribution degrades. TRIAGE gates only negative-advantage/negative-gap tokens inside risky 64-token segments (gate weight floor w_sev=0.1, thresholds τ_neg=−0.5, τ_sev=−1.5, τ_tail=−6.0), renormalizes response update mass, and adds a bounded pseudo-Huber repair (β=0.022, λ_rep=0.015) on positive-advantage responses — while keeping native NVFP4 W4A4 forward on both sampler and learner (no fake-quantized learner). On Qwen3-4B-Base with GRPO on DAPO, NVFP4+TRIAGE reaches a five-benchmark average (AIME24/25, MATH500, AMC23, GSM8K) of 58.49% at step 600 vs 58.26% for BF16 (+0.23), while naive NVFP4 collapses at ~300 steps (47.10% avg, +5.93 for TRIAGE at the matched checkpoint) and NVFP4+TIS stalls at 50.76%; on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base TRIAGE scores 70.96% vs 72.41% for BF16 (−1.45), vs 65.87% for collapsing naive NVFP4 and 63.18% for full-horizon TIS. Rollout throughput is 2.27–2.30× BF16 and end-to-end iteration speedup 1.21–1.30×, at a median learner-update overhead of 0.84%, across >34,500 B300 GPU-hours.

## 核心贡献

- **方向性 mismatch 表征（新视角）**：对 mismatch 能量 D=½Σδ² 做一阶展开，ΔD = η(B_amp − B_con + R_cross)，证明在 vanilla GRPO 下失配是否自我放大由符号交互 A_{i,t}δ_{i,t} 决定——两个放大区 A⁻={A<0, δ<0} 与 A⁺={A>0, δ>0} 才是风险所在；幅度大可能已在自我修复，幅度温和反而可能系统性增长，因此 TIS/clipping 这类只看幅度的修正不充分。
- **失配分阶段的实证刻画**：在 4B/30B 两个模型上用 25-update 滑窗（healthy/drift/pre-terminal/terminal）显示：边际 gap 分布在全局崩坏前一直"看起来健康"（|δ|<0.05 的 token 占比 ≥83.0%/91.9%），但优势加权不对称比 ρ_asyn 在 pre-terminal 已从 1.34 升至 1.85（30B）；放大 token 集中在少量片段——top 5% 片段容纳约 ¼ 的 amplifying-tail token（均匀分布的 ~5 倍），且响应级均值会掩盖它（pre-terminal 时含 tail-heavy 片段的响应中 97.2%(4B)/79.9%(30B) 的响应均值 |δ̄|<0.05）。
- **TRIAGE 方法**：片段级诊断（W=64）→ 方向选择性门控（只削弱 A<0 且 δ<0 的 token，保留天然收缩的 token；不动 A⁺ 区以免压制学习信号）→ 响应内归一化重分配更新质量 → 对正优势响应加有界 pseudo-Huber 修复项；全程保留 sampler 与 learner 的原生 NVFP4 W4A4 前向（区别于 fake-quantization 对齐方案）。
- **大规模验证**：>34,500 B300 GPU-hours；4B 上 TRIAGE 平均分反超 BF16，30B 上仅差 1.45 分，同时保持 2.27–2.30× rollout 吞吐与 1.21–1.30× 端到端加速，learner 附加开销中位数 0.84%；消融确认 gating 与 repair 组件互补、缺一不可。

## Key Contributions

- A **directional characterization** of learner–sampler mismatch: a first-order expansion of the mismatch energy D=½Σδ_{i,t}² yields ΔD = η(B_amp − B_con + R_cross); under vanilla GRPO the sign interaction A_{i,t}·δ_{i,t} decides whether an update amplifies or contracts mismatch, with two amplifying regions A⁻={A<0, δ<0} and A⁺={A>0, δ>0}. Magnitude-only corrections (TIS, clipping) cannot distinguish self-correcting from self-amplifying gaps.
- An empirical **staged failure analysis** on Qwen3-4B/30B-A3B Base: directional asymmetry (advantage-weighted token occupancy ratio ρ_asyn of A⁻ vs A⁺, rising 1.34→1.85 on 30B) precedes the terminal negative-tail explosion (5% gap quantile −42.75 on 4B, −10.94 on 30B); amplifying-tail tokens concentrate in top-5% of 64-token segments (~5× uniform mass) while response means hide the localization (97.2%/79.9% of affected responses show |δ̄|<0.05 pre-terminal).
- **TRIAGE**, a stabilization method combining segment-level diagnosis with direction-selective token gating (gate only A<0, δ<0 tokens in risky segments; contracting tokens and the A⁺ region untouched), within-response update-mass rebalancing, and a bounded pseudo-Huber repair on positive-advantage responses — all under native NVFP4 W4A4 forward execution on both the sampler and the learner (no fake-quantized computation).
- An **end-to-end validation at scale**: stable optimization over the full horizon on both models with near-BF16 or better benchmark averages (58.49% vs 58.26% on 4B), 2.27–2.30× rollout throughput, 1.21–1.30× end-to-end speedup, and a median learner overhead of 0.84%, across experiments totaling >34,500 GPU-hours on B300 GPUs.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** Decoupled RL systems sample rollouts with an inference engine (sampler, π_s) and train with a framework like Megatron-LM (learner, π_l). The log-prob gap δ_{i,t} = log π_l(y_t|·) − log π_s(y_t|·) makes the true importance weight π_l/π_s = ρ_{i,t}·exp(δ_{i,t}) — a product of optimization drift and learner–sampler ratio. Naive NVFP4 (per-16-element E4M3 microscale + FP32 second-level scale, W4A4 on both paths) perturbs logits and inflates δ.

**Directional analysis.** With update θ⁺ = θ + ηΣg_{i,t}∇θz_{i,t} and mismatch energy D = ½Σδ², a first-order expansion gives ΔD = ηΣκ_{i,t}δ_{i,t}g_{i,t} + ηR_cross + O(η²), where κ = ||∇z||². Diagonal mass splits into B_amp = Σκδ[gδ]₊-style amplifying terms and B_con contracting terms; for vanilla GRPO (g=A) amplification reduces to sign(A·δ)>0. The analysis predicts instability first appears as an *imbalance between the two amplifying regions* before the marginal gap distribution degrades.

**Measurement protocol.** Four 25-update trailing windows at 25/50/75/100% of each run's horizon ("healthy/drift/pre-terminal/terminal"); advantage-weighted occupancy w_A(R) and asymmetry ρ_asyn = w_A(A⁻)/w_A(A⁺); responses partitioned into W=64-token segments (never crossing response boundaries), with a "tail-heavy" label at threshold τ_ana calibrated once per model from the healthy window's 5% gap quantile (τ_ana = −0.242 on 4B, −0.039 on 30B).

**TRIAGE objective.** The effective update coefficient is g_{i,t} = α_{i,t}·g^base_{i,t} + ψ_{i,t}:
1. *Segment diagnosis & direction-selective gating.* For each segment S of a negative-advantage response, compute mean gap δ̄_S and deep-tail fraction f^tail_S (token δ < τ_tail). The gate weight w_S = min(w^mean_S, w^tail_S): a mean-gap component (τ_sev ≤ δ̄_S < τ_neg → w_neg; δ̄_S < τ_sev → w_sev) and a tail component w^tail_S = 1−(1−w_sev)·min(k^tail_S/K_tail, 1). Operating values: W_gate=64, τ_neg=−0.5, τ_sev=−1.5, τ_tail=−6.0, w_neg=0.3, w_sev=0.1 (so w_min=0.1), K_tail=2. The weight is applied *only* to tokens with A_i<0 and δ_{i,t}<0 inside the risky segment; contracting tokens (δ>0) keep weight 1, and positive-advantage responses are never gated (attenuating A⁺ updates would suppress the learning signal).
2. *Rebalancing.* The weighted policy loss normalizes per response (α_{i,t}=w_{i,t}/w̄_i), so mass is reallocated away from the gated amplifying subset rather than uniformly weakening the response.
3. *Bounded repair on positive-advantage responses.* Since gating is multiplicative and cannot reverse sign, a repair term φ_β(q_R) = β²(√(1+(q/β)²)−1) (pseudo-Huber) with q_R = [τ_rep − δ̄_R]₊ over W_rep=128-token repair segments adds corrective pressure that, for negative-gap tokens, contributes contraction. Values: τ_rep=−0.03, β=0.022, λ_rep=0.015, active from fixed update k_rep=0 onward.

All thresholds are fixed before each run; TRIAGE modifies only the optimization objective — native NVFP4 W4A4 execution is retained on both sampler and learner.

**Experimental setup.** Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base (primary, MoE) and Qwen3-4B-Base (replication/ablation); GRPO on the DAPO dataset; max response length 20,480; four settings: BF16, native NVFP4, NVFP4+TIS (truncation C=2.0), NVFP4+TRIAGE. Evaluated on AIME24, AIME25, MATH500, AMC23, GSM8K, plus training reward, entropy, and mean absolute train-rollout log-prob gap. Ablations branch from the full-TRIAGE 4B checkpoint at step 300; TRIAGE's incremental learner cost measured on 8 B300 GPUs with five paired repetitions of a frozen batch.

## Results & Highlights

- **Stability horizon (4B).** Naive NVFP4 becomes unstable after ~300 steps on Qwen3-4B-Base (sharp reward drop, rapid growth of mean |log-prob gap|); TIS delays but does not prevent the same failure. TRIAGE remains stable through the full 600-step schedule — roughly doubling the stable training horizon relative to naive NVFP4.
- **Benchmarks (Table 1, five-benchmark average).** On Qwen3-4B-Base, NVFP4+TRIAGE at step 600 reaches 58.49% vs 58.26% for BF16 (+0.23, i.e., full-precision-level performance), while naive NVFP4 collapses at 47.10% (step 300) and NVFP4+TIS at 50.76% (step 300); at the matched step-300 checkpoint TRIAGE leads naive NVFP4 by +5.93 and TIS by +2.27, showing the gain is not just longer training. On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, TRIAGE scores 70.96% at step 1200 vs 72.41% for BF16 at step 1700 (−1.45), vs 65.87% for naive NVFP4 (collapses at step 700) and 63.18% for TIS even at the full 1700 steps (+5.09 over naive NVFP4, +7.78 over TIS).
- **TIS completes training yet underperforms.** NVFP4+TIS trained the full 1,700 steps on 30B scores 63.18% — *below* the 65.87% of the pre-collapse naive NVFP4 checkpoint at step 700, and its reward trajectory stays below BF16 and TRIAGE in later training: merely surviving to the end of the horizon does not translate into downstream performance, supporting the paper's directional (not magnitude-only) thesis.
- **Per-benchmark detail (30B).** TRIAGE beats BF16 on AIME25 (58.33% vs 36.67%) and GSM8K (95.47% vs 90.81%), is close on MATH500 (91.60% vs 92.65%), but trails on AIME24 (51.67% vs 55.89%) and AMC23 (77.72% vs 88.04%).
- **Efficiency.** With TRIAGE, native NVFP4 sustains 6,745 output tokens/s at global batch 256 (2.27× BF16's 2,977) and 8,717 tokens/s at 512 (2.30× vs 3,798); end-to-end iteration time drops from 208.2→172.1 s (1.21×) and 254.5→195.8 s (1.30×). The incremental learner-update overhead of TRIAGE is a median 0.84% (median absolute +0.225 s on 8 B300 GPUs).
- **Ablations (4B, from full-TRIAGE step-300 checkpoint).** Removing repair ("Gate+TIS") keeps the log-prob gap low but reward declines — the advantage-driven imbalance is left uncorrected; TIS alone continued from the TRIAGE checkpoint stays stable but fails when trained from the beginning, evidence that intervention timing interacts with the stage-dependent mismatch dynamics of Section 3.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but the math is light.** The core observation — mismatch risk is the sign interaction A·δ, not magnitude — is a genuinely useful reframing, and the staged (asymmetry-before-tail-explosion) empirics are the strongest part of the paper. But the "energy decomposition" is a straightforward first-order Taylor expansion; R_cross is dismissed by assumption rather than bounded, optimizer-history effects are relegated to a residual (Appendix A), and no experiment isolates the predictive power of ΔD itself against simpler signals.
- **The 30B benchmark table has soft spots.** The "near-BF16" headline (−1.45) mixes a suspicious outlier with real regressions: TRIAGE's +21.66 on AIME25 rests on BF16 scoring an anomalously low 36.67% (on a model that scores 92.65% on MATH500 — AIME has only 30 problems, so ±1 problem ≈ 3.3 pts), while AMC23 drops a full 10.32 points (77.72% vs 88.04%) and AIME24 4.22 points. No error bars, sample counts, or pass@k variance are reported for any benchmark, and the winning numbers (bolded 58.33/91.60/77.72/95.47) are partly luck of small-sample noise.
- **Unmatched evaluation checkpoints.** On 30B, TRIAGE is evaluated at step 1200 vs BF16 at 1700 — the comparison of averages across different training budgets is confounded, and the paper does not show BF16's reward curve at step 1200 for an apples-to-apples read.
- **Narrow external validity.** Only two Qwen3-family models, one algorithm (GRPO), one dataset (DAPO, math-only), synchronous RL only. The authors honestly flag all of this (no 100B-scale, no long-horizon, no async), but the method's thresholds (τ_sev=−1.5, τ_tail=−6.0, K_tail=2, β=0.022, λ_rep=0.015) are hand-fixed with no sensitivity analysis in the main text — τ_ana is even calibrated per model — so transfer to other models/reward scales is untested.
- **TIS baseline is a single setting (C=2.0)** with no tuning sweep; given the paper's central claim that TIS is the insufficient status quo, a stronger effort to tune the competing baseline would have made the 63.18%-vs-70.96% gap more damning.
- **Reproducibility:** code is released and thresholds are fully specified (a plus), but replicating any headline number requires B300-class hardware and the better part of 34,500 GPU-hours; academic groups cannot cheaply verify the 30B results.

## Strengths

- A crisp, well-motivated conceptual contribution: separating *amplifying* from *contracting* mismatch via A·δ, with a prediction (asymmetry precedes tail explosion) that the measurements actually confirm across two models.
- The method follows directly from the diagnosis — segment scale chosen because response-level means provably hide the localization (97.2%/79.9% of affected responses look healthy pre-terminal); direction-selective gating because contracting tokens should not be touched; repair only on the positive-advantage side because gating is multiplicative.
- Preserves *native* NVFP4 W4A4 execution on both sampler and learner, unlike fake-quantization or QAT-alignment alternatives — the efficiency numbers (2.27–2.30× rollout, 0.84% overhead) are therefore trustworthy and meaningful.
- Unusually honest limitations section and a complete hyperparameter table; ablations isolate each component's role (repair fixes reward, gating fixes the gap, and their effect depends on intervention timing).
- Practical significance: ~2.3× rollout throughput at full-precision-level accuracy is exactly the trade the RL-systems community is trying to make on Blackwell.

## Limitations

- **Scale and horizon:** no 100B-scale models; main runs are terminated once trajectories show convergence or stable late-stage behavior, so whether directional dynamics stay benign over substantially longer training is unknown (the authors state this).
- **Synchronous RL only:** the directional criterion is not validated under asynchronous sampling, where policy staleness adds another mismatch source — arguably the setting where it would matter most.
- **End-to-end speedup gap:** overall RL iteration speedup is ~1.21–1.30× vs 2.27–2.30× rollout, because a growing fraction of runtime sits in operations outside accelerated GEMMs (parameter synchronization, quantization reductions/Amax computation) — the headline "2.3×" applies to rollout only.
- **Fixed, hand-set thresholds** (τ_neg, τ_sev, τ_tail, K_tail, β, λ_rep) with no sensitivity study; per-model calibration of τ_ana hints the constants may not transfer.
- **Benchmark noise unquantified:** small benchmarks (AIME: 30 problems) with no confidence intervals make the 30B "−1.45 vs BF16" and the AIME25 outlier hard to interpret precisely; AMC23's −10.32 regression is unexplained.

## Relevance

这项工作对做 LLM 强化学习训练系统（低精度 rollout、训练-推理不一致、RL 基础设施效率）的人直接相关：它给出了原生 NVFP4 端到端 RL 首次稳定的可复现配方，并把 mismatch 治理从"压幅度"升级为"辨方向"，对异步 RL 系统设计也有直接启发。若你在 GRPO/长序列 rollout 上做 FP8/FP4 量化训练，其分阶段失坏分析和阈值设计值得精读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the A·δ directional criterion retain its predictive power under *asynchronous* RL with stale sampler weights, where δ mixes quantization error with policy lag?
- How sensitive is TRIAGE to its fixed thresholds (τ_sev=−1.5, τ_tail=−6.0, K_tail=2) across reward scales and model families — could τ_ana-style per-model calibration of the gate replace them?
- The 30B AMC23 regression (−10.32 vs BF16) is unexplained: is it a genuine capability loss from gating/repair, or small-sample noise — and does it grow with training length?
- Can the 1.3× end-to-end ceiling be raised by co-designing the gate/repair kernels with fused quantization paths, or does parameter synchronization fundamentally cap native-FP4 RL speedups?
- Would a fine-grained repair that adapts to mismatch magnitude, locality, and persistence (as the authors sketch) outperform the current uniform pseudo-Huber penalty on positive-advantage responses?
- Does the directional-asymmetry precursor (ρ_asyn crossing ~1.5) serve as a cheap online early-warning signal that could trigger intervention automatically?
