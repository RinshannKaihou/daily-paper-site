How High Is 0.6? Floors, Ceilings, and Headroom in Interpretability Probing · arXiv:2610.08544

Pranjal Garg (Independent Researcher) · 2026-10-05 probing interpretability evaluation-methodology LLM single-cell

Key metric

0.74<0.80

OthelloGPT linear probe vs. a text-only floor

A baseline that knows only which player placed each disc reaches 0.80 — above the celebrated linear black/white probe’s 0.74 (UH = −0.30). Under the 3-way encoding, a linear probe at 0.99 recovers 99% of headroom.

Why this matters

Gives interpretability a cheap, reusable yardstick for every probe score — and stops us misreading distribution-shift drops as representation degradation.

为“线性探针分数到底说明什么”提供了便宜且可复现的规范化标尺，审读各类 probing 论文都可直接套用。

Critical edge

The headline shift result is demonstrated only on ≤794k-param toy transformers; whether it transfers to frontier LLMs is asserted, not shown — and published-probe ceilings are set to 1, making UH a floor-relative approximation.

  • Two ways headroom vanishes: weak target sensitivity (target barely depends on the latent) vs. weak identification (evidence barely reveals the latent) — separated in a controlled grid.
  • Estimation is cheap: a fixed decoder bank (GBM, shallow GBM, MLP, Ridge) trained on the declared restricted inputs; validated against exact numerical integration where the generative model is known.
  • Testbeds: meta-analysis transformers (3.3k–794k params), synthetic + real single-cell (scGPT), and n-gram floors re-auditing four published LLM-probe literatures.

Results — what survives the floor

Distribution shift, reinterpreted: on the 794k-param testbed, raw R² for log S1 falls 0.64 → 0.40 and prediction error rises 12–15× — yet the headroom share rises 0.78 → 0.82. Exact ceilings collapse (0.82 → 0.47) while floors barely move (0.01 → 0.09): the data lost information, not the representation. Conditional probing concludes the opposite — wrongly.

Strengths

  • Attacks a documented failure mode head-on: raw probe scores compared across datasets or regimes are not commensurable.
  • Cheap to apply — a decoder bank on restricted inputs — and validated against exact reference points where possible.
  • The shift result cleanly separates “data became less informative” from “representation degraded” — and shows conditional probing gives the wrong answer.
  • One framework, broad coverage: meta-analysis, single-cell, and four LLM-probe literatures, with floors chosen independently of the probes.
  • Honest limitations and an actionable reporting recipe: publish floor, ceiling, UH; use nested baselines O₁ ⊂ O₂ ⊂ … when no unique contrast exists.

Limitations

  • Verdicts are relative to the chosen contrast (O, I, T); open-ended LLM behaviors often have no unique natural O.
  • Published-probe ceilings set to 1; floors reconstructed from descriptions — any mismatch (e.g. the NYC n-gram choice) propagates straight into the verdicts.
  • Scale gap: controlled evidence comes from ≤794k-param transformers; the LLM audits are observational re-analyses, not controlled tests.
  • Estimated floors/ceilings carry sampling error; an underestimated ceiling inflates UH.
  • Fresh single-author preprint — no peer review yet and no visible code release; reproducibility rests on appendix decoder specs. The UH = −0.30 below-floor regime is exposed but not resolved.

Questions for further exploration

  1. How should O be chosen and defended for open-ended LLM capabilities with no surface-form baseline — does the nested-baseline sequence scale?
  2. Can floor–ceiling extend from correlational probes to causal-intervention scores (activation patching), where “predictability from O” isn’t the quantity of interest?
  3. Does weak identification still dominate for probes on multi-billion-parameter models under realistic distribution shift?
  4. What is the right interpretation and reporting convention when a probe lands below the floor (Othello, UH = −0.30)?
  5. Would richer-but-still-restricted floors close the “explained by the text” vs. “computed by the model” gap for user-attribute probes?
