Critical edge

“Steerability is not specific” is strength-dependent: the authors’ own β=3 pilot shows the two referring+query-span sets steering far above all randoms — yet the framing still leads with the null.

Also: “Gaze Heads is null” judges one score under this paper’s forced-choice readout — the original 83.1% steered-panel result is not refuted.

Methodology · the audit design

01Factorize the discovery score

Attention-based head discovery averages each head’s attention to a target region over “source” tokens. Crossing query type (ordinal vs referring) × attention source (last token / query-span / answer tokens) × corpus (comic strips vs COCO boxes) yields 12 head-sets — flat top-100 plus size sweeps — all on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, N=400 items per corpus.

02Run two causal tests on every set

CERS steers attention (+β=104, inherited from the released Gaze Heads code) into the correct vs a distractor cell of a 2×2 grid and diffs forced-choice probability. NCS masks the set’s image attention and diffs the drop on matched vs mismatched 4-way ImageNet questions, so generic masking damage cancels.

03Rank against many randoms

Each score is ranked against 20 layer-matched random head-sets with identical per-layer counts (250 for the two referring+query-span sets), plus attention-matched and generic-damage controls; Holm-corrected rank p, split-half noise ceiling.

04Stress-test outward

Eight grounding benchmarks leak-audited then converted to the same formats; a 14-task pointing suite; discovery redone on five further models across three architectures.

2×3×2 settings → 12 head-sets → CERS + NCS → rank vs 20–250 randoms → Holm

Proposed reporting protocol: ① layer+attention-matched multi-random controls  ② generic-damage controls  ③ benchmark leak audits  ④ head-set size sweeps.

  • Strength matters. At β=3 the two referring+query-span sets do separate: CERS 0.689 / 0.789 vs random means −0.103 / 0.204 — but that pilot has only 5 random sets.
  • Where necessity acts. Masking only the question/option token rows preserves the full effect (0.140 vs 0.126 masking everywhere); masking the last row: −0.001.
  • It travels. On 8 leak-audited out-of-domain benchmarks, cm-ref-qsp hits CERS 0.288 (random draw −0.117) and NCS 0.225 (random 0.020); across 5 more models all 25 query-span/output sets beat their randoms on NCS, shrinking to ~40% (Qwen3-VL-4B) and ~23% (Qwen2-VL-7B) of the 8B effect.
  • The audit bites both ways. Converted benchmarks fail basic checks — SpatialRGPT oracle 0.28, mismatched NCS arms up to 0.738 (MathVista), 53% of Spatial457 items accept “1” as “False”.
Strengths
  • Quantifies why single-random-draw validation cannot work: random draws span CERS −0.34 to +0.84, and discovered sets sit inside that band.
  • The two-test design (CERS + NCS) cleanly separates “a lever the model ignores” from “a route the model needs”.
  • Exemplary controls: layer- and attention-matched randoms, generic-damage stress sets (≤ 0.025 NCS), split-half ceilings, Holm correction, per-layer robustness.
  • A mechanistic nugget: necessity localizes to the question/option rows (0.140 preserved; last row −0.001).
  • The 8-benchmark leak audit is a service to the whole steering-evaluation subfield — honestly reported, with every design level borrowed from the audited methods.
Limitations
  • Depth confined to Qwen3-VL-8B; every cross-model / out-of-domain comparison uses a single random draw — the very practice the paper criticizes.
  • Steering tested at one inherited strength (β=104); the β=3 specificity pilot has only 5 random sets (rank-p floor 1/6).
  • Necessity = attention masking on forced-choice probability only: no activation patching, no resample ablation, no free-form generation.
  • Attention-matching control is approximate: random sets span a 3–6× narrower image-attention mass range.
  • Effects are small on near-ceiling baselines (NCS ~0.12 off a 0.995 baseline); code and corrected conversions arrive only with camera-ready.
Questions for further exploration
  1. Where on the β axis does steering specificity emerge — and is there a principled criterion for choosing the strength?
  2. Do referring+query-span heads stay necessary under free-form answers, or under activation patching instead of masking?
  3. Why does the NCS effect shrink to ~23–40% on Qwen2-VL-7B / Qwen3-VL-4B — and is Qwen3.5-9B’s last-token NCS 0.223 an artifact or an architectural difference?
  4. Can question/option-row necessity be tied to a concrete circuit — and do the null corpus-specific heads do anything at all?
  5. Will the leak-audited benchmark conversions become a standard suite, and do the conclusions survive off-ceiling tasks?
