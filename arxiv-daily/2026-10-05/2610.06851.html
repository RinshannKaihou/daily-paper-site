Prefilling just two response tokens lifts a base model to RL-level reasoning — with zero weight updates.

Base Models Can Reason By Taking a Cue From Training Data

Sophie L. Wang · Amil Dravid · Rulin Shao · et al. MIT · UC Berkeley · UW · Allen Institute for AI

Key metric · MATH-500 pass@1
42%→78%

Olmo-3-7B base with the cue \n\n Okay prefilled — beating its GRPO RL-Zero counterpart at 75%. Cue found by a label-free beam search.

Why this matters

RL adds elicitation, not new capability: direct consequences for how we read RLVR gains, synthetic-data bias, and alignment robustness.

Critical edge

The “matches RL” headline is scoped to single-stage RL-Zero: no instruct or multi-stage pipeline is tested, and Llama-3.1-8B yielded no effective cue at all among the openings tried — the finding shrinks to models whose base training already seeded latent reasoning.

How they showed it

1

Discover a cue, label-free

Beam search (width 20, depth 2) over openings on 30 MATH training problems, ranked by average model probability → 20 candidates.

2

Select by answer agreement

16 continuations per problem; discard openings with >10 pts extra missing answers; keep the lowest answer-entropy. Lands within 1.1 pts of the best of 500 openings.

3

Prefill & evaluate

Fix the opening c, sample from p(y | x, c) with no weight updates; pass@1 over 32 rollouts, 31,744-token cap. Models: Olmo-3-7B, Qwen3-14B vs their RL-Zero (GRPO) twins.

4

Localize what RL changed

Per-position KL divergence base↔RL; cue-forced GRPO (cue prefilled, excluded from loss); a targeted weight-edit control that raises cue probability without RL.

5

Retrain on edited data

Re-train Olmo-3-7B (and SmolLM3-3B) from a mid-training checkpoint for 10B tokens on counterfactual mixes: "okay"→"chicken", a redirect mix, "step by step"→"duck duck goose"; a 100B-token run confirms at higher accuracy.

6

Attribute in hidden states

Layer-24 response states vs hidden states of labeled training-source groups (reasoning traces, refusals, code…) by cosine similarity.

  • RL’s change is front-loaded: base↔RL KL peaks in the first two positions; p(\n\n Okay) goes 0.14 → 0.65 (Alright [: 0.04 → 0.58). Cue-forced RL matches full RL after ~100 (Olmo) / ~50 (Qwen) GRPO steps, then plateaus.
  • Not just longer outputs: the cue lengthens replies 4.8k → 6.7k tokens, but the $\text{ opening emits ~2× tokens and scores below no cue; under matched budgets the cue wins from 1k tokens and hits RL-level accuracy at ~half the budget.
  • Position matters: “Okay” inserted at paragraphs 1–2 yields 69–71%, at paragraphs 3–6 only 23–30% (no cue 38%); successful cues sustain hidden-state similarity to reasoning traces for ~128 tokens.
  • Safety hinges on punctuation: on unsafe XSTest prompts, harmful-response rate is 42.4% with Okay [ vs 0.2% with \n\n Okay [; I [ I'm sorry over-refuses harmless requests (8 rollouts per point — a case study, not certification).

Strengths

  • Rare causal rigor for an LLM-behavior analysis: full 10B/100B-token retrains of Olmo-3-7B plus a SmolLM3-3B replication on counterfactually edited public data.
  • Practical label-free cue discovery (beam search + answer agreement) — within 1.1 pts of an exhaustive 500-opening search, no reference answers needed.
  • Confound ruled out properly: token-matched truncation and rescoring kill the “it just generates longer” explanation.
  • Lands on two live debates: what RLVR actually adds over the base model, and how synthetic-data biases shape downstream (and safety) behavior.
  • Honest limitations and unusually complete appendices; code, project page, Olmo-3 checkpoints and recipes all public.

Limitations

  • R1-Zero setting only: multi-stage pipelines (SFT + RL, instruction tuning) may add real capability beyond cue elicitation; no instruct model is tested.
  • Model-dependent: no effective cue found for Llama-3.1-8B; search is not guaranteed to find anything and must be redone per model–prompt pair. Qwen gains nothing on HumanEval.
  • Almost ideal conditions for the effect: 83% of synthetic reasoning traces in the mix open with “Okay”, and the Okay cue already works before mid-training — part of it is seeded in pretraining.
  • Narrow evaluation: one cue per model–prompt pair across benchmarks; robustness to templates, temperature, and non-math domains underexplored.
  • Safety result is thin: 8 rollouts per point, one model family, and no mitigation offered — alignment is documented as “a few tokens deep” but not stabilized.

Questions for further exploration

Does cue-explains-RL hold in multi-stage pipelines (SFT + RL + preference tuning) on instruct models, or do later stages add genuine continuation quality?

Could cue-association audits become a pre-RL diagnostic — scanning synthetic data mixes for unintended cue–behavior pairings before spending RL compute?

Since the Okay cue survives removal from mid-training, can pretraining-stage cue associations be measured and disentangled from mid-training ones?

Would cue prefilling stack multiplicatively with agreement-based sampling or budget forcing, or has the cue already captured most of the elicitation headroom?

Can alignment training make refusal behavior invariant to opening tokens an adversary controls — alignment more than a few tokens deep?
