Align, Then Correct: Training-Free Two-Stage Low-Rank Compensation for Extremely Quantized Large Language Models ↗

Seobin Song · Geonho Lee · Janghwan Lee · Jungwook Choi — Hanyang University · Qualcomm AI Research

10.26
WikiText-2 perplexity · Qwen3-8B · 2-bit QuIP# · rank-64

vs 11.62 best baseline (LQ-LoRA), 12.43 QuIP# alone, 9.73 FP16 → −1.36 PPL, ≈80% of the FP16 gap closed (strongest baseline: ≈30%).

Why this matters

Run LLMs near 2-bit without retraining: a closed-form recipe recovers most of what quantization destroys — practical for on-device deployment.

Critical edge

On the only non-Qwen model tested (LLaMA-3.1-8B, 3-bit RTN), the win over QERA is just +0.21 CSQA — within plausible eval noise; the dramatic gains all come from 2-bit QuIP# on Qwen3 models.

  • Two shared flaws diagnosed. Every prior closed-form compensator (EoRA, QERA, ProQ, LQ-LoRA) calibrates symmetrically on (W−Ŵ)X̂ — making the rank-r target itself high-rank — and minimizes only the quadratic term, assuming g≈0. Direct measurement: the neglected first-order natural-gradient norm exceeds the second-order residual by >10× in every decoder layer.
  • Stage 1 — align. First LQEC objective using the full output empirical Fisher H_s with asymmetric calibration (adapter sees quantized x̂, target is the full-precision output); optimal rank-r solution is a single truncated SVD, with shrinkage η on selected singular values; blocks solved front-to-back.
  • Stage 2 — correct. Statistics re-measured on the compensated model; one natural-gradient step under K-FAC, folded into a generalized rank-constrained SVD(Y₀ − αM) — exactly rank-r by construction, no re-projection; LARS-style trust ratio α with τ ≈ 0.2–0.3.
8K tokens for Stage 2 (16×512) vs 262K for Stage 1 & all baselines · ≈53 min build (Qwen3-8B 2-bit, one A100 40GB) · adapters 264–349 MB

Strengths

  • Diagnosis measured, not asserted — rank-k energy spectra (Fig. 1) and per-layer natural-gradient vs residual norms (Fig. 2) recorded directly before being exploited.
  • Fully closed-form: two truncated SVDs per layer, rank-exact by construction, no iterative optimization, sequential blocks fit in A100 40GB.
  • Ablations that genuinely isolate each claim — Fisher vs objective, stage order, τ sweep, shrinkage with an out-of-sample singular-value check.
  • Honest reporting: construction time, 47.5 GiB Fisher cache, the τ=1 failure, and WikiText-2 calibration with C4 held out are all disclosed.
  • Consistent across 3 bit-widths, 2 quantizers, 3 model families — with the largest absolute gains on the weaker Qwen3-4B.

Limitations

  • Scale: validated only at 1B–8B; error structure and Fisher estimation may differ at 13B–70B.
  • Evaluation surface: perplexity + seven zero-shot QA tasks only — no generative, math/code, or long-context evidence that 2-bit compensation survives real use.
  • Does not close the gap on smaller models: Qwen3-4B 2-bit CSQA 60.05 still trails FP16 (66.73) by ≈6.7 pts.
  • “Training-free” hides two hyperparameters (η, τ) retuned per quantizer, with stability collapsing outside a narrow trust window (τ=1 → PPL 83.38).
  • Heavier than baselines: 1.4–3.3× slower construction (53 min vs 16–37 min) plus a 30–47 GiB reusable Fisher cache.
  • Uniform rank-64 budget ignores layer-dependent target energy; no adaptive rank allocation explored.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Does the first-order signal's dominance persist at 13B–70B, or does Stage 2's benefit shrink as models grow?
  2. Q2Can the natural-gradient step iterate with re-measurement — and where does the local quadratic model break (τ=1 hints at a hard boundary)?
  3. Q3How does the asymmetric objective behave under joint weight–activation quantization (e.g. W4A8), where x̂ itself carries error?
  4. Q4Is there a principled per-layer rule for choosing η and τ — from the singular spectrum or ‖M‖F/‖Y₀‖F — instead of a global sweep?
  5. Q5Would non-uniform rank allocation — spending budget where the asymmetric target keeps high-rank energy — beat uniform rank-64 at equal adapter size?
