Why This Matters

Rare industrial transparency on scaling agentic RL: groupwise grading, router diagnostics and hack defenses are directly reusable, with ~7k environments and the framework open-sourced.

Critical Edge

Largest gains are measured against the team's own previous generation (V2.5-Pro); where third-party benchmarks exist, results are mixed — ExploitBench 47.9 vs Claude Opus 5's 70.0.

Methodology distilled

Family. Pro: 1.02T-param MoE (42B active, 384 experts); Flash: 310B (15B active). Context extended to 1M in mid-training; 681M ViT + audio tokenizer (20M hours); DFlash speculative decoding. MXFP4 QAT

Scaled RL. Asynchronous GRPO (staleness 4): 1,568 prompts × G=16 ≈ 25K trajectories/step at 2.7–3.7B tokens/step, Muown optimizer, entropy-adaptive importance clipping.

Synthesize environments→ Roll out in mini-harnesses→ Grade: tests × GRS · GAR rank→ Advantage update→ Audit

Environments. Five code-synthesis pathways, multi-agent general workflows, visual replication, OSS-Fuzz-derived cyber tasks — each rollout-audited 4× with 8-rerun stability checks, served via composable Code/General/Visual/Cyber mini-harnesses.

Grading. GRS multiplies binary test rewards by offline solution/behavior rubric scores (R = R^test · s^sol · s^beh); GAR jointly ranks each passing group, zeroes confirmed hacks, and redistributes sequence-level advantage with a capped rescale.

Anti-hacking. Environment scrubbing (100%→28% hackable over 4 rounds) → dedicated hack agent → offline audits; <2% confirmed in the final run.

Router. Frozen after diagnosing expert-load collapse as router drift, not expert degradation. Behavioral regularizers: group-relative length penalty, segment-level format penalties.

  • Router ablation: trainable router lets cold experts go 0.5%→22% in 20 steps; freezing restores balance at zero benchmark cost — restoring original router weights alone recovers it.
  • GAR ablation: without online grading, pass rate stalls (~30 steps) while turns blow past 130; with GAR, gains sustain to step 52.
  • Open 9B distill: SWE-bench Pro 32.0→47.6, Terminal Bench 2.1 27.0→52.8 (SFT→RL).
  • Held-out harness transfer: mean Pass@1 on codex / claude code / mini-swe-agent rises ~50%→66%.

Strengths

  • Verifiable transparency: public RL training log, disclosed $2.6M spend, failure post-mortems, and verbatim hack transcripts.
  • GRS + GAR grading attacks binary-reward saturation directly, with a concrete ablation showing sustained pass-rate gains and stable turn counts.
  • Transfer measured, not asserted: multi-harness training evaluated on held-out harnesses (50%→66%).
  • Textbook causal diagnosis: the router-freeze control experiment cleanly attributes MoE load collapse to router drift.
  • Full-stack open release: 9B distill, ~7k verified environments, RL framework — lowering the reproduction bar.

Limitations

  • Flagship weights stay closed; open claims rest on a 9B distill whose ceiling differs fundamentally from 1.02T.
  • Offense-side security (ExploitGym / ExploitBench / SEC Bench Pro) and Terminal-Bench 4.0 trail frontier models by wide margins.
  • In-house benchmarks + self-hosted LLM graders dominate evaluation; grading-loop risk only partially controlled.
  • Single runs, no error bars — RL trajectories fluctuate by several points, so deltas under ~2 pts are not meaningful.
  • Multimodal unevaluated: audio encoder and ViT described architecturally, with no quantitative evaluation anywhere in the report.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does GAR's capped advantage redistribution stay stable well past step 52, or does grader-ranking noise accumulate into reward-model overfitting?
  2. Could periodic router re-annealing (or low-LR updates) recover balance without full freezing — and does freezing cost exploratory capacity?
  3. Does multi-harness training transfer beyond coding — do code mini-harnesses improve general/cyber held-out harnesses?
  4. What does the cost-performance frontier look like beyond $2.6M — Figure 3's slope appears to flatten near the end of the run?
