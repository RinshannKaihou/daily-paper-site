> **一句话总结：** 本文发现异步 rollout 会把上下文处理"集中"到更少的 decode 步上（KV concentration），使 KV cache 量化成为比权重/激活量化更有效的加速目标；并提出 KV4-RL——免校准 INT4 KV 存储 + 对 attention sink（16 个位置）与近期上下文（128 个位置）做有界 INT8 选择性保护——在 Qwen3-8B-Base 上 400 次 GRPO 迭代中取得 1.80× 累计 rollout 加速、1.27× 整迭代加速，最终六基准平均精度与 BF16 rollout 训练相差 ≤0.5 个百分点，而裸 INT4 KV 即使配合 TIS 也会在 MATH-500 上掉 5.24 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** Asynchronous RLVR rollout concentrates KV work into fewer decode steps, making KV cache quantization the best acceleration target (KV4 decode replay: 1.53–1.86× on Qwen3-4B vs ~1.0× for W4A16/W8A8). But naive INT4 KV rollout silently breaks learning — on Qwen3-8B-Base GRPO it finishes 5.24 pp below BF16 rollout on MATH-500 avg@8 (82.9% vs 88.2%) even with token-level TIS. KV4-RL fixes this with calibration-free INT4 KV storage (Hadamard-rotated keys, 4.25 bits/elem) plus a bounded INT8 protection of the first 16 (sink) and last 128 (recent) positions, cutting sampler mismatch 7.0× at ~94–98% of KV4 throughput. Over 400 iterations on Qwen3-8B-Base it delivers 1.80× cumulative rollout speedup and 1.27× full-iteration speedup vs BF16, with final six-benchmark mean accuracy within 0.5 pp of BF16-rollout training (52.8 vs 52.4).

## 核心贡献

- **识别并形式化"KV 集中化"（KV concentration）**：quota-based 异步 rollout 在不改变总 attention 计算量的前提下，把同样的 KV 处理压缩到更少的顺序 decode 步中；Proposition 4.1 证明这是调度结构的必然结果（连续补位下 C_KV = E[M_B]/E[L] ≥ 1），并在 Qwen3-4B 真实 trace 中验证：异步采集使平均活跃 batch 大 8.27×、常驻 KV 体积大 7.82×、单步 decode 延迟高 4.52×，换来 1.83× 的 decode 服务吞吐。
- **匹配工作负载测量指出新的量化目标**：高并发下权重读取被摊薄、attention 读取随常驻上下文增长，工作负载进入 KV-bound 区间；固定工作负载重放显示 KV4 在 4B 早/中/晚期 checkpoint 分别加速 decode-forward 1.53×/1.71×/1.86×，远超 KV8（1.30×/1.41×/1.41×）和 W4A16/W8A8（≈0.90–1.02×）。
- **首个 sub-8-bit KV 量化用于 RLVR rollout 的研究**：证明仅靠 token-level TIS 无法挽救 INT4 KV 训练（8B MATH-500 低 5.24 pp），并提出 KV4-RL：免校准的 head-wise INT4 KV（键做固定 Hadamard 旋转，4 + 32/128 = 4.25 bits/元素）+ 有界 INT8 选择性保护（前 16 sink + 最近 128 位置，开销与上下文长度无关），把 shared-prefix 条件 KL 降低 7.0×。
- **端到端全参数 GRPO 验证**：Qwen3-8B-Base 400 次迭代累计 rollout 加速 1.80×（最后 50 次达 1.97×）、整迭代加速 1.27×；Qwen3-4B-Base 600 次迭代为 1.57×/1.17×；共同 BF16 评测器下两个尺寸的最终六任务平均精度均与 BF16 rollout 相差 ≤0.5 pp。
- **可执行的工程方案**：paged INT4 cache 与固定容量 INT8 side-buffer 解耦，两区域用各自数值路径计算后做精确 softmax 合并；位置滑出 recent 窗口时无需重量化（INT4 副本本已存在）。

## Key Contributions

- Identifies and formally characterizes **KV concentration**: quota-based asynchronous rollout keeps the total logical KV work fixed but compresses it into fewer sequential decode steps. Proposition 4.1 proves this follows from scheduling structure alone, and a matched Qwen3-4B trace shows 8.27× larger mean active batch, 7.82× larger resident KV volume, and 4.52× higher per-step decode latency (1.83× decode-service throughput gain).
- Shows via matched-workload replay that **KV quantization, not weight quantization, is the right acceleration target** in this regime: KV4 speeds up decode-forward 1.53×/1.71×/1.86× (early/mid/late Qwen3-4B checkpoints) while W4A16 and W8A8 stay at ~0.90–1.02×.
- Presents the **first study of sub-8-bit KV cache quantization for RLVR rollout**: INT4 KV degrades learning despite token-level TIS (−5.24 pp on MATH-500 avg@8 vs BF16 rollout), and KV4-RL restores learning via bounded INT8 protection of attention-sink (s=16) and recent-context (w=128) positions, reducing shared-prefix conditional KL by 7.0× while keeping overhead fixed at s+w positions per response.
- End-to-end validation with full-parameter GRPO on Qwen3-4B/8B-Base: **1.80× cumulative rollout / 1.27× full-iteration speedup** at 8B (1.97× rollout speedup over the final 50 iterations), 1.57×/1.17× at 4B, with final six-task mean accuracy within 0.5 pp of BF16-rollout training under a common BF16 evaluator.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Dense Qwen3-4B-Base (600 updates) and Qwen3-8B-Base (400 updates) trained with full-parameter GRPO on the English split of open-r1/DAPO-Math-17k-Processed. Each configuration uses 4×H200 with two TP2 asynchronous rollout replicas and an FSDP learner colocated on the same GPUs. Each iteration collects 128 groups of 8 responses with a 16,384-token response cap; TIS clips the token-local correction at C=5, KL coefficient 0.001. All checkpoints are evaluated with BF16 weights/caches (temp 0.6, top-p 0.95): avg@8 on MATH-500, OlympiadBench, MinervaMath; avg@32 on AIME 2024/2025 and AMC 2023, three evaluation seeds.

**Quota-based partial rollout.** An oversubscribed request pool admits new responses whenever decode capacity frees up; unfinished responses carry across policy updates (re-prefilled under the updated policy, recorded sampling probabilities preserved), and collection stops once the T-group quota is reached. This removes the synchronous low-occupancy tail but concentrates context into fewer decode steps.

**Why KV (Section 4).** With a wide active batch, weight reads amortize across responses while attention must process every retained context — the memory-per-token split is roughly W_bytes/B + κℓ_t, so concurrency grows the KV share. The authors formalize the length-bias/occupancy effect (Proposition 4.1: synchronous long-run context t̄_sync = P + E[L(L−1)]/(2E[L]); continuous refill keeps C_KV ≥ 1) and confirm it on mid-training traces. Fixed-workload replay on H200 (vLLM Mache for W4A16, adapted CUTLASS FP8 GEMM for W8A8, in-house integer paged-attention kernels for KV8/KV4) then compares decode paths on identical decode states.

**KV4-RL (Section 5).** Three coordinated components:
1. *Calibration-free INT4 KV.* Quantization parameters are computed online per newly produced K/V vector — no stale-calibration resync. One affine INT4 group per 128-dim token-head (scale+offset in FP16, packed 4-bit codes → 4.25 bits/elem including metadata); attention scores are recovered as qᵀk̂ = Δ·qᵀc + m·Σy_i. A fixed normalized Hadamard rotation is applied to keys (and queries) after RoPE, preserving QKᵀ exactly while spreading outliers; values stay unrotated.
2. *Bounded selective INT8 protection.* The first s=16 positions (attention sink) and most recent w=128 positions of each response are mirrored in an INT8 side cache. This fixed 144-position budget cuts shared-prefix conditional KL (Eq. 5.2, a controlled next-token distribution metric at BF16-generated prefixes) by 7.0× vs unguarded KV4, and beats spending the whole budget on sink-only or recent-only.
3. *Efficient attention execution.* The INT4 main cache and protected region form disjoint sets: the main path uses integer QK/PV MMA with FP32 softmax statistics; the protected path dequantizes INT8→BF16 with FP32 accumulation. Regional outputs merge via an exact softmax merge, so no full-cache reconstruction ever happens; a position leaving the recent window needs no requantization since its INT4 copy already exists.

**Timing protocol.** Three nested scopes — fixed-workload replay (isolates execution), rollout collection (adds prefill/re-prefill/scheduling/reward), full iteration (adds learner) — with cumulative speedup computed as ratio-of-sums over the same update interval.

## Results & Highlights

- **End-to-end speedup (Qwen3-8B-Base, 400 GRPO iterations):** KV4-RL achieves 1.80× cumulative rollout speedup and 1.27× cumulative full-iteration speedup over BF16 rollout, with rollout speedup reaching 1.97× over the final 50 iterations. On Qwen3-4B-Base (600 iterations): 1.57× rollout / 1.17× full-iteration. Mean response lengths are matched (1.45k vs 1.46k tokens at 4B), so the timing gap is not from generating less work.
- **Learning quality (Table 1, final checkpoints, common BF16 evaluator):** six-task mean at 8B — BF16 52.4±0.5, KV8 52.8±0.4, KV4 47.8±0.1, KV4-RL 52.8±0.1; at 4B — BF16 47.0±0.1, KV8 46.6±0.3, KV4 46.2±0.0, KV4-RL 46.6±0.3. KV4-RL stays within 0.5 pp of BF16 at both sizes, whereas plain KV4 falls 4.6 pp behind at 8B, including −5.24 pp on MATH-500 avg@8 (82.9±0.2 vs 88.2±0.6) despite TIS. At the mid-training 8B checkpoint, KV4-RL reaches 87.68% MATH-500 avg@8 vs 88.16% for BF16 under the same TIS recipe.
- **Matched-workload decode replay (H200, TP2):** KV4 accelerates decode-forward by 1.53×/1.71×/1.86× at early/mid/late 4B checkpoints (1.44× early, 1.97× late at 8B); KV8 INT8 manages 1.30×/1.41×/1.41×, while W4A16 (0.90–0.95×) and W8A8 (~1.0×) are at or below BF16 speed; an FP8 KV8 FlashInfer path yields only 0.97–1.09× vs 1.22–1.52× for the integer KV8 path.
- **Cost of selective protection:** the default (sink 16 + recent 128) reduces shared-prefix conditional KL by 7.0× relative to unguarded KV4 at 4B-300 (5.7× at 8B) — better than allocating all 144 protected positions to either region alone — while retaining ~94% (4B) / ~98% (8B) of KV4 decode throughput (default configuration: 1.90× decode-forward speedup over BF16).
- **KV concentration trace (Qwen3-4B, iteration 300):** asynchronous collection sustains an 8.27× larger mean active batch and 7.82× larger nominal resident KV volume; per-step decode latency rises 4.52× but decode-service throughput is still 1.83× higher. Ablations show K-only Hadamard rotation is essential (sampler KL drops from ~10³ to ~10 on the ×10⁻⁴ axis), finer groups (g=32, 5.0 bits) improve fidelity but at storage cost, and NVFP4/MXFP4 without rotation show ~4× higher KL than rotated head-grouped INT4.

## Authors & Credibility

Sihwan Park and Eunho Yang (KAIST AI, corresponding), Sung-Yub Kim (NAVER Cloud), Doohyuk Jang (upstage, AIRS). The team combines an academic ML lab with industry inference-infrastructure experience, which shows: the paper ships a real in-house rollout engine (continuous batching, CUDA graphs, custom integer paged-attention kernels), a formal proposition with proofs in the appendix, matched-workload replay methodology, and 95% response-cluster bootstrap intervals for fidelity measurements. The writing is unusually careful about distinguishing sampler mismatch, shared-prefix conditional KL, and reference-KL — three quantities often conflated in low-precision RL discussions.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but narrower than the framing suggests.** The first-sub-8-bit-KV-for-RLVR-rollout claim is fair, and the KV-concentration analysis is a clean systems contribution. However, sink/recent protection itself is imported from inference-time selective-precision work (KVSink, SKVQ, Duannu, PM-KVQ are cited but never empirically compared against); the contribution is porting that idea to rollout with bounded overhead and a no-requantization execution scheme, not the protection principle.
- **Single training run per configuration.** Each rollout precision uses one GRPO run (training seed 43); the reported ±0.1–0.5 standard deviations come from three *evaluation* seeds at fixed checkpoints. The headline equivalence claim (KV4-RL 52.8±0.1 vs BF16 52.4±0.5 at 8B) is therefore within evaluation noise and is not backed by training-seed replication — the paper is transparent about this, but the equivalence should be read as "no observed gap," not a certified bound.
- **Kernel confound in the timing comparison.** The BF16 baseline decodes with FlashInfer Tensor Core kernels while KV4/KV4-RL use the authors' own integer paged-attention engine, so precision and kernel implementation change together in the end-to-end comparison. The FP8-KV8-on-FlashInfer control (0.97–1.09×) partially mitigates this, and matched-state replay helps, but a BF16 path on the in-house engine would have isolated precision cleanly.
- **Gains are length-regime dependent, and the paper shows it.** At 4B the full-iteration gain is only 1.17×; at the late 8B checkpoint the async engine approaches pool capacity (91.7 GB active KV per GPU vs 93.9 GB pool) and decode-service throughput drops to 0.98× synchronous. Speedups concentrate in the late-training phase when responses lengthen (~2k tokens at 4B, ~5.5k at 8B); short-response workloads would see much less.
- **Task-level regression survives the average.** At 4B, AIME25 drops from 16.5±0.4 (BF16) to 13.6±0.1 (KV4-RL), offset by gains elsewhere; with single-seed training, per-task swings of this size cannot be ruled out as precision-induced.
- **Reproducibility.** No code release is mentioned; the in-house engine's optimizations are only described in Appendix B. Reproducing the 1.80× number requires re-implementing a competitive integer attention kernel, which is the hard part.

## Strengths

- Reframes a systems phenomenon (KV concentration) precisely, proves it structurally (Proposition 4.1), and validates it on real training traces — the motivation for quantizing KV rather than weights is measured, not assumed.
- Genuinely end-to-end: 400–600 full-parameter GRPO iterations per configuration with a common BF16 evaluator, not just inference microbenchmarks.
- The KV4-RL design is engineering-clean: fixed s+w overhead independent of context length, exact softmax merge between disjoint regions, and no requantization on window eviction.
- Methodological discipline: three nested timing scopes, matched-workload replay, response-cluster bootstrap CIs, and careful separation of three distinct KL-type quantities.
- Honest limitations section (dense models only, math only, colocated GRPO only) that matches the actual scope of the evidence.

## Limitations

- Only two dense Qwen3 models and a single math dataset (DAPO-Math-17k English split); no MoE, hybrid/linear-attention, disaggregated-rollout, or agentic/multi-turn settings — precisely the long-horizon regimes the discussion section motivates.
- One training seed per configuration; accuracy equivalence within 0.5 pp is not statistically certified across training runs.
- Full-iteration speedup is capped by the learner (BF16 FSDP remains 60.5% of KV4-RL iteration time over the final 50 updates at 8B), so further rollout acceleration has diminishing returns.
- The 16+128 protection budget is a fixed heuristic; no adaptive/per-head allocation is explored, and behavior beyond the 16k-token scale is untested.
- No comparison with existing selective-precision KV methods (KVSink, SKVQ, PM-KVQ) adapted to rollout, and no released code.

## Relevance

这篇文章正好踩在用户关心的 LLM 训练系统与低精度加速交叉点上：它说明异步 RL rollout 的瓶颈会系统性地转移到 KV cache，并把 KV 量化从推理技巧升级为 RL 训练加速手段。对做 RLVR 基础设施、推理内核或量化训练的人，Table 1 和第 4 节的工作负载分析都值得直接引用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the KV-concentration argument and the 1.8× speedup survive on MoE or hybrid-attention models, where the balance between weight traffic and KV traffic differs substantially?
- How does fixed 144-position protection behave in agentic multi-turn rollouts with contexts far beyond 16k tokens — does the sink+recent heuristic still capture the high-KL positions?
- Is the ≤0.5 pp equivalence claim robust to training-seed variance, and does the 4B AIME25 regression (16.5→13.6) persist across seeds?
- Could the protection budget be made adaptive (per-head, entropy-based, or KL-estimated online) to trade a few more INT8 positions for even lower sampler mismatch at late-training contexts?
- What fraction of the measured speedup comes from the integer kernel itself rather than INT4 precision, given the FlashInfer-vs-in-house-kernel confound?
