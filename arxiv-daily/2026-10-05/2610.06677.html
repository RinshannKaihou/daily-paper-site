How Sparse Probability Maps Shape Mixture-of-Experts Routing

Tomás Brogueira · Marcos Treviso · Miguel Couceiro — IST Lisbon / INESC-ID Mixture-of-Experts Sparse Routing sparsemax · entmax LLM Efficiency

Key metric

≈ 0.5×

Sparse routers learn score spreads with about half the RMS of the softmax router — 0.32–0.38 vs 0.86. The learned scores, not just the map, decide participation.

1B router diagnostics · 285,107 targets / layer

Why this matters

Designing adaptive MoE routers? Swapping in a sparse map isn’t enough — the map and the router’s learned score distribution must be co‑designed.

对评估或设计基于 sparsemax/entmax 的 MoE 路由器是必读的实证参考。

Critical edge

One seed per configuration: the entire 0.008–0.012‑nat spread between maps cannot rank them — “no quality gain” really means “no detectable difference at one seed.”

Methodology

A controlled ablation: matched MoE language models that differ only in the router’s probability map.

top‑2 scores → gap g vs map’s fixed threshold → support size s → s≥3 drops mass · s=1 singletons
  • Models: Llama‑3‑style, every 2nd FFN block is MoE. 300M (8 layers, 26 experts) and 1B (9 layers, 27 experts, 302M active). Top‑2 Mixtral‑style selection, no capacity limits, no token dropping.
  • Training: DCLM, 10B tokens (19,073 updates × 524,288), AdamW + cosine, bf16; identical Switch‑style aux loss (0.01) everywhere; dense reference per scale.
  • Maps: regularized argmax differing only in entropy — Shannon → softmax, Tsallis α=1.5 → 1.5‑entmax, Tsallis α=2 → sparsemax, norm γ=2 → normmax‑2.
  • Measurements: discarded mass δ̄ (= TV distance), contributor count K₋, load CV, DCLM val loss, LAMBADA, OLMES; frozen‑weight re‑runs at K∈{1,2,4,8}.

4 · Depth dependence & the ablation

  • Singletons concentrate early: normmax‑2 routes 54% of tokens to one expert in the first MoE layer but only 7% in the last; sparsemax 19%→4%; 1.5‑entmax ≈ none anywhere.
  • Dense‑aux balancing (300M) doesn’t help: entmax CV 0.400→0.485, sparsemax CV 0.450→0.668 — participation and load balance move in opposite directions, supporting the “zero‑probability experts get no balancing gradient” account.

Strengths

  • Genuinely controlled: identical data, seed, hyperparameters, and objective at two scales — isolates the map’s causal effect.
  • Sharp mechanism: fixed singleton thresholds (g≥2 vs g≥1) cleanly separate what the map fixes from what learned scores determine; sparsemax vs normmax‑2 is an elegant natural experiment.
  • Conceptual clarity: retention, participation, and predictive quality shown to be three distinct, separately‑tunable quantities.
  • Novel depth dependence (54%→7%) directly relevant to per‑layer K design.
  • Ablation tests the mechanism rather than leaving it as speculation; unusually candid limitations; full recipe with code and configs promised.

Limitations

  • Single training seed: ≤0.012‑nat differences support no ranking claim between maps.
  • Modest scale: ≤1B active params, 10B tokens, ≤27 experts, no capacity constraints or token dropping as production systems rely on.
  • One balancing objective, one normmax variant; contributor counts measure dispatch, not compute — zero‑weight fillers may not be skipped.
  • Routing diagnostics not verified disjoint from training data.
  • Score‑scale story is descriptive: the mechanism that shrinks sparse routers’ spreads is unidentified.
  • Budget robustness has no demonstrated practical use case — loss is lowest at the trained budget for every map.

Questions for further exploration

Q1Does score‑spread co‑adaptation persist at production scale (tens of billions of params) and under capacity constraints with token dropping?
Q2Can the router’s score scale be explicitly controlled (e.g. a learned per‑layer temperature) to decouple participation from the map’s fixed threshold — letting one map emulate another’s participation profile?
Q3Would selecting experts on the support (rather than a fixed top‑K) capture the budget robustness without paying the trained‑budget penalty?
Q4How do alternative balancing objectives (φ‑balancing, balanced assignment, loss‑free bias adjustment) interact with sparse maps’ missing balancing gradients for zero‑probability experts?
Q5What is the training‑dynamics mechanism behind depth dependence — why do early MoE layers learn the sparsest routers, and is coarse early routing functionally necessary?
