> **一句话总结：** 本文发现只需固定基座模型回答的开头几个 token（如 `\n\n Okay`）就能让 Olmo-3-7B 在 MATH-500 上从 42% 升到 78%（其 RL-Zero 版本为 75%），并进一步通过把训练数据里的 "okay" 全部替换成 "chicken" 后重新做 10B/100B token 中期训练的因果实验证明：是训练数据中的 token-推理共现关联（而非 RL 学到的新能力）决定了哪些 token 能触发推理行为。

> **TL;DR:** On MATH-500, prefilling just the first two response tokens (`\n\n Okay` for Olmo-3-7B, `Alright [` for Qwen3-14B) raises base-model pass@1 from 42% to 78% and 72% to 87% respectively — matching their RL-Zero (GRPO) counterparts at 75% and 87% with zero weight updates. KL analysis shows RL concentrates its changes in these first cue positions (raising `\n\n Okay` probability from 0.14 to 0.65), and retraining Olmo-3-7B on a 10B-token mid-training mix with "okay" renamed to "chicken" transfers the reasoning-eliciting effect to the arbitrary token `\n\n Chicken` (MATH-500: 2.4% → 37.2%, vs 38.3% for `\n\n Okay` on the same mix), demonstrating causally that token cues work because of training-data associations.

## 核心贡献

- **Token cue 即可逼近 RL 性能：** 固定回答开头 1-2 个 token（无需参考答案的 beam search + 答案一致性选择方法自动发现），就能把 Olmo-3-7B 在五个 benchmark（MATH-500/GSM8K/AMC 23/AIME 2024/HumanEval）上的 pass@1 提升 1.4×–1.9×，Qwen3-14B 在四个数学 benchmark 上提升 1.1×–3×，多数情况下达到甚至超过其 RL-Zero 版本。
- **RL 的作用被重新解释为"提高 cue 概率"：** base 与 RL 模型的逐位置 KL 散度集中在前两个 token；RL 把 `\n\n Okay` 的概率从 0.14 提到 0.65（Qwen3-14B 的 `Alright [` 从 0.04 到 0.58）；预填 cue 再做 RL，约 100 步（Olmo）/50 步（Qwen）GRPO 就能达到无 cue RL 的最终精度，之后 cue-forced 训练几乎没有额外收益。
- **训练数据因果实验（论文最关键部分）：** 将中期训练数据中推理轨迹里的 "okay" 全部替换为 "chicken" 后重新训练，任意词 `\n\n Chicken` 变成有效推理 cue（MATH-500 从 2.4% → 37.2%）；把短问答数据里的 "Question" 换成 "Okay"（redirect mix）后，`\n\n Okay` 反而让模型去生成新问题、精度跌到 0.2%；把 "step by step" 换成 "duck duck goose" 后，无意义指令 "Think duck duck goose" 变得与 "Think step by step" 一样有效（17% vs 15%）。
- **超越"更长生成"的解释：** `\n\n Okay` 使平均回答长度从 4.8k 增至 6.7k token，但生成约 2× token 长度的 `$\text{` 开头反而低于无 cue 基线；在相同 token 预算下截断后，cue 从 1k token 起就优于无 cue，用约一半预算即达 RL 精度。
- **安全案例研究：** 在 XSTest 上，`I [ I'm sorry` cue 触发对安全请求的过度拒答，而 `Okay [`（无 `\n\n` 前缀）使有害回答率高达 42.4%，`\n\n Okay` 仅 0.2% —— 仅改标点和空格就大幅改变合规行为；隐藏状态相似度分析显示不同 cue 把模型内部状态拉向不同训练数据源（推理轨迹/拒答文本/代码）。

## Key Contributions

- **Token cues alone rival RL:** Fixing only the first 1–2 response tokens — discovered by a label-free procedure (beam search over openings, then selecting by answer agreement/entropy across sampled continuations) — lifts Olmo-3-7B pass@1 by 1.4×–1.9× across five benchmarks and Qwen3-14B by 1.1×–3× on four math benchmarks, in most cases matching or exceeding RL-Zero counterparts trained with GRPO and binary correctness rewards.
- **RL is reinterpreted as upweighting cue probability:** Per-position KL divergence between base and RL models peaks within the first two output tokens; RL raises the probability of `\n\n Okay` from 0.14 to 0.65 (and `Alright [` from 0.04 to 0.58 on Qwen3-14B). Cue-forced RL (prefilling the cue, excluded from loss) matches full RL accuracy after only ~100 GRPO steps on Olmo and ~50 on Qwen, with little added benefit beyond.
- **Causal training-data edits (the paper's centerpiece):** Retraining Olmo-3-7B on an edited 10B-token mid-training mix shows associations, not semantics, determine what elicits reasoning: renaming "okay"→"chicken" in reasoning traces makes `\n\n Chicken` an effective cue (2.4% → 37.2%); redirecting "Okay" into short-Q&A documents destroys the cue (accuracy → 0.2%); renaming "step by step"→"duck duck goose" makes the nonsensical instruction "Think duck duck goose" as effective as "Think step by step" (17% vs 15%).
- **Accuracy gains are not just longer generation:** Although `\n\n Okay` raises mean response length from 4.8k to 6.7k tokens, openings producing ~2× as many tokens (`$\text{`) score below no cue; under matched token budgets the cue outperforms no cue from 1k tokens and reaches RL-level accuracy at roughly half the budget.
- **Safety implications:** On XSTest, cue `I [ I'm sorry` induces over-refusal of harmless requests while `Okay [` yields a 42.4% harmful-response rate on unsafe requests vs 0.2% for `\n\n Okay [` — punctuation and whitespace alone shift refusal vs compliance, and hidden-state similarity shifts track the training-source groups (refusal text, reasoning traces, code).

## Methodology

- **Models and RL baselines:** Olmo-3-7B and Qwen3-14B base models vs their RL-Zero counterparts (RL applied directly to the base, no SFT; GRPO with binary correctness rewards; RL-Zero prompt for Olmo, boxed prompt for Qwen). Evaluation: pass@1 over 32 rollouts per problem with a 31,744-token budget on MATH-500, GSM8K, AMC 23, AIME 2024, HumanEval, using prefilling (fix the opening c, sample the continuation from p(y | x, c) without weight updates).
- **Label-free cue discovery:** Beam search (width 20, depth 2) ranks openings by model probability averaged over 30 MATH training problems, yielding 20 candidates. For each candidate, 16 continuations per problem are sampled; openings whose missing-answer rate exceeds the no-cue rate by >10 points are discarded, and the opening with the lowest average answer-entropy (agreement proxy for accuracy) is selected. On Olmo-3-7B this selection lands within 1.1 points of the best of 500 tested openings on MATH-500.
- **Where RL's change lives:** KL divergence between base and RL next-token distributions at each position of RL-generated responses; decomposition into opening probability p(c | x) vs continuation p(y | x, c); cue-forced GRPO runs (300 steps) that fix the opening and exclude it from the loss; a targeted weight edit (App. D.5) raising cue probability as an RL-free control.
- **Training-data counterfactuals:** Using released data, checkpoints, and recipes, the authors retrain Olmo-3-7B (and replicate on SmolLM3-3B) from an intermediate checkpoint for 10B tokens on edited versions of the 10B mid-training mix: a *rename mix* replacing "okay" with "chicken" throughout reasoning traces (83% of synthetic reasoning traces open with "Okay" in the original mix), and a *redirect mix* additionally replacing "Question:" with "Okay" in short Q&A documents. A full 100B-token counterfactual run confirms the effect at higher accuracy. A separate experiment renames "step by step"→"duck duck goose" and tests it as a prompt instruction (no prefilling).
- **Hidden-state analysis:** Layer-24 hidden states of generated responses are compared (cosine similarity) against hidden states of training documents from labeled source groups (reasoning traces, expository math, short Q&A, meta-reasoning, refusal text, code, science PDFs); each response state's ten nearest training-document states are attributed to sources, reported relative to no cue. Cue-position experiments insert "Okay" at paragraphs 1–6 of the response.
- **Safety case study:** XSTest (250 safe / 200 unsafe prompts) with WildGuard refusal classification; candidate cues from depth-3 beam searches over 100 unsafe CoCoNot and 100 benign WildChat prompts, compared against the model's own safety-evaluation cues (`I [ I'm sorry`, `\n\nOkay [`).

## Results & Highlights

- **MATH-500 pass@1:** Olmo-3-7B base 42% (no cue) → 78% with the `\n\n Okay` cue prefill, versus 75% for its RL-Zero counterpart; Qwen3-14B base 72% → 87% with `Alright [`, exactly matching its RL variant (87%).
- **Cross-benchmark gains:** The fixed cues raise Olmo-3-7B pass@1 by 1.4× to 1.9× across all five benchmarks (often matching or exceeding RL-Zero), and Qwen3-14B by 1.1× to 3× on the four math benchmarks; Qwen's HumanEval stays at its already-high base level. Cue discovery is label-free yet lands within 1.1 points of the best of 500 candidate openings on Olmo-3-7B.
- **RL concentrates on cues:** RL raises the next-token probability of `\n\n Okay` from 0.14 to 0.65 on Olmo-3-7B and `Alright [` from 0.04 to 0.58 on Qwen3-14B; base–RL KL divergence peaks in the first two token positions and stays near zero afterwards. Prefilling the cue before RL updates matches about 100 GRPO steps (Olmo) / 50 steps (Qwen) of standard RL, and further cue-forced training adds almost nothing.
- **Length is not the mechanism:** The cue raises mean response length from 4.8k to 6.7k tokens, but the `$\text{` opening generates ~2× as many tokens yet scores below no cue; under truncation to matched budgets, `\n\n Okay` outperforms no cue from 1k tokens and reaches RL-level accuracy at roughly half its token cap.
- **Causal data edits:** After renaming "okay"→"chicken" in the mid-training mix, `\n\n Chicken` raises MATH-500 from 2.4% (base mix) to 37.2% (rename mix), close to `\n\n Okay`'s 38.3% on the same mix; on GSM8K, `\n\n Chicken`-cued accuracy rises from 2.2% to 60.6%. Removing "okay" from reasoning traces drops its post-`\n\n` probability from 0.15 to ~5×10⁻⁷ yet prefilling `\n\n Okay` still works (the cue is effective even before mid-training). In the redirect mix, `\n\n Okay` makes the model generate a question instead of solving, and accuracy falls to 0.2%. "Think duck duck goose" reaches 17% pass@1 vs 6% on the base mix, matching "Think step by step" (15%).
- **Cue position and hidden states:** Inserting "Okay" at paragraphs 1–2 yields 69–71% MATH-500 pass@1 versus 23–30% at paragraphs 3–6 (38% no cue); successful cues sustain an increased hidden-state similarity to reasoning traces over the next 128 tokens. Across 2,000 responses per opening, checking phrases occur in 97% of `\n\n Okay` responses vs 5% of `To` responses; the `Problem` cue (6.3% pass@1) shifts similarity toward meta-reasoning text but never calculates the answer.
- **Safety:** On XSTest, `I [ I'm sorry` triggers refusal of both unsafe and harmless requests (harmless requests "such as stopping a Python process"), while the harmful-response rate on unsafe requests is 42.4% with `Okay [` versus 0.2% with `\n\n Okay [` — punctuation/whitespace alone flips compliance; hidden-state similarity shifts toward refusal text (`I'm sorry`), reasoning traces (both `Okay` forms), and code/science text (`How`).

## Critical Assessment

- **The "matches RL" headline is scoped to R1-Zero and the authors say so:** All RL comparisons are single-stage RL directly on the base model (GRPO, binary correctness rewards). The Limitations section honestly concedes that complex multi-stage post-training pipelines (SFT + RL, instruction tuning) may introduce capabilities beyond what cue elicitation explains, and there is no experiment on an instruct-style model where the cue-matches-RL claim is tested. This is the paper's main external-validity gap.
- **The causal-edit setting is almost ideal for the effect to appear:** The Olmo mid-training mix contains synthetic reasoning traces that open with "Okay" in 83% of cases; the authors themselves note these traces "repeatedly pair similar openings with reasoning," so the cue effect may be unusually strong in this synthetic-heavy regime. The "chicken" result (37.2%) is clean and striking, but the retention of the `Okay` effect after removal (effective even before mid-training, per App. F.6) hints that part of the cue behavior is seeded in pretraining and the mid-training edit only redirects it — muddying a purely "training data determines the cue" narrative.
- **Label-free cue selection has a circularity risk:** Answer-agreement (entropy) as a proxy for accuracy biases selection toward openings that elicit internally consistent answers, which correlates with correctness on math but says nothing about calibration — an opening that produces consistently wrong answers with high confidence could in principle be selected on domains where majority-vote agreement is uncorrelated with truth.
- **Model-dependence is a real red flag for generality:** No effective cue was found for Llama-3.1-8B among the tested openings (App. C.5), consistent with that model's weak RL gains — meaning the headline finding applies to models whose base training already instilled latent reasoning, and the cue-discovery procedure has no guarantee of finding anything on a given model. Qwen3-14B gains nothing on HumanEval. The paper reports these negatives honestly, but they shrink the practical scope.
- **Missing ablation on the KL analysis:** The claim that RL's accuracy gain is largely explained by cue upweighting rests on cue-forced RL and a targeted weight edit in appendices; the main-text evidence for "KL peaks in the first two positions" is suggestive but the decomposition of RL's gain into cue-probability vs continuation-quality components is not fully quantified in the main paper.
- The safety section is framed as a case study, correctly, but the 42.4% vs 0.2% punctuation result (one punctuation/whitespace difference) is presented with only eight rollouts per point (per whisker note) and no mechanism beyond the similarity analysis — it is an evocative demonstration, not a safety-certification-grade result.

## Strengths

- Rare causal rigor for an LLM-behavior analysis: full retrains of Olmo-3-7B (10B and 100B tokens) and SmolLM3-3B on counterfactually edited data, using public checkpoints/recipes, turning a correlational observation into an intervention result ("chicken" as an arbitrary reasoning cue).
- Practical, label-free cue-discovery method (beam search + answer agreement) that needs no reference answers and lands within 1.1 points of an exhaustive 500-opening search.
- Systematic ruling out of the "longer responses" confound via token-matched truncation and rescoring, plus cue-position experiments that link behavioral gains to sustained hidden-state shifts toward reasoning traces.
- Direct relevance to two live debates: what RLVR actually adds over the base model (aligning with Yue et al. 2025 and Zhao et al. 2025's "elicitation not new capability" view) and how synthetic-data biases shape downstream behavior — including safety behavior.
- Honest limitations section and unusually complete appendices (alternative cues, additional models/benchmarks, training details, weight-edit control).

## Limitations

- Findings are established in the R1-Zero setting only; multi-stage post-training pipelines may add capabilities beyond cue elicitation, and no instruct/SFT-stage model is tested.
- Cue effectiveness is model-dependent: no effective cue was found for Llama-3.1-8B (attributed to its math-focused base training), and cue search is not guaranteed to find anything; cue selection differs per model and prompt format, requiring per-pair search.
- The counterfactual data edits rely on mixes with abundant synthetic reasoning traces (83% of traces open with "Okay"), which plausibly amplify cue effects relative to more natural data distributions; the `Okay` cue was already partially effective before mid-training.
- Single-cue evaluations use one cue per model-prompt pair across all benchmarks; robustness to prompt template shifts, sampling temperature, and non-math domains (only HumanEval and GSM8K outside MATH-style tasks) is underexplored in the main text.
- The safety experiments use eight rollouts per point and one model family, and the paper offers no mitigation — it documents that refusal/compliance hinges on a few tokens but does not propose how to stabilize alignment "more than a few tokens deep."

## Relevance

这项工作直接回应了"RL 到底教会了模型什么"这一后训练核心问题，给出了可操作的因果证据：RL 主要是提高已有 cue 的概率而非注入新能力，提示词效果应被理解为训练数据关联。对于关心推理模型训练、合成数据偏差以及安全对齐鲁棒性（对齐深度仅几个 token）的研究方向都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the cue-explains-RL finding hold in multi-stage pipelines (SFT + RL + preference tuning) on instruct models, or do those stages acquire genuinely new continuation quality beyond the first tokens?
- Could cue-association audits become a pre-RL diagnostic: scan a synthetic data mix for unintended cue–behavior pairings (e.g., "Okay" tied to reasoning, punctuation tied to compliance) before spending RL compute?
- The `Okay` cue retains its effect even after removal from mid-training (seeded in pretraining) — can pretraining-stage cue associations be measured and disentangled from mid-training ones quantitatively?
- Would combining cue prefilling with agreement-based sampling or budget forcing stack multiplicatively, or does the cue already capture most of the available elicitation headroom?
- Given the 42.4% vs 0.2% punctuation effect on safety, can alignment training be designed to make refusal behavior invariant to the opening tokens an adversary controls (cf. "alignment should be more than a few tokens deep")?
