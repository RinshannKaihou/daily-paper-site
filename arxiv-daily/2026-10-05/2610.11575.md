> **一句话总结：** 提出 Elastic Expert Routing：训练时将每个 token 的激活专家数从固定 k 改为从以 k 为中心的离散高斯分布（如 6–10、σ=1.5）中随机采样，把 top-k 的硬排名边界平滑成渐进的暴露概率分布，同时保持期望激活专家数不变、推理仍用静态 top-8——OLMoE-1B-7B 微调九任务平均从 30.57 提到 31.41（+0.84），Qwen3-30B-A3B 从 56.42 提到 58.44（+2.02），1.42B 从零预训练验证损失从 2.6465 降到 2.6085、LAMBADA 困惑度从 54.2 降到 42.7。

> **TL;DR:** Elastic Expert Routing replaces the fixed top-k active-expert count with a per-token budget sampled from a discrete Gaussian centered at k (e.g. 6–10, σ=1.5) during training, smoothing the brittle rank-exposure boundary while preserving the expected active-expert count and keeping static top-8 inference untouched. On OLMoE-1B-7B SFT it lifts the nine-task average from 30.57 (static top-8 baseline) to 31.41 (+0.84), and on Qwen3-30B-A3B from 56.42 to 58.44 (+2.02, with BBH jumping +14.0); from-scratch pretraining of a 1.42B-A365M MoE on 26.25B FineWeb-Edu tokens cuts validation LM loss from 2.6465 to 2.6085 and LAMBADA perplexity from 54.2 to 42.7, outperforming Noisy top-k, expert FFN dropout, and DefaultMoE under matched compute.

## 核心贡献

- **问题形式化：** 将静态 top-k MoE 训练刻画为"硬排名暴露边界"——排名恰好在 cutoff 两侧、路由得分几乎相同的专家，一个拿到全部梯度监督、另一个任务损失反馈为零，使 cutoff 附近的优化极其脆弱。
- **方法：** 提出 Elastic Expert Routing，在训练时从以目标预算 k₀ 为中心的局部离散高斯分布（默认 σ=1.5，如 6–10 调度）随机采样每个 token 的激活专家数；采样邻域关于 k 对称，保证期望激活专家数 E[K]=k₀ 不变，因此推理阶段保留标准静态 top-8 路由，零结构性改动、零延迟开销。
- **暴露守恒分析：** 建立 rank-exposure 会计框架，证明居中调度的暴露总和等于 E[K]（守恒），cutoff 以上的暴露增量恰好由以下的部分暴露平衡（式 9–10），把"平滑"从启发式变成可分析的性质。
- **实证：** 在 OLMoE-1B-7B 与 Qwen3-30B-A3B 监督微调、以及 1.42B 从零预训练上验证一致增益；诊断实验（JS 散度、路由交换、逐层限制）表明增益来自中间层 L3–L7 的路由精修与专家优先级重排，而非简单激活更多专家。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates static top-k MoE training as a hard rank-exposure boundary and introduces Elastic Expert Routing, which stochastically samples the active-expert budget from a local discrete Gaussian to soften it into a gradual selection-probability distribution — without modifying the inference mechanism (static top-8 is preserved exactly).
- Provides a rank-neighborhood smoothing analysis proving exposure conservation: a budget distribution centered at the deployment budget keeps the expected active-expert count fixed while trading full exposure above the cutoff against fractional exposure below it.
- Demonstrates consistent gains across two supervised fine-tuning backbones (OLMoE-1B-7B, Qwen3-30B-A3B) and from-scratch pretraining of a 1.42B MoE, under matched compute and token budgets, beating Noisy top-k, expert FFN dropout, and DefaultMoE.
- Delivers router diagnostics (per-layer JS divergence, top-1/top-8 disagreement metrics, router-swap interventions, depth-restricted ablations) localizing the gains to middle-layer routing refinement (L3–L7) and improved expert prioritization near the cutoff.

## Methodology

**Motivation.** With static top-k routing, the router computes expert probabilities p = softmax(W_gate h), selects the top-k, and dispatches only those. An expert ranked k+1 with virtually identical score receives zero task-loss feedback while rank k receives full supervision. Repeated across training steps, this makes the learned router brittle at the cutoff. Prior dynamic-routing work removes the threshold but changes the deployment compute budget or adds serving complexity; Elastic Expert Routing keeps the fixed-budget inference contract intact.

**Budget-neighborhood training.** Instead of optimizing L_fixed(θ) = E[g(f_θ(x; k₀), y)] with a constant schedule k₀, elastic routing samples a per-token, per-layer budget κ_{i,ℓ} ~ Q and optimizes L_elastic(θ) = E[g(f_θ(x; κ), y)]. Q is a discrete Gaussian over K_{a,b} = {a, …, b}: q(r) ∝ exp(−(r−k₀)²/2σ²), with center k₀ = 8 and σ = 1.5 by default. Schedules tested: 7–9, 6–10, 5–11 — all centered at 8 so expected active experts and expert-side training FLOPs match the static top-8 baseline. Experts outside the sampled prefix are masked out and load-balancing statistics are computed on the realized dispatches.

**Rank-neighborhood smoothing.** For budget distribution q, the task-loss exposure of router rank j is a_q(j) = Pr(K ≥ j) = Σ_{r≥j} q(r): ranks ≤ K_min are always exposed, K_min < j ≤ K_max get fractional exposure, and ranks above K_max are never exposed. The distribution q is the discrete derivative of the exposure curve, and Σ_j a_q(j) = E[K] (exposure conservation). When E[K] = k₀, extra exposure above the cutoff is exactly balanced by partial exposure below (Σ_{j>k₀} a_q(j) = ½E|K − k₀|), so schedule width controls how much supervision shifts across the deployment cutoff.

**Experiments.** SFT: OLMoE-1B-7B-0924 on Doli-DoLa-Construct and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base on Infinity-Instruct 7M Core; every checkpoint evaluated with the same static top-8 rule and decoding config as its baseline. Pretraining: four 1.42B-total/0.365B-active MoE models, 128 experts/layer, 619 GFLOPs/token, 26.25B FineWeb-Edu tokens. Evaluation via lm-eval-harness: Avg(9) for SFT (MMLU, AGIEval-En, GPQA, BBH, GSM8K, MATH, MBPP, IFEval, TruthfulQA-MC2) and an eleven-task suite for pretraining (incl. LAMBADA, PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC, OpenBookQA).

## Results & Highlights

- **SFT on OLMoE-1B-7B (Avg over 9 tasks, static top-8 inference):** the 6–10 schedule reaches 31.41 vs 30.57 for the static top-8 baseline (+0.84); 5–11 scores 31.12 and 7–9 scores 30.81 — all three elastic schedules beat the baseline. Task-level, 6–10 wins MMLU 51.25 vs 50.47, BBH 35.28 vs 34.86, GSM8K 21.46 vs 20.09 (+1.37), MBPP 25.40 vs 24.20, and TruthfulQA-MC2 41.58 vs 40.32.
- **SFT on Qwen3-30B-A3B:** 6–10 raises Avg(9) from 56.42 to 58.44 (+2.02), driven by an outsized BBH Exact Match gain of 63.29 vs 49.29 (+14.0 pts) and GPQA 39.06 vs 37.72 (+1.34); 5–11 (57.69) and 7–9 (57.18) also beat the baseline, and 5–11 takes the best GSM8K (82.94 vs 81.43) and MBPP (76.00).
- **From-scratch pretraining (1.42B-A365M MoE, 26.25B FineWeb-Edu tokens):** 6–10 cuts validation LM loss from 2.6465 (static top-8) to 2.6085 (−0.038, the paper's "up to 0.04") and LAMBADA perplexity from 54.2 to 42.7, while raising the eleven-task average from 41.3 to 42.9 (+1.6).
- **Versus alternative MoE training methods under matched settings:** on Qwen3 SFT, elastic 6–10 (58.44) beats Expert FFN dropout (57.72), DefaultMoE (57.63), and Noisy top-k (57.03); on pretraining it wins the eleven-task average (42.9 vs DefaultMoE 42.0, Noisy top-k 42.1, FFN dropout 40.1) and posts the lowest loss (2.6085 vs 2.6252/2.6399/2.6671).
- **Gains are not just more compute:** fixed top-9 (30.71), top-10 (30.81), and top-p routing (29.72, activating 13 experts/token on average) all trail elastic 6–10 (31.41) at static top-8 inference; uniform sampling over {6,8,10} only reaches 30.93 vs 31.41 for the Gaussian, and σ=1.5 (31.41) beats σ=1.0 (31.30) and σ=3.0 (30.66).
- **Depth localization and diagnostics:** routing change (JS divergence) concentrates in middle layers L3–L7; restricting 6–10 to L3–L7 gives 31.50 (slightly above all-layer 31.41) while an L8–L12 control gives only 30.94. Elastic training changes the top-1 expert for 13.62% of tokens but the top-8 set for only 7.08% on average — it refines rankings rather than replacing the active set. Swapping the static top-8 router into the 6–10 checkpoint drops 31.29 → 30.86, showing router–expert co-adaptation.

Tong Zhu and Xiaoye Qu (Shanghai AI Lab) have prior work on LLM instruction tuning and MoE efficiency. The group has access to serious compute — they fine-tuned Qwen3-30B-A3B and pretrained four MoE models from scratch — and follows solid practice: matched optimizer/data/budget across all comparisons, standard lm-eval-harness metrics, and an honest Limitations section acknowledging pretraining scale constraints.

## Critical Assessment

- **Single-run results with no variance reporting.** Every configuration appears to be one checkpoint. On OLMoE the margin over the baseline is +0.84 but the σ ablation separates 31.30 (σ=1.0), 31.41 (σ=1.5), and 30.66 (σ=3.0) — differences of ~0.1 pt between σ values are presented as ordering evidence without any seed-level error bars, which is below the threshold of statistical comfort for pretraining/finetuning noise.
- **The BBH +14.0 on Qwen3 is an outlier that carries the headline.** Avg improves +2.02 but roughly 9 of those points come from a single task (63.29 vs 49.29); TruthfulQA-MC2 actually drops (51.93 for 6–10 vs 53.14 baseline). A +14 jump on one benchmark with modest movement elsewhere warrants a deeper explanation (data mix? format sensitivity of Doli-DoLa-Construct?) than the paper provides.
- **The "moderate interval is best" narrative is partly post-hoc.** Three schedules were tried and 6–10 won on both backbones, which is decent evidence, but the rank-exposure theory does not predict the optimal interval width a priori — the guidance ("useful smoothing should expose experts near the cutoff without allocating excessive probability to low-ranked candidates") is fitted to the results. L3–L7 restriction scoring 31.50 vs 31.41 for all layers is framed as a diagnostic, not an optimized method — appropriately hedged, but also a hint the layer story is descriptive.
- **Pretraining validation is genuinely small-scale.** 1.42B total / 0.365B active parameters and 26.25B tokens versus the 7B/30.5B SFT backbones; the authors say this plainly in Limitations, and MoE training dynamics at 100B+ tokens could differ. Strengths of the evaluation design: the top-9/top-10/top-p controls cleanly rule out "just train with more FLOPs" as the explanation, and the router-swap experiment (neither swap recovers the full 6–10 score) is an honest negative-style control supporting co-adaptation.

## Strengths

- Zero inference-cost design: preserves the static top-k serving contract exactly, unlike dynamic/threshold routing that changes per-token compute — directly deployable on existing infrastructure.
- Matched-compute experimental discipline: all schedules centered at k₀=8 with identical expected active experts and expert-side FLOPs, plus explicit over-compute controls (top-9, top-10, top-p) showing the gain is not a budget artifact.
- Evaluated in three regimes (two SFT backbones + from-scratch pretraining) with consistent direction, and against three named training-time baselines under identical data, optimizer, and evaluation rules.
- The rank-exposure conservation framework is a clean conceptual contribution that turns an empirical trick into an analyzable object.
- Strong diagnostics section: per-layer JS divergence, D1/D8 disagreement metrics, router-swap intervention, and depth-restricted ablation together pin the effect to middle-layer ranking refinement.

## Limitations

- From-scratch pretraining is only 1.42B/0.365B parameters at 26.25B tokens — the authors themselves flag that large-scale from-scratch validation remains open (explicitly stated in the paper's Limitations).
- No variance/seeds: single checkpoints per configuration make sub-0.5-pt orderings (schedule choice, σ choice) statistically fragile.
- Hyperparameters (σ=1.5, interval width, uniform-vs-Gaussian) are chosen empirically on the evaluated backbones; no transferable rule for picking the neighborhood on a new model.
- The headline Qwen3 gain leans heavily on one benchmark (BBH); one metric (TruthfulQA-MC2) regresses, and the paper does not probe why.
- Evaluation is standard lm-eval-harness accuracy-style metrics; no long-context, multilingual, or safety evaluations, and no measurement of training-time overhead beyond FLOPs matching.

## Relevance

该方法与稀疏 MoE 训练效率直接相关：它以近乎零成本（只改训练时的专家数采样、不改推理）稳定拿到 0.8–2 点的平均增益，是"训练目标层面改进 MoE 路由"这一方向的干净范例，对从零预训练或微调 MoE 模型的工作都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why do the beneficial routing changes concentrate in middle layers (L3–L7), and does the optimal elastic layer range shift with model depth (e.g., 30+ layer models)?
- Can the budget distribution be learned or made layer-adaptive (per-layer σ/support) rather than a fixed symmetric Gaussian centered at k?
- Does elastic routing compose with auxiliary-loss-free load balancing (e.g., DeepSeek-style bias adjustment), or do the two interventions conflict on the router's score landscape?
- Would the gains survive at production pretraining scale (≥100B tokens, ≥7B active), where router convergence dynamics differ substantially?
- What explains the disproportionate BBH jump on Qwen3 — is it benchmark-specific format sensitivity, or genuine multi-step reasoning improvement from near-cutoff exposure smoothing?
