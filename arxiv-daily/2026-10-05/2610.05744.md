> **一句话总结：** CIPHER-MoE 不改动 Top-K 路由器，而是在专家侧用余弦相似度阈值 + 显式容量预算对接纳的 token 做亲和度过滤（strict drop 或 reroute），在 DeepSeek-V3（671B）的 OR 域 SFT 上取得 1.94× 端到端训练加速且加权基准分 61.40 vs 基线 60.60，并让 vanilla 训练直接 OOM 的 1.6T DeepSeek-V4-Pro 以全局 batch size 8192 稳定完成训练。

> **TL;DR:** CIPHER-MoE is a plug-and-play, expert-side token filter: it keeps the standard Top-K router but drops (strict) or reroutes token-expert assignments whose cosine affinity falls below a threshold or exceed an explicit per-expert capacity budget, bounding hot-expert workloads without expert replication, migration, or custom runtimes. On DeepSeek-V3 (671B) operations-research SFT it trains 1.94x faster than vanilla MoE training with a slightly better weighted OR score (61.40 vs 60.60); on GLM-5 (744B) it lifts NL4Opt from 85.47 to 90.66 at 1.14x speedup against the LocMoE baseline's 62.51 at 1.12x; and on the 1.6T DeepSeek-V4-Pro — where vanilla training OOMs before the first iteration — it trains stably at global batch size 8192. A counterintuitive ablation shows strict dropping beats rerouting, and the reported Qwen3.6-35B-A3B failure case (<5% speedup) matches the theory's dimension-margin prediction.

## 核心贡献

- **算法层面（CIPHER-MoE）**：保持 router 的 Top-K token 侧选择不变，在专家侧计算 token–expert 余弦相似度，用相似度阈值 τ 过滤低亲和 token、用每 micro-batch 的专家容量 C_e 截断超额 token；被拒绝的分配可用 strict token drop（门控置零）或 token-expert reroute（转投有剩余容量且过阈值的候选专家）处理，均不触碰 shared experts，也无需专家复制、参数迁移或定制 runtime。
- **反直觉实证发现**：在 domain-specific 训练中 strict drop 一致优于 reroute——reroute 虽然保留了更多计算，但会把更新分布偏移到专家已特化的方向之外（余弦亲和度并不约束任务梯度方向），造成梯度干扰；strict drop 让每个专家只在预算内训练最高亲和的候选。
- **理论基础（定理 1）**：在 A1–A3 假设（曝光分布、球面参考模型、条件排序界）下，按相似度接纳所需容量 C_CIPHER ≤ B_e，而自然顺序接纳需 C_natural ≥ ((1−ε)a₀/βE)·e^{G(d,δ)}，其中 G(d,δ) = −(d−1)/2·log(1−δ²) 随表示维度 d 指数增长——例如认证边际 δ=0.07 时，10× 容量缩减要求 d ≥ 4,012（Table 7）。
- **大规模验证**：覆盖 284B（DeepSeek-V4-Flash）、671B（DeepSeek-V3）、744B（GLM-5）、1.6T（DeepSeek-V4-Pro）四个 MoE 模型 × 3 个领域数据集（OR / MedMCQA / SIQA），实测 1.10×–1.94× 训练加速；1.6T 模型上 vanilla 路由 OOM、fixed router 可训但质量次优，CIPHER-MoE 兼得稳定性与质量。
- **诚实的小模型失败案例**：Qwen3.6-35B-A3B（隐藏维 2,048）不满足定理要求的维度边际区间（Table 7 要求 d ≥ 3,357），实测效率增益 <5%，作者据此明确指出方法适用于大规模 MoE。

## Key Contributions

- **CIPHER-MoE algorithm**: affinity-aware Expert-to-Token filtering with explicit capacity control after the unchanged Top-K router; two handling policies (strict drop vs reroute) for rejected assignments, applied only to routed experts.
- **Counterintuitive finding**: on domain-specific SFT, strict token dropping consistently outperforms rerouting (e.g., DeepSeek-V3 OR: 1.94x vs 1.68x speedup, both at comparable quality), because cosine affinity does not constrain task-gradient direction and reroute assignments interfere with existing expert specialization.
- **Theorem 1 (Similarity-based Capacity Reduction)**: under assumptions A1–A3, similarity-ranked admission needs capacity C_CIPHER ≤ B_e while natural-order admission requires C_natural ≥ ((1−ε)a₀/βE)·e^{G(d,δ)}; a factor-κ capacity reduction is guaranteed whenever the natural-order lower bound is at least κB_e.
- **Trillion-scale evidence**: on DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T), vanilla MoE training OOMs immediately; fixed routing trains but homogenizes experts (Table 1); CIPHER-MoE trains stably (GBS 8192) with a higher weighted OR score than the fixed router (69.02 vs 68.59).
- **Honest boundary**: on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (hidden dim 2,048, below the certified dimension-margin regime), the efficiency gain falls below 5%, delimiting where the method applies.

## Methodology

**Motivation.** On domain-specific data, token routing is severely skewed: on DeepSeek-V4-Flash, 10% of experts consume over 88% of routing workload (Fig. 1). Existing fixes are either system-level (expert migration/replication/re-layout — added replicas, collectives, runtime complexity) or algorithmic (auxiliary balancing losses, fixed routing, batch balancing) that either ignore instantaneous hot-expert memory peaks or sacrifice specialization. Fixed routing (ρ = 1 + ((tK+j) mod E)) guarantees perfect balance but homogenizes experts: Table 1 shows it trains 1.37× faster than vanilla on DeepSeek-V4-Flash (28.30 vs 38.69 s/iter) but drops NL4Opt from 93.08 to 88.93; on DeepSeek-V4-Pro, vanilla routing OOMs outright.

**Expert-side filtering.** For expert e with gating vector w_e and token x_i, compute cosine similarity c_{i,e} = w_eᵀx_i / (||w_e||·||x_i||). Per micro-batch, expert e retains tokens in P_e^τ = {i : c_{i,e} ≥ τ}; if more than C_e qualify, it keeps the top-C_e by similarity (Top_min(C, |P_e^τ|)); otherwise all threshold-passing tokens are retained. A rejected assignment on one expert does not remove the token from its other selected experts. Two policies handle rejections: **Strict Token Drop** zeroes the gate (token still flows through its other accepted paths); **Token-Expert Reroute** redirects the assignment to another candidate expert that satisfies the threshold and has spare capacity. Shared experts process all tokens untouched.

**Training objective.** Task loss plus the LocMoE locality loss: KL divergence between the current expert-assignment distribution and the fully-localized assignment distribution over the same EP rank (λ_loc = 0.01), softly encouraging locally-deployed experts.

**Theory.** Under A1 (exposure distribution with discriminative-token probability π_e; spherical reference model giving exposure-conditioned margin δ), A2 (a witness token reaches the ranking pool with prob. ≥ 1−η), and A3 (conditional competitor-rank bound with sum ≤ μ), Theorem 1 defines ℓ = log(1/(ε−η)) and B_e = min{N_e, 1 + μ + ℓ/3 + sqrt(2μℓ + ℓ²/9)}, proves C_CIPHER ≤ B_e, and derives C_natural ≥ ((1−ε)a₀/βE)·e^{G(d,δ)} with G(d,δ) = −(d−1)/2·log(1−δ²). Illustrative regimes (Table 7): δ = 0.05 needs d ≥ 6,586 for a 2× reduction and d ≥ 7,872 for 10×; δ = 0.07 needs d ≥ 3,357 / 4,012. Appendices add reroute retention guarantees and output-fidelity bounds (||Δoutput|| ≤ 2VL(1−r) for strict drop).

**Configuration.** Capacity factor 8 (capacity limit as a multiple of uniform expert load) for both variants; reroute additionally uses a full-target factor of 4; τ = 0.01 was selected for the LocMoE baseline from {0.003, 0.01, 0.02, 0.03} on DeepSeek-V4-Flash. Baselines: vanilla MoE training (official Megatron recipes), fixed routing, and LocMoE.

## Results & Highlights

- **DeepSeek-V3 (671B), OR SFT**: CIPHER-strict reaches S_train = 1.94× vs vanilla MoE training with weighted OR average 61.40 vs baseline 60.60 (+0.80; NL4Opt 83.74 vs 83.39, B4O-Feas 61.34 vs 58.43); CIPHER-reroute is 1.68×, and LocMoE (τ=0.01) is 1.75× with weighted 61.46.
- **GLM-5 (744B), OR SFT**: CIPHER-strict achieves the best quality and speed: NL4Opt 90.66 vs 85.47 baseline (+5.19), weighted average 63.86 vs 63.06, at 1.14× speedup; LocMoE scores 62.51 at 1.12×, CIPHER-reroute 62.75 at 1.10×.
- **DeepSeek-V4-Flash (284B)**: CIPHER-strict 1.42× OR speedup vs LocMoE (τ=0.01) at 1.12×; MedMCQA accuracy 77.36 vs 76.60 baseline (1.50× speedup), SIQA 54.81 vs 51.84 (1.58×). Per-step training time speeds up by up to 1.47× (Fig. 3), with 1.38–1.42× average step-time speedup; allocated memory stays around 87.5% of device capacity; strict/reroute overheads are only 1.57% / 3.85% of total training time.
- **Trillion scale (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, 1.6T)**: vanilla MoE training OOMs before the first iteration; CIPHER-MoE trains stably at global batch size 8192 and beats the fixed-router baseline on NL4Opt 92.04 vs 89.27 (+2.77) and B4O-OR 59.14 vs 54.31 (+4.83), weighted 69.02 vs 68.59 — though B4O-Feas drops to 67.44 vs 74.42 (−6.98).
- **Workload balance**: CIPHER-strict cuts peak expert traffic from 83.6% (baseline) to 18.7% (Fig. 7); the abstract reports up to 64-percentage-point Top-1 expert workload reduction.
- **General-capability transfer**: after SIQA SFT, DeepSeek-V3 + CIPHER-strict improves the macro-average over ARC, BBH, GPQA-D, HellaSwag, MMLU-Pro by 4.3% relative to baseline (e.g., HellaSwag 87.61 vs 78.87, MMLU-Pro 73.99 vs 71.67, Table 6).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The ingredients are not new individually: capacity factors date to sparse MoE's origins (Shazeer et al., 2017), LocMoE already derives capacity from token-expert angular relationships, and Expert Choice Routing showed token-side selection balances workloads. The genuine deltas are (a) applying capacity control on the expert side *after* a preserved Top-K router on modern sigmoid-gate models, (b) the drop-vs-reroute ablation, and (c) a clean capacity-separation theorem — an incremental but practically useful combination rather than a conceptual breakthrough.

**Rigor of the theory.** Theorem 1 rests on strong assumptions: an isotropic spherical reference model, exposure-conditioned candidate pools, and conditional ranking bounds. The authors candidly admit the theorem "does not guarantee unchanged training loss or final model quality," that Table 7 values are "illustrative rather than fitted," and that "nominal hidden width or total parameter count does not by itself certify the effective spherical dimension of an anisotropic learned representation." No experiment directly validates the theorem's constants on a real checkpoint; the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B failure (hidden dim 2,048 < required 3,357) is consistent with, but does not prove, the theory.

**Quality claims are not uniform.** Table 2's caption says CIPHER "achieves the optimal speedup and quality," but on DeepSeek-V4-Flash OR the CIPHER-strict weighted average (68.59) is *below* both the vanilla baseline (69.09) and LocMoE τ=0.01 (69.46); on DeepSeek-V3 OR, LocMoE (61.46) edges CIPHER-strict (61.40). The consistent win is speedup (1.42× vs 1.12×; 1.94× vs 1.75×), not per-benchmark quality — the paper's headline is defensible but the "optimal quality" wording overreaches on one of three models.

**Missing ablations.** The affinity threshold τ for CIPHER itself is never swept (only the LocMoE baseline's τ is tuned over four values), the capacity factor 8 is fixed without sensitivity analysis, and the theory's per-expert budget B_e is not instantiated as an adaptive per-layer/per-step capacity — the practice uses one static factor. No comparison against Global-Batch Balancing or Expert-Choice routing, and no system-level baseline (Tutel/MegaScale) run on equal footing.

**Scope and comparability quirks.** Despite the "trillion-scale MoE training" title, all experiments are domain-specific SFT (OR, MedMCQA, SIQA) — the skewed workloads motivating the method are induced by post-training data, and nothing is shown for pretraining. On DeepSeek-V4-Pro the only surviving algorithmic comparator is the fixed router (vanilla OOMs) — a weak baseline by necessity — and the efficiency table compares different global batch sizes (256 vs 8192), making the 45.16 s vs 538.65 s per-iteration latencies non-comparable. Reproducibility is limited until code release given 671B–1.6T checkpoints.

## Strengths

- Unmatched evaluation scale for an algorithmic load-balancing paper: 284B–1.6T across four model families and three domains, with end-to-end (not simulated) training-time measurements.
- The strict-drop-beats-reroute finding is counterintuitive, consistently replicated across models and datasets, and given a plausible gradient-interference explanation.
- Overheads are quantified rather than ignored (1.57%/3.85% compute overhead; memory ≤ 87.5% of device capacity), and the 1.6T OOM-rescue case is a compelling practical artifact.
- An honest negative result (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, <5% gain) tied explicitly back to the theory's dimension-margin condition, plus general-capability checks on 10 benchmarks after every SFT rather than in-domain scores alone.
- Clear motivation via Table 1: fixed routing trades 1.37× latency for −4.15 NL4Opt on DeepSeek-V4-Flash, quantifying the balance-vs-fidelity tension the method targets.

## Limitations

- SFT-only evidence; no pretraining or RL-stage experiments where routing statistics differ fundamentally.
- Theory assumes isotropic representations; no empirical verification of the margin δ or spherical concentration on real models, and the theorem guarantees signal retention, not loss or quality invariance.
- Static configuration: single capacity factor 8, unswept affinity threshold for CIPHER itself, no adaptive/learned per-expert budgets despite the theory providing them.
- Baseline set is thin on the algorithmic side (only LocMoE); no Expert-Choice, Global-Batch Balancing, or system-level comparisons; code not yet released and models are proprietary, so independent verification is expensive.
- Speedups vary widely (1.10×–1.94×); on GLM-5 the gain is modest (1.14×), and below ~3.3K hidden dimensions the method provides <5% benefit per the authors' own test.

## Relevance

对关注大规模 LLM 训练效率与 MoE 系统的读者，这是少有的在真实 1.6T 模型上验证的算法级负载均衡工作，strict-drop 优于 reroute 的结论与"相似度排序可指数级降低专家容量需求"的定理都值得在自家训练栈中复现验证；但它只覆盖 SFT 场景，预训练阶段的适用性仍是空白。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does CIPHER-MoE help (or hurt) during pretraining, where expert specialization is still forming and no stable affinity structure exists for the threshold to exploit?
- Can the theoretical per-expert budget B_e be instantiated as an adaptive, per-layer/per-step capacity instead of a static capacity factor of 8, and how sensitive is the speedup-quality tradeoff to that factor?
- What is the sensitivity of CIPHER's own affinity threshold τ? The paper sweeps τ only for the LocMoE baseline.
- Why exactly does strict dropping beat rerouting — can the gradient-interference hypothesis be tested directly (e.g., measuring update-distribution shift on rerouted assignments)?
- Does the isotropic-sphere assumption hold on real checkpoints? Measuring the effective spherical dimension of learned expert gating vectors would close the theory-practice gap.
- Since CIPHER-strict still leaves 18.7% peak expert traffic, would composing it with lightweight system-level replication recover the remaining tail without the full complexity of migration-based approaches?
