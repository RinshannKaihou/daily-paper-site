Key Metric · Training-free Rescue

51.7%

peak · Llama-ja · α = 1.0

of Llama’s cross-lingual two-hop failures recovered by amplifying the general BRH pool — zero parameter updates. Layer-matched random heads manage 10.2%; mean across languages 45.3%.

Why This Matters

Settles whether multilingual LLMs share one reasoning circuit — and shows inference-time steering, not fine-tuning, can repair cross-lingual failures.

Critical Edge

The headline near-zero overlap is partly true by construction: the general pool is masked before specific pools are selected, so disjointness is enforced, not discovered.

And the payoff is bounded: intra-lingual rescue tops out at 17.2%, while amplification taxes already-correct items (89.5% / 87.5% accuracy at α = 1.0).

Methodology — a three-stage hunt for the routing circuit

  1. Dataset built for composition, not memory 82,020 Wikidata two-hop items (A→r1→B→r2→C) in EN/KO/ZH/JA/ES via per-language templates, spanning word order, family and script. A knowledge-verified pool keeps only items whose two hops are known and whose shortcut controls fail — failures must be compositional, not factual gaps.
  2. Stage 1 · Localize the module Cross-language residual patching: MLP curves track each other (r = 0.989 / 0.955) but attention fans out (r = 0.34 / 0.386) → the action is in attention heads.
  3. Stage 2 · Three filters find the heads (i) LAHIS gradient scoring combined as BRS = z(I_FH) + z(I_TH) − z(I_SH) — heads that matter for two-hop but not when B is given; (ii) per-head mean ablation on 450 correct prompts, ΔNLL > 0 required; (iii) Patchscopes check that the head decodes the bridge entity. Intersecting 5 languages gives the general pool; the remainder yields per-language pools.
  4. Stage 3 · Causal verification Jaccard overlap across all 10 language pairs; set mean-ablation vs layer-matched random sets (20 repeats, 1,000-resample bootstrap CIs); training-free amplification ×(1+α), α ≤ 1.0, on failing items.

Layer bands differ sharply: Llama clusters its heads in layers 14–38, Qwen in 47–57. Cost is bounded but real — correct two-hop items dip to 89.5% (Llama) / 87.5% (Qwen) at α = 1.0; balanced sets still net positive (+90/−22 and +57/−24).

Strengths

  • The BRS contrast (FH + TH − SH) isolates chaining-specificity; the naive TH-only score yields a 60% larger, less specific pool.
  • Knowledge-verified pool + shortcut negatives make “routing, not knowledge” airtight: EN-success ∩ X-fail items start at 0% accuracy by construction.
  • Layer-matched random controls (20 repeats) with bootstrap CIs — the 39–89× margins are not against strawmen.
  • The paper measures its own side-effects (MMLU, correct-item cost, threshold sensitivity) — rare self-audit hygiene.
  • Cross-model layer bands (14–38 vs 47–57) converge independently with the Stage-1 patching curves.

Limitations

  • Two ~70B models only, differing along confounded axes (pretraining mix, tokenizer, post-training).
  • Wikidata factual chaining only — no text-grounded multi-hop (HotpotQA/Musique), no low-resource languages.
  • Set-level evidence: mean ablation can hide heterogeneity; a few heads may dominate the small pools.
  • Permissive Patchscopes pass (≥1 of 3): stricter criteria shrink Llama’s ja pool from 31 to 13 heads.
  • Amplification capped at α = 1.0 and taxes correct items; the “under-weighting” mechanism itself stays unconfirmed (output norms ~1% apart — directional, not magnitude).
  • Source entity kept in English throughout: English-anchored reasoning, not fully native querying.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the general-vs-specific allocation track pretraining language mix once architecture, tokenizer and post-training are controlled?
  2. Do BRH-like circuits exist for text-grounded multi-hop, where the bridge entity is never a single token?
  3. Can per-head decomposition find a small dominant subset — cheaper amplification with less collateral damage?
  4. Is steering BRH output directions, or gating amplification by a failure detector, more surgical than global scaling?
  5. Why does Spanish saturate at α = 0.5 while other languages keep improving to α = 1.0 — script overlap, typological proximity, or pretraining exposure?
