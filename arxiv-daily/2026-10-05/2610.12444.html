Why this matters

Unbiased state-space rounding can still silently destabilize LLM pretraining; this is a borrowable numerical-analysis playbook for finding and fixing it at zero memory cost.

Critical edge

The 70% headline is the best of seven size×family cells — Llama-style gains run as low as 16.6%, and the propositions carry no end-to-end convergence guarantee.

Methodology

quantizer = codebook C · rounding coordinate φ · rule ρ
mt, vtAdamW moments → 4-bit codebookblock-128 + FP32 absmax → round at φthe paper’s design axis → next updateh(v) feeds the step

TorchAO rounds in state space (φ = v); this paper rounds in preconditioner space (φ = h(v) = 1/√(v̂/(1−β₂)) + ε) — the coordinate the update actually consumes.

  • Zero-cell theory. Prop 1: State-SR rounds near-zero v to zero with Θ(1) probability → next-step preconditioner error diverges as Θ(ε⁻¹); Update-SR bounds it at Θ(ε). Prop 2: on a scalar quadratic, State-RTN locks into a two-cycle while Update-RTN converges geometrically.
  • ZIP-SR — zero-inclusive Dyn4 second-moment codebook + Update-SR.
  • ZE-EDEN — zero-excluding Dyn4-NZ codebook + guarded EDEN block calibration that rescales quantized blocks (embedding Log-RMSE 0.252 → 0.028 at 834M).
  • Shared first-moment recipe. NF4-RTN everywhere; the LM-head first moment switches to NF4-SR for the final 10% of training — a patch for a 2.7B late-cooldown divergence traced to a negative LM-head radial bias under RTN.
  • Evidence base. GPT-style 162M–2.7B + Llama-style 130M–1.1B pretraining (3 paired seeds, FineWeb-Edu), component attribution at 834M, Tulu 3 SFT on Qwen3-8B / Llama-3.2-3B (5 paired seeds).

Strengths

what holds up
  • Reframes a design choice prior work treated as fixed (rounding coordinate), with clean, checkable propositions whose predictions are confirmed in controlled runs.
  • Strictly better accuracy at identical storage to the deployed TorchAO baseline — no memory/quality tradeoff to argue.
  • Excellent diagnostics: matched-checkpoint interventions and a radial-bias metric give a mechanistic story for the 2.7B RTN instability.
  • Multi-scale, multi-seed paired-difference evaluation with SDs; honest handling of the divergent baseline endpoint.
  • Open-source implementation with training scripts.

Limitations

read before adopting
  • Theory proves only one-step conditional bias on a toy scalar quadratic — no end-to-end convergence guarantee for the quantized optimizer.
  • Gains are family-dependent: 16.6%–70.1%, consistently smaller for Llama-style; untested beyond 2.7B pretraining / 8B SFT.
  • The LM-head SR fix is a correlated, not causal, patch; its final-10% phase boundary is not ablated.
  • Only one same-structure baseline (TorchAO); no comparison against GaLore, Adam-mini, COAT, or concurrent log-coordinate SR work.
  • At 8B SFT the four downstream benchmarks show no consistent win — practical significance rests on validation loss.
  • AdamW’s two moments only; Muon/Shampoo-style optimizers are named as future work.

Questions for Further Exploration

open threads from the source
  1. Does Update-SR’s advantage survive alongside BF16 weights and FP8 activations, where error budgets are already tight?
  2. Is the LM-head radial-bias instability specific to tied embeddings, or does it hit other gradient-norm-heavy tensors (e.g. MoE routers) at larger scale?
  3. Can the one-step Θ(ε) bounds be lifted to multi-step drift bounds that predict gap magnitudes, not just their ordering?
  4. Does the fix hold under non-WSD schedules (e.g. cosine), given the instability appears precisely during cooldown?
  5. Does preconditioner-space rounding transfer to Muon/Shampoo, where the preconditioner is a matrix rather than a diagonal?
