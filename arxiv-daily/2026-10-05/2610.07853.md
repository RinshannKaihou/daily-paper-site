> **一句话总结：** 作者审计了 Falcon-E、BitNet b1.58 2B-4T、BitCPM 三家实验室的官方导出流水线，发现 fp32 潜权重微调三元模型后，导出时的一次 bf16 取整会把落在阈值舍入单元内的码字回滚——低学习率端点上贪婪 GSM8K strict 准确率从 58.79% 跌到 0.78%（Falcon-E-1B-Base）、从 36.13% 跌到 0.39%（BitCPM-CANN-0.5B），BitNet 2B-4T 的 bf16 保存-重载损失 27.54 分；直接写出训练量化器码本（fixed export）或对 bf16 输入做逐坐标投影（projection）两种修复在三个模型上均满足 4 分非劣性判据。

> **TL;DR:** Auditing the documented export paths of Falcon-E (THI's onebitllms), BitNet b1.58 2B-4T (Microsoft), and BitCPM-CANN (OpenBMB), the authors show a single bf16 cast silently reverts most ternary codes changed by low-learning-rate fine-tuning: on 512 GSM8K dev prompts, greedy strict accuracy collapses from 58.79% to 0.78% for Falcon-E-1B-Base and from 36.13% to 0.39% for BitCPM-CANN-0.5B relative to online fp32-latent evaluation, while BitNet 2B-4T loses 27.54 strict points yet gains 7.62 under a last-number rule. Two remedies — writing the training quantizer's codes directly (fixed export) or iteratively adjusting bf16 inputs so the unchanged tools emit those codes (projection) — are non-inferior (4-point margin) to online evaluation on all three models.

## 核心贡献

- **发布审计（release audit）**：对三家实验室的匹配发布做 fp32 重量化，发现发布权重与部署码本不一致：Falcon-E 各版本 1.07–1.78%、BitCPM-CANN 0.83%、BitNet 2B-4T 1.5301%（双向）；Falcon-E/BitCPM 的不一致几乎全部源自 bf16 乘积恰好落在阈值 |w·r|=1/2 上、被 ties-to-even 映到 0。未修改的 onebitllms 0.0.4 导出器可逐字节复现全部四个 Falcon-E 发布——证明这是发布权重的数值特性，不是需要恢复的"丢失权重"。
- **机理解释**：低学习率下，微调新越阈值的潜权重只越过阈值 0.1–0.4 个 bf16 ULP，仍停留在原舍入单元内；导出时的 bf16 round-to-nearest 把它送回起始 cell，码字回滚。2B-4T 的全部 31,895,216 个不一致码都落在阈值 0.5 ULP 内，与这一机理一致。
- **微调端点的灾难性后果**：在按"学习率/bf16-ULP 标称比"匹配的低学习率端点上，文档化导出使 Falcon-E-1B-Base 的 GSM8K greedy strict 准确率从 58.79% 崩到 0.78%（答案格式遵循率 97.1%→21.7%），BitCPM-CANN-0.5B 从 36.13% 崩到 0.39%；BitNet 2B-4T 的 bf16 保存-重载损失 27.54 分 strict 准确率，但 last-number 规则下反而上升 7.62 分（评分规则依赖）。
- **两种修复**：(1) fixed export——把 fp32 训练量化器产出的码字直接写入部署表示，替换导出步骤；(2) projection——保持实验室工具不变，逐坐标把 fp32 量化器输出向阈值内侧移动至多 8 个 bf16 ULP（相对裕度 μ=2^-16），最多 16 ULP 偏离朴素 bf16 取整，必要时配单 ULP 尺度补偿。两者在三个模型上对在线评估均非劣（4 分判据）；projection 在全部坐标上复现在线码本（358,612,992 / 1,585,446,912 / 2,084,044,800 个码字），546 个张量两次迭代内收敛，CPU 阶段约 10 分钟。
- **哪些码会被微调改变**：2B-4T 微调改变 12,905,594 个码（占 0.62%），99.97% 落在最贴近阈值的存储层级；观测上"同层提升"随初始阈值距离单调上升（<0.1 ULP 处 −0.063 → 0.5–1 ULP 处 +0.157），并在两个模型家族的随机化因果干预（fp32 中把配对坐标分开至 0.5× 与 1.5× 距离、角色互换、起始计算逐位相同）中得到支持——微调富集的是"靠近阈值"的码，而非更多有效编辑。

## Key Contributions

- A release audit of three labs' documented pipelines showing fp32 requantization of released latents disagrees with deployed ternary codes on 0.83–1.78% of codes (Falcon-E, BitCPM) and 1.530% (BitNet 2B-4T), with the unmodified onebitllms exporter reproducing all four Falcon-E releases byte-for-byte — an operational artifact, not a claim of restoration.
- A mechanism: bf16 round-to-nearest reverts any latent that fine-tuning pushed across a ternary threshold by less than a rounding cell; at low learning rates this captures ~91–96% of code additions (within 0.25 ULP of threshold).
- Demonstration that the documented export at fine-tuned endpoints destroys task accuracy (58.79%→0.78% on Falcon-E) and that two drop-in remedies — fixed export (write training-quantizer codes) and a bounded projection on bf16 inputs — meet a pre-declared 4-point strict-accuracy non-inferiority criterion on all three models.
- An observational plus randomized-intervention analysis showing fine-tuning preferentially selects codes by initial distance from the ternary threshold, not by number of useful edits.

## Methodology

**Background.** BitNet-style absmean quantization maps a latent W to codes q = round(clamp(W·r, −1, 1)) with scale a = mean|W| and r = 1/a (per-tensor for Falcon-E and 2B-4T, per-row for BitCPM; 2B-4T deploys q/a). bf16 has an 8-bit significand; near the ternary threshold the ULP is between 0.4% and 0.8% of t. When a latent sits so that |w·r| = 1/2 exactly (a bf16 representable product), `torch.round` ties-to-even maps it to 0 — the export rule changes the dominant direction without removing precision-induced disagreement.

**Release audit.** For each matched release, the released bf16 latents were quantized with fp32 absmean arithmetic (onebitllms and Transformers' online BitLinear) and compared against the deployed codes (Table 2: e.g., Falcon-E-1B-Base 28,312,333 of 1,585,446,912 codes; BitNet 2B-4T 31,895,216 of 2,084,044,800, both directions).

**Fine-tuning setup.** Each model fully fine-tuned on GSM8K (Cobbe et al.) for 300 steps, gradient accumulation 16 (4,800 rows from a 6,833-row training pool), fp32 latents, PagedAdamW8bit, 30-step warm-up, cosine schedule. Learning rates: 10^-4 for 2B-4T; 3.125×10^-5 for Falcon-E-1B-Base and BitCPM-CANN-0.5B, chosen to match 2B-4T's nominal median learning-rate-to-bf16-ULP ratio. Evaluation: 512 GSM8K dev prompts carved from the official training split, greedy zero-shot decoding; strict accuracy (first `#### N`) and a last-number rule; 95% paired row bootstrap (2,000 draws). Each comparison was declared before the run, with one or two trajectories per arm; Appendix A separates exploratory/adaptive/post-hoc analyses.

**Remedies.** (1) *Fixed export* (Experiment 26): write the codes captured when the fp32 training quantizer ran into each model's declared deployment representation, evaluate in bf16 without autocast — replaces the export step. (2) *Projection* (Experiment 27, Algorithm 1): keep each lab's tool unchanged and adjust its input — per row for qat-convert, per tensor for BitCPM-CANN; each iteration moves every mis-exported coordinate's fp32 quantizer output exactly 16 ULP (relative margin μ = 2^-16) inside the rounding threshold by at most 8 bf16 ULPs, never across zero and at most 16 ULPs from the plain cast; single-ULP compensating moves on matching coordinates restore the tool's scale; iterates accepted only if they create no new mismatch, verified against the real tool's output (2B-4T via compiled reload).

**Which codes fine-tuning changes.** Eligible coordinates lie within 2 bf16 ULP of the threshold and not exactly zero; a first-order base-gradient score b_i = −g_i·a·Δa predicts the loss reduction of the candidate code change. A "same-level lift" analysis averages positive-score share among crossovers vs. level-mates; a causal intervention moves coordinate pairs apart in the fp32 latent (one to half, one to 1.5× their distance, roles swapped across two arms, bitwise-identical starting computation), and "finite edits" revert far- vs. near-role crossovers to compare dev-loss consequences.

## Results & Highlights

- **Release audit (Table 2):** fp32 quantization of released latents disagrees with deployed codes on 1.7796% (28,312,333 / 1,585,446,912) of Falcon-E-1B-Base codes, 0.8341% (2,990,911 / 358,612,992) for BitCPM-CANN-0.5B, and 1.5301% (31,895,216 / 2,084,044,800) for BitNet 2B-4T (both directions, its historical source unobserved). The unmodified onebitllms 0.0.4 `quantize_to_bit` reproduces all four Falcon-E releases byte-for-byte.
- **Documented export at fine-tuned endpoints (GSM8K, 512 dev rows, greedy strict, vs. online; Table 3):** Falcon-E-1B-Base falls 58.79% → 0.78% (−58.01 pts, ΔCE +0.14513 nats/token; 96.3% of 4,055,607 code additions reverted); BitCPM-CANN-0.5B falls 36.13% → 0.39% (−35.74 pts, 95.5% reverted); the bf16 save-and-reload of BitNet 2B-4T loses 27.54 strict points (58.40% → 30.86%) while last-number accuracy *rises* 7.62 points — 2B-4T's harm is mostly to the fine-tuned answer-format style, not math content.
- **Scoring-rule dependence:** Falcon-E's loss shrinks to −11.72 pts under the last-number rule (58.79% → 47.07%), where the exported model still beats the base model by +23.24 [+17.97, +28.52] points — the documented state is damaged but not destroyed; a single metric would hide one lab's harm or another's.
- **Remedies (Table 4):** both fixed export and projection satisfy the pre-declared 4-point non-inferiority rule on all three models — e.g., BitCPM fixed − online = +0.39 [−1.86, +1.17] and projected − online = +0.20 [−1.17, +1.56]; against the documented state, 2B-4T's strict accuracy changes by +30.18 [+25.10, +35.16] points under the fixed export, though its last-number accuracy changes by −5.08 [−9.77, −0.38].
- **Projection cost and behavior (Table 5):** the projection moved 1.669% (BitCPM), 1.909% (Falcon-E), and 0.629% (2B-4T) of coordinates by 1–3 ULPs, reproducing the online codes on every coordinate (358,612,992 / 1,585,446,912 / 2,084,044,800); all 546 projection tensors converged within two iterations (~10 CPU-minutes for all three models); median relative L2 distance to the fp32 latents rises only from ~3×10^-4 to ~4×10^-3.
- **Learning-rate dependence:** the collapse is a low-LR endpoint property — at 32× the rate (BitCPM, 10^-4), the same pipeline leaves strict accuracy essentially unchanged (+0.20 [−2.04, +3.04]) with a small CE cost (+0.02049), and the cast then returns only 4.0% of 14,972,249 changed codes; for Falcon-E, export − online CE decays from +0.145 nats/token at 3.125×10^-5 to −0.00147 at 5×10^-4.
- **Distance-dependent selection (Table 6):** causal far-vs-near lift is +0.0372 [+0.0320, +0.0423] (2B-4T seed 1), +0.0391 [+0.0330, +0.0449] (seed 2), and +0.109 (Falcon-E, prediction +0.125, SUPPORTED but below the frozen magnitude); observational same-level lift generalizes to BoolQ (+0.035 [+0.003, +0.068]) and MATH (+0.041 [+0.026, +0.060]) beyond GSM8K's +0.094 [+0.081, +0.111]; moving a coordinate farther also lowers its crossing probability (−0.133 [−0.147, −0.119] on Falcon-E).

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors (Avichal Sahai, Nishant Raj, Animesh Srivastava) are at Ofbusiness, an Indian industrial-B2B/fintech company — an unusual provenance for a ternary-LLM systems audit, but the work's execution compensates: comparisons declared before runs with frozen decision rules, pre-registered non-inferiority margins, paired row bootstraps, a randomized causal intervention with role-swapped arms, and an appendix explicitly separating exploratory, adaptive, and post-hoc analyses (with labeled exceptions like NOT_REPLICATED and SUPPORTED-but-below-prediction). The authors audit external labs' pipelines (TII, Microsoft, OpenBMB) and deliberately scope their claims: they claim neither a new rounding mechanism nor a general export penalty. Honest negative-result reporting (e.g., failing to replicate one Falcon-E intervention at the required rate) raises credibility.

## Critical Assessment

- **Scope discipline is a strength but also a limit.** The headline 0.78% and 0.39% numbers come from learning rates specifically chosen to match a nominal LR-to-bf16-ULP ratio — a constructed regime. At 32× the rate the same BitCPM pipeline shows no detectable strict-accuracy change, so "export destroys your fine-tune" holds in a narrow, low-LR corner; practitioners running higher LRs or LoRA-style updates may never hit it. The paper says this plainly, but the title invites broader reading.
- **Evaluation set is not fresh.** The 512 GSM8K prompts are carved from the official *training* split and were used in earlier analyses; the authors flag this themselves. For the 58-point collapse this hardly matters (an artifact cannot plausibly produce exactly 0.78%), but the subtler remedy comparisons (±2-4 point margins) ride on bootstrap intervals over 512 possibly-contaminated rows, and results are sensitive to batch configuration per their own limitations section.
- **BitNet 2B-4T's audit is necessarily weaker.** Its original fp32 master and export history are unavailable, so its 1.53% release disagreements can only be described as "compatible with rounding," not attributed; and the remedies' 2B-4T fixed-export arm was evaluated while sharing a GPU with a production job, with no continuous checks — sampled occupancy cannot prove exclusivity. Honest disclosure, but a reproducibility wrinkle in an otherwise forensic-quality paper.
- **Non-inferiority is not equivalence.** The 4-point margin is resolution-based, not an equivalence bound; the intervals still include losses of up to 1.95–2.73 points (fixed export) and 1.17–2.54 points (projection), and the projected 2B-4T model sits −1.76 [−4.10, +0.59] from online — the projection "reproduces codes" but not provably the trained model's behavior (CE differs detectably for BitCPM, +0.00013).
- **Tool-version fragility.** The projection's guarantees hold only for the exact tool versions on this host; recomputing the codes on another backend moved up to 852 threshold decisions, and the Falcon-E fine-tunes ran through Transformers' AutoBitLinear rather than THI's Triton BitNetLinear, so the lab's training stack is not replicated in full.
- **Novelty is deliberately modest.** The mechanism (bf16 cancellation of small updates, ties-to-even at exact midpoints) is known in pieces (Zamirai et al.; Mihić & Bellowsky 2026); the contribution is the first controlled attribution of task-level damage to specific released pipelines plus tested fixes — an audit, not a new algorithm.

## Strengths

- A genuine end-to-end causal chain: release-artifact audit → exact mechanism (ULP-level, with the ties-to-even wrinkle) → catastrophic task-level consequence → two practical, verified remedies → a deployment checklist (check codes before scoring; export under the training quantizer; score more than one way).
- Pre-registration hygiene rare in systems papers: declared comparisons, frozen rules, labeled post-hoc analyses, explicit exceptions (Experiment 13 NOT_REPLICATED), and paired row bootstraps throughout.
- The projection is elegant and cheap: keeps labs' tools untouched, bounded (≤8 ULP moves, ≤16 ULPs from the plain cast), converges in two iterations, and reproduces the online codes on *every* coordinate — verified against the tools' own output.
- The randomized distance-selection intervention (role-swapped pairs, bitwise-identical starting compute) converts an observational correlation into causal evidence in two model families.
- Scoring-rule analysis (strict vs. last-number) exposes that 2B-4T's "accuracy restored" story is metric-dependent — the kind of honesty most deployment papers omit.

## Limitations

- One recipe family (GSM8K full fine-tune, 300 steps), one or two trajectories per arm, 512 training-split dev prompts under greedy decoding; no fresh held-out evaluation and sensitivity to batch configuration.
- Constructed low-LR regime: the dramatic failures occur at learning rates matched by nominal LR/ULP ratio; higher-rate endpoints show no detectable strict-accuracy harm, so external validity to common fine-tuning practice is untested.
- BitNet 2B-4T's release audit is circumstantial (no fp32 master available), and parts of its evaluation shared a GPU with an unrelated production job.
- Remedies are demonstrated at one existing endpoint per model, one attempt each; non-inferiority at a resolution-based 4-point margin is not equivalence, and the projection holds only for these tool versions and this host.
- Kernel coverage is PyTorch bf16 on GPUs only — not bfloat cpp, CANN, or integer kernels; RL fine-tuning exposure to export reversion remains a hypothesis.

## Relevance

对任何微调并部署 BitNet/Falcon-E/BitCPM 类三元模型的人来说，这是一份可直接落地的工程警示：低学习率微调带来的码字修改大部分落在 bf16 舍入单元内，标准导出会无声回滚并可能摧毁任务准确率。论文给出的两条修复路径（直接写出训练量化器码本，或对 bf16 输入做有界投影让原工具产出正确码字）以及"导出前先核对码本"的建议都很容易复现。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does export reversion also corrupt RL fine-tuning (GRPO/PPO on ternary models), where per-step latent updates are small but accumulate over many more iterations than 300 SFT steps?
- Do adapter-based updates (LoRA on fp32 latents) concentrate code changes near thresholds the same way full fine-tuning does, and does the same reversion arithmetic apply at merge time?
- Would a "rounding-aware" ternary training objective (e.g., penalizing latents within 0.5 bf16 ULP of the threshold) eliminate the reversion regime outright, removing the need for export-time projection?
- How much of the strict-vs-last-number divergence (2B-4T gaining 7.62 points while losing 27.54) reflects genuine format damage versus GSM8K-specific answer parsing — would a generative grader (LLM-as-judge) rank the documented export differently?
- Do CANN/integer-kernel backends (BitCPM's actual deployment target) exhibit the same threshold-decision drift (852 moved decisions on a second backend here), and can code-level census checks be wired into CI for ternary release pipelines?
