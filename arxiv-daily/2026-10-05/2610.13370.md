> **一句话总结：** EPFL 提出 COAX：把辅助无损负载均衡的 router bias 机制反向利用，在预训练时按输入类别给一组"偏好专家子集"加固定正偏置 σ（默认 0.03），让低资源类别（如占训练数据 0.85% 的斯瓦希里语）获得远超其 token 占比的专家容量（r_c=4 即 4 倍专家份额），在 345M/3B/10.6B 三个 MoE 规模上使斯瓦希里语验证损失下降 0.55%/0.44%/0.30%、八语言混合下 7 个少数语言平均降 0.286%（EDL 基线反升 +1.016%），且等价于给基线多喂 18.5%（345M）~2.7%（10.6B）的训练 token，推理时无需类别标签、不改动标准 top-K MoE。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces COAX (Category-Oriented Allocation of eXperts), which repurposes the per-expert selection bias from auxiliary-loss-free load balancing as a category-conditioned routing prior during MoE pretraining: each data category (language, modality, or domain) gets a designated expert subset whose top-K selection scores receive a fixed positive bias σ, giving low-resource categories a larger "preferred expert share" than their token share. On a four-language pretraining mixture (86.4% English / 0.85% Swahili) across MoE scales of 345M-A147M to 10.6B-A1.2B (10B tokens each), COAX lowers Swahili validation loss by 0.55% (345M), 0.44% (3B), and 0.30% (10.6B) versus matched-seed unbiased baselines, with English unharmed; on an eight-language 10.6B mixture it improves seven of eight minority languages (mean -0.286%) while the EDL baseline (ICLR 2026) regresses them (+1.016%) and DEMix-style hard routing is neutral (+0.047%) at 10x COAX's iteration overhead. The smallest/largest-scale gains equal 18.5%/2.7% additional baseline training tokens, and the bias is removed at inference — no category labels or architectural changes needed.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 COAX 机制**：复用 Wang et al. (2024) 为负载均衡设计的 per-expert router bias，将其改为按输入类别 c 对指定专家子集 P_c（大小 M_c）在选择阶段加正偏置 b_{c,i} = σ·1[i ∈ P_c]；top-K 选择用带偏置分数，但专家组合权重仍用原始无偏分数——是一种"软路由先验"，所有专家对 token 依然可达，保留跨类别知识共享。
- **引入 preferred expert share（q_c = M_c/N）与 relative expert share（r_c = q_c/p_c）两个可控旋钮**：例如占训练混合 0.85% 的斯瓦希里语可偏向 4/128 = 3.1% 的路由专家（r_c ≈ 3.7），实现显式的"专家容量再分配"，且不增加每 token 激活专家数 K（per-token 计算量不变）。
- **三类数据不平衡场景 × 三个模型规模的系统验证**：多语言（4 语言与 8 语言混合）、多模态（文本/视觉/音频）、多领域（代码/数学/法律/医疗），模型为 345M-A147M、3B-A654M、10.6B-A1.2B（64/128/256 个路由专家，top-8，各训 10B token，64 张 GH200，matched seeds + 90%/95% 置信区间）。
- **关键实证发现**：增益随资源水平降低而增大（越低资源语言获益越多）；保留跨类别共享的软偏置优于硬分区（DEMix 式）和路由分布散度目标（EDL）；粗粒度二分组（英语 vs 非英语）与逐语言划分效果相当甚至更好；多数类（英语）也必须加偏置，否则增益减半。
- **部署友好**：偏置只在预训练期起作用，推理时移除后保留绝大部分增益（标准 top-K MoE、无需类别标签）；训练开销随规模骤降至 10.6B 的 1.3–2.4%，而其收益等价于 2.7% 额外基线训练 token——训练收益大于训练成本。

## Key Contributions

- **COAX**: a category-conditioned expert-selection bias that repurposes load-balancing router biases (Wang et al., 2024) to give each data category a soft "preferred expert subset" during pretraining, controlling expert-capacity allocation through an explicit preferred-expert-share knob (r_c) without changing per-token compute (K stays fixed).
- **A comprehensive empirical study** across three imbalance regimes (multilingual, multimodal, multi-domain) at three MoE scales (345M-A147M, 3B-A654M, 10.6B-A1.2B), with matched seeds, paired-difference confidence intervals, and honest training-token-equivalence accounting (gains = 18.5% extra tokens at 345M, 2.7% at 10.6B).
- **Evidence that soft, sharing-preserving specialization beats alternatives**: on the eight-language 10.6B mixture, COAX achieves -0.286% mean loss change over seven minority languages versus +0.047% for DEMix-style hard routing (at 17.57% vs 1.79% iteration overhead) and +1.016% for Expert Divergence Learning.
- **Analysis of the induced structure**: COAX does not sharpen the learned router (final entropy 4.492 vs 4.422 baseline, retaining ~83% of baseline entropy reduction) but makes functionality functionally localized — disabling the 4 Swahili-designated experts raises Swahili loss by 107.1% versus only 9.3% when disabling the experts the baseline uses most for Swahili.
- **Practical deployment story**: the bias can be dropped at inference with most gains retained (standard top-K MoE, no category labels), complements conventional data upsampling (-3.738% combined vs -3.579% upsampling alone), and works for directing newly added capacity in continual pretraining (code -0.177%, vision -0.066% new-category loss).

## Methodology

**Mechanism.** In a standard top-K MoE, the router computes affinity scores s_{i,t} = G(u_t^T e_i) for each token t and expert i, selects the top-K experts, and weights them by normalized scores. COAX augments only the selection step: for a token from category c, biased scores s̃_{i,t} = s_{i,t} + b_{c,i} with b_{c,i} = σ·1[i ∈ P_c] determine which K experts are chosen (ã_t = TopK(s̃_t, K)), but combination weights use the original unbiased scores. This makes COAX a soft prior — any expert remains reachable if learned scores outweigh σ — and preserves cross-category knowledge sharing. The bias applies at every MoE layer during pretraining only; at inference it is removed, yielding a standard MoE that needs no category labels. Following the load-balancing bias of Wang et al. (2024), σ is a fixed hyperparameter (default 0.03), shared across categories; expert subsets P_c are mutually exclusive by default.

**Capacity knob.** The preferred expert share q_c = M_c/N and relative expert share r_c = q_c/p_c (expert share over token share) let the developer allocate capacity independently of data prevalence; r_c > 1 means category c is biased toward more experts than its data fraction would suggest.

**Experimental setup.** Three MoE scales — 345M-A147M (64 experts), 3B-A654M (128 experts), 10.6B-A1.2B (256 experts) — all with 32 layers, one shared expert, top-8 sigmoid routing, GQA, SwiGLU, trained with Megatron-LM on 64 GH200 GPUs for 10B tokens (sequence length 4096, WSD schedule, auxiliary load-balancing loss coefficient 1e-3). Settings: (1) multilingual — a four-language mix (86.42% English, 10% Korean, 2.73% Urdu, 0.85% Swahili; r_c = 4 for minorities, English residual ≈ 0.8) and a practical eight-language mix at 10.6B (63.24% English through Khmer at 6%); (2) multimodal — 75% text / 12.5% vision / 12.5% audio (audio-only, ASR, interleaved; vision and audio get r_c = 2, text residual ≈ 0.7); (3) domains — 85% English, 5% code, 5% math, 2.5% legal, 2.5% medical. Evaluation is relative change in held-out validation loss (Δ%, negative better) against matched-seed unbiased baselines, with 3–5 seeds at 345M and 4 seeds elsewhere; comparisons include DEMix-style hard routing and Expert Divergence Learning (EDL). Ablations cover bias strength α ∈ [0.003, 0.3], expert-share sensitivity, layer placement, load-balancing interaction, training horizon (up to 100B tokens), majority-category biasing, and data-vs-capacity allocation.

## Results & Highlights

- **Gains grow as languages become lower-resource (four-language mix, validation loss Δ%)**: Swahili (0.85% of data) improves -0.55% (345M), -0.44% (3B), and -0.30% (10.6B) versus matched-seed unbiased baselines (all significant at 95% CI); Urdu (2.73%) improves -0.23%/-0.38%/-0.22%; Korean (10%) changes only -0.01%/-0.18%/-0.13%; English (86.42%) is essentially unaffected (-0.05%/-0.09%/-0.09%).
- **Beats both specialization baselines on the practical eight-language 10.6B mixture**: COAX improves seven of eight languages (mean over seven minorities -0.286%; Georgian -0.576%, Tamil -0.418%, Swahili -0.486%, Urdu -0.308%), while EDL increases loss for every language (mean +1.016% at β=5×10⁻⁴; still +0.369% at its best β) and DEMix-style hard routing is neutral (+0.047%) despite a 17.57% iteration-time overhead versus COAX's 1.79%; dropping the hard constraint at evaluation catastrophically regresses +251%.
- **Training-token equivalence**: at the smallest scale (345M multimodal) COAX's gains equal 18.5% additional baseline training tokens (baseline needs 12.1B vs 10B tokens to catch up), and at 10.6B (two-group multilingual configuration) 2.7% (3.8% if the bias is kept active at inference) — while COAX's throughput overhead falls sharply with scale, from +20.2% wall-clock at 345M to +2.4% at 10.6B and +1.3% with the coarser expert grouping.
- **Multimodal validation loss improves across all six sub-sets at 345M** (r_c = 2 for vision/audio): text -0.88%, image-to-text captioning -0.76%, text-to-image -0.59%, audio-only -0.48%, audio-text -0.38%, interleaved -0.65%; the gains transfer to teacher-forced task metrics — ASR top-5 word accuracy rises from 47.50% to 49.38% (+1.88) and captioning top-5 word accuracy from 78.52% to 79.57%, with all twelve token/word-level top-k metrics improved on the 345M model.
- **Functional localization confirms real specialization**: removing the 4 Swahili-designated experts (of 128 per layer, 3B model) increases Swahili loss by +107.1% (COAX α=0.03) vs +9.3% when removing the experts the unbiased baseline routes most Swahili traffic through; Swahili traffic through its designated experts reaches 12.15% vs the 3.12% uniform-random rate (26.96% under annealed σ=0.3), while English/Korean/Urdu losses change ≤0.19%.
- **Scale and ablation behavior**: with the inference-time bias removed, gains peak at α=0.03 (minority mean -0.42% on the 3B four-language mix) and turn catastrophically negative at α=0.3 (+19.32% from train-test mismatch) — linearly annealing α=0.3 to zero during cooldown recovers 76.4% of the with-bias gain (-1.26% vs -1.65%); a coarse two-group partition (English vs all non-English) matches or beats per-language partitioning (e.g., Swahili -0.20% vs -0.49% per-lang at 10.6B, at 1.3% vs 2.4% overhead); gains persist undecayed through 100B training tokens (+0.118 pp advantage at 100B), and doubling Urdu/Swahili data plus COAX gives -3.738% minority mean vs -3.579% for upsampling alone.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is modest and honestly framed.** The core mechanism is a direct repurposing of the expert-selection bias that Wang et al. (2024) introduced for auxiliary-loss-free load balancing; COAX's algorithmic delta is conditioning the fixed bias on the input category instead of expert underuse. The paper's real contribution is empirical and analytical — demonstrating that capacity allocation (not router sharpening, since COAX's router entropy stays flatter: 4.492 vs 4.422) is the operative lever, and mapping out where it works. Practitioners get a near-free knob; researchers learn that prior category-routing objectives (EDL) optimize the wrong quantity in long-tailed regimes.

**All headline evidence is validation loss; downstream evaluation is underpowered and the authors say so.** MultiBLiMP differences are small and inconsistent across scoring schemes (both models sit at 21.6–47.0% accuracy, Belebele is at the 25.10% chance level for both, SIB-200 identical at 25.10%), and free-running generation is degenerate at this scale (greedy ASR exceeds 100% WER, captioning CIDEr-D ≈ 0.032 for both arms). The task-level wins (ASR/captioning top-k accuracy) come only from the smallest 345M model under teacher forcing. So the claim "COAX improves low-resource categories" is, strictly, "improves held-out loss at ≤10.6B/10B-token scale" — a reasonable but bounded claim.

**Absolute gains are small and one language consistently regresses.** The flagship eight-language result is a -0.286% mean minority validation-loss improvement — the training-token-equivalence framing (2.7% at 10.6B) is more interpretable, but 2.7% is a modest dividend. Khmer is worse in every COAX run at 10.6B (+0.254% at the reference checkpoint; the only consistent exception in the 100B stress test too), and the byte-fallback-tokenization explanation, while plausible, is post-hoc — the paper cannot predict which categories will misbehave.

**The multi-domain setting undercuts the "no inference change" selling point.** Unlike multilingual/multimodal results, without the inference-time bias only code consistently improves (10.6B: -0.19%); math, legal, and medical gains materialize only when the bias is retained at inference, which reintroduces category-label dependence and train/test mismatch risk. The paper reports this but the abstract's "bias can be removed" message does not carry to all three settings.

**Two controls have acknowledged confounds.** The data-reduction experiment (F.2) removes 33.7% of total training tokens, so "reducing majority data doesn't reproduce COAX" (+2.015% minority regression) is directional, not quantitative; and the EDL/hard-routing baselines are the authors' own reimplementations (EDL did get a 3-value β sweep). None of this overturns the conclusions, but the baselines' poor showing should be read with that provenance in mind.

**Reproducibility.** No code release is mentioned. Architectures, data sources (FineWeb-Edu, FineWeb-2, LLaVA-OneVision, People's Speech, GigaSpeech, Libriheavy, StarCoder, FineMath3+, MultiLegalPile, Biomed-enriched), and hyperparameters are documented in enough detail (appendices A, E–G) that a well-resourced lab could replicate, but 64-GH200 × multiple seeds × three scales is a heavy bill for independent verification.

## Strengths

- Extremely clean core idea with an explicit, interpretable control (relative expert share r_c) that decouples expert-capacity allocation from data prevalence without touching per-token compute.
- Rigorous evaluation culture: matched seeds, paired-difference confidence intervals, multi-seed variance, a 100B-token run refuting the under-training artifact, throughput measured with an A/B/A/B protocol, and training-token-equivalence framing that makes tiny Δ% numbers interpretable.
- Comprehensive ablations that actually locate the mechanism: coarse grouping sufficiency (Table 5), majority-category bias being necessary (Table 20: -0.286% → -0.131% without it), functional-modularity lesion tests (Table 11), router-entropy analysis (Figure 3), and load-balancing interaction (Table 18).
- Honest negative space: Khmer regressions, EDL comparisons, the compute confound in the data-reduction control, and underpowered downstream benchmarks are all disclosed rather than buried.
- Deployment-friendly: standard top-K MoE at inference, no category labels, 1.3–2.4% training overhead at 10.6B, complementary with data upsampling, and usable in continual pretraining for steering newly added experts.

## Limitations

- Primary evidence is held-out validation loss at ≤10.6B / ≤100B-token scale; downstream benchmarks are underpowered at this scale, and free-running generation quality could not be meaningfully measured, so transfer to real model quality is unproven.
- Absolute gains are modest (mean -0.286% minority validation loss at 10.6B; equivalent to 2.7% extra training tokens) and shrink with scale (Swahili: -0.55% at 345M → -0.30% at 10.6B), so the benefit at frontier scale remains extrapolative.
- The multi-domain regime breaks the inference-time-removal story: math/legal/medical gains require keeping the bias (and category labels) at inference; only code benefits persist without it.
- Khmer consistently regresses at 10.6B with only a post-hoc tokenization-based explanation; the method offers no way to predict or protect categories that will be hurt.
- Domain and language experiments use mutually exclusive expert subsets and require choosing per-category r_c values; although the paper shows coarse grouping and r_c ≥ 2 robustness, the allocation still must be specified from data statistics rather than learned.
- No code release stated; replication requires substantial compute (64 GH200s, multi-seed, three scales).

## Relevance

这篇论文与 MoE 训练效率和多语言/低资源 LLM 预训练方向高度相关：它把负载均衡中现成的 router bias 变成几乎零成本的类别→专家容量分配旋钮，是继数据上采样、专家数扩展之外第三条改善长尾类别性能的实用路线，且推理侧完全无侵入，对做多语言或多模态 MoE 预训练的团队有直接的可操作价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does COAX's benefit survive at frontier scale (≥100B total parameters, ≥1T training tokens), where the per-scale trend shows gains shrinking from 345M to 10.6B?
- Can the preferred expert share r_c be learned or allocated automatically from data statistics and validation feedback, instead of hand-set per category — and can categories at risk of regression (like Khmer) be detected before training?
- Why do domain gains (math/legal/medical) require the inference-time bias while language/modality gains do not — is this a property of how domain knowledge is distributed across shared expert structure?
- Would combining COAX with the auxiliary-loss-free balancing scheme it borrows from (rather than the standard auxiliary loss used here) change the picture, given that stronger load balancing suppresses COAX's gains (Table 18)?
- Does category-conditioned capacity allocation compound with expert-parallel topology constraints (the authors' own future-work question about systems-aware biasing), and can the annealing schedule (α→0 during cooldown, recovering 76.4% of the with-bias gain) be tuned per category?
