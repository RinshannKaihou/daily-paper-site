> **一句话总结：** 提出「单元级 ⊂ 正交 ⊂ 线性」的嵌套模块性层级与统一的子空间分解优化框架，证明单神经元缺乏选择性 ≠ 网络缺乏模块化：在 tanh 多任务 RNN 中单元级消融的双重分离指数（DDI）为 0.00，而学习到的线性子空间分解将 DDI 提升至 0.47；同样，视觉模型的内容/风格与语言模型的句法/语义在子空间层面清晰分离，并与视觉皮层、语言皮层的 fMRI 活动差异化对齐。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces a formal hierarchy of modularity — unit-level (permutation) ⊂ orthogonal ⊂ linear (bounded invertible) — and a unified optimization framework that learns task-relevant subspace decompositions, evaluated by causal ablation (RNNs) or retrieval selectivity (vision/language). On Yang et al.'s 11-task cognitive RNN benchmark, tanh networks have unit-level double-dissociation index (DDI) = 0.00 and the strongest unit-selection baseline (Sinkhorn permutation) only 0.08, yet the learned linear decomposition recovers DDI = 0.47 and orthogonal 0.37. In vision (Swin-Base content-style benchmark with 99 same-content + 99 same-style distractors), Top-10 content retrieval accuracy rises from 0.0125 under the unit-level permutation baseline to 0.4884 under invertible transforms (style: 0.0546 → 0.8940), and in Pythia-410M on the Microsoft Research Paraphrase Corpus, Top-5 syntax retrieval climbs from 0.3953 to 0.9439. The same subspaces align differentially with brain data from the Natural Scenes Dataset (style → V1/V2, content → higher visual areas; semantics → language cortex).

## 核心贡献

- **形式化模块性层级**：将「模块性」拆成三层嵌套定义——单元级（置换变换，任务占据互不相交的神经元集合）、正交级（旋转后存在功能独立的子空间）、线性级（有界可逆变换即可分离，基可不正交）。三者构成诊断性嵌套：能分离的最受限变换族揭示了表征的几何组织方式。
- **统一的分解学习框架**：RNN 上用任务充分性目标（每个子空间接线性读出头直接优化任务损失 + 层级特定正则：置换用 Sinkhorn 熵、正交用 Q^T Q、可逆用条件数惩罚）；预训练视觉/语言模型上用「最小对」对比目标（同一场景不同风格、同义改写 vs 同模板换词），两个对比损失只更新 Q 的不相交列块，构造上保证几何分离。
- **因果性评估协议**：RNN 通过逐步消融子空间测任务精度，要求「选择性损伤 + 保全另一任务」的双重分离（DDI）；视觉/语言用检索选择性指标（内容/风格命中率、R_c、R_s 比值），在保留集上评估。
- **三域一致的实证结论**：缺乏单元级模块性的网络（tanh RNN、视觉/语言 transformer）普遍具有清晰的子空间级功能模块化；子空间还与脑数据差异化对齐——风格子空间更好预测 V1/V2、内容子空间更好预测高阶视觉区，语义子空间优于句法子空间预测语言皮层。
- **对神经科学的直接含义**：人类语言研究中「单个体素对句法/语义无选择性」的阴性结果不能推出「无功能分离」——分离可能存在于群体活动的子空间层面。

## Key Contributions

- A formally nested hierarchy of modularity — **unit-level (permutation) ⊂ orthogonal ⊂ linear (invertible with bounded condition number κ(Q) ≤ κ_max)** — that separates the claim "no neuron is specialized" from "the computation is not modular," showing the first can be true while the second is false.
- A **unified optimization framework** for learning decompositions: a task-sufficiency objective for RNNs (per-subspace linear readout heads trained with task loss plus hierarchy-specific regularizers: Sinkhorn entropy for permutations, Cayley parameterization for orthogonal, condition-number penalty for invertible) and a **contrastive minimal-pairs objective** for pretrained models (content vs style in vision, syntax vs semantics in language), where each contrastive loss updates disjoint coordinate blocks of Q.
- **Causal interventional evaluation** for RNNs (selective ablation requiring double dissociation on held-out data) and **retrieval-based selectivity metrics** (Top-K content/style matching, selectivity ratios R_c, R_s) for vision and language models.
- Cross-domain evidence that networks lacking unit-level modularity nevertheless carry clear **subspace-level functional modularity**, and that learned subspaces **differentially align with fMRI data** from visual and language cortex (Natural Scenes Dataset; Fedorenko words-vs-nonwords localizer).

## Methodology

**Hierarchy of linear decompositions.** Given a representation h(x) ∈ R^D at a chosen layer, the method learns Q ∈ R^{D×D} such that z = Qh(x) splits into non-overlapping coordinate blocks z = [z_A, z_B, z_res] with d_A + d_B + d_res = D, where z_A captures function A, z_B function B, and the residual keeps all other structure (explicitly retained to avoid conflating compression with decomposition). Three nested transformation families are studied: **Permutation** (pure reordering; tests axis-alignment), **Orthogonal** (Q^T Q = I; rigid rotations preserving inner products), and **Invertible** (general invertible Q with κ(Q) ≤ κ_max; non-orthogonal bases allowed). The most constrained family under which separation succeeds diagnoses the geometry of organization.

**Training objectives.** For RNNs trained on explicit tasks, a **task-sufficiency objective** trains linear readout heads f_A, f_B on each subspace against task targets: L = L_task(f_A(z_A), y^A) + L(f_B(z_B), y^B) + λ L_reg, with L_reg chosen per family (Sinkhorn entropy for permutations, Cayley parameterization for orthogonal, condition-number penalty for invertible). For pretrained vision/language models without paired task labels, a **contrastive minimal-pairs objective** constructs counterfactual inputs x_A^+ (varying along A, fixed along B) and x_B^+, then trains Q with two InfoNCE-style losses (Eq. 2, following Khosla et al. 2020) acting on disjoint blocks of z, so gradients update disjoint columns of Q by construction.

**Evaluation.** RNNs: **causal ablation** — zero out subspace z_A (or z_B) at every timestep via Q, set the coordinates to zero, map back via Q^{-1}, and measure task accuracy; functional modularity means ablating each subspace selectively impairs one computation while sparing the other (**double dissociation**, quantified by DDI). Vision/language: **retrieval-based evaluation** on held-out data — top-k nearest neighbors retrieved within each subspace, scored by whether they share the query's intended property; selectivity ratios R_c = C_c/P_c and R_s = S_s/P_s aggregate per-image selectivity (a modular decomposition yields R_c, R_s >> 1).

**Domains and data.** (1) Multitask cognitive RNNs from Yang et al. (2019): networks trained on 11 cognitive tasks (softplus and tanh activations), with the disjoint Anti vs DM1 pair as the two target functions. (2) Vision: pretrained models (Swin-Base primary; Swin transformers, ConvNeXts, VGGs, self-supervised ViTs, ResNets in the appendix) on a content-style test set where every image has 99 same-content and 99 same-style distractors, using penultimate-layer (average-pooled) representations. (3) Language: Pythia models (410M primary, 160M and larger in the appendix) on the Microsoft Research Paraphrase Corpus, with paraphrase (semantic) counterparts and same-template syntax counterparts, evaluated across layers. (4) Brain alignment: encoding models on the Natural Scenes Dataset fMRI (1,000 images, 4 subjects) using content/style subspaces as separate feature sets; language cortex activity predicted from syntax/semantic subspaces and from cross-modal caption/image subspaces.

## Results & Highlights

- **Tanh RNNs: unit-level analysis finds nothing, subspace analysis finds modularity.** On Yang et al.'s Anti vs DM1 pair, tanh networks have a broad unimodal fractional-task-variance (FTV) distribution (FTV = 0.39; 30% of units specialized), and unit-level ablation yields **DDI = 0.00** — ablation interference from collaterals overwhelms selective effects. A Sinkhorn-permutation decomposition (a stronger unit-assignment baseline than FTV ranking) reaches only **DDI = 0.08**, but the orthogonal decomposition achieves **DDI = 0.37** and the linear decomposition **DDI = 0.47** — recovering a clear double dissociation in a network where unit-level analysis finds none. Softplus networks, by contrast, are modular at all levels (FTV = 0.74, 74% of units specialized; DDI 0.59 at unit level → 0.73 orthogonal → 0.70 linear), so the level at which modularity emerges itself depends on the network's computational organization.
- **Vision (Swin-Base, content vs style): a monotone hierarchy across transformations.** Top-10 content retrieval accuracy goes from **0.0125 (permutation) → 0.2435 (orthogonal) → 0.4884 (invertible)**, and Top-10 style retrieval from **0.0546 → 0.7013 → 0.8940** (Table 1). Selectivity ratios confirm it: permutation is near chance (R_c = 1.10, R_s = 1.02), orthogonal moderate (R_c = 2.23, R_s = 2.56), invertible strong (R_c = 16.08, R_s = 3.54). The pattern generalizes across Swin transformers, ConvNeXts, VGGs, and self-supervised ViTs, with the **ResNet family the principled exception**: style retrieval is weak across all subspace types there, suggesting ResNets encode style in a form less amenable to linear separation.
- **Language (Pythia-410M, syntax vs semantics on MSR Paraphrase Corpus):** Top-5 syntax retrieval S_1 rises from **0.3953 (permutation) to 0.9286 (orthogonal) and 0.9439 (invertible)**; Top-1 semantics retrieval S_2 goes from 0.1564 to 0.6667 (orthogonal) / 0.6556 (invertible) (Table 2). Orthogonal and invertible perform comparably, while permutation fails badly (Top-10 S_1 = 0.3971 vs 0.9417 orthogonal). Selectivity is robust across all layers of the model, so the disentanglement persists through full depth rather than being confined to specific layers; the finding holds across Pythia scales in the appendix.
- **Brain alignment: subspaces, not whole representations, localize function.** On NSD fMRI (4 subjects, 1,000-image encoding evaluation), **early visual areas (V1, V2) are better predicted by the style subspace while higher visual regions are better predicted by the content subspace** — mirroring the established cortical hierarchy from texture statistics to semantics. In language cortex (words-vs-nonwords localizer), the **semantic subspace outperforms the syntactic subspace** at predicting activity, consistent with language networks being more meaning-driven; notably, the original (undecomposed) sentence representations still yield the strongest overall brain predictivity, indicating neither dimension alone fully captures language-network representations. Cross-modally, semantic representations from image captions predict visual cortex better than syntactic ones, and content representations of generated images predict language cortex as well as the original sentence representations.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The reframing is genuinely useful rather than cosmetic: the nested permutation ⊂ orthogonal ⊂ invertible hierarchy turns "is the network modular?" — an ill-posed yes/no question — into "at which level of description is it modular?", which is answerable. The distinction from distributed coding / superposition work is well argued: those literatures ask how features share neurons or coordinates, but never whether two computations are *causally independently supported*; the double-dissociation criterion supplies exactly that.

**Rigor and confounds.** The main methodological worry is that the hierarchy levels differ in optimization capacity, not just constraint: an invertible Q has strictly more freedom than an orthogonal one, so stronger "modularity" at higher levels could partly reflect the optimizer's extra degrees of freedom rather than structure already present. The authors partially address this (appendix ablations E.7/E.9 argue learned transformations reveal pre-existing structure rather than manufacturing it, and the contrastive losses act on disjoint coordinate blocks), but a control where Q is trained on shuffled/null function labels to give a false-positive rate for DDI and R_c is not reported in the main text. The tanh result is also weaker than the headline suggests: DDI = 0.47 (linear) is far from the 0.59–0.73 achieved in softplus networks, so this is a *partial* recovery, not full restoration of the modularity softplus shows at unit level.

**Paper-specific weaknesses.** (1) The strong semantic disentanglement in language is partly baked in by the data: the authors themselves note MSR Paraphrase pairs share many common tokens, so the semantic retrieval advantage is unsurprising — the syntax result is the more informative one, and it arrives with a token-overlap caveat of its own. (2) Vision and language modularity is assessed only by retrieval metrics; there is no behavioral or decoding ablation (e.g., does ablating the content subspace impair object recognition while sparing texture judgments, as demonstrated for the RNNs?) — the Discussion concedes this. (3) The ResNet exception is reported but unexplained, leaving the framework's scope conditioned on architecture in a way the title's generality does not advertise. (4) Decompositions are learned per function *pair* with sensitive non-convex optimization (subspace dimensionality, condition-number bound, learning rate all matter), so reproducibility across random seeds and hyperparameter choices is not quantified in the main text. (5) The residual subspace — most of the dimensions — is explicitly uncharacterized (Appendix E.10: it is not pure nuisance and can support task-relevant information), which limits how much of the representation's organization these two-way decompositions actually explain.

**Significance.** High for interpretability and neuroscience interface work: the causal-ablation criterion is portable, and the finding that unit-level negativity (e.g., the failure to find syntactic/semantic separation in individual language-voxels, Fedorenko-era results) is uninformative about subspace-level organization directly challenges how null results in neuroscience have been interpreted.

## Strengths

- A crisp formal contribution (nested modularity levels) that resolves an apparent contradiction between "mixed selectivity" findings and modularity intuitions, with the key claim tested causally (ablation double dissociation) rather than correlationally.
- Consistent cross-domain replication of the same qualitative pattern (RNNs, 5+ vision architectures, multiple language-model scales) using one unified optimization framework.
- The brain-alignment experiments add external, out-of-model validation: subspaces localize function in cortex (style → V1/V2, semantics → language cortex) in ways that match known hierarchies.
- Honest, thorough Discussion: the authors flag the non-convexity, the retrieval-only evaluation for vision/language, and the unexplained residual subspace themselves.

## Limitations

- Higher-capacity transformations could inflate apparent modularity; no null-label / shuffled-control calibration of DDI or selectivity ratios is reported in the main text.
- Tanh-network recovery is partial (DDI = 0.47 vs 0.59–0.73 for softplus), so "recovers modularity" should be read as graded, not binary.
- Vision/language evaluation is retrieval-based only; no behavioral ablations, no downstream-task causal tests for pretrained models.
- Two-function decompositions only; extension to multi-way decompositions and the functional role of the large residual subspace are open.
- Non-convex optimization with sensitivity to subspace dimensionality, condition-number bound, and learning rate; seed-level variance and reproducibility details are relegated to (or absent from) the main text.
- The ResNet-family failure is unexplained, bounding the generality of the claim across vision architectures.

## Relevance

这项工作与可解释性研究高度相关：它为"叠加态/多义神经元"文献补上了因果性的模块化判据，提供了一套可直接复用的子空间分解+消融/检索评估工具，适用于分析 LLM 与视觉表征中不同功能（内容 vs 风格、句法 vs 语义）的分离层级。同时其人工-生物神经网络对齐结果对计算神经科学方向的读者也有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does subspace-level modularity emerge gradually over training, or is it present at initialization and merely reshaped? What does the level of modularity imply for generalization and robustness?
- Can the framework extend to multi-way (beyond two-function) decompositions, and what functional information does the large residual subspace carry — is it shared, redundant, or selectively task-associated?
- Why does the ResNet family resist linear content-style separation while Swin/ConvNeXt/VGG/ViT do not — is it pooling architecture, training objective, or receptive-field statistics?
- Would behavioral ablations in vision (selectively impairing object recognition while sparing texture judgment) replicate the RNN double-dissociation results for pretrained models?
- Does the unit-level-absent / subspace-level-present dissociation hold for attention heads and MLP layers within transformers, i.e., can targeted intervention on subspaces replace neuron-level steering?
