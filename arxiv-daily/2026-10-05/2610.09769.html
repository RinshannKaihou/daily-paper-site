Bolzano: From Expert-Guided Proof Search to Automated Open-Problem Solving

Adrián Zámečník, Matěj Kripner, Martin Koutecký, et al. · Charles University & Czech Academy of Sciences · 2026-10-05

Key metric
214 / 3,800

open problems reported solved (~5.6%) across four paper collections — a ~27× scale-up over the 8 problems of the earlier expert-guided study.

Every count except STOC 2026 is an LLM-based estimate.

Why this matters

First end-to-end multi-agent run on real research-level open problems — with an honest ladder separating LLM-estimated from author-verified success.

面向真实科研级开放问题的多智能体端到端流程；其「哪些结果算被解决」的诚实分级方式尤其值得 AI-for-Science 工作借鉴。

Critical edge

Only 4 of ~214 claimed solutions are author-verified (two at idea level only), and no baseline tests whether the agent loop beats direct prompting.

Methodology

  • P×kParallel provers work the same task in isolation — no web, no code execution, no files: pure reasoning over supplied text.
  • VA single LLM verifier reads all prover outputs and is the only writer of the shared state, via schema-validated updates.
  • SA summarizer logs each round, so later rounds build on prior work without re-reading every response.
  • ↻State = four documents (problem · notes · proofs · output) + short round records; the loop is resumable and human-auditable.

Problem extraction

A separate harness reads each paper, lifts explicitly stated open problems, web-checks they remain unsolved, and assembles a self-contained task card with definitions and related work.

Run configurations

GPT-5.5, 4 rounds on arXiv math.CO/cs.DS and STOC 2026; GPT-5.6 Sol, 2 rounds on earlier FOCS/SODA/STOC and the Midsummer Combinatorial Workshop. The 4 STOC results were author-confirmed in June–July 2026.

LLM screening→ Duplicate registry→ Source-intent check→ Second audit→ Author confirmation (4/420)
  • Planted bicliques: one-pass, O(log n)-bit detection for both sides ≥ C·n2/3 — matches the lower bound up to log factors.
  • Monotone rank programs: exponential separation — 2m rows suffice as span programs, while rank programs need exactly m·22m.
  • Convex quartics: a strongly convex quartic whose zero sublevel set is a single irrational point — a feasible problem with no rational witness.
  • Biased CAT states: Ω(log n) circuit-depth lower bound for joining/splitting, incl. the open H(α)=2H(β) case.

Strengths

  • Unusually honest accounting: estimated-vs-verified split, a failure taxonomy, and a duplicate-output registry.
  • Frontier target: STOC 2026 accepted papers, not re-solved competition problems; the 4 confirmed results are genuine contributions.
  • Tool-free agents isolate pure mathematical reasoning, making the evaluation interpretable.
  • Persistent human-readable state enables post-hoc auditing, mid-run expert steering, and reproducibility.
  • Domain theorists (Balko, Šámal, Hubáček, Koutecký) on the team perform the human-review stage.

Limitations

  • 4 of ~214 claimed solutions author-verified — two only at idea level; no proof-assistant verification anywhere.
  • No baseline vs direct GPT-5.5/5.6 prompting or prior systems (Aletheia, AlphaEvolve); the scaffolding’s marginal value is unknown.
  • Cross-collection runs confound model, rounds, and corpus; compute cost per solved problem unreported.
  • Scope limited to combinatorics / theoretical CS; untested in analysis, algebraic geometry, probability.
  • “Solved” is fuzzy by construction: unflagged runs cannot be declared unsolved; screening can mis-process outputs.

Questions for further exploration

  1. What fraction of the ~210 estimated solutions would survive full expert review or Lean formalization?
  2. Does the prover–verifier loop beat single-shot prompting of the same model at equal token budget? explicitly open
  3. How do yield and quality scale with rounds (2 vs 4) and model capability (GPT-5.5 vs GPT-5.6 Sol) at fixed corpus and budget?
  4. Can the persistent research state feed a proof assistant that closes proof gaps automatically?
  5. How often do incorrect lemmas propagate through the verifier-maintained notes — and can round summaries detect it?
