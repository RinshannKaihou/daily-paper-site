> **一句话总结：** 将"从成对校准测量学习一个共享线性映射去除两数据源间的均值漂移"形式化为硬失真预算下的统计决策问题，推导出精确的有限样本极小极大风险 R*₍d₎(b,κ) = (d−k)·E[1/(d+2J)]（J~Pois(κ/2)，k 为可删除方向数，κ 为校准信噪比），据此证明检测只需 κ ≳ √d 而修复需 κ ≫ d——d=256、κ=128 时水平 0.05 检验以 0.999 概率发现漂移，但最优单方向删除编辑器仍留下 67% 的平方分离度，而 MP、SAL、LEACE 三个经典擦除器恰好（exact）达到该前沿。

> **TL;DR:** The paper derives the exact finite-sample minimax residual of any budget-constrained linear "editor" that removes a mean shift shared by two sources: R*₍d₎(b,κ) = (d−k)·E[1/(d+2J)], J~Pois(κ/2). This yields a sharp detection–repair gap: detection needs calibration SNR κ ≳ √d but repair at constant distortion needs κ ≫ d — on the Gaussian benchmark at d=256, κ=128, a level-0.05 test detects the shift with probability 0.999 while the best one-deletion editor leaves 67% of the squared separation. A positive corollary: the classic concept erasers MP, SAL, and LEACE (which all delete the empirical mean difference) exactly attain this frontier at cost one, and on wearable-vs-clinical EEG (BOAS dataset, d=118) the theory predicts held-out residual within Monte Carlo error (e.g., 8 participants × 8 epochs: predicted 0.501 vs observed 0.507±0.024, while detection succeeds in 97% of draws).

## 核心贡献

- **精确极小极大前沿（Theorem 2）**：对所有预算为 b 的共享线性编辑器（硬失真 ‖M−I‖²_F ≤ b），给出闭式极小极大风险 (d−k)·E_{J~Pois(κ/2)}[1/(d+2J)]，k=⌊b⌋；达到该值的编辑器为 M = I_d − Π_K，其中 K = span(D/‖D‖) ⊕ 一个从 u^⊥ 均匀抽取的 (k−1) 维子空间。该公式不是渐近率而是精确值，且对非高斯共享内容仍精确（Theorem 9）。
- **检测–修复鸿沟（Corollary 3）**：水平 α 检测在 κ/√d → ∞ 时极小极大功效趋于 1（需 κ ≳ √d），而把期望残差压到 ε 需要 κ > (d−1)/ε − d（即 κ ≫ d）；当 κ/√d → 0 时任何有限预算 b 的编辑器极小极大残差趋于 1。检测成功完全不代表可修复。
- **现有擦除器的精确定位（Corollary 4/5）**：MP、SAL（居中二值）与 LEACE（含奇异协方差情形）都以 span{D} 为核，恰好达到单方向前沿 R*₍d₎(1,κ)=(d−1)E[1/(d+2J)]；任何其他拟合子空间的超额残差由 Theorem 5 给出（用于量化固定核维度下 INLP/R-LACE 的损失）。
- **校准需求与收集单位（Theorem 8 / Corollary 8）**：d=64、κ=32 时把残差降到 1/2 和 1/4 分别需要 N=63 和 190 对校准数据；按参与者聚类的校准存在硬上限 κ̄ ≤ Pθ²d/trΣ_w——同一参与者加再多 epoch 只缩小第二项，更多参与者才提升上限，并给出 Cochran 型最优分配 n* ∝ √(c·trΣ_w)。
- **何时行动（Section 5）**：选择性修复（只挑有利校准）无法闭合鸿沟；给出不依赖未知漂移强度 θ 的置信球与有限混合证书，后者在任何强度下行动都更频繁（d=κ=256 时混合证书半径 559.4 vs 置信球 588.6）。
- **真实设备验证**：在 BOAS 可穿戴-vs-临床脑电（d=118、55 名合格参与者）与 Neuroscan/Flex N170 第二设备对上，两级校准预测与实测held-out残差吻合（RMSE 0.016 / 0.005）。

## Key Contributions

- **Exact minimax frontier (Theorem 2):** for every shared linear editor under a hard distortion budget ‖M−I‖²_F ≤ b, the paper computes the exact finite-sample risk (d−k)·E_{J~Pois(κ/2)}[1/(d+2J)] with k = ⌊b⌋ deletions; the attaining editor projects onto span(D̂) plus k−1 uniformly random directions orthogonal to u. The frontier stays exact for any shared content law (Theorem 9), not just Gaussians.
- **A sharp detection–repair gap (Corollary 3):** no level-α test has minimax power tending to one when κ/√d → 0 (detection needs κ ≳ √d), yet an expected residual ≤ ε requires κ > (d−1)/ε − d (repair needs κ ≫ d); simultaneously, any fixed budget b < 1 has minimax repair risk tending to one exactly in the regime where detection becomes easy.
- **Placement of existing erasers (Corollaries 4–5):** mean projection (MP), centered binary SAL, and exact binary LEACE all share kernel span{D̂} and therefore attain the population one-deletion frontier exactly — their empirically observed residual on fresh data is not a defect but the frontier itself; Theorem 5 prices the excess of any other fitted subspace (e.g., fixed-rank INLP/R-LACE).
- **Calibration budgeting rules:** closed-form pair counts (N=63 and 190 pairs to reach residual 1/2 and 1/4 at d=64, κ=32), a participant cap under clustered calibration (Corollary 8: residual ≤ d/(d+κ̄) with κ̄ ≤ Pθ²d/trΣ_w regardless of epochs per participant), an optimal participant/pair split, and certified act-or-leave rules (finite-mixture certificate) that work without knowing the shift strength.
- **Validation on real device shifts:** simulations over 120 conditions, a semisynthetic check on three EEG feature pools, and two real paired-device EEG datasets (BOAS clinical-vs-wearable, d=118; Neuroscan/Flex N170, d=225) confirm the formulas within Monte Carlo error.

## Methodology

- **Statistical experiment (Section 3):** two sources share content; a fresh representation is H = V + (S/2)δ with δ = θu, V ~ N(0, I_d), S ∈ {−1,+1}. Calibration consists of N independent contrasts D_i ~ N(δ, s²I_d) with unknown isotropic noise scale s; the total SNR is κ = Nθ²/s². An editor M ∈ R^{d×d} is fit from the calibration sample with an independent random seed, then frozen and shared by both sources. The loss is a covariance-adjusted residual L(M,u) = ‖Π_{row(M)} u‖² (invariant to rescaling and affine offsets), and the distortion cost is the hard budget ‖M−I‖²_F ≤ b.
- **Exact risk derivation (Section 4):** Lemma 1 shows only deleted directions matter and each costs ≥ 1. For one deletion the posterior of u given the data is von Mises–Fisher on the sphere; a Bayes argument (Eaton et al. 1989; Marchand 1997 Poisson-mixture reduction) gives the exact risk (d−1)E[1/(d+2J)], J~Pois(κ/2), and randomizing the k−1 remaining directions makes every editor frequency-equivalent, so the Bayes risk is the minimax risk. Energy accounting (Jensen + Cauchy–Schwarz) gives the two-sided sandwich (d−k)/(d+κ) ≤ R* ≤ (d−k)/(d−1) · d/(d+κ).
- **Detection side:** the level-α radial test that rejects when ‖X‖² exceeds the 1−α χ²_d quantile is minimax among level-α tests; Corollary 3 contrasts its power with the repair frontier.
- **Extensions:** exact frontier for arbitrary shared content (Theorem 9, pairs cancel content), bracketing when the source label is known (Theorem 10: share left between R*(1,κ) and d/(d+κ)), selective-repair oracle with risk–coverage (Theorem 6), certificates that act without knowing θ (Theorems 13–18), anisotropic bounds via Cauchy–Schwarz on the mean of D (Proposition 7), and two-level participant clustering (Corollary 8) with Cochran-style optimal allocation.
- **Empirics (Section 7):** Monte Carlo over 120 (d,κ) cells; semisynthetic shifts injected into three frozen EEG feature pools with MP/SAL/LEACE refit; BOAS (100 participants sleeping once in a clinical polysomnograph and a forehead EEG headband; d=118 after channel-quality filtering leaves 55, split 43 calibration / 12 test; log-power spectra 0.5–30 Hz, one calibration pair per 30-s epoch) with P ≤ 32 participants and n ≤ 128 epochs; replication on Neuroscan/Flex N170 (d=225); INLP and fixed-budget R-LACE comparisons; certified vs uncertified acting rules.

## Results & Highlights

- **Frontier verified to Monte Carlo precision:** the exact cost-one residual at (d,κ)=(256,128) is 0.6652 vs Monte Carlo 0.6644 ± 0.0009 (8,192 draws); at (256,512), exact 0.3326 vs 0.3325 ± 0.0006; across 120 conditions the deviations are 0.004 RMSE in root mean square.
- **The detection–repair gap is large in practice:** at d=256, κ=128 a level-0.05 test detects the shift with probability 0.999, yet the best editor allowed to delete one direction leaves about two-thirds (67%) of the squared separation; in Figure 1b at κ=8√d detection is near-certain (99.9%) while editing still leaves 58% of the signal share (25% even at κ=32√d).
- **MP, SAL, LEACE exactly hit the frontier on real content:** at d=64 with N*=63 pairs (target residual 0.50), the three erasers leave 0.504, 0.501, 0.503 (±0.007) vs the exact 0.5000; with N*=190 pairs (target 0.25) they leave 0.251, 0.247, 0.248 (±0.004) vs exact 0.2495 — the residuals coincide to within Monte Carlo error even though their distortion differs (LEACE's median linear cost is 2.13 vs 1 for MP and SAL at d=N=64).
- **INLP's excess is predicted and confirmed:** one-step INLP at d=N=32, k=1 leaves mean residual 0.687 ± 0.014 against 0.013 for MP, an observed excess of 0.186 ± 0.012 matching the Theorem-5 prediction 0.181; at four deletions the excess shrinks to 0.017 ± 0.008 (predicted 0.012).
- **Wearable-vs-clinical EEG (BOAS, d=118) matches the two-level theory:** one participant with 128 epochs leaves 0.690 ± 0.033 of the population shift (predicted cap 0.757, per-participant κ̄ ≈ 35); 8 participants × 8 epochs leave 0.507 ± 0.024 (predicted 0.501) while the shift is detected in 97% of draws (predicted 0.95) — detected but half-repaired; 32 participants × 128 epochs leave 0.125 ± 0.027 (predicted 0.101). Deviation between prediction and observation over 20 random splits is 0.016 RMSE, and d/(d+κ̄) is never exceeded by more than 1.96 SE; the second device pair (Flex, d=225, P=8, n=16) gives 0.504 ± 0.030 vs predicted 0.508.
- **Acting selectively cannot rescue a weak calibration:** at d=64, κ=32, acting on only the top 10⁻⁵ most informative fits lowers the optimal conditional residual from 0.661 to just 0.549; without certification, a radial test at (256,128) acts and misses the ε=0.5 target with probability 0.9986, while the certified finite-mixture ball acts with probability 0.114 [0.107, 0.121] (vs 0.026 for the plain confidence ball) and its radius is 559.4 vs 588.6.
- **Participant count, not epochs, is the binding constraint at scale:** halving the shift needs about 4, 6, 8, and 12 participants at d = 28, 58, 118, 236 (growing with dimension as Theorem 3 predicts), whereas half-detection power needs only ~4 participants across that whole range; subtracting the unshrunk mean with a known device label is worse than the erasers at every calibration size.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is genuine but bounded:** the detection rates themselves are classical (Baraud 2002; Collier et al. 2017; Johnstone–Lu 2009), and the paper is explicit that those scales are familiar. The new contribution is the exact *repair* bound and the proof that three named existing erasers (MP, SAL, LEACE) already sit on it — a unification rather than a new algorithm. Readers expecting a better editor will not find one; by design the frontier is a lower bound, and "more recordings, more participants, or a better method" is the actionable output.
- **Rigor is high:** the exactness claims are backed by full proofs in appendix Sections A–P, and the empirical agreement is unusually tight (e.g., observed 0.504/0.501/0.503 against exact 0.5000 for the three erasers; INLP excess 0.186 ± 0.012 vs predicted 0.181). The BOAS analysis is robust to dropping the channel-quality rule (agreement holds for all sleep stages, d from 28 to 236), and the Flex dataset serves as an out-of-study replication (deviation 0.005).
- **The model contract is narrow and the authors say so:** exactness requires independent calibration contrasts, isotropic Gaussian noise, a single shift direction, linear/affine editing, and the covariance-adjusted residual. Under anisotropic noise the minimax value is only bracketed (Proposition 7: the isotropic model is the best case), multi-direction or multi-source shifts are open, and the distortion budget treats all directions alike — no notion of deleting task-relevant directions.
- **What "repair" means is narrower than the title may suggest:** the residual L only measures the first-moment difference; between-device second-moment discrepancies (noted as substantial in their Section N) are beyond any linear correction, so a low residual does not certify downstream comparability of the two sources. The covariance-adjusted loss is also scale-invariant, so a downstream classifier that rescales features can undo part of what L penalizes or rewards.
- **Red flags are minor:** no true baseline beyond the linear class is compared (nonlinear harmonization methods are cited but not benchmarked — defensible given the theory targets the linear class, but the practical claim "a correction that falls short needs a better method" is only lower-bounded within linear maps); the participant-level cap experiment leans on one dataset's per-participant κ̄ ≈ 35, and predicted 0.101 vs observed 0.125 ± 0.027 at P=32 hints the two-level bound is slightly optimistic there.

## Strengths

- Exact, closed-form finite-sample limits rather than asymptotic rates — rare and usable directly as a power-analysis tool before any calibration is collected (three pilot numbers: θ², within-participant variation trΣ_h, per-unit noise trΣ_w fix κ and hence the required participants/epochs).
- A clean, falsifiable explanation of an empirically observed phenomenon (leftover residual of concept erasers on fresh data) that turns scattered observations about MP/SAL/LEACE into a theorem, plus a quantitative diagnostic (Theorem 5 excess) for iterative erasers like INLP and R-LACE.
- Honest framing: the paper separates three failure reasons — suboptimal editor, too-small budget, calibration collected on the wrong unit — and gives certified act-or-leave rules instead of overclaiming that repair is always possible.
- Strong empirical hygiene: exact formulas vs Monte Carlo, semisynthetic real-content pools, two independent real device pairs, released code and saved results for every figure and table.

## Limitations

- Exactness assumes independent calibration, isotropic Gaussian contrasts, a single shift direction, shared linear or affine editing, and the covariance-adjusted residual and distortion; under anisotropic noise only bounds are known, and the noise-weighted direction estimator that the theory suggests is left unanalyzed.
- Only first-moment shift removal is covered: covariance differences between sources, shifts spread over several directions, and more than two sources are all open problems.
- Clustered calibration results target the population mean shift; correcting each person's own shift (combining both levels) and sequential/adaptive versions of the certificates remain future work.
- The distortion budget is unitless and direction-blind; trading residual against downstream task utility is not modeled.
- Frontier results are a lower bound within (and below) the linear class — they do not bound what nonlinear or generative repair could achieve.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the exact frontier extend to rank-r subspace shifts (δ spread over r directions) — does the detection–repair gap widen by a factor related to r?
- Under anisotropic noise, is the noise-weighted (rather than empirical) direction estimator minimax, and can the Proposition-7 bracket be tightened to an exact value?
- Can sequential versions of the finite-mixture certificate retain the same guarantees for adaptive calibration collection (stop-when-certified)?
- How do the substantial second-moment between-device differences (reported in their Section N) interact with first-moment repair guarantees in downstream task performance — is d/(d+κ̄) predictive of task-level metrics?
- Does the participant-cap phenomenon (Corollary 8) generalize to other harmonization settings, e.g., multi-site fMRI ComBat-style calibration or LLM steering-vector removal where "participants" become prompts or users?
