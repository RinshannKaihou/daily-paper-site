> **一句话总结：** CacheLink 在冻结的 per-head Ridge 跨模型 KV 重建之上学习零初始化的 K/V 耦合残差（分离 K/V stem + 后融合共享因子），并通过冻结接收方的 continuation CE + forward KL 做"功能监督"（不做任何张量重建损失），使 4 对 sender→receiver 在 7 个评测面板上的等权均值全部超过 Ridge-k8（+6.84 / +2.96 / +16.01 / +11.14），其中 Qwen3-14B→32B 达到 50.32、甚至反超重预填的原生接收方（48.14），32K 上下文切换延迟仅 0.969s，比重预填快 7.27×。

> **TL;DR:** CacheLink addresses zero-prefill cross-model context handoff: a sender's KV cache is translated into the receiver's cache space so the receiver continues without re-prefilling. The paper first shows that reconstruction fidelity (the objective of prior affine transport like Ridge-k8) is a poor proxy for receiver utility — on a NoLiMa length sweep (8K→32K) Ridge's score falls 19%→9%→2% while its K-MSE *improves* from 1.161 to 1.054. CacheLink therefore starts from the frozen Ridge initialization, learns a zero-initialized coupled K/V residual (separate K/V stems fused into shared latent factors), and trains it through the frozen receiver with continuation cross-entropy + forward KL against a native receiver that prefilled the same prefix — no reconstruction loss at all. Across four sender→receiver pairs (Qwen3-14B→32B, Ministral-3.8B→14B, Qwen3-8B→14B, Yi-1.5-9B→34B) and seven fixed panels (SciQ, MMLU-Pro/-Plus, OpenBookQA, Oolong-Synth 32K, LongBench-v2, BABILong 32K), CacheLink beats Ridge-k8 by +6.84, +2.96, +16.01, +11.14 equal-weight points (26/28 panels), exceeds the re-prefilling native receiver on 2 of 4 pairs, and keeps 32K handoff latency at 0.969s vs 0.839s for Ridge — 7.27× faster than receiver re-prefill — while shifting KV squared error by less than 0.4%.

## 核心贡献

- **问题刻画与反直觉诊断**：首次系统性地证明"KV 重建保真度 ≠ 接收方可用性"——NoLiMa 8K→32K 扫描中 Ridge 的 K/V 重建 MSE 持续变好（K-MSE 1.161→1.054，V-MSE 3.44→1.11），可复用前缀得分却从 19% 崩到 2%；匹配诊断显示 CacheLink 残差只占 Ridge RMS 的 1.52–1.79%（K）/ 1.88–2.40%（V）、且与 Native−Ridge 方向近似正交，KV 误差变化 <0.4% 即可大幅改变 prefix attention 与 next-token 行为（居中 Pearson 相关仅 −0.023 / 0.067）。
- **K/V 耦合假设**：K 与 V 来自同一隐状态的不同投影，source-swap 干预（RR/NR/RN/NN 四种组合、5 个任务）表明只修正单一分量不会一致改善，必须联合修正——由此导出"分离 stem + 后期融合"的耦合残差结构。
- **CacheLink 方法**：以冻结的 Ridge 仿射映射作为结构性初始化（64 层平均 held-out R²：K 0.838 / V 0.609），叠加零初始化残差头，仅训练残差映射器；目标函数是冻结接收方上的 continuation CE + forward KL，完全去掉 cache 重建损失；部署严格满足 zero-prefill（无接收侧预填、无稀疏重放）。
- **全面实验**：4 对 sender→receiver（Qwen3、Ministral、Yi 三个家族）× 7 个固定面板，CacheLink 在 28 组 pair-panel 对比中 26 次超过 Ridge-k8、21 次超过原生 sender，等权均值全面超过 Ridge、CacheBridge 与 LatentAlign；损失消融证明 CE+forward KL（40/100）优于 CE only（37）、forward KL only（29）、reverse KL only（23）。

## Key Contributions

- **Finding**: reconstruction fidelity does not determine receiver utility in cross-model KV transport. Matched diagnostics on Qwen3-14B→32B show the learned functional residual is tiny (1.52–2.40% of Ridge RMS) and nearly orthogonal to the Native−Ridge gap, yet example-level correlation between KV-error change and attention/next-token change is ≈0 (r = −0.023 / 0.067) — tensor-space proximity carries almost no information about behavior.
- **Method**: CacheLink = frozen Ridge structural initialization + zero-initialized coupled K/V residual (separate stems, late fusion into a shared latent u) + functional supervision (continuation CE + forward KL through the frozen receiver). Gradients flow through receiver attention back into the transported cache; the training objective contains no cache-reconstruction term.
- **Evaluation**: across four sender→receiver pairs spanning three model families and seven fixed short/long-prefix panels, CacheLink achieves the best transport-method equal-weight mean on every pair (beats Ridge-k8 in 26 of 28 pair–panel comparisons, native sender in 21 of 28), and preserves the zero-prefill latency advantage (7.27× and 6.18× faster than receiver re-prefill at 32K).

## Methodology

**Problem setup (strict zero-prefill).** Sender S processes a reusable prefix x_{1:n} once and produces its K/V cache; a learned transport F: C^S → C̃^T produces the entire receiver cache. Deployment performs no receiver-side prefill, no sparse replay, and no receiver-side reconstruction pass over x_{1:n}; the receiver consumes only new query and generated tokens.

**Reconstruction baseline.** The Ridge baseline (Heo et al., 2026) treats handoff as supervised reconstruction: for each target layer, k=8 sender layers are selected by held-out predictability, source keys are de-RoPE'd and concatenated with values, and independent ridge-regularized affine maps (fit on a 500×1024 FineWeb-Edu calibration protocol) predict each target-layer K/V head; receiver RoPE is reapplied before injection. This initialization is strong: mean held-out R² across the 64 receiver layers is 0.838 for K and 0.609 for V (mean NMSE 0.108 / 0.364).

**Diagnostics motivating the design.** (1) On a NoLiMa 8K→16K→32K sweep (Qwen3-14B→32B), Ridge's reusable-prefix score drops 19%→9%→2% (native receiver: 58%→36%→14%) while K-MSE improves 1.161→1.054 and V-MSE 3.44→1.11. (2) A matched diagnostic compares native, Ridge, and step-600 CacheLink caches on 50 examples per panel: the CacheLink residual's RMS is 1.52–1.79% (K) and 1.88–2.40% (V) of Ridge's, its cosine with the Native−Ridge direction is ≈0 (sometimes negative), mean KV error changes <0.4%, yet prefix-attention JS and next-token JS change substantially. (3) A source-swap intervention (Ridge K/Ridge V, Native K/Ridge V, Ridge K/Native V, Native K/Native V on 5 tasks) shows correcting one component alone does not uniformly help — K and V errors share latent structure and must be corrected jointly.

**CacheLink architecture.** The frozen Ridge maps provide the receiver-cache initialization B^K, B^V. Zero-initialized residual heads predict ΔK, ΔV so training starts exactly at the Ridge solution. Per target layer: separate GELU stems embed the concatenated K and V ridge inputs (8192→256 for the 8-layer × 8-head × 128 Qwen3-14B→32B layout), the two views are fused into a shared representation u (512→512), and two heads (512→1024 each) emit all eight target K or V heads — 3,508,096 trainable parameters per target layer, 358,518,144 across 64 receiver layers. The final cache is RoPE_T(B^K + ΔK) and B^V + ΔV. The mapper operates directly on the sender cache: no source-side solo forward pass and no receiver replay.

**Functional supervision.** The translated cache is injected into the frozen receiver; the loss is continuation cross-entropy on the target continuation plus λ_KL × forward KL(native receiver || mapper policy), where the native receiver prefilled the same prefix. Gradients propagate through receiver attention into the cache itself. The loss ablation (Table 2, NeedleBench MR32K, correct/100): CE+forward KL 40, CE only 37, CE+reverse KL 39, forward-KL only 29, reverse-KL only 23, vs Ridge 28, native sender 36, native receiver 39.

**Training cost.** At most 1,200 examples and 600 optimizer steps per pair (Qwen3-14B→32B: 400 samples + 200 LongAlign reasoning samples; Qwen3-8B→14B and Yi-1.5-9B→34B: 1,200 examples from CommonsenseQA, CosmosQA, SQuAD v2, DROP, MultiDoc2Dial, ContractNLI — all disjoint from the evaluation panels); the Ridge fits stay frozen throughout.

**Evaluation protocol.** Four sender→receiver pairs — Qwen3-14B→32B, Qwen3-8B→14B, Ministral-3.8B→Ministral-3-14B, Yi-1.5-9B→34B — and seven fixed panels: SciQ, MMLU-Pro, MMLU-Pro-Plus, OpenBookQA (short-prefix multiple choice) and Oolong-Synth 32K, LongBench-v2, BABILong 32K (long reusable prefixes). Baselines: native sender, native receiver (re-prefill reference, excluded from boldfacing), Ridge-k8, CacheBridge (CB), LatentAlign (LA). Runs use Transformers + FlashAttention-2 (custom per-layer cache injection is unsupported by the standard vLLM interface), with the 14B sender and 32B receiver on separate H100s.

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen3-14B→32B, equal-weight 7-panel mean**: CacheLink 50.32 vs Ridge-k8 43.48 (+6.84), CacheBridge 32.32, LatentAlign 10.14, native sender 38.70 — and *above* the native receiver's 48.14 despite never prefilling. Per panel: SciQ 83.90 vs Ridge 74.80 (native receiver only 73.30); BABILong 32K 55.40 vs Ridge 43.60; MMLU-Pro 47.46 vs 41.20.
- **Qwen3-8B→14B**: the largest gain — mean 44.62 vs Ridge 28.61 (+16.01), with SciQ 81.90 vs 47.50 (native receiver 59.70) and MMLU-Pro 44.28 vs 20.78. Here Ridge collapses badly (BABILong 9.00 vs CacheLink 35.40, native receiver 53.80).
- **Panel-level sweep**: CacheLink exceeds Ridge-k8 in 26 of 28 pair–panel comparisons and the native sender in 21 of 28; its equal-weight mean is highest among transport methods on all four pairs (+2.96 for Ministral-3.8B→14B, +11.14 for Yi-1.5-9B→34B, where Ridge means are 38.56 and 25.96).
- **Latency (32K reusable prefix)**: CacheLink 0.969s vs Ridge 0.839s on Qwen3-14B→32B — 7.27× faster than receiver re-prefill; 0.406s vs 0.364s on Ministral-3.8B→14B — 6.18× faster. The residual correction adds only ~0.13s of overhead, and the gap vs re-prefill widens with prefix length.
- **Mechanism**: matched diagnostics show the correction changes KV squared error by <0.4% while substantially changing prefix attention and next-token behavior; within-panel correlation between KV-error reduction and behavior change is −0.023 (attention JS) and 0.067 (next-token JS), rising to only 0.181 against gold-answer log-probability on short-prefix panels. On a 200-example multiple-choice subset, length-normalized accuracy moves 48%→51% (12 wrong-to-correct vs 6 correct-to-wrong flips).
- **Loss ablation (NeedleBench MR32K, correct/100)**: CE+forward KL 40 > CE+reverse KL 39 > CE only 37 > forward KL only 29 > reverse KL only 23; Ridge 28, native sender 36, native receiver 39 — functional supervision closes most of the gap to the native receiver on this panel.

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans Harbin Institute of Technology (Shenzhen), Great Bay University, and Zhejiang University; Haomiao Qiu and Yining Tang contribute equally, with Ziyue Qiao and Miao Zhang (Zhejiang University) as corresponding authors. The work sits in the LLM-serving/systems line (KV reuse, routing, cross-model transfer) and engages directly with the newest related systems — Ridge-based transfer (Heo et al., 2026), CacheBridge (Qu et al., 2026), and LatentAlign (Dery et al., 2026) — all reproduced under a matched zero-prefill protocol. The paper is unusually transparent about methodology: a reproducibility statement specifies cache conventions, mapper construction, prompts, and scoring; an AI-use statement discloses generative-AI assistance for orchestration and editing with humans responsible for numerical claims. No prior public track record of the authors in this exact subfield is verifiable from the paper alone, but the experimental hygiene (matched diagnostics, pilot studies in the appendix, ablations) suggests a competent systems-ML group.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline claim slightly overreaches.** The abstract states CacheLink "improves the equal-weight mean over all methods for every pair," but the native receiver — the actual upper reference — beats CacheLink on 2 of 4 pairs (Ministral 45.24 vs 41.52; Yi-1.5-9B→34B 42.96 vs 37.10). "All methods" silently excludes the native receiver; a reader skimming the abstract could conclude handoff is now free, when on half the pairs it still costs 3.7–5.9 mean points.
- **Two of the three competing baselines are nearly broken, inflating the win margin.** LatentAlign averages ~10 points on every pair and scores 0.00 on all three long-prefix panels for three of four pairs; CacheBridge scores 0.00 on BABILong and LongBench-v2 for Qwen3-14B→32B. Beating these is not informative — the only live comparison is Ridge-k8, and CacheLink's edge is entirely its own functional-training recipe stacked on top of Ridge, which is algorithmically a modest residual-distillation contribution rather than a new transport mechanism.
- **Task-dependent regressions are real and concentrated.** For Ministral-3.8B→14B, Ridge remains stronger on Oolong-Synth 32K (42.01 vs 36.14, a 5.9-point regression) and LongBench-v2 (42.24 vs 39.66). Long-context transfer is therefore not uniformly solved, and the Yi pair's BABILong result (21.80 vs native 41.40) shows a large residual long-context gap even where CacheLink wins over Ridge (8.40).
- **No significance testing anywhere.** Several panels use only 100 questions at 32K, the matched diagnostic uses 50 examples per panel, and the +2.96 Ministral mean gain is not accompanied by confidence intervals or tests; the authors themselves note cross-panel means are "descriptive summaries, not pooled accuracy" (different tokenizers, metrics, and preprocessing), and MMLU-Pro / MMLU-Pro-Plus share question stems, so the 7-panel count slightly double-counts one benchmark family.
- **Latency numbers come from a non-production harness.** Because vLLM does not expose per-layer cache injection, measurements use Transformers + FlashAttention-2 with a one-slot two-GPU pipeline; the 7.27× ratio is plausible, but the absolute sub-second latencies will not transfer to optimized serving kernels, and throughput effects (the acknowledged batching change) are not measured.
- **Reproducibility is partial.** The anonymized artifact contains training/evaluation code but explicitly does not bundle model weights, Ridge fits, benchmark panels, predictions, or run logs — everything must be reassembled per the appendix. Combined with per-pair mapper training (~358.5M parameters for the 64-layer Qwen3-14B→32B mapper), reproducing one number requires nontrivial pipeline reconstruction.
- **Strength of the core insight is genuine.** The fidelity–utility decoupling (Observations 1–2) is demonstrated example-by-example with orthogonal-residual and correlation evidence, and the K/V coupling intervention (Observation 3) is a clean causal test — this diagnostic contribution is more durable than the specific architecture.

## Strengths

- Reframes the problem convincingly: the paper does not just propose a method, it first falsifies the field's implicit objective (reconstruction MSE) with a length-sweep paradox (utility 19%→2% while MSE improves), matched example-level diagnostics, and a K/V swap intervention.
- Strict zero-prefill constraint is honored in both training and deployment, and end-to-end handoff latency is actually measured against receiver re-prefill rather than claimed analytically.
- Breadth with honesty: four pairs across three model families, seven fixed panels covering short and 32K-long prefixes, per-panel tables with regressions left visible, an archived loss ablation, and explicit caveats about what the equal-weight mean does and does not mean.
- Remarkably cheap adaptation: frozen Ridge maps plus zero-initialized residuals trained on ≤1,200 examples for ≤600 steps, no reconstruction dataset curation, and the mapper touches only the sender cache (no receiver forward pass on the prefix).

## Limitations

- Still trails the native re-prefilling receiver on 2 of 4 pairs (Ministral −3.72, Yi −5.86 equal-weight mean), so cross-model handoff remains a quality-for-latency trade in general.
- Long-context behavior is uneven: regressions on Oolong-Synth 32K and LongBench-v2 for the Ministral pair, and a 19.6-point gap to native on Yi BABILong 32K.
- Per-pair training: every sender→receiver combination needs its own ~350M-parameter mapper and training run; no multi-target or family-level amortization is explored.
- Latency and throughput measured on a custom Transformers + FlashAttention-2 harness, not a production serving stack (vLLM/SGLang), limiting external validity of the systems claims.
- No statistical uncertainty estimates despite small panels (down to 100 questions); hyperparameter λ_KL is deferred to the appendix and not sensitivity-analyzed in the main text.
- The public artifact omits weights, Ridge fits, panels, and run logs, so full reproduction requires substantial independent setup.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击 LLM 服务体系中"模型路由/级联时上下文无法跨模型复用"这一实际痛点，用扎实的诊断否定了"重建误差"这一默认优化目标，并给出免重预填的跨模型 KV 迁移方案（最高 +16.01 等权均值、32K 下 7.27× 加速），对关注推理效率、KV cache 管理与多模型 serving 的研究非常有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does CacheLink interact with quantized or compressed caches (e.g., KIVI-style 2-bit K / 4-bit V) — does functional supervision still recover behavior when the transported cache is quantized for memory?
- The mapper is trained per sender→receiver pair; can a single mapper serve multiple receivers (or a receiver updated by continued fine-tuning), and how far does the residual drift when the receiver checkpoint moves?
- Why does Ridge collapse so catastrophically on BABILong for Qwen3-8B→14B (9.00 vs CacheLink 35.40) — is it attention-entropy degradation with prefix length, and can the fidelity–utility decoupling predict *which* (pair, length) regimes will fail?
- What does λ_KL sensitivity look like, and does CE alone saturate with more training data, reducing the need for the native-receiver KL teacher at training time?
- Would the 7.27× latency advantage survive integration into vLLM/SGLang with fused attention kernels, and how does the one-slot pipeline behave under realistic batched routing workloads?
- Can the "functional residual" idea transfer beyond KV caches — e.g., to cross-model handoff of other recurrent states (SSM/Mamba states, cross-attention KV in encoders)?
