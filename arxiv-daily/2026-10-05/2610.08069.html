Methodology

R*d(b,κ) = (d−k) · E[1/(d+2J)],  J~Pois(κ/2)

N calibration contrasts Dᵢ ~ N(δ, s²I) → fit editor M under hard budget ‖M−I‖²_F ≤ b, k=⌊b⌋ → freeze & share across both sources → residual ‖Π(row(M)) u‖²
  • Setup: two sources share content; a fresh point deviates by θu along one unknown direction u; total calibration SNR is κ = Nθ²/s².
  • Exact risk: the posterior of u is von Mises–Fisher; a Poisson-mixture Bayes argument gives the exact finite-sample value above (not a rate) — and it stays exact for any shared content law (Thm 9). Randomized spare deletions make Bayes = minimax.
  • Detection side: the radial χ² test is minimax among level-α tests; Corollary 3 contrasts its κ ≳ √d scaling with repair’s κ ≫ d.
  • Calibration budgeting: closed-form pair counts, a participant cap under clustered collection (κ𝓁 ≤ Pθ²d/trΣₖ regardless of epochs), and certified act-or-leave rules that work without knowing shift strength.
  • Validation: 120-cell Monte Carlo, semisynthetic shifts in three frozen EEG feature pools, and two real paired-device datasets (BOAS d=118; Neuroscan/Flex N170 d=225).

At 8×8 the shift is detected in 97% of draws (predicted 0.95) — detected but half-repaired. Replication on Flex (d=225): 0.504 ± 0.030 vs predicted 0.508.

  • MP, SAL, LEACE land exactly on the one-deletion frontier: 0.504 / 0.501 / 0.503 (±0.007) vs exact 0.5000 at d=64, N=63 — their leftover residual on fresh data is the frontier itself, not a defect.
  • INLP’s excess is priced and confirmed: observed 0.186 ± 0.012 vs Theorem-5 prediction 0.181 at one deletion (d=N=32).
  • Participants, not epochs, bind at scale: halving the shift needs ~4 → 12 participants as d grows 28 → 236; half-detection power needs ~4 across the whole range.
  • Selective repair cannot rescue weak calibration: acting on only the top 10⁻&sup5; most informative fits lowers the residual from 0.661 to just 0.549.

Strengths

  • Exact closed-form finite-sample limits, not asymptotic rates — usable as a power-analysis tool before any calibration is collected (three pilot numbers fix κ).
  • Turns the folklore “erasers leave residual on fresh data” into a falsifiable theorem; Theorem 5 quantitatively diagnoses iterative erasers (INLP / R-LACE).
  • Honest failure taxonomy — weak editor vs small budget vs wrong calibration unit — with certified act-or-leave rules instead of overclaiming.
  • Strong empirical hygiene: formulas vs Monte Carlo, two real device pairs, released code and saved results for every figure and table.

Limitations

  • Exactness assumes independent isotropic Gaussian contrasts, a single shift direction, and linear editing; under anisotropic noise only brackets are known.
  • First-moment shifts only — covariance gaps, multi-direction shifts, and more than two sources are open.
  • The distortion budget is unitless and direction-blind; no residual-vs-task-utility trade-off is modeled.
  • The frontier lower-bounds only the linear class — it says nothing about nonlinear or generative repair.
  • The two-level bound looks slightly optimistic at P=32 (predicted 0.101 vs observed 0.125 ± 0.027).

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the exact frontier extend to rank-r subspace shifts — does the detection–repair gap widen by a factor of ~r?
  2. Under anisotropic noise, is the noise-weighted (rather than empirical) direction estimator minimax, and can the Proposition-7 bracket be tightened?
  3. Can sequential finite-mixture certificates keep the same guarantees for adaptive, stop-when-certified calibration collection?
  4. How do the substantial second-moment between-device differences interact with first-moment repair guarantees on downstream task metrics?
  5. Does the participant-cap phenomenon generalize — multi-site fMRI ComBat-style harmonization, or LLM steering-vector removal where “participants” become prompts or users?
