Key metric — matched compute

+66.0%

Min‑max normalized average score at equal compute, 13 long‑horizon tasks: 0.78 vs 0.47 single‑run.

Also +44.4% over best‑of‑4 (0.54); highest score on 12 of 13 tasks. As reported in Table 1 of the source.

Why this matters

Reveals a widely ignored variance bottleneck in long‑horizon agent research — and a zero‑modification, drop‑in test‑time‑compute fix for existing autoresearch loops like AI Scientist or AlphaEvolve.

⚠ Critical edge

The headline rests on a weak control — best‑of‑4 gets B/4 with no mid‑run selection — with just 5 runs per condition, no significance tests, and proprietary models third parties can’t cheaply replicate.

01Methodology

  1. A
    Harness

    A GitHub Copilot CLI agent edits, executes, and submits solutions in an isolated container, earning a grader score per episode. State is a lineage of (workspace W, chat context C) pairs; the goal is the max score found within budget B.

  2. B
    Diagnose: idea basins

    Submissions → functional specs → LLM‑judged similarity triplets → t‑STE 2‑D map. 85% held‑out triplet agreement (50% chance, 56% heuristic; SOE replication 0.83). Each run’s submissions cluster in one localized region: its basin.

  3. C
    Intervene: fork + flush

    Forking creates K parallel branches that inherit the workspace; flushing resets chat context (W,C)→(W,∅) so branches can’t re‑derive their parent’s local reasoning. The highest‑scoring branch continues.

  4. D
    Evaluate

    Equal global budget vs single‑run (8 runs) and best‑of‑4 (5 runs); fork‑and‑flush gets 5 runs per task; min‑max normalized scores across tasks.

fork ×4→ leader→ re‑fork @33% B→ leader→ re‑fork @66% B→ final best

13 tasks: Frontier‑CS ×9 · EdgeBench ×2 · VLIW kernel · tinyCIFAR  |  budgets 6K–100K Copilot credits · runs to 107 h  |  GPT‑5.6‑terra (11 tasks) / GPT‑5.5 (2) · fixed cadence, no plateau detector

Where does variance come from? MaxSAT × Qwen3.5‑4B/9B/27B: outcome spread collapses to ~0 when every run solves or fails a formula, peaking at attainment ≈0.50 (quadratic fit 0.497). Variance is a capability‑frontier effect — so it should persist as models scale.

03Strengths

  • +
    A striking, practically important phenomenon: same task, same start — 8 runs plateau between 68.3 and 100.0 (median 81.9), and gaps survive substantial extra compute.
  • +
    The intervention is simple, agent‑agnostic, and drop‑in: workspace copy + context reset + score‑based selection. No plateau detector, no prompt surgery.
  • +
    Strict equal‑compute comparison on partly external benchmarks (Frontier‑CS, EdgeBench, Anthropic’s kernel task), not only self‑made tasks.
  • +
    Clean ablation splits the gain (0.45 → 0.55 / 0.57 → 0.71); the 43‑unit post‑flush jump gives mechanistic, not just aggregate, evidence.
  • +
    The MaxSAT frontier study (3 model scales, bootstrap CIs) thoughtfully pre‑empts “stronger models won’t need this.”

04Limitations

  • −
    Enormous evaluation cost (107‑hour runs, 100K credits) caps the study at 13 tasks × 5 runs, one agent harness, one frontier model family.
  • −
    Branches never communicate — no coordination or role specialization; a collaborative variant might beat independent fan‑out.
  • −
    One operating point in the main text: fixed 3 rounds, K=4, ~0.06B per branch; sensitivity to the schedule lives in appendices.
  • −
    Raw sd grows on 10 of 13 tasks — practitioners who need predictable runtimes may still prefer best‑of‑N; the consistency win is normalized‑only.
  • −
    “Basins” are an LLM‑judged construction, and the won‑t‑vanish‑with‑scale claim rests only on the MaxSAT proxy, not the 13 tasks themselves.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Q1
    How do gains scale with K and fork count at fixed budget — and does the optimal cadence shift with task difficulty or model strength?
  • Q2
    Would adaptive triggers (plateauness, basin‑entropy signals on the embedding) beat the fixed 33%/66% schedule, and by how much?
  • Q3
    Can branches share partial findings (communication, role specialization) to outperform fully independent fan‑out?
  • Q4
    Do basins — and the benefit of flushing — persist for open‑weight models and non‑code domains where artifacts resist functional‑spec rewriting?
  • Q5
    Is the MaxSAT frontier law predictive on the real 13 tasks — can a cheap attainment measurement forecast per‑task gains before committing budget?
  • Q6
    How does fork‑and‑flush compare against successive halving / racing over larger populations (N=8, 16) under the same global budget?
