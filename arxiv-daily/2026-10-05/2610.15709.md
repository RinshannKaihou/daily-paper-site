> **一句话总结：** 来自 CUHK 的这篇工作首次系统分析了线性注意力/循环模型低比特状态量化中"误差逐积累加"的根源——每步更新太小（中位数仅为量化网格间距的 2%–21%），68%–96% 的更新元素在舍入中被直接丢失、其误差跨步高度相干（而非像噪声一样抵消），据此提出训练免费的 LRB（利用更新 k·vᵀ 外积结构、把小更新积累在 rank-k 低秩旁路缓冲中周期性合并）+ BDR（把被舍入"反向"的元素朝更新方向多挪一格、并用阈值 ε_B 约束过冲放大）；在 GDN/GLA/Mamba2/RetNet 四个 1.3B 模型上无需任何微调或校准即把 WikiText-103 8K 困境从 INT4 的 1193.21（RetNet）拉回 28.80（FP32 为 24.30），同时状态显存较 FP32 最高省 5.3×、解码吞吐最高提速 1.9×。

> **TL;DR:** Unlike weights or KV caches, a linear-attention/recurrent state is re-quantized after every token, so per-step rounding errors accumulate coherently instead of canceling — the paper shows this coherence comes from small updates (median |update|/grid-spacing of 0.02–0.21) being lost in rounding (68–96% of elements per step on WikiText-103). It proposes two training-free fixes: Low-Rank Bypass (LRB) accumulates pending updates in a rank-k FP16 buffer exploiting the rank-1 outer-product structure of each update, merging them into the quantized state every m steps, and Bounded Directional Rounding (BDR) rounds "reversed" elements one grid step toward their update, gated by a threshold that bounds overshoot amplification. Without any fine-tuning or calibration, across 1.3B Gated DeltaNet/GLA/Mamba2/RetNet the method cuts WikiText-103 8K perplexity vs the strongest INT4+Hadamard baseline by up to 890 points (RetNet: 28.80 vs 918.77; FP32 = 24.30), keeps LAMBADA accuracy within 0.5 pts of FP32 on three of four models, and reduces state memory by up to 5.3× while speeding up decoding by up to 1.9× on an RTX 3060 — at only ~0.75–1.1 extra bits per state element over INT4.

## 核心贡献
- **误差相干性分析（新视角）：** 证明循环状态量化的每步误差不是独立噪声——其互内积几乎处处为正，且衰减权重让状态保留数千步，所以离 diagonal 的交叉项二次增长并主导累积偏差；相干增益 g_T 在 4K 上下文处可达 86×。仅压低单步误差（如 Hadamard 旋转）无法阻止累积。
- **把相干性溯源到"被丢失的更新"：** 状态更新 B_t = k_t·v_tᵀ 的元素幅度中位数只有网格间距 Δ_t 的 0.02–0.21 倍，每步 68%（GDN）到 96.4%（RetNet）的更新元素舍入前后结果完全相同——这些元素上的误差携带"整个更新取反"，因此天然继承更新间的跨步相关性（丢失部分的相干增益高达 10²–10⁴，而"被挪动"部分接近 1）。
- **LRB（Low-Rank Bypass）：** 舍入前干预。利用每步更新秩为 1 的结构，把小更新积累在 FP16 因子形式 (K,V) 的低秩旁路里（查询可通过因子直接读旁路），每 m 步用 randomized SVD（8 个随机投影 + 2 次幂迭代、Triton kernel）做 rank-k 近似合并进量化状态；被网格"困住"的元素留在旁路继续积累。仅增加 (k+m)(d_s+d_v) 个元素的存储。
- **BDR（Bounded Directional Rounding）：** 舍入时干预。发现舍入会把更新"反向"写入（reversal，d̃ 与 d 异号）——去除 reversal 误差在四个模型上降低 PPL 的幅度都最大。BDR 对 |d| > ε_B·Δ_t 的反向元素朝 d 方向多舍入一格，并用引理证明过冲不超过 |d|/ε_B；零额外存储。
- **无需训练、跨架构验证：** 在 Gated DeltaNet、GLA、Mamba2、RetNet 四个 1.3B 模型的公开/自训 checkpoint 上直接量化，困惑度接近 FP32，状态显存省 4.7–5.3×，解码加速 1.3–1.9×（RTX 3060、batch 16–48）。

## Key Contributions
- **Coherent-error analysis for recurrent state quantization:** The first quantification of how per-step quantization errors coherently accumulate through the recurrence S_t = A_t·S_{t-1} + B_t. The off-diagonal (cross-step inner-product) terms grow quadratically and dominate ‖E_T‖²_F; the measured coherent gain reaches 86× at 4K context. Hence minimizing single-step error (Hadamard rotations, outlier suppression) is provably insufficient.
- **Root cause — lost updates:** The median nonzero update element is only 0.02–0.21× the quantization grid spacing, so 68–96% of update elements leave the rounded state unchanged at every step. Errors on these lost elements contain the full update with opposite sign and inherit its cross-step correlation; splitting errors into lost vs. moved parts shows the coherent gain is concentrated in the lost part (up to 10⁴ vs ≈1).
- **LRB:** a training-free low-rank bypass that preserves the *magnitude* of small updates before rounding, exploiting that every update is the rank-1 outer product k·vᵀ; merges are done every m steps via rank-k randomized SVD, and elements still below the grid are held back in the bypass.
- **BDR:** a training-free rounding rule that preserves the *direction* of updates still lost at rounding time. It corrects only reversals (the empirically most harmful distortion), moving the element one grid step toward its update when |d| > ε_B·Δ_t, with a lemma bounding the introduced overshoot.
- **Breadth of validation:** consistent gains across four distinct recurrent architectures (GDN, GLA, Mamba2, RetNet) at 1.3B scale, on perplexity (PG-19, WikiText-103 at 1K–8K), zero-shot commonsense (LAMBADA, SciQ, COPA, HellaSwag), recall-intensive tasks (FDA, SWDE, NQ, SQuADv2, TriviaQA, DROP), plus Triton-kernel efficiency measurements.

## Methodology
**Setup.** Four recurrent models share the state recurrence S_t = A_t·S_{t-1} + B_t, with scalar/diagonal decay A_t and rank-1 update B_t = k_t·v_tᵀ (per Table 1 of the paper). States are stored in groups of 128 contiguous value-dimension elements with group-wise asymmetric uniform quantization; a 4-bit grid gives 4.5 effective bits/element (codes + FP32 z/Δ overhead), refit at every write.

**Error-accumulation analysis.** Unrolling the quantized recurrence gives E_T = Σ_τ A_{τ:T}·R_τ. For scalar-decay models the squared Frobenius norm splits into diagonal terms (own error norms, what conventional quantizers minimize) and off-diagonal inner products ⟨R_τ, R_τ'⟩_F weighted by decay products a_{τ:T}·a_{τ':T}. On WikiText-103 sequences, inner products are positive for nearly all step pairs and decay weights stay large for thousands of steps (Mamba2's diagonal decay forgets within a few hundred, explaining its milder degradation). Coherent gain g_T compares accumulated vs. diagonal-only deviation. The lost-update mechanism is verified by masking elements M_t where rounding leaves the update unwritten: g_T^lost (GDN ×32, Mamba2 ×9, GLA ×22, RetNet ×78) dwarfs g_T^moved (≈1).

**LRB.** Between merges the quantized state only decays; each incoming update is appended as one column to the FP16 factors L_t = [A·K_{t-1}, k]·[A·V_{t-1}, v]ᵀ, so queries read o_t = (Ŝ_t + L_t)ᵀq_t. Every m steps a merge computes a rank-k randomized-SVD approximation T_t of the accumulated bypass, holds back elements whose accumulated update is below ε_L·Δ_t (they stay in the bypass), and writes the residual D_t = L_t − L'_t into the quantized state. Bypass storage is (k+m)(d_s+d_v) elements; reported bits/element include it.

**BDR.** The effective update d̃ = Q_t(s+d) − s splits into a rounded update (sign-preserving) and a re-rounding shift (grid refit can push s off-grid, so the shift can oppose d → reversal, which arises only from small lost updates). BDR rewrites reversed elements with |d| > ε_B·Δ_t as Q_t(s+d) + sign(d)·Δ_t, turning the reversal into a bounded overshoot (Lemma 1: |d̃'| < |d|/ε_B). Implemented inside the rounding rule; zero extra storage.

**Experiments.** GDN and GLA use public flash-linear-attention checkpoints; Mamba2 and RetNet are pretrained by the authors on 100B FineWeb-Edu tokens (seq len 4096, AdamW, peak LR 4e-4, 0.5M-token batches). All state quantization is post-training ("Ours" applied directly, no fine-tuning). Baselines: FP32 (32 bits), INT4, INT4+Hadamard (4.5 bits). Evaluation runs every model in recurrent form, one token at a time, with the state kept in low precision throughout — emulating real decoding. Per-model configs (Table 6): k=1 everywhere; m=15 (Mamba2: 5); ε_L = 0.1–0.7; ε_B = 0.25–0.30; average 5.25–5.64 bits/element vs 4.5 for INT4. Efficiency measured with fused Triton kernels on an RTX 3060 at batch 16/32/48.

## Results & Highlights
- **Long-context perplexity collapse averted (WikiText-103, 1.3B models):** At 8K context under 4-bit storage, RetNet perplexity is 1193.21 with INT4 and 918.77 with INT4+Hadamard vs 24.30 for FP32; the proposed method lands at **28.80** — an 890-point improvement over the strongest baseline, within ~4.5 pts of FP32. On GDN at 8K: Ours 26.85 vs INT4+Had 53.71 vs FP32 24.19; on GLA: 24.86 vs 41.69 (FP32 21.57); on Mamba2: 28.76 vs 37.43 (FP32 25.19).
- **The gap grows with context:** On GDN/WikiText-103, INT4+Had degrades from 35.23 (1K) to 53.71 (8K) while Ours goes 31.38 → 26.85 (even improving over FP32's 24.19 trend line); on PG-19/RetNet at 8K, Ours reaches 73.96 vs 3249.92 for INT4+Had and 60.02 for FP32. The advantage over both baselines widens monotonically with context length, matching the coherent-gain analysis.
- **Zero-shot commonsense (Table 3):** LAMBADA accuracy on GDN: 46.01 (Ours) vs 46.38 (FP32) vs 39.90 (INT4); LAMBADA perplexity 12.85 vs 12.53 (FP32) vs 17.94 (INT4). On GDN, GLA, and Mamba2, accuracy stays within 0.5 pts of FP32; only RetNet shows a residual gap (42.42 vs 43.08).
- **Recall-intensive tasks (8-task average, Table 4):** GDN 38.5 (Ours) vs 39.4 (FP32) vs 30.8 (INT4); GLA 39.7 vs 40.7 vs 27.7; Mamba2 35.1 vs 37.9 vs 29.0; RetNet 36.0 vs 40.1 vs 21.4 — the method recovers most of the recall lost to INT4, with FDA (exact-value copying) the task where INT4 degrades hardest.
- **Efficiency (RTX 3060, fused Triton kernels, Fig. 5):** State memory reduced 5.3× on GDN/GLA/RetNet and 4.7× on Mamba2 (the smaller ratio because Mamba2's per-head scalar needs a larger bypass: 5.64 avg bits); end-to-end decoding throughput speedup of 1.3–1.9× vs FP32 states at batch 16–48, growing with batch size as the state becomes a larger bandwidth bottleneck — despite the extra LRB/BDR compute.
- **Ablations (Table 7, WikiText-103):** BDR alone cuts RetNet 8K perplexity from 1193.21 (INT4) to 607.65; LRB alone is stronger (75.19); LRB+BDR combined is best everywhere (28.80). Rank k=1 captures nearly all the benefit over a pure threshold bypass (k=0) — e.g., RetNet 8K drops 75.19 → 31.18 at essentially no extra storage; increasing k beyond 1 only raises bits and worsens PPL. The smallest bypass (k+m=8, +0.38–0.76 bits over INT4) already removes most 8K degradation.

## Authors & Credibility
The paper comes from The Chinese University of Hong Kong (Haotian Li and Liran Dong with equal contribution, Kaicheng Xiao, Guoliang Xing). Guoliang Xing's group is well known for edge-AI and efficient ML-systems research, which fits the paper's systems flavor: the method is implemented with fused Triton kernels and measured on real hardware (RTX 3060), not just in FLOPs. The evaluation is unusually thorough for a quantization paper: four distinct recurrent architectures, two pretrained on the authors' own 100B-token corpus (Mamba2, RetNet) alongside two public flash-linear-attention checkpoints (GDN, GLA), three task suites, a decoding-faithful evaluation protocol (state kept in low precision, one token at a time), per-component ablations, and hyperparameter sensitivity studies (Tables 6–9, Figs. 6–8), plus a formal lemma bounding BDR's overshoot. The arXiv listing (Oct 2026, cs.LG) and its citation of contemporaneous work (DAMP, Quamba2, hybrid Mamba-transformers) indicate current, active engagement with the linear-attention efficiency community.

## Critical Assessment
- **No head-to-head against prior recurrent-state quantizers.** This is the paper's most significant weakness. Related work includes Quamba2 (quantizes Mamba2's state cache to 4 bits with per-channel scales and calibration), MambaQuant (rotation-aligned state quantization for Mamba), and DAMP (mixed-precision GDN/Kimi-Delta-Attention state quantization) — yet all experimental baselines are naive INT4 and INT4+Hadamard. The paper argues its differences (coherence-targeted vs. per-state error; training-free vs. calibration-based) but never measures them; whether LRB+BDR actually beats a calibrated Quamba2 on Mamba2, or DAMP on GDN, is unknowable from this paper. INT4+Had is a weak proxy for the state of the art.
- **Two of four models are self-pretrained.** Mamba2 and RetNet 1.3B checkpoints were trained by the authors on 100B FineWeb-Edu tokens, not official releases. Because all quantization comparisons are within-checkpoint this does not bias method-vs-baseline numbers, but absolute perplexities (e.g., FP32 Mamba2 8K WikiText-103 = 25.19) are not comparable to published Mamba2 numbers, and the claim "substantially outperforms INT4 across the board" is only ever demonstrated on this particular training recipe.
- **Hyperparameter count vs. "training-free" framing.** Four per-model hyperparameters (k, m, ε_L, ε_B) are hand-set differently per architecture (Table 6: ε_L ranges 0.10–0.70). The sensitivity studies (Figs. 6–8) show modest sensitivity and the authors note the optimal ε_L tracks how coherently each model's errors accumulate — but selecting ε_L per model in practice requires the same long-context validation perplexity sweeps the authors ran, which is a form of light calibration the framing glosses over.
- **Residual gap is not negligible everywhere.** RetNet PG-19 at 8K remains 73.96 vs FP32's 60.02 (+23%), and Mamba2's avg 5.64 bits exceeds the flat 4.5 bits of the baselines; the method buys quality partly with ~0.75–1.1 extra bits per element, and only GDN/GLA/RetNet at small context approach FP32 quality.
- **Missing details / scope limits the authors themselves flag:** only recurrent states are quantized (weights/activations stay at original precision, so total inference-cost gains will be smaller than the 5.3× state-memory figure suggests), and hybrid recurrent+softmax-attention models (e.g., Nemotron-style) are untested — arguably the setting where fixed-size states matter most in production.
- On the positive side, the analysis–method loop is genuinely tight: the coherent-gain metric motivates LRB's hold-back threshold ε_L, the reversal finding directly motivates BDR, and the ablation (Table 7) confirms each component's predicted effect, including the predicted architecture ordering (worst coherent accumulation RetNet → largest gains).

## Strengths
- A crisp, quantitative diagnosis (coherent gain, lost-update fraction) that explains *why* existing per-tensor quantization tricks fail on recurrent states, with the diagnosis validated by targeted interventions (removing reversal vs. undershoot vs. overshoot errors, Figs. 2 and 4).
- Both interventions are training-free, calibration-free, architecture-agnostic, and composable; BDR costs zero storage and LRB exploits the rank-1 update structure so its overhead is tiny (k=1 suffices).
- Wide empirical coverage: 4 architectures × 2 LM benchmarks (1K–8K contexts) × zero-shot commonsense × recall-intensive tasks × component/hyperparameter ablations, with a decoding-faithful evaluation protocol.
- Real systems work: fused Triton kernels, on-GPU memory and throughput measurements showing the speedup grows with batch size (1.3× at bs=16 to 1.9× at bs=48), and the reported bits/element honestly include bypass and quantizer metadata overhead.
- Intellectual honesty in analysis: the paper openly reports the counterintuitive result that BDR *increases* single-step rounding error yet improves the model, and resolves it with the direction-matters-more-than-error argument plus a formal bound.

## Limitations
- Baselines omit the closest prior work (Quamba2, MambaQuant, DAMP), so superiority over existing recurrent-state quantization methods is argued, not shown.
- Only 1.3B-scale models; no evidence the coherent-accumulation behavior and hyperparameter settings transfer to larger scales or to hybrid attention/recurrent architectures.
- Quantizes only the recurrent state; weights and activations remain at original precision, so end-to-end inference savings are bounded by the state's share of memory traffic.
- Four hand-tuned per-model hyperparameters (k, m, ε_L, ε_B); the paper shows each is individually insensitive, but their joint selection relies on 8K validation perplexity sweeps per architecture.
- Residual quality gaps persist in the hardest settings (RetNet PG-19 8K: 73.96 vs 60.02 FP32), and Mamba2 requires a larger bypass (m=5, 5.64 avg bits) due to its fast diagonal decay.
- Efficiency results are limited to one consumer GPU (RTX 3060); no datacenter-grade hardware or comparison against FP16/BF16 states.

## Relevance
这篇论文正中"线性注意力/SSM 长上下文推理效率"的核心痛点：它指出状态量化不能照搬权重/KV cache 量化（误差会跨步相干累积），并给出训练免费、可跨 GDN/GLA/Mamba2/RetNet 复用的 LRB+BDR 方案，对任何想把循环模型部署到长上下文、大批量场景的工作都有直接参考价值；其对"小更新被舍入丢失"的分析视角对低精度训练中的状态维护问题同样有启发。

## Questions for Further Exploration
- How does LRB+BDR compare quantitatively against Quamba2/MambaQuant/DAMP under matched bit budgets — do calibration-based per-state methods and coherence-targeted update preservation combine additively?
- Does the coherent-error analysis extend to hybrid Mamba-softmax architectures, where a fraction of state is exact attention — does the lost-update fraction of the recurrent layers still predict end-to-end degradation?
- Can the bypass rank k=1 result (one rank suffices) be proven from the spectrum of accumulated outer products, or is it an empirical coincidence of these four architectures?
- Would joint (not alternating) optimization of ε_L and ε_B, or a per-group adaptive ε_L tied to the live grid spacing, close the remaining RetNet PG-19 gap without per-model tuning?
- How do the Triton merge kernels behave at much larger batch sizes or on H100-class hardware, where the state bandwidth bottleneck and the merge compute scale differently?
- Does preserving small updates also change downstream fine-tuning dynamics — i.e., can QLoRA-style adaptation be run on top of LRB+BDR states without rebuilding the bypass?
