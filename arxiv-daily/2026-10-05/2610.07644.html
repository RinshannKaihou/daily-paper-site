Critical edge

The 12× headline was measured during a 130× fleet ramp, organic failure rate only reached vendor parity, and every comparison is Meta-self-reported against unnamed vendor NICs.

Methodology

Isolate→ Driver-first→ HIL CI→ Observe→ Roll out

Isolate. A 4-host multi-host NIC on a 38 mm² 15 nm part: each host owns its MAC, firmware instance, PCIe interface and 384 KB buffers; only board power and the transceiver are shared. No pause-frame propagation; independent failure and observability domains. The tradeoff is deliberate — per-host buffers forgo statistical multiplexing yet save 28/40/52% per host at 100/200/400 Gbps.

Simplify control. Data-path logic lives in the Linux driver (upstream in v6.11); firmware speaks only 3 request/response pairs, <10 runtime commands vs 170+ on a vendor NIC. Data-path fixes ship as day-scale kernel live-patches, not month-scale firmware certification.

Prove it in hardware. A repurposed rack-switch lab runs 58 HIL cases in two stages: daily Stage 1 pins regressions to firmware/driver/kernel; weekly Stage 2 replays 4 production traffic profiles (2,000 threads, 24 h). Seven isolation tests, derived from real incidents, require >90% line rate and zero pause leakage on undisturbed hosts.

Watch & roll. An on-host agent co-samples NIC + rack-switch counters every 5 min for cross-layer fault attribution; a sub-sled orchestrator upgrades one NIC’s hosts without draining the whole sled.

  • Isolation holds under duress: undisturbed hosts sustain 92.7 of 100 Gbps with zero pause-frame leakage; a congested host amasses >1,000,000 pause frames while its neighbors on the same NIC see <60.
  • Traffic profiles: 4 profiles × 2 tiers, 24 h at up to 179.1 Gbps — zero FEC-uncorrectable errors, zero dropped frames.
  • Service health parity-or-better: TCP retransmit 0.19 vs 0.24 (cache); rack-switch FEC errors ~3× lower; vendor RX out-of-buffer drops 8,958 vs fbNIC 0.0 (cache).
  • Firmware velocity: orchestrator-assisted FW-B reached 96% coverage in 5 wk vs 10 wk trains-only; handled 35–40% of upgrades and closed the 5–10% tail maintenance trains never reached.

Strengths

  • Complete lab-to-fleet narrative — silicon validation, CI, observability, rollout, repair, post-mortems — where most custom-hardware papers stop at performance.
  • Same-platform, 10-month, six-metric quantified comparison against a real vendor baseline (UU, TTR CDF, swap rate, reset rate, diagnostic accuracy, per-service health).
  • Reusable isolation test suite: 7 scenarios with explicit pass criteria (>90% line rate, zero pause leakage) any multi-tenant NIC/DPU team can adopt.
  • Exceptional honesty: two post-mortems with tooling gaps, an admitted 49.2% diagnostic accuracy, explicit buffer/die-area tradeoff accounting.
  • The orchestrator generalizes (already used for retimer and BMC firmware) and solves the general sibling-in-repair upgrade-blocking problem.

Limitations

  • All baselines are Meta-internal — unnamed vendor NICs, Meta-chosen platforms and services; no third-party verification of TTR or swap-rate deltas.
  • Front-end fabric only: AI back-ends with RDMA and per-packet programmability are out of scope, so driver-first tradeoffs there are untested.
  • Diagnostic accuracy below 50% — humans still resolve most ambiguous cases; the observability pipeline’s diagnostic value is only partially realized.
  • Four of eight contributions require custom silicon or in-house firmware/driver ownership, limiting direct adoption.
  • Only 10 months with two dominant software incidents; long-horizon wear-out, first-gen firmware security, and >4 hosts/NIC scaling remain open.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does per-host isolation stay economic at >4 hosts/NIC or 800G, as the fixed 384 KB buffer’s 50–70 µs CPU-stall exposure grows?
  2. Would driver-first survive AI back-end demands — RDMA, congestion control, programmable parsing — that justify vendors’ 170+ firmware commands?
  3. Can 49.2% diagnostic accuracy reach human parity with LLM-based triage over the fleet time-series store?
  4. What break-glass and safety-analysis regime lets a sub-sled orchestrator safely flash BMC firmware, where a bad flash bricks the host’s out-of-band channel?
  5. Which of the seven isolation tests would commodity SmartNICs fail today — and is there an OCP-style standardization path?
