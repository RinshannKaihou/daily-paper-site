Why this matters

Turns “how many samples does my monitoring probe need?” from guesswork into a formula — budget before any deployment traffic exists.

Critical edge

Every label is LLM-produced — high-stakes’ near-ceiling transfer (T = 0.98) may partly reflect shared construction conventions between generator and evaluator, not genuine behavioral coverage.

Method A factorial probe-data instrument

kind-tag the set→ own / mixed / set-minus-own cuts→ read R · G · T→ R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T)
  • Probe: linear head on the residual stream — Gemma-3-27B-IT layer 32, softmax-weighted mean pooling; replicated on Qwen3-8B (L18), Mistral-NeMo-12B (L20), Llama-3.2-1B (L8).
  • Factorial sweep: 3 concepts × 14 held-out eval distributions (6,462 conversations) × 4 probe models × 2 prompt levels × 4 generators (Llama-3.3-70B, GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B; 600 samples per concept each).
  • Learning curves: class-balanced training at n ∈ {10, 30, 80, 110, 170, 350, 590}, eight resampled draws each (492 per-split curves), fit to a log-logistic; half-gain size m = samples securing half of U−L.
  • Attribution: sequential ANOVA over 383 non-flat curves decomposes log m; bootstrap-over-draws intervals; ceilings ≈ 0.98 via 5-fold CV on each eval distribution.
  • Coverage test: three cuts per target split yield R = m_mixed/m_own, G (gain kept without the own split-kind), and T (cross-kind transfer AUROC — computable from a generated set alone).

Real dev-set samples reproduce the order: R = 1.5 / 2.5 / 4.7 · G = 0.95 / 0.76 / 0.51 · T = 0.87 / 0.75 / 0.59. Ceilings ≈ 0.98 for all three concepts, so the gaps are gaps in the synthetic data.

  • The gap is worth real AUROC: cutting training from 590 → 80 samples costs instruction up to 0.21 AUROC on Gemma-3-27B-IT; high-stakes loses only a few hundredths.
  • Concept sets the need: concept identity + split explain 42–45% of log m variance over 383 curves; generator, probe model, prompt detail each <10%. Coverage accounts for 79% (generated) / 66% (real) of the instruction-vs-others gap.
  • Ordering survives models (Qwen3-8B, Mistral-NeMo-12B) — but instruction is unlearnable on Llama-3.2-1B, and harmful’s saturation drifts with model scale (110–350 → past 590).
  • Recipes move level, not ordering: LLM-written prompts lift AUROC up to +0.23; red-teaming gives instruction +0.12 but harmful only +0.01 — never beating the detailed-prompt ordering.
  • Deployment-free budgeting: R ≈ 1 + 2(K−1)(1−T) lands within 2× of measured need on 12/14 real and 8/13 generated splits, with zero deployment traffic.

+ Strengths

  • Cleanest attribution yet: architecture, generators, prompts, protocol all held fixed while the concept varies.
  • Mechanism over observation: the R/G/T coverage test explains 79% of why instruction is hungrier — not just that it is.
  • Replicated on real samples: every ordering claim re-checked on dev-set draws; the effect persists.
  • Honest statistics: bootstrap intervals, sequential ANOVA, CV-derived ceilings, explicit saturation criterion (≤0.001 AUROC per doubling).
  • Practical payoff + full release of evaluation suites, dev sets, and all generated sets.

− Limitations

  • Only three concepts; the concept-level ordering rests on three points (the split-level result on fourteen is stronger but conditional within concept).
  • All supervision is LLM-labeled (30–40% hand-verified); concept validity is bounded by judge conventions.
  • The harmful ceiling is a lower bound (still rising at 590); a third of curve fits have half-gain intervals spanning >10×.
  • Instruction is unlearnable on Llama-3.2-1B — a capability floor under the “ordering holds on smaller models” claim.
  • No genuine deployment traffic; the synthetic-to-deployment gap (real T = 0.59 for instruction) is under-sampled.
  • Absolute counts are suite-specific; only the ordering and the T-based readout are claimed to transfer — and only within a factor of two.

? Questions for further exploration

  • Does the coverage ordering extend to representational concepts — deception, sycophancy, power-seeking?
  • How do half-gain sizes shift when synthetic data is mixed with small amounts of real deployment traffic?
  • Would non-linear probes (MLP heads, attention pooling) shrink per-kind half-gains or change which split-kinds substitute?
  • Is instruction’s failure on Llama-3.2-1B a representational absence at layer 8 or an optimization issue?
  • Does high-stakes’ near-perfect transfer (T = 0.98) survive genuinely deployed conversations rather than constructed suites?
  • Can T be calibrated per concept to tighten the factor-of-two accuracy of the budget formula?
