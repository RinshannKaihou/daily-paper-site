> **一句话总结：** 本文通过把"执行动词 write 换成分析动词 discuss 就能翻转 Qwen3-8B 是否调用工具"这一最小对照对，定位出第 24 层的一个紧凑 tool-call 向量 μ_Δ（加到分析型提示上 100% 诱导 <tool_call>，从执行型提示中减去 100% 抑制调用），并发现该向量本质是"抑制信号的反向"——scaffold 先建立调用先验，分析动词经由 L20–L23 的 MLP 抑制特征把它压下去，且该机制在 Qwen3/Mistral/Granite 共 7 个模型上复现。

> **TL;DR:** Using minimal contrastive pairs that differ only in the request verb (write vs. discuss), built from MBPP/APPS/HumanEval coding tasks, the authors isolate a single "tool-call vector" at layer 24 of Qwen3-8B that causally controls the call-or-no-call decision: adding it to analysis prompts flips tool-call top-1 from 0.00% to 100.00%, and removing it from execution prompts drops it from 100.00% to 0.00% (vs. a norm-matched random vector that flips ~0–1.5% of decisions). The vector transfers without re-estimation to unseen tools (web/SQL/email, 100% each), native τ²-Bench Telecom multi-turn trajectories, and verb-free requests (93.1% suppression at 1× gain vs. 16.2% for the random control). Transcoder decomposition shows the vector is the inverse of a suppression signal: analysis verbs activate suppressor MLP features in layers 20–23 (MLPs supply 77.7% of the formation-window write) that write against a scaffold-induced call prior, and the mechanism replicates across seven models from Qwen3-4B to Mistral-Small-3.2-24B and Granite-3.3-8B.

## 核心贡献
- **把长 agent 提示变成可控因果接口**：发现请求动词是"一个词翻转调用决策"的可控变量——执行动词（write/add/build…）触发工具调用，分析动词（discuss/explore/…）触发直接文本回答，而提示 scaffold、任务与可用工具保持不变；据此构造 500 对 Python/Java/C++ 配对提示（300 对用于机制分析，200 对留作评估），任务来自 MBPP、APPS、HumanEval、CodeContents。
- **定位并验证 tool-call 向量 μ_Δ**：activation patching 把决策定位在 L24 的预测位置（对该位置残差态做 patch 可在 100% 的 held-out 分析提示上恢复 <tool_call> top-1）；μ_Δ 即该位置 clean−corrupt 平均激活差。加入 μ_Δ 使分析提示的调用率从 0.00% 升至 100.00%（logit +7.39，充分性 1.03），去除后执行提示从 100.00% 降至 0.00%（logit −7.48，必要性 1.04）。
- **向量具有模型内普适性**：不重新估计，同一编码任务衍生的向量即可迁移到 web 检索 / SQL 执行 / 邮件派发（每域 100 对，诱导与抑制均 100%，norm-matched 随机向量为 0）、τ²-Bench Telecom 原生多轮轨迹（5,385–15,600 token、16–43 轮），以及不含任何动作动词的 verb-free 请求（1× 增益抑制 93.1%，1.5× 达 100%，随机对照仅 16.2%）。
- **机制解释：向量是抑制的反向**：scaffold（尤其 format template）建立接近饱和的调用先验（完整 scaffold 下中性请求调用概率 0.8486，分析请求 0.0038；去掉 format template 后仅剩 6.32e-7/8.31e-10）。形成窗口 L20–L23 中 MLP 贡献 77.7% 的投影写入；Transcoder 分解显示分析侧抑制特征主导（L23 κ_corrupt/κ_clean = 4.18，窗口级 1.99），执行动词基本只是"不激活抑制特征"。
- **下游读出与跨模型复现**：scaffold-reading heads（L29H9、L33H11、L33H29）与 MLP34/特征 F109925 把 L24 状态读入首个生成 token；机制在 7 个模型（Qwen3-4B/8B/14B/32B/30B-A3B、Mistral-Small-3.2-24B、Granite-3.3-8B）上复现——全态 patch 恢复率 93.5–100%，且每个模型的 κ_corrupt > κ_clean，尽管具体层号与组件各不相同。

## Key Contributions
- A controlled causal interface for long agentic prompts: replacing a single request verb (execution verb like *write* with an analysis verb like *discuss*) reliably flips the call-or-no-call decision while keeping scaffold, task, and tools fixed; 500 paired coding prompts across Python/Java/C++ (300 for analysis, 200 held out).
- A compact tool-call vector μ_Δ, estimated as the mean execution−analysis activation difference at the L24 prediction position of Qwen3-8B, is causally sufficient and necessary: adding it induces `<tool_call>` top-1 on 100% of held-out analysis prompts; removing it suppresses calls on 100% of execution prompts.
- The vector generalizes within the model without re-estimation: unseen tools/domains (web retrieval, SQL execution, email dispatch), native multi-turn τ²-Bench Telecom trajectories with 5,385–15,600 context tokens, and verb-free implicit requests — all far above norm-matched random-vector controls.
- A mechanistic account of how the vector forms: the scaffold installs a near-ceiling call prior; analysis verbs trigger suppressor features in the L20–L23 formation window (MLPs supply 77.7% of the projected write, decomposed via Transcoders) that write against μ_Δ — the vector is the inverse of suppression, not an execution signal.
- A readout story (scaffold-reading heads L29H9/L33H11/L33H29, plus MLP34 and feature F109925 committing to the `<tool_call>` opening) and a replication of the core findings across seven models from three families (Qwen, Mistral, Granite).

## Methodology
- **Behavioral interface.** In Qwen3-8B (non-thinking mode), the call decision reduces to whether `<tool_call>` is the top-1 prediction for the first generated token. Matched prompt pairs share role instructions, tool schemas, format templates, task body, and available tool; only the request verb differs (execution → clean x⁺, analysis → corrupt x⁻). Tasks come from MBPP, APPS, HumanEval, and CodeContents; 500 pairs across Python, Java, C++ (300 training / 200 held-out).
- **Localization.** Activation patching: for each pair, the corrupt run's activation at layer l, position p is replaced with the clean run's; recovery rate r(l,p) is the fraction of prompts whose top-1 becomes `<tool_call>`. Sweeping layers and positions shows the signal migrates from the verb position to the prediction position by L24, where patching recovers 100% of calls.
- **Vector estimation and intervention.** μ_Δ = mean over the 300 training pairs of h_p^(24)(x⁺) − h_p^(24)(x⁻). Interventions add μ_Δ to analysis prompts and subtract it from execution prompts before downstream computation; sufficiency and necessity are measured as normalized logit changes on 200 held-out pairs, with norm-matched random vectors as controls.
- **Generalization tests.** (i) Domain transfer to web_search / run_sql / send_email with new verbs and tool schemas, rescaling the fixed coding direction to the target-domain call/no-call contrast norm; (ii) native τ²-Bench Telecom trajectories (200 decision points per arm, fixed 1× gain, no re-estimation); (iii) 600 verb-free non-imperative requests (questions and statements implying tool need), 160 of which have a baseline tool call and are evaluated for suppression at 1× and 1.5× gains.
- **Formation analysis.** Scaffold-component ablations (role instructions R, tool schemas T, format templates F) on 200 held-out tasks quantify the call prior. Projecting residual and component outputs onto μ̂_Δ identifies the L20–L23 formation window; Transcoders approximate each MLP output as a bias plus ReLU feature writes, each feature scored by κ (activation contrast × projection onto μ_Δ). The top suppressors are zeroed as a causal validation, with layer-matched random suppressor controls.
- **Readout analysis.** Direct logoit attribution (DLA) and single-component patching over L25–L35 identify scaffold-reading attention heads and the structural MLP34 feature F109925; interventions re-check whether these heads/features shift toward execution-side values when μ_Δ is added.
- **Cross-model replication.** The same protocol (localization, intervention, transfer, formation statistics, readout) is applied at fixed block-input layers to Qwen3-4B/8B/14B/32B/30B-A3B, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B, and Granite-3.3-8B.

## Results & Highlights
- **Causal control at L24 (Table 1, 200 held-out pairs):** adding μ_Δ to analysis prompts moves tool-call top-1 from 0.00% to 100.00% (mean `<tool_call>` logit 25.11 → 32.50, Δ +7.39, sufficiency 1.03); removing μ_Δ from execution prompts moves it from 100.00% to 0.00% (logit 32.28 → 24.80, Δ −7.48, necessity 1.04). Patching the L24 prediction-position state alone recovers calls on 100% of held-out analysis prompts.
- **Cross-domain transfer beats random control (Table 2, 100 pairs per domain):** the coding-derived vector achieves 100.0% induction and 100.0% suppression on web retrieval, SQL execution, and email dispatch (mean normalized logit shift 0.88 / 0.94), while a norm-matched random vector changes 0 top-1 decisions.
- **Native multi-turn trajectories (Table 3, τ²-Bench Telecom, 200 decision points per arm):** at 1× gain, vector removal suppresses 36.7% of baseline tool calls (top-1 call rate 99.5% → 63.0%, mean Δ logit −5.55) and addition induces calls on 70.0% of baseline text-response turns (0.0% → 70.0%, Δ +19.42); the norm-matched random vector flips only 1.5% and 0.0% of turns.
- **Verb-free implicit requests:** among 160 evaluated requests where Qwen3-8B would call a tool, μ_Δ removal suppresses 93.1% of calls at 1× gain and 100.0% at 1.5× gain, versus 16.2% for a norm-matched random vector at the same gain.
- **Scaffold ablation (Table 4, 200 held-out tasks):** the full scaffold yields a mean call probability of 0.8486 on neutral requests vs. 0.0038 on analysis requests; removing the format template collapses calls to 6.32e-7 (neutral) / 8.31e-10 (analysis), while the format template alone gives 1.0000 / 0.9952 — the prior is gated by task-relevant content.
- **Formation is MLP- and suppression-dominated:** in the L20–L23 window, MLPs supply 77.7% of the projected write onto μ_Δ (attention 22.3%); MLP23 is the largest single write (22.5× the g1 scale). Features more active on analysis prompts dominate (L23 κ_corrupt/κ_clean = 4.18; window-level 1.99, i.e., 7.28 vs. 14.34); the largest suppressor family has 115 analysis-non-execution features with mean held-out |κ| 0.0633 vs. 0.0156 for the 292-feature execution-request family. Zeroing the 5 trained suppressors raises the call rate from 3.0% to 25.5% (+22.5% strict recovery, 45/200) vs. +0.5% for the layer-matched random control, and restoring the lost μ_Δ projection at L24 recovers the baseline gap and 100% clean calls (200/200).
- **Readout components:** adding μ_Δ at L24 raises scaffold-reading head L33H29's DLA from 11.13 to 24.43 (attention-head DLA increase +13.45); patching MLP34 alone yields a 41.0% call rate, and swapping only feature F109925's analysis-side activation for its execution-side value yields 37.0% vs. 6.5% for the non-structural control P91365.
- **Cross-model replication (Table 6, seven models):** full-state patching recovers calls on 93.5–100.0% of analysis prompts; normalized sufficiency 0.81–1.03 and necessity 0.61–1.04; domain-transfer rates span 43.0–100.0% (Granite-3.3-8B lowest at 43.0%) and verb-free suppression 35.5–100.0%; κ_corrupt > κ_clean holds in every model.

## Critical Assessment
- **Novelty is real but positioned at a junction.** Prior mechanistic work studied short 10–30-token prompts or which tool the model picks; extending function-vector style analysis to the binary call-or-no-call decision under realistic, heavily scaffolded agent prompts is a genuine contribution. The "shaped by suppression" framing (μ_Δ as the inverse of analysis-verb suppressor features, rather than an execution signal) is the most interesting claim and is supported by the κ statistics and the zeroing experiment — not just narrative.
- **Selection bias in the interface.** The paired prompts are behaviorally screened: only pairs where the verb actually flips the decision enter the dataset (the abstract says analysis verbs "reliably flip" the decision, but Figure 2 shows analysis-verb call rates of roughly 5–25% depending on the model, i.e., many flips fail screening). The formation analysis therefore characterizes the mechanism on a curated subset where a single-token decision boundary exists; the paper's own Limitations section concedes the applicability to other prompt constructions "remains to be established."
- **Native-trajectory transfer is much weaker than the synthetic setting.** The headline 100% induction/suppression numbers come from the constructed pairs; on native τ²-Bench Telecom trajectories, 1× removal suppresses only 36.7% of calls and 1× addition reaches 70.0% induction (hence the fixed-gain and norm-matched random controls of 1.5%/0.0%). This gap is honestly reported but undercuts any reading of μ_Δ as a complete steering knob in production agent contexts; the verb-free test also evaluates only the 160 of 600 constructed requests that had a baseline call.
- **Single-direction abstraction.** The authors themselves note (Section 3) that high-fidelity replay requires distributed computation and that circuit searches alone are insufficient; a single mean-difference direction is a lossy summary, and no comparison is made against alternative steering approaches (e.g., SAE-derived directions or per-component ablations as steering baselines). The cross-model table shows the layer, components, and transfer strength all vary (Granite: 43.0% domain transfer, 35.5% verb-free suppression), so the replicated invariant is the qualitative suppression asymmetry (κ_corrupt > κ_clean), not a universal circuit.
- **Scope is the binary decision only.** No evaluation of whether induced calls are appropriate, have valid arguments, or complete the underlying task — a 100% induced call rate says nothing about usefulness. The hybrid-attention Qwen3-30B-A3B partially omits attention statistics, a small but real gap in the "seven models" claim.
- **Reproducibility is good on paper:** public benchmarks (MBPP, APPS, HumanEval, τ²-Bench), a released codebase, appendices covering dataset construction, prompts, and per-model settings. Overall: a careful, unusually honest mechanistic study whose main risk is overgeneralizing a coding-pair-derived vector to "how agentic LLMs decide" writ large.

## Strengths
- A rare causal handle on a decision that actually matters in deployed agents (call vs. no-call), with clean sufficiency/necessity numbers (0%↔100% flips) and proper controls (norm-matched random vectors for every intervention, layer-matched random suppressors, length-matched scaffold replacements).
- The suppression account is mechanistically grounded and quantitatively backed: formation-window write shares (77.7% MLP), per-layer κ ratios (L23 = 4.18), a feature-family contrast (0.0633 vs. 0.0156), and a causal zeroing experiment (3.0% → 25.5% call rate vs. +0.5% control).
- Thorough generalization ladder within one model — new tools/domains, native multi-turn trajectories, and implicit verb-free requests — before any cross-model claims.
- Honest reporting: weaker native-trajectory numbers (36.7% suppression at 1×) are stated plainly, and the Limitations section correctly bounds the claims to the binary decision and the fixed scaffold.
- Code released; public benchmarks only; the paper is self-contained with a clear roadmap (Figure 1) and per-model appendix details.

## Limitations
- Formation analysis is confined to behaviorally screened coding pairs under one fixed scaffold; transfer to other prompt constructions is untested (acknowledged by the authors).
- Only the binary call-or-no-call outcome is studied — correctness of arguments and task completion after (suppressed or induced) calls are out of scope, so the work cannot speak to whether steering improves or degrades agent utility.
- Cross-model replication shows qualitative but not quantitative uniformity: the decision layer varies from L22 to L35, and Granite-3.3-8B reaches only 43.0% domain transfer and 35.5% verb-free suppression, so practitioners should not expect a portable "tool-call direction" across families.
- The formation analysis resolves a scaffold-induced prior and broad suppressive feature families, but not the fine-grained computation mapping scaffold content and request semantics onto the vector; identical formation mechanisms for explicit vs. implicit requests are not established.
- Native-context steering needs gain tuning (1.5× for full verb-free suppression), and the hybrid-attention model's attention statistics are partially omitted.

## Relevance
这篇工作把机制可解释性（activation patching、向量干预、Transcoder 分解）第一次系统地用在了 agent 的"是否调用工具"这一核心决策上，对你关注的 LLM 智能体可靠性（过度调用/漏调用）与可解释性交叉方向直接相关，其 tool-call 向量也提供了干预 agent 行为的轻量手段。

## Questions for Further Exploration
- Does injecting μ_Δ (or zeroing suppressor features) degrade the *quality* of induced calls — are arguments valid and tasks completed, or does steering increase spurious calls on requests that need no tool?
- How does the vector behave under thinking-mode Qwen3, where the call decision may not reduce to a single first-token prediction, and on non-coding scaffolds (e.g., general assistant frameworks like ReAct or tool-use RAG)?
- Is the suppression mechanism (analysis-verb suppressor features in a mid-layer MLP window) also how RLHF- or agentic-SFT-trained models encode "when not to call," and can training interventions amplify or remove it?
- The 1× native-trajectory suppression rate is only 36.7% — what state component is missing in real trajectories, and would a trajectory-conditioned vector (estimated in-context) close the gap to the 100% seen on synthetic pairs?
- Do the identified suppressor feature families overlap with refusal/refusal-direction machinery (the paper cites refusal directions), suggesting a shared "override the default action" circuit across safety and tool-calling?
