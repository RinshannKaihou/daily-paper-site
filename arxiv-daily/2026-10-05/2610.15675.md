> **一句话总结：** 针对量化 VLA 策略"短任务可恢复、长时程/分布外仍拉胯"的问题，本文先通过 5 个 VLA 模型、4,050 次真机试验的系统对比证实 SQL 类 QAT 虽优于 RTN PTQ 但无法恢复长时程失败（π0.5 折叠任务 QAT 进度仅 40.0%、完整成功率仍为 0%，而 FP 为 60.0%），随后提出 QCRL：在 RECAP 优势条件化行为克隆上引入 (1) 无需新专家示教的 FP 策略真机 rollout 扩充、(2) 按子目标完成比例缩放的失败终局惩罚 r_T = -λ_f(1-p(τ))、(3) 区分 reference/quantized 数据来源的条件化标签（部署时取 q=ref、零推理开销）；仅用分布内数据即在 INT4 W4A16 下把 π0.5 三个视觉偏移条件的平均分从 QAT 的 73.5% 拉到 FP 的 99.3%，配合 42.9% 模型显存节省；NVFP4 W4A4 下更达到 FP 的 100.7%，且 NVFP4/FP8 在 Jetson Thor 上相对 FP16 取得 1.73× 加速。

> **TL;DR:** A 5-model, 4,050-trial real-robot study shows QAT (SQL) recovers short-horizon quantized VLA failures but not long-horizon or OOD ones (on π0.5 folding, QAT lifts milestone progress from PTQ's 15.0% to 40.0% while full success stays at 0.0% vs 40.0% for FP). The authors propose QCRL, an offline-RL extension of RECAP's advantage-conditioned cloning that (a) augments demonstrations with real-robot rollouts from the task-trained FP policy, (b) scales the terminal failure penalty by progress p(τ), and (c) conditions on a source label (ref/quant) so deployment imitates reference behavior with zero inference overhead. On INT4 W4A16, QCRL raises π0.5's mean score on three visual-shift conditions from 73.5% of FP (QAT baseline) to 99.3% of FP with 42.9% memory savings; on NVFP4 W4A4 it reaches 100.7% of FP, and NVFP4/FP8 runs 1.73x faster than FP16 on Jetson Thor (96.61 ms -> 55.85 ms latency).

## 核心贡献

- **系统对比研究**：在 π0、π0.5、π0-FAST、SmolVLA、GR00T N1.5 五个 VLA 上，用统一真机协议（Trossen ViperX，扫地/抓放/叠杯/叠衣/清桌 5 任务，4,050 次真机试验）对比 FP、RTN PTQ 与 SQL QAT，量化揭示"短任务可恢复、长时程与视觉/语义 OOD 恢复不全"的结构性缺陷——QAT 的模仿学习目标只用动作监督 + FP 输出蒸馏，从未显式利用 rollout 成败或部分完成信号。
- **QCRL 方法**：三组件的离线 RL 恢复方案——(1) FP rollout 扩充：用同骨干任务训练后的 FP 策略在真机采集成功/部分成功轨迹，构造 D_mix = D_SFT ∪ D_FP ∪ D_Q，无需新专家示教；(2) 进度缩放终局惩罚：失败回报从 -λ_f 变为 -λ_f(1-p(τ))，让部分完成的失败轨迹比彻底失败获得更轻的惩罚并进入优势估计；(3) 量化感知条件化：每个优势组内加来源标签 q_t∈{ref,quant}，部署时固定 I_t=pos、q_t=ref，推理零额外开销。
- **理论支撑（自认是设计指南而非保证）**：有限时域代理界 J_π(π_s) − J_π(π_Q^0) ≥ S_r − 2T·A_max·ε_BC − 2·A_max·T(T−1)·α_s²，把"FP 源选择有益"与 PTQ 状态邻近性、行为差异 TV 距离联系起来。
- **诊断与消融**：动作空间 L2 距离、Action Chunk Entropy (ACE)、Nuisance Mass Ratio (NMR) 三套诊断，加 8 组合消融，把增益归因到各组件（FP rollout 数据是最大单项贡献，进度缩放次之）。
- **端侧部署验证**：INT4 W4A16（省 42.9% 显存）与 NVFP4 W4A4（Jetson Thor 上 1.73× 加速、89.11 W vs 105.80 W）两种量化路线下均验证恢复效果。

## Key Contributions

- A controlled comparative study across five VLA models (π0, π0.5, π0-FAST, SmolVLA, GR00T N1.5) and 4,050 real-world trials on a Trossen ViperX showing that SQL-style QAT systematically fails to restore long-horizon task completion (folding/clean-desk) and visual/semantic OOD robustness, because the imitation objective never uses rollout success or partial-completion signals.
- QCRL, an offline RL method extending RECAP's advantage-conditioned behavior cloning with three targeted changes: FP rollout augmentation (reference trajectories from the same-backbone FP policy, no new expert demonstrations), a progress-scaled terminal penalty r_T(τ) = -λ_f(1 - p(τ)) that credits partial completion, and quantization-aware source conditioning (q ∈ {ref, quant}) that lets the deployed quantized policy select reference behavior at no inference cost.
- A finite-horizon surrogate bound (Appendix B) that explicitly frames FP source selection as a design guide tied to reference-action benefit near PTQ states, with honest discussion that it is not a policy-improvement guarantee.
- Component-resolved evidence (Table III diagnostics, 8-combination ablation) plus hardware measurements on Jetson Thor and B200, separating "low-bit recovery" from "general adaptation" via FP-control deltas (ΔFP vs ΔQ).

## Methodology

**Comparative study (Sec. III).** Five VLA families with different backbones/action heads get task-trained FP checkpoints, then RTN PTQ and SQL QAT (both quantize the language tokenizer to INT4, group size 128, keeping vision/action modules at 16-bit). Evaluation: 3 short tasks (sweep, pick-and-place, stacking; 30 trials each) and 2 long-horizon tasks (folding, K=2 folds; clean desk, K=4 items; 10 trials), plus visual OOD (appearance, illumination, sensor degradation, distractors) and semantic OOD (object/role replacement, added context) at 10 trials per condition. Metrics: full success and milestone progress S_progress = (1/K)·Σ n_k/M.

**QCRL (Sec. IV).** Built on RECAP: fit a value model V_φ, compute N-step advantages Â_t, threshold into labels I_t ∈ {pos, neg}, train with advantage-conditioned behavior cloning. QCRL changes three things:
1. *FP rollout augmentation*: the task-trained FP policy π_FP^0 executes on the real robot to collect D_FP; the training mixture is D_mix = D_SFT ∪ D_FP ∪ D_Q (Eq. 5). Unlike SQL's action-level distillation, this adds reference *experience* — Table III shows FP rollouts reach 36.7% folding success and 86.0±0.0 short-task success where PTQ rollouts get 0.0% and 69.3±6.7, and the FP policy's action-space L2 distance to PTQ is 0.87±0.21 vs 18.07±3.57 for an external policy, so FP behavior is transferable near PTQ states.
2. *Progress-scaled terminal penalty*: r_T(τ) = -λ_f(1 - p(τ)) (Eq. 6) replaces RECAP's flat -λ_f, where p(τ) is the fraction of required subgoals completed; value targets then distinguish degrees of completion so partially successful failures still inform per-step advantages.
3. *Quantization-aware conditioning*: each advantage group carries a source label q_t (Eq. 7-8), so reference and quantized behavior are separately selectable; deployment fixes l_t = pos, q_t = ref (Eq. 9), meaning the quantized policy imitates reference behavior — value model, progress annotation, and FP rollouts are offline-only, so inference cost is unchanged.

**Theory (Sec. IV-D, App. B).** A performance-difference identity plus finite-horizon TV coupling yields the bound J_π(π_s) − J_π(π_Q^0) ≥ S_r − 2T·A_max·ε_BC − 2·A_max·T(T−1)·α_s², positive when reference-action benefit at PTQ states exceeds approximation and state-distribution penalties (both grow with horizon T) — motivating source selection but explicitly not certifying it.

**Training setup (Table IV).** Value model 18k updates, policy 30k updates, AdamW, γ=1, N=8, β_u=5/7; RECAP+QAT (same 30k budget, FP rollups but without QCRL's three components) is the matched-budget baseline; an FP RECAP control distinguishes general adaptation from low-bit recovery.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline recovery (π0.5, real-world visual shifts)**: with INT4 W4A16, QAT recovers only 73.5% of FP's mean task score across the three visual-shift conditions; QCRL reaches 99.3% of FP with 42.9% model-memory savings. Under NVFP4 W4A4, QAT sits at 87.0% of FP while QCRL reaches 100.7%.
- **Long-horizon real-world (Table VI, INT4 W4A16)**: π0.5 folding full success goes PTQ 0.0% -> QAT 40.0% -> QCRL 60.0%, matching FP's 60.0% (Fig. 4 shows QAT failing at the second fold where QCRL completes); GR00T N1.5 sweep, broken by QAT to 66.7% from FP's 100.0% and PTQ's 93.3%, is fully restored to 100.0% by QCRL. Clean desk remains partial only: π0.5 progress 70.0% (FP) vs 40.0% (PTQ), 52.5% (QAT), 65.0% (QCRL).
- **Beyond-general-adaptation evidence (NVFP4, Table VIII)**: FP-control deltas are ΔFP = 0.0 pp on appearance shifts and 7.5 pp on added context, while QCRL's gains over QAT are ΔQ = 23.3 pp and 30.0 pp respectively — the recovery exceeds what FP adaptation itself gains; on added context QCRL reaches 75.0% vs QAT's 45.0%.
- **LIBERO (Table IX, INT4 W4A16)**: π0.5 LIBERO-Long: FP 93.2, PTQ 92.4, RECAP+QAT 93.0, QCRL 94.8 (+2.4 pts over PTQ, +1.8 over RECAP+QAT, above unadapted FP but below RECAP-adapted FP's 94.3); GR00T LIBERO-Long 87.8 vs FP's 87.0.
- **Ablation (Table X)**: all-off (RECAP+QAT) 93.0 Long / 58.3 Sensor -> all-on QCRL 94.8 / 68.3; FP rollout augmentation alone is the largest single component (Sensor 58.3 -> 65.0); deployment conditioning matters (q=ref 94.8/68.3 vs q=quant 93.8/65.0).
- **Hardware (Tables VII-VIII, Jetson Thor)**: FP16 6.46 GiB model memory -> 3.60 GiB (INT4 W4A16, -42.9%) or 2.80 GiB (NVFP4); NVFP4/FP8 latency 55.85±0.10 ms vs FP16's 96.61±2.37 ms (1.73x) and power 89.11 W vs 105.80 W; on B200 14.16 ms vs 17.68 ms.
- **Honest negatives**: GR00T real-world stacking QCRL (23.3%) lands below QAT (33.3%); π0-FAST and SmolVLA QAT stay below FP on pick-and-place and stacking and are not rescued by QCRL (not evaluated); a longer-trained SmolVLA-L (10x FP steps, 20x QAT steps) cannot complete folding or clean desk at ANY precision (FP full success 0.0%).

## Critical Assessment

The comparative study is the strongest asset: 4,050 real trials, matched tokenization across methods, and honest treatment of noise (the authors explicitly warn that Table X point estimates "do not establish the statistical significance of small differences" — with 10 trials per OOD cell, one trial moves a score by 10 pp, so differences like NVFP4's 94.0 vs 93.0 on LIBERO-Long are thin). The paper is commendably self-critical about scope: the Appendix B bound is presented as a design guide, not a theorem-backed guarantee; ACE and NMR are declared non-monotonic diagnostics rather than objectives; and null-input results are separated from instruction-following success.

But the attribution story deserves scrutiny. First, FP rollout augmentation is a *data* intervention — the ablation itself shows it is the largest single contributor (sensor degradation 58.3% -> 65.0% alone), so part of QCRL's win is "more and better real-robot data from a strong teacher," which a budget-matched RECAP+QAT without FP rollouts cannot access; the cleanest matched-data comparison (FP-rollout-only, no progress scaling, no conditioning) is 94.0/65.0 vs QCRL's 94.8/68.3, meaning the two novel RL components add only +0.8/+3.3 pp on this evidence. Second, QCRL's pipeline is heavy: it needs the task-trained FP checkpoint executing on real hardware (50 FP episodes per real task), a separately trained SinLIP2+Gamma-270M value model, and per-task progress annotation — a large operational cost relative to plain QAT, only amortizable if the 42.9% memory saving is the binding constraint. Third, coverage of models is asymmetric: the five-model study motivates the problem, but QCRL is only evaluated on π0.5 (INT4 + NVFP4) and GR00T N1.5 (INT4) — the weakest models (π0-FAST, SmolVLA), where QAT recovery visibly fails, get no QCRL rescue, and GR00T stacking actually regresses under QCRL (23.3% vs QAT 33.3%). Fourth, the headline "99.3% of FP" aggregates only the three visual-shift conditions for π0.5; semantic shifts and clean desk remain open (65.0% vs 70.0% FP progress), and Fig. 3(c) shows QAT still trailing FP on carrot/plate/pot transfers. No code release is mentioned, which limits reproducibility of the real-robot protocol despite the excellent documentation.

## Strengths

- Rare scale and candor for real-robot quantization work: 4,050 trials, five architecturally diverse VLAs, per-cell OOD tables in the appendix, and explicit warnings about 10-trial noise and non-normalized diagnostics.
- The failure analysis (short tasks recover, long-horizon and OOD do not; QAT restores task stages without restoring completion) is specific, mechanistically motivated, and directly generates the method.
- Every design choice is ablated against a matched-budget RECAP+QAT and an FP-adaptation control, cleanly separating low-bit recovery from general adaptation (ΔFP=0.0 vs ΔQ=23.3 on appearance).
- Deployment realism: zero inference overhead (conditioning collapses to fixed labels), measured TensorRT latency/power/memory on Jetson Thor and B200, and two quantization formats (weight-only INT4 and joint NVFP4 W4A4).
- Intellectual honesty throughout: theory scoped as a guide, negative results reported (GR00T stack regression, SmolVLA-L unsolvable at any precision, clean-desk gains partial only).

## Limitations

- Requires the FP teacher to execute on the real robot to collect rollouts plus a trained value model — substantial data-collection and training infrastructure beyond QAT; the paper does not report how gains degrade with fewer FP rollouts.
- Novel-component gains over the FP-rollout-only ablation are modest (+0.8 Long / +3.3 Sensor), and small deltas rest on 10-trial OOD cells without significance testing (acknowledged by the authors).
- Validated on only 2 of 5 model families (π0.5, GR00T N1.5); π0-FAST and SmolVLA — where QAT failure is most severe — are left unaddressed, and GR00T real-world stacking regresses under QCRL.
- Residual gaps persist on semantic shifts and the hardest long-horizon task (clean desk: 65.0% vs FP 70.0% progress); the method recovers visual-shift failures most cleanly.
- No code release stated; reproducibility of the real-robot protocol (Trossen ViperX, CLIPSeg-thresholded masks, fixed seeds) depends on the appendix text alone.

## Relevance

对量化/端侧机器人部署方向极具参考价值：它用大规模真机数据定量刻画了"QAT 恢复不了长时程与 OOD 失败"这一普遍痛点，并给出一个推理零开销、可直接套在 RECAP 类优势条件化训练上的恢复方案。若你的工作涉及 VLA 压缩、离线 RL 微调或边缘部署，Table II/VIII 的失败结构与 ΔFP/ΔQ 归因方法都值得直接借用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much does QCRL degrade as the number of FP teacher rollouts shrinks — is there a data-efficiency curve, and can FP rollouts be replaced by simulated or learned world-model rollouts?
- Would progress-scaled penalties and source conditioning rescue the weakest models (π0-FAST, SmolVLA) where QAT fails most, or does the approach presuppose a reasonably competent quantized policy?
- The bound's state-distribution term grows as T(T-1): does an explicit state-coverage objective (beyond fixed thresholding ε_t at the 30th percentile) close the remaining clean-desk gap?
- Can the significance problem at 10 trials/cell be addressed with hierarchical Bayesian estimates or sequential testing, and do the +0.8/+3.3 pp component gains survive?
- Does combining QCRL with activation-quantization-aware conditioning (W4A4 vision/action towers, currently kept 16-bit) change which failure modes dominate?
