Spurious forgetting is loss of access, not of storage — removing one singular direction per weight update restores most “lost” facts.

When Forgetting is not Catastrophic: On the Mechanics of Spurious Forgetting

Vedant Palit · Florent Draye · Nicolas Zucchet · Zhijing Jin, et al. — MPI for Intelligent Systems, Tübingen

Key metric
1,106
old facts collapse — then mostly come back

Recall of 1,106 previously-correct CounterFact facts collapses under no-replay finetuning of OLMo 2 1B on 1,415 new facts. Subtracting the top singular direction of every weight update restores most of them; a random direction of the same norm restores nothing.

Evidence is 3-seed training curves; per-step recovery sizes are not tabulated in the paper.

Why this matters

An operational criterion for no-replay finetuning and model editing: whether new data shares an answer-predicting cue decides if old knowledge is merely hidden — or slowly eroded for good.

Critical edge

The condition closest to natural data — real EntityQuestions entities — is precisely the one where recovery never happens without the retrospective edit, tempering the “forgetting is reversible” message.

▸ Methodology — one mechanism, three levels of realism

  1. Minimal associative-memory model Two layers: W₁ → RMSNorm → softmax(W₂·). Keys mix a shared component with orthogonal idiosyncratic parts (strength α); old and new answers are drawn from vocabulary halves. A gradient-flow ODE predicts when the shared “common shift” grows or retracts.
  2. Controlled Transformer 8 layers, d=512, attention-only, pre-LN; pretrained 16k steps on synthetic biographies (2,000+2,000 individuals). Finetuned on 300 unseen individuals whose answers sit in one vocabulary half (overlap) or both (control). Logit changes decomposed into common shift + per-fact drift.
  3. Pretrained OLMo 2 1B Finetuned without replay (3 seeds). Old facts: 1,106 correct CounterFact facts (422 held out). New facts: synthetic individuals whose names reveal nothing, vs 1,415 real product→manufacturer facts whose subjects do hint at the answer type.
Rescue intervention: at every step, subtract the top singular direction of each weight update — the rank-one direction the new facts jointly write. Control: a random direction of the same norm. Metric throughout: top-1 first-answer-token accuracy on held-out phrasings.

▸ Results — beyond the headline

  • The regime asymmetry is the point. With synthetic individuals the shared shift gets withdrawn once new facts are learned (recovery); with real entities the shift keeps helping answer selection, so it is never retracted and old recall keeps sliding.
  • The intervention beats its matched control decisively. The edited model recalls more old facts than the trained model did at the same new-fact accuracy; the random-direction control leaves recall exactly where training left it.
  • Scale tunes the collapse, not its cause. 250→1,000 new individuals deepens and lengthens it; across batch sizes the collapse bottom moves ~3× in steps yet always lands at nearly the same new-fact accuracy — as the theory’s margin condition predicts.
  • Decomposition confirms causality. Removing only the common part of the logit change erases the collapse entirely; keeping only the common part reproduces collapse-and-recovery by itself.

▸ Strengths

  • Three-level design with no gaps: every mechanism claim is re-checked at the next level of realism, mapped analysis-by-analysis across models.
  • Matched negative controls done right: random-direction ablation of equal norm, full-vocabulary value condition, ReLU / no-norm variants — each necessity claim has a specific control.
  • Predictive, not decorative theory: the shift-retraction ODE is confirmed by the batch-size experiment (collapse ends at a fixed new-fact accuracy, not a fixed step count).
  • A useful conceptual output: forgetting = reversible shared loss of access + slow per-fact erosion, reconciling “SFT hurts” findings with recovery phenomena. Code released; setups specified to the seed.

▸ Limitations

  • Factual recall only: skills, reasoning, and distribution-level capabilities are out of scope — “not catastrophic” should not be over-generalized.
  • Practicality gap: the edit is retrospective, needs every update’s top singular direction, has no cost analysis, and is untested against a simple replay buffer.
  • Single pretrained model (OLMo 2 1B, pre-norm only); MoE, post-norm, and RLHF-style updates untested.
  • Confound the authors concede: the synthetic-vs-real contrast is attributed to “subjects reveal answers,” but the regimes differ in other ways and no experiment isolates that factor. Headline effects are read from curves, not tabulated.

▸ Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the shared-direction account survive post-norm architectures, MoE models, or larger scale?
  2. Can the edit become a cheap online estimate (power iteration), and does it beat a replay buffer at matched compute?
  3. What governs the per-fact erosion rate (the 1/nₖ scaling) — could permanent loss be predicted in advance?
  4. Do instruction tuning or RLHF-style updates show the same common-shift signature?
  5. In the realistic regime, is there a data-side fix (subjects spanning answer regions) that lets normalization retract the shift on its own?
