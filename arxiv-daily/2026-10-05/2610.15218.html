In recurrent-depth LMs, cutting inference loops destroys refusal far faster than capability — inference compute is itself a safety parameter.

“Running Fewer Loops Removes Refusal Behaviour in Recurrent-Depth Language Models” — arxiv.org/abs/2610.15218

S. Azizi, E. Baghaei Potraghloo, A. Fayyazi, et al. · USC
safety-alignment recurrent-depth-LLM test-time-compute adaptive-computation
Key metric
5.3×
rise in harmful compliance from halving the loops

Ouro-1.4B-Thinking at 2 of 4 loops: compliance 0.038 → 0.205, while GSM8K keeps 89% of full-depth accuracy (0.775 → 0.692). On Ouro-2.6B-Thinking at 1 loop the jump is 9.9× (0.035 → 0.344).

Why this matters

Test-time compute savings can silently strip safety alignment. Anyone deploying recurrent-depth models must audit refusal at every loop count, not just accuracy.

推理计算量本身就是安全参数：省算力的部署会静默丢失拒答能力。

Critical edge

The headline ordering is softer than the abstract implies: “refusal falls faster than task ability” is significant in only 1 of 3 checkpoints at one loop — the robust claim is refusal vs harm detection, not vs capability.

01 Methodology

a

Freeze weights, turn one knob

Four released Ouro checkpoints (1.4B/2.6B × base/Thinking, r=4 whole-stack loops) plus Huginn-0125 (core block repeats, avg 32 reps). bfloat16 on one H100; weights never modified — only the loop count changes.

b

Two-sided safety metric

260 harmful + 200 topic-matched harmless prompts (JailbreakBench / AdvBench / XSTest). Headline: refusal gap D = Pr[refuse|harmful] − Pr[refuse|harmless]; compliance judged by Qwen3Guard-Gen-4B + refusal-phrase check; GSM8K (120 problems) for ability.

c

Hygiene against shallow-text confounds

Text-quality filter (trigram rep ≤0.25, dup-word ≤0.02) with per-cell keep rates; four cross-checking instruments including a generation-free forced-choice test; an 8B second judge; 3-seed sampled decoding.

d

Probe, then intervene

Logistic probes on post-loop states (5-fold CV, AUC); a difference-of-means refusal direction, ablated / re-added via a hook on the shared norm layer with random-direction controls.

e

Three training arms

LoRA (r16) on 1,200 refusal pairs + 600 general + 600 GSM8K; arms differ only in which readout the loss supervises — final-loop, exit-gate mixture, or one uniformly random loop.

  • Dense controls invert the order. Pruning Qwen3-1.7B to 26/28 layers keeps 0.894 of its refusal gap (compliance falls 0.089 → 0.047); Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct retains +0.297. Refusal is last to go in dense models, first in looped ones — “less compute” alone doesn’t explain it.
  • A direction, not information, is missing. Ablating the refusal direction at full depth drops Ouro-1.4B harmful refusal 0.950 → 0.130; adding it at 1 loop moves the gap −0.170 → +0.220 (random-direction control −0.050).
  • Run-time repairs fail. The early-exit gate’s harmful-vs-harmless depth difference never exceeds 0.058 loops; a label-told router stays within [−0.060, +0.111] of a fixed depth, while the per-prompt oracle hits D = 0.896 at just 1.52 mean loops.
  • Random-loop training wins. 1-loop refusal gap +0.118 → +0.399 (3.4×); harmless over-refusal 0.865 → 0.585 (exit-gate arm: 0.926); full-depth GSM8K unchanged (0.783 = 0.783); seed ranges disjoint (+0.371..+0.520 vs +0.024..+0.118).
  • Second family holds. On a retrained Huginn-0125, random-depth supervision keeps compliance ≤0.040 at every depth 1–32 (final-loop training reaches 0.194 at 4/32 loops, 6.2× full depth).

Threat model needs no attacker: a serving stack cuts loops for cost and re-tests quality but not safety. With jailbreak templates the effects compose — 0.080 full depth → 0.447 one loop → 0.830 both.

03 Strengths

  • First-mover on a real deployment knob (loop count) with a threat model requiring no attacker and no weight tampering.
  • Measurement hygiene rare in safety evals: four instruments that fail differently, per-cell keep rates, two-sided refusal-gap metric, second judge, seed retrains, conservative numbers throughout.
  • Causal mechanism, not just probing: ablating / re-adding one direction moves refusal, with random-direction and per-model controls.
  • Cheap, actionable fix validated at zero full-depth accuracy cost, across seeds, on unseen jailbreak templates, and on a second architecture with an 8× depth range.

04 Limitations

  • Ordering vs task ability is fragile: significant in only 1 of 3 Ouro checkpoints at one loop; the unambiguous contrast is vs harm detection.
  • Small scale: 260/200 prompts, 120 GSM8K problems per cell; 1.4B 1-loop cells survive filtering at only 0.377–0.585 keep rates.
  • Fix shown narrowly: one Ouro size plus a self-trained Huginn adapter (the released Huginn has almost no refusal to lose); not yet tested with RLHF/DPO-scale alignment.
  • Mechanism incomplete: adding the direction restores separation on only one checkpoint; why the direction settles late is unexplained.
  • No red-team-grade attacks: three fixed templates, no GCG/adaptive sweeps; only 1.4–2.6B-class models, scale interaction unexplored.

05 Questions for further exploration

Scaling the fix. Does the random-loop objective survive RLHF/DPO and ≥2.6B models, or is the LoRA-SFT result an artifact of a small recipe?

Why late settlement? Is the refusal direction’s late convergence in weight-tied recurrence predictable from direction cosine alone on a new checkpoint?

Smarter routers. Could a learned per-prompt depth router close the gap to the oracle (D = 0.896 @ 1.52 mean loops) — and would that router itself be a new attack surface?

Generality. Is “refusal first-to-go” specific to weight tying, or does it extend to mixture-of-depths and early-exit transformers in production?

Stronger attacks. Does low depth combined with GCG or multi-turn attacks exceed the 0.830 observed with templates, and can depth-aware training close that too?
