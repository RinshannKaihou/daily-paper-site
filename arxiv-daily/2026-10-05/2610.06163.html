In agentic vulnerability repair, 92.6% of silent failures originate in security-reasoning decisions — not in the code-writing step.

Where Did the Repair First Go Wrong? Localizing the Origins of Silent Failures in Agentic Vulnerability Repair

Why this matters

Moves agent failure analysis from “did the patch pass” to “where did security intent first diverge” — a blueprint for diagnosing agent behavior and LLM-as-a-judge evaluation.

Critical edge

The origin construct has no gold standard: repeatability of α = 0.96 is self-consistency, not correctness — and 87 of the 95 cases come from just 15 CVEfixes tasks.

Methodology · SAGE in three phases

01

Mine silent failures

150 security-repair tasks (SecurityEval + CVEfixes) × 6 frameworks × 6 base models = 5,400 runs → 3,684 valid traces. Patches pass L0 syntax → L1 functional → L2 Bandit → L3 exploitability screening; manual confirmation (κ = 0.73) leaves 95 confirmed silent failures.

02

Localize the origin turn

Traces are normalized into canonical turns. An external judge (GPT-5.6 Sol, outside the evaluated set) scores each turn prefix-only — anti-hindsight — on 4 security dimensions (0–3, every score citing a verbatim quotation). Per-write artefact snapshots give provenance; a fixed decision rule picks the earliest qualifying turn.

03

Validate from multiple sources

3× repeated scoring, blind cross-judge replication (Claude Opus 5), 1,000 task-disjoint resamples + leave-one-task-out, and dual human annotators (R1: all 95 cases; R2: 60).

  • 72/73 inherited-vulnerability cases still get an origin from recorded reasoning — a diff-based analysis alone would explain none of them.
  • Missed detection opportunities flagged in 59/95 (62.11%) of cases — but this rests on the two least-stable scoring dimensions (cross-judge α = 0.68).
  • Final-only traces (no intermediate snapshots) are consistently the least localizable — preserve per-write artefact history.

Strengths

  • Precisely operationalized problem: localizing divergence in test-passing patches, where no failure signal exists for conventional attribution methods.
  • Strict separation of judge-dependent outputs (origin type/turn) from artefact-derived outputs (provenance, introducing write) — with honest discussion of what each metric does and doesn’t measure.
  • Reliability protocol well above the norm: repeated scoring, blind cross-judge, 1,000 task-disjoint partitions, dual annotators — reporting intervals, not single aggregates.
  • Deterministic, auditable decision rule with mandatory verbatim quotations and automated consistency checks.
  • Actionable engineering implication backed by data: keep per-write artefact snapshots.

Limitations

  • Small, concentrated corpus: 95 failures from 19 tasks (87 from CVEfixes); one SSRF CVE alone yields 16 cases; subgroups like CAMEL n=4, Qwen3.8-Max n=1 are descriptive only.
  • No ground truth for the origin turn itself; human validation covers only provenance and the introducing write — where the two annotators agree relatively weakly (κ = 0.58).
  • Possible inflated agreement: judge-family overlap (GPT-5.6 Sol ↔ GPT-5.5; Claude Opus 5 ↔ Claude Opus 4.8) and annotator R1’s dual role in corpus and rule design.
  • Python + security benchmarks only; generalization to other languages, tasks, or deployed agents is untested.
  • Observational: no evidence yet that safeguards placed at origins actually reduce silent-failure rates.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Would an explicit security-requirement checkpoint at the flagged missed-opportunity turns measurably reduce silent failures — and at what token/latency overhead?
  2. Can origin-turn localization be validated against controlled fault injection with known positions, instead of only repeatability and provenance agreement?
  3. Would a cross-family judge ensemble with disagreement-triggered human review close the α = 0.68 cross-judge gap on Verification Rigour and Scope Awareness?
  4. How does artefact-history reconstruction behave on repository-level, multi-file repairs where snapshotting every write is expensive or unavailable?
