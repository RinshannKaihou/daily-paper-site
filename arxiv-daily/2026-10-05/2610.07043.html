NVFP4+TRIAGE matches full-precision accuracy while naive NVFP4 collapses to 47.10% and TIS stalls at 50.76%.

Why this matters

First reproducible recipe for stable end-to-end native-FP4 RL: mismatch control upgrades from amplitude to direction — directly relevant to low-precision LLM training systems.

Critical edge

The 30B “near-BF16” headline leans on an AIME25 outlier (30 problems, no error bars) while AMC23 silently drops −10.32 points.

■ Methodology · gate by direction

  1. Diagnose per 64-token segment. Mean gap δ̄ < τₗₕₘ=−0.5 (or < τₗₖₙ=−1.5) or deep-tail hits (δ < −6.0, K = 2) mark a risky segment.
  2. Direction-selective gate. Down-weight only A<0, δ<0 tokens inside it (floor w_sev=0.1); contracting tokens keep weight 1; positive-advantage responses are never gated.
  3. Rebalance. Per-response renormalization reallocates update mass away from gated tokens instead of uniformly weakening the response.
  4. Repair the A⁺ side. Bounded pseudo-Huber term (β=0.022, λ₁ₑₖ=0.015) on 128-token segments adds pressure gating can’t (it is multiplicative).

Native NVFP4 W4A4 forward is kept on both sampler and learner — no fake-quantized alignment. Thresholds fixed pre-run; only the objective changes.

■ Strengths

  • Crisp reframing with a confirmed prediction: A·δ separates amplifying from contracting mismatch, and the asymmetry-before-tail-explosion signature holds across both models.
  • Method follows the diagnosis: segment scale because response means provably hide localization; gating confined to A⁻δ⁻; repair only where multiplicative gating cannot act.
  • Native W4A4 on both paths makes the efficiency numbers meaningful and trustworthy — unlike fake-quantization or QAT-alignment alternatives.
  • Clean ablations: repair fixes reward, gating fixes the gap; each component’s role is isolated, and the limitations section is unusually candid.
  • The exact trade the RL-systems community wants on Blackwell: ~2.3× rollout at full-precision-level accuracy.

■ Limitations

  • Scale and horizon: no 100B-scale models; runs end at convergence, so long-run directional dynamics are unknown.
  • Synchronous RL only: untested under asynchronous sampling, where policy staleness adds a second mismatch source — arguably where it matters most.
  • End-to-end ceiling: ~1.21–1.30× overall vs 2.3× rollout; synchronization and quantization reductions sit outside accelerated GEMMs.
  • Hand-fixed thresholds (τₗₖₙ=−1.5, τₙₖₘₜ=−6.0, K = 2, β, λ) with no sensitivity study; per-model τₖₗₖ calibration hints they may not transfer.
  • Benchmark noise unquantified: no CIs on 30-problem benchmarks; the AMC23 −10.32 regression is unexplained; TIS baseline tuned at a single C=2.0.

■ Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the A·δ criterion keep its predictive power under asynchronous RL, where δ mixes quantization error with policy lag?
  2. How sensitive is TRIAGE to its fixed thresholds across reward scales and model families — could τₖₗₖ-style per-model calibration replace them?
  3. Is the 30B AMC23 regression (−10.32) genuine capability loss from gating/repair, or small-sample noise — and does it grow with training length?
  4. Can the 1.3× end-to-end ceiling be raised by co-designing gate/repair kernels with fused quantization paths, or does parameter sync fundamentally cap native-FP4 RL speedups?
  5. Would ρₖₗₜₗ crossing ~1.5 serve as a cheap online early-warning signal that triggers intervention automatically?
