02 Why this matters

First systematic quantification of cross-query KV reuse in sparse prefill — a practical blueprint for long-context serving kernels, with one lossy approximation to scrutinize.

03 Critical edge

Every number comes from Huawei’s own stack measured against its own in-house baseline — no GPU results, no third-party sparse kernels — so competitiveness outside Ascend is unverified.

04 Methodology — the execution recipe

  1. S1Profile. On Ascend 910C, KV loading + dequantization dominate query-parallel sparse prefill; neighboring queries’ Top-2048 sets overlap 78.06–85.91% at group size 2 (>90% in layer 1).
  2. S2Group & share. Execute neighboring queries jointly; shared KV entries are HBM-loaded and dequantized once per group. Sparsifier-agnostic — GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-3.2 trainable indexers stay untouched.
  3. S3SCSD. Set intersection/difference recast as a fixed data-parallel sequence: concatenate with source labels → sort → bidirectional shift-and-compare → Boolean-mask extraction. No scalar loops, no branches; runs on vector units.
  4. S4Pipeline. SCSD occupies the vector pipeline while the matrix pipeline computes QK/PV (producer–consumer warm-up, multi-stage splitting) — decomposition leaves the critical path. A naive Torch set-intersection is 21.84×/33.65× slower.
  5. S5Cascade + tiles. Hierarchical 8→4→2→1 sharing processes each KV at its largest shareable granularity; a 50% tile-utilization threshold pads or sinks ragged tails; final-level residuals are clipped by indexer importance.

  • Cascaded sharing processes 45.3% of baseline KVs vs. 50.0% for the best fixed group — up to 29.6% extra kernel-latency gain (TP).
  • Importance-aware clipping preserves outputs far better than random pruning: head-level cosine 0.9999 vs. 0.9990 mean, 0.546 vs. 0.312 worst-case.
  • Weakest cell — DeepSeek-3.2 · SP · NarrativeQA: kernel gain just −1.0%; gains hinge on workload overlap and pipeline amortization.

06 Strengths

  • Motivation is measured, not asserted: overlap ratios plus NPU latency breakdowns tie redundant KV loads to real kernel time.
  • SCSD is an elegant, general primitive; the naive → vectorized → pipelined ablation cleanly isolates why each layer of engineering is needed.
  • Strong system thinking: exploits vector/matrix idle asymmetry, drops the fixed-group-size commitment, connects KV savings to actual tile utilization.
  • Workload-agnostic: plugs into two model-native indexers (GLM-5.3, DeepSeek-3.2 DSA) without modifying them.
  • Comprehensive ablations with consistent numbers across two models and both parallel modes.

07 Limitations

  • One vendor platform (Ascend 910C / CANN 9.10 / XYServe), one in-house baseline; no GPU or third-party sparse-kernel comparisons.
  • Latency measured on only 4 of 21 LongBench datasets — all high-overlap, favorable cases; no low-overlap stress test of the break-even point.
  • End-to-end gains degrade sharply under SP: GLM-5.3 TTFT −35.6% (TP) → −10.4% (SP); DeepSeek-3.2 SP only 2.4–4.3%.
  • Importance-aware clipping is lossy; its task-level accuracy impact is not isolated from sharing rearrangement (hotpotqa −1.45 swing).
  • No sensitivity study for the group-8 default or 50% tile threshold; no Top-K ≠ 2048 or longer-context analysis; no code release.

08 Questions for further exploration

  • Q1Do the gains transfer to GPU / Tensor Cores — does SCSD’s shift-and-compare mix map onto warp-level operations and non-Ascend memory hierarchies?
  • Q2What is importance-aware clipping’s task-level accuracy cost alone — unclipped vs. clipped QUILT across all 21 LongBench datasets, especially retrieval-heavy hotpotqa?
  • Q3Where is the break-even on intrinsically low-overlap workloads (code generation, heterogeneous contexts) where SCSD overhead exceeds sharing gains?
  • Q4Is the SP warm-up amortization loss (TTFT −35.6% TP → −10.4% SP) fixable by overlapping SCSD setup across sequence-parallel chunks, or is it structural?
  • Q5Could indexers be trained to emit more shareable Top-K patterns across neighboring queries — turning an execution-side win into a co-design opportunity?
