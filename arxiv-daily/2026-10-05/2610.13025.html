Evo²: Evolving the Evolver through Weight-Harness Co-Evolution

Chejian Xu, Shujian Zhang, Wenxuan Zhou, John Lambert, et al. 2026-10-05 · Google DeepMind & UIUC weight–harness co-evolution GRPO SWE-bench Polyglot

Key metric

+19.5

points pass@1 on Polyglot — 82.1% vs 62.6% for GEA, the strongest of four self-evolution baselines.

Why this matters

Google DeepMind closes the self-improvement loop: harness edits and their outcomes train the evolver itself — a core mechanism for self-improving agents.

Critical edge

The headline +19.5 rides an unusually weak baseline cluster, and the proprietary “nearly 2×” GPT-5-mini claim carries a best-of-2 selection advantage on a 60-task subset.

Methodology — how the loop closes

01Parent harness (tree node)
→
02Proposer edit · Type A autonomous / Type B directed
→
03Evaluate candidate (executor fixed)
→
04Reflector · REVISE / CROSSOVER / KEEP
→
05EvoTIDE · recover → SFT → GRPO
⟲
06Updated policy θ loops back
  • Tree search over harnesses. Each node stores a harness plus evaluation records; the executor model stays frozen, so score deltas isolate the harness effect. A single shared Qwen3.8-27B policy plays both Proposer and Reflector via role prompts.
  • Crossover searching. The Reflector can import a donor strategy from a library of harnesses evaluated on other branches, combining discoveries across the tree; KEEP lets it learn when further edits won’t help.
  • EvoTIDE trajectory recovery. Failed rollouts are salvaged — edit extraction mines recorded reasoning for unapplied edits; prefix continuation resumes from the last successfully applied edit — then SFT warm-starts the policy and interleaved GRPO trains both roles against a frozen counterpart snapshot.
  • Reward engineering. Rewards equal the induced task-score change; no-ops, unapplied edits and unusable harnesses are penalized, and Reflector gains count only if execution traces show the added mechanism actually ran — an anti-reward-hacking guard.
  • Parent pool. The root keeps a permanent share ρ; other parents are sampled by w(h)=ε_P+|V(h)−½|, deliberately mixing strong and weak candidates so the evolver learns to improve and repair.

  • Frozen-weight proprietary executors (60-task SWE subset): GPT-5-mini jumps 24.4% → 48.3% (~2× the best baseline); Claude Haiku 4.5 reaches 47.8% vs 45.0% — search alone, no weight updates.
  • Ablation isolates weight evolution (Table 5): the EvoTIDE-trained evolver beats the untrained base by +16.3 on Polyglot (78.0 vs 61.8, p=.001); crossover searching adds a further +4.1 (→ 82.1).
  • Cheaper at inference: highest LiveCodeBench accuracy at 3.6K completion tokens / 10.2 calls per task vs 6.6–6.9K tokens / 26–37 calls for HGM/DGM.
  • Transfer is broad but not universal: best in 5 of 6 directions; it loses Polyglot→LCB (44.0 vs 45.0) and Polyglot→SWE stays benchmark-specific (21.0 vs 20.0).

Strengths

  • Large, consistent gains exactly where prior self-evolution plateaus: Polyglot +19.5, LoCoMo +4.2 over the best baseline, across two domains.
  • Frozen-weight dual-role search alone doubles GPT-5-mini’s resolve rate — the harness mechanism earns its keep independently of weight evolution.
  • Higher accuracy at roughly half the tokens and a third of the LLM calls of HGM/DGM — efficiency of design, not prompt bloat.
  • Honest accounting: held-out evaluation, a dedicated weight-evolution ablation with a p-value, and per-search ranges (67.9–73.9) rather than only means.

Limitations

  • Compute-heavy and unreproducible as released: thousands of harness evaluations plus 27B GRPO training, with no code or harness artifacts mentioned.
  • Uneven gains: only +2.0 on LiveCodeBench and a Polyglot→LCB transfer loss as baselines saturate a benchmark.
  • The proprietary study is confined to a 60-task subset with a best-of-2 selection asymmetry favoring Evo² over seed-averaged baselines.
  • Executor = evolver in all weight-evolution runs (cross-model generalization untested), and no sandboxing or safety analysis of self-modifying harnesses.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the EvoTIDE-trained evolver transfer to executor families it never co-evolved with, or does weight evolution overfit the Qwen3.8-27B pair?
  2. Is trace-evidence sufficient against a reward-hacking evolver — could a mechanism that runs but is causally inert still farm Reflector credit?
  3. How sensitive is the pipeline to parent-pool hyperparameters (root share ρ, ε_P) and to reward shaping (file-matching bonus, no-op penalties)?
  4. Compute-matched: how much of the gain comes from searching many candidate harnesses versus co-evolution itself — e.g., against GPT-5-mini writing the harness once?
