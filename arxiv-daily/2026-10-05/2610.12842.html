Networks with no specialized single neurons can still be cleanly modular — one level up, in learned linear subspaces.

Neural networks are more modular than single neurons suggest

Shreya Saha · Benjamin Bergen · Meenakshi Khosla — UC San Diego · 2026-10-05

NeurIPS 2026 interpretability modularity subspace decomposition comp. neuroscience

Key metric — double dissociation index

DDI0.00→0.47

tanh 11-task cognitive RNN: unit-level ablation finds zero double dissociation; the learned linear subspace decomposition recovers 0.47 (Sinkhorn-permutation baseline: 0.08; orthogonal: 0.37; softplus nets are modular at every level: 0.59–0.73).

Why this matters

Gives interpretability a causal modularity test and reusable subspace tooling — and warns that single-voxel null results in neuroscience don’t rule out functional separation.

单神经元缺乏选择性 ≠ 网络缺乏模块化 — 分离可能藏在子空间层面。

Critical edge

Higher-capacity transformations could inflate apparent modularity, and no null-label or shuffled-control calibration of DDI or selectivity ratios is reported in the main text.

Methodology

h(x) ∈ ℝD  →  Q  →  z = [ z_A z_B z_res ]

An invertible Q splits a layer’s representation into two disjoint function blocks plus an explicitly retained residual. Three nested transformation families are tried — the most constrained family that separates the functions diagnoses the geometry:

  • Permutationpure reordering — tests axis-aligned, neuron-disjoint codes
  • Orthogonalrigid rotation (QTQ = I) — non-overlapping subspaces
  • Invertiblebounded condition number κ(Q) — non-orthogonal bases allowed
  • RNNs — task-sufficiency objective: a linear readout head per subspace trained on task loss, plus family-specific regularizers (Sinkhorn entropy / Cayley parameterization / condition-number penalty).
  • Pretrained models — contrastive minimal pairs: InfoNCE-style losses on counterfactual inputs (same scene, new style; paraphrase vs. template-swapped syntax), updating disjoint columns of Q by construction.
  • Evaluation: causal subspace ablation requiring double dissociation (DDI) for RNNs; Top-K retrieval selectivity ratios (Rc, Rs » 1) for vision/language; fMRI encoding alignment (NSD, 4 subjects; Fedorenko language localizer).

Strengths

  • +Crisp formal contribution: turns the ill-posed “is it modular?” into “at which level?” — and answers causally (ablation double dissociation), not correlationally.
  • +Same qualitative pattern replicated across RNNs, 5+ vision architectures, and multiple LM scales with one unified framework.
  • +Brain-alignment experiments add external validation that matches known cortical hierarchies (texture → semantics).
  • +Honest Discussion: the authors flag non-convexity, retrieval-only evaluation, and the uncharacterized residual themselves.

Limitations

  • !Higher-capacity transforms could inflate apparent modularity; no null-label / shuffled control calibrates DDI or selectivity in the main text.
  • !tanh recovery is partial (DDI 0.47 vs. softplus 0.59–0.73) — graded, not binary.
  • !Vision/language assessed by retrieval only; no behavioral ablations or downstream causal tests.
  • !Two-function decompositions only; the large residual subspace is uncharacterized.
  • !Non-convex optimization; seed and hyperparameter sensitivity not quantified; ResNet failure unexplained.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does subspace-level modularity emerge gradually over training, or exist at initialization and merely get reshaped — and what does the level imply for generalization and robustness?
  2. Can the framework extend to multi-way (beyond two-function) decompositions, and is the residual subspace shared, redundant, or selectively task-associated?
  3. Why does ResNet resist linear content–style separation while Swin / ConvNeXt / VGG / ViT do not — pooling, training objective, or receptive-field statistics?
  4. Would behavioral ablations in vision (impair object recognition, spare texture judgment) replicate the RNN double dissociation for pretrained models?
  5. Does unit-absent / subspace-present hold for attention heads and MLP layers — can targeted subspace intervention replace neuron-level steering?
