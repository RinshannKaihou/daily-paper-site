> **一句话总结：** 在 5 个不同架构的开源模型（Qwen3.5-4B/9B、Qwen3-8B、Gemma-4-E4B/E2B）中，用因果头消融 + 必要性/充分性激活修补 + 匹配负样本筛选，从 128–1152 个候选头中找出 1–9 个"网络信息头"，其线性检测器在 DeepURLBench 域名/IP 检索任务上达到 99.5–100% 留出准确率（AUC 1.000），且无需重训练即可对独立采集的 CAIDA 反向 DNS 数据 100% 命中；但"责任是否收敛到单个神经元"完全取决于模型——Qwen3.5-4B 中单个神经元（层 15、头 1、维 0）即可复现全头 AUC 1.000，而 Qwen3-8B 中最佳因果排序神经元只有 AUC 0.665，与随机神经元基线（0.667±0.186）无法区分。

> **TL;DR:** The paper asks which attention heads (and which neurons inside them) let an LLM recognize that its context contains network/infrastructure records (domain–IP pairs from DeepURLBench). Two independent causal algorithms — single-head ablation screening with necessity/sufficiency activation patching, and greedy joint circuit discovery — plus matched negative controls (entity/vocabulary/arithmetic questions on the same record) yield 1–9 causally validated heads per model across five Qwen3/Gemma-4 models; a logistic-regression detector on those heads reaches 99.5–100% held-out accuracy (AUC 1.000), and transfers with zero retraining to an independent CAIDA reverse-DNS dataset (full-head detectors flag 500/500 records) while single-neuron detectors collapse (down to AUC 0.285, below chance). Neuron-level concentration is model-specific, not universal: Qwen3.5-4B's top head compresses into one neuron (AUC 1.000, causal and information-gain rankings agree exactly), whereas Qwen3-8B's best causally ranked neuron inside its single clean head manages only AUC 0.665 vs 1.000 for the full head — statistically indistinguishable from the random-neuron control (0.667±0.186).

## 核心贡献

- **首个针对"网络/基础设施信息"实体的因果定位工作：** 与知识神经元文献（事实性回忆）不同，本文研究上下文内检索——模型只需从 prompt 中复制域名/IP 字段——并首次在此类实体上组合"因果头发现 + 第二种独立发现算法交叉验证 + 神经元级因果消融 + 独立真实数据源验证"四件事。
- **双算法交叉验证的干净头集：** 算法 A（HeadSuppressor：逐头消融 + 必要性/充分性修补排序 + 选择性筛选）与算法 B（贪心联合电路发现）各自独立找到的头集在身份上仅部分重叠（Jaccard 0.12–0.20，即 3–5/20 个头），但对检测器同样具有破坏性——因果信号不依赖于发现它的具体算法。
- **头部结论跨模型、跨数据源成立：** 每个模型都存在经因果验证、选择性确认的头集（1–9 个头）；其线性检测器达到 99.5–100% 留出准确率，且在 CAIDA 独立数据集上零重训练全对 500 条正向记录命中。
- **神经元级结论是"模型特异"而非普适规律：** 5 个模型中只有 Qwen3.5-4B 的责任真正收敛到单个神经元（且因果消融排序与纯相关性信息增益排序精确一致）；Qwen3-8B 中整头有效但内部单个神经元接近随机；且单神经元检测器无法迁移到独立数据集（最低 AUC 0.285，低于随机）。
- **诚实的混淆控制与压力测试：** 选择性筛选在 Qwen 三个模型上把 context-free 问题误报率从 100% 降到 0.00，但对 Gemma 无效（仍 1.000）；附录的 DNS-TTL/MAC 泛化与自然文本监控用例进一步暴露了检测器真正响应的是"address-like 字段的检索"而非"网络信息"本身。

## Key Contributions

- **First causal localization for network/infrastructure entities:** unlike the knowledge-neuron literature (factual recall), this targets in-context retrieval — copying a domain/IP field present in the prompt — and is, to the authors' knowledge, the first work to combine causal head discovery, a second cross-validating discovery algorithm, neuron-level causal ablation, and independent real-world data-source validation for this entity type.
- **A dual-algorithm clean head set:** Algorithm A (HeadSuppressor: per-head ablation screening, necessity/sufficiency activation patching, selectivity filter) and Algorithm B (greedy joint circuit discovery) recover head sets that overlap only partially in identity (Jaccard 0.12–0.20, i.e., 3–5 of 20 heads) yet are equally damaging when suppressed — the causal signal does not depend on which algorithm finds it.
- **The head-level result generalizes across models and data sources:** every model tested (Qwen3.5-4B/9B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-4-E4B/E2B) has a causally validated, selectivity-confirmed set of 1–9 heads whose detector reaches 99.5–100% held-out accuracy, and full-head detectors flag all 500 CAIDA reverse-DNS records with no retraining.
- **The neuron-level result is model-specific, not a fixed property:** responsibility concentrates into a single neuron only in Qwen3.5-4B (where causal-ablation and correlational information-gain rankings agree exactly on layer 15, head 1, dim 0); in Qwen3-8B the head works as a whole and the best causally ranked neuron is statistically indistinguishable from random (AUC 0.665 vs 1.000 for the full head).
- **Honest confound control and stress tests:** the selectivity filter cuts the context-free false-positive flag rate from 100% to 0.00 on the three Qwen models but fails on Gemma (still 1.000); appendix generalization tests (DNS-TTL, MAC addresses) and a naturalistic monitoring use case reveal the detector really responds to "address-like fields asked for in IP/URL-like language," not network information in general.

## Methodology

**Task and data.** Each example presents a short record (`Record: Website: <domain> / IP address: <ip>`) and asks the model to copy back one field (ask_ip or ask_url directions; two question templates). Positives come from DeepURLBench domain–IP records. Four additional conditions control what a head may be credited for: *paraphrase* (reworded retrieval question), and three matched negatives — *entity* (same record, unrelated classification question), *vocabulary* (definitional question, e.g., "What does 'IP' stand for?"), *ordinary* (arithmetic). Two context-free conditions (*question_only*, *vocab_no_context*) stress-test false positives. Models: Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma-4-E4B-it, Gemma-4-E2B-it (hybrid linear attention, dense attention, interleaved sliding-window/global, GQA). Calibration split: 200 items; ablation screening and greedy circuit search use 120 validation items.

**Head discovery (Algorithm A, HeadSuppressor).** Every intervention zeros terms of Eq. 3: ablating head *h* sets O_h ← (1−β)O_h at every position; neuron *j* zeroes only one column O_h[:,j] — a neuron is one additive term inside a head's own contribution. Δ_abl(h) measures the drop in the correct answer's first-token log-prob. On the top-50 screened heads, necessity N(h) (removing the head's retrieval-condition output from the target prompt should hurt) and sufficiency S(h) (injecting it into an entity-control prompt should raise the control answer's probability) are computed via activation patching (Zhang & Nanda, 2024). Heads with positive selectivity Sel(h) = Δ_net − ¼(Δ_ent + Δ_voc + Δ_ord) form the candidate pool; the top 20 by combined N+S form H_E ("clean_subset"). A per-head linear probe against the vocabulary-only hard negative discards heads that fire on mere network vocabulary.

**Cross-validation (Algorithm B).** A structurally different greedy joint circuit discovery (Commy et al., 2023 style) builds a head set step by step, choosing the head with the largest additional drop given already-suppressed heads. It recovers a set overlapping H_E only partially (Jaccard 0.12–0.20) but equally damaging — evidence the discovery is not an artifact of one algorithm.

**Neuron discovery.** NeuronSuppressor ablates single columns O_h[:,j] (causal ranking, Eq. 10) and this is compared with a purely correlational information-gain ranking (quantile-binned activations, Eq. 11). Four logistic-regression classifiers (single best causal neuron, top-5 causal neurons, single best IG neuron, full clean_subset head) are trained on captured activations. A neuron-level random control compares the actual best neuron against 20 random dimensions from the same head and 20 random (layer, head, dimension) triples.

**Transfer.** Detectors are evaluated without retraining on 500 positive records from CAIDA's IPv4 routed /24 DNS names (reverse-DNS records from a different institution and methodology) with 500 TriviaQA items as negatives, plus appendix tests on never-seen DNS-TTL and synthetic MAC-address fields and a 10-item naturalistic monitoring stress test.

## Results & Highlights

- **A clean head set exists in every model, and the filter matters more than accuracy.** From 128–1,152 candidate heads per model, the selectivity filter leaves 9 (Qwen3.5-4B), 1 (Qwen3-8B), 4 (Qwen3.5-9B), 5 (Gemma-4-E4B), and 2 (Gemma-4-E2B) heads; the resulting detectors reach 100.0%/100.0%/100.0%/99.9%/99.9% held-out accuracy with AUC 1.000 in all five models (Table 3). The unfiltered top-20 set also achieves AUC 1.000, but it flags 100% of context-free question_only prompts in all five models — the clean_subset removes this confound on the three Qwen models (flag rate 0.00) while Gemma's stays at 1.000, so its high scores partly reflect ignoring the wording (Table 2).
- **Neuron-level concentration is the exception, not the rule (Table 4/5/6).** In Qwen3.5-4B a single neuron (layer 15, head 1, dim 0) matches the full 9-head detector — 99.4% accuracy, AUC 1.000 vs 1.000 — and both the causal-ablation and information-gain rankings pick exactly this neuron. In Qwen3-8B the opposite holds: the full head is perfect (100%, AUC 1.000) but the best causally ranked neuron reaches only AUC 0.665 (53.9% accuracy), inside the fully-random-neuron range (same-head random 0.728±0.179; any-neuron random 0.667±0.186). In the other three models the best neuron is strong in-distribution (AUC 0.969–1.000, each 1.3–2.5 SD above random) but the two rankings disagree on the exact dimension (Qwen3.5-9B) or head (Gemma models).
- **Head-level transfer holds; neuron-level transfer fails (Table 7, CAIDA).** With no retraining, full-head detectors classify all 500 CAIDA reverse-DNS records correctly in four of five models (Qwen3.5-9B: 100% accuracy, AUC 1.000; Gemma-4-E2B degrades to 91.5% overall, 83.0% negative accuracy, AUC 0.971 — losing points only on negative-class TriviaQA false flags). Single-neuron detectors do not transfer: AUCs of 0.535 (Qwen3.5-4B), 0.422 (Qwen3-8B), 0.285 (Qwen3.5-9B — below chance despite AUC 1.000 in-distribution), 0.568, and 0.463.
- **Generalization to unseen sub-types is asymmetric (Appendix B/C).** The synthetic MAC-address field is flagged at rate ≥0.985 in four of five models (Gemma-4-E2B only 0.275); the real DNS-TTL field is flagged at 0.990 by Qwen3.5-4B but 0.000 by Gemma-4-E4B. Joint necessity/sufficiency patching shows the same clean_subset heads causally carry the TTL and MAC values in every model (e.g., Qwen3.5-4B dns_ttl sufficiency S = 10.02 nats vs −0.03 for a random-head control) — the detectors' disagreement is a linear-classifier artifact, not absent head involvement.
- **A realistic monitoring use case is not ready (Appendix D).** On 10 naturalistic items never seen in training, all 25 positive (model, item) cells are flagged (P ≥ 0.883 across server logs, nginx configs, incident reports, IT emails, chats), but only 12 of 25 matched negatives are correctly left unflagged (48%), and a non-network numeric retrieval item (flight gate number) is flagged in 4 of 5 models (e.g., P = 0.998 for Qwen3-8B) — out-of-template, the detector tracks "a short, specific token is being retrieved" more than network content.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is real but narrower than the framing suggests.** The head-level machinery (ablation screening + patching + selectivity) is assembled from standard parts (Michel et al. 2019; Geva et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2025); the genuine novelty is the entity type (network/infrastructure records, an agent-safety-relevant class) and the four-way combination with cross-algorithm validation and an independent data source. **The headline "network information" claim is partly undercut by the paper's own appendices:** the Gemma models' clean_subset detectors flag 100% of context-free questions (Table 2), so for half the models the detector is partly a wording detector ("IP" mentioned → flag), and the Appendix D stress test shows the detector degrades into a generic "short-token-retrieval" detector out of template (flight-gate item flagged at P = 0.998 in Qwen3-8B). The paper says this itself, which is commendable, but the abstract's "99.5–100% hold-out accuracy" numbers come entirely from the training template. **Statistical rigor is thin in places:** every result is a single run per model; the 120-item ablation calibration and 200-item calibration splits give no confidence intervals on the headline accuracies; and the neuron-random control uses 20 repeats, fine for AUC but not for the 53.9% accuracy point estimate. **The two-algorithm Jaccard overlap of 0.12–0.20 is spun as validation, but it also means the "clean set" identity is largely algorithm-dependent** — only effect-equivalence is established, so claims about *which* heads matter should be read loosely. **Missing ablations:** the authors admit they did not test whether ablating the top neuron promotes the next-ranked neuron (the direct redundancy test for their "partial concentration" account), did not vary architecture in a controlled way (5 models, confounded differences), and the DNS-TTL generalization gap is explained post hoc ("address-like fields asked for in IP/URL-like language") without a follow-up experiment that would confirm it. **Reproducibility is good on paper** — public models, public datasets (DeepURLBench, CAIDA, TriviaQA), a fully specified linear probe (sklearn, C=1, balanced) — but no code release is mentioned, and the hand-written naturalistic test items are only partially shown.

## Strengths

- Causal validation at every level: heads and neurons are located by interventions (ablation and necessity/sufficiency patching), not just correlational probes, and the correlational-vs-causal comparison is itself a result (they agree exactly in only one model).
- Two structurally independent discovery algorithms cross-validate each other, and the matched negative conditions (entity / vocabulary / ordinary) plus context-free stress tests are a model of confound control for localization claims.
- The independent CAIDA evaluation with zero retraining is the strongest single piece of evidence — a different institution, a different measurement methodology, and the head-level detector still flags 500/500 records in four models.
- Intellectual honesty: the paper foregrounds its own negative results (Qwen3-8B neuron spread, neuron-level transfer failure, Gemma wording sensitivity, Appendix D's 48% negative specificity) instead of burying them.
- The mechanistic framing (neuron = one column of O_h; head = removable additive term, Eq. 3) is clean and exactly connects the ablation operator to the architecture.

## Limitations

- One retrieval task (domain–IP), five models, one run each: no variance estimates on headline numbers, and no controlled way to vary architecture while holding everything else fixed — the model-specificity of neuron concentration is observed, not explained.
- The neuron-level non-transfer (AUC as low as 0.285 on CAIDA) means single-neuron findings are unsafe for any out-of-distribution monitoring goal, limiting the practical payoff of the finer-grained analysis.
- Detector behavior out-of-template is genuinely weak: 48% negative specificity on naturalistic text, wording-driven false flags in Gemma (question_only flag rate 1.000 even after filtering), and a generic retrieval-signal confound on non-network numeric fields.
- The proposed monitoring application (flagging when an LLM agent is about to act on network information) remains a proof of concept; no deployment-ready system, no in-template normalization or naturalistic-negative retraining is attempted.
- Generalization to unseen sub-types is asymmetric and post-hoc explained (DNS-TTL near-floor for two models); whether the head-neuron circuit is predictable from architectural properties is left open.

## Relevance

这篇论文与可解释性方向的"因果定位 + 混淆控制"方法论高度相关，同时为 LLM Agent 安全监控（检测模型即将输出网络/基础设施信息）提供了一个可复用的头级检测器思路；其"头部结论可迁移、神经元结论不可迁移"的对照对任何想做机制级 Agent 监控的人都是重要的反面警示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Is neuron-level concentration predictable from architecture (GQA group size, residual width, head dimension) or training recipe — and does it change with scale within one family, given Qwen3.5-4B concentrates but Qwen3-8B does not?
2. Does ablating the top-ranked neuron promote the next-ranked neuron (the unrun redundancy test)? A knockout-cascade experiment would directly adjudicate the "partial concentration" account.
3. Can retraining the linear detector with naturalistic negatives (Appendix D items) restore out-of-template specificity above 48% without losing the 100% in-template accuracy or the CAIDA transfer?
4. Do the same clean_subset heads carry other network-entity types beyond DNS-TTL and MAC (certificates, ASNs, ports), and is there a shared "address-like field" circuit?
5. Would the head-level transfer survive on frontier-scale models with different tokenizers, and can the detector be turned into a real-time steering/intervention signal (suppressing the heads) rather than a post-hoc classifier?
