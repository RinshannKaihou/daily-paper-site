> **一句话总结：** 本文指出视觉 token 剪枝的常用评测（聚合准确率 + 保留 token 数）存在系统性误导——错误→正确的"伪增益"会掩盖能力损失、大量易样本稀释方法差异、token 数不等于真实加速——并提出即插即用的 PAA-Bench 评测框架（Random-Pass@k 难度分层 + 稠密正确性保持率 + 保持约束加速比 PCS），在 8 种方法、3 种 VLM 骨干、9 个基准上发现排名会反转、更强压缩未必更快。

> **TL;DR:** An NTU team shows that the standard way of evaluating visual-token pruning for VLMs — aggregate benchmark accuracy at matched retained-token budgets — is misleading: on benchmarks like MMBench-EN/POPE/MME over 80% of examples stay correct under random pruning, wrong-to-correct "spurious gains" (7.76% vs 8.85% C→W on Qwen3-VL-8B at 20% retention) mask capability loss, and identical token budgets yield different end-to-end speedups. Their PAA-Bench framework (Random-Pass@k difficulty stratification, dense-correct preservation, and Preservation-Constrained Speedup) reverses method rankings (e.g., CDPruner beats SparseVLM on relative accuracy 96.9 vs 88.1 but loses on preservation 89.3 vs 93.3 on LLaVA-1.5@64) and shows a method retaining 49 tokens (DivPrune, 1.95×) can be faster than one retaining 4 (SparseVLM, 1.83×) on MMBench, while VisionZip at 289 tokens on TextVQA actually runs at 0.95× (slower than dense).

## 核心贡献

- **揭示两大评测缺陷**：(1) 聚合准确率把"原本答对→剪枝后答错"的损失与"原本答错→碰巧答对"的伪增益混在一起——伪增益来自偶然剪掉视觉干扰项或模型退回文本先验（如"Hammer time"式语言捷径），可掩盖过半甚至几乎全部的能力损失；(2) 保留 token 数不是效率的可靠代理——剪枝发生的位置和选择算法的开销不同，相同 token 预算的端到端成本可以差很多。
- **Random-Pass@k 选择难度度量**：对每个稠密模型答对的样本做 k=10 次随机剪枝，按随机通过次数分为 Easy（8-10）/Medium（4-7）/Hard（0-3），并给出 Proposition 1 理论刻画"随机剪枝成功率越高、聪明选择的空间（headroom）越小"。
- **正确性保持率（Correctness Preservation）**：只在稠密模型答对的子集 D+ 上评测剪枝后仍答对的比例，从构造上排除伪增益。
- **保持约束加速比 PCS（Preservation-Constrained Speedup）**：在共享保持目标（如 ≥95% D+ 正确）下报告方法的最大实测端到端加速（含剪枝自身开销），把比较基础从"相同 token 数"改为"相同能力保持"。
- **大规模实证**：8 种剪枝方法（CDPruner、DivPrune、FastV、VisionZip、DART、PruMerge、SparseVLM、VisPruner）+ 随机基线，3 个骨干（LLaVA-1.5-7B、LLaVA-NeXT-7B、Qwen3-VL-8B），9 个基准（GQA、VizWiz、ScienceQA、TextVQA、POPE、MME、VQAv2、MMBench-EN/CN）。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnosis of evaluation pitfalls.** Decomposes aggregate accuracy into original accuracy, correct-to-wrong, and wrong-to-correct terms (Eq. 2), showing correction gains of similar magnitude to losses (7.76% vs 8.85% on Qwen3-VL-8B at 20% retention) can mask capability loss; identifies two spurious-gain mechanisms (accidental distractor removal, textual-prior shortcuts — stable corrections' overlap with text-only-correct examples rises from ~45% at 196 tokens to 76% at 16 tokens).
- **Random-Pass@k.** A method-independent, plug-and-play per-example difficulty measure: k repeated random-pruning trials on dense-correct examples, stratified into Easy/Medium/Hard. Grounded by Proposition 1, which bounds the informed-selection headroom (q_N − p_N ≤ 1 − p_N) by the random-pruning success rate.
- **Correctness preservation on the dense-correct subset D+.** Excludes wrong-to-correct transitions by construction, so the score measures preserved capability rather than net benchmark movement.
- **Preservation-Constrained Speedup (PCS@τ).** The maximum measured end-to-end speedup over a method's evaluated configurations subject to Acc_{D+} ≥ τ, with runtime covering the complete pipeline including token-selection and gathering overhead — replacing matched-token-budget comparisons with matched-preservation comparisons.
- **PAA-Bench as a framework.** A three-stage protocol (build D+, stratify by Random-Pass@k, compare PCS at shared τ) applicable to existing benchmarks without new data collection.

## Methodology

The paper is an evaluation-methodology ("bench") paper, not a new pruning method. Section 3 formalizes the evaluation objective as maximizing measured speedup T_orig/T_p(M,c) subject to accuracy ≥ τ, then audits both sides of that objective. On the accuracy side, it decomposes post-pruning aggregate accuracy into α (dense accuracy), r_{C→W} and r_{W→C} (Eq. 2), empirically quantifies both transition rates on LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen3-VL-8B, and traces wrong-to-correct gains to distractor removal and textual-prior reliance using a text-only control. On the efficiency side, it shows matched token budgets place methods at different operating points both in preservation and in realized runtime. Section 4 then introduces PAA-Bench: (1) restrict evaluation to D+ (examples the dense model answers correctly); (2) stratify D+ by Random-Pass@k with k=10 into Easy (8-10 successes), Medium (4-7), Hard (0-3); (3) report per-stratum correctness preservation and PCS@τ — the max measured end-to-end speedup (dense runtime / pruned runtime, including pruning overhead) among configurations meeting the shared preservation target τ (e.g., 0.95). Experiments cover 8 training-free pruning methods plus a random baseline across LLaVA-1.5-7B (128/64/32-token budgets), LLaVA-NeXT-7B (640/320/160), and Qwen3-VL-8B (10%/20% retention) on 9 benchmarks; appendix adds Qwen3-VL-2B and LLaVA-1.5-13B selection results. Runtime is measured end-to-end on a single PRO 6000 GPU at batch size 1 with a 128-token output cap, with prompts and decoding matched between dense and pruned runs.

## Results & Highlights

- **Easy examples dominate standard benchmarks**: over 80% of examples remain correct in 8–10 of 10 random-pruning trials on MMBench-EN, POPE, and MME (LLaVA-1.5 @64 tokens), diluting method differences; TextVQA is the outlier with 48.49% Hard examples, explaining why prior work found it discriminative.
- **Spurious corrections mask preservation loss**: on Qwen3-VL-8B at 20% token retention, the wrong-to-correct rate (7.76%) is nearly as large as the correct-to-wrong rate (8.85%); on LLaVA-1.5 the W→C contribution is about half to two-thirds of C→W — correction gains can mask over half, sometimes nearly all, of the accuracy loss.
- **Preservation-aware scoring reverses rankings** (Table 1, LLaVA-1.5 @64 tokens): CDPruner beats SparseVLM on relative accuracy (RelAcc 96.9 vs 88.1) but loses on dense-correct preservation (W-Avg 89.3 vs 93.3); 5 of 10 pairwise orderings at 128 tokens and 3 of 10 at 64 tokens flip between RelAcc and W-Avg, and SparseVLM moves from 4th (RelAcc) to 1st (W-Avg) at both budgets.
- **Method gaps widen with selection difficulty** (Fig. 3, LLaVA-1.5 @64 tokens): the best-to-worst spread among pruning methods grows from 6.9 pts on Easy to 18.8 on Medium and 27.6 on Hard; on LLaVA-NeXT @320 tokens, the Random-vs-DART gap grows from 7.5 pts under sample weighting (84.8 vs 92.3) to 25.5 pts under stratum-equal weighting (56.0 vs 81.5).
- **Fewer tokens ≠ faster**: at 95% preservation on LLaVA-1.5/POPE, CDPruner needs only 16 tokens vs FastV's 270, yet on MMBench @90% SparseVLM retains just 4 tokens for 1.83× while DivPrune retains 49 tokens and is faster at 1.95×; VisionZip on Qwen3-VL/TextVQA @95% retains 289 tokens and delivers only 0.95× — slower than the dense model.
- **Best speedup is context-dependent**: at PCS@95, CDPruner reaches 4.37× on LLaVA-NeXT/POPE while SparseVLM leads Qwen3-VL/TextVQA with only 1.23× (1.31× at 90%); rankings also flip with the target — on LLaVA-1.5/MMBench, SparseVLM (1.74×) beats DivPrune (1.53×) at 95% but DivPrune (1.95×) overtakes SparseVLM (1.83×) at 90%.

## Authors & Credibility

All four authors (Geng Li, Guohao Chen, Shilin Shan, Jianfei Yang — first two with equal contribution) are from Nanyang Technological University (NTU Singapore); Jianfei Yang is a faculty member known for edge-AI and efficient-sensing work. The paper is a v1 preprint (cs.AI, Oct 2026), formatted for a conference (ACM-style "Preprint") with an explicit AI-use statement (generative AI assisted prose organization from a research outline) and a reproducibility statement pointing to appendices for metric definitions, budgets, and runtime-measurement boundaries. The evaluation scope — 8 methods × 3 primary backbones × 9 benchmarks, with paired dense/pruned predictions, 10-trial random interventions, and measured (not FLOP-estimated) runtimes — is unusually thorough for a benchmark paper, and the diagnoses are supported by both a theoretical proposition and quantitative evidence.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: incremental but genuinely useful. VTC-Bench and EffiVLM-Bench already studied compression sensitivity and measured speed; the distinct additions here are (a) explicitly excluding wrong-to-correct gains via the dense-correct conditioning, (b) Random-Pass@k as a per-example difficulty calibration, and (c) preservation-matched (rather than token-matched) speedup comparison. The core insight — aggregate accuracy mixes preservation with spurious corrections — is simple, and the Eq. 2 decomposition is elementary; the value is in the systematic quantification.
- **Rigor**: the paired-dense-reference protocol (same prompt, evaluator, decoding rule; only the token mask varies) is careful, and speedups are measured end-to-end including pruning overhead rather than extrapolated from token counts. However, Random-Pass@k costs k=10 extra forward passes per example per setting, and the paper never reports this evaluation cost or discusses variance of the Easy/Medium/Hard cutoffs (the 8-10/4-7/0-3 bins are heuristic; no sensitivity analysis on k).
- **Reproducibility**: metric definitions and budgets are documented in appendices, but no code or data release is mentioned anywhere — a significant gap for a paper whose entire contribution is an evaluation protocol that it wants the community to adopt.
- **Efficiency measurement caveats**: all runtimes use a single PRO 6000 GPU at batch size 1 with 128-token max output. Under this regime prefill (vision-token) compute dominates, which inflates pruning's payoff; batched serving, longer generations, or prefill-cache reuse could change PCS rankings. The 0.95× VisionZip result already hints how sensitive realized speedup is to overhead placement.
- **Scope limits**: preservation conditions on D+, so on benchmarks where the dense model is weak the denominator shrinks and PCS targets become noisy; only image VLMs up to Qwen3-VL-8B are covered (no video, no larger models with runtime); PCS takes a max over each method's evaluated configuration grid, which can favor methods with denser budget sweeps.
- **Red flags**: none severe — no obvious cherry-picking, and the AI-use statement is transparent. The main risk is overclaiming generality from batch-size-1 single-GPU timing.

## Strengths

- Frames and answers the right question — "does pruning succeed through meaningful token selection, and does it translate into real speedup" — with one coherent protocol instead of three disconnected metrics.
- The Random-Pass@k idea is elegant and method-agnostic: it turns any existing benchmark into a selection-sensitive evaluation and comes with a simple theoretical justification (Proposition 1) for why high random-pass rates limit discriminative headroom.
- Diagnoses are quantified, not anecdotal: exact transition rates, overlap statistics with the text-only control, and pairwise ranking-reversal counts back every claim.
- PCS corrects a real, widely repeated malpractice in the pruning literature (comparing at matched token counts) and exposes that a 4-token method can be slower than a 49-token one.
- Plug-and-play design: no new benchmark data needed, so adoption cost is low.

## Limitations

- Heavy evaluation cost: Random-Pass@k multiplies inference passes by ~10 per example per setting, plus a dense reference pass; the paper does not report or discuss this overhead.
- All timing at batch size 1 on one GPU with short (≤128-token) outputs; conclusions about realized speedup may not transfer to batched or long-generation serving scenarios.
- The Easy/Medium/Hard thresholds and k=10 are heuristic; no sensitivity study of stratification choices.
- Conditioning on D+ ties the metrics to the dense model's own accuracy, which varies with prompt/evaluator choices (acknowledged in Appendix C.2) and shrinks the evaluation set on hard benchmarks.
- No code release announced; no video-VLM or >8B-model runtime results; PCS's max-over-configurations depends on each method's budget grid, which is not uniform across methods.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击 VLM 高效推理评测的方法论缺陷，与视觉 token 剪枝/训练免加速方向的调研高度相关：它提供的 Random-Pass@k 难度分层和"按能力保持而非 token 数比较加速比"的 PCS 协议，可作为筛选和公平比较剪枝方法的实用工具。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do Random-Pass@k distributions and PCS rankings change under batched serving (batch size >1) and long-form generation, where decode-time KV-cache savings rather than prefill compute dominate?
- Can the k=10 random-pruning trials be replaced by an analytical estimator of p_N (e.g., from attention-map statistics) to cut the 10× evaluation overhead?
- Do wrong-to-correct "spurious gains" correlate with hallucination metrics? On POPE-style binary benchmarks, does pruning-induced textual-prior reliance show up as increased yes-bias?
- Would fixing the preservation target τ per difficulty stratum (rather than globally) change the PCS Pareto frontier, given that Hard examples dominate the preservation loss?
- How does the framework extend to video VLMs, where token redundancy is much higher and temporal selection difficulty may stratify very differently?
- Since rankings flip between τ = 95% and 90%, is there a principled way to choose the operationally meaningful preservation target for a given downstream application?
