> **一句话总结：** 对 7 个模型家族的 12 个 checkpoint 逐一消融注意力头后发现：在 10 个模型中，单独置零某一个头就能使 8.0–51.5% 的 FLORES-200 续写偏离提示语言（同层其他头最多只影响 1.4%），且把该头输出替换为另一语言的均值可将 84–99% 的非英语 LCB 单语回复整体改写成替换语言——语言 adherence 竟然悬在单个注意力头上。

> **TL;DR:** A systematic single-head ablation study across 12 checkpoints (Qwen2.5/Qwen3, Gemma-3, OLMo-2/3, Llama-3.2, SmolLM3; 1B–7B) using FLORES-200 continuations (5 languages) and Language Confusion Benchmark (LCB) chat replies finds that in 10 of 12 models zeroing ONE attention head (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B L22H6) moves 8.0–51.5% of FLORES continuations out of the prompt language versus ≤1.4% for any other same-layer head, and drops LCB pass rates by up to −0.711 (Llama-3.2-1B L8H25 crosslingual, from 0.874) while random same-layer controls move LPR by at most ±0.038; replacing the head's output with another language's mean redirects 84.4–99.4% of non-English monolingual LCB replies into that language in 5 of 7 models, yet restoring the requested language works only in the two Gemma models. The effect is dissociated from general damage — zeroing Qwen2.5-7B's L19H1 raises loss by only +0.026 nats while flipping 20% of continuations — and is amplified, not created, by instruction tuning and chat templates.

## 核心贡献

- **跨家族系统性证据**：在 12 个 checkpoint、7 个模型系列上逐一消融每个注意力头，证明其中 10 个模型存在"单头主导"的语言维持机制——这是对语言混淆（language confusion）现象首次如此大范围的因果性定位。
- **区分"隐式续写"与"显式请求"两类头**：7 个在 FLORES-200 续写上筛选出的头影响提示语言的隐式延续；3 个在 LCB 上筛选出的头（Qwen3-4B L24H27、Llama-3.2-1B L8H25、Llama-3.2-3B L13H19）主要影响用户显式指定的回复语言。
- **机制层面的因果验证**：Qwen2.5-1.5B L22H6 与 Gemma-3-1B L11H3 在最后一个提示位置强注意力于"语言名称" token；把该头在语言名处读取的 value 替换为另一语言的均值可使 90/43 个回复失败并把 44/24 个回复整体变成替换语言，而等量随机方向的扰动只使 4–8/1–6 个失败（p < 10⁻¹⁰）——证明这些 value 携带语言特异性信息而非仅仅量级效应。
- **大效应与小损失的解耦**：置零 Qwen2.5-7B 的 L19H1 只使 loss 上升 0.026 nats（层内其他头平均 ≤0.022），却使 20% 的续写偏离提示语言——说明这不是一般性生成损伤。
- **后训练与输入格式的交互**：L22H6 在 Base 模型 + 纯文本上只影响 8.9–15.0% 的续写，但在 Instruct 模型 + chat template 下影响 50.0%；OLMo-2-1B L12H8 的效应沿 Base(0.005)→SFT(0.028)→DPO(0.071)→最终(0.080) 递增，说明对单头的依赖随后训练加深。
- **重定向容易、修复困难的不对称性**：注入另一语言的均值可重定向 84–99% 的回复语言，但用"请求语言"的均值修复跨语言回复、且优于所有对照头的只有两个 Gemma 模型（基线本就低至 0.118/0.133）——"对维持语言重要的头"不等于"可靠设置语言的旋钮"。

## Key Contributions

- **Cross-family causal localization of language confusion**: ablating every attention head one at a time in 12 checkpoints spanning seven series shows that in 10 models a single head dominates language adherence — far more systematic than prior single-family or neuron-level studies.
- **Two functional classes of heads**: seven heads found on FLORES-200 plain continuations (implicit prompt language) vs three found on LCB (explicitly requested reply languages), with head selection guarded by ΔNLL ≤ 1 and ≥90% English retention to exclude general generation damage.
- **Value-level mechanism with matched-magnitude controls**: patching the values the head reads at the requested-language name with another language's mean fails 90 (Qwen2.5-1.5B) and 43 (Gemma-3-1B) baseline-passing replies and flips 44/24 entirely into the donor language, while a random-direction patch of equal or larger output change (2.03–2.04 vs 2.007 norm) fails only 4–8 and 1–6 (p < 10⁻¹⁰) — the name's values carry language-specific information.
- **Timing analysis**: zeroing L22H6 only during decoding (after the first generated token) makes 31 of 96 replies fail versus 2 when zeroed only during prompt processing — the head acts mainly while the reply is being generated.
- **Post-training trajectory**: OLMo-2-1B's L12H8 FLORES effect grows monotonically across released checkpoints (Base 0.005 → SFT 0.028 → DPO 0.071 → final 0.080), with crosslingual LCB damage already at −0.247 after SFT.
- **Honest negative results**: SmolLM3-3B has no qualifying head (its candidate L1H12 has ΔNLL +2.58 and emits repeated `</think>`); OLMo-3-7B's head is reported separately because its LCB effect vanishes under mean ablation; steering with the requested language's mean beats all control heads only in Gemma-3-1B (+0.440, 0.118→0.560) and Gemma-3-4B (+0.079).

## Methodology

- **Models**: 12 instruction-tuned checkpoints across seven families — Qwen2.5 1.5B/3B/7B, Qwen3 1.7B/4B, Gemma-3 1B/4B, OLMo-2 1B, OLMo-3 7B, Llama-3.2 1B/3B, SmolLM3 3B — plus the corresponding base checkpoints for the seven FLORES heads and OLMo-2's Base/SFT/DPO/final sequence.
- **Data**: 2,500 FLORES-200 continuation prompts (500 each in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian; greedy, ≤40 new tokens) and LCB chat prompts (800 non-English monolingual + 200 English controls + 1,196 crosslingual English prompts that request one of the four non-English languages; ≤100 tokens, benchmark's own fastText line-level scoring, pass rate = LPR).
- **Interventions**: (1) zero ablation of the head-output slice z_t^(h) before o_proj at all positions; (2) mean ablation with the pooled FLORES mean μ_h; (3) attention masking of the name token in decoding steps only; (4) value patching at the language-name positions with same-/other-/second-language means plus a random-direction control of matched distance; (5) full output replacement with language-conditioned means μ_h,ℓ.
- **Metrics**: directional correct-to-wrong rate C→W on FLORES (langdetect labels; ignores wrong-to-correct flips); paired ΔLPR with 2,000-resample bootstrap CIs on LCB; ΔNLL per-token loss change on 100 FLORES dev sentences per language.
- **Selection protocol**: 125-prompt FLORES screen of every head with ΔNLL ≤ 1 and ≥90% English retention and C→W ≥ 0.10; 2,500-prompt follow-up rerunning all heads of the layers holding the strongest candidates; models without a qualifying FLORES head are screened head-by-head on 100 LCB crosslingual prompts. Each selected head is compared only against other heads of its own layer plus three random same-layer controls. Statistics use pre-specified exact McNemar tests and intersection-union logic; several controls (random-direction, second-language) had readings fixed in code before being run.
- **Specificity checks**: alternative detectors (langid, fastText, 2-of-3 vote — C→W changes ≤0.02); English LCB controls unaffected (pass rates 0.990–1.000); switched continuations stay closer to the prompt in Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B cosine similarity (0.558–0.683) than the same article's next sentence (0.384); a 14-language LCB extension on six models.

## Results & Highlights

- **Single-head dominance on FLORES**: the seven FLORES heads move 8.0–51.5% of all 2,500 continuations out of the prompt's language (Zero C→W), versus at most 1.4% for any other head in the same layer — e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B L22H6: C→W 0.500 vs 0.007 for the best same-layer head; Qwen2.5-3B L27H13: 0.515 vs 0.014.
- **LCB chat replies confirm it**: zero ablation of Llama-3.2-1B L8H25 drops crosslingual LPR from 0.874 by −0.711 (95% CI [−0.737, −0.684]) and turns 71.0% of screening replies from pass to fail (≤4.0% for any other head of the layer); Qwen2.5-3B L27H13 drops monolingual LPR by −0.537 from a 0.982 baseline; Gemma-3-1B L11H3 drops monolingual LPR by −0.628 from 0.984 — while random same-layer control heads change LPR by at most ±0.038.
- **Persistence under mean ablation**: each of the ten counted heads keeps 39–89% of its zero-ablation LCB drop with the 95% CI below zero on the task it affects most (e.g., L22H6 monolingual −0.246 [−0.276, −0.215]; L8H25 crosslingual −0.357 [−0.384, −0.329]).
- **Effect decoupled from loss**: the seven heads raise plain-text loss by only +0.026 to +0.720 nats (layer mean for other heads ≤ +0.022) — Qwen2.5-7B L19H1 at +0.026 nats still moves 20% of continuations (441 of its 500 departures go to Chinese, not English).
- **Value patching at the language name**: replacing the values L22H6/L11H3 read at the requested-language name with another language's mean fails 90/323 and 43/51 baseline-passing replies and puts 44 and 24 entire replies into the donor language (none under same-language values, p < 10⁻⁶); the matched random-direction control (output change ≥ that of the real patch) fails only 4–8 and 1–6 replies across three seeds (p < 10⁻¹⁰).
- **Language steering via output replacement**: writing another language's mean μ_h,ℓ into the head redirects 90.4–99.7% of the 2,000 non-English FLORES continuations and 84.4–99.4% of scored non-English monolingual LCB replies entirely into the swapped-in language in five models (Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen3-1.7B, both Gemma, OLMo-2), versus 0% for any of three same-layer control heads; Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B are outliers at 18.2%/4.3% and 41.7%/12.2%.
- **Requested-language steering works only where baselines are weak**: crosslingual LPR improves beyond every control head only in Gemma-3-1B (0.118 → 0.560, ΔLPR +0.440 [+0.410, +0.469]) and Gemma-3-4B (0.133 → 0.213, +0.079 [+0.064, +0.095]); corrected replies keep cosine similarity 0.69–0.86 to the same prompt's baseline reply (vs 0.15–0.21 to another prompt's).
- **Post-training amplification**: OLMo-2-1B L12H8's FLORES C→W grows 0.005 (Base) → 0.028 (SFT) → 0.071 (DPO) → 0.080 (final), and in Qwen2.5-1.5B the same L22H6 moves 50.0% of continuations under Instruct+chat-template vs 17.6% Instruct+raw-text and 8.9% Base+chat-template.
- **Agreement with prior translation-head scores**: against Lasnier et al.'s released base-model rankings, Qwen3-1.7B L18H12 tops 20/20 translation directions (rank 1/448 heads) and Llama-3.2-1B L8H25 ranks 1/512 (7/20 directions); Gemma-3-1B L11H3 ranks only 3/104, suggesting family-dependent overlap.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The single most valuable contribution is breadth — prior work localized language control in neurons (Tang et al. 2024; Nie et al. 2025) or in heads of 2–3 models for few-shot translation (Lasnier et al. 2026b; Liu et al. 2026 LAHIS); showing one-head sensitivity across ten instruction-tuned checkpoints of seven families, and dissociating implicit-continuation heads from explicit-request heads, is a genuine advance. The value-patching experiment at the language name is the cleanest mechanism result.
- **Rigor**: Strong on controls (matched-norm random directions, second-language donors, control heads, timing splits, three detectors, held-out prompts) but weakened by selection circularity the authors themselves flag: heads are chosen by their largest observed ablation effect, which favors upward noise, and the FLORES swap rates in Table 9 are measured on the same 2,500 prompts used to estimate the language means (only LCB transfers to unseen prompts). The decision to exclude OLMo-3-7B was made after seeing its LCB results, and the English-retention condition was written after a first Qwen2.5-7B run selected L0H25 (which kept only 51% of English continuations in English) — post-hoc criteria that conveniently remove disconfirming cases.
- **Reproducibility**: Public code and per-model precision disclosure are good; but bf16 nondeterminism is nontrivial — batched vs single-prompt runs of Qwen2.5-7B produce identical continuations on only 15/20 checked prompts (10/20 for OLMo-3-7B), so the small effects in exactly those two models (−0.074, −0.076) rest on shaky numerics, which the authors acknowledge but do not resolve.
- **Significance**: Interpretability-moderate, applied-low. No claim is made that naturally occurring language confusion uses these heads — all departures are intervention-induced. The steering result is a probe, not a mitigation: it fails in 5 of 7 models and the paper explicitly declines to propose a repair method.
- **Red flags**: (1) The "ten of twelve" headline quietly becomes seven FLORES heads + three LCB heads selected under different screens with different tasks, and two models (SmolLM3, OLMo-3-7B) fail the pattern — a nontrivial 2/12 exception rate that complicates "across model families." (2) The mechanism analysis covers only two models and 480 prompts, with just 51 baseline-passing Gemma-3-1B replies — the strongest mechanism claims rest on 34–105 reply flips. (3) All five FLORES languages are European Latin-script; the 14-language extension covers only six models, and Qwen2.5-1.5B already fails on Japanese and Russian, hinting the "single head" story may not survive non-Latin scripts. (4) The size trend (≥4B models show ≤0.076 drops in 3 of 4 cases) confounds parameter count with model family, admitted but unresolved.

## Strengths

- Unusual breadth: every head of 12 checkpoints screened, not a case study of one model.
- Methodological hygiene rare in interpretability papers: pre-specified tests, controls fixed before running, disclosure of post-hoc decisions, and honest separation of counted vs uncounted heads.
- The random-direction control is the paper's best experiment: it cleanly separates "the name's values encode language identity" from "any perturbation of this size breaks the head," which pure ablation studies cannot do.
- Correct-to-wrong (C→W) instead of raw switch rates avoids crediting accidental fixes; paired bootstrap CIs and per-language breakdowns make the effect sizes interpretable.
- Quantified dissociation between language-adherence damage and LM loss (+0.026 nats vs 20% language flips) is exactly the kind of specificity evidence the field needs.

## Limitations

- Language coverage is five Latin-script European languages for FLORES and four for LCB's main eval; no claims survive contact with non-Latin scripts except partially in the 6-model, 14-language extension (Qwen2.5-1.5B already fails Japanese and Russian).
- Only 1B–7B models; effects are smaller in most ≥4B models and the size-vs-family confound is unresolved.
- The procedure reports at most one head per model and never ablates sets of heads, so it cannot measure redundancy, shared function, or self-repair (the hydra effect).
- All language confusion here is induced by intervention; natural confusion could involve different circuits.
- LCB's line-level metric leaves replies without a ≥5-word line unscored, systematically omitting short answers and some script-switching cases; LPR measures language adherence, not answer correctness.
- Mechanism results rest on two models with small baseline-passing reply counts (51 for Gemma-3-1B); bf16 nondeterminism undermines the two largest models' small effects.
- Steering with the requested language improves replies beyond controls only in the two Gemma models whose baselines are floor-level (0.118/0.133), so the practical repair story is thin.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是对语言混淆现象的因果性机制解释工作——单头消融、value patching、语言条件化输出替换都是本领域核心方法，且跨 12 个 checkpoint 的系统性与预注册式统计控制可作为消融研究的范式参考。虽然不涉及训练稳定性或推理可靠性，但"微小干预引起大行为翻转、且与 loss 解耦"的发现对理解模型内部脆弱性有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the single-head pattern survive non-Latin scripts (Chinese, Arabic, Korean) in the FLORES screen, or is it an artifact of Latin-script language separability that Qwen2.5-1.5B's Japanese/Russian failures hint at?
- Do naturally occurring language-confusion failures (without intervention) route through these same heads — testable by patching the head back to its clean state on confusions collected from production logs?
- How much of the function is distributed? Ablating pairs or triples of the top-k heads per layer would quantify redundancy and hydra-effect self-repair.
- Does the instruction-tuning amplification (OLMo-2: 0.005 → 0.080 across SFT/DPO/RLVR) reflect the chat template teaching the head to read language names, or preference data rewarding language adherence — and can a controlled fine-tuning run isolate it?
- Since zeroing L19H1 costs only +0.026 nats on Qwen2.5-7B, could head-level regularization during post-training (penalizing single-head criticality for language adherence) reduce confusion without quality loss?
- Do the LCB-screened "explicit request" heads (L8H25, L13H19) and the FLORES "implicit continuation" heads form one circuit, and where do they read the reply-language signal in prompts that never name a language?
