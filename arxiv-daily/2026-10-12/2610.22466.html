Critical edge

The 54%/14% averages aggregate heterogeneous settings over small test splits (50–124 items) with no confidence intervals on the O/T/G components — medians or per-benchmark statistics would be less headline-friendly but more honest.

01 Methodology · the audit protocol

A fixed-weight executor runs with harness H (prompts, skills, tools, code); a proposer agent iteratively edits it, keeping the best search-set score S(Ĥ). HDA asks where the gain ΔS comes from:

O
shortcut neutralized → score drop (lower bound)
+
T
compute-matched baseline recovers it
+
G
residual = generalizable gain (upper bound)
  1. Compute-match. Rerun the baseline at Ĥ’s inference budget (majority vote / pass@k). Whatever it recovers is T.
  2. Neutralize. An analysis agent makes the smallest edits disabling detected shortcuts — constant answers, hard-coded lookup tables, string-match rules, benchmark-fitted thresholds. The drop is O.
  3. Attribute. G = ΔS − O − T; paired bootstrap gates every attribution before it counts.

Suite: 11 executors (Qwen 0.8B–35B, Claude Haiku 4.5 / Opus 4.8) × 3 evolution frameworks (Meta-Harness, SkillOpt, AHE) × 4 benchmarks (ALFWorld, LiveMath, CREATE, SWE-bench Verified) = 25 settings, 21 with significant gains. Released as a reusable “HDA skill” with two human checkpoints.

03 Strengths

  • Clean additive identity ΔS = O + T + G with honest bound semantics — O a lower bound, G an upper bound; the SWE-bench held-out regressions empirically confirm G ≠ transfer.
  • Unusually broad autopsy: 11 executors × 3 frameworks × 4 benchmarks, with held-out splits carved along meaningful axes (task types, rooms, relation types).
  • Auditable evidence, not vibes: full code excerpts of each shortcut — the 47-object LOCATION_PRIORS table, the LiveMath MARKERS string-match branch, the +12/−8-line patch heuristic.
  • The compute-matched baseline is a cheap, devastating control any future paper can adopt.
  • Released as a reusable HDA skill, plus actionable reporting recommendations.

04 Limitations

  • Detection-dependent: only detected, separable mechanisms are neutralized — O underestimates true overfitting, G overestimates real gain.
  • Two human checkpoints (compute matching, neutralization) make the protocol semi-deterministic; different teams could get different O.
  • Small test splits (ALFWorld 120 · LiveMath 124 · SWE-bench 50) and no confidence intervals on the O/T/G components.
  • Three frameworks and a Qwen-heavy executor pool; GEPA / DSPy / AlphaEvolve-style optimizers discussed but not analyzed.
  • LiveMath’s meta-option is an extreme benchmark defect — the 54% average is not a universal constant.
  • No latency or dollar-cost analysis of the extra inference compute behind T.

A corrective paper for a subfield that routinely reports search-set gains — its value is the audit tool, not the headline constant.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Can neutralization be made deterministic — automated artifact detection, no human checkpoints, reproducible O across teams?
  • How do O/T/G shares shift on genuinely low-artifact, freshly collected benchmarks where string-match shortcuts cannot exist?
  • Would budget-constrained evolution (quality at matched cost) discover different mechanisms than post-hoc attribution finds?
  • Is the ALFWorld “proposer distillation” effect benchmark-specific, or would a strong proposer hard-code larger benchmarks like SWE-bench too?
  • Could HDA run inside the evolution loop as a regularizer, penalizing harnesses whose gains vanish under neutralization or compute matching?
  • What are the per-setting confidence intervals on O/T/G, and which reported splits are statistically distinguishable from alternatives?
