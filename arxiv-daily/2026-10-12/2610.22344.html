Key Finding

Windowed Interaction RL turns a 35B open-scale model into an autonomous ML engineer that ties frontier models on Kaggle.

MLExpert: Towards a Generalist Agentic Model for Autonomous Machine Learning Engineering

Shangheng Du, Jinxin Shi, Shuaiyu Zhang, Yiming Wu, et al. — 10 authors · ECNU + Shanghai AI Laboratory

arXiv 2610.22344 · cs.AI · 2026-10-12 · 23 pages

autonomous MLE agent agentic RL AI4AI long-horizon agent Kaggle benchmarks

一句话总结（节选）：上海 AI Lab 提出 MLExpert（35B MoE 自主机器学习工程智能体），通过 Kaggle-MLE Factory 构建可执行任务、分层智能体 SFT 蒸馏长程优化轨迹，再用窗口化交互强化学习（WIRL）……在 MLE-Bench Lite 上把基座的任意奖牌率从 37.9% 提升到 72.7%（+34.9 个百分点），追平 GLM-5.3 与 Kimi-K3 等更大前沿模型。

22 Kaggle tasks, mean of 3 runs (±2.6) — ties GLM-5.3 and Kimi-K3 at 72.7; best same-scale baseline reaches only 47.0. Under Claude Code it still scores 69.7% (base: 42.4). Gold rate: 22.7 → 50.0.

Why This Matters

A working recipe for agents that run their own experiments and improve their own solutions — directly relevant to self-evolving agents and automated research loops.

Critical Edge

Each frontier baseline was evaluated once on 22 tasks, where a single task swings Any Medal by 4.55 pts — the “ties GLM-5.3 / Kimi-K3” claim (72.7 vs 72.7) sits within noise.

Med+ 84.9 vs Kimi-K3’s 95.5: parity comes via a broader bronze/silver base, not top-tier wins.

Methodology

One end-to-end recipe: the policy itself — not an external workflow — organizes a whole Kaggle-length optimization run. Four components:

01

Kaggle-MLE Factory

Agent pipeline (discover → analyze → generate → execute → validate) distills 11,649 Meta-Kaggle competitions into 154 human-reviewed executable tasks with hidden test splits and one unified evaluator.

02

Interaction Harness

ReAct loop over an “experiment tree” of parent-linked solution nodes; 13 tools — execute_code, patch_code, invalidate_node, update_answer, an independent-context analyze sub-agent. History summarized near the context limit.

03

Hierarchical Agentic SFT

General coding/tool SFT, then 636 outcome-filtered teacher trajectories (GLM-5.2, Qwen3.8-Max; 5,539 records, ~302M tokens). Keep only leaderboard-relative quality η ≥ 0.4; truncate post-peak tails (δ > 0.05); loss on generated tokens only.

04

Windowed Interaction RL

One shared medal-tiered reward (−1.0 … 2.0+T, trend T = ±0.2) per execution window — all decisions between two experiment results. No group normalization; windows collected asynchronously; token-level off-policy correction, ratio clipped to [0.2, 4.0].

eval budget: ≤3,000 steps · 12 h/task 20 vCPU · 220 GB RAM · 1× H200 SFT: 262k-token seqs · 8 epochs RL: 32 windows/batch · lr 1e-6 → 1e-7

Transfer detail

  • NatureBench: Match-SOTA also rises 40.0 → 70.0; Surpass-SOTA 50.0 matches GLM-5.3, trails Kimi-K3 (70.0). Single 10-task eval.
  • AIME (fixed GPT-4.1-nano solver): 43.7% held-out at 8.40 calls/problem — beats Kimi-K3 (40.0% at 8.65), below GLM-5.3 (48.1% at 9.39).
  • MLS-Bench-Lite: wins 10 of 12 domains (causal discovery 0.000 → 0.371), loses time-series (0.446 → 0.428).
  • Case study (MLSP ’13 Birds): ROC-AUC 0.58 → 0.964 within one 12-h run; SFT-only variant reaches 0.88.

Strengths

  • Complete end-to-end recipe (task factory → harness → SFT → RL) — every stage has an ablation isolating its contribution.
  • WIRL attacks the two hardest problems of long-horizon agentic RL — delayed aggregated feedback and rollout staleness — with mechanisms that cut learner waiting by 48.3 pts vs GRPO.
  • Evaluation breadth above the norm: two harnesses, a science benchmark, and two out-of-domain transfer settings, all with per-task breakdowns.
  • Honest failure reporting (time-series regression, a 0/0 domain, Med+ gap) plus full hyperparameter disclosure.

Limitations

  • 22-task benchmark granularity with single-run frontier baselines — several headline comparisons sit within noise.
  • SFT corpus depends on proprietary teachers (GLM-5.2, Qwen3.8-Max); the community cannot regenerate it exactly.
  • Expensive protocol (12 h × H200 + 220 GB RAM per task); total training compute is not reported.
  • Medal-threshold reward is Kaggle-specific; porting WIRL to domains without leaderboards is only implicitly addressed.
  • Generalization is uneven — wins 10/12 MLS domains yet loses time-series and ties 0/0 on vision densification, with no analysis of why transfer fails.

Questions for Further Exploration

Q1Is the bronze/silver/gold discretization load-bearing — would raw normalized score work, or does the tiering cap gold-chasing exploration?

Q2With single-eval frontier baselines, would repeated runs of Kimi-K3 / GPT-5.6 Sol reorder the top of Table 1 (MLExpert SEM alone is ±2.62)?

Q3Can the closed GLM-5.2 teacher trajectories be replaced by self-generated ones without losing the 47.0 → 62.1 MLE-SFT jump?

Q4Why does time-series regress (0.446 → 0.428) while causal discovery gains +0.37 — a modality- or metric-shape-dependent failure of the window reward?

Q5Does the token-level correction bound [0.2, 4.0] interact with window length — how should bounds and trend reward scale as experiments get shorter or longer?

Q6Toward recursive self-improvement: can MLExpert optimize its own scaffold/harness rather than a fixed GPT-4.1-nano solver — and does the capability compound?
