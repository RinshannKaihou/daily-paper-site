ETH: Data-Balanced Heterogeneous Pipeline Parallelism for Training Large Models

Key Metric
1.36–2.00×
faster training than HexiScale (GPT-3 350M–175B, Ascend 910B clusters); 1.20–1.84× vs Metis — plus config search up to 56.61× faster than Metis.
Why This Matters

Mixed NPU generations and GPUs are the norm under supply constraints — data-granular balancing turns today's mismatched clusters into far faster LLM training.

⚠ Critical Edge

The headline 2× is the best case: on mild heterogeneity it's just 1.20–1.40× vs Metis, and at small global batch sizes the advantage collapses to 1.02–1.10×.

Methodology — How ETH Works

16× vs <5×
compute vs memory growth in 5 years — strong devices starved for memory idle up to 32% of compute
1 layer = 20%
workload swing at GPT-3 13B / 8-way PP — layer granularity adds 16% pipeline bubbles
GBS ≥251×
DeepSeek-V3's global batch vs layer count — data granularity is free in real training regimes

ETH runs multiple independent heterogeneous pipelines as data-parallel replicas, each with its own local batch size (Lbs), micro-batch size, and per-stage TP/DP/layer split — so balance granularity scales with batch size, not layer count.

01 · ENUMERATE
Pipelines as unordered device sets; stages ordered (1F1B memory differs by position)
→
02 · OPTIMIZE
Aceso-style bottleneck-driven search per stage: TP/DP, layers, recompute, Mbs
→
03 · ALLOCATE
Greedy Lbs: add each micro-batch to whichever pipeline raises max end-time least
→
04 · REFINE
Re-derive stage configurations under the assigned local batches
SYNC · Block-wise All-Reduce

Partitions gradients (operator × TP-shard) on low-TP devices → combines to the max-TP layout → fuses blocks sharing a DP group — fixing address/size mismatches across heterogeneous TP degrees without device-wise padding (+25%) or tensor-wise stream explosion.

SEARCH · Exponential → O(N³)

Config caching + early skipping (Hungarian-algorithm dominance bound, scaled-throughput bound with tolerance α) + variance-ordered evaluation + dynamic programming. Unoptimized search took 9,844 s at 13B — these cut it to a practical overhead.

Testbed: Ascend 910B2/B3/B4 + NVIDIA A100 / RTX 3090 · GPT-3 350M–175B (seq 2048, GBS 1024) · LLaMA-2 70B · baselines: HexiScale (MLSys '26), Metis (ATC '24).

Strengths

  • Root cause pinned with hardware data — compute ~16× vs memory <5× growth over five years, not vague "device differences".
  • Data-granularity balancing is free in modern regimes: GBS dwarfs layer counts (DeepSeek-V3 ≥251×).
  • Block-wise All-Reduce is an engineered middle ground, independently reusable in any system mixing TP degrees within a DP group.
  • Search cost engineered as seriously as training speed: four ablated optimizations turn an exponential space into O(N³) (9,844 s → practical).
  • Honest reporting: admits higher communication overhead, no optimality guarantee, and that unoptimized ETH is slower than baselines.

Limitations

  • PP/TP/DP only — no context or expert parallelism, so long-context training and MoE models are out of scope.
  • Cost models assume the 1F1B schedule; zero-bubble and interleaved schedules would need new models.
  • Heuristic balancing with no global-optimality guarantee; greedy Lbs allocation (increments of Mbs) is coarse when Mbs is large.
  • Convergence shown on a single corpus (Enwiki, 2,000 iters); synthetic GPT-3 shapes plus one LLaMA-2 run, no production-scale workload.
  • Fundamentally needs large-batch regimes (GBS 4 → only 1.02–1.10×); heavier DP traffic means the win depends on fast interconnects.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does per-pipeline gradient noise-scale divergence (unequal Lbs, equal All-Reduce weight) affect late-stage training — should gradients be weighted by local batch size?
  2. Can block-wise All-Reduce extend to expert parallelism, where routing-dependent gradient sparsity breaks the uniform-block assumption?
  3. Does the variance-based search ordering hold on clusters mixing more than two or three device types?
  4. Is there a hybrid that falls back to layer balancing for small critical batch sizes without re-paying search cost?
  5. How much of the 1.4–2× speedup survives on 25–50 Gbps interconnects, and could block fusion be made topology-aware to compensate?
