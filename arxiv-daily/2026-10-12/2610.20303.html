Key Finding

Quantization rotations have a closed form, Q = U·V, matching learned rotations at a fraction of the calibration cost.

从协方差最大化第一性原理导出闭式旋转 Q = U·V：U 对齐激活谱（KLT），V 为方差均衡混合分量（Hadamard / 蝶形均为特例），在共享尺度量化下可证明最优。

Key Metric · 70B calibration

21×

faster than SpinQuant — 2.25 GPU-h & 22.42 GiB vs 46.40 h & 218.72 GiB, with 9.8× less memory. Calibrates on one A6000 in 43 min, or pure CPU in 51 min.

Why This Matters

Rotation-based LLM quantization finally gets a theoretical footing and a closed-form drop-in — slashing the time and memory barrier for quantizing large models.

Critical Edge

Parity, not superiority: statistically tied with DartQuant on 4 of 5 models, clearly behind on Llama-2 7B (21.44 vs 20.46 avg PPL); only the W4-A4-KV4 regime is ever tested.

Methodology distilled

  1. 128 WikiText2 seqs → Welford Σ
  2. Σ = UΛUᵀ → alignment U
  3. mixer V = H or B
  4. Q = U·V → R1/R2
  5. absorb into weights
  6. GPTQ W4-A4-KV4
  • Variance equalization is the objective. Maximizing off-diagonal covariance energy of QᵀΣQ over the Stiefel manifold is proven equivalent (Frobenius invariance + Cauchy–Schwarz) to equalizing every rotated variance at λ̄ = Tr(Σ)/d.
  • Every optimal rotation factors as Q = U·V. U is the covariance eigenbasis (KLT alignment); admissible mixers V carry entries ±1/√d — exactly the Hadamard family, with π/4-butterfly matrices as an O(d log d) subset. This retroactively explains why random Hadamard works in QuaRot/QuIP#: it is optimal only after data alignment.
  • Provably optimal under shared-scale quantization. Total distortion d·c₀·σ²max·2⁻²ᵇ hits its lower bound iff per-coordinate variances are equalized — upgrading a heuristic to a criterion, with zero iterative search.
  • Zero inference overhead. RMSNorm commutes with orthogonal matrices, so all R1–R4 rotations fold into the surrounding weights; measured decode speed is unchanged.

Strengths

  • Closed form, provably optimal under shared-scale quantization — no iterative search, no orthogonality-projection heuristics, no convergence concerns.
  • One framework unifies data-agnostic and data-dependent rotations (Hadamard, butterfly, learned) and explains QuaRot/QuIP#'s empirical success.
  • Decisive efficiency win: 21× faster / 9.8× lighter than SpinQuant at 70B, under one-sixth of DartQuant's memory — CPU-calibratable.
  • Hardware-verified zero overhead on a real M4, plus careful statistics throughout (5 seeds, stds, significance language).

Limitations

  • On par, not ahead, of the strongest baseline — a statistical tie with DartQuant on 4/5 models and a clear loss on Llama-2 7B.
  • Single regime tested (W4-A4-KV4): no 3-bit or mixed-precision results, exactly where rotation quality matters most.
  • One layer-pooled global U for R1 may underfit layer-specific outliers; per-layer fit unexplored, and R3/R4 stay random.
  • Narrow coverage: five Llama/Mistral checkpoints; perplexity + eight zero-shot tasks only — no long-context, code, or multilingual evals.
  • Proof leans on high-rate + Gaussian-like assumptions; heavy-tailed activations make the optimality suggestive rather than airtight.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the optimality argument survive per-channel scales, lattice codebooks (QuIP#-style), or learned clipping?
  • At W3-A3 / W4-A3, does the closed-form rotation still tie DartQuant, or does iterative optimization become necessary?
  • Would per-layer alignment matrices U beat the single pooled global U — and how much of DartQuant's Llama-2 7B lead comes from calibrating R3/R4?
  • Does the calibrated U transfer across domains — WikiText2 calibration deployed on code or long-context inputs?
  • Can non-orthogonal components (scales, as in OSTQuant/WUSH) join while keeping zero-overhead absorption?
