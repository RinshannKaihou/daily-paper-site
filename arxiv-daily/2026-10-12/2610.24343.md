> **一句话总结：** 本文在 8B 稠密与 30B-A3B MoE 混合 Mamba-Transformer 模型上、以 1T token 训练预算系统消融了各家的 NVFP4 训练配方，并提出融合各家所长的 "Mosaic NVFP4" 配方（1D 权重量化 + 反向重量化、双反向 GEMM 的 dim-128 Random Hadamard 变换、MS-EDEN 无偏梯度估计、Four Over Six 自适应 scale），在 MoE 模型上把相对 BF16 的训练 loss 差距压到 0.81% 以内（比 NVIDIA '25 配方再降 0.36 个百分点），并证明保留最后 15% 层为 BF16 在该规模下不可省略。

> **TL;DR:** A systematic ablation of NVFP4 (4-bit floating-point) pretraining recipes on Nemotron-H 8B (dense) and Nemotron 3 Nano 30B-A3B (MoE) hybrid Mamba-Transformer models trained to 1T tokens. The proposed "Mosaic NVFP4" recipe — combining 1D weight quantization with backward re-quantization (TetraJet/Quartet), dim-128 tiled Random Hadamard Transforms on both backward GEMMs, MS-EDEN unbiased gradient estimation (Quartet II), and Four Over Six adaptive scale selection (Cook et al.) — cuts the relative train-loss gap to BF16 to <0.81% on the 30B-A3B MoE (a 0.36 pp improvement over the NVIDIA '25 NVFP4 baseline; <1.2% and +0.24 pp on 8B), the best FP4 result reported at this scale. Two key negative findings: keeping the last 15% of layers in BF16 is mandatory (full quantization diverges past 300B tokens on 8B), and de-biasing helps gradients most — MS-EDEN on gradients plus Four Over Six on X.T/W.T matches or beats applying MS-EDEN to all backward tensors.

## 核心贡献

- **首个统一消融框架**：在共同的训练栈、相同 GPU 架构与相同数据/预算下，横向对比 NVIDIA '25、TetraJet 系列、Quartet/Quartet II、Four Over Six 等主流 NVFP4 配方的各个组件（权重 1D/2D 量化、反向重量化、RHT 尺寸与符号向量、随机舍入 vs MS-EDEN、Four Over Six scale 选择、选择性高精度层），按"相对 BF16 的 loss 差距"逐项量化贡献。
- **提出 Mosaic NVFP4 组合配方**：1D 权重量化 + 反向重量化 → 双反向 GEMM 的 dim-128 tiled RHT（符号向量训练中重采样）→ 梯度用 MS-EDEN 替代随机舍入 → 其余前向/反向张量用 Four Over Six。作者称这是该规模下与 BF16 loss 差距最小的 FP4 训练结果。
- **证明选择性高精度层不可省**：即使采用优化后的配方，把 8B 模型所有层全部量化也会在 300B token 之后发散；保留最后 15% 层 BF16 是规模化下恢复精度所必需的。
- **揭示反向去偏的差异化收益**：并非所有反向张量都同等受益于去偏——MS-EDEN 只用于梯度（G、G.T）加上 Four Over Six 处理其余反向张量（X.T、W.T），在全层量化时 loss 差距最低，说明偏差校正对梯度张量最重要。
- **方法论警示**：配方间的相对排序在 150B token 之前不稳定、弱配方早期常显得更好，短程 NVFP4 消融只能发现灾难性不稳定，不足以判断长程精度保持能力。

## Key Contributions

- **First unified, at-scale ablation of NVFP4 training techniques**, instantiated in one training stack on identical hardware: 1D vs 2D weight quantization, backward re-quantization, Random Hadamard Transform (RHT) tile dimension (16 vs 128) and static vs resampled sign vectors, stochastic rounding vs the MS-EDEN unbiased estimator, Four Over Six adaptive scale selection, and selective high-precision layers — each measured by the relative train/validation loss gap to BF16.
- **The "Mosaic NVFP4" recipe**, which sequentially stacks the winning choices from prior work: 1D weight quantization with backward re-quantization (TetraJet line), dim-128 tiled RHTs on both backward GEMMs with resampled sign vectors, MS-EDEN unbiased gradients (from Quartet II, combining random rotations with per-block corrective rescaling for lower quantization error than stochastic rounding), and Four Over Six scale selection on the remaining forward and backward tensors. Claimed as the strongest FP4 training result at this scale in loss gap to BF16, while remaining compatible with efficient kernels.
- **Selective high-precision layers are non-negotiable at scale**: with every layer quantized, even the Mosaic recipe diverges past 300B tokens on the 8B dense model, whereas keeping the last 15% of layers in BF16 is stable — evidence of a representation failure mode that no studied error-reduction technique fixes.
- **Tensor-selective de-biasing**: applying MS-EDEN to all backward tensors gives no clear benefit over gradients-only; the lowest loss gap under full quantization comes from MS-EDEN on G/G.T plus Four Over Six on X.T/W.T, suggesting bias correction matters most for gradient tensors and complementary error reduction suffices elsewhere.
- **A cautionary methodological finding**: recipe rankings do not stabilize until well past 150B tokens — short FP4 ablations can detect catastrophic instability but cannot predict long-horizon accuracy retention.

## Methodology

All NVFP4 variants are implemented in a common training stack and trained on the same GPU architecture (NVIDIA Blackwell tensor cores execute NVFP4 GEMMs). Two backbones from the Nemotron family, both hybrid Mamba-Transformer: the dense Nemotron-H 8B (24 Mamba-2 layers, 4 attention, 24 FFN) and the MoE Nemotron 3 Nano 30B-A3B (23 Mamba-2 / 6 attention / 23 MoE layers; 128 routed experts with top-6 routing plus 2 shared experts; 3.2B active of 31.6B total parameters). Both are trained to 1T tokens with AdamW, a Warmup-Stable-Decay schedule (annealing over the final 20%/15% of the horizon), weight decay 0.1, and sequence length 8192; the 8B model uses the Nemotron-H phased data blend (diverse data for 600B tokens, then high-quality data), the MoE uses the first 1T tokens of Nemotron 3's Phase-1 mixture.

Quantization applies to linear/grouped-linear layers, organized around the three GEMMs — forward Y=XW, DGRAD=G·W.T, WGRAD=G.T·X.T — so each tensor's treatment can be specified independently. The ablation design stacks four cumulative updates on the NVIDIA '25 recipe (Table 1): (1) swap 2D weight quantization for 1D quantization with backward re-quantization; (2) extend RHTs to dim-128 tiles on both WGRAD and DGRAD, with sign vectors resampled throughout training; (3) replace stochastic rounding with MS-EDEN on gradient tensors and apply Four Over Six scale selection to backward tensors W.T and X.T; (4) extend Four Over Six to forward tensors W and X. Full 1T-token runs compare Mosaic vs NVIDIA '25 with the last 15% of layers kept in BF16; ablations run to 400B tokens on the MoE. Run-to-run noise is calibrated from three repeats of the NVIDIA '25 recipe (relative loss range ~0.027–0.064%), and downstream deltas are judged against a per-task noise reference from the same repeats.

## Results & Highlights

- **MoE (Nemotron 3 Nano 30B-A3B, 1T tokens)**: Mosaic NVFP4 finishes with a relative train-loss gap to BF16 of **<0.81%, improving the NVIDIA '25 NVFP4 recipe's gap by 0.36 percentage points**, with sustained separation emerging after the first 75B tokens.
- **Dense (Nemotron-H 8B, 1T tokens)**: Mosaic NVFP4 ends with a **<1.2% relative train-loss gap to BF16, a 0.24 pp improvement over NVIDIA '25**, with the benefit most pronounced early in training and during the LR annealing phase.
- **Cumulative stacking ablation (400B tokens, 30B-A3B)**: the four sequential recipe updates together yield a **0.36 pp total loss-gap improvement**, with the largest single gain from replacing stochastic rounding with MS-EDEN plus Four Over Six on backward tensors — attributed to MS-EDEN achieving unbiasedness at lower quantization-error cost; two further stochasticity tweaks (resampled SR patterns, per-data-parallel-replica DGRAD RHT signs) added another 0.028 pp, so the headline numbers are conservative.
- **Stability**: with the last 15% of layers in BF16 the Mosaic recipe is stable to 1T tokens, but **quantizing all layers makes it diverge past 300B tokens on the 8B model** — and these separations exceed the measured run-to-run variability (0.06% train / 0.04% val on the MoE; 0.027% / 0.064% on 8B).
- **Downstream (Table 4)**: on the MoE, MMLU 5-shot improves 67.5 → 68.4 (+1.3%, outside the 0.9% noise reference) and MMLU-Pro 5-shot CoT 45.0 → 47.1 (+4.7%, within the 6.3% noise band); on 8B, RACE improves 77.7 → 78.8 (+1.4%, outside the 1.1% noise band) while MMLU-Pro drops 29.7 → 27.6 (−7.1%, within its 9.8% noise band) — i.e., most task-level deltas sit inside run-to-run noise, and the loss-gap gains translate only partially into benchmark movement.

## Authors & Credibility

A team spanning NVIDIA (Agrusa, Wei, Wyss, Gibbons, Choi, Sun, Blankevoort), IST Austria (Panferov, Schultheis, Alistarh), and Red Hat AI. Dan Alistarh's group is behind the Quartet/Quartet II line and MS-EDEN, and Blankevoort's group at NVIDIA authored the Four Over Six paper — so the authors have deep firsthand expertise in nearly every technique being compared, and access to the compute (Blackwell clusters, 1T-token runs at 8B/30B-A3B) that few academic groups can match. The work was accepted at the ICML 2026 Workshop on Resource-Adaptive Foundation Model Inference (AdaptFM) — a workshop paper, not a main-conference submission. The experimental hygiene is notably good: common implementation stack, identical hardware/data per comparison, three-run noise calibration, and explicit statistical framing of downstream deltas against a noise reference.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Moderate and honest — this is a comparison/ablation study plus a sensible combination ("Mosaic") of existing components rather than a new algorithm; its value lies in being the first to test these ingredients head-to-head at 8B–30B scale and 1T-token horizons. The framing ("strongest FP4 result at this scale in loss gap to BF16") is carefully scoped and plausible, but "to the best of our knowledge" does a lot of work given concurrent recipes (e.g., TetraJet-v2) are not run head-to-head — the only full-run baseline is NVIDIA '25.
- **Rigor**: Strong on the loss side (run-to-run calibration via three repeats, consistency across train/validation loss and two architectures), weaker on downstream evidence: only ~7 aggregate task rows, most deltas within noise, and the noise reference itself is built from just three runs, which the authors admit may understate true variability. The headline 8B MMLU-Pro drop of −7.1% is waved off as within-noise but is large enough to be concerning.
- **The efficiency story is missing its punchline**: the entire motivation of FP4 training is throughput and energy, yet the paper reports **no wall-clock, throughput, or energy numbers** for Mosaic vs BF16 or vs NVIDIA '25 — MS-EDEN's extra rotations/rescaling and Four Over Six's adaptive scaling surely cost something, and only the unquantified claim "compatible with efficient kernels" is offered.
- **Ablation design caveats**: the main stacking study is sequential (cumulative updates), not factorial, so interaction effects between components are only partially explored (partially mitigated by the separate backward-tensor study in Fig. 3); the Mosaic recipe itself appears to be run once, without repeats.
- **Generalizability red flags**: both backbones are hybrid Mamba-Transformer models (unusual for the FP4-training literature, which mostly targets pure Transformers), one MoE and one dense, at ≤1T tokens and ≤30B total parameters; the authors themselves flag that larger production runs, other modalities, post-training, and other FP4 formats are untested. The authors' dual role — Quartet II/MS-EDEN authors evaluating their own technique against NVIDIA's — is disclosed by authorship but is still a mild conflict-of-interest in how components got picked.
- **Reproducibility**: good on paper (named models, data mixtures, hyperparameters in Appendix A, explicit recipe table), but reproducing requires Blackwell-class FP4 tensor cores and 1T-token budgets; no code release is mentioned.

## Strengths

- First unified, same-stack, same-hardware comparison of the recent NVFP4 recipe explosion at meaningful scale (8B dense + 30B-A3B MoE, 1T tokens) instead of the small-model/short-horizon ablations that dominate the literature.
- Each recipe component is attributed a measured contribution to the BF16 loss gap, ending in a concrete, actionable final recipe (Table 1) that practitioners can adopt.
- Two genuinely useful negative results: full quantization still diverges at scale despite state-of-the-art error correction, and de-biasing should be targeted at gradient tensors rather than sprayed over all backward tensors (leaving room for cheaper complementary techniques like Four Over Six elsewhere).
- The demonstration that recipe rankings invert early in training (weak recipes can look better before ~150B tokens) is an important, widely applicable methodological warning for anyone doing low-precision ablations.
- Clean experimental hygiene: run-to-run noise calibration with three repeats, train and validation loss tracked, results shown to be conservative relative to unexplored extra stochasticity sources.

## Limitations

- No throughput, wall-clock, or energy measurements — the efficiency benefit of FP4 training, the paper's own motivation, is never quantified, and the overhead of MS-EDEN/Four Over Six is not reported.
- Downstream evaluation is thin (a handful of aggregate tasks) with most differences inside run-to-run noise; the noise reference itself rests on only three repeated runs.
- Only one full-run baseline (NVIDIA '25 NVFP4); concurrent recipes such as TetraJet-v2 or Quartet II are ablated as components but never run end-to-end to 1T tokens under this stack.
- Findings are tied to hybrid Mamba-Transformer architectures (Nemotron-H / Nemotron 3 Nano) at medium scale — transfer to pure-Transformer LLMs, larger production runs, post-training, or MXFP4-class formats is unverified (acknowledged in §6).
- The cumulative-stacking ablation design leaves component interactions underexplored, and the final Mosaic configuration itself has no repeated-seed runs.
- The 8B MMLU-Pro result (−7.1% relative, 29.7 → 27.6) is a nontrivial regression even if within the (3-run, possibly underestimated) noise band.

## Relevance

该论文直接命中"低精度/量化 LLM 训练"方向：它系统回答了在真实规模（8B 稠密 + 30B-A3B MoE、1T token）下 NVFP4 预训练配方中哪些组件真正必要，并给出了当前与 BF16 loss 差距最小的可复用配方，对研究 FP4 训练、高效预训练与 Blackwell 上的量化系统实现都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the actual end-to-end throughput and energy cost of Mosaic NVFP4 vs BF16 and vs NVIDIA '25 on Blackwell — does MS-EDEN's extra rotation/rescaling and Four Over Six's adaptive scaling eat into the FP4 speedup?
- Does the "last 15% of layers in BF16" requirement (and the late-training divergence when it is removed) transfer to pure Transformer LLMs, and can a smarter criterion than "last 15%" (e.g., per-layer sensitivity scoring) find a smaller high-precision set?
- Why do recipe rankings invert before ~150B tokens — is there a predictable signature (e.g., gradient-noise scale or outlier-channel growth) that could let short ablations forecast long-horizon accuracy?
- Can the Four Over Six scale selection be unified with MS-EDEN's corrective rescaling into a single lower-overhead operator, given they target overlapping error sources on different tensors?
- How do the conclusions change for post-training (SFT/RL) and for inference-side NVFP4 quantization of models pretrained with Mosaic NVFP4 — does training in FP4 change the quantization robustness of the final weights?
- Would a factorial ablation (all 2^4 combinations of the four updates) reveal interaction effects that the cumulative stacking design misses, particularly between 1D re-quantization and MS-EDEN?
