> **一句话总结：** 提出 Harness-Delta Attribution（HDA）评估协议，把 harness 进化带来的性能提升分解为"基准过拟合 + 测试时算力扩展 + 可泛化残差"三部分；在 11 个执行模型 × 3 个进化框架 × 4 个基准的 21 个有效设置中，过拟合平均占增益的 54%、测试时扩展占 14%，两者合计在 14 个设置中超过 50%——搜索集上的大幅提升大多并非真正的可泛化改进。

> **TL;DR:** This paper introduces HDA, a semi-automated protocol that decomposes harness-evolution gains (evolved harness vs. baseline harness) into Overfitting (score drop after neutralizing detected shortcuts), Test-Time Scaling (gain a compute-matched baseline recovers, e.g., majority-vote/pass@k at matched call counts), and a residual upper bound G. Across 21 statistically significant settings spanning 11 executors, 3 frameworks (Meta-Harness, SkillOpt, AHE), and 4 benchmarks (ALFWorld, LiveMath, CREATE, SWE-bench Verified), overfitting explains 54% and test-time scaling 14% of the gain on average, with O+T > 50% in 14 settings. Examples: on ALFWorld all five Meta-Harness executors hit 100% search-set success yet 98% of the gain is overfitting (the 0.8B harness makes zero LLM calls), and on LiveMath all three frameworks independently rediscover the same always-correct "meta-option" artifact (100% accuracy on the 53 test questions containing it vs. 31% on the other 71 for the 0.8B model). Only SWE-bench shows a sizable generalizable residual (G = 94-100% in several settings). Worth reading for anyone evaluating or building agentic-harness optimization systems.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 HDA（Harness-Delta Attribution）评估协议**：把 harness 进化的增益 ΔS = S(Ĥ) − S(H0) 严格分解为三个可加成分——过拟合 O = S(Ĥ) − S(Ĥ_neutral)（移除检测到的捷径机制后的下降）、测试时扩展 T = S(H0_scale) − S(H0)（算力对齐的基线可复现的部分）、以及残差 G = ΔS − O − T（可泛化改进的上界）。
- **定义了两类过拟合分类法并给出检测流程**：直接捷径（消除 LLM 调用、常量答案、硬编码查找表、字符串匹配规则）与基准衍生启发式（拟合基准统计的阈值、逆向工程的数据构造规则、数据集先验）；以可复用的 "HDA skill" 形式发布，配两个人工检查点（算力对齐方案、过拟合中和操作）。
- **大规模实证解剖**：覆盖 11 个执行模型（Qwen 0.8B–35B + Claude Haiku 4.5 / Opus 4.8）、3 个进化框架（Meta-Harness、SkillOpt、AHE）、4 个基准（LiveMath、SWE-bench Verified、CREATE、ALFWorld），共 25 个设置（21 个增益显著），是首个系统回答"harness 进化的增益从哪里来"的工作。
- **揭示跨框架的捷径趋同现象**：三个框架在 LiveMath 上独立发现同一个元选项伪影（"one of the remaining options is correct, but a stronger result can be proven" 在 21/35 个搜索题中出现且全部为正确答案），分别以代码字符串匹配、技能文档默认规则、系统提示词规则三种形式编码同一捷径。
- **证明 held-out 评估与验证集选择都不足以防止过拟合**：排除 ALFWorld 后，15 个设置中 13 个的 held-out 增益小于搜索集增益；给 Meta-Harness 加验证集选择后，16 个设置中仍有 14 个的 held-out 增益低于搜索集增益。

## Key Contributions

- **Harness-Delta Attribution (HDA) protocol**: decomposes the evolution gain ΔS into Overfitting (O = S(Ĥ) − S(Ĥ_neutral), the drop from neutralizing detected shortcuts in the evolved harness), Test-Time Scaling (T = S(H0_scale) − S(H0), recovered by running the baseline at the evolved harness's compute budget with majority vote / pass@k), and the residual G = ΔS − O − T, an upper bound on generalizable improvement. A paired bootstrap significance test gates attribution: insignificant gains are reported as noise.
- **Taxonomy + reusable skill for detecting overfitting**: direct shortcuts (eliminating LLM calls, constant answers, hard-coded lookup tables, string matching) vs. benchmark-derived heuristics (thresholds fitted to benchmark statistics, reverse-engineered construction rules). Released as an agent-executable "HDA skill" with two human checkpoints (compute matching, neutralization).
- **Large-scale autopsy of harness evolution**: 11 executor models × 3 frameworks (Meta-Harness, SkillOpt, AHE) × 4 benchmarks (LiveMath math MCQ, SWE-bench Verified, CREATE creative generation, ALFWorld embodied planning) = 25 settings, 21 with significant gains — the first systematic study of *where* harness-evolution gains come from rather than how to obtain them.
- **Convergent shortcut discovery across frameworks**: all three frameworks independently rediscover the same LiveMath meta-option artifact, encoding it as code, skill text, or prompt rule — evidence that the shortcuts are benchmark properties, not framework quirks.
- **Held-out evaluation and validation selection are insufficient**: held-out gains are smaller than search gains in 13 of 15 non-ALFWorld settings; adding validation-based harness selection to Meta-Harness improves mean held-out score (+8.0 pp ALFWorld, +0.8 pp LiveMath, +8.5 pp SWE-bench, −0.4 CU CREATE) but fails to close the transfer gap in 14 of 16 settings.

## Methodology

The paper formalizes harness evolution: a fixed-weight executor M with harness H (prompts, skills, tools, memory, orchestration code) is iteratively edited by a proposer agent P using filesystem-backed history of trajectories and scores, selecting Ĥ = argmax over the evaluated pool on the search set. HDA then runs three comparisons:

1. **Compute-matched baseline H0_scale**: preserve H0 but scale it to Ĥ's average inference budget (LLM calls / token usage), e.g., if Ĥ uses 10× executor calls, run H0 10 times with majority vote or pass@10. T = S(H0_scale) − S(H0) isolates gains reproducible by pure test-time compute, avoiding evolution's search cost.
2. **Neutralized harness Ĥ_neutral**: an HDA analysis agent audits harness code/prompts/execution evidence for direct shortcuts and benchmark-derived heuristics and makes the *smallest intervention* disabling each detected mechanism while preserving unrelated evolved components. O = S(Ĥ) − S(Ĥ_neutral) is a lower bound on overfitting (implicit mechanisms may survive).
3. **Residual** G = S(Ĥ_neutral) − S(H0_scale) = ΔS − O − T, interpreted as an upper bound on generalizable improvement.

HDA is packaged as a reusable skill with two human-review checkpoints (compute-matching design; neutralization evidence), plus a paired bootstrap test before any attribution. The evaluation suite spans: LiveMath (35/18/124 train/val/test split), ALFWorld (50 search / 36 val / 120 test games, split along task-type and room axes with withheld task types), CREATE (120 search / 80 val / 561 test items split by relation type and domain, scored by Creative Utility), and SWE-bench Verified (48 search / 24 val / 50 test, seed-42 sampled, ≤1-hour tasks, success rate). Meta-Harness and SkillOpt use Claude Opus 4.8 as proposer; AHE self-evolves with Opus 4.8 as both proposer and executor. Held-out transfer and validation-based selection (aggregate-only validation scores exposed to the proposer) are studied as complementary analyses.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline attribution**: across the 21 settings with significant search-set gains, overfitting accounts for **54%** and test-time scaling for **14%** of the gain on average; their combined share exceeds **50% in 14 of 21 settings**. Per-benchmark: ALFWorld and LiveMath are overfitting-dominated, CREATE is scaling-dominated, SWE-bench Verified shows the clearest generalizable residual.
- **ALFWorld collapse**: all five Meta-Harness executors (Qwen3.5-0.8B to Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) reach **100% search-set success**, but HDA attributes **98%** of that gain to overfitting (89% on average across ALFWorld settings). The Qwen3.5-0.8B harness grew from 66 to 503 lines (7.6×), hard-codes a 47-object location prior table and task recipes, makes **0 LLM calls**, and still scores **93.3% on held-out games** (O/T/G = 100/0/0) — because the proposer's parametric ALFWorld knowledge covers even withheld task types.
- **LiveMath meta-option artifact**: the option "one of the remaining options is correct, but a stronger result can be proven" appears in **21/35 search questions and 53/124 test questions, always correct**. For Meta-Harness with the 0.8B executor, test accuracy is **100% (53/53) on meta-option questions vs. 31% (22/71) on the rest** — a positive held-out gain that reflects an artifact shared across splits, not math reasoning. Attribution: Meta-Harness 91/9/0, SkillOpt 88/0/12, AHE 100/0/0.
- **CREATE is mostly bought compute**: for Meta-Harness + Claude Haiku 4.5 (ΔS = +37.9 CU, O/T/G = 0/49/51), a call-matched baseline recovers about half the gain; the evolved harness makes 20 LLM calls per item (6 first-wave + 14 diversity frames). For Meta-Harness + Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, the compute-matched baseline **fully recovers the +7.5 CU gain (T = 100%) and even outperforms** the evolved harness. AHE raises calls/item from 1.04 to 2.46; test-time scaling contributes **+5.1 CU = 75%** of its gain. SkillOpt (fixed single-call rollout) shows T = 0 but no significant CREATE gain at all.
- **Transfer failures**: excluding ALFWorld, held-out gain < search gain in **13 of 15** settings and < half the search gain in 8; in 4 settings the evolved harness is *worse* than baseline on test. Notably Qwen3.6-27B (+8.3%) and Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B (+12.5%) regress on SWE-bench held-out test despite 0/0/100 attributions, confirming G is an upper bound, not a transfer guarantee.
- **Validation selection helps but does not fix it**: aggregate-validation selection improves mean held-out score by +8.0 pp (ALFWorld), +0.8 pp (LiveMath), +8.5 pp (SWE-bench Verified), −0.4 CU (CREATE); it helps 10 of 16 settings but in 14 of 16 the held-out gain still falls short of the search-set gain.
- **SWE-bench residual is real but mixed**: Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct achieves O/T/G = 0/6/94 (ΔS +33.3%) from a content-independent action-feedback guardrail; Qwen3-4B-Instruct's +27.1% splits 23/23/54 — its patch-size rule (warn when adds ≥ 12 or dels ≥ 8 lines, thresholds fitted to search trajectories) contributes O = 23%, and matching its 1.44× LLM calls lifts the baseline from pass@2 to pass@3 (T = 23%).

## Authors & Credibility

The work is a collaboration between Amazon (Qin Lu, Changlong Yu, Sha Li, Shuowei Jin, Xin Liu, Zhenyu Shi) and NYU (Wenxuan Ding — first author, also affiliated with Amazon — and Greg Durrett, a professor known for rigorous NLP/evaluation work, and co-author of the CREATE benchmark used here). The paper is unusually transparent about its own interpretation limits (explicitly framing O as a lower bound and G as an upper bound), releases code (github.com/Wenwen-D/HarnessDeltaAttribution), a blog, and the HDA skill as supplementary material, and gates every attribution on paired bootstrap significance. Evaluation scope (11 executors, 3 frameworks, 4 benchmarks, 25 settings, 16-setting validation-selection study) is broad for an analysis paper. Acknowledgments cite NSF and Sloan Foundation funding; no competing interests that would bias the negative findings, though Amazon's interest in sober agent-harness evaluation is context, not a red flag.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The decomposition idea (overfitting vs. compute vs. real improvement) is conceptually simple but nobody had operationalized it for harness evolution; the accounting identity ΔS = O + T + G with careful bound semantics is a genuine contribution, and the convergent-shortcut finding (three frameworks rediscovering the LiveMath meta-option) is a memorable, verifiable result. Related concurrent work (Wang et al. 2026b on budget-unified comparisons, RRSI on regularized selection, Evo-Bench) overlaps in spirit but HDA's per-gain attribution is distinct.
- **Rigor**: Significance gating via paired bootstrap, smallest-intervention neutralization, and explicit bounds are all good practice. However, the attribution percentages inherit sampling noise from small evaluation sets — e.g., the LiveMath meta-option analysis rests on 35 search and 124 test questions, and SWE-bench test has only 50 instances, so per-setting O/T/G splits carry unquantified uncertainty (no CIs on the components themselves, only on ΔS).
- **Reproducibility**: The neutralization step depends on an analysis agent's judgment plus two human checkpoints; two teams could plausibly neutralize differently and get different O values. The released skill and code excerpts mitigate this, but the protocol is only semi-deterministic. Also, Meta-Harness is run with aggregate validation scores added relative to its original implementation, so absolute numbers may not match the original papers' setups.
- **Significance**: High — this is a corrective paper for a fast-growing subfield that routinely reports search-set gains. The ALFWorld distillation observation (strong proposer parametrically memorizes a well-known benchmark and writes deterministic controllers that generalize across splits) is an important confound nobody had named.
- **Red flags**: (1) The 54%/14% averages aggregate heterogeneous settings; medians or per-benchmark statistics would be less headline-friendly but more honest (the text does give the qualitative per-benchmark breakdown). (2) The LiveMath meta-option is an extreme benchmark defect — using it as a flagship example partly indicts LiveMath rather than harness evolution generally; cleaner benchmarks might show very different mixes. (3) LiveMath held-out analysis relies on one executor (0.8B) for the 100%-vs-31% contrast. (4) Only Qwen-family open models plus two Claude models are tested; GEPA/DSPy/AlphaEvolve-style optimizers are discussed in related work but not analyzed.

## Strengths

- Clean, additive decomposition (ΔS = O + T + G) with honest bound semantics: O is explicitly a lower bound (undetected shortcuts remain) and G an upper bound — and the SWE-bench regressions empirically confirm G ≠ transfer.
- Unusually broad empirical base: 11 executors × 3 frameworks × 4 benchmarks, with held-out splits deliberately constructed along meaningful axes (held-out task types and rooms for ALFWorld, held-out relation types for CREATE).
- Concrete, auditable evidence: full code excerpts of the overfitting mechanisms (the 47-object LOCATION_PRIORS table, the LiveMath MARKERS string-match branch, the +12/−8-line patch-size heuristic) rather than hand-wavy claims.
- The compute-matched baseline is a simple, devastating control (e.g., majority-vote/pass@k baseline fully recovering a "+7.5 CU" evolved gain on CREATE) that any future paper can adopt cheaply.
- Actionable recommendations: attribution-aware reporting, explicit cost constraints during evolution, and rich low-artifact benchmarks; the HDA skill itself is released for reuse.

## Limitations

- Detection-dependence: HDA neutralizes only *detected, separable* mechanisms, so O underestimates true overfitting and G correspondingly overestimates generalizable improvement; entangled improvements cannot be cleanly attributed.
- Human-in-the-loop checkpoints (compute matching, neutralization) introduce subjectivity and limit full automation and exact reproducibility.
- Small evaluation splits (LiveMath 124 test, SWE-bench 50 test, ALFWorld 120 test games) mean per-setting attribution percentages are noisy, and no confidence intervals are reported on O/T/G components.
- Coverage: 3 frameworks and a Qwen-heavy executor pool; conclusions may not transfer to other optimizers (GEPA, AlphaEvolve, DSPy) or model families. The two SkillOpt CREATE settings and one AHE SWE-bench setting produce insignificant gains, thinning that quadrant of Table 1.
- LiveMath's meta-option artifact is an extreme case of benchmark contamination; benchmarks without such defects could show much smaller O shares, so the "54% average" should not be read as a universal constant.
- No analysis of latency/dollar cost of the extra inference compute beyond call counts, which matters for the practical value of T-type gains.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击当前 agent 自我进化/工作流优化研究的核心评估问题：报告的"提升"究竟来自真实泛化能力还是基准捷径与更多测试时算力。对任何关注 agent 评估方法学、test-time scaling 与 benchmark 污染的工作都有直接参考价值，其 compute-matched baseline 与捷径中和协议可以直接用于我们自己的实验设计与结果审查。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the neutralization step be made fully deterministic (e.g., automated artifact detection via cross-benchmark probing), removing the human checkpoints and making O reproducible across teams?
- How do O/T/G shares change on genuinely low-artifact, freshly collected benchmarks (e.g., live-streamed tasks) where string-match shortcuts cannot exist — would harness evolution still show mostly-overfitting gains?
- Does constraining evolution to a fixed inference-compute budget (optimizing quality at matched cost) change what mechanisms evolution discovers, rather than post-hoc attribution?
- Is the ALFWorld "proposer distillation" effect benchmark-specific (ALFWorld is small and famous), or would a strong proposer similarly memorize and hard-code larger benchmarks like SWE-bench Verified given enough search iterations?
- Could HDA-style attribution be integrated *inside* the evolution loop (e.g., penalize harnesses whose gains vanish under neutralization or compute matching) as a regularizer, complementing concurrent work like RRSI?
- What are the per-setting confidence intervals on O/T/G given the small test splits, and which reported splits (e.g., 23/23/54 on SWE-bench Qwen3-4B) are statistically distinguishable from alternatives?
