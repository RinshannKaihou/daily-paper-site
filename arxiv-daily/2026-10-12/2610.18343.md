> **一句话总结：** 本文利用 206 道题在巴伐利亚方言、标准德语和英语之间逐题对齐的 BAVGROUND 基准，对 14 个 7B–10B 开源指令模型系统评测了训练后量化（BF16 vs GPTQ-8/GPTQ-4/AWQ-4），发现 8-bit GPTQ 基本无损（平均仅 +0.03 个百分点），而 4-bit 平均掉 1.4–1.5 个百分点、改变 11.9–16.0% 的逐题决策且集中于模型本就不确定的题目，但巴伐利亚方言并不因此一致地比德语更脆弱——脆弱性取决于具体模型、量化方法与校准数据的语言构成和来源，多选题精度还会掩盖生成质量的大幅退化（AWQ-4 自由生成净偏好 −17.6 分）。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled study of post-training quantisation on the aligned BAVGROUND benchmark (206 questions x Bavarian/German/English = 618 MC instances) across 14 open-weight 7B–10B instruct models, each compared against its own BF16 baseline. GPTQ-8 is essentially lossless (mean +0.03 pts), but 4-bit GPTQ/AWQ lose ~1.4–1.5 pts on average with huge per-model spread (GPTQ-4: +2.3 for Qwen2.5-7B to −6.6 for GENBA-10B), flip 11.9–16.0% of item-level decisions (concentrated in the lowest BF16-margin decile at ~40–43%), and calibration language can swing the Bavarian–German gap from −3.16 pts (German-only calibration, GPTQ-4) to +0.12 (Bavarian-only). Crucially, the low-resource dialect is NOT consistently more fragile than Standard German, and free-form LLM-judge evaluation reveals degradation (AWQ-4 net preference −17.6 vs BF16) that multiple-choice accuracy largely misses.

## 核心贡献

- **对齐式方言–标准语对照实验设计**：首次在"内容逐题对齐、仅语言不同"的设定下研究量化对区域语言变体的影响。BAVGROUND 的 206 道源题同时实现为巴伐利亚方言、标准德语和英语（共 618 个多选题实例，240 个 GEN 一般文化题 + 378 个 GRD 地方文献题），使巴伐利亚语与德语的差异不能归咎于题目内容不同——这是 MMLU/MGSM/FLORES 等标准多语基准做不到的。
- **迄今最大规模的方言量化横评**：14 个 7B–10B 开源指令模型（含巴伐利亚适配的 GENBA-10B、德语向的 LeoLM/LLaMmlein、欧洲多语的 EuroLLM/Teuken/Salamandra 等、通用多语的 Llama 3.1/Qwen2.5/Gemma 2 等），56 个"模型 × 精度"配置，每个量化模型都和自己的 BF16 基线比较。
- **拆解校准数据对跨语言稳健性的影响**：系统变化校准语言（均衡 / 仅英 / 仅德 / 仅巴伐利亚）与校准文本来源（翻译指令数据 vs 维基媒体文本），发现校准构成可以不仅改变方言–标准语差距的大小，还会翻转其方向。
- **揭示"平均精度掩盖逐题不稳定"**：提出按 BF16 决策边距（margin）分桶分析预测翻转，证明量化改变的主要是模型本就犹豫的题目，而非按语言区分。
- **多选题之外的证据**：用盲评成对 LLM 裁判（gpt-5.6-luna）在 5 个模型上对比量化与 BF16 的自由生成回答，证明固定选项精度几乎无损的配置（如 Mistral GPTQ-4）在生成质量上仍可明显退化。

## Key Contributions

- **Aligned dialect-vs-standard experimental design**: the first quantisation study where language varies but the underlying content is held fixed — BAVGROUND's 206 source questions are realised identically in Bavarian, Standard German, and English (618 MC instances; 240 general-knowledge GEN + 378 regionally grounded GRD), enabling paired per-item comparison that standard multilingual benchmarks (MMLU, MGSM, FLORES-200) cannot provide.
- **Broadest dialect-quantisation sweep to date**: 14 open-weight 7B–10B instruction models x {BF16, GPTQ-8, GPTQ-4, AWQ-4} = 56 configurations, covering Bavarian-adapted (GENBA-10B), German-focused (LeoLM, LLaMmlein), European multilingual (EuroLLM, Teuken, Salamandra, Occiglot, Mistral), and general multilingual (Llama 3.1, Qwen2.5, Gemma 2, Aya Expanse, Granite 3.0, OLMo 2) families, each quantised model compared to its own BF16 baseline.
- **Calibration-data forensics**: varies both the language composition (balanced / English-only / German-only / Bavarian-only) and the source (translated Alpaca/Dolly/OpenHermes vs Wikimedia) of the ~250k-token calibration set, showing calibration can flip the sign of the Bavarian–German gap, and that GPTQ-4 and AWQ-4 respond very differently.
- **Item-level instability analysis**: introduces margin-decile analysis of prediction flips, showing 4-bit methods alter ~40–43% of predictions in the lowest BF16-margin decile but only 1–2% in the highest — pre-quantisation uncertainty, not language, is the dominant predictor of instability.
- **Free-form cross-check**: blinded pairwise LLM judging of BF16 vs quantised generations on all 618 instances for five models, exposing degradation invisible to MC accuracy (AWQ-4 net preference −17.6 pts) and checking position bias on a random 20% reversed subset.

## Methodology

The authors evaluate post-training weight-only quantisation on BAVGROUND (John et al., 2026), a multiple-choice benchmark of Bavarian cultural knowledge whose 206 source questions each exist in aligned Bavarian, Standard German, and English versions (618 instances total; 240 GEN broadly-accessible + 378 GRD built from regional newspapers, historical references, and anthropological sources). Because all three language versions share source items, cross-language comparisons are paired rather than across independent benchmarks.

The model panel comprises 14 open-weight 7B–10B instruction models spanning four linguistic profiles. Each model is quantised with GPTQ-8, GPTQ-4, and AWQ-4 (group size 128; embeddings and output head kept at original precision), calibrated on ~250k tokens balanced across the three languages using translated instruction data (Alpaca, Dolly, OpenHermes), giving 56 model–precision configurations. Every quantised model is compared to its own BF16 checkpoint.

Primary scoring is multiple-choice accuracy via the option text's mean token-level conditional log-likelihood (length-normalised, avoiding A–D letter priors); answer-letter likelihood and greedy decoding serve only as diagnostics. The key statistics are the BF16-relative change Δ(m,q,ℓ) and the **dialect-gap amplification** A = Δ_BAR − Δ_comparator, which measures whether quantisation widens the pre-existing Bavarian–German/English gap. Inference uses 5,000 bootstrap replicates (resampling source questions; resampling models+questions for gap amplification) with a ±2 percentage-point practical-equivalence region (90% CI, sensitivity-checked at ±1/±3). Calibration sensitivity is tested on four models by re-running GPTQ-4/AWQ-4 under balanced, English-only, German-only, and Bavarian-only calibration (seed 42), plus a three-seed calibration-source comparison (instruction vs version-pinned Wikimedia text) and a supplementary Qwen-family analysis across Qwen2/2.5/3 generations. Free-form responses are compared to BF16 by a blinded pairwise LLM judge (gpt-5.6-luna) across five representative models, allowing ties, with 20% of judgements repeated in reversed order. Peak GPU memory and batch-size-1 throughput are measured on a single NVIDIA GH200.

## Results & Highlights

- **8-bit is essentially lossless; 4-bit costs ~1.5 points on average**: on BAVGROUND MC accuracy, GPTQ-8's mean change vs BF16 is **+0.03 pts** (95% CI includes zero), while **GPTQ-4 loses 1.4 pts and AWQ-4 1.5 pts** on average — but per-model effects swing wildly: GPTQ-4 ranges from **+2.3 pts for Qwen2.5-7B (63.1 → 65.4)** to **−6.6 pts for GENBA-10B (57.6 → 51.0)**, and AWQ-4 from **+3.2 pts for Gemma-2-9B (55.2 → 58.4)** to **−5.7 pts for Granite-3.0-8B (62.8 → 57.1)**.
- **Linguistic specialisation does not protect against compression**: the Bavarian-adapted GENBA-10B is among the worst GPTQ-4 casualties (−6.6 overall, with −3.4 BAR / −5.3 DE / **−11.2 EN**), while generic Llama-3.1-8B gains +0.5 under GPTQ-4; Bavarian accuracy drops in 10 of 14 models under GPTQ-4 and 8 of 14 under AWQ-4, versus only 5 under GPTQ-8.
- **The dialect is not consistently more fragile than the standard language**: Bavarian starts lower in BF16 (56.9% vs German 60.7% and English 62.4%) yet macro-averaged dialect-gap amplification is only **−1.4 pts (BAR–DE) for GPTQ-4 and +1.0 for AWQ-4** (neither meets the ±2-pt equivalence criterion, but they point in opposite directions); under GPTQ-4 Bavarian degrades more than German for 9 models and less for 4. Similarly, the harder GRD subset (BF16 57.9% vs GEN 63.4%) shows nearly identical 4-bit losses to GEN — lower baseline does not predict greater fragility.
- **Calibration data reshape cross-language effects and can flip their direction**: in the four-model ablation, GPTQ-4 with German-only calibration gives Δ_BAR = −3.52 and a BAR–DE contrast of **−3.16 pts**, while Bavarian-only calibration nearly erases it (**+0.12 pts**, Δ_BAR = −0.36); AWQ-4's BAR–DE contrast instead stays positive (+1.82 to +4.25) under all four regimes, and German-only calibration makes GPTQ-4 and AWQ-4 produce opposite BAR–DE directions for 3 of 4 models. Swapping instruction data for Wikimedia text shifts AWQ-4's BAR–DE by **−4.63 pts** while leaving GPTQ-4 stable.
- **Small average losses hide large item-level instability**: GPTQ-8 flips only **1.18%** of matched BF16 predictions vs **11.93% (GPTQ-4)** and **16.00% (AWQ-4)**; in the lowest BF16-margin decile the 4-bit methods change **39.95% / 43.42%** of predictions (GPTQ-8: 10.16%), but only 1.62% / 1.27% in the highest decile — pre-quantisation decision margin is a far stronger predictor of flips than language or prompt length.
- **Free-form evaluation exposes losses MC accuracy misses**: across five models and all 618 instances (n ≈ 3,047–3,049 judgements), GPTQ-8 ties BF16 in 76.1% of cases (net preference only −2.3), GPTQ-4 drops to a **net −6.4**, and AWQ-4 to a **net −17.6** (BF16 preferred in 45.7% of comparisons) — despite GPTQ-4 and AWQ-4 having nearly identical MC losses; Qwen2.5-7B even *gains* +2.3 MC points under GPTQ-4 yet still loses the free-form preference.
- **Memory savings can be large without MC loss, but the quality metric matters**: 11 quantised configurations cut peak reserved GPU memory by ≥50% while losing ≤1 MC point; Mistral-7B GPTQ-4 is the standout (19.2 GB → 7.9 GB, −58.9%, MC 66.8 → 67.2) yet still shows a −3.9 net free-form preference (−5.1 in Bavarian). GPTQ-4 matches or beats GPTQ-8 on MC for 5 of 14 models.

## Authors & Credibility

The team combines regional-supercomputing and dialect-NLP expertise: Jophin John and Michael Hoffmann are at the Leibniz Supercomputing Centre (LRZ, Garching), Piroska Lendvai at the Bavarian Academy of Sciences and Humanities, and Florent Dufour at TUM University Hospital / Technical University of Munich. They are also the authors of both the BAVGROUND benchmark (John et al., 2026) and the Bavarian-adapted GENBA-10B model (Hoffmann et al., 2025) used here — deep domain familiarity, though it means the evaluation infrastructure is self-built (mitigated by always comparing each quantised model to its own BF16 baseline rather than across benchmarks). The methodology is unusually careful for an empirical PTQ study: 5,000-replicate clustered bootstrap with a pre-declared ±2-pt practical-equivalence region (sensitivity-checked at ±1/±3), position-bias audits for the LLM judge, calibration-data overlap checks against benchmark items, version-pinned calibration corpora, and multi-seed replication for the calibration-source experiment. Quantisation was run on a single NVIDIA GH200 with a disclosed hardware/software stack, and an AI-assistance usage statement is included. The paper transparently reports per-model heterogeneity rather than only aggregates.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: solid rather than groundbreaking. The core question extends Marchisio et al. (2024) and calibration work (Zeng et al., 2024; Chimoto et al., 2026) from cross-language to a dialect–standard pair with aligned content; the genuinely new bits are the aligned regional-variety setting and the margin-based explanation of instability. The headline negative result ("Bavarian is not consistently more fragile") is a useful corrective to intuitive claims that low-resource varieties suffer most under compression.
- **Rigor**: strong on statistics for the main panel, but the two most interesting findings sit on thinner ground. The calibration-language ablation uses only **four models and a single seed (42) per condition** — the paper itself flags this — so the headline −3.16 vs +0.12 BAR–DE swing rests on a narrow base, and the calibration-source claim for AWQ-4 (−4.63 shift) is from a three-seed, four-model comparison that also required excluding an invalid Gemma 2 AWQ-4 Wikimedia run (and a 3.12-pt AWQ-4 loss in the broader panel).
- **Reproducibility**: good — named checkpoints (Appendix C), fixed seeds, version-pinned corpora, released-style detailed appendix tables, and public open-weight models. The LLM judge is named (gpt-5.6-luna), though that model's availability to others may limit exact replication of the free-form study.
- **Significance**: the practical takeaways are real (8-bit is safe; 4-bit requires per-model, per-language, per-output-mode validation; MC accuracy is an unreliable proxy), but generalisation is limited: one dialect, one benchmark of 206 items, one domain family (Bavarian cultural knowledge).
- **Red flags / weaknesses to weigh**: (1) the benchmark is small — 206 source questions means per-language contrasts ride on modest effective sample sizes, and GEN/GRD subset analyses (240 vs 378 instances) are even smaller; (2) the free-form conclusion relies on a **single LLM judge with no human validation** of Bavarian naturalness — the authors concede this is "complementary evidence, not human validation"; 112 of 9,270 planned comparisons were dropped for truncation and 14 for malformed IDs; (3) self-evaluation circularity — the authors evaluate their own benchmark and their own Bavarian model (GENBA-10B), though the within-model BF16-relative design limits the damage; (4) method coverage is narrow: only weight-only GPTQ/AWQ — no SmoothQuant/activation quantisation, FP8, or QAT comparison, so "quantisation robustness" here means only two PTQ families at group size 128; (5) despite the framing, the paper cannot say whether effects hold for dialects less closely related to their standard language than Bavarian is to German (Latin script, shared vocabulary, likely shared tokenisation coverage).

## Strengths

- The aligned three-way design (same 206 questions in BAR/DE/EN) is exactly the right control for isolating language-variety effects from content effects — a cleaner instrument than any prior multilingual quantisation study.
- Exceptionally honest reporting: per-model tables showing gains next to losses, pre-registered equivalence margins, sensitivity analyses at ±1/±2/±3 pts, and explicit reporting of excluded/invalid runs.
- The margin-decile instability analysis gives a mechanistic, actionable insight: quantisation mostly perturbs decisions the model was already unsure about, which explains why aggregate deltas are small.
- The MC-vs-free-form mismatch (AWQ-4: near-equal MC loss to GPTQ-4 but −17.6 net free-form preference) is a genuinely useful deployment warning that fixed-choice benchmarks under-detect generation degradation.
- Practical deployment guidance backed by measurements: concrete memory numbers (Mistral GPTQ-4: 19.2 → 7.9 GB, −58.9%) and a quality–memory frontier figure on real GH200 hardware.

## Limitations

- Single dialect and single benchmark: findings may not transfer to other regional varieties, languages with different scripts, or non-cultural-knowledge tasks; Bavarian's internal regional/orthographic variation is not modelled.
- The calibration-language ablation covers only 4 models with 1 seed; the calibration-source comparison uses 3 seeds on 4 models — broader replication is needed before treating the calibration effects as established.
- Free-form evaluation depends on one proprietary LLM judge; no human ratings of Bavarian response quality or dialect appropriateness.
- Only two weight-only PTQ methods (GPTQ, AWQ) at group size 128; activation quantisation, FP8, QAT, and other methods (e.g., SmoothQuant) are cited but not compared, so conclusions about "quantisation" are about this slice of the space.
- Memory/throughput numbers are specific to a single GH200 with batch-size-1 inference and a particular Transformers stack, and models are limited to 7B–10B — behaviour at larger or smaller scales is untested (the Qwen-family supplement only partially addresses this).

## Relevance

该论文对关注多语言/低资源 LLM 评测与高效部署方向的研究有直接参考价值：它给出了"量化是否对弱势语言变体不公平"这一问题的严谨实证答案，并示范了内容对齐基准 + 逐题决策分析 + 自由生成盲评的组合评测方法，可为其他方言/低资源语言的量化部署评估提供模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the calibration-language effects (German-only calibration hurting Bavarian by −3.16 BAR–DE under GPTQ-4) replicate across more models and seeds, and is there a cheap proxy (e.g., tokeniser coverage or calibration-set perplexity per language) that predicts which calibration mix protects a given variety?
- Is the margin-concentration of quantisation-induced flips specific to multiple-choice scoring, or does 4-bit quantisation similarly perturb low-confidence regions of free-form generation (e.g., measured via token-level entropy)?
- Would activation quantisation (e.g., SmoothQuant) or FP8 show the same dialect–standard asymmetries as weight-only GPTQ/AWQ, given that activation outliers are known to be language-dependent?
- How do these effects scale beyond 7B–10B — does the 4-bit instability and calibration sensitivity shrink for 30B–70B models as aggregate-robustness results in the literature suggest?
- Can the AWQ-4 free-form degradation (net −17.6 vs GPTQ-4's −6.4 despite near-equal MC loss) be traced to a specific mechanism, such as AWQ's activation-based channel rescaling interacting with morphologically rich dialect tokens?
