The Accuracy Well: Nonmonotone Accuracy Dynamics at Vanishing Initialization

training-dynamics learning-theory small-initialization deep-linear-networks optimization
Key metric
−65pts
0.859 → 0.209 accuracy

Whitened MNIST: the degeneracy-bottleneck classifier (top singular block of the target) vs the full target — theory predicts this mid-training collapse.

Why this matters

Loss can fall monotonically while accuracy collapses then recovers — a health-monitoring and early-stopping hazard for small-init / μP-style training runs.

Critical edge

Theorems cover only linear networks, squared loss, isotropic inputs — the MNIST/CIFAR wells remain empirically characterized, not proven.

Methodology

Three instruments, one story

Controlled phenomenology + mechanism diagnostics + asymptotic theory.

Empirics. Deep linear factorizations M = W_L···W_1; one-hidden-layer ReLU nets on MNIST (widths 32–8192, σ down to 1e-4, SGD lr 0.005, batch 1024, 10k updates, matched random streams); a 2-hidden-layer CNN on CIFAR-10; synthetic unary tasks f : [512] → [512] (random maps vs permutations, full-batch 15k updates). Sweeps cover init scale, width, depth, bias placement, loss, optimizer, and 5 seeds.

Theory. Squared loss with isotropic inputs reduces to fitting an effective target T in Frobenius distance. In the small-σ limit, the L=2 predictor linearizes to M(τ) = Σ_j sinh(2 s_j τ) p_j q_jᵀ: the target-driven term beats finite-width noise early, then sinh growth concentrates on the top singular block. L≥3 results invoke rank-one saddle escape (Jacot et al., 2021).

  • The well needs factorization, not just small init. L=2/L=3 linear nets develop wells; the directly optimized L=1 model stays monotone across σ = 1e-1…1e-9, drop ≤ 1 − A(T) (Thm 4.1).
  • Robust across 5/5 seeds (init + minibatch streams varied), cross-entropy, and SGD / momentum / Adam (drop shallower under Adam, at lr 1e-5).
  • Architecture surgery: a hidden bias keeps the well; a trainable output bias removes it. Wider CNNs (128,256 ch.) give an earlier, deeper well; CIFAR-10 test accuracy tracks train.
  • Early beats late: an early microscopic predictor can classify strictly better than a later one with smaller loss.
Strengths

Why it holds up

  • Rare triangulation: clean experiments, interpretable diagnostics (score-matrix snapshots, participation ratios), and genuine asymptotic theorems in one coherent story.
  • Controls isolate the cause — L=1 monotonicity, permutation flat-spectrum monotonicity, output-bias suppression — not just the phenomenon.
  • Breadth tested systematically: init scale, width, depth, bias placement, loss family, optimizer, seeds, train vs test.
  • The L=2 sinh(2 sjτ) mode-growth mechanism is explicit and checkable.
  • Honest scoping; limitations stated plainly.
Limitations

Where it stops short

  • Rigorous results: linear networks, squared loss, gradient flow, isotropic inputs only — nonlinear wells are empirical, not proven.
  • L≥3 needs an isolated top singular value; probability ≥ 1/2 only for L > 3; recovery proven only under spectrally aligned dynamics; deep-permutation well unexplained.
  • All experiments toy-scale (MNIST, CIFAR-10, p=512); wells need σ ≲ 1e-4 — no evidence at modern pretraining scale.
  • Thin practitioner guidance: no recipe for detecting or avoiding harmful wells.
  • No public code or data release.
Questions for further exploration

Open threads

  • Do wells occur at realistic pretraining scales and standard inits (μP-scaled layers), or only at σ ≲ 1e-4?
  • Can the degeneracy bottleneck be linked to grokking, feature-learning transitions, or silent alignment — and predict the best early-stopping checkpoints?
  • Are participation-ratio dips a measurable early-warning signal for training monitors?
  • Does output-bias suppression generalize to LayerNorm affine params / per-class heads as a design lever against wells?
  • Can L≥3 recovery be proven for generic Gaussian initialization, closing the authors' open gap?
