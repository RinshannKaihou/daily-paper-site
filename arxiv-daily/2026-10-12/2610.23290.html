CoTA++: Understanding the Repeat Curse in dMLLMs from an Information Flow Perspective ↗
Qiyan Zhao, Xiaofeng Zhang, Xu-Yao Zhang, Shijie Han, et al. · NLPR CAS · SJTU · HKU · UCAS · diffusion LM KV cache inference acceleration interpretability
Key metric · sample repetition rate
99.6%→8.2%

LLaDA-V + dLLM-Cache, 512-token detailed captions (500 MS-COCO images). Longest repeat run: 455 → 3 tokens; vanilla, uncached: 2.4%.

Why this matters

A textbook case of optimization breaking correctness: the default dMLLM speedup silently corrupts nearly all long outputs; interpretability-driven fixes repair it cheaply.

优化引入正确性问题的典型案例——用可解释性分析直接指导推理系统的修复设计。

⚠ Critical edge

Intervention sites are hard-coded layer ranges tied to 32-layer towers (CTAR band D={25..32}, CTEV window W={26..30}) with no automatic band-selection procedure — and two of the three "models" share the same language tower.

Methodology 方法 — one remedy per disruption

A diffusion multimodal LLM decodes a fully masked suffix by iteratively committing the top-k most confident tokens per step. Bidirectional attention invalidates exact KV caching, so cache backends recompute only ~25% of suffix positions per step, chosen by feature similarity — the proximate cause of the curse. Information-flow tracing along three axes finds three compounding disruptions:

F1Within-step routing — anchoring dispersed

Cached queries lose their local anchors on ±5 context tokens; repeat regions lose 2.8–7.6× more anchoring than distant ones.

→
CTARContext-Token Attention Re-anchoring

Re-forms attention routing against the cache's current keys in deep layers 25–32, only where a monitor flags new nearby commits. 1.06×

F2Cross-step staleness — refresh is blind

Similarity-based refresh favors already-committed positions; repeat spots decode on median 4-step-stale KV (normal: 1), repetition doubling across the refresh cycle.

→
DARDecode-Aware Refresh

Reserves r=4 refresh slots for the top-confidence masked positions about to commit, guaranteeing staleness Δ=0 at commit — redirects budget, adds none. 1.01×

F3Cross-layer consolidation — entropy never settles

79% of repeat positions (vs 36% normal) fail to halve their plateau entropy by layer 31; the top entropy fifth holds 54% of all repeats.

→
CTEVContext-Token Entropy-Guided Voting

Discounts commit confidence by neighbors' deep-window entropy (λ=0.25), reordering — never forbidding — unmasking. 1.07×

The three disruptions are non-redundant: tokens hit by all three repeat at 55% vs 2.4% with none; staleness explains 81% of shallow-band anchoring loss but only 5% in the deep band. Components share no state and compose with any dMLLM / cache backend.

Strengths 优势

What holds up
  • Clean diagnosis-to-remedy mapping — each component intervenes exactly where its disruption arises, and per-component analyses verify the intervention moved its own quantity.
  • Training-free and plug-and-play — DAR is essentially free (1.01×) because it redirects existing refresh budget; composes with both backends and all three models.
  • Actionable failure characterization — single runs consume 80–91% of the token budget; function words open 54% of runs; repetition tracks the refresh cycle (4.9%→9.7%).
  • Honest reporting — worst cases shown (17.0% residual), conference version's weaknesses quantified, negative results for cheaper design alternatives published.

Limitations 局限

What to watch
  • Only 8B-class models and two cache backends (Fast-dLLM, dkv-cache positioned but untested); authors cite compute limits.
  • Hard-coded layer bands D={25..32} / W={26..30}; no automatic selection procedure for other depths — and LLaDA-V and LaViDa share one language tower.
  • Headline repeat statistics rest on one captioning task, 500 images, a single instruction; quality judged by a single LLM judge, no human raters.
  • Recovery is partial on MathVista (1.3 of 4.5 pts) and LaViDa's LLaVA-Bench; budgets κ/r/λ fixed per run; CTEV's vocabulary-sized projections dominate its cost.

Questions for further exploration 待探索

  1. Do F1–F3 and CoTA++ transfer to cache-free repetition under aggressive step reduction — one curse, two triggers?
  2. Can the CTAR band / CTEV window be auto-selected from layer-wise entropy profiles instead of hard-coded layers, and hold on deeper or text-only dLLMs?
  3. Would an adaptive risk-score budget (merging monitor count, confidence rank, context entropy) beat the fixed κ/r/λ allocation?
  4. Can anchor-aware refresh move the fix into the cache backend itself — and beat the 1.15× wrapper overhead?
  5. Would training-time use of the CTEV signal teach dMLLMs to be cache-robust, eliminating the curse at the source?

Credibility 可信度

  • TPAMI extension of the authors' ICLR'26 "Context Tokens are Anchors" — adds the F2 axis, DAR, and a 3-model × 2-backend × 9-benchmark study.
  • Unusually careful statistics for a systems paper: Wilcoxon signed-rank throughout (p to 3.4e-15), 95% intervals, controlled comparisons.
  • Code + exact 500-image COCO list public: github.com/ErikZ719/CoTA; all hyperparameters stated.
