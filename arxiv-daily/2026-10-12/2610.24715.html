Critical edge

Both DRY and DRY-C crush requested verbatim copying — exact completion falls from >90% to ~10%, a real cost on legitimately repeated short-line blocks.

Methodology · exit, not onset

From one forward pass to a provable fix

  1. Loop taken as given. Study when a started verbatim loop still has an exit; context along a loop is deterministic, so survival under any sampler is computable exactly from one teacher-forced forward pass (Prop 1).
  2. Exact lock criteria (Thm 1). Pure sampling never locks; top-p (π>½) locks iff q ≥ π; analogues for min-p and greedy. Temperature before top-p (transformers default) at τ=0.7 lowers the bar 0.9 → 0.823.
  3. Bounded vs growing penalties (Thm 2). Bounded penalties merely shift the threshold (σ(logit π + D)); growth beyond ln(q/r°) guarantees escape. Presence / windowed-frequency = bounded; DRY = exponential only without breakers.
  4. DRY's cap (Prop 4). Breakers (newline, colon, quote, asterisk) cap the penalty at λbg−1−a — D₇=7.5 vs D₈=13.1 against measured reinforcement 6–20: a lock/unlock phase change between g=7 and g=8.
  5. DRY-C. Adds a count term dcnt=λc·bcc(v)−c₀ (a′=24, c₀=2, bc=3) over verbatim recurrences, counted across breakers — provably exponential along every loop up to the look-back window, logarithmic unlock time.
  6. Measure & test. 150 EN + 150 ZH sentences + 22 structured units × ≤200 repeats → exact 64-bit q, a, r° per position; reimplementation validated position-by-position against llama.cpp (03aa006); 11 open models, 1.5B–35B.

Strengths

  • Exactly checkable theory: lock status computable from one forward pass; criteria matched exact computation at every one of ~160k positions × 4 models, plus a Monte-Carlo cross-check (0.789 ± 0.013 vs 0.786).
  • Falsifiable prediction about a deployed system, confirmed three independent ways with a semicolon control and retokenization-aware regrouping.
  • Minimal, safe fix: default-off llama.cpp patch, bit-identical when disabled; logarithmic unlock time; completion ≈ DRY while cutting duplication.
  • Exceptional self-audit: copying cost, near-loop displacement, and out-of-loop count activations all reported, not hidden.

Limitations

  • Verbatim loops only: some eliminated loops become drifting near-repeats — the sampler is verbatim-proofed, not the model.
  • Sampler coverage: criteria exist for top-p / min-p / greedy only; Mirostat and XTC are out of scope.
  • Windows bound everything: DRY-C's exponential growth stops at the look-back window (units ≤ 814 tokens at W=4096, Δ=20) — easy to over-read.
  • Small free-generation scope: three quantized models, 3,000-token budget, automatic metrics only, no human evaluation; the 35B datapoint is thin.
  • Copying cost & untuned parameters: count-term parameters (a′, c₀, bc) chosen from format considerations, not optimized.

Questions for further exploration

  • Do exclusion-based samplers (XTC) or Mirostat lock differently than truncation-based ones?
  • Does the blind spot vanish at frontier scale — do larger models' reinforcement curves saturate below D₇ = 7.5?
  • Can the count term activate only after a detected loop (≥5 repeats), removing the 37.9% out-of-loop activation rate and the copying cost?
  • Should samplers warn when q crosses the effective threshold (0.823 with τ=0.7 before top-p 0.9)?
  • Is the saturating reinforcement curve derivable from induction-head mechanics?
  • Does grammar-constrained decoding (XGrammar, outliners) renormalize its way into new lock blind spots?
