01Methodology — how forgetting is measured and bounded

Teacher-forcing under a shared continuation isolates the prompt's fingerprint from everything else.

Two prompts are followed by the same human-written continuation (WikiText-103), so the hidden-state difference — the prompt offset Δ — is attributable to the prompt alone. The part shared across continuations is the signal S(t); an unbiased pairwise estimator separates it from noise (β≈0, so one exponent α summarizes forgetting). Exponents come from log-log slopes, centre-labelled octave windows, 95% bootstrap intervals.

  • Prompt sets: 20 hand-written scenes × 5 variants (word swap, paraphrase, genre, translation) + 100 real-corpus pairs per axis (WikiText / MRPC / WMT14).
  • Theory covers pre-LN softmax decoders, rotary or no positional encoding, GQA; a dilution lemma unrolls a one-layer recursion into Theorem 1.
  • An exactly solvable linear relay model proves the (log t)L−1 factor is tight, not a proof artefact.
  • Causal test: add γ·log((q+1)/P) to prompt attention logits — uniform or per-head-calibrated, with a placebo control.
  • Everything runs in float32 on one Apple-silicon laptop; toolkit promised for release.

  • Content-agnostic rate: unrelated sentences are forgotten at the same rate as a one-word swap (α 0.70 vs 0.69, trained L11); only the amplitude tracks prompt difference.
  • Forgetting precedes attention sinks: α jumps 0.41→0.68 between training steps 128–512, while sink mass stays ≤0.003 until step 1000.
  • Relay explains the tail: the exact direct/relay split reproduces measured α within 0.06 at every layer and checkpoint; attention weights alone mislead.
  • The solvable model fits untrained Pythia almost exactly: layer-1 fit recovers prompt length P̂=28.8 vs 25.7 true (R²=0.9999).
  • What survives rides the generated text: prompt language is decodable from output at 0.84 accuracy while detail/genre fall to chance — the no-hair analogy. Side effect: instruction echoing rises 19%→56–69% (1.5B).

+ Strengths

  • One quantitative law unifies everything — theory, measurement, training dynamics, and causal intervention all speak the same exponent α (β≈0).
  • Theory–experiment loop closes tightly: the exactly solvable relay model proves the logarithmic factors are real and fits fifteen Pythia-1b curves at R² ≥ 0.992.
  • Causality, not correlation: dilution compensation (with per-head calibration and a placebo) establishes necessity; untrained networks supply sufficiency.
  • Hygiene above the interpretability norm: unbiased late-time estimator, quantified window-labelling bias, sink-controlled pairs, three excluded alternative explanations, bootstrap CIs on essentially every number.
  • Useful negative results stated without hedging: attention weights are an unreliable proxy; the log t compensation shape beats no constant boost.

– Limitations

  • The theorem constrains only asymptotic shape: its constant is exponentially large in depth and, with trained logit spreads (eΔ > 107), bites beyond any measured window — it does not predict the fitted exponents.
  • Assumption 1 is empirical, not derived, with a 0.012 margin in Qwen's first block; ALiBi, sliding-window, Scalable-Softmax and non-softmax attention are out of scope.
  • Scale stops at 1.5B, pretrained-only; English prompts, WikiText continuations, one temperature — no instruction-tuned, RLHF'd, or frontier-scale confirmation.
  • Corpus-dependence: shuffled continuations shift deep-layer α by up to 0.18; trained Qwen shows an unexplained late-time oscillation around the 1/t law.
  • Small behavioural samples (32 conversations/condition — trends, not significance) and a severe instruction-echo side effect (19%→56–69%).
  • Not yet reproducible: toolkit and prompt pairs unreleased at v1; single-author preprint without peer review.

03Questions for further exploration

  • Q1Do trained deep-layer exponents (α≈0.55–0.7) persist at 7B–70B scale, and does the depth-flat profile survive much deeper or GQA/MLA architectures?
  • Q2Does instruction tuning or RLHF change relay suppression — do dialogue-trained models forget system prompts at a measurably different α?
  • Q3The analysis predicts Scalable-Softmax retains prompt information longer — can this be measured directly, and at what perplexity cost?
  • Q4Can trained Qwen's layer-1 oscillation around 1/t be tied quantitatively to rotary frequency bases, and does it appear with context extension/interpolation?
  • Q5Is there a training objective or architectural change that preserves relay without the instruction-echo pathology and perplexity cost?
  • Q6Does the no-hair picture extend to KV-cache compression — can early prompt tokens' caches be evicted or quantized more aggressively than t−1 implies?
