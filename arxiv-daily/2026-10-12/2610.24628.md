> **一句话总结：** Meta 开源的 30B 稠密多模态 Agent 模型 Muse Glimmer（蒸馏自前沿模型 Muse Spark，Apache 2.0 发布权重 + 4-bit 量化版 + DFlash 投投机解码头），专为 32GB 显存的消费级本地 Agent 场景设计：在 GAIA2 (43.3 vs 40.0)、WildClawBench (47.6 vs 43.2)、MCPAtlas (75.5 vs 62.5)、SWE-Bench Pro (51.2 vs 50.2) 等 Agent/工具调用基准上领先同量级的 Qwen3.6-27B 和 Gemma4-31B，但在 OSWorld-Verified (65.9 vs 75.6) 和 TerminalBench 2.1 (51.7 vs 60.7) 上明显落后于 Qwen3.6-27B，多语言 Agent 能力（OmnilingualGAIA2 目标语言均值 60.4 vs 45.0）是其最突出的差异化优势。

> **TL;DR:** Meta releases Muse Glimmer, a ~29.6B dense text+image causal LM distilled from its frontier teacher Muse Spark and purpose-built for autonomous agents on consumer hardware (32 GB VRAM, ~4-bit quantized, DFlash speculative decoding, Apache 2.0). Against its two chosen size-class rivals it wins most agentic/tool-use benchmarks — GAIA2 43.3 vs 40.0 for Qwen3.6-27B (+3.3) and 36.4 for Gemma4-31B, WildClawBench 47.6 vs 43.2/37.6, MCPAtlas 75.5 vs 62.5/54.2, SWE-Bench Pro 51.2 vs 50.2/36.9, and multilingual agentic OmnilingualGAIA2 60.4 vs 45.0/37.5 — but trails Qwen3.6-27B on OSWorld-Verified (65.9 vs 75.6), TerminalBench 2.1 (51.7 vs 60.7), and GDPval-AA (953 vs 1141), and trails Gemma4-31B on GPQA Diamond (83.5 vs 85.7), HLE, and machine translation, so "SOTA for its size class" holds on the agentic/multilingual slice rather than uniformly. The quantization study is unusually thorough (EvoPress 4-bit: 75.6% average accuracy over 15 tasks vs 75.7% BF16), but no concrete on-device latency/throughput numbers are reported despite that being the headline use case.

## 核心贡献

- **发布 Muse Glimmer 30B**：一个 ~29.6B 参数的稠密因果 Transformer（+ ~1.8B 冻结的 Perception Encoder ViT-G/14 视觉编码器），支持文本+图像输入、128K+ 上下文，在 Agent、工具调用与多步推理基准上与 Gemma4-31B 和 Qwen3.6-27B 竞争并以 Apache 2.0 协议开源（含全精度权重、4-bit 量化权重和 DFlash 投机解码草稿头）。
- **验证了"前沿教师定向蒸馏"路线**：从 Meta 前沿模型 Muse Spark 进行 logit 蒸馏（co-distillation，记录 top-256 logits；前 95% 仅用 KL 损失，最后 5% 切换为纯交叉熵以修正 top-k 截断偏差），再用 SFT → 在线策略蒸馏（on-policy distillation）→ 多目标 RL 的后训练流水线对齐为 Agent。
- **在固定 32GB 显存约束下的架构Scaling 实验**：用逐步放大的架构阶梯（dense vs 1:8 / 1:16 sparse MoE）在固定内存足迹下拟合 bits-per-byte 幂律曲线，发现稠密架构的算力效率是 MoE 的 2×–2.5×，据此选择 dense 30B 而非 MoE。
- **端侧推理优化组合**：保守的混合精度 4-bit 量化（EvoPress 配置 19.65GB，15 个下游任务平均精度 75.6% vs BF16 的 75.7%，且思维链长度无显著变化）+ DFlash 块扩散草稿模型做投机解码，使模型可部署在单张 RTX 5090 / 24–32GB 设备。
- **面向本地 Agent 的可信行为训练**：基于语境完整性（Contextual Integrity）理论合成隐私轨迹训练"恰当信息流"（CIMemories 违规率 26.4 vs Qwen3.6-27B 的 53.4，但高于 Gemma4-31B 的 12.1），并通过拒采样合成数据 + RL 惩罚抵抗间接提示注入（Siren AgentDojo 攻击成功率 28.4 vs Qwen 的 40.3，效用 94.2 三者最高）。
- **可配置推理强度**：low/medium/high/xhigh 四档推理深度通过系统提示中的单条指令控制（RL 阶段用分组长度惩罚 + 路由方案训练），无需重训即可在延迟与推理质量间权衡。

## Key Contributions

- **Open-weights on-device agentic model**: a ~29.6B dense causal transformer plus a frozen ~1.8B Perception Encoder (ViT-G/14, NaViT-style native-resolution processing) accepting interleaved text+image input with 128K+ context, released under Apache 2.0 together with 4-bit quantized weights and a DFlash speculative-decoding head.
- **Frontier-teacher distillation recipe**: pre-training via logit co-distillation from Muse Spark (top-256 logged logits; KL-only for the first 95% of training, cross-entropy-only for the final 5% to correct top-k truncation bias), checkpoint averaging ("souping": last checkpoint averaged with three prior checkpoints spaced 600 steps apart), two mid-training phases (domain infusion, then agentic data + context extension 32K→128K), and post-training of SFT → on-policy distillation → multi-objective RL.
- **Fixed-memory dense-vs-MoE scaling study**: architecture ladders trained under a constant 32 GB memory ceiling show dense configurations deliver 2×–2.5× better compute efficiency than 1:8/1:16 sparse MoE at the same budget, motivating the dense 30B design.
- **Conservative mixed-precision quantization pipeline**: starting from llama.cpp's Q4_K_M allocation and extending to GPTQ/AWQ/NVFP4/MXFP8 plus a new Greedy-KL search and an adapted EvoPress evolutionary search over 418 weight units; the chosen EvoPress profile (19.65 GB) loses only 0.1 points of average accuracy over 15 downstream tasks (75.6% vs 75.7% BF16) with no chain-of-thought length inflation.
- **Trustworthy local agent training**: contextual-integrity-based information-flow training (synthetic personal-assistant "universes" with 10–14 contacts per persona), adversarial-robustness traces against indirect prompt injection, and a principal hierarchy for conflicting instructions.
- **Configurable reasoning strength**: four deployment-time levels (low/medium/high/xhigh) trained via per-subgroup length penalties in RL plus a routing scheme, controlled by a single system-prompt directive.

## Methodology

**Architecture.** 52 layers, hidden dimension 6656, untied input/output embeddings over a 202,048-token vocabulary (Llama 4 BPE tokenizer). Attention is GQA with 32 query / 2 KV heads (16:1) at head dimension 128, with a 2048-token sliding window in a repeating [Local, Local, Local, Global] pattern to bound KV-cache growth; RoPE (θ = 500,000) is applied to local layers only. FFNs are SwiGLU with intermediate dimension 19,968. Images enter through a frozen ~1.8B ViT-G/14 Perception Encoder: single aspect-ratio-preserving resize (no dynamic tiling), 2×2 pixel-shuffle downsampling, two-layer MLP adapter, then a bias-free linear projection with RMSNorm; up to 4096 visual tokens per image. A simplified MLA variant tested on global layers underperformed GQA on RULER long-context benchmarks and was dropped.

**Training.** Pre-training by logit distillation from Muse Spark (KL on teacher top-256 logits, CE for the final 5%), followed by checkpoint souping, mid-training Phase 1 (domain infusion with synthetic reasoning traces at reduced LR), and mid-training Phase 2 (context 32K→128K with tool-use/agentic trajectory data under CE loss). Post-training runs SFT → on-policy distillation (the student generates rollouts; the teacher provides per-token distributional targets on the student's visited prefixes — reported to consistently improve downstream RL versus going straight from SFT to RL) → multi-objective RL over reward models, verifiers, and task-success signals. Reasoning strength is trained by splitting per-prompt samples into subgroups with length penalties, with a routing scheme sending a fraction of prompts exclusively to high strengths.

**Synthetic agentic data.** Built on Agents Research Environments principles: personas expanded from demographic + Big Five attributes, materialized into "universes" (mock email/calendar/WhatsApp/code-repo/CLI tool states with weeks of grounded history) in Docker containers with real-web access; scenarios proposed from universe summaries and expanded into runnable tasks with verifiable rubrics. Post-processing applies deterministic quality filtering, masking of undesirable behaviors (bad tool calls), and LLM-judged grounding verification. Privacy (contextual integrity) and safety (prompt-injection resistance, principal hierarchy) traces are generated via prompt-distilled Muse Spark traces with rejection filtering.

**Inference optimization.** BF16 weights occupy 55.7 GB; the target is <20 GB for LM weights. PTQ candidates (Q4KM, GPTQ, AWQ, NVFP4/MXFP8 mixed, uniform variants, Greedy-KL, EvoPress) are compared by ΔNLL on held-out text/code, then on 15 downstream tasks. EvoPress (19.65 GB, lowest ΔNLL) is chosen for 32 GB deployments and Q4KM (smallest footprint) for 24 GB. Speculative decoding uses DFlash: a 5-layer block-diffusion drafter with block size 16 and sliding-window attention, trained offline on target-model rollouts with the D-PACE objective.

## Results & Highlights

All comparisons below are Muse Glimmer (high reasoning) vs Gemma4-31B (thinking) vs Qwen3.6-27B (thinking) from the paper's scorecard (Table 2), screened for contamination.

- **Agentic & tool use is where the model wins**: GAIA2 43.3 vs 36.4 (Gemma4, +6.9) and 40.0 (Qwen, +3.3); WildClawBench 47.6 vs 37.6/43.2 (+10.0/++4.4); τ³-Banking 23.5 vs 15.1/16.7 (+8.4 over Gemma4); MCPAtlas tool-use 75.5 vs 54.2/62.5 (+13.0 over Qwen, +21.3 over Gemma4); DeepSearchQA 74.6 vs 61.7/71.1; SWE-Bench Pro 51.2 vs 36.9 (Gemma4) and 50.2 (Qwen, +1.0); SWE-Bench Verified 76.0 vs 66.6/77.2.
- **But it clearly loses several agentic benchmarks to Qwen3.6-27B**: OSWorld-Verified 65.9 vs 75.6 (−9.7), TerminalBench 2.1 51.7 vs 60.7 (−9.0), GDPval-AA 953 vs 1141 (−188), and to Gemma4-31B on GPQA Diamond (83.5 vs 85.7), HLE (22.0 vs 23.6), and machine translation (WMT24++ X→eng 55.7 vs 57.2 chrF++).
- **Multilingual agentic capability is the standout**: OmnilingualGAIA2 target-language mean 60.4 vs 45.0 (Qwen, +15.4) and 37.5 (Gemma4, +22.9); on Linguini metalinguistic reasoning it solves 8.8 whole problems — more than twice Gemma4-31B's 3.8 and 3.5× Qwen's 2.5 (though per-question accuracy reverses the order: 17.9 vs Gemma4's 18.8). On MMLU-ProX it posts the tightest cross-lingual spread (2.6-point average English-to-language gap vs 3.7 for Gemma4 and 5.2 for Qwen, excluding Wolof) at 80.1 mean pass@1 vs Gemma4's 82.5.
- **Token efficiency**: on MMLU-ProX, Muse Glimmer (high) uses 2,453 tokens/rollout vs 2,671 for Gemma4-31B and 4,532 for Qwen3.6-27B — Qwen spends 1.8× the tokens for no accuracy advantage (80.5 vs 80.1).
- **Quantization barely hurts**: averaged over 15 downstream tasks, accuracy is 75.7% (BF16), 75.6% (EvoPress), 75.3% (Greedy-KL), 74.8% (Q4KM), 74.4% (NVFP4/MXFP8), with average response length essentially unchanged (11,070 tokens BF16 vs 11,417 EvoPress) — i.e., no chain-of-thought inflation from 4-bit quantization.
- **Privacy/safety trade-offs**: CIMemories violation rate 26.4 vs 53.4 for Qwen (better) but 12.1 for Gemma4 (worse), while coverage 64.8 vs 53.0/66.9; Siren AgentDojo ASR@5 28.4 vs 40.3 for Qwen and 25.6 for Gemma4, with the highest utility of all three (94.2). PrivacyLens leakage 49.2 (high mode).
- **Reasoning strength sweep**: AIME 2026 rises from 69.7 (low) to 94.7 (high) vs 89.2 Gemma4/94.1 Qwen; ARC-AGI from 34.6 (low) to 66.9 (high)/67.9 (xhigh); LiveCodeBench Pro 2025Q2 from 36.2 (low) to 64.1 (high); raising high→xhigh moves multilingual results by less than run-to-run noise at 10–27% more tokens, so high is the default.

## Authors & Credibility

This is a large industrial technical report from Meta Superintelligence Labs ("Muse Glimmer team"), with 76 core contributors (including known senior researchers such as Hugo Touvron, Luke Zettlemoyer, Mike Lewis, Rob Fergus) and 200+ additional contributors. The report is detailed on architecture, distillation, quantization, and synthetic-data methodology, includes a full appendix with per-benchmark evaluation protocols (run counts, harnesses, judge models like gpt-oss-120b and Claude Sonnet 4.6), and releases all artifacts (weights, quantized weights, speculation head) under Apache 2.0 on Hugging Face. Credibility is high on the systems-engineering side; the teacher model Muse Spark is proprietary and not released, so the training recipe is only partially reproducible externally.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The individual components (GQA + sliding-window attention, logit distillation, on-policy distillation, contextual-integrity privacy training, block-diffusion speculation) are mostly assembled from known techniques; the novelty lies in the integration and the fixed-32GB-memory dense-vs-MoE ladder study, plus the unusually complete open artifact set. It is a strong engineering report rather than a methods breakthrough.
- **Rigor — selective "SOTA" framing**: The abstract claims state-of-the-art for its size class, but the scorecard shows clear losses to Qwen3.6-27B on OSWorld-Verified (65.9 vs 75.6), TerminalBench 2.1 (51.7 vs 60.7), and GDPval-AA (953 vs 1141), and to Qwen3.6-27B on SkillsBench (44.3 vs 46.6) and MMMU-Pro (74 vs 75), and to Gemma4-31B on GPQA Diamond (83.5 vs 85.7), HLE (22.0 vs 23.6), and all translation benchmarks. Only two competitors are compared; several baseline numbers are self-reported (AIME 2026 for both rivals, SWE-Bench Verified for Qwen), and the OSWorld comparison confounds models with different computer-use action spaces (Claude computer-use for Glimmer/Qwen vs the Gemini 2.5 Flash interface for Gemma4), which the paper itself flags but does not resolve.
- **Missing on-device measurements**: The central claim — practical agentic speeds on consumer hardware — is never quantified: no tokens/sec, latency, memory-resident measurements, or DFlash speedup factor appear anywhere in the report (only "runs at practical speeds on a single RTX 5090 with minimal quality loss"). For an on-device paper this is a notable omission.
- **Missing ablations**: the claim that on-policy distillation "consistently improves downstream RL outcomes" is given without numbers; the souping, reasoning-strength routing, and contextual-integrity data recipes likewise lack controlled ablations.
- **Privacy results cut both ways**: the paper trains specifically for contextual integrity, yet CIMemories violations (26.4) are more than double Gemma4-31B's (12.1), and its English→target-language drop on OmnilingualGAIA2 (−13.3) is steeper than Gemma4's (−5.7); the Wolof exclusion in the multilingual-spread calculation is a small but convenient data quirk.
- **Reproducibility**: weights are open, but training depends on the proprietary Muse Spark teacher, internal synthetic pipelines, and unquantified GPU budgets, so only deployment/fine-tuning — not training — is reproducible.

## Strengths

- Genuinely useful open release: full-precision weights, 4-bit quantized variants for both 32 GB and 24 GB targets, and the DFlash speculation head under Apache 2.0 — few labs ship the drafter too.
- The quantization chapter is one of the most careful in this class of report: ΔNLL-based candidate selection validated against 15 downstream tasks, showing a 19.65 GB EvoPress profile within 0.1 point of BF16 accuracy (75.6% vs 75.7%) and no reasoning-length inflation.
- Transparent per-benchmark appendix documenting harnesses, judge models, number of runs (3–10 attempts per task on most benchmarks), and which numbers are self-reported vs internally run — better disclosure practice than most model cards.
- The dense-vs-MoE decision is made with an actual scaling-ladder experiment under the deployment memory constraint rather than by fashion.
- Multilingual agentic evaluation (OmnilingualGAIA2 across 10 languages, Linguini metalinguistic reasoning) goes well beyond the usual English-only scorecard, and the token-efficiency analysis (2,453 vs Qwen's 4,532 tokens/rollout) addresses real deployment cost.

## Limitations

- No measured on-device latency/throughput or speculative-decoding speedup numbers anywhere in the paper; the headline use case is supported only qualitatively.
- Not uniformly SOTA: trails Qwen3.6-27B on computer-use (OSWorld −9.7), terminal (TerminalBench −9.0), and knowledge-work (GDPval-AA −188) benchmarks; trails Gemma4-31B on graduate-level reasoning (GPQA Diamond −2.2), HLE, instruction-following edge cases, and translation.
- Video input is ingested as frames but not optimized; multilingual multimodal understanding is explicitly not evaluated.
- Coverage-oriented privacy training shows up as higher CIMemories violation rates than Gemma4-31B (26.4 vs 12.1), suggesting the coverage/violation trade-off was tuned toward helpfulness.
- PrivacyLens leakage is non-monotonic across reasoning strengths (59.6 low → 49.2 high → 59.6 xhigh), hinting at unstable privacy behavior that the paper does not analyze.
- Dependence on a proprietary frontier teacher (Muse Spark) and internal synthetic pipelines limits external reproducibility of the training recipe.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What tokens/sec and end-to-end latency does the EvoPress-quantized model with DFlash actually achieve on an RTX 5090 / Apple Silicon / 24 GB laptop GPU, and how large is the speculative-decoding speedup versus autoregressive decoding?
- Why does the model trail Qwen3.6-27B so specifically on GUI computer use (OSWorld −9.7) and terminal tasks (−9.0) while winning on API/tool benchmarks like MCPAtlas — is it training-data mix, perception encoding, or the computer-use action space?
- Can the contextual-integrity training be tuned to push CIMemories violations below Gemma4-31B's 12.1 without sacrificing its coverage advantage (64.8 vs 53.0), and why does PrivacyLens leakage swing non-monotonically (59.6 → 49.2 → 59.6) across reasoning strengths?
- How much of the agentic gain comes from on-policy distillation versus RL alone, given that the paper asserts but does not quantify the improvement?
- Since the dense-vs-MoE ladder used 550 TPP (dense) versus 2,800/6,000 TPP (MoE), would a differently allocated token budget flip the architecture conclusion for other memory ceilings (e.g., 16 GB or 64 GB)?
