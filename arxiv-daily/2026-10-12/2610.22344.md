> **一句话总结：** 上海 AI Lab 提出 MLExpert（35B MoE 自主机器学习工程智能体），通过 Kaggle-MLE Factory 构建可执行任务、分层智能体 SFT 蒸馏长程优化轨迹，再用窗口化交互强化学习（WIRL）把"两次实验结果之间的多轮决策"作为一个执行窗口共享基于结果（奖牌）的奖励，并配合异步收集与 token 级策略修正做在线更新——在 MLE-Bench Lite 上把基座 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 的任意奖牌率从 37.9% 提升到 72.7%（+34.9 个百分点），追平 GLM-5.3 与 Kimi-K3 等更大前沿模型，且能力迁移到 NatureBench Lite（Surpass-SOTA 0%→50%）、AIME 脚手架优化（held-out 23.3%→43.7%）与 MLS-Bench-Lite 算法组件优化（平均奖励 0.322→0.425）。

> **TL;DR:** A 35B MoE agentic model (initialized from Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) post-trained for autonomous, model-directed machine learning engineering: hierarchical agentic SFT on outcome-filtered teacher trajectories, then Windowed Interaction RL (WIRL) that assigns each execution window (decisions between successive experiment results) a shared medal-based reward with token-level policy correction for asynchronous updates. On MLE-Bench Lite (22 Kaggle tasks) it lifts Any Medal from 37.9% (base) to 72.7% (+34.9 pts), tying GLM-5.3 and Kimi-K3 and beating same-scale baselines (best: Nex-N2.5-mini 47.0%); it keeps a 69.7% medal rate under Claude Code (vs 42.4% base), raises NatureBench Lite Surpass-SOTA from 0.0% to 50.0%, and transfers to AIME scaffold optimization (43.7% vs 23.3% held-out) and MLS-Bench-Lite component optimization (0.425 vs 0.322 mean reward). Caveats: frontier baselines were evaluated once each on a 22-task set, teacher trajectories come from the much stronger closed GLM-5.2, and Med+/Gold still trail Kimi-K3.

## 核心贡献

- **MLExpert 智能体模型**：基于 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B（35B 总参 / ~3B 激活 MoE）训练的自主 MLE 智能体，模型自己决定何时分析、修改、执行、回溯或改变优化方向，而非由外部工作流/搜索规则编排。
- **Kaggle-MLE Factory**：多智能体流水线（发现→分析→生成→执行→校验）把 Meta-Kaggle 的 11,649 个历史比赛记录转化为带公开工作区、隐藏测试集与统一评测接口的可执行任务；人工复核后保留 154 个任务，覆盖视觉/文本/音频/表格/时序多模态。
- **模型主导的长程交互环境（Interaction Harness）**：ReAct 式循环 + 由父链修订构成的"实验树"，提供 execute_code / patch_code / invalidate_node（可剔除过拟合或泄漏导致的虚高候选）/ update_answer 等 13 个工具，上下文超限时摘要压缩，复杂分析委托给独立上下文的分析子智能体。
- **分层智能体 SFT + 结果导向的监督筛选**：先用通用编码/工具轨迹做第一阶段 SFT，再用 GLM-5.2（MLE）与 Qwen3.8-Max（科学实验）教师轨迹做领域适配；以私有榜单相对名次（阈值 η=0.4）筛选轨迹，若峰值后退化超过 δ=0.05 则在首个最优提交处截断，剔除"变差的长尾"；损失只作用于模型生成 token。
- **窗口化交互强化学习（WIRL）**：把两次实验结果之间的多轮决策划为一个执行窗口，窗口内共享奖牌分级结果奖励（执行错误 -1.0 → 金牌 2.0，外加 ±0.2 的局部趋势项），不做组内归一化；异步收集窗口使学习器无需等整条轨迹结束，并用 rollout log 概率做 token 级策略修正（比值限于 [0.2, 4.0]）处理 off-policy 陈旧性。
- **跨任务泛化证据**：学到的优化能力迁移到 NatureBench Lite 科学实验、AIME 智能体脚手架优化（ShinkaEvolve 协议）与 MLS-Bench-Lite 算法组件优化，支持"长程优化决策是可训练的模型能力"这一论断。

## Key Contributions

- **MLExpert**, a 35B MoE (~3B active) agentic model post-trained from Qwen3.6-35B-A3B for model-directed (not workflow-orchestrated) long-horizon MLE optimization.
- **Kaggle-MLE Factory**: a multi-agent construction/validation/repair pipeline that turns historical Kaggle competitions into executable MLE tasks — from 11,649 Meta-Kaggle entries down to 154 human-reviewed tasks with hidden test splits and a unified evaluation interface.
- **An Interaction Harness** exposing an experiment tree (solution nodes with code, logs, metadata) plus 13 tools (analyze, execute_code, patch_code, invalidate_node for untrustworthy validation scores, update_answer, plan management, ...), with context summarization and an independent-context analysis sub-agent.
- **Hierarchical agentic SFT with outcome-guided supervision**: general coding/tool-use SFT first, then MLE-domain SFT on 636 retained teacher trajectories (412 MLE from GLM-5.2 + 224 scientific from GLM-5.2/Qwen3.8-Max, 164 Nature-family papers); trajectory filtering by leaderboard-relative quality (η=0.4) and truncation at the first best submission when post-peak degradation exceeds δ=0.05.
- **Windowed Interaction RL (WIRL)**: execution windows anchored at experiment results with shared outcome rewards (−1.0 execution error … 2.0+T for gold, T∈{−0.2,0,0.2} local trend), no group-relative normalization, asynchronous window collection, and token-level policy correction with recorded rollout log-probabilities (ratio bounded [0.2, 4.0]) — a concrete recipe for credit assignment over delayed, multi-decision feedback.
- **Generalization evidence** beyond MLE-Bench: NatureBench Lite scientific tasks, AIME agent-scaffold optimization, and MLS-Bench-Lite algorithm-component optimization.

## Methodology

The paper formulates agentic MLE as maximizing the expected score of the finally selected solution under a time budget, with the policy (not an external controller) organizing experiments. Three components:

1. **Kaggle-MLE Factory (task construction).** A discovery agent screens competitions (completed, ≥50 teams, ≤200 GB data, offline-evaluable); an analyzer extracts data structures/modality into a task spec; a generator prepares data, held-out splits with hidden labels, and task-specific evaluators; an executor+validator run and iteratively repair tasks (checking data integrity, submission-format consistency, non-degenerate labels, train–test identifier overlap). Of 160 registered tasks, 154 survive manual review.

2. **Interaction Harness.** A ReAct-style loop where solution nodes with parent-linked revisions form an experiment tree the model can revisit, backtrack, or abandon. Key tools: `execute_code`/`control_run` (launch/control experiments), `write_full_code`/`patch_code`, `analyze` (delegates to an analysis sub-agent with its own context, returning only a summary), `invalidate_node` (excludes candidates whose validation scores are inflated by overfitting/leakage), `update_answer` (maintains the submission candidate). Earlier history is summarized as context approaches its limit. Evaluation used ≤3,000 interaction steps and 12 h per task on 20 vCPUs / 220 GB RAM / one H200.

3. **Hierarchical agentic SFT.** Stage 1: general coding/tool-use trajectories. Stage 2: 636 retained MLE/scientific teacher trajectories (GLM-5.2 for MLE; GLM-5.2 + Qwen3.8-Max for 164 Nature-family-paper-derived tasks), serialized into 5,539 input records (~302 M tokens, 8 epochs, 262k-token sequences). Supervision is outcome-guided: trajectories whose best submission's leaderboard-relative score b < η=0.4 are discarded; for retained ones, if average post-peak degradation d(τ) > δ=0.05 the trajectory prefix is anchored at the first best submission (dropping degraded tails); loss is masked to newly generated reasoning/response/tool-call tokens only.

4. **WIRL.** Each execution window u_m covers model decisions since the previous experiment result; window reward R_m ∈ {−1.0 (execution error), −0.6 (no scorable submission), −0.4+T, 1.0+T (bronze/silver), 2.0+T (gold)} with T ∈ {−0.2, 0, +0.2} tracking local score trend vs the previous valid submission. Unlike GRPO, the raw verifiable reward is used directly as the window's shared advantage (no group normalization). Because experiments run asynchronously and a long trajectory spans several policy updates, WIRL collects finished windows immediately and applies token-level importance correction q = exp(ℓ_old − ℓ_roll) with tokens outside [0.2, 4.0] excluded; the PPO-style clipped loss (ε−, ε+) = (0.3, 3.0) is window-normalized by valid output tokens, plus KL regularization to the SFT reference (λ_KL=1e-3) and entropy bonus (λ_H=1e-4). Batches are 32 windows; lr 1e-6 → 1e-7 cosine, no warmup.

## Results & Highlights

- **MLE-Bench Lite main result (MLExpert Harness, 22 tasks, mean of 3 evals):** MLExpert reaches **72.73% ± 2.62 Any Medal vs 37.88% ± 5.46 for its base Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (+34.9 pts)**, with Gold rate rising from 22.73% to 50.00%. This ties GLM-5.3 (72.73%) and Kimi-K3 (72.73%), trails GPT-5.6 Sol (77.27%), and far exceeds the best same-scale baseline Nex-N2.5-mini (46.97% ± 1.52, i.e., +25.8 pts).
- **General-purpose harness transfer:** under Claude Code, MLExpert scores **69.70% ± 1.52 Any Medal vs 42.42% ± 4.01 for the base (+27.3 pts)**, beating the strongest same-scale baseline (Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B, 46.97%) by 22.7 pts — the gain is not an artifact of the model's own harness.
- **Stage ablation (MLExpert Harness):** Base 37.88% → +General Agent SFT 46.97% → +MLE SFT 62.12% → +WIRL **72.73%**; each stage adds a significant increment.
- **RL ablations (same MLE-SFT init):** WIRL **72.73%** vs trajectory-level **GRPO 56.06% ± 5.46** and **PPO-style 60.61% ± 4.01** (both *below* the 62.1% SFT starting point); removing the trend reward drops WIRL to 66.67%, removing policy correction to 65.15%. Asynchronous window collection also cuts the learner waiting fraction from **87.6% (GRPO) to 39.3% (WIRL)**.
- **NatureBench Lite (10 tasks, Claude Code):** Surpass-SOTA improves **0.0% → 50.0%** and Match-SOTA **40.0% → 70.0%** over the base; 50.0% Surpass-SOTA matches GLM-5.3, beats Nex-N2.5-mini (20.0%) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro Preview (20.0%), but trails Kimi-K3 (70.0%).
- **AIME scaffold optimization (fixed GPT-4.1-nano solver, ShinkaEvolve protocol, held-out = AIME 2023/24/26):** MLExpert-optimized scaffold reaches **43.7% held-out accuracy vs 23.3% for the base model (+20.4 pts)** with 8.40 calls/problem, exceeding Kimi-K3 (40.0%, 8.65 calls) though below GLM-5.3 (48.1%, 9.39 calls); MLExpert-SFT alone hits 38.1% with only 3.51 calls.
- **MLS-Bench-Lite (12 domains, 5 h budget):** mean normalized verifier reward improves **0.322 → 0.425**; MLExpert wins 10 tasks, ties 1, loses 1 (time-series forecasting), with the largest gains on causal graph discovery (0.000→0.371), LM demasking (0.148→0.387), and multi-objective optimization (0.161→0.350).
- **Case study (MLSP 2013 Birds):** within a 12-h run MLExpert climbs ROC-AUC **0.58 → 0.964** (audio features+LightGBM → PANNs/AST fine-tuning + snapshot averaging), vs 0.60 → 0.88 for MLExpert-SFT.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: solid, well-positioned.** The execution-window formulation — grouping multi-turn decisions between experiment results under one verifiable reward, with token-level off-policy correction — is a genuine and practical answer to delayed, aggregated feedback in long-horizon RL, and the positioning vs ML-Agent (single-step RL) and Frontis-MA1 (RL for operators inside an external evolutionary loop) is fair. The GRPO/PPO ablation (both *degrading* an SFT model that already scored 62.1%) is the paper's most convincing evidence.
- **Rigor: mixed.** Same-scale models get 3 runs with SEM, but **each larger frontier model is evaluated once** on a 22-task benchmark where a single task swings Any Medal by 4.55 pts — the "comparable to Kimi-K3/GLM-5.3" claim (72.7 vs 72.7) is within noise, and the borrowed Kimi-K3-Claude-Code / GPT-5.6-Sol-Codex numbers come from the Frontis-MA1 paper rather than the authors' own runs. On NatureBench Lite MLExpert is reported from a **single evaluation of 10 tasks**.
- **"Comparable to frontier" needs qualification:** MLExpert's Med+ (84.85%) is clearly below Kimi-K3 (95.45%) and GLM-5.3 (90.91%), and Gold (50.0%) trails Kimi-K3 (63.64%) — it matches on Any Medal largely via a broader bronze/silver distribution, not top-tier wins.
- **Teacher provenance weakens the "35B catches up" narrative:** the MLE-SFT corpus is distilled from GLM-5.2 and Qwen3.8-Max trajectories, i.e., a much larger closed model's competence is being compressed into 35B — impressive, but it is distillation plus RL, not emergent parity.
- **Harness asymmetry is partially addressed but not eliminated:** the headline table uses the MLExpert Harness (the exact toolset the model was trained on); frontier models use it too, but they were not trained on it. The Claude Code column (69.7%) is the more honest cross-harness number and is still strong, yet GPT-5.6 Sol's 72.7 there exceeds it.
- **Reproducibility:** hyperparameters, reward constants, filtering thresholds (η=0.4, δ=0.05), and per-task numbers are all disclosed, and tasks derive from public Kaggle data — good. But reproducing requires the closed GLM-5.2/Qwen3.8-Max teachers for SFT data, plus very large compute (12 h/task × 22 tasks × repeated evals on H200 + 220 GB RAM each), so independent verification is costly.
- **Red flags / quirks:** the case study (MLSP 2013 Birds) is a single cherry-picked success; on MLS-Bench-Lite both models score exactly 0.000 on Gaussian-splatting densification and MLExpert *regresses* on time-series forecasting (0.446→0.428); the reward design bakes Kaggle medal thresholds into RL (bronze/silver lumped at 1.0) which may discourage gold-chasing risk-taking; and 6 of 160 factory tasks were dropped by manual review without quantifying what the automated validator missed.

## Strengths

- A complete, end-to-end recipe (task factory → harness → SFT → RL) rather than an algorithm-only paper; every stage has an ablation isolating its contribution.
- WIRL directly attacks the two hardest practical problems of long-horizon agentic RL — delayed aggregated feedback and rollout-policy staleness — with mechanisms (shared window reward, token-level correction bounds, async collection) that reduced learner waiting time by 48.3 pts vs GRPO.
- Evaluation breadth is above the norm: two harnesses, a scientific benchmark, and two out-of-domain transfer settings (agent scaffold optimization, algorithm-component optimization), all with per-task breakdowns.
- Honest reporting of failures (time-series regression, a 0/0 domain, Med+ gap to Kimi-K3) and full hyperparameter disclosure.

## Limitations

- Small benchmark granularity: MLE-Bench Lite has 22 tasks (one task = 4.55 pts) and NatureBench Lite 10; frontier baselines run once, so several headline comparisons sit within run-to-run noise.
- Dependence on proprietary frontier teachers (GLM-5.2, Qwen3.8-Max) for SFT data; the community cannot regenerate the corpus exactly.
- Training/evaluation cost: 12-hour task budgets with an H200 + 220 GB RAM each make the protocol expensive to replicate or scale; the paper does not report total training compute.
- The medal-based reward is Kaggle-specific (medal thresholds as reward tiers); how WIRL ports to domains without leaderboard references is only implicitly addressed by the trend term.
- Generalization is demonstrated but uneven — it wins 10/12 MLS-Bench-Lite domains yet loses time-series and ties at zero on vision densification; no analysis of when transfer fails.

## Relevance

直接命中"Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research"方向：MLExpert 是一个在真实 Kaggle/Nature 任务上自主组织长程实验、依据执行反馈持续改进解法的 AI4AI 智能体，其"实验结果窗口 + 异步策略修正"的 agentic RL 方案对研究长时程自主科研循环与自我改进系统的训练动力学很有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the WIRL gain survives when the medal-threshold reward is replaced by raw normalized score — is the bronze/silver/gold discretization load-bearing, or does it cap gold-chasing exploration?
- With single-eval frontier baselines on a 22-task benchmark, would repeated runs of Kimi-K3 / GPT-5.6 Sol reorder the top of Table 1 (SEM on MLExpert alone is ±2.62)?
- Can the GLM-5.2 teacher trajectories be replaced by self-generated or open-model trajectories (rejection-sampled from the 35B itself) without losing the MLE-SFT jump from 47.0% to 62.1%?
- Why does MLExpert regress on time-series forecasting in MLS-Bench-Lite (0.446→0.428) while gaining +0.37 on causal discovery — is there a modality- or metric-shape-dependent failure mode of the window reward?
- Does the token-level policy-correction bound [0.2, 4.0] interact with window length: as hardware makes experiments shorter/longer, how should the correction bounds and trend reward scale?
- The paper claims a path toward recursive self-improvement; can MLExpert optimize its own scaffold/harness (as in the AIME experiment) rather than a fixed GPT-4.1-nano solver, and does the capability compound?
