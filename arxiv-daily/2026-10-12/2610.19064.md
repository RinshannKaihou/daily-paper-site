> **一句话总结：** 该论文从理论与实验两方面证明，DiLoCo 类局部优化训练中"副本数越多性能越差"的主要根源是内层优化器输出的伪梯度协方差结构和局部 batch 缩小带来的二阶矩估计误差——采用白化型内层优化器（SOAP/SPlus）并按 token 半衰期规则放大 β₂，可在 96 个副本下将验证损失相对 DP 的退化压到 +2.1%（AdamW 为 +12%），并让廉价的 IsoLoCo 反超 MuLoCo。

> **TL;DR:** In DiLoCo-style low-communication LLM pretraining on DCLM (178M and 512M LLaMA models, 3.55B/10.25B tokens), the authors decompose the gap to data-parallel (DP) training into a mean-mismatch term and a stochastic term, and show both are governed by the inner optimizer: whitening-style inner optimizers keep the pseudo-gradient covariance near-isotropic (anisotropy 0.43 for SPlus vs 35 for AdamW at R=64), while scaling AdamW's β₂ with the number of replicas R preserves second-moment memory. At 512M/96 replicas on held-out validation loss, SPlus degrades only +2.1% vs its own DP baseline (2.663 vs 2.610) while AdamW degrades +12% (2.958 vs 2.646) and Muon +7.7% (2.787 vs 2.586); with β₂ tuned, the cheap IsoLoCo (2.702) beats the more expensive Muon-based MuLoCo (2.738) at 512M/R=64. If you care about cross-datacenter training, the practical recipe is: SOAP at moderate replica counts, SPlus at high replica counts.

## 核心贡献

- **误差分解框架（命题1）**：将局部优化（DiLoCo）与对应 DP 更新的方向误差严格分解为"均值失配（mean mismatch）"+"随机误差（stochastic error）"两项，并证明随机误差由聚合伪梯度的最大方向方差 λ_max(C_agg) 控制——这直接把"内层优化器如何变换梯度噪声"与副本扩展性联系起来。
- **白化是内层优化器的关键性质**：在固定总方差下，各向同性（白化）协方差最小化最大方向方差；且当各副本协方差一致时，均匀平均（DiLoCo 默认聚合方式）是最优的。逐优化器分析表明：AdamW 保留相关性（不白化）、Shampoo 仅将协方差条件数开方、SOAP 在理想条件下精确白化、SPlus 通过符号变换白化且无需估计坐标尺度（利用 Gauss arcsine 律证明残差相关性收缩，白化误差 ≤ SOAP 的）、Muon 仅在旋转对称假设下各向同性。
- **β₂ 随副本数扩展的规则**：证明 Adam 类内层优化器的单步均值失配上界随 R−1 线性增长（命题3），而按"二阶矩半衰期以 token 计保持不变"的规则 β₂(R) = β₂(R₀)^{R₀/R} 可使上界有界。据此，AdamW 内核的 IsoLoCo 在大副本数下反超更昂贵的 MuLoCo。
- **大规模实证**：在 178M/512M LLaMA + DCLM 上系统比较 SOAP、SPlus、Muon、AdamW、Shampoo（8–96 副本），白化型优化器扩展性最强：512M/R=96 时 SPlus 相对自身 DP 仅 +2.1%，比文献已有方法最多减少约 6% 的退化。
- **机制验证实验**：直接测量各优化器的更新协方差各向异性、均值/噪声比值（均值项仅为噪声项的 7.5–19%）、以及高噪声方向的曲率（AdamW 的极端噪声方向曲率是随机方向的 460–730 倍，SPlus 仅 1.5 倍），把理论量与实测扩展性排序对应起来。

## Key Contributions

- **A bias–variance decomposition for local optimization (Proposition 1):** the directional error between the averaged local (DiLoCo) update and the expected DP update splits into a mean-mismatch term and a stochastic term bounded by the largest eigenvalue of the aggregated pseudo-gradient covariance, λ_max(C_agg). This reframes replica scaling as a question about how the inner optimizer transforms gradient noise, not just about client drift.
- **Whitening as the desideratum for inner optimizers:** at fixed total variance, isotropy minimizes the largest directional variance (λ_max(C) ≥ τ/d with equality iff C = (τ/d)I), and matching spherical covariances across replicas makes DiLoCo's default uniform averaging optimal (Aitken). Per-optimizer covariance analysis: AdamW preserves input correlations even with true per-coordinate variances; canonical Shampoo only square-roots the covariance condition number; SOAP whitens exactly if its eigenbasis decorrelates and variances are exact; SPlus whitens via the sign map with no scale estimates needed — using the Gaussian arcsine law, its whitening error provably satisfies ‖C_SPlus − I‖²_F ≤ ‖C_SOAP − I‖²_F under imperfect decorrelation; Muon is isotropic only under rotational-invariance assumptions.
- **A β₂ replica-scaling rule with a bound (Proposition 3):** with fresh second moments, the one-step Adam mean-mismatch bound grows linearly in R−1; adopting the token-half-life rule β₂(R) = β₂(R₀)^{R₀/R} keeps the bound bounded as local batch shrinks. Empirically this lets IsoLoCo (AdamW inner, isotropic outer merging) outperform the more compute-expensive MuLoCo (Muon inner) at large worker counts.
- **Systematic replica-scaling study:** 178M and 512M LLaMA models trained on DCLM with H=30 local steps, comparing SOAP/SPlus/Muon/AdamW/Shampoo across R ∈ {8,16,32,64,96} against each optimizer's own DP reference — whitening-based inner optimizers scale best (up to ~6% less degradation vs prior methods at 96 replicas).
- **Mechanistic probes that connect theory to measurement:** update-covariance anisotropy at a shared checkpoint (SPlus 0.43 vs AdamW 35 after one local step), mean-mismatch-to-noise ratios along high-noise directions (only 7.5–19%), and curvature along fitted high-noise directions (AdamW 460–730x average curvature vs SPlus 1.5x) — explaining why noise concentrated in high-curvature directions is so damaging and why whitening helps.

## Methodology

The paper combines theory with controlled pretraining experiments inside the DiLoCo framework (Algorithm 1: R replicas each run H steps of an inner optimizer on B/R tokens per step, displacements are uniformly averaged, and a Nesterov-momentum outer optimizer applies the aggregate).

**Theory.** Section 4.2 decomposes, for any unit direction q, the error E[(q^T(α_R Δ̄ − d_DP))²] into (q^T b_R)² + α_R² q^T C_agg q, bounding the worst direction by ‖b_R‖² + α_R² λ_max(C_agg). Section 4.3 computes how each optimizer core transforms the covariance of a random matrix proposal: Adam's diagonal normalization preserves correlations; Shampoo's (L+εI)^{-1/4} M (R+εI)^{-1/4} only square-roots the condition number; SOAP (Adam in Shampoo's eigenbasis) yields C = I under exact decorrelation and exact variances; SPlus (sign of momentum in the historical eigenbasis) yields C = I for Gaussian proposals without any variance estimation, and contracts residual correlations via the arcsine law; Muon's polar map yields γ²/d_max · I only under one-sided rotational invariance. Section 4.4 analyzes the single-step mean mismatch: for SPlus (Proposition 2), under a Gaussian signal-plus-noise model for each rotated momentum coordinate with per-coordinate SNR a_j, the mismatch with a √R calibration is third-order in a and its squared norm is sixth order, while the covariance stays ⪯ I. For Adam (Proposition 3), fresh second moments inflate the local v-estimates by (1−β₂)(R−1)diag(Σ_g), so the one-step mean bound grows linearly in R−1 at fixed β₂; setting β₂(R) = β₂(R₀)^{R₀/R} (preserving the EMA half-life in tokens, adapting Marek et al. 2025) keeps the bound O(1). Appendix A.4 additionally separates SOAP's whitening error into coordinate-scale-estimation error plus residual basis correlations, showing SPlus eliminates the scale term entirely.

**Experiments.** 178M (9 layers, width 1024) and 512M (12 layers, width 1536) LLaMA-style models trained on DCLM with sequence length 2048, evaluated on a held-out 100M-token validation split; global batch fixed per scale (524K tokens / 256 sequences at 178M over ~3.55B tokens; 2M tokens / 1024 sequences at 512M over ~10.25B tokens), so the local batch shrinks as B/R; H=30 inner steps between synchronizations, Nesterov SGD outer optimizer; learning rates and outer momentum tuned per optimizer with ≥10 grid points at R ∈ {8, 64, 96} (R=16/32 interpolated). Beyond the main sweep: an H=1 pure-averaging control that isolates the nonlinear-update effect from multi-step drift; the β₂-tuning comparison for DiLoCo/IsoLoCo/MuLoCo at 178M and 512M; and three probe analyses at the 178M round-75 checkpoint — (i) conditional covariance replay over 64 replicas × 16 paired rollouts measuring an anisotropy coefficient a_h, (ii) paired DP-referenced estimation of mean mismatch vs stochastic error along fitted high-noise directions across all 63 hidden matrices, and (iii) Hessian-vector-product curvature ratios along those directions.

## Results & Highlights

- **178M replica scaling, R=64, DCLM validation loss vs each optimizer's own DP baseline:** SPlus +3.1% (2.942 vs DP 2.853), SOAP +4.8% (2.951 vs 2.816), Muon +8.5% (3.099 vs 2.858), Shampoo +8.0% (3.069 vs 2.842), AdamW +11.0% (3.208 vs 2.880) — whitening-based inner optimizers cut the R=64 degradation by roughly two-thirds relative to AdamW. Notably at R=8, SPlus (2.851) actually beats its own DP reference (2.853), which the authors attribute to a signSGD-style majority-vote effect.
- **512M replica scaling, R=96:** SPlus degrades only +2.1% (2.663 vs DP 2.610), versus SOAP +4.4% (2.676 vs 2.563), Muon +7.7% (2.787 vs 2.586), and AdamW +12% (2.958 vs 2.646); SPlus overtakes SOAP in absolute loss at R=64 (2.646 vs 2.649) and R=96 (2.663 vs 2.676), even though SOAP has the stronger DP baseline (2.563 vs 2.610).
- **β₂ scaling rule, 512M, R=64 (Figure 5):** changing only β₂ (published R=8 hyperparameters otherwise untouched) reduces DiLoCo's loss from 2.884 to 2.849 and IsoLoCo's from 2.734 to 2.702 (~1.2% each); the tuned IsoLoCo (2.702, AdamW inner) then outperforms MuLoCo (2.738, Muon inner) — reversing the prior-work ordering and at lower cost, since IsoLoCo only does spectral decomposition in the outer step.
- **H=1 single-step control (178M, Table 3):** with pure parameter averaging (no outer optimizer), replica scaling damage already appears — and Muon collapses (3.417 at R=64 vs DP 2.858, +19.6%, worse than AdamW's 3.157), while SPlus (2.912) and SOAP (2.958) stay robust. This shows the nonlinear inner-optimizer update, not multi-step client drift, is the dominant source of divergence from DP.
- **Mechanistic probes (178M checkpoint, round 75, R=64):** one-step update anisotropy a_1 is 0.43 for SPlus and 2.3 for SOAP, vs 3.8 for Muon and 35 for AdamW (~80x gap between SPlus and AdamW); the mean-mismatch term is only 7.5–19% of the stochastic term along high-noise directions (SOAP 7.5%, SPlus 7.9%, Muon 14%, AdamW 19%), validating the focus on the covariance term; and AdamW's extreme-noise directions carry 460–730x the curvature of random directions at R=64 (Muon 47–100x, SOAP 4.2–5.2x, SPlus 1.5x), so unwhitened noise concentrates exactly where errors are most costly.
- **Practical takeaway stated by the authors:** with SOAP or SPlus as the inner optimizer, 50+ replicas incur <3% validation-loss degradation relative to DP; scaling model/data size improves replica scaling for all optimizers tested (178M → 512M comparison in Figure 1).

## Authors & Credibility

The team is from Mila, Concordia University, Université de Montréal, and CNRS: Eugene Belilovsky (Mila/Concordia, known for work on distributed/decentralized training), Stefan Horoi and Benjamin Thérien (Mila/UdeM — the same group behind MuLoCo and IsoLoCo cited here), and Edouard Oyallon (CNRS). The paper is a preprint (arXiv:2610.19064v1, 13 Oct 2026, cs.LG), so it has not yet passed peer review. The authors are transparently positioned relative to their own prior methods (MuLoCo, IsoLoCo): rather than promoting them, the paper shows a simple β₂ fix neutralizes MuLoCo's advantage. Compute was provided by the Digital Research Alliance Canada with NSERC funding; hyperparameter sweeps (≥10 grid points at R ∈ {8,64,96} per optimizer, two model scales, five optimizers plus ablations) suggest a genuinely substantial but still academic-scale budget. The 25-page write-up includes full proofs (Appendix A), exact replay protocols and estimators (Appendix C), and unusually candid scope caveats (e.g., Appendix C.1.4 explicitly states the covariance probe "is not a same-state causal ablation").

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core reframing — replica scaling in DiLoCo is governed by pseudo-gradient covariance structure and second-moment estimation error, not primarily client drift — is genuinely new and well-executed. The covariance analysis of optimizer cores (arcsine-law argument that SPlus dominates SOAP in whitening error under imperfect decorrelation) and the transfer of the β₂ token-half-life rule to replica scaling are both non-obvious. However, the individual ingredients (DiLoCo framework, SOAP/SPlus/Muon optimizers, the Marek et al. small-batch rule, IsoLoCo) are all borrowed; the contribution is the unifying analysis and the systematic comparison.
- **Rigor:** Theory is honest about its assumptions (fixed bases, Gaussian proposals, single-step mean analysis, frozen-state model) and the empirical probes match the predicted ordering. Two wrinkles: (1) the H=1 experiment reveals Muon is *worse than AdamW* at R=64 (3.417 vs 3.157), contradicting its H=30 ranking — the authors only "hypothesise" the outer optimizer is responsible and never resolve this, which leaves the Muon story incomplete. (2) The covariance/curvature probes evaluate each optimizer at its *own* trained checkpoint, so as the authors admit, cross-method differences confound the update rule with the reached state. (3) In the H=1 table, R=16/32 hyperparameters were interpolated rather than tuned, slightly favoring the tuned endpoints' methods.
- **Reproducibility:** Good by preprint standards — architecture tables, token budgets, sweep protocol, and probe estimators (with finite-sample corrections) are all specified. But no code release is mentioned, and the SPlus variant used here deliberately omits the original's iterate averaging, so exact reimplementation requires care.
- **Significance:** Directly relevant to the increasingly practical problem of cross-datacenter LLM training. The "<3% degradation at 50+ replicas with SOAP/SPlus" and "β₂-tuned IsoLoCo beats MuLoCo" results are actionable recipes today. The chief significance limit is scale: 178M/512M on DCLM is two orders of magnitude below frontier models, and claims like SOAP being "on trend to become the best inner optimizer at scale" rest on a two-point extrapolation.
- **Red flags:** Evaluation is validation loss only — no downstream benchmarks (MMLU-style), no wall-clock/communication-volume comparison despite the motivating setting being bandwidth-constrained (SOAP/SPlus need more optimizer-state memory than Muon, acknowledged but not quantified in the main results). Single dataset (DCLM) and single architecture family (LLaMA). The headline "6% less degradation at 96 replicas vs existing methods" compares against the *weakest* published baselines (AdamW-based DiLoCo at 12%); against Muon the margin is ~5.6 points, and in absolute loss SOAP wins at most R — the abstract's emphasis on relative-to-own-DP framing, while methodologically justified, flatters SPlus.

## Strengths

- A clean, falsifiable decomposition (mean mismatch vs stochastic error) that turns a folk observation ("Muon scales better than AdamW in DiLoCo") into a quantitative theory with testable per-optimizer predictions — which the probes then confirm in the predicted order (SPlus > SOAP > Muon > AdamW on anisotropy, curvature concentration, and scaling).
- The β₂ scaling rule is nearly free to apply, comes with a proven bound, and its payoff is demonstrated on *other people's* methods (DiLoCo, IsoLoCo), including reversing the IsoLoCo-vs-MuLoCo ordering — a rare case of a simple hyperparameter fix beating a more expensive architectural change.
- The H=1 pure-averaging control is a clever isolation experiment: it shows replica-scaling damage exists before any client drift, overturning the classical local-SGD narrative for adaptive optimizers.
- Mechanistic depth beyond loss curves: measured anisotropy coefficients, mean/noise ratios with paired finite-sample corrections, and Hessian-vector curvature along fitted noise directions give a coherent causal story for *why* whitening helps.
- Unusually forthright appendix about estimator limitations (biased ratios, non-causal cross-method probes, coupled finite-sample errors).

## Limitations

- Scale ceiling: 178M/512M parameters, 3.55B/10.25B tokens, one dataset (DCLM), one architecture family (LLaMA). Whether SOAP/SPlus keep their advantage (and memory overhead stays manageable) at multi-billion parameters is extrapolated from two points, though cited large-scale SOAP results (Khona et al. 2026) partially mitigate this.
- No end-task evaluation — validation perplexity/loss only; no evidence the loss deltas translate to downstream capability differences.
- The mean-mismatch analysis is exact only for H=1 from common state; the multi-step case is handled by assuming temporal covariances are small, which is asserted rather than proven, and the stochastic-term dominance (7.5–19%) is measured along *selected* high-noise directions, not uniformly.
- Muon's H=1 failure versus its H=30 success is left as an untested hypothesis about the outer optimizer; the interaction between inner optimizer and outer Nesterov momentum is otherwise unexamined.
- Practical costs are under-specified: SOAP/SPlus maintain two Gram factors per matrix, increasing memory and compute versus Muon; the paper defers to sharding arguments rather than measuring throughput or communication volume in its own setting.
- R=16/32 configurations use interpolated hyperparameters (H=1 table), and hyperparameters are tuned per-optimizer-per-R at only three replica counts, leaving some risk that baselines are under-tuned.

## Relevance

与分布式/低通信 LLM 训练（DiLoCo、跨数据中心训练）方向高度相关：论文回答了"为什么内层优化器决定副本扩展性"并给出可直接落地的配方（白化型内核 SOAP/SPlus + 随副本数扩展的 β₂ 规则），对大规模分布式训练与优化器设计研究都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the whitening advantage persist at 1B+ parameters and on downstream benchmarks (MMLU/GPQA-style), where SOAP's absolute-loss edge at low R might compound — or does SPlus's sign-flavored update lose fine-grained calibration at scale?
- What exactly makes Muon robust at H=30 but fragile at H=1 with pure averaging — is it the outer Nesterov momentum, the interaction of the polar map with parameter averaging, or a per-step noise structure the covariance probe misses?
- Can the β₂ token-half-life rule be merged with learning-rate/outer-momentum schedules (e.g., the optimal-HP drift Charles et al. observed with R) into a single replica-scaling recipe, and does it also fix second-moment methods in asynchronous DiLoCo variants?
- Is there a whitening inner optimizer with Muon-level memory cost — e.g., a low-rank or diagonal-plus-sign hybrid — that keeps SPlus's covariance contraction without storing two Gram factors per matrix?
- The curvature probe suggests noise in high-curvature directions is disproportionately harmful; would explicitly re-weighting the aggregate by local Hessian estimates (beyond isotropization like IsoLoCo) buy further scaling at 100+ replicas?
