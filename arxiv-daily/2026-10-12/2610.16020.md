> **一句话总结：** 该理论工作严格刻画了逐层补偿继承量化误差何时安全：对任意深度线性网络，在舍入误差零条件均值 + 确定性二阶矩映射（如无界网格上的减性抖动）假设下，预先固定强度 ∈ [0,1] 的补偿保证均方输出误差不增，并把增益精确分解为"直接消除的误差"与"降低后续舍入噪声激励"；但该保证在 ReLU 网络与有界码本下均会失效，论文还用一个两层标量反例推翻了 QEP 论文已发表的普适保证，并在 Qwen3-8B/Llama-3-8B 4-bit 实验中发现固定半强度补偿普遍优于 RTN，但任何自适应选强度规则都不能稳定胜出、且在校准数据上的收益未必迁移到新文本。

> **TL;DR:** A theory paper that settles when layer-wise compensation for inherited quantization error (the QEP/GPTAQ/Qronos-style objective) provably cannot hurt: for linear networks of any depth under subtractive dither on an unbounded grid, fixed-strength compensation gives mean-square output error no larger than uncompensated RTN, with the gain split exactly into direct error removal plus reduced excitation of later rounding noise. The guarantee fails for ReLU networks (two independent mechanisms, bounded only by projection-activation commutators under stable activations) and for bounded codebooks (even unique exact layer-wise minimizers without overload), and a two-scalar-layer counterexample refutes the published universal guarantee of QEP (Theorem B.5) outright. Experiments on Qwen3-8B and Llama-3-8B at 4 bits show fixed α=1/2 always beats RTN (e.g., WikiText-2 perplexity 12.643 → 12.320 on Qwen3-8B, −0.32), yet no adaptive strength-selection rule (local/block/network/gradient) beats the simple local rule consistently, and a rule that selects by full-network loss on 4096 selection tokens is better than local there by 0.0210 loss/token but worse on held-out WikiText-2 (+0.0069) and novel text (+0.075) — the ordering reverses on new data.

## 核心贡献

- **线性网络的网络级保证（定理 8 + 命题 9）：** 在每层舍入误差满足零条件均值且两方案共享确定性二阶矩映射 Ψ_l（减性抖动量化是其主要实例）的假设下，沿各自轨迹比较，补偿方案（强度预先固定于 [0,1]）的均方输出误差不超过未补偿方案；增益被精确递推分解为"直接移除的误差" S_l 与"降低后续舍入噪声激励"两部分——后者是逐层比较完全看不到的效应。
- **ReLU 网络的两个独立失效机制（第 5 节）：** (1) 噪声改变激活状态（例 12）；(2) 即使无任何状态改变，校正也会把误差从神经元关闭的样本搬到开放的样本上（例 14，超额项为 (2/27)t² + O(t³)）。在"稳定激活"假设下，定理 15 用校准投影与激活模式分块的交换子范数给出超额误差的上界。
- **推翻 QEP 已发表保证（定理 17/19）：** 在共享固定扰动模型下，仅两层标量线性网络、E=(ε,−ε)，即有 U^QEP − U^BASE = ε > 0，且输出误差 ε+ε² 对 ε²——对一切 0<ε<1/2 补偿严格更差；误差是一阶的，一阶近似约定无法吸收；并证明强度单调性（QEP 命题 5.4）同样失效。失效根源被精确定位：原证明误用三角不等式（"缩小差的一项不会缩小差本身"）并非法地把两条轨迹替换为一条。
- **真实码本下输出级支配也失效（定理 22）：** 三层标量 ReLU 链、四级码本 {0,1,2,3}，即使每层最小化子唯一、目标都在码本内部、无过载，QEP 仍可在开参数集上严格更差（例：teacher 输出 2.016 时，未补偿误差 0.016，QEP 误差 0.984），且对 α 非单调。
- **严格与实验的诚实对照（第 9 节）：** 在两个小模型上实测逐层交叉项比 c/d 绝对值不超过 0.07（远低于失效阈值 3/4），说明固定舍入下的抵消失效在实践中罕见；在两个 8B 模型上量化后发现：固定半强度补偿总优于 RTN，但按全网络损失选强度的规则会过拟合选择数据，收益不能可靠迁移到新文本。
- **Lean 形式化（第 10 节）：** 96 个模块、969 条具名定理声明在 Lean 4.34.1 + Mathlib 中通过核检查，含 10 条故意为假的声明作为阴性对照；代码与反例见证（saved witnesses）全部开源。

## Key Contributions

- **A network-level guarantee for linear networks (Theorem 8, Proposition 9):** Following each scheme along its own trajectory and carrying two moments through the layers — the error moment and the (uncentred) activation moment that governs how much later rounding noise is amplified — compensation with strengths in [0,1] fixed in advance gives mean-square output error no larger than no compensation, provided each layer's rounding error has zero conditional mean and a deterministic second-moment map common to both schemes (satisfied by subtractive dither on an unbounded grid, and by dithered rounding with fixed feedback as in GPTQ-style sequential quantization). The exact gain separates error removed directly (S_l) from reduced excitation of later rounding noise — a term invisible to any per-layer comparison.
- **Two independent obstructions for ReLU (Section 5):** the comparison fails when noise changes activation states (Example 12: 0.00564 → 0.00817 mean-square error, i.e., compensation ~45% worse on a scalar two-layer net), and even with no state changes, because the correction mixes calibration samples across activation patterns since the projection P does not commute with the activation mask D (Proposition 13 characterizes commutation as necessary and sufficient; Example 14's excess is (2/27)t² + O(t³)). Theorem 15 bounds the excess by commutator norms under stable activations; Corollary 16 gives O(ε₀t² + t³) small-error order.
- **Refutation of the published QEP guarantee (Theorems 17, 19):** In the shared-perturbation model of QEP's own appendix, two scalar linear layers with E = (ε, −ε) give U^QEP − U^BASE = ε > 0 and output error ε + ε² vs ε² for every 0 < ε < 1/2 — a first-order failure, so the first-order convention of the original proof cannot absorb it; monotonicity in strength (QEP Proposition 5.4) fails likewise. The exact proof gaps are pinpointed: an invalid triangle-inequality step ("shrinking one term of a difference does not shrink the difference") and an unjustified substitution of one scheme's trajectory for the other's.
- **Failure for actual finite codebooks (Theorem 22):** a three-layer scalar ReLU chain with codebook {0,1,2,3}, unique exact layer-wise minimizers, interior targets and no overload still admits an open parameter family where QEP's output error is strictly larger (uncorrected picks (1,2,1) → output 2 vs QEP's (1,3,1) → output 3; QEP worse iff teacher output t < 5/2), plus non-monotonicity in the strength α₂ with a switch at α₂ = 8/9.
- **An exact residual identity as the local criterion (Section 3.1):** ∥R_α∥² − ∥R₀∥² = −α(2−α)d + 2αc shows compensation helps on a common input iff 2c ≤ (2−α)d — i.e., iff the new rounding perturbation does not nearly cancel the inherited error; this single identity unifies all the positive and negative results.
- **Honest experiments + formal verification:** a pre-registered layer-wise diagnostic on SmolLM2-135M/Pythia-160M (c/d never above 0.07 in absolute value), 8B-scale strength-selection experiments exposing a selection-overestimate effect, and a scoped Lean 4 formalization (96 modules, 969 declarations, 10 deliberately false controls) with released code and machine-checkable witnesses.

## Methodology

The paper is a mathematical study of two layer-wise post-training quantization schemes: BASE (uncorrected, Ŵ = W + E) and QEP-style compensation Ŵ*(α) = W + αWδX̂ᵀĤ⁻¹ with strength α ∈ [0,1], where δ is the inherited input error and Ĥ = X̂X̂ᵀ the calibration Gram matrix. The analysis proceeds layer by layer upward in generality:

1. **One layer, one common input (Section 3):** an exact quadratic residual identity in α (Proposition 1) with the necessary-and-sufficient condition 2c ≤ (2−α)d where d = ∥AP∥²_F and c = ⟨AP, B⟩_F (inherited error projected vs. new perturbation); taking expectations gives the mean-square version under E[B | A,P] = 0 (Theorem 4), and subtractive dither on an unbounded grid is shown to supply both required conditions (zero conditional mean, α-independent perturbation energy). Bounded codebooks are shown to break the statement via overload examples.
2. **Linear networks of any depth (Section 4):** the two schemes are compared along their own trajectories by propagating two matrix moments jointly — error covariance C_l and activation second moment G_l — via two pointwise projection identities; a supermartingale-like induction yields C^QEP ⪯ C^BASE, G^QEP ⪯ G^BASE. Proposition 9 keeps the discarded PSD terms to give the exact gain; Corollary 10 adds row-space conditions under which full strength is optimal; Example 11 derives a closed-form depth gain (ratio → L, i.e., 10·log₁₀L dB at fine resolution) in a random-teacher model.
3. **ReLU networks (Section 5):** two explicit counterexamples (with and without activation-state changes), a masked-contraction characterization via commutators PD = DP, and Theorem 15 bounding the excess by the spectral norms ∥P_lJ_c − J_cP_l∥ of projection-vs-activation-cell commutators under the stable-activation assumption (a), with certificates showing the bound can be attained to a ratio of ≈0.999994.
4. **Fixed shared perturbations (Section 6):** explicit two-layer counterexamples to the published QEP Theorem B.5, output-error monotonicity (Proposition 5.4), and the composite inequality ∥δ^QEP∥ ≤ U^BASE, plus a repaired first-order version (Appendix B).
5. **Actual quantizers (Section 7):** the correction is piecewise-constant in α for nearest-codeword maps (Remark 20); exact minimization is locally harmless at full strength (Remark 21) yet Theorem 22 shows output-level domination still fails.
6. **Numerics, experiments, formalization (Sections 8–10):** Monte Carlo with two independent implementations (one library-free); a pre-registered layer-wise diagnostic on SmolLM2-135M and Pythia-160M measuring c/d on 207/207 and 46/46 linear maps; 8B experiments quantizing Qwen3-8B and Meta-Llama-3-8B to 4 bits (asymmetric uniform codebook, group size 128, ridge least-squares fit on 2048 WikiText-2 tokens, strength chosen on 4096 held-back tokens) comparing RTN, fixed α = 1/2, and four adaptive rules (local/block/network/gradient) over three calibration draws, evaluated on 16384 tokens of WikiText-2 test and Pride and Prejudice; and a Lean 4 + Mathlib formalization of the core counterexamples and identities.

## Results & Highlights

- **8B 4-bit perplexity, compensation vs. RTN (WikiText-2 test, mean over 3 calibration draws, Table 4):** fixed α = 1/2 beats RTN in all 12 model×text×draw combinations — Qwen3-8B WikiText-2 12.643 → 12.320 (−0.323), Qwen3-8B novel 12.875 → 12.618 (−0.257), Llama-3-8B WikiText-2 8.264 → 8.031 (−0.233), Llama-3-8B novel 5.786 → 5.625 (−0.161). The best adaptive rule (gradient) reaches 11.870 on Qwen3-8B WikiText-2 (−0.773 vs RTN), but **no adaptive rule beats the simple local rule consistently**: the gradient rule wins all 3 draws on Qwen3-8B/WikiText-2 and 0 of 3 on Llama-3-8B/WikiText-2, and the network rule loses to the local rule in all six Llama-3-8B comparisons despite never increasing the full-network loss on its own selection data (0 of 816 decisions).
- **Selection gains do not transfer to new text:** with 4096 selection tokens on Llama-3-8B (averaged over 2 draws), the network policy beats the local policy by 0.0210 loss/token on the selection data but is *worse* on the held-out diagnostic set (+0.0084), WikiText-2 (+0.0069) and the novel (+0.075) — the policy ordering reverses when the data change. Measured overestimate (selection gain minus diagnostic gain, relative to RTN): +0.022 (Llama-3-8B) and +0.034 (Qwen3-8B) for the network rule vs. −0.007/−0.006 for the local rule; the fixed α = 1/2 policy makes no selection-data choice and pays no such cost. Fresh (non-reused) selection windows did *not* reduce the network rule's overestimate (it grew from 0.031–0.043 to 0.070–0.083).
- **The published guarantee's failure mode is real but not observed in practice at the layer level (Table 3, α = 1/2, 4096 tokens):** across 207 SmolLM2-135M and 46 Pythia-160M linear maps on two calibration texts, the cross-term ratio c/d never exceeds 0.07 in absolute value (ranges [−0.063, 0.070] and [−0.038, 0.023]) — far below the Corollary 2 failure threshold of 3/4 — and 0 maps get worse with the rounding error held fixed; with dither (16 seeds, 8096 map-seed pairs) |c/d| stays below 0.20 and still no pair is worse. However, the correction fitted on calibration tokens makes up to 12 of 48 Pythia-160M maps worse on 2048 held-out tokens (11 and 12 of 48 on the two texts), and corrected weights leave the original row's range for 9.1–9.3% of Pythia-160M weights at α = 1/2 (17–18% at α = 1), implying overload for a range-matched bounded codebook.
- **Synthetic verification of the theory (Figures 1–2, Table 2):** in the random-teacher linear class (d = 4, depths to 64), the gain of full compensation is 18.1–26.1 dB at depth 64 for ν = 10⁻⁴…5×10⁻² against the closed-form prediction 10·log₁₀64 ≈ 18.1 dB, with 156 of 168 simulated values within two standard errors; in a two-bottleneck linear network (8→6→4→2, dither step 0.3), the predicted gain matches the measured gain within two standard errors at every α (e.g., 0.1686 predicted vs. 0.1686 ± 0.0024 measured at α = 1, uncompensated MSE 0.2991), with only 2–3% of the gain coming from reduced excitation of later noise; in Example 14's ReLU network, the simulated excess/t² is 0.071–0.076 against the proved leading coefficient 2/27 ≈ 0.074 over 10⁶ runs per t.
- **Minimal counterexample to QEP's Theorem B.5 (Theorem 17):** two scalar linear layers, W₁ = W₂ = X = 1, E = (ε, −ε): U^QEP − U^BASE = ε and QEP output error ε + ε² vs. BASE ε² for every 0 < ε < 1/2 — strictly worse, at first order, with all of the theorem's own hypotheses (including Gram and weight invertibility) satisfied; Theorem 19 shows the error grows by (β−α)ε when raising α₂, refuting monotonicity. Re-analysis of QEP's own reported numbers corroborates non-monotonicity: GPTQ at 3 bits on Llama-2-7B gives 10.881 (no QEP) → 7.898 at α = 1/2 but 8.169 at α = 1, and on Llama-2-70B 4.860 → 4.102 → 4.771; in QEP's Table 1, QEP is worse in 7 of 60 pairs, all at 2 bits with perplexity above 10³.
- **2-bit strength selection by the fixed-error quadratic (Section 9.3):** the clipped minimizer of ∥R₀ − αS∥² ranks the re-quantized strengths with mean rank correlation 0.977–0.994 across models and draws, and lowers the group's normalized residual by 6.4–9.2% relative to α = 1/2 on diagnostic data (in 47 of 48 slices); a two-point grid {1/2, 1} with a strength-0 fallback retains 98.2–99.6% of the diagnostic gain of a ten-point grid. The gradient rule costs 4.1–4.8× the construction time of the local rule.
- **Formal verification scale:** Lean 4 (4.34.1) with Mathlib at commit d13f23b — 96 modules, 969 named theorem declarations depending only on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, including Theorem 17, Theorem 19, Theorem 22 (with both numerical instances and the α₂ switch at 8/9), and ten deliberately false statements required to fail; witness certificates support near-attainment of the commutator bound to a ratio of ≈0.999994.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is single-authored by Tianhao Guo (College of Physics and Electronic Engineering, Shanxi University, Taiyuan; ORCID 0000-0002-1317-868X) — an unusual but not disqualifying provenance for a deep theory contribution; the rigor here is verifiable rather than trust-based. Credibility signals are exceptionally strong: (1) the core claims are backed by a Lean 4 + Mathlib formalization whose scope *and* limits are explicitly enumerated (what is formalized vs. paper-only is listed statement by statement); (2) all Monte Carlo results were computed twice with two independent implementations, one free of any numerical library; (3) experimental protocols, thresholds and stopping rules were fixed before measurement (pre-registration style), and the artifact includes saved witnesses with rational certificates and replay scripts; (4) the code is released (github.com/tellsgo/quantization-compensation); (5) the author discloses AI-tool assistance (Claude, ChatGPT, Codex) for counterexample development, numerics, Lean code and revisions while stating that the mathematical arguments are in the paper. The refutation of QEP's Theorem B.5 is handled with unusual care: it quotes the exact proof steps that fail, respects the original paper's first-order convention, and explicitly preserves the parts of the original analysis that remain valid. The tone is consistently conservative — "we propose no new quantizer and do not dispute reported empirical gains" — and the experiments are explicitly labeled as not satisfying the theorems' hypotheses.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High for the analysis layer, none for methods (by design — the paper proposes no quantizer). The two-trajectory moment-coupling technique (jointly propagating error and uncentred activation moments along each scheme's own path) and the exact-gain decomposition are new; the refutation of a published guarantee (QEP, Theorem B.5) with a two-scalar counterexample is a clean, decisive contribution, as is the observation that the failure is a *first-order* effect that the original proof convention cannot absorb. The connection drawn to classical roundoff-noise/error-feedback analysis of digital filters is honest about what is borrowed and what is specific (the realized-activation-dependent projection, two-trajectory comparison).
- **Rigor:** Very high — formalized counterexamples, pre-registered experiments, double implementations, explicit positive and negative controls. The proof gaps in the rebutted theorem are isolated one at a time (Example 26 separates the trajectory-substitution gap from the triangle-inequality gap).
- **Reproducibility:** Strong: public code, saved witnesses with rational intervals, exact Mathlib commit, and exhaustive protocol description (group structure, ridge λ = tr(ZZᵀ)/d, token counts). The 8B experiments use the author's own RTN-backed implementation rather than a reproduction of QEP/GPTQ/AWQ/QuIP#, which is disclosed but means the numbers are not directly comparable to published PTQ leaderboards.
- **Significance:** The positive guarantee (Theorem 8) is the first network-level ordering result for compensated vs. uncompensated layer-wise PTQ, but its hypotheses (subtractive dither, unbounded grid, linear network) describe an idealized scheme no deployed system uses — the author concedes dither's decoder-side cost and accuracy cost were "not measured" and dithered propagation was never run end-to-end on a trained model. The practical takeaways are thus mostly cautionary: local layer-wise improvement is not a network guarantee; selecting strength by full-network loss on selection data overfits (a genuinely useful negative result); and QEP's theoretical selling point over GPTAQ does not exist as stated.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) The theory–practice gap is fundamental: all theorems hold the calibration input fixed, while the largest observed losses are on held-out inputs — exactly the regime the theory says nothing about; the cause of the held-out degradation is explicitly unidentified (finite fitting data, selection-set size, the ridge, and layer interactions are confounded and not separated). (2) The stable-activations hypothesis of Theorem 15 "has not been established in trained networks", making the ReLU bound conditional. (3) The experiments' ridge/damping is not covered by any theorem — admitted in Section 11, but it means the 8B numbers illustrate rather than instantiate the theory. (4) Scope of the empirical study is narrow: two models, 2–3 draws, two texts that may appear in pretraining data (WikiText-2, Pride and Prejudice), 512-token windows with context resets rather than documents, and dequantized BF16 execution with no speed claim. (5) Counterexamples are tiny (2–3 layers, scalar or 2×2); no search for failures in large structured networks was attempted. (6) The c/d diagnostic was measured only on 135M/160M models with single-layer interventions, not along each scheme's own trajectory, and not at all on the 8B models. (7) Single-author provenance and heavy disclosed AI assistance place unusual weight on the Lean artifact — which fortunately is exactly where the most consequential claims live.

## Strengths

- Settles a precise, previously open question (does inherited-error compensation provably never hurt?) with matching positive and negative results, and an exact residual identity that makes the boundary legible: compensation helps iff the new perturbation does not cancel the inherited error.
- The refutation of QEP's Theorem B.5 is constructive, minimal, formalized in Lean, and surgically fair — it preserves the original paper's valid bounds (Corollary B.6) while invalidating only the false comparisons, and even reconciles its counterexample with QEP's own reported 3-bit numbers where α = 1 loses to α = 1/2.
- Methodological hygiene at a level rare for ML venues: pre-registered protocols, two independent numerical implementations, rational-arithmetic witness certificates, ten deliberately-false Lean controls, and an explicit "what is not formalized" list.
- The 8B experiments deliver a practically valuable negative result: full-network-loss-based strength selection overfits the selection data (overestimate +0.022/+0.034 vs. −0.007/−0.006 for the local rule), fixed α = 1/2 is a robust and essentially cost-free default, and a {1/2, 1} grid retains 98.2–99.6% of a ten-point grid's diagnostic gain.
- Complete, precise scoping: every result's hypotheses, and their failure modes, are stated before use; nothing is overclaimed.

## Limitations

- All guarantees fix the calibration input; nothing is proven about generalization to new inputs, which the experiments identify as the practically dominant failure mode (up to 12 of 48 Pythia-160M maps worse on held-out tokens; policy orderings reversing on new text).
- The positive theorem covers linear networks only, under subtractive dither on an unbounded grid — a quantizer not used in practice (dither requires decoder-side regeneration and can cost accuracy; neither cost was measured). The ReLU result requires stable activations plus commutator control, neither established for trained networks; whether E(U^QEP)² ≤ E(U^BASE)² holds under uniform dither is left open, as is any statement tolerating a small probability of activation changes.
- The experiments' ridge-regularized correction is outside the theory (P_λ is not a projection), and the 8B study is small: two models, two texts possibly seen in pretraining, window-level rather than document-level evaluation, 2–3 draws, own implementation, no wall-clock/speed evaluation (gradient rule 4.1–4.8× slower to build).
- The layer-wise diagnostic (c/d ≤ 0.07) covers two tiny models and single-layer interventions only; the cross term was not measured on the 8B models, at 2–3 bits, or along each scheme's own trajectory.
- No analysis of damping (used in the experiments), no counterexample search beyond small unstructured instances, and the cause of the held-out losses is not pinned down (fitting-data size, selection-data size, ridge, and cross-layer interaction are confounded).

## Relevance

该论文对 LLM 后训练量化（GPTQ/AWQ/QuIP/QEP/GPTAQ/Qronos 这一整族逐层补偿式方法）给出了何时安全、何时可能反噬的理论边界，并推翻了 QEP 论文赖以区分自身的核心理论保证；其"校准数据上的收益未必迁移到新文本、按全网络损失选强度会过拟合"的实证结论，对我们评估和设计量化流水线中的误差补偿策略（包括强度选择与校准协议）有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the cross-term condition 2c ≤ (2−α)d continue to hold along each scheme's own trajectory (not just one-layer interventions on a shared RTN trajectory) in 8B-class models at 2–3 bits, where the published QEP regressions (7 of 60 pairs, all at 2 bits) actually occur?
- Can Theorem 15 be extended to tolerate a small probability of activation-state changes, and can stable activations plus small commutators be certified (e.g., from teacher pre-activation margins) for real transformer blocks rather than assumed?
- What actually causes the held-out degradation — fitting-data size, selection-set size/domain, the ridge shrinkage, or cross-layer interaction — and would block-level or pipeline-style calibration (calibrating against the output of already-quantized preceding blocks, as in Qronos/GPTAQ) shrink the selection overestimate?
- Does dithered rounding with fixed feedback coefficients (the GPTQ-style sequential scheme shown to satisfy the per-layer conditions) yield a network-level guarantee end-to-end on a trained model, and what is the realized perplexity/accuracy cost of subtractive dither at 4 and 3 bits?
- Since 9–18% of corrected weights leave the original row range, would combining compensation with learned clipping ranges or AWQ-style scale search eliminate the overload failure mode of Theorem 22-class instances?
- Is the closed-form depth gain 10·log₁₀L dB (Example 11) observable in deep real networks, or do bottlenecks and row-space mismatch (which break Corollary 10) dominate in practice?
