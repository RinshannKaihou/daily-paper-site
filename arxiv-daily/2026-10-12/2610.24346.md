> **一句话总结：** 该工作提出了一个四分支反事实框架来量化"低精度 agent 在选定决策点回退到 BF16 模型"的真实任务价值（救回的任务数减去毁掉的任务数），在 τ²-bench 上用 Qwen3-4B/8B（模拟 4-bit 权重量化）发现：只替换一个动作会在两个方向翻转 15.5–20.9% 的任务结果但净收益约为零，在预注册的 34 个电信 holdout 任务上动作级回退的净收益甚至是 −4.57 pp（90% CI [−7.17, −1.96]），而换一个同样 4-bit 的 checkpoint 或仅仅调换工具参数键的顺序也能翻转同样多的结果——说明结果变化未必来自"更多位数"；只有在低比特压力设置下"动作+续写全部接管"的联合模式才出现正的净收益（8B RTN-W3 上 +18.4 pp [+9.4, +28.2]），且基于 token margin 的不确定性触发器能找到更多结果变化（20% 率下翻转 27 vs 随机 14.30，p=.0002）但其净收益与随机选择相比仍不显著。

> **TL;DR:** This paper measures what a precision fallback is actually worth in a closed-loop agent: instead of KL-disagreement routing or end-to-end scores, it forks restored states into four branches (action source × continuing model: BB/BQ/QB/QQ) and scores the signed net gain — tasks rescued minus tasks spoiled — of three upgrade modes against the quantized QQ baseline. On τ²-bench with fake-quantized Qwen3-4B/8B (GPTQ/AWQ-style W4), replacing just the first divergent action flips 15.5–20.9% of task–block units but rescues ≈ damages (pooled net gain +0.7 pp, 95% CI [−2.1, +3.4]); on the pre-registered 34-task telecom holdout the action-only estimate is negative (−4.57 pp, crossed 90% CI [−7.17, −1.96], damages 9.77% vs rescues 5.21%); joint action+continuation replacement is the only mode with positive new-task intervals, and only in two exploratory 3-block stress settings (+12.4 pp for 4B KV4-rot, +18.4 pp for 8B RTN-W3). Crucially, a second 4-bit checkpoint built from resampled calibration data produces about as much turnover as the BF16 swap (17.7% vs 16.8%, ΔM = −1.0 pp [−3.9, +1.9]), and merely reordering tool-argument keys flips 6.7% of outcomes — so outcome changes at fixed bit width are real, and attributing them to "more bits" requires same-precision references the field rarely runs.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"回退价值 = 救回 − 毁掉"的符号化任务级度量**：论证 KL 散度（衡量分歧大小但不指明方向）和端到端成功率（衡量从头运行的整个策略）都不能度量"在别的模型写好的历史中插入一次 BF16 干预"的价值；一个动作替换既可能挽救失败任务也可能毁掉成功任务，只有带符号的净收益才是正确的目标量。
- **四分支配对反事实设计（BB/BQ/QB/QQ）**：从恢复的冻结状态出发，交叉"当前动作由谁提供"与"后续由谁继续执行"，把 G（只换这一步）、H（从下一步接管）、F（联合替换）三种回退模式分离开；证明不同模式下"救援"不可互通——约 4% 的状态只有联合替换才成功（joint rescue），约 4.8% 的状态 BF16 动作配合量化模型续写能成功、换成 BF16 续写反而失败（rescue undone）。
- **同精度参照实验（Same-precision references）**：用"重采样校准数据构建的第二个 4-bit checkpoint（Q'）"和"调换工具参数键顺序（执行的操作完全不变，只变 token 序列）"两个固定比特宽度的对照，证明结果翻转并不蕴含"需要更多位数"——Q' 在各自首次分歧点的翻转率（17.7%）与 BF16（16.8%）相当，键序调换也能翻转 6.7% 的结果。
- **预注册的电信域 holdout**：34 个新任务、协议（含任务、配置、12 个 prompt 块、估计量与读数规则）在任何运行前以哈希固定，给出动作级回退的负向点估计（−4.57 pp）并完整披露两级检查与分层分析。
- **"找到敏感决策"与"选中受益决策"是两个独立检验**：冻结的 token-margin 不确定性触发器（只读量化模型输出，可在调用 BF16 前决策）在每个选择率下都捕获比随机选择更多的结果变化（20% 率下 27 vs 14.30，精确单侧 p=.0002），但经 Holm 校正后其净收益在每个率下都与随机选择无法区分——为学习型路由/升级方法提出了符号化收益检验的必要性。
- **一套可复用的确定性实验基建**：vLLM 批不变内核 + 独立进程 + 无共享前缀缓存，924/924 开发状态与 1202/1202 holdout 分歧状态通过重放审计（no-op 分支完全复现原结果），代码开源（github.com/W4ng77/cloq）。

## Key Contributions

- **A signed, task-level value metric for precision fallback**: a fallback's value is the number of tasks it rescues minus the number it spoils, measured for the intervention that will actually run. Neither KL-divergence routing signals (size of disagreement, direction unknown) nor end-to-end success (a model running from the start) measures the value of inserting the BF16 model into a history built by the quantized model.
- **A four-branch paired counterfactual design (BB/BQ/QB/QQ)** from restored frozen states, crossing which model supplies the current action with which model continues. This separates action replacement (G = Y_BQ − Y_QQ), takeover (H = Y_QB − Y_QQ) and joint replacement (F = Y_BB − Y_QQ), and shows rescues do not transfer across modes: 3.96% of W4 divergent states are joint rescues (only BB succeeds, above the +0.47 pp label-permutation reference, fixed-pool CI [+0.06, +0.87]) while 4.80% are "rescues undone" (BQ succeeds but BB fails, −0.99 pp below reference) — an action-only evaluation misses the first kind and a joint evaluation misses the second.
- **Same-precision references that separate "sensitivity" from "need for bits"**: a second W4 checkpoint Q′ built by resampling calibration states/rows from the same trajectories, and a key-reversal control that reorders tool-argument keys while keeping the executed call identical. Q′ at its own first divergence caused 17.7% turnover vs 16.8% for BF16 (ΔM = −1.0 pp, [−3.9, +1.9], ±3 pp equivalence unresolved); key reversal alone flipped 6.7% of 300 eligible states.
- **A pre-registered telecom holdout** (34 unseen tasks, 12 blocks, hash-locked protocol) that yields a negative action-only estimate (−4.57 pp, crossed 90% CI [−7.17, −1.96]) with both registered secondary checks passing (rescues and damages both positive; cross-half fragility correlation 0.588).
- **A clean separation of "finding changes" from "finding benefit"**: a frozen minimum-token-margin trigger, selected on one development block before validation, captures more outcome changes than matched-count random selection at every rate (clearest at 20%: 27 vs 14.30 turnovers, exact one-sided p = .0002), yet its net benefit stays unresolved after Holm correction at every rate (5% rate nominal p = .0345 → Holm p = .1035).
- **Deterministic, audited infrastructure**: vLLM with batch-invariant kernels, deterministic compilation and separate server processes without shared prefix cache; all 924 sampled development states and all 1202 holdout divergent states passed outcome-independent replay audits; code released at https://github.com/W4ng77/cloq.

## Methodology

The core instrument is the **fork**: before decision t of the quantized model's trajectory, the full state s_t is saved — message history, the user simulator's visible conversation, step/error budgets, and the replayed environment and device databases. Four complete task executions then start from identical prompt tokens: BB (BF16 action, BF16 continues), BQ (BF16 action, quantized continues), QB (quantized action, BF16 takes over), QQ (the quantized baseline). An "action" is a complete assistant turn including language and structured tool calls; its message and tool results enter subsequent history. Each task–block unit receives one intervention, because a router that triggers repeatedly is a different policy needing end-to-end evaluation.

Three state sources feed the forks: (1) **first divergent states** — the earliest decision where the two models differ in action kind, tool calls or normalized arguments (text-only differences skipped); (2) **ordinary states** — one hash-selected decision per task and block, fixed before generation, independent of disagreement and outcome; (3) **common states** — ordinary states at which BF16, Q and the second checkpoint Q′ all supply the current action under the same continuation, measuring K(c) = V(B,c) − V(Q′,c), the BF16 action's added value over another W4 action.

The setup: τ²-bench in text mode (58 retail + 27 airline development tasks × 20 prompt blocks per W4 configuration; 80 telecom development tasks; 34-task telecom holdout), Qwen3-4B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B with thinking disabled, one greedy Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct user simulator for all agents. Quantization is **simulated** (fake-quantized checkpoints served with BF16 compute): GPTQ-W4 or AWQ-style W4 with group size 128, INT8 KV-cache (KV8) configurations, and five lower-bit stress settings (W3, rotated 4-bit KV cache, RTN). Calibration uses BF16 agent trajectories from 57 tasks disjoint from the development panel; the second checkpoint Q′ redraws states and activation rows from the same trajectories. Retail/airline success requires final-database match plus normal termination; telecom uses the official task-basis success.

Inference is careful: paired fixed-pool intervals (block bootstrap) vs "new-task" crossed task×block bootstrap intervals; an interval containing zero leaves an effect unresolved; equivalence is tested by TOST at ±3 pp; joint-rescue / rescue-undone patterns are compared against a label-permutation reference that keeps the per-state number of successful branches. Replay audits re-execute no-op branches and verify identical outcomes. Scope checks extend to BFCL multi-turn, ALFWorld (no LLM user) and Mistral-Small-3.2-24B. The frozen selection rule (minimum token margin over the quantized action, thresholded per configuration at 5/10/20% of states) is validated on two later blocks of the same development tasks against matched-count random selection and a matched hindsight oracle that bounds any selector.

## Results & Highlights

- **Action-only replacement flips many outcomes but nets ~zero on development**: at the first divergent state of four W4 configurations on τ²-bench retail+airline (85 tasks × 20 blocks), BF16 action replacement changed 15.5–20.9% of task–block units (pooled turnover 18.6%), with pooled rescues 9.6% vs damages 9.0% and a full-pool net gain of **+0.7 pp with new-task 95% CI [−2.1, +3.4] covering zero**; per-configuration fixed-pool estimates ranged from −1.9 pp (4B GPTQ-W4, [−3.7, −0.1]) to +2.9 pp (8B GPTQ-W4, [+1.4, +4.4]) but every new-task interval covered zero.
- **The pre-registered telecom holdout is negative for action-only fallback**: on 34 unseen telecom tasks × 12 blocks across three W4 configurations, damages (9.77%) exceeded rescues (5.21%), giving a full-pool net gain of **−4.57 pp with crossed 90% CI [−7.17, −1.96], entirely below zero and outside the registered ±3 pp small-effect region** (the registered reading categories had no "negative" class, so the protocol labels it inconclusive); both secondary checks passed (rescue/damage positivity, cross-half fragility correlation 0.588), and all 1202 divergent states passed full QQ replay.
- **Joint replacement is the only mode with positive intervals, and only under stress**: on divergent states the W4 pool gives G = +0.5, H = +0.2, F = +0.7 pp (all new-task CIs cover zero; 5,576 states), but in the five exploratory 3-block lower-bit stress settings joint replacement F reached **+12.4 pp [+3.0, +21.6] on 4B KV4-rot and +18.4 pp [+9.4, +28.2] on 8B RTN-W3**, while the other three settings stayed unresolved; in RTN-W3 the takeover mode H = +15.3 pp did the work with G ≈ 0.
- **Outcome changes occur at fixed bit width**: substituting the second 4-bit checkpoint Q′ at its own first divergence caused **17.7% turnover vs 16.8% for the BF16 substitution on the matched 1020-unit W4 panel (ΔM = −1.0 pp, 95% CI [−3.9, +1.9]; ±3 pp equivalence unresolved)**; when the substitute also continued, BF16 flipped more units than Q′ (193 vs 149, +4.3 pp [+0.4, +8.3]); key-order reversal alone flipped **6.7% of 300 eligible states (10 rescues, 10 damages, net 0.0)** vs 9.0% for the BF16 action, paired difference +2.3 pp [−2.9, +8.4] unresolved — while the executed tool call stayed identical.
- **The BF16 action's added value over another W4 checkpoint remains unresolved**: at 510 common Qwen3-8B states, K(Q) = **+1.0 pp (two-look 97% CI [−4.2, +6.2])** with the quantized model continuing and K(B) = +0.8 pp [−2.2, +3.8] with BF16 continuing — both intervals cover zero yet extend past the ±3 pp equivalence margin, so a useful BF16 advantage of a few points remains possible; Q′ actually departed from the quantized action slightly more often than BF16 did (46.1% vs 44.3% of states).
- **Finding changes ≠ finding benefit**: the frozen margin trigger on 1020 validation states (38 rescues, 31 damages) captured **27 turnovers at the 20% rate vs 14.30 expected under matched random selection (exact one-sided p = .0002)**, but its net gain (+5 successes vs +2.38 random) was unresolved after Holm correction (p = .2617; the 5% rate's nominal p = .0345 became Holm p = .1035) — far below the hindsight oracle bound (+34 to +38 successes).
- **End-to-end context**: quantized models are not even reliably worse here — Qwen3-4B GPTQ-W4 scored **40.0% end-to-end success vs 35.8% for its own BF16 version (+4.2 pp, CI [−2.4, +10.5])** on the same tasks and blocks, and the pooled Q−BF16 difference of +1.5 pp [−2.7, +5.6] failed TOST equivalence at ±3 pp.
- **Scope checks**: BFCL multi-turn action-only net gain pooled to +0.1 pp [−1.0, +1.2] with one negative configuration (4B AWQ-style W4: −3.8 pp [−6.5, −1.3]); ALFWorld 8B pool −3.7 pp [−9.7, +1.9]; Mistral-Small-3.2-24B GPTQ-W4 at its own ordinary states rescued 4.6% and damaged 5.4% of states with every mode's net value unresolved; under a second user simulator (Sonnet 5.5), only 7 of 39 text-difference flips kept their direction, 6 reversed and 26 vanished.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are Sicheng Lyu and Xinyu Wang (equal contribution) and Yu Gu from McGill University, Lei Ding (University of Manitoba), Ziyang Song (Ohio University), and Xiao-Wen Chang (McGill, corresponding author) — a numerical-linear-algebra / scientific-computing group, which fits the paper's unusually careful treatment of interval estimation, TOST equivalence testing, Holm correction and label-permutation references. Credibility signals are strong throughout: the telecom holdout protocol was hash-registered before any run and the analysis script refuses data on hash mismatch; all forks passed outcome-independent no-op replay audits (924/924 development, 1202/1202 holdout states); serving is deterministic (byte-identical episodes across nodes and concurrency levels; 4,113 of 4,114 reused arms token-identical); post-hoc amendments after interim looks are dated and listed (Appendix A.6), and the same-precision reading was deliberately made stricter after an interim look. Code is released at https://github.com/W4ng77/cloq. The main risks the authors themselves flag are over-reading small forked-state gains and the gap to deployment certification.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The paired four-branch design crossing action source with continuation at a frozen state is, as far as the related work shows, new for precision fallback; the closest prior work either swaps action+continuation together (Replay Gap) or compares quantized vs BF16 agents episode-by-episode (Jang et al., 2026). The same-precision references (second checkpoint Q′, key-order reversal) are a genuinely novel control that most quantization papers lack.
- **Rigor**: Above average for this area — pre-registration with hashes, exact permutation tests, Holm correction, TOST equivalence margins, and honest reporting of unresolved effects. However, the pre-registered reading categories for the holdout contained "positive" and "small" but **no "negative" category**, so a 90% interval entirely below zero ([−7.17, −1.96]) is classified "inconclusive" — a pre-registration gap that conveniently softens the paper's own headline negative result, even though the authors are transparent about it.
- **External validity / red flags**: (1) All quantization is **simulated** (fake-quantized weights served with BF16 compute on a deterministic stack); deployed low-bit kernels — the actual reason one wants fallback — are explicitly out of scope, and one real AWQ kernel path was found not replay-exact and dropped. (2) The crossed designs cover only Qwen3-4B/8B on τ²-bench; the Llama-3.1-8B check was abandoned at its gate because BF16 solved only 5/120 gate items (no outcome variance), and Qwen3-14B/32B have end-to-end runs only. (3) The negative telecom holdout **confounds domain shift with task shift** — the 8B configurations gave +2.7/−0.6 pp on 80 telecom development tasks vs −5.6/−6.6 pp on the holdout — and the authors admit the cause is open. (4) The telecom development comparison rests on six blocks, giving intervals wide enough ([−4.7, +10.1]) to hide the effect. (5) The stress results (the paper's only positive F intervals) are 3-block exploratory cells with very wide CIs, and the common-state design needed a post-first-look extension from three to six blocks under a two-look rule the authors themselves note only partly controls error. (6) One greedy user simulator throughout; under a second simulator most text-difference flip labels vanished or reversed (7/39 kept), so individual labels are simulator-specific. (7) The end-to-end table shows the quantized 4B model *above* BF16 (+4.2 pp), which undercuts the framing of BF16 as an "upgrade" reference at this scale.

## Strengths

- Reframes precision fallback evaluation around the correct estimand — the signed net task benefit of the intervention that will actually run — and demonstrates concretely (joint rescues ≈ 4%, undone rescues ≈ 4.8%) why disagreement-based routing and action-only or joint-only evaluations each miss rescues the other mode would deliver.
- The same-precision references are a reusable methodological contribution: a second 4-bit checkpoint and a key-reversal control that changes only tokens, not the executed operation, both reproduce the "fallback changes outcomes" phenomenon without any extra bits — a direct caution for the precision-routing literature.
- Exceptional measurement hygiene: hash-registered holdout, no-op replay audits, deterministic serving, block/task-separated bootstrap intervals, permutation references for outcome patterns, and dated disclosure of every post-hoc amendment.
- Honest negative and unresolved reporting throughout; the paper resists claiming a BF16 advantage its intervals cannot support and explicitly warns against certifying quantized agents from small forked-state gains.

## Limitations

- Simulated quantization on a deterministic BF16-compute serving stack: no deployed low-bit kernels, no latency, no fallback-cost accounting (a takeover uses many more BF16 decisions than one borrowed action — unmeasured).
- Crossed conclusions cover Qwen3-4B/8B on τ²-bench only; BFCL, ALFWorld and Mistral-Small-3.2-24B are scope checks, and the Llama-3.1-8B family was dropped at its gate.
- All τ²-bench episodes use a single greedy Qwen3-30B-A3B user simulator; individual rescue/damage labels changed under an alternative simulator (7/39 flips kept direction), so the averages describe this simulator, and the simulator control itself changed model, sampling and relay interface together.
- The telecom holdout changed domain and tasks simultaneously, leaving the cause of its −4.57 pp estimate open; the proposed next measurement (three holdout configurations on the 80 telecom development tasks × 20 blocks) was not run.
- Positive joint-replacement results appear only in exploratory 3-block stress settings (W3/KV4-rot/RTN) with wide confidence intervals; the common-state BF16-vs-Q′ comparison was extended after an unresolved first look and its equivalence stays unclaimed.
- One intervention per task–block is measured; a deployed router that fires repeatedly is a different policy requiring end-to-end evaluation, which the paper does not provide for any router.

## 相关性

该论文直接命中"LLM 量化"与"LLM Agent/工具使用评测"两个核心兴趣的交叉点：它为混合精度路由（precision routing / escalation）提供了正确的评估方法论——带符号的任务级净收益 + 同精度参照，而不是 KL 分歧或端到端均分——并给出了量化 agent 在 τ²-bench/BFCL/ALFWorld 上反事实分支的实证证据。对任何研究"量化模型何时值得回退到全精度"或"agent 评测噪声"的工作，这既是方法模板也是警示（结果翻转 ≠ 需要更多位数）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the action-only negative estimate (−4.57 pp on telecom holdout) survive when domain and task shift are separated — i.e., running the same three W4 configurations on the 80 telecom development tasks over 20 blocks, as the authors propose?
- Do the conclusions transfer to real deployed low-bit kernels (real W4 AWQ/GPTQ kernels, Marlin-style paths), where kernel numerics — not just weight rounding — drive divergence, and where fallback latency/cost interacts with the mode choice (a takeover costs many BF16 decisions)?
- Can a learned router be trained directly against the signed net-benefit target (rather than KL labels), and would it beat the margin trigger's unresolved net value on truly held-out tasks and states?
- Why does the hybrid branch (BF16 action + quantized continuation) so often fail to rescue tasks that BB rescues — is it the "handoff tax" of continuing a non-native trajectory, and can action formatting (e.g., matching the quantized model's style) reduce undone rescues?
- Is the ~4% joint-rescue and ~4.8% rescue-undone structure stable across model scales (14B/32B/70B) and families, or does it grow with the BF16–Q capability gap that larger models might not even exhibit (given the 4B quantized model already matched or beat BF16 end-to-end)?
- Could the key-reversal and second-checkpoint references be adopted as standard controls in quantized-agent benchmarks to distinguish "precision-sensitive" from merely "token-sensitive" outcomes?
