> **一句话总结：** RoLaQ 针对 MLA（多头潜在注意力）共享潜在缓存这一"一份缓存同时供 K/V 两条路径使用"的结构，在冻结模型权重的前提下联合学习共享旋转（按 K/V 投影能量划分子空间）与量化范围（阈值决定增益、强制单位线性响应 κ=1），在三个 MLA 模型、六个基准上取得比较方法中最高的 2-bit 平均精度（无 token 保护时超出最强基线 1.1–3.8 个点），SGLang 实现在 DeepSeek-V2-Lite 上将同等显存预算下的 KV 容量提升 3.96 倍、256K 输入解码吞吐提升 2.51 倍。

> **TL;DR:** RoLaQ is an offline, weight-frozen calibration method that jointly learns a K/V-projection-aware shared rotation and a per-layer quantization threshold (with gain coupled as g=1/C(T) to keep unit reconstruction response) for the 2-bit content latent of Multi-head Latent Attention. On MMLU/CMMLU/TriviaQA/GSM8K/HumanEval/MBPP across DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Moonlight-16B-A3B and MiniCPM3-4B it beats the strongest 2-bit baseline (QuaRot or OSCAR) by +3.8/+2.8/+1.3 points in six-task average without token protection — including GSM8K on DeepSeek-V2-Lite jumping from OSCAR's 18.3 to 32.1 — and its SGLang INT2 implementation delivers 3.96x BF16 KV capacity (4.56M vs 1.15M tokens) and 2.51x decode throughput at 256K input (67.7 vs 27.0 tok/s) on one A100 80GB, though throughput at 1K input is 0.88x (slower than BF16).

## 核心贡献

- **针对 MLA 共享缓存的联合校准框架**：首次（相对既有 MHA/GQA 方法）把 MLA 中"同一份 2-bit 内容潜在向量同时决定注意力匹配（K）与数值读出（V）"的双角色误差结构作为一等公民，联合校准旋转基与量化范围，而非只调其中之一。
- **K/V 感知的共享旋转搜索空间**：以 OSCAR 的协方差旋转为初始化，在 H = HK + HV（迹归一化）的联合谱基上按特征值高低拆成两个等维子空间，各自学习反对称生成元；可训练参数仅 d²/2（完整指数旋转的一半），且验证 KL 反而比全参数旋转低 6.3%/9.1%（DS-Lite/Moonlight）。
- **响应保持的范围校准**：利用 Bussgang 分解将量化重建拆为线性响应 κx 与残余噪声，通过 g(T)=1/C(T) 强制 κ=1（MSE 最优增益会导致 κ≈0.88 的信号衰减）；每层只学阈值 T，增益由约束导出。MiniCPM3 上 κ=1 耦合将验证 KL 从自由增益的 0.606 降到 0.527。
- **双层监督的联合校准目标**：BF16 教师提供逐 token 分布（KL）与逐层注意力输出（归一化 Frobenius 损失），L = L_KL + 0.1·L_attn；1.43M Pile token、单卡 A100 约 22–34 分钟完成。
- **可落地的 INT2 推理实现**：读侧旋转吸收进 MLA 投影（无独立旋转乘法），SGLang + Triton 打包 INT2 写入与注意力核，带来容量、吞吐与 P99 TTFT 的实际收益。

## Key Contributions

- **Joint calibration designed for MLA's one-cache-two-roles structure**: instead of treating K and V caches separately (KIVI/KVQuant/GEAR/QuaRot) or only rotating (OSCAR), RoLaQ recognizes that a single perturbation of the shared latent hits both attention matching and value readout, and calibrates the latent basis and quantizer together.
- **K/V-aware shared rotation**: starting from OSCAR's covariance rotation R0, it diagonalizes R0ᵀ(HK+HV)R0, splits the eigenbasis into two equal-dimensional high/low joint-energy subspaces, and learns a skew-symmetric generator in each — d²/2 trainable parameters, half of a full d×d generator, orthogonal throughout, exporting to the same dense-rotation inference kernels.
- **Response-preserving range calibration**: via the Bussgang decomposition g·aT(x) = κx + ε, the reconstruction gain is coupled to the threshold as g(T) = 1/C(T) to enforce unit linear response κ = 1; per-layer thresholds (bounded [0.5, 1.25]) adapt the four-level assignments while learned thresholds concentrate around T ≈ 0.91/0.89/0.85, below OSCAR's Lloyd–Max 0.981.
- **Two-level supervision with a frozen model**: a BF16 teacher supplies next-token distributions (prediction KL over the whole model) and per-layer attention outputs (energy-normalized), combined as L = L_KL + λL_attn with λ = 0.1; straight-through estimators carry gradients through the hard quantizer; calibration uses 1.43M Pile tokens and takes ~22 min (16B models) / 34 min (MiniCPM3) on one A100.
- **Practical SGLang serving**: packed INT2 cache writes with fused read-side rotations folded into MLA's absorbed projections (q̂h = qhWK,hR; ŴO = R⊤WV⊤WO), removing standalone rotation multiplications when attending to history.

## Methodology

RoLaQ quantizes MLA's content latent c_t to 2 bits (RoPE key k_t^R stays BF16) with a group size of G = 128 standardized coordinates and a four-level affine grid {±a_in, ±a_out} (a_out = 3a_in) with boundaries (−T, 0, T), initialized from OSCAR's Lloyd–Max constants (T0 ≈ 0.9811, g0 = 1.16).

**Rotation (§3.1).** Two empirical observations motivate the design: (1) the leading half of the eigenvectors of trace-normalized H = HK + HV captures 62–75% of each projection's energy across the three models, so error direction matters; (2) with OSCAR's rotation/threshold fixed, sweeping only the gain reduces DeepSeek-V2-Lite's next-token KL to BF16 by 52%, so reconstruction scale matters. The refinement R = R0·U·diag(e^S1, e^S2)·U⊤ learns skew-symmetric S_i within the two spectral subspaces (zero-init recovers R0), keeping R orthogonal throughout training and exporting as one dense matrix per layer.

**Range (§3.2).** For standardized coordinate x, reconstruction is g·aT(x) = κx + ε with κ = g·C(T) and C(T) = E[x·aT(x)] in closed form; setting g(T) = 1/C(T) anchors the aligned component (κ = 1) while letting each layer's threshold T_ℓ move the decision boundaries. This matters because the MSE-optimal gain yields κ ≈ 0.88 near T = 1, i.e., pure reconstruction minimization silently attenuates the shared signal that both K matching and V readout reuse.

**Calibration and inference (§3.3).** All layers' generators and thresholds are optimized jointly on 1.43M Pile tokens (sequences 512–4,096; Adam; 427 updates; seeds 42/43/44) with the backbone frozen; checkpoints are selected by held-out prediction KL relative to OSCAR across short/2K/4K contexts. No tokens are protected during calibration, so the learned parameters see the quantizer over whole sequences, and the same checkpoints are reused across all protection budgets. At inference the read-side rotation folds into MLA's projections so both matching and readout operate in the rotated basis with no extra rotation multiplies; per unprotected token, storage is d/4 + 8d/G + 2d_r bytes per layer (288 bytes for the 16B models, 144 for MiniCPM3).

## Results & Highlights

- **Six-task average without token protection** (MMLU/CMMLU/TriviaQA/GSM8K/HumanEval/MBPP): RoLaQ reaches **46.7% on DeepSeek-V2-Lite vs 42.9% for QuaRot (+3.8)**, **60.1% on Moonlight-16B-A3B vs 57.3% for OSCAR (+2.8)**, and **59.4% on MiniCPM3-4B vs 58.1% for QuaRot (+1.3**; the paper summarizes the range as 1.1–3.8 across settings/rounding). It is the best 2-bit method on all three models both without and with 4-sink + 16-recent BF16 protection (47.7 vs QuaRot's 45.3 on DS-Lite).
- **Largest single-task gain**: GSM8K on DeepSeek-V2-Lite (no protection) improves from **OSCAR's 18.3 to 32.1 (+13.8 points)**, against BF16's 37.4; notably, RoLaQ *without* any protection (46.7 avg) already beats the strongest baseline *with* 20 protected tokens (QuaRot, 45.3). Case study: across 1319 GSM8K problems, RoLaQ solves 277 that OSCAR misses and misses 69 that OSCAR solves.
- **Long-context RULER retrieval beyond the 4K calibration length**: at 16K on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, RoLaQ averages **73.7% vs 67.4% for KVarN (+6.3)**, with the MK2 multi-key task jumping from **OSCAR's 7.0 to 55.0 (+48)**; at 8K on Moonlight it reaches 94.8% vs BF16's 96.2% and OSCAR's 94.2%.
- **Serving on one A100 80GB (SGLang, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, 4 concurrent requests)**: same-memory-budget KV capacity of **4.56M vs 1.15M tokens for BF16 (3.96×)**; decode throughput of **67.7 vs 27.0 tok/s with 256K input (2.51×)** and **84.4 vs 34.8 tok/s over the 128K–256K output interval (2.43×)**; P99 TTFT at 256 concurrent requests falls **37.8%, from 588.6 s to 366.0 s**.
- **Ablations confirm each piece**: joint rotation+range gives DS-Lite MMLU 55.9 / MBPP 44.0 vs 52.0 / 39.4 at initialization; rotation improves MBPP by +2.2 with fixed range but +4.0 with learned range; the KL + attention-output objective beats KL-only (48.1 vs 47.5 avg on DS-Lite; 62.6 vs 62.0 on Moonlight); the H_K+H_V search space with half the parameters cuts validation KL by 6.3%/9.1% vs full exponential rotation and beats a random-subspace control; κ = 1 is the KL-optimal response across contexts 2K–32K, and coupling reduces MiniCPM3's validation KL from 0.606±0.031 (free gain) to 0.527±0.008.

The paper is unusually transparent for a systems-adjacent quantization paper: full baseline implementations (RTN/QuaRot/KVarN/OSCAR formulas spelled out in Appendix A.2), per-seed tables with standard deviations (Appendix C.1), explicit calibration hyperparameters (1.43M Pile tokens, 427 Adam updates, learning rates, λ = 0.1), an AI-assistance disclosure, and a public code release at github.com/noforit/RoLaQ. The evaluation spans MoE and dense architectures (DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Moonlight-16B-A3B, MiniCPM3-4B), simulated-quantization accuracy plus a real SGLang deployment with hardware measurements — a combination many quantization papers skip.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: incremental but well-motivated relative to OSCAR and OptR — RoLaQ *initializes from OSCAR's rotation and grid* and adds two learned components (subspace rotation refinement, coupled threshold/gain). The genuinely new ideas are the H_K+H_V spectral subspace parameterization (halving trainable parameters while improving KL) and the κ = 1 response constraint derived from the Bussgang decomposition, which cleanly explains *why* MSE-optimal gains attenuate the signal (κ_MSE ≈ 0.88). The framing of MLA's dual-role error propagation is clear and correct.
- **Rigor**: strong on average — three seeds with stds, component/objective/search-space ablations, a random-subspace control, case studies, and honest reporting of the motivating gain-sweep (52% KL reduction). But serving results use **one timed run per workload** (Appendix D), and code-task variance across seeds is non-trivial (HumanEval std up to 2.66 on Moonlight), so some per-task deltas (e.g., ±1–2 points) are within noise; the paper wisely leans on six-task averages.
- **A specific overclaim risk**: the 2.51×/3.96× serving numbers come from the packed INT2 kernels that **OSCAR also uses** — Figure 6 explicitly notes OSCAR's curves reuse RoLaQ's timings — so the *speedup* is a property of 2-bit caching in SGLang, not of the calibration; RoLaQ's contribution there is only preserving accuracy. Also, at 1K–2K inputs the speedup is **0.88× (slower than BF16)** because quantize/unpack overhead dominates — the headline hides a crossover around 8K.
- **Generalization caveats**: gains shrink with token protection (+2.4/+1.0/+0.9 with 4+16 on the three models) and on the strongest setting (Moonlight with protection, 62.3 vs OSCAR's 61.3, is only +1.0); RULER at 16K on DS-Lite still leaves an **11-point gap to BF16 (73.7 vs 84.7)**, and the MK3 task collapses to 14.3 vs BF16's 47.0. Concurrent MLA-specific methods QuantMLA and SnapMLA are cited but not compared.
- **Red flags**: none serious. Minor: KVarN's catastrophic no-protection collapse on Moonlight (26.4 avg) suggests the baseline comparison there may be fragile; calibration data is Pile-only, so distribution shift is examined only indirectly via RULER length transfer, not domain transfer.

## Strengths

- Correctly identifies and exploits the structural uniqueness of MLA (one latent, two consumers) rather than porting MHA/GQA tricks wholesale.
- The κ = 1 response constraint is theoretically grounded (Bussgang), cheap (zero extra learnable parameters — gain is derived from the threshold), and validated both as a gain sweep at fixed rotation and as reduced seed variance (0.527±0.008 vs 0.606±0.031 on MiniCPM3).
- Compact rotation search (d²/2 params in two spectral blocks) that beats the unrestricted exponential/Cayley alternatives and exports to the same dense kernels — no inference-time penalty for the structured parameterization.
- Read-side rotations fold into MLA's absorbed projections, so attention over cached history needs no standalone rotation multiplies.
- Ends-to-end validation: accuracy (3 models × 6 benchmarks × 2 protection settings × 3 seeds), long-context retrieval beyond the calibration length, ablations, qualitative case studies, and a deployed SGLang implementation with capacity/throughput/tail-latency numbers.
- Cheap calibration: ~22–34 minutes on one A100 with model weights frozen, and checkpoints reused across protection budgets.

## Limitations

- Only the content latent is quantized; the RoPE key stays BF16, and positional keys plus group metadata remain a substantial share of the cache (288 bytes/token/layer for the 16B models) — the authors themselves flag coordinated content+positional quantization as open.
- Serving benefits are workload- and hardware-dependent: sub-8K contexts are *slower* than BF16 (0.88× at 1K), and all measurements are single-GPU A100 numbers for DeepSeek-V2-Lite only; kernels for H100/Blackwell or tensor-parallel setups are untested.
- Residual accuracy gap to BF16 is large at 2 bits without protection (e.g., DS-Lite 46.7 vs 49.4 avg; GSM8K 32.1 vs 37.4; RULER 16K 73.7 vs 84.7), and multi-key retrieval (MK3 14.3 vs 47.0) degrades severely.
- Calibration relies on a BF16 teacher and Pile text; sensitivity to calibration-domain shift (code-heavy, multilingual, agentic traces) is not measured.
- No comparison against the concurrent MLA-specific quantizers it cites (QuantMLA, SnapMLA), leaving the head-to-head state of the art unsettled.

## Relevance

对关注大模型推理效率与低成本长上下文服务的研究非常有价值：MLA 已是 DeepSeek-V3、GLM-5.3 等主流模型的标配注意力结构，RoLaQ 提供了在 2-bit KV 缓存上几乎不损精度、可直接在 SGLang 中落地的旋转+范围联合校准方案，与量化、KV 压缩和高效推理方向的兴趣高度契合。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the decoupled RoPE key be quantized jointly with the content latent under a coordinated content+positional precision scheme, and what does that do to attention's ability to distinguish positions?
- Does calibrating on domain-matched data (code, multilingual, agentic traces) instead of Pile close more of the remaining gap on HumanEval/GSM8K-style tasks?
- Why do learned thresholds concentrate below the Lloyd–Max value (T ≈ 0.91/0.89/0.85) — is narrower central assignment with compensating gain a general property of attention-consumed signals?
- Do the capacity/throughput gains persist on H100/Blackwell with FP8/INT4 hardware paths and with tensor parallelism > 1, where the unpacking bottleneck shifts?
- Can the rotation+range calibration transfer to 1-bit or per-layer mixed-precision grids, and does the κ = 1 constraint remain optimal at other bitwidths?
- How does RoLaQ interact with prefix caching, speculative decoding, and cache-aware scheduling in SGLang — do stale calibrated statistics under distribution drift hurt long-running serving?
