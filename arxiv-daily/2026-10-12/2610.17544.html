Why this matters

Silent staleness is the core safety risk of agent memories, skills, and caches — this gives deployable trust-and-retire rules and a certification budget.

直击智能体记忆、技能库与缓存在事实变化后的「静默错误」痛点，方案可直接落地。

⚠ Critical edge

The 7–13.5× headline compares against idealized ExpeL/ACE transplants — we idealize their LLM-issued votes as one vote per verified use — not the released systems, whose LLM judges may vote differently.

Methodology — memory as a sequential test

Queries instantiate templates (KB relation paths, lemma chains, tool-call sequences) costing m ≥ 2 LLM calls live. A verified trace compiles into a one-step macro; folding pays only above break-even r* = a − (m−1)/L (a = live accuracy, L = error price). A fraction p of queries is audited with exact verifier outcomes.

  1. Retirement = per-memory LR-CUSUM. Theorem 1: every clipped counter is a CUSUM for an implied change; symmetric ±1 votes test the wrong alternative (delay grows as A0.373, not ln A; unclipped counters never retire with probability 0.50–0.89). Corollary 2: weight by likelihood ratio — one failure ≈ 7 successes at q₀ 0.05→0.25.
  2. Certification = betting certificate (Thm 6). Bet on the paired utility of shadow answers vs live reasoning on the same query (IPW e-process); fold when wealth ≥ 1/δ — δ-safe with no live-accuracy estimate (plug-in SPRTs overshoot false folds to 0.34).
  3. Restart & invalidate. Every edit notice restarts certificates; large edits re-form folded macros; the LR-CUSUM is the safety net for unannounced edits.

Budget law Θ = ν·ln(1/δ)/(p·KL) — the share any δ-safe policy must leave to multi-step reasoning; Zipf gap κs·Θ1−1/s. Replay: identical 200k-query Zipf streams · 5 open LLMs (Qwen3-8B/4B, Granite-3.3-8B, Mistral-7B, OLMo-3-7B) · KQA-S TREAM + Wikidata 1992–2016 + 521 EvalPlus tasks.

  • Accuracy up, cost down: +14–33 points over live reasoning across all five models; Qwen3-8B hits 0.648 with 2.56 vs 3.26 calls/query.
  • Limit law matched: restarted-SPRT coverage gap sits at 0.945–1.025× the predicted Fπ(Θ) across 2,520 settings; log-log slopes within 0.008 of the Zipf exponent.
  • LR weighting matters: at 0.05→0.25 failure rate, +7/−1 scoring detects 5.7–13× faster than symmetric votes; per-memory weights beat the best fixed global weight.
  • Honest boundary: on mild natural Wikidata edits (median 3.3% of answers change per year), counters are 3.5 points more accurate — staleness is rare enough that folding still pays. See caveat ↑

Strengths

  • One clean frame — contraction as a sequential test — turns engineering folklore (“when can an agent trust its memory?”) into precise detection & certification theory.
  • The diagnosis is constructive: the math that condemns symmetric counters also yields the fix (LR weights, one failure ≈ 7 successes).
  • Certification-load limit gives practitioners a budget formula pricing how much reasoning any safe system can skip, with a Zipf law for which memories certify.
  • Unusually adversarial evaluation: replayed identical streams, explicit stale-answer counts, missed-notice tests, and the regime where the method loses.
  • Safety is distribution-free over live accuracy and error correlations — and empirically validated (0 / 5,779 false certifications).

Limitations

  • Requires an exact verifier (execution, unit test, exact match); noisy or LLM-judge verifiers — the norm for open-ended text memories — are future work.
  • Evidence from five open models ≤ 8B; KQA-S TREAM is the authors’ own benchmark with synthetic, near-total edits — frontier-scale generalization untested.
  • Per-epoch δ-safety needs change notices covering every change of the certificate’s null (including live-reasoning drift); without an edit log, only per-decision safety survives.
  • Certification costs are real: shadow audits add 17% LLM calls on code; zero-utility ties halve one-step coverage (0.61 → 0.32); mild-staleness invalidation costs 1.9 accuracy points.
  • Baselines are idealized vote-per-audit transplants — the headline multiples are about the retention rule, not full ExpeL/ACE systems.

Questions for further exploration

Do the guarantees survive noisy or LLM-judge verifiers, where audits are no longer exact Bernoulli draws?

Do frontier-scale models shrink the certification load — or does lower q₀ make partial staleness harder to detect?

Can the memory’s own detector replace edit-log notices (restart-only), trading per-epoch safety for independence?

Does the Zipf scaling law κs·Θ1−1/s predict coverage on real production traffic, where demand may not be Zipf?

Do stale intermediate macros propagate differently through downstream reasoning than stale answer caches?
