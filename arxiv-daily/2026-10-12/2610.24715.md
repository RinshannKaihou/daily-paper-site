> **一句话总结：** 本文不研究循环为何开始、而研究循环何时"锁死"（再无逃逸概率）：作者证明 top-p 截断会在续写概率达到阈值 π 时把位置精确锁死、有界惩罚只能抬高锁死门槛，并据此预测出广泛部署的 DRY 采样器存在"盲区"——序列分隔符（换行/冒号/引号/星号）把惩罚封顶在 λb^(g−1−a)，导致 JSON 记录、键值行等短分隔片段（g=6 时 66% 的锁死单元在 DRY 下仍锁死，1,780 次 llama.cpp 自由生成中幸存的全部 109 个循环均有 g≤7）；提出的 DRY-C 增加随逐字复现次数指数增长的计数项后，720 次结构化生成中无一循环跑到长度上限。

> **TL;DR:** This paper analyzes when an already-started verbatim repetition loop still has an exit, proving that top-p truncation locks a position exactly when the loop-continuation probability q reaches the threshold π (e.g., pure sampling locks 0% of 150 teacher-forced units per model vs 80–100% under top-p 0.9 on 11 open models), and that DRY's sequence breakers cap its penalty at λb^(g−1−a) — leaving 66% of locked units with breaker-free run g=6 still locked under DRY vs 1% at g=8 in a dose–response test, and all 109 loops surviving DRY in llama.cpp free generation having g≤7. The proposed DRY-C adds an exponential count-of-recurrences term, eliminating every run-to-the-limit loop across 720 structured-output generations (59→0 on Qwen3-4B, 52→0 on Llama-3.1-8B) while keeping task completion close to DRY's, at the cost of hurting explicitly requested verbatim copying (exact completion falls from >90% to ~10%).

## 核心贡献

- **从"入口"转向"出口"的问题重构：** 以往工作研究循环为何开始（自我强化效应、转移矩阵、训练数据、推理模型的学习错误），本文假设循环已经开始，精确刻画"哪些解码期干预原则上能救出模型"，并用一次前向传播（teacher forcing）精确计算任意采样器下的循环存活概率（命题 1）。
- **截断采样锁死判据（定理 1）：** 纯采样永不锁死任何位置；top-p（π>1/2）当且仅当续写概率 q≥π 时锁死该位置；min-p、贪心解码有对应判据。温度的顺序很重要：温度在 top-p 之前（transformers 默认）且 τ=0.7 时，top-p 0.9 的锁死门槛从 q≥0.9 降到 q≥0.823——降温不只是锐化分布，还降低了"入锁"的门槛。
- **有界 vs 增长惩罚的二分（定理 2/推论 1-2）：** 有界惩罚只能把锁死阈值从 π 抬高到 σ(logit(π)+D)，无法解开自我强化曲线饱和在其上的模型；惩罚一旦超过 ln(q/r°)（r° 为未见过 token 的原始概率质量）即保证逃逸。据此把常见惩罚分类：presence/窗口化 frequency/乘法惩罚均为有界，全上下文 frequency 线性，DRY 无分隔符时指数增长。
- **发现并实证 DRY 的结构性盲区（命题 4/推论 3）：** DRY 的序列分隔符（换行、冒号、引号、星号）会把惩罚封顶在 λb^(g−1−a)（g 为循环单元内最长无分隔符 token 连续段），默认参数下 D₇=7.5、D₈=13.1，而实测自我强化 log-odds 在 6–20 之间——理论预测 g=7 到 g=8 之间存在锁死/解锁相变，即 JSON 记录、键值行、短行、对话引用等恰好是最容易锁死的结构化输出。剂量-响应实验、未修改的 llama.cpp 采样器、自由生成三方验证一致。
- **提出 DRY-C：** 在 DRY 之上增加随"同一续写已逐字复现次数"指数增长的计数项 d_cnt(v)=λ_c·b_c^(c(v)−c₀)（跨分隔符统计、也作用于分隔符 token），证明其沿任何循环指数增长（命题 5）、解锁时间随 Δ 对数级（推论 4），并以 llama.cpp 补丁形式（默认关闭）验证：教师强制实验中无一单元锁死，自由生成中 720 次结构化输出无一循环跑到长度上限，任务完成度与 DRY 持平。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes the question from loop entry to loop exit.** Prior work explains why loops start (self-reinforcement, transition-matrix properties, training data, learning errors in reasoning models); this paper takes the loop as given and characterizes exactly when the sampling configuration leaves zero escape probability. Because the context along a loop is deterministic, survival under ANY sampler (including stateful ones like Mirostat) is computable exactly from one teacher-forced forward pass (Proposition 1).
- **Exact lock criteria for truncation samplers (Theorem 1).** Pure sampling never locks a position; top-p with π>1/2 locks a position iff the loop-continuation probability q≥π (the loop token becomes the only candidate in the nucleus); min-p locks iff ln q − ln a > ln(1/β); greedy iff q>a. Temperature placement matters: with temperature before top-p (the transformers default) at τ=0.7, the sufficient lock threshold for top-p 0.9 drops from q≥0.9 to q≥0.823 — lowering temperature lowers the bar for getting trapped.
- **A bounded-vs-growing dichotomy for penalties (Theorem 2, Corollaries 1–2).** A bounded penalty only raises the lock threshold from π to σ(logit(π)+D) (0.961/0.985/0.9945 for D=1/2/3 at π=0.9) and cannot unlock a model whose reinforcement curve saturates higher; escape is guaranteed under top-p once the penalty outgrows ln(q/r°), where r° is the raw mass of tokens absent from the context. This cleanly classifies deployed penalties: presence and windowed-frequency penalties are bounded; full-context frequency is linear; DRY is exponential only without breakers.
- **Identifies and validates a blind spot in DRY (Proposition 4, Corollary 3).** DRY's sequence breakers (newline, colon, quote, asterisk), which protect formatting, cap its penalty at λb^(g−1−a) for loops whose longest breaker-free run is g tokens. With defaults, D_g = 0.8/1.4/2.45/4.29/7.50/13.1 for g=3..8, while measured post-repetition log-odds lie between ~6 and ~20 — predicting a lock/unlock transition between g=7 and g=8, i.e., JSON records, key-value lines, quoted dialogue and short lines stay locked. Confirmed by a dose–response experiment, the unmodified llama.cpp sampler, and free generation (all 109 DRY-surviving loops have g≤7).
- **Proposes DRY-C, a count term closing the blind spot.** d_cnt(v) = λ_c·b_c^(c(v)−c₀) penalizes the (c₀+1)-th verbatim recurrence of any span longer than a′, counted across breakers and applied to breaker tokens too (a′=24, c₀=2, b_c=3); total penalty is max{d_DRY, d_cnt}. Proposition 5 proves exponential growth along every loop up to the look-back window (window condition holds for units up to 814 tokens at W=4096, Δ=20), and Corollary 4 gives a logarithmic unlock time. Implemented as a default-off llama.cpp patch; with it no unit stays locked in teacher forcing and no structured generation runs to the length limit.

## Methodology

The paper combines exact theory with teacher-forced measurement and free generation.

**Formal setup.** A loop unit u=(u₁..u_L) is repeated n times after a prefix; state (n,l) means the model is about to emit the l-th token of copy n+1. Because the context is fully determined by (n,l), any sampler's probability of continuing is a deterministic number q^{S}_{n,l}, and the probability of surviving m more repetitions is a product of per-copy completions Q_j (Proposition 1 — just the chain rule). A state is "locked" when this probability is 1. The analysis distinguishes position locks, permanent locks, and almost-sure loop persistence.

**Theory.** Theorem 1 derives iff lock criteria for top-p/min-p/greedy from the raw (q, a) pair; Proposition 3 covers temperature placement (before vs after truncation — llama.cpp and transformers differ). Theorem 2 analyzes additive logit penalties that leave unseen tokens untouched, giving two-sided bounds on the penalized continuation probability; Corollary 1 shows bounded penalties merely shift the threshold, Corollary 2 gives the growth condition d − ln(q/r°) → ∞ guaranteeing almost-sure escape. Proposition 4 bounds DRY's penalty via a circular arrangement of the loop unit: the trailing non-breaker arc plus the candidate token cannot exceed the longest breaker-free run g, so the penalty is capped at λb^(g−1−a). Proposition 5 proves the DRY-C count term grows at least like λ_c·b_c^(min(n−⌈a′/L⌉, N_W)−c₀) since each earlier copy's aligned position is preceded by an agreeing suffix.

**Measurement.** 150 English WikiText-103 test sentences + 150 Chinese Wikipedia sentences + 22 hand-written structured units (dialogue lines, list items, code) are repeated up to 200 times; one KV-cached forward pass per unit yields q, a, r° at every position in 64-bit softmax precision, from which lock status under any sampler configuration is computed exactly (penalties applied before truncation, as in llama.cpp). The DRY reimplementation was validated position-by-position against the llama.cpp sampler (commit 03aa006).

**Experiments.** (1) Reinforcement curves: log-odds of continuing vs repetition count on 11 instruction-tuned models (Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B, Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B, Llama-3.2-3B/3.1-8B, Gemma-2-2B/9B, Phi-4-mini, R1-Distill-Qwen-7B). (2) Dose–response: insert a colon (breaker) every g tokens into the first 24 tokens of 40 sentences per model, g ∈ {2,3,4,6,8,12,24}, with a semicolon (non-breaker) control. (3) Free generation in llama.cpp on GGUF quantizations of Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: 20 mixed prompts + 24 structured-output (JSON, tables, YAML, CSV, logs, lists, dialogue) and 12 prose prompts, default samplers vs +DRY vs +DRY-C, ≤3,000 new tokens; a loop is a ≥4-token span occurring ≥5 times consecutively covering ≥60 tokens. (4) Side-effect audits: task-completion metrics (answer produced, ≥90% of requested items, coverage, duplication, JSON parse rate) on 360 structured generations per row, requested-verbatim-copying tests, near-loop detection via zlib compression ratio and difflib line similarity, and count-term activation rates outside detected loops.

## Results & Highlights

- **Truncation locks, pure sampling never does (Table 2, teacher-forced, 150 units per model on 11 models):** top-p 0.9 leaves 80%–100% of units locked through the last ten measured repetitions (e.g., Qwen3-4B 1.00, Gemma-2-2B 0.80), pure sampling 0 on every model; min-p 0.1 behaves like top-p 0.9 (0.85–1.00).
- **The lock criteria are exact, not approximate:** on four models (Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B, Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.2-3B; ~160,000 positions each), Theorem 1's criteria agree with the exactly computed lock status at every position for top-p 0.9/0.95 and min-p 0.1/0.05; a Monte Carlo check with the transformers sampler reproduces the survival formula (0.789 ± 0.013 sampled vs 0.786 computed).
- **Reinforcement is strong and fast (Fig. 1):** log-odds of continuing rise as a saturating power law to between 6 (Llama-3.2-3B) and 20 (Gemma-2-9B) after 200 repetitions on WikiText-103/Chinese-Wikipedia sentences; half of sentences are fully locked (every position q≥0.9) within 5–40 repetitions.
- **DRY's blind spot is sharp and where predicted (Fig. 2, dose–response pooled over 11 models):** among units locked under top-p alone, DRY leaves 400/400 (100%) locked at g=2, 404/409 at g=3, 276/416 (66%) at g=6, but only 5/422 (1%) at g=8 and none beyond; with the semicolon control, 0/2,919 units remain locked — the effect is entirely due to breaker status, not the extra characters. Grouping by actual (retokenized) gaps gives the same transition (277/417 at g=6, 5/418 at g=8).
- **Confirmed in the real, unmodified llama.cpp sampler:** for six ordinary sentences top-p locks between repetitions 5 and 15 and DRY removes every lock; six breaker-dense units (short lines, a key–value line, a JSON object, quoted code) lock under DRY at exactly the repetition at which they lock without it.
- **Free generation matches the theory (Fig. 3, 1,780 generations per setting pooled):** the default sampler produces 233 loops (110 with g≤7, 123 with g≥8 or no breaker); with DRY there are 109 loops and ALL have g≤7 — DRY removes every loop outside the predicted blind spot while leaving the inside count unchanged (110 vs 109). Inside the blind spot the distribution shifts: at g=7 loops drop from 10 to 1, but loops with g≤5 RISE from 70 to 83, i.e., generations steered out of long-run loops fall into shorter-run ones.
- **Loops concentrate in structured output:** with default samplers on prompt set 2, 20.8% (Qwen3-4B) and 16.1% (Llama-3.1-8B) of structured generations contain a loop vs 0% and 7.2% for prose — precisely where breakers are densest and DRY's cap binds.
- **DRY-C eliminates run-to-the-limit loops (Fig. 4, Table 3, 360 structured generations per model × 2 = 720):** loops reaching the length limit fall from 59 (default) and 30 (DRY) to 0 on Qwen3-4B, and from 52 and 32 to 0 on Llama-3.1-8B; the share of generated tokens inside loops drops 12.2% → 6.7% → 0.1% (Qwen3-4B) and 15.0% → 9.4% → 0.1% (Llama). Residual loops (8 and 6 generations) end after a median of 6/5 repetitions, matching the logarithmic unlock time. Task completion stays close to DRY: mean coverage 40.5% → 38.2% (Qwen) and 71.5% → 73.1% (Llama), duplication improves (Qwen 18.5% → 15.0%; Llama 12.9% → 8.7%), JSON validity preserved on Qwen (31.1% both).
- **The cost is explicit and measured:** on 12 requested-verbatim-copying tasks, exact completion falls from >90% (default: 95% Llama, 91% Qwen) to ~10% under both DRY and DRY-C (Table 7); a stricter 16-task test shows DRY-C's extra loss vs DRY (strict success 11.2% vs 18.1% on Qwen), largely recoverable by raising c₀ to 6. Near-loop analysis (8,280 structured generations) shows only a small displacement into drifting near-repeats (no-loop-but->50%-near-duplicate-lines share rises 8.9%→12.2% on Qwen, 17.8%→19.7% on Llama) while median compression ratio improves (0.339→0.347 on Qwen).

## Authors & Credibility

Yichuan Chen and Xin Wang are at the School of Art and Design, Zhejiang Sci-Tech University (Hangzhou, China) — an unusual affiliation for a cs.CL decoding paper, which slightly lowers prior expectations but the work itself is rigorous. Credibility signals are strong: the theory is proved rather than asserted (appendix proofs for all five propositions and corollaries); the lock criteria were verified at every one of ~160,000 positions per model on four models with zero violations, plus a Monte Carlo cross-check against the transformers sampler; the DRY reimplementation was validated position-by-position against the real llama.cpp sampler (and the count term against the patched build, with the patch disabled producing bit-identical outputs); dose–response includes a semicolon control isolating breaker status from content; and the requested-copying cost, near-loop displacement, and out-of-loop count activations of their own method are all reported rather than hidden. The paper transparently discloses extensive AI assistance (Claude used for proofs, code, experiments, and drafting; authors state they reviewed and take responsibility). Limitations are stated in unusual detail.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High and well-differentiated. The exit-not-entrance framing, the exact lock criteria for top-p/min-p/greedy, and the bounded-penalty dichotomy are new; the DRY cap λb^(g−1−a) is a genuinely non-obvious structural finding about a widely deployed sampler. The paper correctly positions itself against Lee et al. (2026), who relate loop persistence to escape mass via fine-tuning — this paper characterizes when that escape mass is exactly zero at decoding time.
- **Rigor:** Theory and measurement are tightly coupled — predictions (the g=7/8 transition) are confirmed quantitatively in three independent settings, with controls (semicolon vs colon, retokenization-aware gap regrouping, multiple loop-detection thresholds in Table 6). The llama.cpp position-by-position validation is more care than most decoding papers take.
- **Reproducibility:** Good. All models are open-weight (1.5B–9B plus one 35B), the sampler is the unmodified llama.cpp, the patch is default-off with verified identical behavior when disabled, and hyperparameters are fully specified. However, no code release is mentioned in the main text, and the count-term parameters (a′=24, c₀=2, b_c=3) are described as chosen "from format considerations," not optimized — the sensitivity grid in Appendix E (4–39 detected loops across settings) is small.
- **Significance:** Practical and immediate: DRY is default in local inference stacks, structured output is where production loops hurt, and DRY-C is a drop-in patch with a logarithmic unlock time. The side-effect analysis (duplication down, coverage roughly flat, JSON validity preserved) is fair.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) The bounded-vs-growing distinction is ultimately relative to the look-back window — every deployed penalty including DRY-C is bounded; the exponential-in-n claim only holds up to the window, and the 814-token unit bound at W=4096/Δ=20, while stated, is easy to over-read. (2) DRY-C seriously damages requested verbatim copying (exact completion >90% → ~10%, and the stricter block-task test shows extra loss vs DRY that c₀=6 only partially recovers: 42.5% → 25.0% strict on Llama block tasks) — a real usability cost on legitimately repeated short-line blocks that breakers were protecting. (3) Free generation covers only three quantized models (Q8_0/Q4_K_M) with a 3,000-token budget and coarse automatic task metrics; no human evaluation, and the 35B model's loop counts are too small (4 surviving loops) to say much. (4) Lock criteria are derived only for top-p/min-p/greedy — Mirostat and XTC (both cited as deployed) are explicitly out of scope. (5) The near-loop displacement (a few verbatim loops becoming drifting near-repeats) means the blind-spot closure is not total: it verbatim-proofs the sampler, not the model. (6) Teacher-forced "locking" is measured under exact 64-bit computation on fp16/bf16 logits; real sampler numerics differ slightly (the paper itself measures differences up to 0.24 vs the GGUF half-precision build near thresholds).

## Strengths

- A clean, exactly-checkable theoretical core (lock criteria computable from one forward pass) that turns a vague failure mode ("the model got stuck") into a binary, measurable property of (model, sampler) pairs.
- The DRY blind-spot analysis is a concrete, falsifiable prediction about a deployed system, confirmed with a dose–response transition (66% locked at g=6 vs 1% at g=8) and a semicolon control.
- The fix (DRY-C) is minimal, provably growth-guaranteed up to the window, implemented as a default-off llama.cpp patch with verified bit-identical behavior when disabled, and costs almost nothing in task completion while cutting duplicated items.
- Exceptional self-audit: requested-copying cost, near-loop displacement, count activations outside loops (37.9% of Qwen structured outputs touch the count term at (24,2)), and per-run breakdowns are all in appendices.

## Limitations

- **Verbatim loops only:** near-loops (incrementing counters, drifting wording) are outside the model; DRY-C converts a few verbatim loops into such near-repeats (Appendix E).
- **Persistence, not onset:** all results are conditional on a loop having begun; the reinforcement curve is an empirical input whose shape is fitted, not derived.
- **Sampler coverage:** lock criteria exist only for top-p, min-p and greedy; Mirostat and XTC are excluded, and for τ>1 with temperature before top-p the sufficient condition is loose.
- **Finite windows bound everything:** DRY-C's exponential growth stops at the look-back window; the sufficient condition covers units only up to 814 tokens with W=4096 and Δ=20.
- **Small free-generation scope:** three quantized models, a 3,000-token limit, coarse automatic metrics, no human evaluation; the 35B model contributes few loops.
- **Copying cost:** both DRY and DRY-C reduce requested verbatim repetition from >90% to ~10% success; DRY-C additionally penalizes legitimately repeated blocks of short lines that breakers used to protect (strict block-task success 42.5% → 25.0% on Llama at c₀=2).
- Count parameters (a′, c₀, b_c) were not tuned; loop detection uses a fixed criterion (≥5 consecutive repetitions, ≥60 tokens), though alternative thresholds change counts but not the pattern.

## Relevance

与研究兴趣高度相关：本文对解码期采样（top-p/min-p 截断、重复惩罚、DRY）给出了可精确计算的锁死判据与逃逸保证，直接服务于本地推理（llama.cpp）中结构化输出（JSON/表格/日志）的可靠性——这正是开放权重模型部署中反复出现的痛点。其"截断把近乎确定的续写变成完全确定的锁死"的洞见，以及 DRY-C 这类即插即用补丁，对任何关心 LLM 推理栈解码控制与生成质量的人都值得精读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the lock-criterion framework be extended to Mirostat and XTC (which explicitly excludes top choices — does exclusion-based sampling lock differently than truncation-based), and to min-p with temperature after truncation?
- Does the DRY blind spot persist in frontier closed models, or do larger/better-trained models have reinforcement curves that saturate below D₇=7.5, making DRY sufficient above some scale? (The 35B data point here is suggestive but thin.)
- Can the count term be made conditional on loop detection (activate only after ≥5 consecutive repetitions rather than after c₀=2 recurrences) to remove the 37.9% out-of-loop activation rate on structured outputs and the copying cost, at the price of a later unlock?
- Does the temperature-before-top-p lock threshold (q≥0.823 at τ=0.7, π=0.9) explain field reports that lower temperature increases perceived "loopiness" in local inference stacks, and should samplers warn when a position's q crosses the effective threshold?
- Is the saturating-power-law shape of the reinforcement curve derivable from induction-head mechanics (Olsson et al., 2022), which would let the unlock time of DRY-C be predicted per-model from a short prefix measurement?
- How do the criteria interact with grammar-constrained decoding (XGrammar, outliners), where the candidate set is restricted before penalties — does constraint-induced renormalization create its own lock blind spots?
