> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"双时钟"理论框架，指出在多步 GRPO（RLVR）训练中，平方根学习率规则只保持每样本的一阶漂移而非学习进度：超过随学习率增大而缩小的临界批次后，大批次样本效率显著退化，但训练崩溃却始终锁定在相同的样本消耗量上；据此推导出"先小批次、临崩溃前切大批次"的调度策略，效果逼近最优固定批次。

> **TL;DR:** In controlled multi-step GRPO runs on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B / DAPO-Math-17k, scaling the rollout batch 8× (512 → 4,096 responses) under the square-root learning-rate rule makes the model need 1.7–2.3× more samples to reach 15% validation accuracy (sample-cost exponent α up to 0.44 at ηS = 1e-5, vs 0.15 at 1e-6), yet without the ratio clip both batch sizes collapse after the same 30–50k samples (56–80 optimizer steps for 4,096 vs 648 for 512); the clip delays failure 2–4× and helps the large batch most, and a simple 512→4,096 batch ramp tracks the best fixed batch while generating 3/4 of its samples at the large batch.

## 核心贡献

- **"双时钟"理论框架**：在局部二次损失模型下证明，平方根规则（η ∝ √批次）保持的是每样本的一阶漂移，而不是学习进度——大批次用更少、更长的更新步覆盖同样的漂移，因曲率损失更多进度，由此导出随学习率变化的**临界批次 B\***（学习率越大，B\* 越小，可远低于梯度噪声尺度，与 McCandlish et al. 2018 的经典结论不同）。
- **样本守恒的失败时钟**：提出"漂移阈值"假设（A3），推导出失败点 N_fail = C·√b/η，即沿平方根规则失败点近似与批次大小无关、只由消耗样本数决定；这与噪声驱动的随机行走机制（N_fail ∝ b/η²）和"批次盲"机制（N_fail ∝ 1/η）给出可区分的标度指数，并实验排除后两者。
- **大规模受控实验验证**：在 16× 批次范围（512–8,192 响应）、3 档学习率、μ ∈ {1, 4, 8, 16} 步数、开关/加宽裁剪、冻结检查点重放等控制下，用 Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B 验证三个预测，并在 DeepScaleR-Preview、Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct 和 Qwen2.5-Math-7B 上复现核心结论。
- **对裁剪（clip）角色的重新定位**：与"裁剪是学习瓶颈"的主流观点相反，重放实验显示大批次仅 0.5% 被裁剪的 token 就承载了 50–70% 的梯度范数——裁剪主要买的是稳定性（对大批次延迟失败最多 4×），代价只是一次性的早期速度损失。
- **实用的批次调度策略**：先在临界批次以下训练（进度高效），在预测失败点之前切换到大批次（稳定、高吞吐）；该 ramp 逼近最优固定批次的表现，且切换点在 25k–74k 样本区间内不敏感。

## Key Contributions

- **A two-clock model of batch scaling** for multi-step GRPO: under a local quadratic loss with noise-dominated Adam normalization, the square-root rule preserves first-order drift per sample (d = κ‖ḡ‖/σ, batch-independent) but not progress per sample (r(b) = r₀ − C_curv·b), yielding a learning-rate-dependent critical batch B\* = μ(2σ‖ḡ‖²/(κḡᵀHḡ) − B_noise) that shrinks as 1/κ — unlike the tuned-LR critical batch of McCandlish et al. (2018), which equals the gradient noise scale.
- **A drift-threshold failure law** N_fail = C·√b/η that is approximately sample-invariant along the square-root rule, with competing noise-driven (b/η²) and batch-blind (1/η) alternatives explicitly formulated and statistically rejected via likelihood-ratio tests on 21 unclipped runs.
- **Controlled GRPO experiments across a 16× rollout-batch range** (512–8,192 responses) on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with DAPO-Math-17k, isolating batch size, learning rate, and clipping (KL penalty, entropy bonus, dynamic sampling, and grad-norm clipping disabled; μ = 1 runs where the ratio clip provably cannot act), with robustness checks on DeepScaleR-Preview, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, and Qwen2.5-Math-7B.
- **Evidence that the PPO-style ratio clip buys stability rather than sustained speed**: frozen-checkpoint replays show the ~0.5% of clipped tokens in the 4,096 batch carry 50–70% of the gradient norm, the clip delays failure 2× for the 512 batch and ≥4× for the 4,096 batch, and widening the clip radius ×√8 trades that protection for a transient early gain that ends in decay or collapse.
- **A practical two-phase batch schedule** (start below B\*, switch to a large batch before the failure-law deadline) that approaches the best fixed batch's accuracy while generating three quarters of its samples at the large batch, with the switch point robust across 25k–74k samples.

## Methodology

The paper combines a closed-form theory with carefully controlled experiments:

**Theory (Section 3, Appendix A).** The authors model one AdamW step on a locally quadratic loss, ĝ = ḡ + ξ with Cov(ξ) = Σ/b, under two assumptions: (A1) optimizer batches far below the gradient noise scale (verified: measured noise scale > 6×10⁴ responses vs optimizer batches of 64–512), and (A2) a local quadratic loss expansion. Under the square-root rule η = κ√b, the mean step is E[Δθ] = −(κ√b/σ)ḡ, so the persistent displacement per consumed response (drift per sample) is batch-independent — this is exactly what the rule fixes. But the expected loss decrease per response becomes r(b) = r₀ − C_curv·b with C_curv = κ²ḡᵀHḡ/2σ²: fewer, longer steps lose more progress to curvature. This gives a progress clock N_τ(B_r) = N_τmin(1 + B_r/B\*) with sample-cost exponent α = B_r/(B_r + B\*), and B\* carrying a 1/κ term that shrinks as the assigned learning rate grows. For failure, a single hypothesis (A3: a run fails when accumulated drift along a slowly rotating mean gradient reaches threshold D) yields N_fail = C√b/η → C/κ under the rule — sample-invariant. The clip is placed on the within-rollout log-ratio spread, which grows √(B_rL/B_rS) ≈ 2.8× faster for the 8× larger batch (verified in replays: the 99th-percentile |log ρ| ratio reaches the predicted ≈2.8 from step k=3 onward).

**Experiments (Sections 4–6, Appendices B–G).** GRPO with the DAPO objective (ε_low = 0.2, ε_high = 0.28, dual clip c = 10) in verl on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, G = 8 responses per prompt, rollout batches 512/1,536/4,096/8,192 split into μ = 8 optimizer steps (μ = 1, 4, 16 as controls), learning rates following the square-root rule from ηS ∈ {1e-6, 5e-6, 1e-5}, budget 98,304 responses per run, evaluation on 512 held-out problems (mean@4) every 8,192 responses. Failure is pre-defined as accuracy halving from peak or a sustained 4× gradient-norm excursion; censored runs are handled with a Tobit likelihood. Controls include clip on/off/widened, gradient-norm clip at 1.0, μ = 1 (clip cannot act), and frozen-checkpoint replays measuring the clipped tokens' gradient share. ~2,000 GPU-hours on 4×A6000 (1.5B) and 4×H100 (7B); code released as "two-clocks-rlvr".

## Results & Highlights

- **Progress slows beyond a critical batch (P1).** On DAPO-Math-17k with Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (μ = 8), the pooled sample-cost exponent α rises from 0.15 (ηS = 1e-6) to 0.28 (5e-6) to 0.44 (1e-5) — a tenfold learning-rate increase roughly triples the exponent — while the implied critical batch B\* falls from ≈9.3k to ≈1.9k responses. Concretely, at ηS = 1e-5 the 4,096 batch needs 2.34× the samples of the 512 batch to reach 15% validation accuracy (N_.15 L/S = 2.34); the gap exists even at μ = 1 where the clip cannot act (20–45% more samples, e.g. DAUC lag 0.022 vs seed noise 0.005).
- **Failure keeps time in samples, not steps (P2).** Without the clip (ηS = 1e-5, μ = 8), the 4,096 batch fails after 7–10 rollouts (56–80 optimizer steps) and the 512 batch after 81 rollouts (648 steps) — a 12× difference in steps — yet both fail after 30–50k samples. Across 21 unclipped runs varying the optimizer batch 32× and learning rate ~6×, the fit gives β − γ/2 = −0.08 (90% CI [−0.23, 0.09], containing the drift law's 0 and excluding the batch-blind −1/2), and the drift form passes a likelihood-ratio test against the free fit (p ≈ 0.3) while the random-walk (γ = 2 predicted vs fitted 0.64–0.72) and batch-blind forms are rejected at p < 10⁻⁴; 14 of 18 failing runs lie within a factor of 1.5 of the law. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the two batches fail 6× apart in rollouts but only 1.3× apart in samples.
- **The clip buys stability, most for the large batch (P3).** With the DAPO clip on, the 512 batch's failure is delayed ~2×; the 4,096 batch never fails within its 98,304-response budget at scaled rates, and when pushed to twice its rate so that it does fail, it lasts 4× longer clipped than unclipped. Replays show why: only ~0.5% of the large batch's tokens are clipped yet they carry 50–70% of the full gradient norm (up to 0.9–1.7× at step 2), versus ~0.05% of tokens carrying 10–20% for the 512 batch.
- **Widening the clip trades stability for a transient gain.** Multiplying the 4,096 batch's log-space clip radius by √8 reaches 15% accuracy after 10k samples instead of 14k, but both seeds later decay — one collapses at 90k samples, ending at 0.078 accuracy vs 0.23 for the plain clipped 4,096 batch.
- **The batch ramp approaches the best fixed batch.** Training with 512 for 24.6k samples then switching to 4,096 or 8,192 (with optimizer-state reset and one-rollout warm-up) tracks the best fixed batch (1,536) while generating 3/4 of its samples at the large batch; switches anywhere from 25k to 74k samples perform similarly, bounded by the failure-law deadline.
- **Robustness of the progress gap.** A positive sample-cost gap also appears on DeepScaleR-Preview (α = 0.37), Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (α = 0.67 — the small batch reaches 8% accuracy after 14k samples vs 86k for the large one), and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (α = 0.36).

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are Yuante Li and Junwei Quan (equal contribution), Kai Qiu, Kate Zhang, and Yicheng Tao, from Carnegie Mellon University and the University of Toronto — both top-tier ML institutions; the senior line-up is consistent with systems/ML groups that have produced rigorous training-dynamics work. The experimental methodology is unusually careful for an empirical scaling paper: pre-registered failure criteria (accuracy halving or sustained 4× gradient-norm excursion), right-censoring handled with a Tobit likelihood, 90% bootstrap intervals over runs, likelihood-ratio tests between competing failure mechanisms, and falsifiable predictions derived before measurement (e.g., the predicted √8 ≈ 2.8× ratio-spread ratio confirmed from step k = 3). Controls isolate each confound: μ = 1 removes clipping, a gradient-norm-clip control (cap 1.0, failure at 36k samples, within the 31–46k seed range) rules out that artifact, and frozen-checkpoint replays measure what the clip removes rather than inferring it. Total compute (~2,000 GPU-hours) is modest but sufficient for the claims. Code is released ("two-clocks-rlvr"). No quantitative comparison against external state-of-the-art RLVR recipes is attempted — the paper is a controlled mechanism study, not a leaderboard entry.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: solid and specific.** The reframing — the square-root rule preserves drift per sample, not progress per sample, so invariance breaks through curvature rather than through clipping (contra Hilton et al. 2022 for one-step PPO) and the critical batch becomes learning-rate-dependent (contra McCandlish et al. 2018's noise-scale critical batch) — is a genuine, testable conceptual contribution, not an incremental benchmark bump.
- **Rigor: high on internal validity, with honest statistics.** The failure-law fits use censoring-aware regression and reject alternatives at p < 10⁻⁴, and the appendix is unusually transparent about numerical traps (e.g., fixed micro-batches of 4 sequences to keep ρ exactly 1 at k = 0 under bf16, since dynamic token packing caused spurious 0.03–0.12 log-ratio deviations).
- **Reproducibility: good.** Full hyperparameter tables (Table 3), every run's failure point listed individually (Table 5), two seeds where available, code released. However, only two seeds for most configurations — and Table 5 shows seed-to-seed failure-point variation up to ~2× (e.g., S at ηS = 1e-5 failing at 41k vs censored >37k), which the authors acknowledge; the conclusions rest on aggregate scaling across 21 runs, and a reader should not expect point predictions to transfer.
- **Significance caveats: the low-accuracy regime.** All headline sample-cost exponents are measured on targets of 15–20% validation accuracy on DAPO-Math-17k with a 1.5B model — early-training dynamics. Whether the critical batch B\* and the α exponents stay stable at the 60%+ accuracies of production RLVR runs is untested; the paper's own Table 2 shows the small-batch lead closing late in training when the small batch approaches failure, hinting the picture may invert over longer horizons (as Khatri et al. 2026's finding that larger batches reach higher final accuracy would suggest).
- **Missing ablations / unmeasured quantities.** The curvature term ḡᵀHḡ is never directly measured ("computationally expensive at this scale") — B\* is inferred indirectly from measured exponents via Remark 7 (direct four-batch fits give 2.2–4.2k vs 4.0k from the exponent route at ηS = 5e-6), so the central object of the theory is validated only through its downstream predictions. The drift threshold D is likewise a free constant, not independently measured. The main text also uses a scalar Adam normalizer, deferring the per-coordinate version to Appendix A.5.
- **External validity limits.** One model family (Qwen2.5), math-only domains (DAPO-Math-17k, DeepScaleR-Preview), one RL algorithm (GRPO with the DAPO objective) — acknowledged in the Limitations section. Wall-clock/compute efficiency of the ramp (small batches mean more optimizer steps and lower GPU utilization for the same samples) is explicitly out of scope, yet it is the quantity a practitioner actually optimizes; the batch-ramp recipe also presumes you already know the failure law of your small-batch run to time the switch.

## Strengths

- A single, simple idea (two clocks: progress vs. failure) that cleanly explains three previously separate empirical phenomena (batch-size slowdown, sample-invariant failure, batch-dependent clip benefit) and generates falsifiable exponent predictions that survive statistical testing.
- Exceptional experimental hygiene: pre-defined failure criteria, censoring-aware statistics, controls for every confound raised (clipping, grad-norm clipping, numerical ρ ≠ 1 artifacts, clip-active step counts as an alternative failure mechanism), and per-run failure tables in the appendix.
- The theory makes a non-obvious, confirmed prediction that separates it from prior art: B\* lies 60–2,000× below the measured gradient noise scale and moves with the learning rate — something neither the tuned-LR critical batch nor one-step invariance results predict.
- Directly actionable takeaway (start below B\*, ramp up before failure) with an honest switch-point sensitivity sweep (25k–74k all work).

## Limitations

- Findings are established in the early-training, low-accuracy regime (15–25% on DAPO-Math-17k, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B); transfer to longer runs, larger models beyond 7B, non-math domains, and PPO-style algorithms is untested.
- The curvature constant and drift threshold at the core of the theory are inferred rather than measured; B\* estimates from different routes differ by up to ~2× (2.2–4.2k direct fit vs 4.0k exponent-implied at ηS = 5e-6).
- Seed count is two (sometimes one, e.g., the 7B and Instruct runs), with acknowledged intrinsic seed variation in failure points; single-run anecdotes (the widened-clip collapse of one seed) carry limited weight.
- Sample efficiency is measured in generated responses; token, GPU-hour, and wall-clock efficiency — which the batch-ramp recommendation is really about — are not measured.
- The failure criterion (accuracy halving or 4× gradient-norm excursion) is a proxy for "collapse"; the appendix itself catalogues heterogeneous failure modes (quiet collapses, KL blow-ups with length-cap saturation, collapse-then-recover) that a single C constant smooths over.

## Relevance

直接命中"RL 后训练动态与失败模式"研究方向：本文系统刻画了多步 GRPO/RLVR 中批次缩放导致的学习减速与样本守恒的训练崩溃（collapse on schedule），并给出可操作的失败预测与批次调度缓解手段，与关注 collapse、漂移及其检测与缓解的兴趣高度一致。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the sample-invariant failure clock persist at production-scale accuracy (e.g., 60%+ on AIME-style splits), or does the small-batch lead invert late in training as the failure law consumes the small batch first?
- Can the drift threshold D be estimated cheaply online (e.g., from within-rollout log-ratio spread or KL divergence) so the batch-ramp switch point becomes adaptive rather than pre-tuned from a failure-law fit?
- How does the two-clock picture interact with asynchronous / off-policy GRPO variants, where staleness adds another drift source and Song et al. (2026) found a 1/η collapse law?
- Does replacing the hard ratio clip with soft trust-region methods (probability smoothing, Mu-GRPO-style relaxation) shift the critical batch B\* by removing the dominant clipped gradient component, or does curvature — not clipping — ultimately bound progress in that case too?
- Can the curvature term ḡᵀHḡ be approximated with Hessian-vector products on a subsample to measure B\* directly, closing the loop between the theory's central quantity and its indirect estimates?
