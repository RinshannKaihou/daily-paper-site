> **一句话总结：** 本文区分了"活跃事实"（可支撑新知识泛化）与"惰性事实"（只能回忆但不能泛化），通过对 11 个开源模型（2B–27B）和 70 个关系的设计精巧的"随机重标签 + 微调"实验发现：微调前一个关系在预训练表征中能被线性探针读出的程度（Pre-linearity）强烈预测在其上构建的新关系微调后的泛化能力（每个模型内 Pearson r = 0.87–0.92），且这一关联在控制回忆准确率、关系规模和主题频率后依然稳健——线性可读性比"模型是否记得"更能决定"模型能否举一反三"。

> **TL;DR:** The authors introduce the distinction between "active" facts (which support generalization to new knowledge) and "inert" facts (which can be recalled but do not), and test it with a clean relabeling paradigm: take a pretrained relation (e.g., Place → continent), map each answer to a random animal label via a secret bijection (Europe → crab), fine-tune the model on the new relation for most subjects, and test held-out subjects. Across 11 open-weight models (2B–27B, e.g., Qwen3.8-27B, Gemma-4-E2B, OLMo-3-1025-7B) and 70 relations (from Hernandez et al. 2024, BEAR-big, and 6 new Town relations), Pre-linearity (how well a linear probe decodes the original relation from pretrained subject representations) correlates with Generalization at Pearson r = 0.87–0.92 within every model — versus r = 0.63–0.74 for Recall alone; the association survives joint control for Recall, answer count, subject count and subject frequency (r = 0.70–0.82), and is robust to re-randomizing the label bijection and fine-tuning seed (r = 0.92–0.93 across repeats on MiniCPM5-2B-Base) and to 13 non-animal label sets (r = 0.91–0.93). Error analysis over 37,571 held-out errors from 757 fine-tuned models shows the probe's top-3 answers account for 21.5% of errors (vs 8.8% shuffled baseline), beating the pretrained model's own top-3 (11.5% vs 7.3% shuffled) — failures follow the linear structure, not the recalled answer.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"活跃事实 vs 惰性事实"的新区分**：模型能可靠陈述一个事实（recall）不等于它能在学习新知识时利用这个事实（generalization）。这是首个系统研究"哪些已知事实能支撑新知识学习"的工作，与 entailment（必然推理）、paraphrase（语言改写）明确区分开。
- **设计了巧妙的"随机重标签"实验范式**：对预训练关系（如 Paris → Europe）的每个答案用随机双射映射到任意新标签（Europe → crab），构造模型从未见过、但结构继承自已知关系的新关系（Paris → crab）。模型只能通过已有的知识结构才能泛化到 held-out 主题（Munich → crab），直接检验统计泛化。
- **核心实证发现**：在 11 个模型（2B–27B，含 base 和 post-trained）× 70 个关系的规模上，Pre-linearity（微调前线性探针可读性）与 Generalization（微调后 held-out 准确率）在每个模型内相关性达 Pearson r = 0.87–0.92，且 Pre-linearity 是比 Recall 更好的预测器。
- **严谨的因果排除**：关联在分别和联合控制 Recall、答案数、微调主题数、主题预训练频率（用 infini-gram 在 Dolma v1.7 上统计）后依然成立（联合控制后 r = 0.70–0.82）；对随机双射、微调种子、13 种非动物标签集均稳健。
- **错误归因分析**：用 Shapley 分配法分析 757 个微调模型的 37,571 个错误，发现错误答案更多跟随线性探针的排名而非预训练模型自己的输出排名——说明微调确实建立在探针所读出的线性结构之上。

## Key Contributions

- **Active vs inert facts**: A new conceptual distinction between facts that support generalization beyond their own recall and facts that can be recalled but do not support generalization in a given task — the first systematic study of *which* known facts a model can build new facts on, as opposed to whether models can recall or edit facts.
- **The random-relabeling paradigm**: New relations are built by mapping each answer of a pretrained relation (e.g., continent) to an arbitrary label (e.g., an animal name) through a uniformly random bijection. The model is fine-tuned on a subset of subjects under a fictional "novel" framing and evaluated on held-out subjects under a held-out template. Generalization is only possible through the structure of what the model already knows — a direct, clean test of statistical generalization of facts.
- **Scale and consistency of the main result**: Across 11 open-weight models (Gemma-4-E2B to Qwen3.8-27B, base and post-trained) and 70 relations, Pre-linearity (class-balanced accuracy of a nested-cross-validated linear probe on PCA-whitened last-token subject states, minus a majority baseline) predicts Generalization at Pearson r = 0.87–0.92 (Spearman ρ = 0.85–0.90) within every model, with no 95% bootstrap interval dipping below 0.72.
- **Pre-linearity beats Recall as a predictor**: Recall alone correlates with Generalization at r = 0.63–0.74, but this is mostly mediated by linearity — controlling for Pre-linearity drops Recall's correlation to r = 0.40–0.58 (ρ = 0.25–0.42, interval excluding zero in only 8/11 models), whereas Pre-linearity controlling for Recall stays at r = 0.79–0.86. Being *active* is not the same as being *known*.
- **Error analysis via Shapley attribution**: Decoding the fine-tuned model's wrong label back through the bijection (Munich → zebra means the model acted as if Munich's answer were Asia), the probe's top-3 ranked answers account for 21.5% of all errors versus 8.8% under a shuffled baseline and 5.6% under chance; the pretrained model's own top-3 account for 11.5% (7.3% shuffled). Failures follow the linearly readable structure, not the recalled answer.

## Methodology

The experiment pipeline per (model, relation) pair:

1. **Pretrained relations.** 70 relations screened from three sources — 11 from Hernandez et al. (2024) (dropping two spelling relations readable from the subject's own letters), 6 new "Town" relations over a 300-town pool (region, country language, latitude/longitude/elevation/population bands), and 53 from BEAR-big. Each relation needs ≥5 answers, ≥5 subjects per answer (≥4 fine-tuning + 1 held-out), and ≥25 fine-tuning subjects on every model.

2. **New relation construction.** For relation R with answer set E, draw a label subset of the same size from the animal label set (179 WordNet noun.animal names surviving a deterministic filter: common, non-hyponymic, mutually distinct first tokens across all model tokenizers, non-taxonomic neighbors of subjects/answers), plus a uniformly random bijection π: E → labels. Subjects sharing an original answer share a new label.

3. **Fine-tuning.** Full-parameter fine-tuning for 20 epochs under 4 paraphrase templates in a fictional novel frame ("In the novel XYZ, … the emblem of Italy shows the crab"), loss on the label's first token only; AdamW (β = 0.9/0.95, no weight decay, grad clip 1.0), LR 1e-5 linear warmup (first 10%) then cosine decay to 1e-6, batch 16, bf16 forward/backward with fp32 master weights. Every model reaches accuracy 1.00 on its fine-tuning subjects (Table 3), ruling out undertraining. A 5th template is held out for evaluation.

4. **Three measures.** *Recall*: pretrained model's class-balanced candidate (restricted-vocabulary, first-token) accuracy on the original relation. *Generalization*: fine-tuned model's candidate accuracy on held-out subjects under the held-out template. *Pre-linearity*: a softmax linear probe (ridge λ = 0.01) on the 16-dim PCA-whitened last-token state of the subject span; layer chosen by nested cross-validation (10 outer folds / 5 inner folds); class-balanced accuracy minus a majority-answer baseline. Hyperparameters (q = 16, λ = 0.01) were fixed in advance, not tuned.

5. **Controls and robustness.** Partial correlations controlling individually and jointly for Recall, ln|E|, ln|T|, and subject frequency (exact infini-gram counts on Dolma v1.7); re-randomization of bijection and seed (3 repeats); a label-set comparison rerunning every relation with 13 other WordNet categories (state, artifact, person, act, …) on MiniCPM5-2B-Base.

6. **Error attribution.** All 37,571 wrong answers on held-out subjects from the 757 fine-tuned models that err are credited (Shapley split over five explanation families: probe top-3, Recall top-3, most frequent answer, 5 nearest subjects by cosine, answer before fine-tuning), compared against shuffled and chance baselines.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main result (Table 1, Figure 2)**: Within each of the 11 models, Pre-linearity vs Generalization across the 70 relations: Pearson r = 0.87–0.92 (median 0.90), Spearman ρ = 0.85–0.90. Example per-model values: G9v3-3B r = .92, MiniCPM5-2B-Base r = .92, Qwen3.5-9B-Base r = .87, Qwen3.8-27B r = .90 — base and post-trained models from 2B to 27B parameters all land in the same band, and no 95% bootstrap interval falls below 0.72.
- **Controls (Table 1)**: Controlling for any single confound (Recall, answer count, subject count, subject frequency) leaves r between 0.77 and 0.91; controlling for all four jointly leaves r = 0.70–0.82 and ρ = 0.75–0.85 (no 95% interval below 0.56). The weakest joint-control case is Granite-4.2-3B (r = .70) and the strongest is G9v3-3B / MiniCPM5-2B-Base (r = .82).
- **Pre-linearity vs Recall (Figure 3a)**: Pre-linearity controlling for Recall still predicts Generalization at r = 0.79–0.86, while Recall controlling for Pre-linearity falls to r = 0.40–0.58 (ρ = 0.25–0.42, excluding zero in only 8 of 11 models). Recall's raw r = 0.63– 0.74 is mostly mediated by linear readability.
- **Label-set robustness (Figure 3b, Table 7)**: On MiniCPM5-2B-Base, rerunning all 70 relations with labels from each of 13 other WordNet categories keeps r between 0.91 and 0.93, and each category orders the relations by Generalization almost identically to the animal set (ρ = 0.95–0.97 vs the animal ordering).
- **Re-randomization (Table 2, Table 4)**: Three MiniCPM5-2B-Base runs differing only in bijection and seed give r = .92, .93, .92; the 17 Hernandez + Town relations rerun three times on ten models keep every run's r between 0.78 and 0.95.
- **Error analysis (Figure 4)**: Of 37,571 held-out errors from 757 fine-tuned models, the probe's top-3 answers account for 21.5% (vs 8.8% shuffled, 5.6% chance); the pretrained model's top-3 account for 11.5% (vs 7.3% shuffled, 6.4% chance); all five explanation families together explain 48.3% of errors (vs 26.7% shuffled, 17.8% chance). Decoded back through the bijection, wrong labels track the probe's ranking more than the model's own ranking — fine-tuning builds on the structure the probe reads.
- **Training-fit sanity (Table 3)**: Every one of the 11 models reaches candidate accuracy 1.00 on all 70 relations' fine-tuning subjects, so generalization gaps reflect representation structure, not undertraining.

## Authors & Credibility

Faridoun Mehri and Hinrich Schütze are from the Center for Information and Language Processing (CIS) at LMU Munich and the Munich Center for Machine Learning (MCML) — Schütze is a senior, highly established NLP researcher, and the group has a strong track record in probing and lexical semantics (e.g., co-authorship on Elazar et al. 2021, cited here). The paper's craft supports its credibility: hyperparameters fixed in advance, nested cross-validation with held-out subjects never used to fit or select anything, exact (not sampled) baseline expectations for the error analysis, 95% percentile bootstrap intervals with clearly stated resampling units, licensing of BEAR/WordNet/Hernandez data handled explicitly, and full code, prompts, label sets and relation splits released at github.com/NightMachinery/active-inert-facts. The writing is careful about what is shown (correlation with extensive controls) versus what is hypothesized.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel question and framing. Prior work studied entailment-style inference, paraphrase consistency, factual recall and knowledge editing; nobody had asked which recalled facts support learning *new* knowledge. The random-relabeling paradigm is an elegant instrument — the bijection guarantees the new relation is unknown while inheriting the old one's equivalence structure. The connection to Paccanaro & Hinton's (2000) linear relational embeddings gives it a satisfying historical arc.
- **Rigor**: High. Nested CV, class-balanced metrics with majority baselines subtracted, four confound controls computed separately and jointly, seed/bijection re-randomization, 13-category label-set comparison, and a Shapley error attribution with both shuffled and chance baselines. The observation that the "most frequent answer" explanation cannot be validated by shuffling (its shuffled share actually rises to 6.1% from 5.0% observed) and is instead compared to chance shows unusual care.
- **Causality gap (main weakness)**: The evidence is correlational at the relation level. The natural causal test — intervening on the linear structure itself (e.g., rotating/ablating the probe-read direction, or transplanting linear structure between relations) and showing generalization changes — is absent. "Fine-tuning builds on the structure the probe reads" is supported by the error analysis (21.5% vs 8.8% shuffled) but 78.5% of errors are *not* named by the probe's top-3, and overall only 48.3% of errors are explained by all five families combined; the mechanism story is suggestive, not established.
- **Generalization-benchmark quirk**: All 70 relations must have ≥5 answers, ≥5 subjects per answer and ≥25 fine-tuning subjects on *every* model. This screens out sparse/long-tail relations — precisely where Kandpal et al. (2023) showed models struggle — so the studied regime is biased toward well-populated, head-knowledge relations (13 of 47 Hernandez relations, 53 of 77 BEAR relations qualify). Whether Pre-linearity predicts generalization on long-tail knowledge is untested.
- **Scale and scoring limitations**: The model pool skews tiny (six models from the Artificial Analysis *tiny* leaderboard; only Qwen3.8-27B exceeds 9B) — frontier-scale behavior is extrapolated, not measured. Scoring is first-token-only under a single held-out template, and Generalization is a relation-level aggregate over few held-out subjects, so the "prediction" is about relation averages, not per-fact guarantees. The subject-frequency control uses Dolma v1.7 counts via infini-gram, which is only truly the pretraining corpus for OLMo — a proxy everywhere else.
- **Regime specificity**: Full-parameter fine-tuning to 100% train accuracy over 20 epochs is one corner of the learning regime space; LoRA / low-epoch / in-context analogues of the finding are untested, and the paper does not probe whether Pre-linearity would also predict knowledge-editing outcomes (a natural sibling question).
- **Red flags**: None serious. Claims are stated at the strength the data supports; I found no cherry-picked headline numbers — the weakest joint-control numbers (r = .70) are reported as prominently as the strongest (r = .92).

## Strengths

- A crisp, previously unstudied question (which known facts support new learning) answered with a paradigm whose construction rules out the usual shortcuts: the new relation is unknowable a priori, so any held-out success is statistical generalization over pretrained structure.
- Remarkably consistent effect sizes across 11 diverse models (r = 0.87–0.92) — including a base/post-trained pair (Qwen3.5-9B vs Qwen3.5-9B-Base) that lets the reader partially decouple post-training as a factor.
- Exhaustive robustness battery: joint four-way controls, bijection/seed re-randomization, and 13 alternative label categories all leave the association essentially unchanged.
- The error analysis goes beyond correlation between aggregate scores to a per-subject mechanistic read: decoded wrong answers follow the probe's ranking (21.5% vs 8.8% shuffled), which turns the probe into a predictor of *how* models fail, not just whether they will.
- Full reproducibility package (code, templates, label sets, relations, leaderboard snapshot) with pre-registered-style fixed probe hyperparameters.

## Limitations

- Correlational design without intervention experiments; the causal claim that linear readability *enables* generalization remains a well-supported hypothesis.
- Relation-selection filter (≥5 answers, ≥25 fine-tuning subjects) excludes sparse and long-tail relations, biasing toward well-populated head knowledge; 70 relations is modest, and results are aggregated across relations rather than tested per fact.
- Small-model skew: mostly 2B–9B models plus one 27B; the paper itself acknowledges models were drawn from a "tiny models" leaderboard, so frontier-scale validity is unknown.
- First-token scoring, one held-out evaluation template, and few held-out subjects per relation bound the granularity of the Generalization measure; multi-token answers and multi-template robustness are unexplored.
- The subject-frequency control is a Dolma-based proxy that matches none of the proprietary pretraining corpora of most models tested.
- Fine-tuning regime (full parameters, 20 epochs, 100% train accuracy) is deliberately saturated; practically relevant regimes (LoRA, early stopping, in-context learning) may behave differently, and no links are drawn to knowledge-editing or hallucination-mitigation practice.

## Relevance

这篇论文属于"LLM 隐藏状态与可解释性"方向的核心工作：它把线性探针可读性（linear readability）与微调后的事实泛化直接定量挂钩，为"表征几何结构决定下游可学习性"提供了跨 11 个模型的一致证据。对任何关心 LLM 内部表征结构、知识注入（fine-tuning 学新知识）与泛化可靠性的研究都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a causal intervention on the linear structure — e.g., rotating the probe-read subspace, or adding a linearly-decodable direction to an inert relation's subjects — causally convert inert facts into active ones?
- Does Pre-linearity predict generalization under LoRA, early-stopped fine-tuning, or in-context learning instead of full-parameter fine-tuning, or is the effect specific to saturated full fine-tuning?
- Do sparse/long-tail relations (excluded by the ≥5-answers/≥25-subjects filter) show the same Pre-linearity → Generalization link, or does subject frequency dominate there?
- Does post-training shift Pre-linearity itself? A paired base/post-trained analysis beyond the single Qwen3.5-9B pair could quantify whether instruction tuning makes knowledge more or less linearly readable.
- Can Pre-linearity be used prospectively — e.g., as a cheap screening metric for which relations a model can be taught (knowledge injection, domain adaptation), or to predict knowledge-editing ripple effects (Cohen et al., 2024)?
- What explains the 51.7% of held-out errors that none of the five explanation families name — non-linear structure, attention-mediated recall, or label-space interference?
