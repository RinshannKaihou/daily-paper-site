> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了带显式回溯（BACK token）的算术搜索任务 ChainSearch，在一个从头训练的 300 万参数 Transformer 上系统搜索"推理正确性方向"——线性探针能以约 0.94 AUROC 解码最终答案正误，且方向在注意力头上相当稳定，但无论用探针、CAA 还是直接优化的向量做因果干预，都不能稳定提升推理正确率（最好配置也只在 256 题中改善 5 题、恶化 4 题），说明"线性可解码"不等于"因果可用"，对线性表示假说在推理场景的适用性提出质疑。

> **TL;DR:** A rigorous negative result on steering reasoning correctness. On ChainSearch (a Countdown-derived task with explicit backtracking, solved by a 3M-parameter transformer trained from scratch at 71.12% final-answer accuracy), linear probes decode final-answer correctness at up to ~0.94 AUROC on the 16,114-trace held-out test split, and probe/CAA directions are stable across token positions at attention heads — yet steering with any of 236 selected configurations (probe, CAA, or directly optimized vectors, across heads/layers/positions/strengths/schedules, 68,096 paired evaluations) never beats the unsteered baseline consistently: the best setting flips final-answer correctness on only 5 of 256 problems (+5/−4 for CAA; +5/−3 for Direct Optimization; +3/−7 for probe steering over all 22 attention heads), and multipoint steering mostly worsens outcomes. Decodable ≠ causally useful; read it if you care about whether the linear representation hypothesis survives contact with reasoning.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 ChainSearch 测试床**：在 Countdown 数字谜题（6 个输入数、目标 100–999）基础上引入显式的搜索与回溯表示——状态转移 + BACK（恢复先前状态）+ SUCCESS + EXPR（最终表达式）——把"推理是否在正轨上"变成每个 token 位置都可精确标注的可控实验环境；训练轨迹由改版 A*（非可采纳启发式、借鉴人类玩家策略）生成，SFT 集含 1,016,676 条样本，另生成 6,948,231 条探索性预训练轨迹。
- **从头训练 3M 参数模型**并获得"留有余地"的能力水平：微调后最终答案正确率 71.12%（预训练后仅 25.20%），表达式可解析率 99.33%、合法运算率 96.37%、状态一致性 95.16%——既有能力又有 29% 的失败轨迹，为探针/CAA 提供均衡的正负样本。
- **四轴系统化搜索正确性方向**：标签（状态可达性 vs 最终答案正误）× 语义 token 位置（操作数、运算符、结果、状态、BACK、SUCCESS、EXPR）× 构造方法（线性探针、CAA 均值差、直接优化向量）× 模型组件（注意力头、残差流、MLP 输出、最终隐状态），并用五类稳定性指标刻画方向的几何一致性。
- **关键的因果检验（阴性结果）**：在 236 个经验证集挑选的干预配置、68,096 次配对评估（每次干预与不干预对同一题各生成一遍）中，没有任何方向能稳定改善下一状态可达性或最终答案正确率；即便是显式以"提高正确答案似然"为目标的直接优化向量也一样失效。干预并非无效——它会改变生成长度、BACK 使用率和表达式可解析性——只是没有指向"更好的推理"。
- **对线性表示假说的质疑与对先前工作的批评**：指出 Zhang et al. (2025) 只在最后一层做探针、完全回避因果干预，且其 10,000 token 截断在他们的 AIME 2025 复现（DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B）中会丢弃 56.7% 的推理轨迹，另有 MATH 测试集污染风险——而 ChainSearch 从头训练、目标不相交切分，正好堵住这些漏洞。

## Key Contributions

- **ChainSearch testbed**: extends the Countdown numbers puzzle with explicit search and backtracking — arithmetic state transitions, `BACK` (restore an earlier state), `SUCCESS`, and `EXPR` (the final expression that must evaluate to the target) — making "is the reasoning on track" an exactly labelable property at every token position. Traces are generated by a modified A* (deliberately non-admissible, human-inspired heuristics); 1,016,676 SFT examples plus 6,948,231 exploratory pretraining traces.
- **A compact, from-scratch subject model**: a 3-million-parameter decoder-only transformer, fine-tuned to 71.12% final-answer correctness (up from 25.20% after pretraining) with 99.33% parseable final expressions — strong enough to reason, weak enough (29% failed traces) to supply balanced positive/negative examples for probing.
- **A four-axis search over correctness directions**: label (local reachability vs final-answer correctness) × semantic token position × construction method (linear probe, CAA mean-difference, directly optimized vectors) × model component (attention heads, residual stream, MLP outputs, final hidden state), summarized by five stability metrics (successive-position, cross-position, cross-label, and two cross-method measures).
- **The headline causal null result**: across 236 validation-selected steering configurations and 68,096 paired generations, no direction consistently improves next-state reachability or final-answer correctness. Steering is not causally inert — it changes trace length, BACK usage, and parseability (Appendix Fig. 17) — but the changes have no common signature of improved reasoning.
- **A sharper critique of prior work**: Zhang et al. (2025) probe only the last layer, avoid causal steering entirely, and truncate at 10,000 tokens — in the authors' AIME 2025 reproduction with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, 56.7% of reasoning traces exceeded that cap, and MATH contamination is unresolved; ChainSearch's from-scratch training and target-disjoint splits (all problems sharing a target go to one split) close both holes.

## Methodology

The paper is organized as a decodability study followed by a causal intervention study, all on a controlled synthetic task that makes both tractable at every token position and model component.

**Task and data.** ChainSearch gives the model six inputs (drawn from 1–10 twice plus 25/50/75/100) and a target in 100–999; a move combines two numbers with `+ − × ÷` (positive integers only, each input used at most once), and the answer is the final `EXPR`. Unlike plain Countdown, abandoned branches are marked with `BACK` and state restoration, so search and backtracking are explicit in the trace. Training traces come from a modified A*: the branching search is linearized into a sequence of state transitions with `BACK` inserted whenever a branch is abandoned, and the heuristic h(n) uses human Countdown strategies rather than admissibility (the goal is informative search behavior, not shortest paths). Splits are target-disjoint, because nearly identical solution traces occur for shared targets (their Example 2), which would leak between train and test.

**Direction construction.** A candidate correctness direction u(m, ℓ, p, c) is indexed by label ℓ (reachability of the current state, or eventual final-answer correctness), semantic position p (operands, operators, result token, resulting/restored state, `BACK`, `SUCCESS`, `EXPR`), method m (a trained linear probe's weight vector; CAA = positive-minus-negative class-mean difference; Direct Optimization = a vector trained by gradient descent on token-level cross-entropy to raise the likelihood of a valid final answer, model frozen), and component c (every attention head, residual stream, MLP output, final LayerNorm'd hidden state). All vectors are unit-normalized for fair steering-strength comparison. Analysis uses 115,367 traces including the full 16,114-trace held-out test split for probe reporting.

**Stability geometry.** Five metrics ask whether directions persist across neighboring positions, across all positions, across the two labels, and across construction methods (pairwise cosine, plus whether the Direct Optimization vector lies in the span of the Probe and CAA vectors).

**Causal steering.** The search space adds intervention strength β; configurations are selected on validation data and evaluated on held-out problems, each steered generation paired with an unsteered generation of the same problem. A staged protocol uses 256 or 512 paired problems first, escalating to the full 16k split only for effects large enough to warrant it (hence 68,096 paired evaluations over 236 conditions). Outcomes are scored two ways: next-state reachability (can the first post-intervention state still reach the target without backtracking) and final-answer correctness (does the completed trace end in a correct EXPR). They vary head counts (top-4/top-12/random/all), strength allocation (fixed total vs per-head), direction sign, token position, intervention frequency (single / repeated at every transition / multipoint), and component — with appendix controls for head-selection strategy, raw (unnormalized) vector magnitudes, and effects on trace properties.

## Results & Highlights

- **Linear decodability is strong.** Final-answer correctness probes on the 16,114-trace held-out test split are above chance early in the trace and reach approximately **0.94 AUROC** by the end of generation, across attention heads, residual streams, MLP outputs, and the final hidden state (Fig. 2); the trajectory survives restricting to traces with at most 8 BACK transitions, so it is not driven by long heavy-backtracking traces.
- **The subject model is competent but fallible.** After fine-tuning (9,000 test problems): final-answer correctness **71.12%** vs **25.20%** after pretraining; EXPR parseability **99.33%** (vs 95.63%), legal arithmetic operations **96.37%** (vs 93.58%), state-transition consistency **95.16%** (vs 92.93%) — Table 4.
- **Steering never consistently wins.** The largest improvement in final-answer correctness across the Fig. 6 configurations is **5 out of 256 paired problems**: CAA improves 5 while worsening 4; Direct Optimization improves 5 while worsening 3; Probe steering over all 22 attention heads improves 3 and worsens **7** (net negative vs the unsteered baseline). Next-state reachability changes are similarly rare and bidirectional.
- **More intervention makes it worse, not better.** Repeated and multipoint steering change more outcomes but mainly increase worsened cases, with the largest worsened counts under multipoint steering (one Probe condition loses 39 problems' correctness vs at most ~10 gained; 512 paired problems per setting, Fig. 8). Appendix sweeps confirm no rescue from intervention strength, raw vector magnitudes, direction sign, or head-selection strategy (including a random-heads baseline, Fig. 14).
- **Direction geometry is only partially coherent.** Attention heads show the clearest successive- and cross-position stability (CAA most stable), but cross-label agreement between reachability and final-answer directions is only moderate — cosine around **0.5 (≈60°)** even at the best attention heads — and residual-stream/MLP cross-method stability sits near the random-projection baseline.
- **Prior-work reproducibility finding.** In their AIME 2025 reproduction, **56.7%** of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B reasoning traces exceeded the 10,000-token cap that Zhang et al. (2025)'s early-exit analysis imposes — i.e., that line of "reasoning models know when they're right" evidence leaves over half the traces unexamined.

A team from MBZUAI (UAE), Mila (Canada), RIKEN AIP (Japan), and Tohoku University, led by Munachiso Samuel Nwadike and including Alham Fikri Aji, Zangir Iklassov, and Kentaro Inui — an established NLP/interpretability group. Several authors have directly relevant prior work: Genadi et al. (EACL 2026) on sycophancy directions in attention heads, and Nwadike et al. (ACL 2025) on compact controlled-task models — so the methodology (small from-scratch models, probing, steering) is a continuation of a serious research program, not a one-off. The paper includes an explicit AI-use statement (AI for coding/debugging and writing polish, manually reviewed), a reproducibility statement, and code at https://github.com/samunaai/chainsearch covering dataset generation, training, probing, and steering. The experimental hygiene is unusually careful for a negative-result paper: held-out test-only reporting, target-disjoint splits, validation-selected steering configurations, and paired steered/unsteered generations.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The contribution is not the probing (Zhang et al. 2025 and Ye et al. 2025b already showed correctness is decodable) but the systematic *causal* test — in particular the Direct Optimization control, which rules out the cheap excuse that probe/CAA vectors were simply not trained for causal effect. Nobody appears to have run this test with this level of coverage; prior "steering improves reasoning" results (Chen et al. 2025b; Zhuang et al. 2026) steer *behaviors* (reflection, transitions), not correctness itself, and the paper correctly notes that a truly causal correctness direction should subsume those behavioral gains.
- **Rigor**: High. Five stability metrics, three construction methods, sign flips, random-head baselines, intervention schedules, and a staged evaluation that avoids cherry-picking from a huge search space. The honest reporting of both improved and worsened counts (rather than net deltas alone) is exemplary.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong — code and data generation released, synthetic task fully specified, appendix heuristics documented. The main barrier is compute access for the 68k-generation steering sweep, which is modest by modern standards.
- **Significance / external validity — the main caveat**: everything rests on *one* 3M-parameter model trained from scratch on *one* synthetic arithmetic task. The conclusion that "the linear representation hypothesis is insufficient for reasoning" may be an artifact of scale, of SFT-on-search-traces (rather than RL post-training, which is how frontier reasoning models are actually made), or of the task's token layout. The authors frame the setting as a controlled sandbox and are careful with hedged language ("experimental evidence suggesting"), but the abstract-level claim is stronger than the single-config evidence base.
- **Red flags / missing pieces**: (1) No nonlinear baseline — the closing call for "nonlinear representational geometries" is not backed by any experiment showing a nonlinear probe/steerer does better; a small MLP probe or SAE-feature steering experiment would have converted the negative result into a constructive one. (2) The probe's ~0.94 AUROC for final-answer correctness could partly encode *problem difficulty* (some problems are simply unsolveable-adjacent or harder) rather than on-track-ness; the reachability label mitigates but does not eliminate this, and the two labels' directions only align at cosine ~0.5. (3) Direct Optimization optimizes teacher-forced token-level cross-entropy on a valid answer while evaluation is free-running generation — a train/eval mismatch the paper acknowledges only implicitly. (4) At 71.12% base accuracy the ceiling is far away, so the null result is not explained by "no room to improve" — good — but the flip side is that on a task this algorithmic, correctness may be distributed rather than localized, which limits what any steering study could show.

## Strengths

- Turns a vague question ("do reasoning models know when they're right?") into an exactly labelable, fully controllable experimental design with reachability ground truth at every state.
- The Direct Optimization control is the paper's smartest move: it directly refutes the most obvious objection that probe/CAA directions were never meant to be causal.
- Honest, bidirectional reporting of steering outcomes; the search over 236 configurations makes the null hard to attribute to under-searching.
- Practical task design: target-disjoint splitting deftly handles the near-duplicate-trace leakage problem that plagues synthetic-task evaluation (their Example 2).
- The critique of Zhang et al. (2025) is backed by their own reproduction number (56.7% of AIME 2025 traces over the token cap), not hand-waving.

## Limitations

- Single 3M-parameter model, single synthetic task, SFT-trained — no evidence yet that the pattern transfers to billion-parameter RL-trained reasoners on GSM8K/AIME-style tasks.
- The decodability result (0.94 AUROC) conflates self-monitoring with problem difficulty; no experiment separates the two.
- No nonlinear probing or steering baseline, so the suggested explanation (linear geometry is the wrong shape for reasoning correctness) remains conjecture.
- Steering evaluation escalates to the full 16k split only for promising conditions, which is sensible but means the headline numbers (5/256 etc.) rest on the smaller staged splits.
- Human-inspired A* heuristics shape the training distribution of backtracking; conclusions about "correctness directions" may be sensitive to this particular trace distribution (no ablation over data-generation strategy).

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是关于线性探针、激活转向向量与表示几何（linear representation hypothesis）在推理正确性上的一次系统因果检验，其"可解码但不可因果操纵"的阴性结果对理解隐藏状态中正确性信号的因果结构有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the decodable-but-not-steerable pattern replicate at 1B+ scale on RL-trained reasoning models (e.g., GRPO-trained models on GSM8K/AIME), or is it an artifact of 3M-parameter SFT models?
- Would a nonlinear intervention — an SAE feature, a small MLP probe used as a controller, or a low-rank multi-direction subspace — succeed where single-vector steering fails, substantiating the paper's closing conjecture?
- Can the ~0.94 AUROC correctness signal be decomposed into "problem difficulty" vs "on-track-ness" components (e.g., by probing reachability at matched states across problems of matched difficulty)?
- If correctness directions cannot steer generation, are they still useful non-causally — for example, as a backtracking trigger or early-exit/verification signal that decides *when* to intervene with other tools?
- Does the failure of Direct Optimization under free-running evaluation reflect exposure-bias-style train/eval mismatch, and would optimizing against sampled rollouts (rather than teacher-forced CE on a gold answer) recover causal control?
