The 4,096-response rollout batch needs 2.34× the samples of the 512 batch (ηS = 1e-5) — yet both still collapse at the same 30–50k consumed samples.

Why this matters

Maps how RLVR batch scaling slows learning and locks collapse to sample count — with a batch-ramp schedule that tracks the best fixed batch.

⚠ Critical edge

Every headline result is measured in the early-training, low-accuracy regime (15–25% val accuracy on a 1.5B model) — whether these scaling laws survive production-scale 60%+ runs is untested. limits ↓

01Methodology — one theory, two clocks

Progress clock — runs fast on small batches

r(b) = r₀ − C_curv·b per-sample progress falls linearly with batch; critical batch B* ∝ 1/κ shrinks as LR grows

Failure clock — ticks in samples only

N_fail = C·√b/η → C/κ under the √-rule the failure point is batch-invariant: collapse arrives on a sample schedule

Theory. One AdamW step on a locally quadratic loss (optimizer batches verified far below the measured gradient noise scale) shows the square-root rule η = κ√b fixes drift per consumed response — but progress per response falls with batch, because fewer, longer steps lose more to curvature. Failure follows a single drift-threshold hypothesis; competing noise-driven (b/η²) and batch-blind (1/η) laws were formulated and statistically rejected.

Experiments. Controlled GRPO sweeps with the DAPO objective in verl; clipping toggled on / off / widened; frozen-checkpoint replays measure what the clip removes; censoring-aware (Tobit) fits and likelihood-ratio tests separate the failure laws across 21 unclipped runs.

04Strengths

  • One simple two-clock idea cleanly explains three previously separate phenomena — batch slowdown, sample-invariant failure, batch-dependent clip benefit — with falsifiable exponent predictions that survive statistical testing.
  • Exceptional hygiene: pre-registered failure criteria, censoring-aware (Tobit) statistics, controls for every confound (clip, grad-norm clip, bf16 ρ ≠ 1 artifacts), per-run failure tables in the appendix.
  • Non-obvious confirmed prediction: B* sits 60–2,000× below the measured gradient noise scale and moves with the learning rate — neither prior critical-batch nor one-step invariance results predict this.
  • Directly actionable takeaway (start below B*, ramp before failure) with an honest switch-point sensitivity sweep (25k–74k all work).

05Limitations

  • Established only in the early-training, low-accuracy regime (15–25% on DAPO-Math-17k, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B); larger models (>7B), non-math domains, and PPO-style algorithms untested.
  • The theory's core quantities — curvature ḡᵀHḡ and drift threshold D — are inferred, not measured; B* estimates from different routes differ up to ~2×.
  • Two seeds for most configs (sometimes one); acknowledged seed-to-seed failure-point variation up to ~2×.
  • Efficiency is counted in generated responses — token, GPU-hour, and wall-clock efficiency, which the ramp is really about, are not measured.
  • The failure criterion (accuracy halving / 4× grad-norm excursion) is a proxy that smooths over heterogeneous failure modes catalogued in the appendix.

06Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the sample-invariant failure clock persist at production-scale accuracy (60%+ on AIME-style splits), or does the small-batch lead invert late in training?
  2. Can the drift threshold D be estimated cheaply online (within-rollout log-ratio spread, KL divergence) so the batch-ramp switch becomes adaptive rather than pre-tuned?
  3. How does the two-clock picture interact with asynchronous / off-policy GRPO, where staleness adds another drift source?
  4. Do soft trust-region replacements for the hard ratio clip shift the critical batch B*, or does curvature still bound progress?
  5. Can the curvature term ḡᵀHḡ be approximated with Hessian-vector products on a subsample to measure B* directly?
