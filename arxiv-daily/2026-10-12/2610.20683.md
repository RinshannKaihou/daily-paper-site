> **一句话总结：** 这篇 NeurIPS 2026 LP4FM workshop 论文用统一的因果干预流水线（均值消融、激活修补、贪心电路提取、随机对照显著性检验、激活转向）在 55 个"模型×语言"设置上研究代码模型的 Python 默认倾向是否由小组件承载：偏好本身很大、随规模增长且经指令微调后反而增强 12%，但"是否可局部化"的答案几乎完全由两个常被当作实现细节的选择决定——把组件预算从 12 提到 40 使显著特异性电路从 1/15 变为 6/15，把手造对齐提示换成 XLCoST 并行函数使同一指标的读数从 ≈1.6 崩塌到 ≈0.01（两个数量级），而所有显著指向反方向的电路都源于提取失败而非模型现象。

> **TL;DR:** A mechanistic-interpretability study asking whether code models' Python defaultness ("write a function" → Python idioms) is carried by a small, steerable circuit. One pipeline (mean ablation → activation patching → greedy circuit extraction → Welch t-test vs 30 size-matched random controls with Benjamini–Hochberg correction → difference-in-means steering) runs over 55 settings: 10 open code models (CodeLlama-7B, DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B/6.7B, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B/14B, each base + instruct), 3 target languages (Rust, C#, PHP), 2 prompt suites, 2 component budgets. The preference itself is robust — instruction tuning *increases* separation S by 12% on average (all 15 settings). But every mechanistic conclusion flips with analysis knobs the earlier 15-setting version treated as setup: raising the budget k from 12 to 40 takes specific circuits from 1/15 to 6/15; swapping the hand-built aligned suite for XLCoST parallel functions collapses S from ≈1.6 to ≈0.01 on the same models; patching flips the language in 2/45 and steering flips the argmax token in 9/45 settings — interventions the small-budget study reported as uniformly failing. The transferable lesson: circuit papers should report budget sweeps like ablation papers report seed sweeps, and state localisation as a curve over budgets, not a single number.

## 核心贡献

- **对"编程语言默认倾向"的首次跨模型因果研究：** 定义 programming-language defaultness（提示未指定语言时代码模型倾向 Python），并明确这是机制问题而非规范性问题——关心的是"用户能否在推理时改写这一默认"。覆盖 10 个开源代码模型（5 base + 5 instruct）、3 个目标语言、2 套提示、2 种组件预算，共 55 个设置。
- **五个 boxed 发现，且每条都注明其证据边界：** (1) 偏好很大、随模型规模增长、且指令微调后更强（15/15 设置，平均 +12%）；(2) 测得的偏好大小属于探针而非模型——换用 XLCoST 自然并行函数后 S 从 ≈1.6 跌到 ≈0.01；(3) 局部化与否取决于搜索预算——k=12 时 1/15 设置得到显著电路，k=40 时 6/15；(4) 小预算研究判为"全面失败"的干预有时能成功——修补在 2/45、转向在 9/45 设置翻转输出语言；(5) 所有显著但指向反方向的电路都来自提取失败的设置，是搜索的诊断信号而非模型现象。
- **为每条局部化主张设置随机对照硬门：** 每条主张都要与 30 个同规模随机组件集做 Welch t 检验，并在每个探针网格内用 Benjamini–Hochberg 控制错误发现率（qBH < 0.05）——把因果抽象文献强调的对照要求落成可复现的流程。
- **方法学警示（作者认为最可迁移的结论）：** 两个通常被报告为"实验设置"的旋钮——搜索保留的组件数、读取指标的提示套件——把论文的头条结论从"不可局部化、不可转向"改写为"6/15 可局部化、9/45 可转向"，从"大而稳健的偏置"改写为"小两个数量级的偏置"。主张局部化论文应报告预算扫描曲线。
- **否定"紧凑语法电路"的存在（与自然语言神经元文献对照）：** 电路在各模型间无共享结构（跨越全部深度范围、几乎全是 MLP 介导）；逐个删除组件（leave-one-out）不改变行为；CodeXGLUE 任务在转向下钉在随机水平——方向编码的是表层语法而非任务能力。

## Key Contributions

- **First causal, cross-model study of programming-language defaultness:** defines the behaviour (code models default to Python when the prompt leaves the language open), frames it as a mechanistic question — could an inference-time edit redirect the default, as reported for refusal and natural-language identity? — and runs one intervention pipeline over 55 model–language settings spanning ten open code models, three target languages (Rust, C#, PHP), two prompt suites, and two component budgets.
- **Five findings, each boxed where its evidence is presented:** (1) the preference is large, grows with model size, and is *stronger* after instruction tuning, not weaker (larger S in all 15 base-vs-instruct pairs, +12% on average); (2) the measured size belongs to the probe — the same ΔM metric reads ≈1.6 on the aligned prompt suite but ≈0.01 on XLCoST parallel functions, a two-order-of-magnitude swing on identical models; (3) whether the preference looks localised depends on the component budget (1/15 specific circuits at k=12 vs 6/15 at k=40, both after multiple-comparison correction); (4) interventions a smaller-budget study reported as uniformly failing do sometimes succeed (patching flips the language in 2/45 settings, steering flips the argmax next token in 9/45); (5) every significant away-pointing circuit comes from a setting where greedy extraction returned the full candidate set — such results diagnose the search, not the model.
- **A hard random-control gate on every localisation claim:** each extracted circuit is compared against N = 30 size-matched random component sets with a Welch t-test, FDR-controlled within each probe grid via Benjamini–Hochberg (qBH < 0.05), operationalising the controls that causal-abstraction work demands.
- **The transferable methodological lesson:** two knobs normally reported as setup — how many components the search keeps and which prompts the metric is read on — moved the headline conclusions from "not localisable, never steerable" to "localisable in 6/15, steerable in 9/45". Circuit papers should report a budget sweep the way ablation papers report a seed sweep, and state localisation claims as curves over budgets rather than single numbers.
- **Evidence against a compact, transferable syntax circuit:** surviving circuits share no structure across models (MLP-mediated, spanning L9 in CodeLlama-7B to L29 in DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B and L33–L43 in Qwen2.5-Coder-14B); leave-one-out removal of any component (including the specific circuit itself) leaves marker accuracy/F1 at 1.000; and two CodeXGLUE tasks stay pinned at chance under steering, so the direction encodes surface syntax, not task competence.

## Methodology

The paper studies ten models — CodeLlama-7B, DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B/6.7B, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B/14B, each in base and instruction-tuned form — through TransformerLens(-bridge) hooks, with components addressed uniformly as (kind, layer, head) triples (attention heads via `hook_z`, MLPs via `hook_mlp_out`).

**Preference metric (ΔM and S).** Instead of a discrete language label, the paper reads a continuous signal at a scored position: ΔM(x) = Σ p(t|x) over a hand-built vocabulary of target-syntax-opening tokens (e.g., `fn`, `mut`, `::` for Rust; `public`, `static`, `;` etc. for C#) minus the sum over Python-opening tokens (`def`, `self`, `:`). Overlapping token ids are dropped so shared C-family syntax is never double-counted. Separation S = ΔM(x_t) − ΔM(x_b) summarises a Python/target prompt pair. Two prompt suites: (i) 64 structurally aligned function pairs built from four templates (ADD, SQUARE, GREET, IS_EVEN), each ending in an explicit language tag so the model must commit at a known position; (ii) 128 naturally occurring parallel function pairs from XLCoST, where the scored position is wherever the pair happens to end. Held-out behavioural prompts (translation, completion, and English→French / Python-completion controls) keep generation checks separate from causal estimates.

**Interventions.** Four operators on ΔM: (1) *Necessity* — mean-ablate each component (using suite-averaged activations, not zeroing) and rank by the resulting ΔM drop; carry the top k forward, with k ∈ {12, 40} run as a full grid. (2) *Sufficiency* — transplant target-run activations of a candidate set into the clean Python run and test whether ΔM crosses zero. (3) *Minimality* — greedily add components ranked by individual patched shift until 90% (τ = 0.90) of the full-set effect, a node-level approximation of edge attribution patching. (4) *Specificity gate* — compare the circuit's per-pair patched shifts against 30 size-matched random component sets (Welch t-test) with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction within each probe grid; a circuit is "specific" only if qBH < 0.05 and its mean shift points toward the target language. *Steering* adds the difference-in-means direction d_c = E[act_target − act_base], scaled by α ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8}, during the forward pass on Python prompts, tracked by ΔM, argmax-token checks, and downstream generation with control-contamination checks. Config: seed 0, 1×A100-80GB, fp16 (bf16 for Qwen, which produced NaNs in fp16 on the bridge path); full code and all 55 result files are released.

## Results & Highlights

- **The preference is large, scales, and strengthens with instruction tuning.** The sign of ΔM recovers the tagged language in 45/45 aligned-prompt settings without exception. Mean separation S grows with size within each family — 1.51 (DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B) → 1.61 (6.7B); 1.73 (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B) → 1.81 (14B) — and the instruction-tuned checkpoint beats its base sibling in all 15 settings, by 12% on average and up to 30% (DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B on Rust: S 1.49 → 1.93). On ten untagged instruction prompts the Python default survives but is faint: Python-leaning in 12/15 settings, one to two orders of magnitude smaller than tagged S.
- **The measured size belongs to the probe, not the model.** Rerunning the identical pipeline on 128 XLCoST parallel function pairs collapses separation from ≈1.6 to below 0.012 in every one of the 10 settings run (two orders of magnitude; e.g., CodeLlama-7B/C#: S = 1.551 aligned vs +0.0117 on XLCoST), and 0/10 XLCoST settings yield a specific circuit or a movable metric — at naturally occurring end-of-pair positions almost no probability mass sits on either vocabulary.
- **Localisation depends on the component budget.** After Benjamini–Hochberg correction against 30 size-matched random controls, 1/15 aligned base settings yields a specific circuit at k = 12 (CodeLlama-7B/Rust, shift +0.066, qBH = 0.030) but 6/15 do at k = 40 — two of them large: DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B/C# shifts ΔM by +0.61 and Qwen2.5-Coder-7B/PHP by +1.25, against random-control means below 0.03. Only CodeLlama-7B/Rust is specific at both budgets; the instruction-tuned grid at k = 12 adds 2/15, one of them (+1.27, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B/Rust) larger than anything on the base models.
- **Interventions reported as uniformly failing do sometimes work.** Patching the extracted circuit moves ΔM across zero in 2 of 45 aligned/instruct settings — both Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (base/PHP at k = 40: −0.91 → +0.50 via L22–L24MLP + L27H13; instruct/Rust at k = 12: −0.99 → +0.50 led by L24MLP) — while every other setting stays below −0.6 except DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B/C# at −0.14. Steering changes the argmax next token to target syntax in 9/45 settings (6 base at k = 40, 3 instruct; 0/15 at k = 12 on base, which is what the earlier version reported).
- **Steering successes are fragile and coefficient-dependent.** Three of nine switches occur at α = 1; six need α ≥ 2 and four need α ≥ 4, where behaviour degrades: on Qwen2.5-Coder-14B/C#, α ≥ 4 injects 126–128 C# markers into control prompts that should contain none (control false-positive rate 1.00). The safe window is model- and language-specific — Qwen2.5-Coder-7B tolerates α = 8 on Rust and α = 4 on PHP but α = 0 on C# (i.e., C# cannot be steered safely at any tested strength).
- **Controls hold the metric honest.** Vocabulary-size confound bounded: doubling the target token set (6 → 12 ids) moves mean S only from 1.603 (Rust) to 1.650 (C#) / 1.663 (PHP) — 3.7% of S — with no association across the 15 base settings (Spearman ρ = +0.20, p = 0.48). Leave-one-out steering from the CodeLlama-7B/Rust set leaves marker accuracy and F1 at 1.000 for every removal (ΔM moves < 0.02). Two CodeXGLUE tasks (clone detection, defect detection) stay at 0.50 chance accuracy under steering — the direction carries surface syntax, not competence. All five significant away-pointing circuits (largest: CodeLlama-7B/C# at −0.43, reproducing across budgets and the instruct checkpoint) come from settings where extraction returned the full 40-component candidate set; at k = 40, full-set circuits have median shift −0.04 vs +0.05 for compact ones.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The negative-ish, meta-scientific result is genuinely useful: to my knowledge this and its earlier version are the first causal, cross-model attempts to localise programming-language identity, and the demonstration that the localisation verdict flips with the component budget (1/15 → 6/15) and the prompt suite (S ≈ 1.6 → ≈ 0.01) is a concrete, quotable warning for the circuit-discovery literature. That said, "analysis choices matter" is a known caution in causal-abstraction work (Geiger et al.), which the authors cite; the novelty is in quantifying it for one behaviour across 55 settings rather than in the conceptual point.

**Rigor.** Mostly high: random-control gating with FDR correction is more than most circuit papers do, and the vocabulary-size confound, untagged-prompt control, argmax-vs-metric distinction, and control-contamination stress test are all well designed. Weaknesses: a single seed (0) for everything, including the 30 random control sets; the aligned suite is built from only four trivial templates (ADD, SQUARE, GREET, IS_EVEN), so the "constructed decision point" being narrow is acknowledged but its 64 pairs are highly templated; and only two budgets (12, 40) are tested — the call to report "curves over budgets" is backed by two points per claim, which is a line segment, not a curve.

**Reproducibility.** Strong: released code/configs/result files, TransformerLens hooks with a documented bridge for non-registry models, the fp16-NaN → bf16 fix for Qwen reported, per-run cost stated. The k=12 vs k=40 asymmetry (Table 4's instruct column exists only at k=12) slightly complicates exact replication of the 55-setting grid.

**Significance.** As a workshop paper, its practical impact for practitioners is honestly modest — the authors themselves conclude steering is "not a reliable dial" and prompting/fine-tuning remain the dependable route. The durable contribution is methodological for interpretability researchers.

**Red flags.** (1) The aligned-prompt suite manufactures its own decision point — the authors concede the ≈1.6 separation "is not the size of the bias a developer meets", which means Finding 1's headline magnitude is probe-specific by construction even though its sign survives. (2) The XLCoST collapse (≈0.01) could partly reflect that the scored position in naturally occurring code is mid-function where no language keyword is due next — the authors say this, but it means the two suites are not measuring the same thing at the same position, weakening the "same measurement" framing. (3) C# and PHP vocabularies share markers (`;`, `public`, `foreach`, `array`) with other C-family languages, so target-language attribution for those two is noisier than for Rust's `fn`. (4) The instruction-tuned grid exists only at k = 12, so the k = 40 budget story for tuned models is untested. (5) Away-pointing circuits being extraction failures is convincing for CodeLlama-7B/C# (depth-band analysis), but generalising from five results to "such results are a diagnostic" is a small-n inference.

## Strengths

- A single, unified causal pipeline (ablation → patching → extraction → random-control significance → steering) applied identically across 55 settings, ten models, and four probe variants — the grid itself is the contribution.
- Every localisation claim is gated on size-matched random controls with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction, a standard most circuit papers skip.
- Exemplary self-correction: the paper openly reports that its own earlier version's negative conclusion was an artifact of the k = 12 budget and the aligned prompt suite, and quantifies exactly how each knob moved the answer.
- Honest intervention reading: separating metric crossings from argmax flips and control contamination, and showing the safe steering window is per-setting (α = 8 / 4 / 0 for one model across three languages) rather than claiming a general steering success.
- Clean negative controls: vocabulary-size confound bounded (ρ = +0.20, p = 0.48), leave-one-out shows no load-bearing component, CodeXGLUE tasks pinned at chance under steering.
- Full release of code, configurations, prompt suites, and all 55 result files, enabling exactly the budget/probe sweeps the paper advocates.

## Limitations

- The ΔM metric uses small hand-chosen syntax vocabularies read at a single position; set-size effects are bounded (§4.2) but not eliminated, and C#/PHP markers overlap with other C-family languages.
- Only Python serves as the base language and only three targets (Rust, C#, PHP) are tested — the two most popular non-Python languages in practice (JavaScript, Java/C++ ecosystems) are absent.
- Two sizes per family cannot establish a scaling law (the authors say so); the untagged default even *decreases* from Qwen-7B to Qwen-14B, so the scale trend is probe-dependent.
- Minimality is node-level and only approximates edge attribution patching; steering adds a single difference-in-means direction, so Finding 4 bounds these methods, not all possible interventions (e.g., no SAE-feature or edge-level steering).
- Single seed (0); the instruct grid runs only at k = 12; the aligned suite rests on four templates, so diversity of the "decision point" is limited.
- XLCoST runs cover only C# and PHP (not Rust), leaving the probe-collapse evidence at 10 rather than 15 settings.

## Relevance

如果研究兴趣覆盖 LLM 可解释性/机制分析与代码模型：这是对"代码模型语言偏好是否可局部化"的少见系统性因果研究，其"组件预算与探针选择决定局部化结论"的方法学警告对所有 circuit / activation steering 工作都有直接参考价值；对多语言/代码 LLM 的训练与推理时控制（不重训即可切换默认语言）也给出了明确的能力边界。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the budget-sensitivity finding (1/15 at k=12 vs 6/15 at k=40) persist under sparse-autoencoder feature circuits or edge attribution patching at the node level, where the unit of analysis is finer than attention heads/MLP blocks?
- Does the probe collapse (S ≈ 1.6 aligned vs ≈ 0.01 XLCoST) reflect a genuine property of naturally occurring code positions, or would scoring XLCoST prompts at *injected* decision points (e.g., right after a docstring) recover a measurable preference?
- Why is instruction tuning *strengthening* the separation (+12% on average) — is this an artifact of instruction data composition (Python-heavy), and would a balanced-multilingual instruction tune shrink it?
- Can the two Qwen2.5-Coder-7B settings where patching flips the language (base/PHP, instruct/Rust) be characterised structurally — what makes that model steerable when CodeLlama and DeepSeek are not, and does the property survive further scale (32B, 110B+)?
- Is there a steering coefficient schedule (per-layer α, or projection-based methods like ITI-style null-space projection) that widens the safe window beyond the current narrow, per-setting ranges and avoids control contamination at α ≥ 4?
- Do the away-pointing extraction failures generalise — can "extracted circuit size ≈ full candidate set" be formalised as an automatic validity flag for greedy circuit discovery in other domains (refusal, in-context learning)?
