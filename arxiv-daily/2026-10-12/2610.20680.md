> **一句话总结：** 本文系统研究了稀疏自编码器（SAE）到底需要多少激活数据：在 Gemma-2-2B/Pythia-160M/Qwen3-8B 上用 4 种 SAE 架构、5M–500M token 预算训练后发现，重构等"功能型"指标在 5M–50M token 时即接近 500M 参考（CE loss score 0.980 vs 0.987），但一对一特征可复现性仍随数据持续提升（BatchTopK 严格匹配特征从 2.20% 升至 28.86%），且理论分析表明统计一致性不足以保证特征恢复——因此 SAE 训练预算应按目标匹配，而非默认使用最大语料。

> **TL;DR:** The first systematic study of how much activation data SAE training actually needs. Across 4 SAE architectures (ReLU, BatchTopK, JumpReLU, Matryoshka BatchTopK) on 3 LLMs (Gemma-2-2B, Pythia-160M, Qwen3-8B), the authors train at 5M/25M/50M-token budgets against a 500M-token reference and evaluate with SAEBench plus one-to-one Hungarian feature matching. Functional metrics converge early — on Gemma-2-2B BatchTopK-16k, the CE loss-recovered score is 0.980 at 5M vs 0.987 at 500M — but strict feature reproducibility keeps improving with data: the fraction of shared features (τ≥0.7, both encoder and decoder matched) rises from 2.20% at 5M to 28.86% at 50M for BatchTopK, and from 1.14% to 18.24% on Qwen3-8B. Random sampling preserves reconstruction while farthest-point sampling shifts training toward high-norm activations (+28.8% mean norm, KL 2.28 bits at 5M) and wins AutoInterp in 79% of matched comparisons. Practical upshot: most SAE training can use 10–100x less data if only functional quality matters, but feature-level claims need large budgets and explicit reproducibility checks.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统研究 SAE 的数据需求**：在 3 个语言模型（Gemma-2-2B、Pythia-160M、Qwen3-8B）× 4 种 SAE 架构（ReLU、BatchTopK、JumpReLU、Matryoshka BatchTopK）× 2 种字典宽度（16K/65K）上，以 5M/25M/50M/500M token 预算在线训练，量化"多少激活数据才够"。
- **区分两种收敛概念**：功能行为（SAEBench 上的重构、稀疏探针、AutoInterp 等）与特征级收敛（一对一特征可复现性），并证明二者数据需求截然不同——功能指标早熟，特征复现随数据持续改善。
- **理论刻画**：定理 1 证明在统计一致性 + 优化一致性 + 特征可识别性条件下，子采样训练与全量训练渐近恢复同一特征系统（仅差置换）；推论 2 进一步表明"编码器-解码器匹配一致"弱于"强特征恢复"，解释了实证差距。
- **数据选择策略的权衡**：随机采样保持激活分布（KL ≤ 0.0003 bits）、利于重构与干预类指标；最远点采样（FPS）偏向高范数激活、在 79% 的对比中提升 AutoInterp——"选哪些激活"与"选多少"同样重要。
- **可操作的实践建议**：按用途匹配预算——功能评估无需最大语料，而把单个 latent 当作科学对象使用时必须显式检验特征可复现性。

## Key Contributions

- First systematic data-requirements study for SAEs: a grid of 4 architectures × 3 LLMs × multiple dictionary widths/sparsity operating points, trained online on FineWeb at budgets of 5M/25M/50M tokens vs a 500M-token reference, all following SAEBench configurations for direct comparability.
- A clean conceptual split between **functional convergence** (SAEBench metrics: loss recovered, sparse probing, absorption, SCR, TPP, RAVEL, AutoInterp, unlearning) and **feature convergence** (one-to-one Hungarian matching of encoder/decoder features against the 500M reference, following Paulo & Belrose), showing the two have different data-scaling behavior.
- A theoretical framework (Theorem 1, Corollaries 1–2) separating statistical consistency of subsampled training from the stronger identifiability and optimization conditions required for one-to-one feature recovery, and proving that assignment agreement between encoder- and decoder-based matchings does not imply strong directional alignment at finite budgets.
- A matched-budget comparison of data-selection strategies showing metric-dependent tradeoffs: random sampling is distribution-neutral and favors reconstruction/intervention metrics, while FPS concentrates on high-norm activations (quantified via norm-distribution KL divergence) and frequently improves AutoInterp.
- Actionable guidance: SAE training can often be 10–100x more data-efficient when budgets are matched to the goal, and any feature-level claim should be backed by an explicit reproducibility check.

## Methodology

**Training setup.** SAEs are trained online on FineWeb: text is streamed through a frozen LLM, target-layer activations are buffered, and mini-batches are drawn from selected activations before the buffer refreshes; selected activations are used exactly once (no fixed replay dataset). The reference configuration targets 500M activation tokens with the `dictionary_learning` framework's standard settings; reduced-data runs retain a fraction q ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 1.0} of activations at each buffer refresh while holding the LLM, activation site, dictionary width, sparsity setting, and optimization hyperparameters fixed — isolating data scale from training configuration.

**Models and architectures.** Three LLMs at one residual-stream layer each — Gemma-2-2B (layer 12), Pythia-160M (layer 6), Qwen3-8B (layer 17) — with four SAE architectures (ReLU, BatchTopK, JumpReLU, Matryoshka BatchTopK) at 16K and 65K dictionary widths (65K only for Qwen3-8B), and multiple sparsity operating points (L1 penalties, k ∈ {80, 160}).

**Two axes of study.** (1) Quantity: budget ∈ {5M, 25M, 50M} vs the 500M reference. (2) Selection: random sampling (RS) vs farthest-point sampling (FPS) applied to identical activation pools.

**Evaluation.** All functional metrics come from SAEBench defaults (loss recovered, k-sparse probing with k=1, absorption, SCR, TPP, RAVEL, automated interpretability, unlearning). Feature reproducibility against the 500M reference uses one-to-one Hungarian matching computed independently for encoder and decoder features by cosine similarity; a latent is "shared" at threshold τ if both matchings pick the same reference latent and both cosines exceed τ (τ = 0.7 by default). "Counterpart agreement" is the weaker criterion that both matchings select the same reference latent.

**Theory.** Theorem 1 shows d_E(θ̂_q,N, θ̂_1,N) → 0 and d_D(θ̂_q,N, θ̂_1,N) → 0 (encoder/decoder feature systems align up to permutation) under four assumptions: common population objective, uniform statistical consistency, optimization consistency, and feature identifiability with a positive separation margin. Corollary 2 formalizes why empirical counterpart agreement can be high while strict shared-feature recovery is low.

## Results & Highlights

- **Reconstruction converges at tiny budgets.** On Gemma-2-2B layer-12 BatchTopK-16k (SAEBench CE loss-recovered score, higher = better), the 5M-token SAE reaches 0.980 vs 0.987 for the 500M reference, and 25M/50M runs hit 0.985 — a gap of only 0.002–0.007 from a 100x-smaller budget. Sparse-probing Top-1 accuracy shows the same early plateau: 0.907 (5M), 0.928 (25M), 0.947 (50M) vs 0.950 (500M), with smaller budgets sometimes outperforming the reference on individual configs.
- **But feature reproducibility is far from converged at the same budgets (Gemma-2-2B, Table 2).** For BatchTopK, strict shared-feature recovery at τ≥0.7 rises from 2.20% (5M) to 16.94% (25M) to 28.86% (50M) — the abstract's "2% → 29%" headline — while weaker counterpart agreement rises only from 66.61% to 83.47%. For ReLU SAEs, shared features go from 28.42% to 47.26% and counterpart agreement from 76.15% to 85.28%. At 5M the mean matched decoder cosine for BatchTopK is just 0.454.
- **The pattern generalizes across models (Table 25).** Qwen3-8B BatchTopK shared features at τ≥0.7: 1.14% (5M) → 8.83% (25M) → 18.24% (50M); Qwen3-8B ReLU: 18.58% → 29.51%; Pythia-160M ReLU reaches 79.85% counterpart agreement at 50M. Functional similarity thus demonstrably does not imply feature reproducibility.
- **Selection strategy reshapes quality (Table 1, Gemma-2-2B).** FPS at 5M shifts the selected set's mean activation L2 norm by +28.8% with KL divergence 2.28 bits vs the 500M stream; random sampling is distribution-neutral at every budget (0.0% norm shift, KL ≤ 0.0003 bits, mean-direction cosine 1.000). RS better preserves loss recovered, SCR, TPP, and RAVEL (sometimes exceeding the 500M reference), while FPS wins AutoInterp at every budget in both headline configs and in 79% of matched comparisons across all architectures, models, and budgets.
- **AutoInterp is the most data-hungry functional metric** in the headline config: 0.729 (5M) → 0.765 (50M) vs 0.787 (500M) for Gemma-2-2B BatchTopK-16k, consistent with FPS's high-norm, diversity-seeking selection helping exactly this metric.
- **Qualitative confirmation (Table 3).** Matched Gemma-2-2B ReLU feature pairs with low/medium alignment carry different semantic descriptions (e.g., "medical/cancer words" matched to "mathematical formatting and code"), while only high-alignment (0.8–1.0) pairs recover the same semantics (comparative adjectives followed by "than") — visualizing why counterpart identity alone is a weak recovery criterion.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and useful — the "how much data does an SAE need" question had not been systematically tested, and the functional-vs-feature-convergence split, backed by the identifiability theory, is a real conceptual contribution rather than a benchmark rerun.
- **Rigor — the single-run problem is the biggest weakness for a paper about reproducibility.** The SAEBench metric figures and the feature-matching tables (Tables 2, 25) appear to report single training runs with no error bars or seed replicates. Given that the paper's own citation [9] (Paulo & Belrose) shows SAEs trained on the *same* data with different seeds learn substantially different features, a single 500M reference is a noisy yardstick: some of the "2.20% shared features" gap could be seed variance rather than data-budget effect. Distinguishing data-driven from seed-driven irreproducibility would require multi-seed references, which the study lacks.
- **Reference budget is 500M, not billions.** The abstract motivates the study with practice of "hundreds of millions to billions" of tokens, but all conclusions are relative to a 500M-token reference. Whether feature reproducibility also saturates somewhere between 500M and the multi-billion-token regimes used for GPT-4/Claude-scale SAEs is untested, so "diminishing returns" is established only within this range.
- **Incomplete metric coverage across models.** As the authors admit, SAEBench's RAVEL and unlearning evaluations support only Gemma models, so Qwen3-8B and Pythia-160M functional claims rest on a reduced metric set.
- **Ambiguity in the budgeting design.** The paper fixes the "500M training configuration and optimization steps" while retaining only a fraction of activations per buffer refresh; the exact coupling between gradient-step count and unique activation count across budgets is not spelled out, leaving a residual confound between data scale and optimization exposure (e.g., effective epochs over the retained pool).
- **Missing ablations:** no wall-clock/compute accounting for FPS (which is itself expensive), no test of whether interpolating RS and FPS (or simply clipping high-norm activations) reproduces the AutoInterp gains, and no analysis of how τ thresholds interact with dictionary width on the headline Gemma results.
- **Minor red flag:** the paper's code link is literally the word "here" with no visible URL in the PDF text, and figure-level numbers are sometimes only readable from bar charts rather than tables.
- **Reproducibility of the study itself is good:** standard frameworks (SAEBench, `dictionary_learning`), fully specified trainer indices/sparsity settings in the appendix, and online FineWeb streaming make the pipeline reconstructible.

## Strengths

- Attacks a question with immediate practical value (SAE training cost) that the community had left to default practice, and answers it with a broad, well-controlled experimental grid rather than a single anecdote.
- The functional/feature convergence distinction is sharpened by matching theory (identifiability vs consistency; Corollary 2 predicts the empirical counterpart-agreement gap) — rare discipline in interpretability empirics.
- The FPS analysis goes beyond "FPS helps AutoInterp" to explain *why*: a clean distributional characterization (norm shift, KL divergence, mean-direction cosine) showing FPS silently changes the training distribution while RS is provably neutral.
- Explicit, actionable recommendation (match budget to objective; run reproducibility checks before feature-level claims) that directly serves more reliable SAE science at lower cost.

## Limitations

- Feature-matching and SAEBench results appear to be single-seed; seed variance is a known large effect in SAE feature learning, so absolute shared-feature percentages carry unquantified uncertainty.
- Conclusions are anchored to a 500M-token reference; the billion-token regimes used at frontier scale are not tested, and the largest model studied is 8B parameters.
- RAVEL and unlearning metrics could not be run on Qwen3-8B or Pythia-160M (SAEBench limitation), so functional-metric coverage is uneven across models.
- All findings inherit SAEBench's implementation choices and metric-specific limitations, as the authors themselves note.
- Functional metrics beyond reconstruction are heterogeneous and non-monotonic in budget, which the paper reports honestly but does not fully explain.

## Relevance

直接切中可解释性研究的两个痛点：SAE 训练的数据成本与特征可复现性危机。如果你关注机制可解释性、SAE 评测或数据高效训练，本文给出了"按目标匹配预算"的量化依据，其一对一特征匹配协议也可直接复用于自己 SAE 实验的复现性检验。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the low shared-feature percentage at small budgets is seed variance rather than data scarcity? A multi-seed 500M reference (matching Paulo & Belrose's protocol) would separate the two effects.
- Does strict feature reproducibility keep climbing past 50M toward billion-token budgets, or does it plateau — and at what budget does BatchTopK reach, say, 50% shared features at τ≥0.7?
- Can the FPS AutoInterp gain be replicated by simply upweighting or clipping high-norm activations during training, decoupling "geometric diversity" from "norm shift"?
- Does fine-grained (Matryoshka) or JumpReLU sparsity change the functional-vs-feature divergence pattern, or is the BatchTopK gap representative?
- Would curriculum or importance-sampling strategies (e.g., proxy-model coreset selection) dominate both RS and FPS on all metrics simultaneously?
- Do functional-metric plateaus transfer to frontier-scale models (70B+), where activation distributions and feature granularity may differ qualitatively?
