错误相干性，而非错误率  ·  error coherence, not error rate

Majority-vote rewards track verifiable RL because their label errors are incoherent across prompts and cancel at the batch level.

Wrong Votes, Right Gradients: Why Majority-Vote Rewards Track Verifiable RL

Chengsong Huang · Dylan P. Wilson · Jiaxin Huang  —  Washington Univ. in St. Louis / Dartmouth College  ·  2026-10-12

  • reinforcement learning

Key metric

c∞ = 1.00

Disattenuated cosine between majority-vote and gold batch updates — 95% CI covers 1 on all six arms (Qwen3-4B/1.7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B; base + instruct). MV hits the gold self-consistency ceiling.

Why this matters

Tells you when label-free RL training can replace a verifier — and when shared misconceptions or reward bias make a verifier unavoidable.

Critical edge

The equivalence is local: per-step gradient agreement is not trajectory agreement — gold still ends ahead on held-out accuracy (maj@64 0.845 vs 0.772), and the full-training story rests on one model with two seeds.

01The instrument

Methodology — measure any reward on identical samples

  • Setup. GRPO (verl) on Qwen3-4B-Base, DeepScaleR 29k problems, B=256 prompts × n=16 responses, 26 steps; gradient analysis adds four more models, 1.7B–8B, two families.
  • Instrument. Per-response count sketches (K=216) of ∇log π make the batch update linear in advantages — so every reward is a reweighting of the same sketches (cosine error < 0.004 vs exact fp32).
  • Estimator. Split-half disattenuated cosine c∞ = X/√(Y·Z), benchmarked against the gold-vs-gold self-consistency ceiling (0.371–0.842), bootstrap CIs.
sample 16 responses / prompt→ count-sketch each ∇log π→ reweight by ANY reward→ cos(Gmv, Ggold)
Gmv = (1 − β) · Ggold + E⊥ β = shrinkage — behaves like a smaller learning rate (98% of the gold component delivered at step 20)  ·  E⊥ = misdirection — the only component that changes direction

03Strengths

Why this paper is worth your time

  • Mechanism, not another reward variant — batch-level reframing plus a shrinkage/misdirection decomposition of the MV update.
  • Reusable instrument — gradient sketches make any reward comparable on literally identical samples.
  • Textbook control — matched error rate with opposite error structure (random vs shared-rule flips) cleanly isolates the operative variable.
  • Theory confirmed twice — pairwise cosines and growth-exponent fits agree (crossover B*≈37 vs ≈50).
  • Exceptional honesty — unexplained step-10 anomaly and contamination checks surfaced in the main text; code, sketches, labels, configs and logs promised.

04Limitations

Read with these caveats

  • Thin training evidence — full runs use one model (Qwen3-4B-Base), one dataset, 26 steps, two seeds; the MV run's entropy blow-up after step 15 is unexamined.
  • MATH-500 only — no code generation, no out-of-domain benchmarks.
  • Raw-gradient analysis — Adam's preconditioning could reweight the misdirection component in ways the cosines don't capture.
  • Wide intervals — c∞ can exceed 1 by construction; some Llama-arm CIs are wide, and step-10 arms fall below the reportability threshold.
  • Shared error component grows faster than a random walk (exponent 0.594 vs 0.540, p=0.005) — long-run divergence is extrapolated, not measured.

05Questions for further exploration

Open threads the paper leaves behind

  1. Does the gold-vs-MV gap (0.845 vs 0.772) widen, close, or invert over hundreds of steps?
  2. Does Adam's preconditioner amplify the misdirection component in practice?
  3. Does error coherence predict failure under shared misconceptions (commonsense QA, buggy code patterns) or prompt-revisiting curricula?
  4. Would mixing a small fraction of verified labels into MV recover the gold trajectory at a fraction of the verification cost?
  5. Can gradient sketches scale cheaply enough to become a standard diagnostic for any proposed self-generated reward?
