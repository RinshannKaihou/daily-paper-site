⚠ Critical edge

The “safety readouts” are the authors’ own linear probes — a proxy for downstream computation, not an actual refusal circuit; no causal claim at the model level is supported.

Methodology · how RSS works

Transform → Filter → Freeze → Attribute

01

Transform

Six meaning-preserving prompt transformations, 5 operators × 3 realizations, on 1,000 fixed English prompts.

02

Filter

Keep features with recurrence rf ≥ 0.8 and directional consistency cf ≥ 0.8 (n = 5, ties excluded).

03

Freeze

Top-K by response magnitude form the support S (median 20/19 features); evaluated unchanged on disjoint held-out variants.

04

Attribute

Frozen linear readout on clean data; a readout-only counterfactual restores the selected inputs to clean values.

Feature change δv,f = af(xv) − af(x0) is measured at the aligned target position; mean absolute change hf pools candidates, then recurrence and directional-consistency constraints select the support, summarized by MeanRSS. Proposition 2 guarantees the selected direction survives any single-observation change (≥ 4/5 same-direction at ρ = κ = 0.8).

Scope: 27 pretrained SAEs from GPT-2 Small to Llama 3.1 8B (Gemma Scope / Llama Scope / Pythia / GPT-2 checkpoints); functional study on two frozen WildGuardMix safety probes — Gemma 3 4B layer 17 and Llama 3.1 8B layer 24.

  • Universality: context/wrapper transformations redistribute more activity than local edits in 27/27 SAEs (normalized gap 0.874, 95% CI [0.867, 0.881]); the ordering holds for all 1,000 prompts.
  • Persistence: held-out directional response rate 0.952 vs 0.758 for matched random supports; RSS beats magnitude-only selection (0.876 vs 0.816; 0.871 vs 0.794).

Assessment · upside

Strengths read with confidence

  • Exact theory throughout — Props 1–3 give a constrained-optimum characterization, finite-sample direction stability, and an exact per-feature erosion identity; no approximations.
  • Disciplined evaluation: realization-level selection/holdout split, controls matched on size, pool, and |wf|, prompt-clustered bootstrap CIs.
  • Broad base: 27 SAEs across 8 checkpoints (70M → 8B); the 27/27, 1000/1000 ordering is robust and quotable.
  • Practical payoff: ~20 readout-agnostic features catch ~90% of errors at a 20% review budget and reverse ~72% of transformed-probe errors.
  • Public code and only public SAE checkpoints and datasets.
Assessment · downside

Limitations read with care

  • All downstream conclusions concern frozen linear readouts fit by the authors; the counterfactual is offline — it alters the readout, not model behavior.
  • Holdout reuses the same five operator families, not new transformation types; MCR drops 43.0% → 32.6% for mismatched supports (Gemma).
  • Six hand-designed settings on English prompts; natural shift, multilingual input, and adversarial paraphrase are uncovered.
  • Not retention-optimal when readout info is available — the |ef| ranking retains >90% MCR vs RSS’s 43–56% at K = 20.
  • Intensity calibration is uneven: Spearman 0.95 (punctuation) vs 0.50 (format wrapper).
Open threads · from the paper itself

Questions for further exploration

  1. Do RSS supports transfer across transformation families (adversarial paraphrase, multilingual rewrite), or is 43–56% MCR a ceiling for readout-agnostic selection?
  2. Can the exact erosion decomposition extend beyond linear readouts — attention-value pathways, multi-layer probes, the model’s own refusal direction — while preserving the sign rule?
  3. Does the universal context/wrapper > local-edit ordering persist under natural distribution shift (chat formats, system prompts, tool-use scaffolds)?
  4. Would restoring RSS-selected feature values inside the LM reverse behavioral errors at rates near the ~72% seen at the readout level?
  5. How sensitive are the reported AUROCs to the ≥ 2-of-10 recurrent-failure threshold?
