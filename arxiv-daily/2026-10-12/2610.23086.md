> **一句话总结：** PairSight 通过算子级实测与硬件计数器分析，把 GPU 共享（SM 分区）下的干扰归结为共享 L2/DRAM 通路上的队列延迟，并用两个以双任务逐算子特征（独立延迟 + DRAM 流量 + 停顿计数器）为输入的 XGBoost 回归器在共置前预测各自延迟，在含 Llama-3.2 的 6 个模型、73 万共执行样本上把高优先级任务延迟预测误差降到 4.3%（强度规则基线为 19.0%）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper first characterizes operator-level interference under SM-partitioned GPU sharing (via libsmctrl TPC masks) on A100s and traces it to queueing on the shared L2/DRAM path, then builds PairSight — two XGBoost regressors over per-operator standalone latency and memory-path counters of BOTH co-located tasks — to predict each task's latency before co-location. On 730k co-execution samples from 6 DNN models (ResNet-50/101, MobileNetV2, BERT-base, Llama-3.2-1B/3B) in 20 HP/LP pairs, PairSight reaches 4.3% HP / 2.4% LP latency MAPE vs 19.0%/15.6% for an Orion/Usher-style intensity rule (a ~14.7-point HP reduction, with 90.6% of HP predictions within 10% vs 37.9%), transfers to unseen Llama pairs far better than the model-identity predictor Abacus (30.2% vs 65.9% HP MAPE, adaptable to 13.5% with 100 samples), and composes window predictions to 24–96-operator segments at 5.5%/5.4% error vs 9.8%/17.0% for the intensity rule.

## 核心贡献

- **算子级干扰刻画**：在 SM 分区（TPC 掩码）设置下系统测量算子间干扰，发现干扰是**方向性**的——访存密集型算子（如 BatchNorm）无论旁边跑计算密集还是访存密集的 co-runner 都显著变慢，直接推翻了"计算-访存互补"规则；同一强度类别内部算子间差异巨大（单个 HP 算子的减速从可忽略到超过 2 倍），且在固定 SM 配额下减速仍随 co-runner 变化（ResNet-50 在 ResNet-101 旁边比在 BERT 旁边最多慢 1.31 倍）。
- **机理归因**：提出解析模型 T ≈ T_compute + N_miss·(L_base + Q_shared)，并用 7.7k 共执行算子对上 10 个 Nsight Compute 计数器的相关性分析（目标侧计数器全部显著相关 p<0.05，独立时长与 L2 流量 |r|>0.9；co-runner 侧整体不相关，但在同目标同分组的 4.2k 组中 2/3 随 co-runner 的 DRAM 流量率上升）将干扰源头定位到**共享 L2/DRAM 通路的队列延迟**，其代价取决于双方的 N_miss 与未隐藏访存延迟。
- **PairSight 预测器**：以两个 XGBoost 回归器（HP/LP 各一）在 log δ 上训练，输入是双任务**逐算子 token**（指定 SM 配额下的独立时长、DRAM 总字节数、long-scoreboard / LG-throttle / MIO-throttle 三类停顿计数器）加 p、q 与双段独立延迟；新模型仅需一次 Nsight Compute profile + 每 TPC 数一次独立延迟 profile。
- **外推与适配**：窗口组合（≤16 算子窗口按序配对累加）把预测扩展到 96 算子长段；对未见模型/批大小用少量样本继续加树训练（CPU 上每次更新 ≤5 秒）。
- **全面评测**：在 8×A100 上用 52k 实验、73 万样本、30 次重复，覆盖精度、训练/查询开销、设计消融与泛化四个维度，并对比强度规则、互补表、线性回归、Abacus 与 PairSight-MLP 五个基线。

## Key Contributions

- **Operator-level interference study.** Measures interference between co-located operators under SM partitioning (TPC masks via libsmctrl) and shows it is directional — memory-intensive operators (BatchNorm) slow down markedly beside BOTH compute- and memory-intensive co-runners, invalidating the compute–memory complementarity rule used by Orion and USHER — operator-specific within one intensity class (slowdown spans negligible to >2× for a single HP operator across co-runners), and co-runner-dependent even at fixed SM allocation (ResNet-50 runs up to 1.31× longer beside ResNet-101 than beside BERT).
- **Mechanistic attribution to the shared memory path.** Approximates operator latency as T ≈ T_compute + N_miss(L_base + Q_shared): the co-runner can only raise the queueing term Q_shared, and the damage scales with the target's memory accesses N_miss and its un-hidden memory latency. A correlation study over 7.7k co-executed operator pairs with ten Nsight Compute counters finds every target-side counter significantly correlated with slowdown (p<0.05; |r|>0.9 for standalone duration and L2 traffic) while no co-runner counter correlates globally — yet within the 4.2k groups sharing target and SM split, δ rises with the co-runner's DRAM traffic rate in two thirds, i.e., the co-runner's effect is target-conditional.
- **PairSight, a hardware-aware interference predictor.** Two XGBoost regressors (one per HP/LP side, fitted on log δ) read per-operator tokens of BOTH segments — standalone duration at the assigned allocation, total DRAM bytes (N_miss), long-scoreboard stalls (un-hidden memory latency), and LG-/MIO-throttle stalls (queue pressure) — plus the TPC split (p, q) and segment standalone latencies; the trees route compute-bound segments to leaves where co-runner counters barely matter and memory-bound ones to leaves where they dominate. Deployment cost is one Nsight Compute profile per model plus one standalone-latency profile per TPC count, with CPU training finishing within 45 s (79 s for the full test-set model).
- **Window composition and few-sample adaptation.** Segments longer than the 16-operator training window are predicted by index-paired windows summed with post-overlap standalone latency; adding trees on a few new samples adapts the model to unseen models and batch sizes in ≤5 s per update on CPU.
- **Comprehensive evaluation.** 730k samples from 52k experiments (30 runs each) on eight A100-SXM4-40GB GPUs across 20 ordered HP/LP model/batch pairs, against five baselines spanning rules (intensity, complementarity table), linear contention regression, and learned model-level (Abacus) and same-input MLP (PairSight-MLP) predictors, with accuracy, cost, ablation, and generalization axes.

## Methodology

1. **Setup.** A latency-critical HP inference task shares a GPU with a best-effort LP task; SMs are partitioned at TPC granularity by attaching TPC masks to kernel launches through libsmctrl. This isolates registers, shared memory, L1, and warp schedulers, but L2, DRAM channels, and the interconnect stay shared. Slowdown is defined as δ = T_share(p,q)/T_solo(p), which removes the effect of simply having fewer SMs.
2. **Interference characterization.** Individual operators and operator pairs from four real models are co-executed under varying splits; observations (directionality, within-intensity variation, co-runner dependence) are then explained by the queueing model T ≈ T_compute + N_miss(L_base + Q_shared), and validated by correlating δ against ten Nsight Compute hardware counters of both sides over 7.7k pairs (Fig. 2).
3. **Predictor design.** The segment model f(Φ_a, Φ_b, p, q) → δ̂ is realized as two gradient-boosted tree ensembles (XGBoost on log δ, depth/learning rate tuned on validation, early stopping), one for the HP side and one for the LP side, each seeing both segments. Each operator is one token with standalone duration, four memory-path counters, and a presence flag; per-operator tokens (rather than segment sums) preserve which operator suffers un-hidden latency and which generates traffic. Predicted latency is T̂ = T_solo(p)·δ̂ with T_solo from the per-TPC profile.
4. **Longer segments.** Both segments are cut into ≤16-operator windows; the k-th target window is queried beside the k-th co-runner window, summed for k ≤ K (the shorter side's window count), with remaining windows adding standalone latency — assuming comparable per-window progress, an approximation whose error is measured up to 96 operators.
5. **Adaptation.** For a new model or batch size, trees are added to the existing ensembles from as few as 20–100 new co-execution samples.
6. **Evaluation protocol.** 730k samples from 52k experiments (30 repetitions, CUDA-event timing of T_share) split 80/10/10 by experiment into train/validation/test; baselines: intensity rule (fixed per-intensity slowdown factors, following Orion/USHER), complementarity table (median slowdown per intensity-pair × SM split), Linear (ridge regression on PairSight's own inputs), Abacus (model-identity MLP on positions/batch/SM split/standalone latencies), and PairSight-MLP (same inputs, three-layer perceptron learner).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main accuracy (730k-sample A100 test set, six models incl. Llama-3.2-1B/3B):** PairSight predicts HP latency with **4.3% MAPE and LP with 2.4%**, versus **19.0% HP / 15.6% LP** for the intensity rule (−14.7 pts HP), 14.8%/12.5% for the complementarity table, 12.2%/10.2% for Linear, and 9.2%/8.0% for Abacus. **90.6% of PairSight's HP predictions land within 10%** versus 37.9% for the intensity rule and 80.1% for Abacus; on the Llama pairs specifically, HP MAPE is 10.3% vs 45.0% for the intensity rule and 13.0% for Abacus. PairSight-MLP ties at 4.3%/2.4%, showing the gain comes from the operator-level two-sided input, not the tree learner.
- **Cost:** PairSight trains in **79 s on 64 CPU threads** (vs 111 s for Abacus, 135 s for PairSight-MLP) and answers each candidate query in microseconds (5.3 μs per candidate in 1k batches on 16 cores) — cheap enough for pre-colocation scheduling over many candidate segment pairs and SM splits.
- **Design ablation (Fig. 4):** removing co-runner tokens — the single largest loss — raises HP MAPE from **4.3% to 7.7%** and LP from **2.4% to 6.7%**; summing each segment's tokens into one vector raises HP error to 5.1%, and keeping only operator durations raises it to 4.6%, while swapping DRAM/stall counters for compute/memory utilization keeps it at 4.3%.
- **Generalization (Table II, trained only on the four classic CNN/BERT models at default batch sizes):** on held-out Llama pairs PairSight scores **30.2% HP / 18.5% LP MAPE vs 65.9%/>100% for Abacus** (whose model-identity inputs do not transfer) and 45.0%/27.7% for the intensity rule; on unseen batch sizes 16 and 64 it gives 35.1%/26.8% and 15.0%/9.9%. Continued training on just **100 new samples drops Llama HP error from 30.2% to 13.5%** (9.1% with all samples), each update taking ≤5 s on CPU.
- **Window composition:** on 400 held-out co-executions with 24–96-operator segments and SM splits absent from training, PairSight errs by **5.5% (HP) / 5.4% (LP) vs 9.8%/17.0% for the intensity rule**; on the 80 asymmetric co-executions (where index-paired windows do not coincide in time) it still errs by only 4.9%/5.5%.
- **Study-level findings that break existing rules:** a single memory-intensive HP operator's slowdown spans **negligible to >2×** across co-runners of the same models, and ResNet-50 at a small fixed SM allocation runs up to **1.31× longer beside ResNet-101 than beside BERT** — the spread that bounds any co-runner-agnostic estimator.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from Xiao Liu, Yujia Fu, Yue Yu, and corresponding author Dan Huang, spanning Sun Yat-sen University (Guangzhou) and Peng Cheng Laboratory (Shenzhen) — established groups in systems and GPU resource management. The paper is grounded in the current GPU-sharing literature: it directly builds on and cites Orion (EuroSys'24), USHER (OSDI'24), SGDRc (PPoPP'25), the SoCC'25 deeper interference study by Elvinger et al., Abacus (SC'21), and standard tooling (libsmctrl RTAS'23, Nsight Compute, XGBoost). The methodology is unusually careful for a systems paper: 30 repetitions per experiment, 730k samples split by experiment (not by sample, avoiding leakage), a same-input MLP control that isolates the contribution of the input representation from the learner, and an explicit ablation of every design axis. arXiv cs.PF, 16 Oct 2026.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: solid, incremental.** The queueing view (T ≈ T_compute + N_miss(L_base + Q_shared)) and the "interference is a two-operator interaction" finding refine, rather than overturn, what the SoCC'25 "one level deeper" study and Orion/USHER already suggested; the genuinely new parts are the per-operator two-sided token representation and the demonstration that a cheap XGBoost over standalone-profile counters predicts co-execution latency within ~4–5%.
- **Rigor: high.** Experiment-level 80/10/10 splits prevent sample leakage; the PairSight-MLP control cleanly attributes accuracy to inputs rather than learner; the co-runner ablation quantifies the value of the second task's counters (4.3%→7.7% HP). The correlation analysis honestly reports that no co-runner counter correlates globally — the co-runner signal is conditional on the target — which is exactly why a nonlinear learner is needed.
- **Reproducibility: good but hardware-specific.** All ingredients (libsmctrl, Nsight Compute, XGBoost, public models including Llama-3.2-1B/3B) are available, but the trained models are fitted to one GPU type (A100-SXM4-40GB) and one partitioning mechanism; the paper does not report cross-GPU transfer (e.g., train on A100, test on A10 even though Fig. 1a/1b used an A10 only for the motivating study), nor sensitivity to driver/CUDA versions or Nsight profiling overhead in the serving path.
- **Significance: practical.** A 5.3 μs query makes PairSight directly usable inside co-location schedulers that must screen many (segment, SM split) candidates before placement; the 4.3%-vs-19.0% gap against the rule used by Orion/USHER-class systems is the kind of improvement that changes admission-control decisions.
- **Red flags / weak spots.** (1) The evaluation is strictly two-task (one HP, one LP) — multi-tenant colocation with 3+ tasks, where Q_shared has multiple contributors, is untested. (2) The window-composition index-pairing assumes comparable per-window progress; the authors measure this on 80 asymmetric cases (4.9%/5.5%) but autoregressive LLM decode phases, where successive windows have wildly different durations, are not broken out. (3) Llama HP MAPE in-distribution is 10.3% — 2.4× the headline 4.3% — so the headline number is dominated by the CNN/BERT pairs. (4) Baseline fairness is mostly good, but Abacus is fed model identity it cannot have for unseen models by construction, so its >100% transfer error partly re-states its design premise. (5) Training data comes from only 6 models and 20 pairs on one 8-GPU server; diversity of architectures (no ViTs, no MoE, no diffusion) limits claimed generality.

## Strengths

- Clean cause-and-effect narrative: measurement → analytical model → counter correlation → predictor design, where every design choice traces back to an observation.
- The two-sided, per-operator input representation is the key idea, and the ablations (remove co-runner tokens, sum tokens, drop counters) prove each piece earns its place.
- Interpretability by construction: trees branch on un-hidden memory latency, so compute-bound and memory-bound segments take different paths — the predictor encodes the paper's own mechanism.
- Strong transfer story: 30.2% → 13.5% on unseen Llama pairs with 100 samples and ≤5 s CPU updates makes redeployment realistic.
- Very low serving-time cost (microsecond queries, one offline profile per model) and honest engineering numbers for training (79 s).

## Limitations

- Single GPU model (A100-SXM4-40GB over PCIe); no cross-GPU or cross-partitioning-mechanism (MIG, temporal slicing) validation, and no NVLink/multi-GPU setting despite the hardware having none — that constraint is inherited by the trained models.
- Only pairwise co-location of exactly two tasks under static SM splits; interference among 3+ residents and under dynamic re-partitioning is unaddressed.
- The 16-operator window cap with index-paired composition is a heuristic; its error is measured only up to 96 operators and 80 asymmetric cases.
- Requires an Nsight Compute profile per model and a standalone-latency profile per TPC count — for evolving models (LoRA swaps, growing KV-cache-dependent decode shapes) the "few samples" adaptation burden may recur.
- In-distribution Llama HP error (10.3%) and held-out Llama error (30.2% before adaptation) show the memory-path features do not fully capture transformer decode-phase interference.

## Relevance

这篇论文对"GPU 共享/多租户推理调度 + 可解释性能预测"方向直接相关：它把 SM 分区下算子级干扰归因到共享 L2/DRAM 通路，并给出共置前即可用、微秒级查询的延迟预测器，正好服务于推理服务系统的共置决策与 SLO 保障。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does PairSight transfer across GPU generations (train on A100, test on H100/L4) and across partitioning mechanisms (libsmctrl TPC masks vs MIG vs temporal sharing), or does the queueing-model structure need per-platform retraining?
- How does the predictor extend to three or more co-located tasks, where Q_shared aggregates traffic from several co-runners — can the two-operator interaction features be composed, or must the model be retrained at higher arity?
- For autoregressive LLM serving, do decode-phase windows (whose durations grow with KV-cache length) break the index-paired window composition, and would a progress-rate-aware pairing reduce the 10.3% in-distribution Llama error?
- Can the correlation findings be exploited online — e.g., cheap PMU-sampled substitutes for Nsight counters — so adaptation does not require a full offline profile per new model or batch size?
- What is the end-to-end scheduler value: when PairSight drives admission control and SM-split selection inside an Orion/USHER-style system, how much does LP throughput or HP SLO attainment actually improve over the 19.0%-error intensity rule?
