> **一句话总结：** 作者把量化预训练的损失分解为"容量缺口 + 持续性优化误差"，提出带前向/后向误差项 ϵf、ϵb 的混合精度缩放律 L = A(ρN(ψf)N)^−α + B(ρD(ψb)D)^−β + ϵf + ϵb + E，在自建的 375 次 Qwen3（280M–910M，12.8B–35.8B tokens）和公开的 84 次 Quartet/Llama 网格上，held-out 损失预测 MSE 比 Quartet 律低 1.6–11 倍，并据此推出"最优精度随算力预算增大而升高、且应优先把低精度分配给反向传播"的结论。

> **TL;DR:** This paper derives a mixed-precision scaling law for quantized LLM pretraining that, unlike prior laws (Quartet, Kumar et al., Panferov et al.), adds persistent optimization-error terms ϵf(ψf) + ϵb(ψb) motivated by convergence analysis — because forward quantization causes weight oscillation and backward quantization biases gradients, errors that more data or parameters cannot wash out. On a 375-run Qwen3 grid (280M–910M params, 12.8B–35.8B tokens, C4 validation loss) plus the published 84-run Quartet (Llama) grid, it predicts held-out loss with up to 11× lower MSE than the Quartet law (model-size extrapolation: 1.4e-5 vs 1.6e-4); removing ϵf alone degrades geomean MSE from 1.23e-5 to 3.76e-5, confirming the term matters. The layer-wise extension shows keeping first/last blocks at high precision is what matters in the backward pass (33.0% vs 70.7% of the loss gap at p=0.5), and the compute-optimal analysis predicts — contrary to Kumar et al. (2025) — that optimal precision increases with compute budget. Caveat: everything is validated only up to 910M non-embedding params.

## 核心贡献

- **分解现有 FP4 训练配方**：首次把 NVFP4 配方（Abecassis et al. 2025）和 Quartet II（Panferov et al. 2026a）拆成前向/后向组件单独评测。在 430M Qwen3、12.8B tokens 上发现：只量化反向传播损失仅增加 0.32–0.72%，而只量化前向增加 0.94–1.42% —— 前向量化敏感得多；Quartet II 的优势主要来自前向的 4/6+1D scaling（换掉 MS-EDEN 只差 0.07 个百分点，换掉前向 scaling 再差 0.27 个百分点）。
- **带持续性优化误差的缩放律**：L = A(ρN(ψf)N)^−α + B(ρD(ψb)D)^−β + ϵf(ψf) + ϵb(ψb) + E。理论上把损失差分解为容量缺口（由有效参数量 ρN 建模）和优化缺口；基于凸优化收敛分析（Remark 3.1）识别出两个不随 T 衰减的 O(1) 项 —— 前向权重振荡误差（ϵf）与后向梯度偏差（ϵb），并构造了优化间隙恒为 1/8 的反例（Example 3.1）证明该误差不可通过加数据消除。
- **大规模系统验证**：自建 375 次 Qwen3 全因子网格（前向 BF16/MXFP8/NVFP4 × 后向 BF16/MXFP8/NVFP4-MS-EDEN/NVFP4-RHT+SR/NVFP4-SR）+ 复用公开 Quartet 网格 84 次 Llama 风格运行，在模型尺寸和数据外推两种设置下全面超过 Quartet 律，MSE 降低 1.6×–11×。
- **层级混合精度扩展与算力最优分配**：L = (L2−L1)p^γS + L1 用单一指数 γS 刻画"高精度块放在哪些层"的策略；在 held-out 910M 模型上 R²=0.991。据此求解硬件感知的算力最优分配（Eq. 5），发现最优精度随算力预算升高而升高（与 Kumar et al. 2025 的预算无关结论相反），且有限的高精度预算应优先给前向传播。

## Key Contributions

- **Recipe decomposition (Section 2):** Breaks two SOTA FP4 recipes (NVFP4 recipe, Quartet II) into forward and backward components and measures each in isolation on a 430M Qwen3 model at 12.8B tokens. Forward quantization is far more loss-sensitive (+0.94–1.42%) than backward quantization (+0.32–0.72%); Quartet II's edge comes mostly from its 4/6 + 1D forward scaling, not MS-EDEN.
- **A scaling law with persistent optimization error (Section 3):** L = A(ρN(ψf)N)^−α + B(ρD(ψb)D)^−β + ϵf(ψf) + ϵb(ψb) + E, where ρN/ρD rescale effective parameters/data and ϵf/ϵb are additive, N/D-independent error floors. This is the first quantized-pretraining law (per their Table 2) to model both forward–backward mixed schemes, layer-wise mixed schemes, and persistent optimization error.
- **Theory-grounded derivation:** Decomposes the loss gap into a capacity gap and an optimization gap; a convex-analysis bound (Remark 3.1) yields three terms — O(T^−1/2) (→ effective data ρD·D), an O(1) forward-quantization oscillation term (→ ϵf), and an O(1) biased-gradient term (→ ϵb). A 1-D counterexample (Example 3.1) shows the optimization gap can stay at 1/8 forever.
- **Comprehensive empirical validation:** A 375-run full-factorial Qwen3 grid (280M–910M non-embedding params, 12.8B–35.8B tokens, 3 forward × 5 backward schemes) plus the published 84-run Quartet grid, evaluated under model-size and data extrapolation, with ablations over every design choice (Table 5, Appendix Table 9).
- **Layer-wise extension and compute-optimal allocation (Section 4):** L = (L2−L1)p^γS + L1 captures block-selection strategies with a single exponent; fitted on small models, it transfers to a held-out 910M model (R² = 0.991). Solving the hardware-aware allocation problem predicts optimal precision rises with compute budget, and that low precision should be adopted in the backward pass first.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in three stages.

**1. Recipe decomposition.** The NVFP4 recipe (forward: 2D scaling over 16×16 blocks; backward: stochastic rounding + randomized Hadamard transforms) and Quartet II (forward: 1D scaling over 1×16 blocks with 4/6 scaling, i.e., picking the lower-error of two candidate block scales; backward: MS-EDEN unbiased matmul) are crossed factorially on a 430M-parameter Qwen3 model trained for 12.8B tokens. This isolates which components drive loss and selects the regimes (4/6 + 1D forward; all three backward techniques) used in the scaling-law grid.

**2. Scaling-law construction (Section 3).** The loss gap over irreducible loss E decomposes into capacity gap (depends only on N and forward scheme ψf; modeled as effective parameter count ρN(ψf)N) and optimization gap (bounded in Remark 3.1 for convex losses with quantized weights and biased gradient estimators). The bound has three terms mapped to the law: (a) an O(T^−1/2) SGD term → effective token count ρD(ψb)D; (b) an O(1) term scaling with forward quantization error ‖θ̂t − θt‖ → ϵf(ψf); (c) an O(1) term scaling with gradient bias ‖bt‖ → ϵb(ψb). Notably, letting ρD depend on ψf as well did not improve held-out prediction, so the backward-only dependence is kept. Fitting follows the Quartet two-stage protocol: fit to BF16 runs first, freeze baseline coefficients, then fit quantized-run parameters.

**3. Layer-wise extension and allocation (Section 4).** With two precision configurations ψ1 (high) and ψ2 (low), a selection strategy S assigns ψ2 to a fraction p of Transformer blocks; loss is modeled as (L2−L1)p^γS + L1, where a larger γS means sensitive blocks stay high-precision longer. Two strategies are compared (first/last vs middle blocks at MXFP8, mixing 25/50/75% of blocks, forward-only or backward-only). For compute-optimality, a BF16-equivalent FLOP cost model (Eq. 4: forward ≈ 1/3, backward ≈ 2/3 of 6ND FLOPs, with hardware speedups λ per format; λMXFP8 fixed at 1.8 from NVIDIA Transformer Engine GEMM measurements) is combined with the predicted loss to solve min L s.t. C(N, D, pf, pb) ≤ C0 by numerical optimization.

**Experimental setup.** Own grid: Qwen3-8B-derived architectures (CompleteP parameterization) at 280M/430M/640M/910M non-embedding params, token budgets 12.8B–35.8B, batch 1024×4096 (~4.2M tokens), AdamW, WSD schedule, LR 1.2e-3 scaled as η ∝ D^−0.32; validation loss on C4; all runs of a given size share the same init checkpoint and data order so runs differ only in quantization scheme. Quartet grid: 84 published Llama-style runs at 30M–200M, 25–800 tokens/parameter. Model-size extrapolation fits on the three smaller sizes and predicts the largest (200M / 910M); data extrapolation fits on smaller budgets and predicts the largest (800 TPP / 35.8B tokens).

## Results & Highlights

- **Held-out loss prediction (Table 4), model-size extrapolation on the Quartet grid (Llama, MXFP4):** our law reaches MSE 1.4e-5 vs 1.6e-4 for the Quartet law (**11× lower**), and MAPE 0.096% vs 0.386%, at R² 0.997 vs 0.964 — the largest headline improvement.
- **Data extrapolation on our Qwen3 grid (C4 validation loss, 35.8B tokens held out):** MSE 6.1e-6 vs 2.7e-5 for the Quartet law (**4.4× lower**), MAPE 0.064% vs 0.121%; on the Quartet grid data extrapolation improves MSE from 9.0e-5 to 5.1e-5 (1.76×). Across all four settings the reduction is 1.6×–11×.
- **Ablations (Table 5, geometric-mean held-out MSE across both grids):** full law 1.23e-5 vs Quartet law 4.27e-5; removing the forward persistent-error term ϵf raises MSE to 3.76e-5 — a 3× degradation showing ϵf is the single most important addition — while removing ϵb (1.34e-5) or letting ρD depend on both passes (1.29e-5) barely hurts.
- **Recipe decomposition (Table 1, 430M Qwen3, 12.8B tokens, C4 validation loss):** BF16 baseline 3.245; backward-only NVFP4 quantization costs +0.32% (RHT+SR) to +0.72% (SR), while forward-only quantization costs +0.94% (4/6+1D, BF16 backward) to +1.69% (2D scaling, SR backward) — and even the best full FP4 recipe keeps a clear gap to BF16, motivating mixed precision.
- **Layer-wise law on the held-out 910M model (Figure 4):** with our law's endpoints, R² = 0.991 and MSE = 7.1e-6; substituting Quartet-law endpoints nearly triples the MSE (R² = 0.974, MSE = 2.0e-5) and systematically underestimates observed losses.
- **Block-placement asymmetry (Table 6):** in the backward pass, keeping first/last blocks at MXFP8 gives γS = 1.60 and only 33.0% of the full loss gap at p = 0.5, vs γS = 0.50 and 70.7% for middle blocks; in the forward pass the strategy barely matters (46.7% vs 50.1%) — confirming and quantifying why practical recipes keep first/last blocks precise.
- **Compute-optimal prediction (Figure 1, λMXFP8 = 1.8, λNVFP4 = 3):** the optimal NVFP4 fraction falls as compute grows — the optimum switches to all-MXFP8 forward around C0 = 10^20 FLOPs and in both passes around 5×10^22 FLOPs — i.e., **optimal precision increases with compute budget**, contradicting the budget-independent optimum of Kumar et al. (2025), because persistent error stays fixed while A/B terms shrink.

## Authors & Credibility

The work is by Jinuk Kim (Seoul National University; the work was done during an internship at Amazon) with Rajarshi Saha, Muhammed Fatih Balın, and Youngsuk Park of Amazon Annapurna Labs — the team behind Amazon's silicon/quantization research, which explains the hardware-aware cost model. The paper is careful by empirical-ML standards: it reuses a published third-party grid (Quartet/Castro et al., NeurIPS) for out-of-sample validation rather than only self-reported runs, follows the baseline's own two-stage fitting protocol for fairness, runs a full-factorial grid (375 runs, every forward × backward combination), and ablates every design choice including alternative term placements (Appendix Table 9). It includes AI-use, ethics, and reproducibility statements. The theory (Remark 3.1) is a motivating bound under convexity assumptions rather than a guarantee for deep networks, but the paper is explicit that it "guides the formulation" rather than proving it. No public code release is mentioned.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Incremental but genuine. The core idea — an additive, scale-independent error floor per pass — is a small change to the functional form, but it is the first law to cover forward/backward mixed schemes, layer-wise mixing, and persistent error simultaneously (their Table 2), and the "optimal precision rises with budget" prediction is a clean, falsifiable departure from Kumar et al. (2025).
- **Rigor:** Strong on fitting discipline (two-stage protocol, held-out splits, geometric-mean aggregation across grids with different error scales, full ablation grid). Weak on theory-scope honesty: Remark 3.1 assumes a convex, deterministic-quantization setting that does not hold for LLMs, so the mapping from bound to law is analogy, not derivation. The headline comparison is against the Quartet law only — Kumar et al.'s QPT law and Panferov et al.'s representation-based law appear in the qualitative Table 2 but not in the quantitative Table 4, so "more accurate than existing laws" is only demonstrated against one baseline.
- **Reproducibility:** Good on hyperparameters (architecture derivation, AdamW/WSD settings, LR-scaling rule, calibration/held-out splits all tabulated) but the training corpus is never named — only C4 validation loss is specified — and no code or run-data release is mentioned, while the own grid's 375 runs are not published. The Quartet grid being public partially compensates.
- **Significance:** The scale ceiling is the main concern: the own grid tops out at 910M non-embedding parameters (~1.2B total) and 35.8B tokens, and the Quartet grid at 200M / 800 TPP. Predictions about optimal precision at 10^22–10^24 FLOPs (frontier-scale) extrapolate the functional form 2–3 orders of magnitude beyond any fitted data, exactly where additive-error terms dominate the fit — the paper's most practically interesting claim rests on its least-tested regime.
- **Red flags:** The cost model ignores all non-GEMM operations, assumes per-block costs are equal, fixes λMXFP8 = 1.8 from a single NVIDIA kernel measurement, and assumes the same speedup in both passes and γ-exponents independent of the other pass and the backward technique (Eq. 3) — each assumption is stated but none is stress-tested on real hardware. The layer-wise law is validated on one architecture family, two formats (MXFP8/NVFP4), two strategies, with the exponent fitted on the same small models that fit the endpoints.

## Strengths

- Clean decomposition study (Section 2) that isolates forward vs backward sensitivity of FP4 recipes — useful independently of the scaling law, and the +0.07pp vs +0.27pp swap experiment is a model of how to attribute recipe gains.
- The law is theory-guided, not curve-fit: each term has an identified source (SGD noise, weight oscillation, gradient bias), and the ablations empirically confirm the terms' necessity (ϵf removal triples error).
- Full-factorial 375-run grid with shared init/data order across schemes — quantization is the only varying factor, which many scaling-law studies fail to control.
- Validation on a published external grid (Quartet/Llama) guards against self-consistent overfitting to one architecture.
- Actionable engineering findings: first/last blocks matter most in the backward pass (33.0% vs 70.7% of gap at p=0.5); with a limited high-precision budget, spend it on the forward pass; expect optimal precision to rise with scale.

## Limitations

- Maximum validated scale is 910M non-embedding parameters / 35.8B tokens; all compute-optimal conclusions at 10^20+ FLOPs are extrapolation, not measurement.
- Quantitative comparison is limited to the Quartet law; no head-to-head numbers against Kumar et al. (2025) QPT or Panferov et al. (2026b) laws.
- Training corpus unspecified; no code or self-generated run data released.
- Cost model is matmul-only with fixed speedups (λMXFP8 = 1.8, swept in appendix only for MXFP8); real-kernel speedups vary by hardware generation, format support, and layer shape.
- Layer-wise extension covers only two formats and two strategies; the independence assumptions in Eq. 3 (γf, γb unaffected by the other pass or the backward technique) are convenience assumptions.
- The theoretical analysis assumes convexity and deterministic quantization — far from the LLM pretraining regime it is applied to.

## Relevance

对研究高效 LLM 预训练与低比特量化的工作高度相关：它首次量化了 FP4/FP8 预训练中前向与后向、层级间混合精度的损失代价，并给出可直接使用的算力最优精度分配准则（有限高精度预算优先给前向、首尾层优先保留高精度、最优精度随规模上升），对设计下一代低精度训练配方和 Scaling Law 建模都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the "optimal precision increases with compute budget" prediction survive at 10B+ parameter scale, or does the additive-error model break down where overtraining/token-hungry regimes interact with quantization?
- How do the fitted ϵf/ϵb error floors change with quantization-aware techniques not studied here (e.g., loss-aware scaling, gradient compensation, learned codetypes beyond 4/6 scaling)?
- Can the layer-wise γS exponent be predicted a priori from cheap proxy measurements (e.g., per-layer gradient norm or Fisher sensitivity) instead of being fitted per strategy from mixed-precision runs?
- How sensitive are the compute-optimal allocations to realistic, heterogeneous speedups (per-layer shapes, non-GEMM overheads, communication in distributed training) rather than the fixed λMX8 = 1.8 assumption?
- Does the persistent-error term interact with optimizer choice — e.g., do momentum/second-moment corrections or proximal operators (ProxQuant-style) shrink ϵf in practice?
