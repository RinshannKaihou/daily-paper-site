> **一句话总结：** 提出 LM2LM 框架，用一个冻结 LLM 作为"研究世界模型"(RWM) 基于真实实验记录预测提案收益来充当 RL 奖励，并通过定期真实执行当前策略的高分提案做"在线校准"来防止奖励过优化，使 Qwen3.8-27B 学会提出有效的语言模型改进方案——在 8 个自回归/扩散语言模型训练环境上，以 33% 更低的数据生成成本胜过带 300 条真实记录的 GPT-6-sol，并能泛化到训练中未见过的模型家族。

> **TL;DR:** The paper trains a Qwen3.8-27B proposer with DAPO to suggest language-model training-recipe improvements, replacing prohibitively expensive real training-run feedback (est. ~$16M for full RL) with a frozen-LLM "research world model" (GPT-6-sol grounded on 100 real experiment records) that predicts validation-loss gains, plus periodic on-policy calibration that executes top-RWM-rated proposals to refresh the records. On 60 AR + 18 diffusion held-out research states across 8 model families (OLMo-3, Qwen3, NanoChat, Marin, E2D2, E2D2-SW, MDM, BD3-LM), LM2LM reaches Avg@4 of 17.06×10⁻³ BPB on AR tasks vs 6.15 for GPT-6-sol-ICL (the strongest baseline, +10.9) and 7.10 vs 4.94 on diffusion tasks, with ~33% lower data-generation cost than the K=300 ICL baseline ($40.1k vs $60.0k) and strong out-of-family transfer to held-out Qwen3 (Avg@4 53.72 vs 21.63 for GPT-6-sol-ICL).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 LM2LM 框架**：用强化学习（DAPO）训练 LLM 提议者，使其学会为语言模型开发（架构/训练目标/优化器改动）提出高收益提案，直接面向"递归自我改进"(RSI) 的研究判断能力。
- **用研究世界模型 (RWM) 提供可扩展的 RL 奖励**：冻结的 LLM（GPT-6-sol）以真实实验记录为上下文证据，直接预测提案的验证损失增益，把每次提案评估从一次完整模型训练（约 45 B200 GPU 小时）降为一次 LLM 调用；配以任务级归一化+裁剪、有效性/重复性/机制方向多样性奖励整形。
- **在线校准 (on-policy calibration) 保持反馈可靠**：训练中周期性真实执行当前策略中 RWM 评分最高的提案并将其真实结果写回 RWM 上下文，针对性抑制策略偏移下的奖励过优化——静态记录的 RWM 优势余弦相似度会从 0.69 跌至 −0.42，而校准版始终保持正值。
- **全面实证**：8 个 LM 开发环境（4 自回归 + 4 扩散家族）上的真实执行评测表明，LM2LM 用 200 次真实运行即超过零样本与 300 条记录 ICL 的 GPT-6-sol，且对训练中完全未见的模型家族（Qwen3）有强泛化。

## Key Contributions

- **LM2LM framework**: trains an LLM proposer with RL (DAPO) to generate high-gain improvement proposals for LM development (architecture, training objective, optimization), targeting the "research judgment" bottleneck in automated AI research (AutoResearch) and recursive self-improvement.
- **Research World Model (RWM) as scalable verifier**: a frozen LLM (GPT-6-sol) grounded on real experimental records predicts proposal gains in-context, replacing per-proposal model-training runs (~45 B200 GPU-hours each; naive RL would need ~25,600 evaluations ≈ 1.15M GPU-hours ≈ $16M) with single LLM calls. Rewards are normalized per-task (τₑ), clipped (c=6), and shaped by proposal validity, repetition penalties, and a mechanism-direction diversity bonus.
- **On-policy calibration for feedback reliability**: between RL segments, top-RWM-rated proposals from the *current* policy are really executed (200 real runs total, 3 calibration rounds at steps 16/40/64) and their measured outcomes are appended to the RWM's context — countering reward overoptimization as the proposal distribution shifts.
- **Synthetic state expansion**: sampled state–proposal pairs with RWM-predicted gains are added to the record pool to cheaply expand RL training states beyond the 100 initial real records.
- **Comprehensive evaluation with real execution**: across 8 environments (4 AR: OLMo-3, Qwen3, NanoChat, Marin; 4 diffusion: E2D2-SW, E2D2, MDM, BD3-LM), plus out-of-family generalization and ablations isolating calibration and its selection strategy.

## Methodology

The setting is AutoResearch for LM development: a task e specifies a model family, size, pretraining budget, and a reference configuration with validation loss b̂ₑ; a proposer πθ generates a proposal aₜ given the research state (task + executed-history hₜ); a builder LLM (GPT-6-astra) implements it; a real training run yields validation loss ℓₜ, and the experimental gain is g = b̂ₑ − ℓₜ. Since executing every RL-sampled proposal is infeasible (the paper prices a 486M-param/8B-token run at ~45 B200 GPU-hours, making naive GRPO-style RL ≈ $16M), LM2LM proceeds in three parts:

1. **RWM-based reward**: a frozen GPT-6-sol W predicts ĝ(e, a; M) from records M in context. Predicted gains are normalized by a task-specific scale τₑ (set by reference seed noise and prediction dispersion) and clipped at c=6; within each DAPO group (G=8), invalid or repeated proposals are pushed below all valid ones, and a diversity bonus λσ_G d̃ᵢ (dᵢ = 1/nᵢ, nᵢ = number of valid proposals sharing the same mechanism direction) rewards rare mechanism directions. DAPO uses group-mean-centered advantages without std division to preserve cross-group reward dispersion.
2. **On-policy calibration**: the normalized real objective decomposes as J̄ = Ĵ_M + Δ_M (prediction bias). Because RL optimizes Ĵ_M without seeing Δ_M, high-rated but poorly-covered proposals get overestimated. At each calibration point (steps 16, 40, 64), the Bu highest-RWM-rated current-policy proposals are executed for real and appended to the RWM's context; the reward function stays fixed within each RL segment. This grounds exactly the region RL is about to exploit.
3. **Training configuration**: proposer backbone Qwen3.8-27B; DAPO for 96 steps, 256 training states per refresh, G=8 proposals/state; N₀=100 initial records; 40 training + 10 held-out tasks; evaluation on 60 AR + 18 diffusion held-out research states via real execution of 4 proposals per state (Avg@4, Best@4, negative-gain rate, pairwise win rates; BPB for AR, nats/token for diffusion).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main comparison (Table 1, held-out states)**: LM2LM achieves AR Avg@4 = 17.06×10⁻³ BPB and Best@4 = 23.40, vs the strongest baseline GPT-6-sol-ICL at 6.15 / 11.27 (+10.91 Avg@4) and the Base model (Qwen3.8-27B zero-shot) at −7.94. On diffusion tasks LM2LM gets Avg@4 = 7.10 vs 4.94 for GPT-6-sol-ICL (+2.16) and −4.18 for Base; it beats Base on 93.3% (AR) and 100% (diffusion) of states.
- **RL learns to explore, not just avoid harm**: negative-gain proposal rate drops from 70.4% (Base) to 39.2% (AR) and from 59.7% to 7.6% (diffusion); notably, LM2LM has a *higher* AR negative rate than GPT-6-sol-ICL (39.2% vs 29.6%) yet far higher Avg@4 (17.06 vs 6.15), indicating it pursues higher-gain (riskier) changes.
- **Fine-tuning baselines fail**: SFT (−12.19 AR Avg@4) and RFT-Real (−22.95) underperform even zero-shot Base (−7.94), showing that fine-tuning on a few hundred past proposals is insufficient without RL against a predictive reward.
- **Cost efficiency (Fig. 3)**: with 200 real runs, LM2LM ($40.1k, 343h data generation) beats ICL with K=300 records ($60.0k, 510h) — ~33% lower cost/time; total cost including RL training is still lower ($56.1k vs $60.0k). From steps 40→96 it improves AR Avg@4 from 10.85 to 17.06 for only $11.8k extra, scoring >14k sampled proposals with just 69 additional real calibration records.
- **Out-of-family generalization (Table 2, Qwen3 fully held out)**: LM2LM reaches Avg@4 = 53.72×10⁻³ BPB and Best@4 = 86.69 vs 21.63 / 25.27 for GPT-6-sol-ICL — more than doubling the strongest baseline on an unseen model family.
- **Ablations (Fig. 4)**: on-policy calibration beats a static RWM with the same 200-run budget of random extra records (17.06 vs 14.76 AR Avg@4); replacing top-rated selection with random calibration drops Avg@4 from 17.06 to 10.84 and Best@4 from 23.40 to 18.60.
- **Why it works (Fig. 5)**: RWM feedback stays informative (advantage cosine 0.50–0.71, pairwise accuracy 0.61–0.69 vs chance 0.5), while a static-record RWM degrades to advantage cosine −0.42 and pairwise accuracy 0.37 (below chance) by step 128, with predicted–real reward gap reaching ~2.0 at step 96 vs <1 (0.85→0.17) for calibrated LM2LM. A case study shows the initial RWM overestimated a NanoChat optimizer change (predicted +0.7..+1.25, real −1.46); the static variant's NanoChat proposal share ballooned 30%→79% by step 64, while one calibration cut it to 8%.

The resource scale (hundreds of real training runs, frontier proprietary models GPT-6-sol/GPT-6-astra as RWM/builder) implies an industrial-lab-grade budget.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Solid and timely. The combination of (a) a frozen world-model-as-verifier for RLVR in a domain without cheap verification and (b) *on-policy* calibration targeted at the exact proposals RL favors is a genuine methodological contribution over both ICL-over-records and execution-feedback fine-tuning (RFT). The closest prior line (Si et al. 2026-style execution-feedback RL) is clearly distinguished by cost analysis.
- **Rigor**: Strong on evaluation protocol — held-out tasks AND held-out real histories, real training runs for every gain, cross-family (AR + diffusion) coverage, per-state win rates, and an honest dissection of reward overoptimization with cosine/pairwise/gap metrics. The ablations are well-targeted (static M₀′ with matched 200-run budget is exactly the right control).
- **Reproducibility**: Weak-to-moderate. The pipeline depends on proprietary frontier models (GPT-6-sol as RWM at "high reasoning effort", GPT-6-astra as builder) whose behavior can drift or be unavailable; the curated "library of training-recipe mechanisms" and 300-record corpus are not stated to be released. GPU pricing ($14.208/B200-h) and hyperparameters (96 DAPO steps, G=8, c=6, 3 calibrations) are given, but exact reproduction by academic groups is unlikely at ~$56k per run.
- **Significance**: High if it holds up — it is a concrete, measured step toward learned research judgment / RSI, showing parameter learning beats both frontier-model prompting and record-based fine-tuning. The 33% cost saving vs ICL and the Qwen3 transfer are the most persuasive datapoints.
- **Red flags / weaknesses to note**: (1) The headline "outperforms GPT-6-sol" compares a 27B specialist *trained on this task distribution* against a general-purpose zero/ICL frontier model — an expected-but-narrow victory; the fairer frontier comparison (GPT-6-sol-ICL) is beaten by +10.9 on AR but only +2.16 on diffusion, where LM2LM's negative-gain rate (7.6%) still trails GPT-6-sol-ICL's perfect 0.0%. (2) Absolute gains are small (10⁻³ BPB scale) and the target models are ≤486M-parameter models trained on ~8B tokens — nothing is shown about whether RWM-guided judgment transfers to large-scale pretraining where proposal costs explode. (3) The RWM's pairwise accuracy is only 0.61–0.69 (barely above the 0.5 chance floor), so much of the signal is rank noise; the paper's own analysis admits a static RWM ends up *worse than random*, underscoring how fragile the surrogate is without calibration. (4) Evaluation is proposal-level, not end-to-end campaigns (acknowledged in Limitations); sequential/compounding gains over a full campaign are untested. (5) Held-out Qwen3 Avg@4 (53.72) is ~3× the in-family AR number (17.06), which suggests the held-out task set may be unusually easy — worth scrutiny.

## Strengths

- Directly attacks the two real bottlenecks of learning-based AutoResearch: feedback cost and surrogate reliability under policy shift, with a clean mechanism (in-context record refresh) for the latter.
- Every headline number comes from real model-training executions on held-out states — no simulator-in-the-loop inflation.
- The overoptimization analysis (advantage cosine, pairwise accuracy, predicted–real gap, NanoChat case study) is the paper's most intellectually valuable part and gives a reusable diagnostic toolkit for learned-verifier RL.
- Cost accounting is unusually honest, including RL training cost in the comparison ($56.1k vs $60.0k) rather than hiding it behind "data-generation cost".
- Demonstrates transfer to an unseen model family (Qwen3) with all its records excluded — evidence the learned judgment is not memorization of family-specific quirks.

## Limitations

- Scope is proposal-level (single step) rather than end-to-end research campaigns; sequential decision-making over long campaigns, where records compound and errors cascade, is untested (acknowledged by the authors).
- Calibration still requires a nontrivial number of real model-training runs (100 initial + 100 calibration by step 96, 236 by step 128) — cheap relative to naive RL but far from free, and the budget is fixed by hand (U=3, Bu per round) rather than adaptively chosen.
- The RWM is a proprietary frontier model; the approach's dependence on it (and its token costs, included only in data-generation accounting at "GPT-6-sol token rates") complicates replication and could couple the learner to one vendor's model quirks.
- Target models are small (up to 486M params); scalability of both the RWM's predictive accuracy and the calibration budget to frontier-scale training is unknown.
- Only validation loss (BPB/NLL) defines "gain"; downstream capability, safety, or Pareto effects (compute-for-quality trade-offs) are not part of the objective.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"用 LLM 自动化 AI 研究、朝递归自我改进演进"的兴趣高度相关：它把"研究判断力"变成可用世界模型奖励做 RL 学习的技能，并以真实实验校准解决了学习型验证器的过优化问题，是 AutoResearch/RSI 方向的关键实证进展。 其"冻结 LLM 作世界模型 + 在线真实校准"的思路对任何需要昂贵实验反馈的自我改进系统（模型开发、超参/架构搜索、科学发现代理）都有直接借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the learned proposer transfer across *builder* and *RWM* backbones, or is the policy co-adapted to GPT-6-sol's reward quirks (the paper's own NanoChat case suggests sensitivity)?
- Can the calibration budget be chosen adaptively (e.g., trigger execution when the predicted–real gap or advantage cosine degrades) instead of fixed rounds at steps 16/40/64?
- Would the RWM benefit from fine-tuning or tool use (e.g., small surrogate training runs) for out-of-distribution mechanism directions, given pairwise accuracy is only ~0.61–0.69?
- Do gains compound in end-to-end campaigns where the proposer must interleave exploration/exploitation over dozens of sequential steps with a growing history?
- How does the approach behave when the objective includes compute cost or downstream benchmarks rather than pure validation loss — does diversity-bonus-driven exploration still pay off?
