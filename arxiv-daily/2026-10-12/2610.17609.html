Critical edge

The +9.00-pt headline rests on preference judged by a single same-family judge; under the benchmarks' native metrics the gain shrinks to +1.31 pts and is not uniform across benchmarks.

01 Methodology — the verifier is a program, and only the program evolves

S1
Draft
Fixed actor Fθ₀ emits y₀ from harness state
S2
Diagnose
D turns the weakest criterion into one repair instruction
S3
Repair
One local edit; serve revision only if reward beats draft by ε
S4
Gate memory
Only accepted repairs write bounded procedures
S5
Checkpoint
Re-select verifier by maximin; replace only with no family regression
  • Verifier as a program V = (Φ, A, D) — criterion scorers Φ, aggregator A, diagnosis D. Only Φ and A evolve; the interpreter and verifier model (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) stay frozen.
  • Driving capacity (the selection rule) — candidates are ranked by discrimination on their weakest contrast family (Jj = 2pj − 1, a maximin objective), so strength on easy properties cannot mask a blind spot. Theorem 1: this maximizes worst-case selection over every family mixture; Prop 2: gain = opportunity q × ability Jmix.
  • Response contrasts as verifier test-bed — calibration responses edited to break exactly one property (omitted content, unsupported claims, irrelevant material + mined benchmark families) form (y⁺, y⁻) pairs with a declared preference.
  • Prequential scoring — every response is produced from state inherited from earlier tasks; final-evaluator feedback never leaks into scoring.

  • Ablation (Arena-Hard) — removing current-task repair costs the most: 58.30% → 50.80% (−7.50, back to parity); each evolution component adds another 1.6–1.7 pts on top.
  • Real agent harness — inside a Codex coding-agent on SpreadsheetBench-2 (GPT-5.6 actors): 22.7% vs 17.3% exact match for direct generation and LLM-as-Judge.
  • Cost — $0.0142/task at list prices, 2.2–2.8× the simpler feedback baselines, mostly verifier-model scoring; SpyRL costs more ($0.0160) while being less preferred on all six benchmarks.
  • Significance — bootstrap 95% CI above parity in 11 of 12 open-ended settings; transfers to Qwen3-14B, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.4-mini actors.

03 Strengths

  • Closes a real gap — training-free methods froze the verifier; UCoE evolves it under a safe-by-construction, no-regression update rule.
  • The maximin driving-capacity objective is principled (covariance meaning + worst-case-mixture optimality), directly countering "aggregate scores hide blind spots".
  • Evaluation breadth: 9 benchmarks, 5 actors including closed GPT models, a real Codex harness, native metrics, bootstrap intervals, cost accounting, mechanism study.
  • Conservative evolution: candidates must beat the incumbent with no per-family regression; drift is structurally resisted.
  • Code, proofs, hyperparameters released; the paper reports its own weak spots (MultiChallenge, cost, DREAM's Hard-MATH edge).

04 Limitations

  • Contrast validity is assumed, not established — single-property edits don't guarantee discrimination on natural or interacting errors (conceded in App. A).
  • Coupled risk: one program diagnoses, selects the served response, AND gates memory — a single wrong judgment can poison persistent experience; no independent rollback.
  • Open-ended evaluation rests on one same-family judge; native-metric gains are ~7× smaller than preference margins; MultiChallenge is a consistent weak spot.
  • 2.2–2.8× listed-price cost premium limits practicality at scale.
  • One-revision protocol only; baselines were stripped of their training loops, so results don't speak to training-based pipelines (RaR, rubric-evolution RL).

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. How much of the preference gain survives human evaluation or judges from a different model family than the actors?
  2. Can contrast construction extend to compound edits (two properties broken jointly) so driving capacity measures discrimination on failure modes real drafts exhibit?
  3. Can Prop 2's opportunity term q be estimated online to predict, before deployment, whether a task stream pays for the 2.2–2.8× cost premium?
  4. Does the coupled-risk failure (one bad judgment poisoning procedural memory) occur measurably under adversarial or distribution-shifted streams — and would decoupling the memory gate remove it?
  5. How do gains scale with deeper revision budgets, longer streams, stronger verifier models — and is the multi-turn weakness a fundamental limit of single-property contrast testing?
