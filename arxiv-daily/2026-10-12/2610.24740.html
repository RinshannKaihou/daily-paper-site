01 Methodology — the PAA-Bench protocol

An evaluation-methodology paper, not a new pruning method. Section 3 audits both sides of “maximize speedup subject to accuracy ≥ τ”: post-pruning accuracy is decomposed into dense accuracy, C→W loss, and W→C “spurious gains” (Eq. 2) — traced to accidental distractor removal and textual-prior shortcuts (overlap with text-only-correct answers rises from ~45% at 196 tokens to 76% at 16). On efficiency, matched token budgets place methods at different runtime operating points. Section 4 repairs both:

1

Build D⁺

Keep only examples the dense model answers correctly — wrong-to-correct gains excluded by construction.

2

Stratify by Random-Pass@k

k = 10 random prunings per example → Easy (8–10) / Medium (4–7) / Hard (0–3). Proposition 1: high random-pass rate ⇒ small informed-selection headroom.

3

Compare via PCS@τ

Max measured end-to-end speedup (selection + gathering overhead included) at a shared preservation target, e.g. τ = 95% — matched capability, not matched tokens.

SCOPE — training-free pruners CDPruner · DivPrune · FastV · VisionZip · DART · PruMerge · SparseVLM · VisPruner + random baseline | LLaVA-1.5-7B (128/64/32) · LLaVA-NeXT-7B (640/320/160) · Qwen3-VL-8B (10%/20%) | 9 benchmarks · single PRO 6000 GPU, batch 1, ≤128-token outputs

03 Strengths

  • One coherent protocol answers the right question — meaningful token selection and realized speedup — instead of three disconnected metrics.
  • Random-Pass@k is elegant and method-agnostic: any benchmark becomes selection-sensitive, with Proposition 1 bounding the headroom.
  • Diagnoses are quantified, not anecdotal — exact transition rates, text-only overlap statistics, pairwise ranking-reversal counts.
  • PCS fixes a real malpractice (matched-token comparison) and proves a 4-token method can be slower than a 49-token one.
  • Plug-and-play: no new benchmark data needed, so adoption cost is low.

04 Limitations

  • Heavy evaluation cost: Random-Pass@k ≈ 10× extra forward passes per example per setting — never reported or discussed.
  • Batch-size-1 timing, single GPU, ≤128-token outputs — conclusions may not transfer to batched or long-generation serving.
  • Heuristic stratification: Easy/Medium/Hard cutoffs and k=10 lack any sensitivity analysis.
  • D⁺ conditioning ties metrics to the dense model's own accuracy; the denominator shrinks on hard benchmarks.
  • No code release; no video-VLM or >8B runtimes; PCS's max-over-configurations favors denser budget sweeps.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Do Random-Pass@k distributions and PCS rankings change under batched serving and long-form generation, where decode-time KV-cache savings dominate?
  • Can the 10 random trials be replaced by an analytical estimator of p_N (e.g. from attention-map statistics) to cut the 10× overhead?
  • Do wrong-to-correct “spurious gains” correlate with hallucination metrics — e.g. pruning-induced yes-bias on POPE-style benchmarks?
  • Would fixing τ per difficulty stratum (Hard examples dominate loss) change the PCS Pareto frontier?
  • How does the framework extend to video VLMs, where token redundancy and temporal selection difficulty differ sharply?
  • Rankings flip between τ = 95% and 90% — is there a principled way to pick the operationally meaningful preservation target?
