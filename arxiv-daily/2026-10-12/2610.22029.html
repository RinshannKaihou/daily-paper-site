Why this matters

It diagnoses and fixes the core failure mode of self-evolving LLM loops: a commit gate and an experience archive any self-improvement pipeline can adopt.

Critical edge

The whole loop trains on competition math only — no coding, tool-use, or agentic benchmark is tested, even though the strongest baseline (Agent0) is a tool-integrated method.

Methodology — one round of EVOHEIR

Two co-evolving agents — a Curriculum Scout proposing problems and an Evolving Learner — coordinated by an Evolution Controller; every round ends in a single inheritance decision.

  1. 1

    Explore

    1,024 problem-generation attempts; consensus pseudo-labels from 10 responses (support ≥ 0.7); two isolated branches train from the resumable root on 64-question cohorts (AdamW, lr 10⁻⁶, 15 steps).

  2. 2

    Screen a recipient

    Paired root-relative scoring on a 16-question pool picks the parameter recipient — a branch endpoint or the root itself.

  3. 3

    Recover experience

    Frozen recipient-initialized teachers distill via LoRA forward-KL (rank 8, ≤16 updates): the repair teacher sees the donor reference, the keep teacher only the problem. Qualified records enter a bounded archive (≤256) whatever the commit outcome.

  4. 4

    Confirm · HEIRCOMMIT

    Freeze the proposal; score it against the root on 32 fresh questions (4 responses each); commit only on strictly positive gain, else roll back parameters, optimizer, scheduler, RNGs — the archive keeps the round's experience.

Why this shape: Prop 1 — pre-update observations cannot identify update value (worst-case regret ≥ Δ/2). Theorem 1 — reusing screening evidence for inheritance pays a K-branch union bound; one fresh confirmation pays a single sub-Gaussian tail.

Strengths

  • The failure mode is measured, not asserted — fresh-response audits, exact-valid NLL audits, fixed-target optimism controls.
  • Theory and system cohere: disjoint pools, single-proposal confirmation, an explicit regret decomposition (selection + absorption + confirmation error).
  • HEIRCOMMIT is cheap and source-independent — validated on three external hosts' native updates, not just the authors' loop.
  • Decoupling claims backed by unusually clean interventions: fixed recipients, matched record counts, age-resolved bootstrap CIs.
  • Exceptional documentation: full prompts, budget accounting, and self-disclosed failure modes (including their own round-7 regression).

Limitations

  • Math-only by construction — no coding, tool-use, or agentic evaluation, despite agentic baselines.
  • Modest absolute gains: +0.55–2.40 pp Math Avg; near-noise on the instruction-tuned Gemma-4-E4B-it.
  • Heavy compute: 8–9 h per round on 2×96-GB GPUs; 23.7% accounting overhead; loses its edge at 0.8× budget.
  • The τ = 0 gate carries no formal guarantee, and the 16/32-question decision pools are small; consensus pseudo-labels can ratify confident errors.
  • Absorption can still hurt (−0.20 pp at round 7); archive behavior beyond 10 rounds is unexplored.
  • Three seeds, ten rounds, no code release — every trajectory claim is statistically thin.

Questions for further exploration

  1. How much of the gate's benefit survives when confirmation labels come from an external verifier instead of ≥0.7-support self-consensus?
  2. Does EVOHEIR transfer to code, tool-use, or agentic domains where verification is programmatic rather than answer-matching?
  3. The certified positive-margin mode (τ from the sub-Gaussian bound) is analyzed but never run — what acceptance rate and performance would it buy?
  4. Does the 256-record archive keep helping far beyond 10 rounds, or does stale-reference repair eventually mislead later recipients?
  5. Could a simpler per-update validation baseline (APE-style acceptance, no archive) capture most of the benefit at a fraction of the 23.7% overhead?
  6. Do the conclusions hold under correlated errors across branches, which the union bound may cover too loosely?
