> **一句话总结：** 这篇 NeurIPS 2026 可解释性 workshop 论文系统对比了"符号拆分 + TopK 剪枝的原始 MLP 神经元"与 13 个开源 transcoder：仅给剪枝神经元输出加一个可训练线性映射，KL（替代忠实度）即可低于 transcoder，且在 113 个 sparse-probing 任务上神经元 AUC 全面不输 transcoder（Gemma 3 4B 上 0.854 vs 0.832）；作者进一步定义"神经元特征"（编码器与解码器同时对齐单个神经元的 transcoder 特征），证明它们只占词典 5.9%–37.1% 却贡献了后期层 57.9%–82.8% 的激活、承载了几乎全部 Jacobian 忠实度（对齐 ≈0.3 vs 非神经元特征仅 ≈0.05）、是跨随机种子唯一稳定复现的结构（1M 宽词典只找到约 22k 个稳定特征，与 Gemma 3 4B 的 20,480 个符号拆分神经元相当），而其余特征在 TopK 训练中几乎不离开随机初始化——结论是 MLP 神经元是比普遍认知更强的可解释性基底，transcoder 的独特价值主要剩下用 2–4 个神经元的稀疏组合解析真正的多义性（如 bigram：75.6% vs 0.0% 的 top-1 命中率）。

> **TL;DR:** A mechanistic-interpretability paper asking whether transcoders (the sparse learned MLP replacements underlying attribution-graph methods like Anthropic's circuit tracing) add anything over raw MLP neurons. Sign-split, top-k-pruned neurons plus one small trained d_model×d_model output map beat transcoders on KL replacement divergence while losing on FVU, and match or exceed 8 transcoders on the 113-task Kantamneni sparse-probing suite (Gemma 3 4B: 0.854 vs 0.832 mean AUC at k=1; Gemma 2 2B: 0.851 vs 0.780). Defining "neuron features" (transcoder features whose encoder AND decoder align with one neuron at cosine > 0.6–0.7), the authors show these are 5.9%→37.1% of late-layer dictionaries but 57.9%→82.8% of activations across the Gemma 3 1B→27B scale, carry essentially all Jacobian faithfulness (≈0.3 alone vs ≈0.05 for the rest), and are the only features that reproduce across seeds — while non-neuron features barely move from random initialization in TopK training. The transcoder's clearest genuine win is fine-grained concepts like bigrams (top-1 example match 75.6% vs 0.0% on Gemma 3 4B), which non-neuron features built from sparse 2–4-neuron combinations can resolve. Read this before choosing a substrate for circuit analysis.

## 核心贡献

- **把"剪枝神经元"变成严格的对比基线（第 2 节）：** 对门控 MLP 神经元按激活符号拆分（W_up ± W_gate 为正/负有效编码器，W_down 为解码器），再按层做 TopK（k≈50–64）剪枝，使原始 MLP 本身成为一个"窄 transcoder"。仅在其输出上训练一个 d_model×d_model 线性映射后，KL 散度降到低于 transcoder（FVU 仍更高），推翻了 Ameisen et al. (2025) 中"神经元在 FVU–KL 双轴上帕累托更差"的结论；该结果跨模型复现。
- **定义并量化"神经元特征"（第 3.1–3.2 节）：** 编码器与解码器同时对齐同一个神经元（余弦相似度 > 0.6–0.7）的 transcoder 特征。在 13 个开源 transcoder（Gemma 2/3、Qwen 3、Llama 3.1/3.2，1B–27B）中，它们在后期层占词典 5.9%→37.1%（随模型规模上升），却因平均激发频率高 16.9 倍而占激活的 57.9%→82.8%；在后期层的归因图中占 ≥40% 的节点（除一个例外）。
- **三条独立证据表明神经元特征是 transcoder 学到的唯一一致结构（3.4–3.6 节）：** (1) transcoder 的 Jacobian 与 MLP 的对齐（≈0.3）几乎全部来自神经元特征子集，非神经元子集仅 ≈0.05；(2) 跨随机种子复现的特征中 >50% 是神经元特征，且 1M 宽词典也只找到约 22k 个稳定特征（与 20,480 个符号拆分神经元同量级）——单纯加宽不会发现新的稳定结构；(3) TopK 训练中非神经元特征几乎停留在随机初始化（与初始化的中位余弦 +0.48，而神经元特征为 0.03）。
- **神经元初始化（NI）与训练期干预（3.7 节、附录 R）：** 把编码器初始化为 ±(gate±up) 方向后，对齐神经元的特征占比从 11% 升至 92%（解码器侧 12%→93%），对齐特征覆盖 95% 的激活质量（原 65%），且收敛更快、死特征更少；进一步提出 NFT（按构造聚类神经元）与 SNRL（稀疏神经元重建损失），用 FVU 仅 +0.026 的代价让词典特征变成神经元的稀疏组合。
- **诚实的反例分析（2.3、3.10 节）：** transcoder 确实轻松表示 bigram（Gemma 3 4B 上 top-1 示例命中率 75.6% vs 神经元的 0.0%；Qwen 3 4B 上 21.7% vs 0.6%），且"红色"案例表明非神经元特征可以是 2–4 个多义神经元的稀疏组合（如 F28197 ≈ 0.68·N5039⁺ + 0.38·N7405⁺ + 0.15·N10100⁺ + 0.09·N4137⁺），说明 transcoder 在解析真实多义性上仍有价值；MoE 模型（Gemma 4 26B-A4B 路由神经元）bigram 命中率达 20.1%，提示规模化的 MoE 神经元本身可能接近 transcoder 的单义性。
- **三条给研究者的操作性建议（第 5 节）：** 避免使用宽度扩展 < 4× 的 transcoder（对 MoE 实际上意味着别用）；对比时用 TopK≈64 + 符号拆分的神经元基线、训练词典时考虑神经元式初始化；评估词典学习方法时聚焦跨种子复现且对齐原模块计算的元素。

## Methodology

The comparison is built in two layers. First, **neurons as a feature basis** (Section 2): for gated MLPs (GeGLU/SwiGLU), each neuron is split by activation sign into two features whose effective encoders are W_up ± W_gate and whose decoder is W_down (justified by an approximate-bilinearity argument at high activations, Appendix A.3). Layer-wise top-k thresholding (k≈50–64, layer-adaptive because Qwen 3 lacks post-feedforward LayerNorm) turns the MLP into a sparse replacement model; an optionally trained d_model×d_model linear map on the pruned output (plus optional skip connection and per-neuron rank-1 terms) closes the KL gap. This baseline is evaluated against transcoders on replacement KL / FVU (following Ameisen et al. 2025), Replacement Score at matched L0 for attribution graphs built with RelP, the 113-task Kantamneni sparse-probing suite (L1-penalized logistic probes at k∈{1,…,32}), a new 1,500-bigram probing benchmark sampled from FineWeb by PMI with hard negatives, and sort evals (Claude Haiku 4.5 judge, 500 trials per configuration, top-of-distribution vs quantile sampling).

Second, **the neuron-feature analysis** (Section 3): a transcoder feature is called a neuron feature when its encoder and decoder both exceed a cosine threshold (0.6–0.7, transcoder-dependent) to the *same* neuron — validated by a bimodal joint distribution (Figure 4), activation-predictability (median AUC ≥ 0.977 of neuron activation predicting feature firing), and logit/circuit correspondence. The authors then test where transcoder structure is *consistent with the underlying MLP*: rank-1 Jacobian alignment (Frobenius cosine between summed per-feature Jacobian contributions and the MLP Jacobian, following Lindsey et al.), cross-seed feature matching (two transcoders differing only in init seed, matched at encoder+decoder cosine > 0.7), and distance-from-initialization analysis comparing TopK and JumpReLU pipelines. Case studies re-examine published circuits — character-position/line-breaking features (Gurnee et al. 2025), base-10 addition neurons in Llama 3.1 8B (Feucht et al. 2026), and a "red" translation prompt — asking whether the previously reported transcoder features are neuron features, with ablations (steering-strength α=1 removal, α=2 sign-flip) for causal necessity. Training-time interventions (neuron initialization, NFT, SNRL) test whether the non-neuron structure can be steered toward sparse neuron combinations. Scope: 13 open-source transcoders (GemmaScope, mwhanna, LXTC, mntss) on dense models 1B–27B, plus preliminary MoE (Qwen 3.5 35B-A3B transcoders, Gemma 4 26B-A4B probing) and cross-layer transcoder analyses.

## Results & Highlights

- **Replacement faithfulness (KL):** untrained pruned neurons start with high KL and FVU > 1 (replicating Ameisen et al.), but after training only a linear map on the pruned output, neurons' KL drops *below* that of transcoders on Gemma 3 4B while FVU stays substantially higher — and this pattern replicates across models. Interpretation: much of what transcoders learn improves reconstruction only, not behavior.
- **Sparse probing (113 Kantamneni tasks, mean AUC at k=1):** sign-split neurons match or exceed the best-available transcoder on **all 8** model pairs — e.g., Gemma 3 4B: 0.854 vs 0.832 (+0.022 ± 0.008 per-task mean), Gemma 2 2B: 0.851 vs 0.780 (+0.071 ± 0.012), Llama 3.1 8B: 0.856 vs 0.845 (+0.011 ± 0.007); the sign of the gap is preserved at every k ∈ {1,2,4,8,16,32}.
- **Bigram probing (1,500 FineWeb bigrams) — the clearest transcoder win:** transcoders beat neurons on AUC at every layer and k (Gemma 3 4B layer 0, k=1: 0.991 Gemmascope vs 0.891 neurons), but the qualitative gap is larger: the selected Gemmascope feature's top-1 max-activating example contains the target bigram 75.6% of the time vs ~0.0% for neurons (Qwen 3 4B Matryoshka-TopK: 21.7% vs 0.6%). Neurons still reach a high mean AUC of 0.988, so AUC alone overstates concept presence — the paper flags this as a probing-methodology issue. Gemma 4 26B-A4B routed neurons match bigrams in 20.1% of cases (vs 0.9% dense), suggesting MoE scale improves fine-grained monosemanticity of raw neurons.
- **Neuron-feature prevalence (late layers, 2/3 depth):** share of dictionary rises 5.9% → 8.9% → 15.6% → 37.1% across Gemma 3 1B → 4B → 12B → 27B, while share of activations rises 57.9% → 82.8%; neuron features fire 16.9× more often on average (mean firing fraction 1.2e-3 vs 7.2e-5, Gemma 3 4B Gemmascope-small L22). In late-layer attribution graphs they account for ≥40% of nodes for every transcoder tested except one (Llama 3.2 1B mntss, ~38%), and remain 56–61% under influence pruning from L0=10 to 100.
- **Faithfulness concentrates in neuron features:** the full transcoder's Jacobian alignment with the MLP is ≈0.3; the neuron-feature subset alone reproduces ≈0.3 while the non-aligned subset reaches only ≈0.05 (Gemma 3 4B L22; all layers similar). Unmodified top-k-pruned neurons match or exceed the full transcoder's Jacobian alignment across late layers.
- **Cross-seed stability:** given a feature is shared between two seeds, it is >50% likely to be a neuron feature; scaling width 32k→1M grows stable features only 9.8k→22.0k — the 1M-feature dictionary finds roughly as many stable features as the model has sign-split neurons (20,480). Non-neuron features stay close to initialization in TopK training (median cos to init +0.48, many >0.8) while neuron features rotate away (median 0.03).
- **Neuron initialization (NI):** fraction of features with encoder cosine > 0.5 to a neuron rises from 11% (random init) to 92% (NI), decoder side 12% → 93%; aligned features carry 95% of activation mass, up from 65%.
- **Arithmetic case study (Llama 3.1 8B, Feucht et al.'s 28 addition neurons):** neuron features are the majority of features writing into the addition output subspace and the only ones with strong causal effects — ablating all 87 addition features drops addition accuracy from 94.3% to 30.6% (zeroing the 28 neurons themselves: 94.3% → 23.6%), while mid-aligned features have no noticeable effect at realistic steering strengths.
- **Width sweep:** fraction of neurons matched by ≥1 transcoder feature at |cos| ≥ 0.7 rises 78% (32k width) → 93% (256k) and plateaus through 1M; matched neurons receive median 2 features, and neuron features fire ~3× less often than their neurons (median ratio 0.34).

## Authors & Credibility

Two authors with equal contribution: Stepan Shabalin (Anthropic Fellows program, Georgia Tech; correspondence) and Mateusz Piotrowski (Anthropic). Accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 workshop "Interpretability as a Science" (not the main conference). Funded by the Anthropic Fellows Program, a BlueDot rapid grant, and compute from White Circle; total reported GPU budget is on the order of 1,000–1,200 H200-hours, which is substantial for an academic two-author study. The paper is notable for engaging critically with the attribution-graph methodology used by Anthropic's own circuit-tracing line (Ameisen et al. 2025) while one author is Anthropic-affiliated — and for replicating that prior work's neuron-negative finding first, then showing it reverses with a one-line correction (the trained output map). The appendixes are unusually extensive (21 appendices, A–U), including a compute statement and an AI-assistance disclosure (Claude Code used for writing and code, with selective reruns). Benchmark numbers cross-check against their cited sources (e.g., the Ameisen et al. neuron Pareto-worse claim, Kantamneni et al.'s 113 tasks).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core deflationary claim — that a large, behaviorally relevant fraction of transcoder features are repackaged neurons — is new and well-evidenced, and it lands at a moment when attribution-graph methods are being sold as the interpretability substrate for frontier models. The sign-split + top-k neuron baseline formalizes something practitioners informally knew (and Arora et al. 2026, Ye et al. 2025 gestured at), but the systematic 13-transcoder audit, the Jacobian decomposition, and the seed-stability analysis are genuinely new contributions.
- **Rigor — the threshold is hand-tuned:** the neuron-feature label depends on a cosine threshold that varies 0.6–0.9 *per transcoder*; the clusters are visually bimodal, but the paper's own limitations section concedes no comprehensive account of how architecture decides the threshold, and the "almost neuron features" population in TopK transcoders is left unquantified — some headline fractions would likely shift with the cutoff.
- **Rigor — the KL win needs a trained correction:** "pruned neurons beat transcoders on KL" is only true after training a d_model×d_model linear map (jointly on FVU and KL) on the pruned output. That map is much smaller than a transcoder, but it is still a learned component, so the framing of neurons as an "out of the box" basis should be read with that asterisk.
- **Faithfulness is first-order only:** the claim that neuron features "carry most of the faithfulness" is established for Jacobian (first-order) alignment, not interventional faithfulness — the authors state this restriction explicitly. The cross-layer unfaithfulness claim (correlation ρ ∈ [−0.59, −0.93] between cross-layer decoder usage and neuron alignment) rests on only two narrow models (Gemma 2 2B, Llama 3.2 1B).
- **Automated evaluation only:** there is no systematic human evaluation; sort evals use Claude Haiku 4.5 as judge (50–80k judge calls), and the paper itself warns that sparse probing can reward features merely *correlated* with the target concept — a caveat that applies symmetrically to its own probing wins for neurons.
- **Data quirks:** the sparse-probing margins are modest (+0.011 on Llama 3.1 8B is within noise of ±0.007), the Qwen transcoders are admitted to be weak (one late-layer prevalence claim is quietly downgraded to the circuit claim in a footnote), and the bigram benchmark was constructed by the authors after noticing their first benchmark's transcoder losses — a defensible but post-hoc benchmark-design move. The "red" polysemanticity-resolving case study is a single prompt.
- **Reproducibility:** good — all 13 transcoders are open-source releases (GemmaScope, mwhanna, LXTC, mntss), models are open (Gemma/Qwen/Llama 1B–27B), methodology appendices are detailed (probe penalties, sampling regimes, seeds), and compute is reported. No code release is mentioned, however.
- **Significance:** if the conclusions hold, they materially change tooling choices (the <4× width-expansion recommendation effectively rules transcoders out for MoEs, where most frontier open models live) and reframe a large literature that dismissed neurons via automated interpretability scores. The honest co-presentation of the bigram counter-result strengthens rather than weakens the paper.

## Strengths

- Directly stress-tests the field's default substrate (transcoders/SAE features) against the cheapest possible baseline (the neurons themselves), with the comparison made apples-to-apples via sign-splitting and matched-L0 evaluation.
- Three mutually independent lines of evidence (Jacobian alignment, cross-seed recurrence, distance-from-initialization) all converge on the same conclusion — this triangulation is the paper's strongest methodological feature.
- Publishes and engages with its own counter-evidence: the bigram benchmark where transcoders decisively win, and the "red" case where non-neuron features genuinely resolve polysemanticity.
- Practical, actionable recommendations (TopK≈64 sign-split baselines, neuron initialization, avoid <4× expansion) and training interventions (NI, NFT, SNRL) that others can adopt immediately.
- Exceptional appendix depth: case-study ablations, per-model tables, toy-model controls for imperfect teacher-feature replication, a compute accounting, and an unusually candid limitations section.

## Limitations

- Base models only, mostly English data; the 13 main transcoders are all dense, per-layer, 1B–27B — the MoE and cross-layer extensions are explicitly preliminary, while most current open-weight frontier models are MoEs.
- Only sparse, high-magnitude activations are characterized; low-magnitude and multi-token activation patterns are out of scope, as is any notion of features read across multiple tokens.
- Only TopK and JumpReLU dictionaries from two codebases are studied; the neuron-feature fraction depends on dictionary architecture and on a threshold (0.6/0.7/0.9) chosen per transcoder without a principled account of why.
- The faithfulness argument is first-order (Jacobian) only, not interventional; no systematic human evaluation is run, so interpretability claims rest on the same automated-metric paradigm the paper partially criticizes.
- Cross-seed analysis does not handle features that *approximately* co-occur across seeds, and the diffuse combinations-of-dozens-of-neurons regime is explicitly not characterized.
- Several constructive threads are left open: where transcoder decoder directions come from, how to find specific concepts as neuron combinations, and whether neuron features can be removed from transcoders without breaking the objective.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：它对 SAE/transcoder 这一可解释性核心工具的适用边界做了系统审计，给出的神经元特征定义、跨种子稳定性检验和 TopK≈64 符号拆分基线，对任何要做 circuit 分析、归因图或探针实验的工作都是可直接采用的实操结论。其"评估指标（AUC、自动解释）可能高估概念存在性"的方法论警示，也与探针类内部状态分析的可靠性评估相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the neuron-feature concentration result hold for instruction-tuned / RLHF models and non-English data, where late-layer computations may be organized differently than in base models?
- Can interventional (not just Jacobian) faithfulness attribution replicate the finding that non-neuron features contribute ~nothing to behavior, or would targeted edits reveal causal roles invisible to first-order analysis?
- Why exactly do TopK and JumpReLU pipelines differ so much in how far non-neuron features travel from initialization (width-independent vs width-dependent init scale + L1 penalty) — is the "stuck at init" phenomenon an artifact of specific training recipes?
- For MoE models, does the rising per-expert neuron count genuinely close the fine-grained-feature gap with transcoders (as the Gemma 4 26B-A4B 20.1% bigram-match result hints), and can expert-routing structure substitute for dictionary learning?
- Can the "almost neuron features" — sparse combinations of 2–4 neurons like the "red" features — be recovered systematically (e.g., via the SNRL loss) at dictionary scale, yielding a hybrid basis that keeps neuron faithfulness while resolving genuine polysemanticity?
- How do the recommendations change for cross-layer transcoders, where the paper's preliminary evidence suggests cross-layer decoders are largely unfaithful — is per-layer analysis plus supernode graphs over neurons the better default?
