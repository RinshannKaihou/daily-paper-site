> **一句话总结：** 本文从方差缩减的统一视角系统对比了扩散模型训练中的 EMA、学习率衰减与 Schedule-Free 三种稳定策略：在 SiT-XL/2+REPA（675M，ImageNet 256×256）上，Schedule-Free AdamW 在所有训练阶段的 FID-5K 均稳定优于标准的恒定学习率+EMA（400k 步 9.05 vs 9.21，800k 步 FID-50K 1.87 vs 2.08），且无需预设衰减起点，而衰减+EMA 的收益对开始时机敏感；核心结论是最小化参数波动本身并不等于最好的优化结果，需在稳定性与持续优化之间取得平衡。

> **TL;DR:** Asking whether EMA is truly irreplaceable for diffusion training, the authors unify EMA, LR decay, and Schedule-Free as three variance-reduction schemes with different stability-vs-progress trade-offs, then test them on a 675M SiT-XL/2+REPA ImageNet-256 recipe. Schedule-Free AdamW beats the standard Constant-LR+EMA baseline on FID-5K at every checkpoint from 320k–400k (9.0504 vs 9.2139 at 400k, −0.16) and at 800k improves FID-50K from 2.0770 to 1.8735, while late-stage cosine decay + EMA is timing-sensitive (helps at 4 of 5 branch points, hurts at one) and freezes further loss progress; gains transfer to ReFL reward fine-tuning (HPSv3 6.35 → 9.61 at 20× LR, with reward-hacking warning signs) and generator-side DMD. Practical takeaway: when the decay point is unknown, prefer Schedule-Free; when the budget is fixed, add late decay on top of EMA.

## 核心贡献

- **提出统一的理论框架**：将 EMA、学习率衰减（LR decay）和 Schedule-Free 三种策略统一为解决随机优化中"持续参数波动"问题的不同方差缩减方案。理论上证明：恒定步长轨迹的平均（EMA）存在固有的优化偏差下界（Lemma 2.2 构造强凸反例，EMA 收敛到非最优的平稳均值），而 LR 衰减与 Schedule-Free 在标准非凸假设下具有消失的平稳性保证。
- **证明 LR 衰减与 EMA 是互补而非替代关系**：在恒定 LR 轨迹的五个后期检查点上分支施加 40k 步余弦衰减，衰减+EMA 在 5 个起点中的 4 个上改善 FID-5K（如 360k→400k 分支：在线衰减 9.4895 → 加 EMA 后 9.1248），但收益甚至符号都依赖衰减开始时机（320k→360k 分支反而从 9.2700 恶化到 9.3163）。
- **首次在现代大规模扩散 Transformer 训练中系统验证 Schedule-Free**：Schedule-Free AdamW 在 320k–400k 的每个检查点上 FID-5K 均优于恒定 LR+EMA（改善幅度 0.0784–0.1635），无需预设衰减点；在 800k 步长训练下优势保持（FID-50K 2.0770 → 1.8735）。
- **揭示"最强稳定 ≠ 最好优化"的权衡**：衰减+EMA 的检查点参数方差最低（trace 方差仅为恒定 LR 在线轨迹的 0.0016%）但后期固定评估损失不再下降（400k→405k 从 0.43696 升至 0.43760）；Schedule-Free 在大幅降低波动（0.0201%）的同时保留持续优化能力（0.43461 → 0.43447）。
- **验证跨训练范式的可迁移性**：Schedule-Free 的收益延伸到 ReFL 奖励微调（HPSv3 奖励 6.3500 → 6.8626@4×LR → 9.6134@20×LR）和生成器侧 DMD 蒸馏（3k 步 FID-5K 9.448 → 9.346），但用于引导模型侧反而有害。
- **给出实用训练配方建议**：衰减点不确定时优先 Schedule-Free AdamW（可探索更大基础学习率）；训练预算固定时将衰减作为收尾策略叠加在 EMA 上；选择优化策略时应同时考虑参数稳定性与持续优化进度。

## Key Contributions

- **A unified variance-reduction lens.** EMA (average a noisy constant-step trajectory), LR decay (shrink the trajectory itself), and Schedule-Free (average a growing trajectory while keeping large steps) are placed in one framework. A quadratic analysis shows constant-step SGD has a non-vanishing noise floor (E‖∇f‖² ≤ 2(f₀−f*)/(ηT) + Lησ²), that EMA reduces stationary variance by a factor (1−β)(1+βb)/((1+β)(1−βb)) < 1 yet cannot remove the constant-step optimization bias (Lemma 2.2 constructs a strongly convex objective where the EMA limit point has provably positive suboptimality), while LR decay and Schedule-Free both admit vanishing stationarity guarantees under standard nonconvex assumptions.
- **Decay and EMA are complementary, and decay is timing-sensitive.** Branching 40k-step cosine decays (1e-4 → 1e-6) from five late-stage checkpoints of the same Constant-LR+EMA trajectory, Decay+EMA improves FID-5K at 4 of 5 starting points but slightly hurts at one; EMA on top of a decayed trajectory always helps (360k→400k branch: 9.4895 online → 9.1248 with EMA).
- **Schedule-Free consistently beats the standard recipe in pretraining.** On the 675M SiT-XL/2+REPA ImageNet-1K 256×256 setup (batch 128, up to 800k steps), Schedule-Free AdamW (base LR 4e-4, 5k warmup) achieves lower mean FID-5K than Constant-LR+EMA at every checkpoint from 320k to 400k, plus lower fixed-evaluation loss (mostly via the REPA projection loss; lower denoising MSE at all 8 tested timesteps).
- **Stability-vs-progress trade-off made measurable.** Checkpoint trace variability ranks Decay+EMA < Schedule-Free < Constant-LR+EMA, but FID ranks the opposite for Schedule-Free (9.0504 vs 9.1248 vs 9.2139 at 400k). Post-decay training stops improving the objective while Schedule-Free keeps descending — minimizing parameter variability alone is not the right objective.
- **Transferability across diffusion regimes.** Replacing AdamW with Schedule-Free AdamW in ReFL reward fine-tuning (Z-Image-Turbo, HPSv3 reward) lifts the on-reward score from 6.3500 to 6.8626 (4× LR) and 9.6134 (20× LR); in one-step DMD (DMDR), generator-side Schedule-Free improves FID-5K at all three checkpoints while guidance-side hurts — identifying where the optimizer matters.
- **Actionable recipes.** Prefer Schedule-Free when the decay point is uncertain (and explore larger base LRs — AdamW at the same 4e-4 NaNs after ~218.7k steps where Schedule-Free stays stable); use late decay + EMA as a finishing move when the budget is known; never select a recipe solely for lowest parameter variability.

## Methodology

The paper combines a lightweight theory section with a carefully controlled empirical study.

**Theory (Section 2).** Starting from a 1-D quadratic L(x) = ½dx² with noisy gradients, the authors show constant-step SGD converges to a stationary distribution with variance ησ²/(d(2−ηd)) — a noise floor that never vanishes (Lemma 2.1, the standard smooth-nonconvex SGD bound makes this explicit: optimization error 2(f₀−f*)/(ηT) vanishes but the Lησ² floor does not). Three variance-reduction strategies are then analyzed on the same object: (i) EMA leaves the online dynamics untouched and merely averages the trajectory — it shrinks stationary variance by a factor < 1, but Lemma 2.2 proves via a strongly convex example f(x) = x²/2 + a(sin x − x) that the EMA limit inherits the constant-step bias (excess loss ≥ (1−a)m²/2 > 0); (ii) LR decay rescales both the update and the injected noise by ηt, giving E[L(xt)] = O(t⁻¹) for an inverse-time schedule and vanishing stationarity when Σηt = ∞, Σηt² < ∞ (Lemma 2.3) — at the price of suppressing further optimization steps; (iii) Schedule-Free keeps a constant base LR for the primal sequence zt while evaluating the Polyak-style average xt, achieving E[L(xt)] = O(t⁻¹) on the quadratic and a nonconvex stationarity guarantee (Lemma 2.4, restated from Chae & Kim 2026). The message: averaging a constant-step trajectory has an inherent bias limit; decay and Schedule-Free remove it, but only Schedule-Free does so without shrinking the optimization steps.

**Experiments (Section 3).** Main setting: class-conditional SiT-XL/2 (~675M params) with REPA alignment, flow matching, ImageNet-1K at 256×256, global batch 128, trained up to 800k steps. Three compared recipes hold model/data/objective/batch/evaluation fixed and change only the optimization: (1) Constant-LR+EMA — AdamW at 1e-4 with EMA evaluation (the standard REPA recipe); (2) LR-Decay+EMA — independent 40k-step cosine-decay branches (1e-4 → 1e-6) from matched late-stage checkpoints of the baseline trajectory, isolating decay-start sensitivity; (3) Schedule-Free AdamW — base LR 4e-4 with 5k-step warmup (LRs are optimizer-specific, not numerically matched; Appendix A.4 controls for the LR scale). Evaluation operates at three levels: parameter variability (trace variance over 20 consecutive checkpoints; net ℓ2 displacement over 1k-step windows), optimization quality (REPA objective and fixed-evaluation losses on identical data/noise/conditions/timesteps), and generation quality (FID/sFID/IS with three sampling seeds). Transfer settings: ReFL reward fine-tuning on Z-Image-Turbo with HPSv3 as sole reward (100 updates, 48×12 trajectories, cross-checked on 9 orthogonal reward models) and one-step DMD (DMDR) with Schedule-Free applied to generator or guidance model separately (2× baseline LR, 200-step warmup).

## Results & Highlights

- **Schedule-Free vs the standard recipe, consistently:** mean FID-5K is lower at every checkpoint 320k–400k, with reductions of 0.0784–0.1635; at 400k, 9.0504 vs 9.2139 for Constant-LR+EMA, with higher Inception Score throughout (245.80 vs 242.30 at 400k). At 800k steps the gap widens on the full metric suite: FID-50K 1.8735 vs 2.0770, sFID 4.479 vs 4.539, IS 272.7 vs 265.6.
- **Decay is timing-sensitive and complementary to EMA:** Decay+EMA beats the matched Constant-LR+EMA endpoint at 4 of 5 branch starts but hurts at the 320k→360k branch (9.3163 vs 9.2700). Across all five branches, adding EMA to the decayed trajectory always helps — e.g., 360k→400k improves from 9.4895 (decayed online model) to 9.1248 (Decay+EMA).
- **EMA is doing heavy lifting:** removing EMA from the Constant-LR baseline degrades FID-5K by ~1.8–4.4 points across checkpoints (400k: 11.1509 without EMA vs 9.2139 with), confirming variance reduction matters in this regime.
- **Not just sampling quality — better optimization:** under a fixed-evaluation protocol (same data, noise, classes, timesteps), Schedule-Free has lower total loss throughout; the gain is mostly the REPA projection loss, and Schedule-Free achieves lower denoising MSE at all 8 individually tested timesteps plus under random-timestep evaluation.
- **The LR-scale control:** plain AdamW at the same 4e-4 base LR (5k warmup) diverges with sustained NaNs after ~218.7k steps, while Schedule-Free remains stable — so the gain is not merely "a bigger learning rate."
- **Stability-vs-progress trade-off quantified:** checkpoint trace variance (gap=1) is 1.18e-4 for Decay+EMA (lowest, 0.0016% of the Constant-LR online trajectory's 7.16), 1.44e-3 for Schedule-Free's averaged x (0.0201%), 5.55e-3 for Constant-LR EMA (0.0775%) — yet FID-5K at 400k ranks Schedule-Free best (9.0504) ahead of Decay+EMA (9.1248) and the baseline (9.2139). From 400k→405k, fixed-eval total loss keeps decreasing for Constant-LR+EMA (0.43738→0.43723) and Schedule-Free (0.43461→0.43447) but increases post-decay (0.43696→0.43760).
- **ReFL transfer:** HPSv3 on-reward score improves from 6.3500 (AdamW at 1e-5) to 6.8626 (Schedule-Free, 4×) and 9.6134 (20×) after 100 updates — but cross-reward evaluation is mixed, and at 20× the orthogonal UniPercept scores collapse (IAA 60.71→51.57, IQA 74.81→67.41), i.e., aggressive reward over-optimization degrades general quality.
- **DMD transfer:** generator-side Schedule-Free improves FID-5K at all three checkpoints (1k: 10.126→9.988; 2k: 9.757→9.573; 3k: 9.448→9.346) and lowers both LDMD and the fake-score diffusion loss; guidance-side Schedule-Free is worse than the all-AdamW baseline at all checkpoints (3k: 9.634).

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from Tsinghua University (Chuyu Hu, Huanran Chen, Yinpeng Dong) with Shitong Shao from HKUST and Chuyu Hu also affiliated with Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute. Yinpeng Dong's group has a strong track record in adversarial robustness and diffusion/generative-model research, which lends credibility to the experimental care on display here: controlled branch comparisons from a shared trajectory, a fixed-evaluation protocol that removes sampling noise from loss comparisons, an explicit learning-rate-scale control (Appendix A.4), and unusually honest reporting — they openly note that sFID changes are "small and not uniformly favorable," that cross-reward ReFL results are "mixed," and that their DMD distribution-evolution explanation is "consistent with, but does not establish" their hypothesis. The theoretical lemmas are mostly assembled from known results (Ghadimi & Lan 2013; Bottou et al. 2018; Chae & Kim 2026) rather than new theorems, and the paper is upfront about that (Lemma 2.4 is explicitly restated). A disclosed AI-use statement covers only language polishing. Code availability is referenced via shorthand pointers (SiT+REPA, DiffusionOPSD, DMDR setups), though no explicit repository link appears in the main text.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate, and honestly positioned.** The individual ingredients (Schedule-Free from Defazio et al. 2024, REPA, known SGD theory) are not new; the contribution is the unified variance-reduction framing (Lemma 2.2's EMA-bias counterexample is the most original theoretical piece) plus the first systematic controlled comparison in a modern DiT recipe. The paper frames itself as "is EMA special?" rather than overclaiming a new method — a refreshing stance.
- **Rigor: high within a narrow scope.** Same-trajectory decay branches, three-seed FID means, fixed-evaluation losses, timestep-wise MSE checks, and the AdamW@4e-4 NaN control are all well designed. But learning rates are deliberately not numerically matched (SF at 4e-4 vs AdamW at 1e-4) — the NaN control rules out "bigger LR with AdamW" but not an intermediate 2e-4 AdamW or a 2e-4 Schedule-Free, so the optimizer-vs-LR-scale attribution rests on two points per curve.
- **Scale and generalization are the main caveats.** Everything rests on one architecture (SiT-XL/2, ~675M), one dataset (class-conditional ImageNet-1K 256×256), and a reduced global batch of 128 (half the standard REPA recipe of 256, admitted as a compute constraint) — so absolute FIDs (FID-5K ≈ 9) are weaker than published REPA numbers, and the claim "large diffusion models" is extrapolated from a mid-scale class-conditional setting with no text-to-image or 1B+-scale validation. Only one training seed is used per recipe (seeds are for sampling only).
- **Missing ablations:** no full-trajectory decay schedules (e.g., WSD or cosine-from-start) are tested — decay is only probed as late 40k branches, which limits the "decay alone does not outperform EMA" conclusion to this protocol; no wall-clock/memory overhead accounting for Schedule-Free's extra averaged sequence; no EMA-timescale tuning for the SF runs' comparison partner.
- **Red flags (minor, mostly self-reported):** sFID improvements are small and non-uniform across checkpoints, so the win is not metric-universal; the ReFL 20× result (HPSv3 9.61 with UniPercept collapsing to 51.57/67.41) looks like textbook reward hacking — the authors disclose it but still headline the 9.61; DMD gains (~0.1 FID-5K) are modest and within the noise range typical of FID-5K.
- **Significance: real but incremental in impact.** If the Schedule-Free finding holds at true production scale, it changes a default recipe; at 675M/ImageNet it is a well-executed pointer in that direction rather than proof.

## Strengths

- Clean, falsifiable central question ("is EMA uniquely suited?") answered with a controlled three-way comparison in a genuinely modern recipe (SiT + REPA + flow matching), not a toy DDPM.
- The variance-reduction unification is pedagogically excellent — the quadratic closed forms make each strategy's trade-off intuitive, and Lemma 2.2 gives EMA's weakness a precise face.
- Multi-level evaluation (parameter variability, fixed-eval optimization progress, generation quality) that separates "stable weights" from "better model" — Figure 3's variability-vs-FID inversion is the paper's most instructive single result.
- The stability control (AdamW NaNs at 4e-4 where SF survives) directly addresses the most obvious confound.
- Transfer experiments (ReFL, DMD generator vs guidance) test the boundary of the claim rather than stopping at pretraining, including the instructive negative result on guidance-side SF.
- Unusually transparent reporting of unfavorable details (timing-sensitive decay, non-uniform sFID, mixed cross-reward results).

## Limitations

- Single model scale (~675M), single dataset (ImageNet-1K 256×256, class-conditional), single training seed per recipe; no text-to-image or billion-parameter validation, so "large diffusion models" in the title is aspirational relative to the evidence.
- Global batch 128 deviates from the standard REPA recipe (256), acknowledged as a compute constraint; conclusions may shift at the intended batch size.
- Learning rates are optimizer-specific (1e-4 vs 4e-4) with only the diverging AdamW@4e-4 control — no sweep over intermediate rates for either optimizer.
- LR decay is tested only as late 40k-step branches, not as full-training schedules, so the decay-vs-EMA comparison is protocol-dependent.
- Schedule-Free brings practical costs not analyzed here: extra optimizer state/sequence to maintain and checkpoint, and its own warmup/LR retuning burden in new regimes (the authors note other regimes "may require retuning").
- Most comparisons use FID-5K (noisier than FID-50K); FID-50K appears only at the 800k endpoint.
- ReFL gains at high LR come with clear degradation on orthogonal perceptual metrics, so the practical ReFL recipe needs a reward-overoptimization guardrail the paper does not provide.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击大规模扩散模型训练配方这一被长期沿用却少被系统审视的环节，与训练效率/优化方面的研究兴趣高度契合：它给出的可操作结论（不确定衰减点时用 Schedule-Free AdamW、预算固定时末期衰减叠加 EMA、警惕过度稳定化）可直接迁移到任何 Diffusion Transformer 训练流程中，其方差缩减分析框架对理解其他随机训练场景（如 LLM 预训练）也有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does Schedule-Free's advantage persist at production scale — 1B+ DiTs, text-to-image latent training, and batch sizes of 512–2048 where the REPA recipe's defaults were set?
- How does a full-trajectory WSD/cosine decay (with EMA) compare against Schedule-Free, rather than only late-stage 40k branches — is the timing sensitivity an artifact of the branch protocol?
- Can Schedule-Free and late-stage decay be composed (e.g., SF for most of training, brief decay + EMA as the finisher), and does that dominate either alone?
- Is there a principled way to set the Schedule-Free base LR without a sweep — e.g., via the observed stability margin it gains over AdamW at 4e-4?
- In ReFL, can a KL or perceptual regularizer recover the cross-reward collapse seen at 20× LR while keeping most of the on-reward gain?
- Does the EMA-bias analysis (Lemma 2.2) predict anything measurable about EMA checkpoints in LLM pretraining, where decay is standard and EMA is rare — the mirror image of the diffusion convention?
