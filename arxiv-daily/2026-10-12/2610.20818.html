Why This Matters

Tests whether the discrete multi-codebook route can truly carry unified understanding + generation — plus rare 1,024-NPU Ascend full-stack training evidence.

Critical Edge

Read the wins per-benchmark: they concentrate in document / chart / hallucination niches, while MMMU, MathVista and RealWorldQA trail 7B-class baselines — and most baseline numbers are transplanted from prior papers, not re-run.

Also: the “first multi-codebook dLLM” claim is scoped “to our knowledge” — the design composes AToken + LLaDA2.0 rather than proposing a new primitive.

  • Every task is one query type — captioning, VQA, T2I, editing, video/3D QA are different conditioning-vs-masked partitions of a single packed sequence, trained under one masked-prediction loss; masking a visual position replaces all K codes.
  • Data — InternVL3.5-30B-A3B recaptioning; capability-balanced instruction groups; Objaverse+Cap3D & GAPartNet (3D); OmniEdit / ImgEdit / SEED-Data-Edit (editing).
  • Training — primary run on 1,024 Ascend 910B NPUs (full-stack: operators, parallelism, eval); an NVIDIA reference run (≈200B tokens, same recipe) checks cross-platform transfer.
  • Why PHV — visual-token confidence stays far below text scale (90th pct < 0.32), so fixed-threshold early exit (Fast-dLLM-style) misfires; PHV commits at EMA-smoothed confidence plateaus, spreads remaining uncertainty by Halton ordering, and refreshes KV cache on hidden-state drift.
Results

Where it leads, where it trails

With only 1.4B active parameters: first among compared models on 6 of 10 image-understanding benchmarks, best on all three video benchmarks (Video-MME 56.7, MLVU 64.9N, MVBench 61.8) and on 3D MM-Vet.

Strengths

What genuinely holds up

  • One objective, one checkpoint covering image/video/3D understanding + generation + editing — a wider capability set than any single baseline in its tables.
  • Multi-codebook head is a clean answer to single-codebook capacity ceilings, with ablations (Tables 15–16) honestly testing when factorization is lossless.
  • 4.45× measured speedup from a decoder-aware sampling rule (PHV) — a practical contribution beyond architecture.
  • Rare systems evidence: full-stack 1,024-NPU Ascend training plus a cross-platform NVIDIA reference run.
Limitations

Where to stay skeptical

  • General knowledge / math reasoning / RealWorldQA remain below the best 7B-class unified baselines; video & 3D training data acknowledged as limited.
  • Editing is “close to best”, not leading; the OneIG style win comes with lower sample diversity.
  • Single checkpoint, no seed variance; TIIF reports long-prompt-only, GenEval uses a single seed (42, CFG 4.0).
  • Per-benchmark prompts / decoding configs deferred to the appendix, complicating exact reproduction.
Open Questions

Questions for further exploration

  1. How much of the CharXiv / HallusionBench edge comes from the multi-codebook interface vs the recaptioning data pipeline — no ablation separates them?
  2. Does the PHV plateau-commit rule transfer to other masked-diffusion decoders, or is it an artifact of AToken’s confidence calibration?
  3. Why does the NVIDIA reference run lag most on 3D (43.8 vs 50.5) — data order, tokenizer drift, or optimization differences?
  4. Can the per-position AR head’s conditional-independence approximation be closed without giving up parallel decoding?
  5. Where does K (codes per position) stop paying for its sequence-length cost?
