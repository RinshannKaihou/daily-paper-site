Why this matters

First parameter-level decomposition of a full LM delivers circuits, attribution graphs, and editable weights — a direct challenger to SAE / transcoder interpretability.

⚠ Critical edge

Under matched training objectives, the headline Pareto domination over transcoders disappears — only robustness to objective mismatch survives (§B.1).

  • Adversarial ablatability: any subset of causally-unimportant subcomponents must survive any combination of partial ablations — mechanistic faithfulness becomes trainable and measurable.
  • Frequency-minimality splits polysemantic "one subcomponent, two unrelated jobs" solutions that rank-1 alone permits.
  • Built on top: QK-circuit subcomponent pairs, stop-gradient attribution graphs, adversarial post-hoc pruning, single-subcomponent write-vector editing.

Versus transcoders
  • Pareto-dominates BatchTopK PLTs & CLTs with all 4 MLP layers replaced — under all three sparsity definitions (standard objectives).
  • No feature splitting: alive count flat at 6,500–7,000 across 0.5×→4× capacity, while PLT/CLT latents grow linearly (4k→32k).
  • Attention subcomponents span heads by construction — in layer 1, zero WQ/WK subcomponents live in a single head; three behaviors decomposed (previous-token, syntactic-boundary, "there is"/"it is").

03Strengths
  • First end-to-end parameter decomposition of a full LM, with honest, well-instrumented evaluation (CE ladder, compute-recovered, adversarial-KL curves, per-layer L₀).
  • Turns "mechanistic faithfulness" from slogan into a trainable, measurable property — and shows what goes wrong without it.
  • Principled, empirically clean treatment of feature splitting (flat alive-count across 4× capacity).
  • Cross-head attention decomposition is a genuine advance where activation-based methods struggle.
  • Exceptional transparency: own negative results flagged (matched-objective §B.1, 160-step robustness collapse, cherry-picked edit), code released.
04Limitations
  • Demonstrated only on a 67M, 4-layer Pile-subset model — no GPT-2-small-scale or larger checkpoint; compute scaling unquantified.
  • Adversarial robustness holds only against ~20 PGD steps with an admittedly primitive sampler.
  • Pareto-dominance over transcoders weakens to "no objective overfitting" once objectives are matched.
  • Interpretability & editing evidence rests on hand-picked prompts — no systematic behavioral benchmark (no IOI-style suite).
  • Gradient attributions capture only local, linearized direct effects; nonlinear interactions abstracted away.
  • The manual emoticon edit loses to a 947-example LoRA on both off-target KL metrics, on a task selected for clean subcomponents.
05Questions for further exploration
  1. How do cost and quality scale to GPT-2-small (124M), Pythia-410M, and beyond — and what is the adversarial inner loop's wall-clock overhead vs SPD?
  2. Can a stronger adversarial sampler (mask-space subspace search) push robustness past 160 PGD steps without hurting reconstruction?
  3. Do cross-head distributed attention subcomponents persist in models with many more heads, or do larger models re-localize?
  4. What would a systematic prompt battery (IOI / Greater-Of style) say about adversarially-pruned vs CI-masked graph faithfulness?
  5. Can write-vector editing be refined (avoiding intermediate-layer interference) to beat ~1,000-example LoRAs on both KL metrics?
  6. Do explicit-clustering parameter components (§A.8) refine the same circuits as cross-layer transcoders, or carve the model differently?
