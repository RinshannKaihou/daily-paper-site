No halting gate can be both sound and complete for self-improvement — evaluator hacking is structural, not statistical error.

The Self-Improvement Problem

“evaluator hacking 是结构性不可避免的，而非统计误差” — 任何会停机的门控都无法同时做到可靠（绝不接受非改进）与不保守（绝不漏掉真改进）。

A chain of accepted updates on n = 2k tasks: every round the proxy gains +1 while the truth loses −1 — no adversary, no self-reference.

[no benchmark metrics reported — proof-only paper; this is the sharpest quantity in it]

Why this matters

Sets exact design limits for self-improving agents and AI-Scientist pipelines: timeouts, proposal budgets, periodic re-evaluation, occasional human grounding.

Critical edge

Worst-case only — the distance from “impossible in the limit” to “fails for today’s agents” is unmeasured, and the paper runs no experiments at all.

Methodology

Computability theory applied to ML evaluation loops — the whole system as one machine.

ProposerE(p,i) — total computable enumerator
→
Candidateq — program transformation

GateV(p,q) ∈ {0,1} — must halt
→
Accept / Reject

↺ diagonal feedback: q is built from V’s own verdict — Kleene fixed point (Thm 1)

judged by J(q) > J(p): strict semantic improvement — J may be uncomputable

  • Agents are programs (indices p, behavior φ p); gates are any terminating procedure — reward models, test suites, automated reviewers idealized as total computable certifiers or searchers.
  • The diagonal: via s-m-n + Kleene’s recursion theorem, build a candidate that outputs 0 if accepted and 1 if rejected — “bad if accepted, good if rejected.” The gate becomes an oracle: evaluator hacking, formalized.
  • Escape sweeps: randomized gates derandomize through Kőnig’s lemma (Prop 1); hidden and stateful gates relocate but don’t remove bad inputs; multi-agent pipelines compile into one program (Appendix A).
  • Recovery (Thm 3): exhaustive simulation under computable bounds — time limit T, proposal budget B, generation bound T E — is sound and weakly complete for the bounded score J T.
  • Theorems 1–3 ship with a public Lean 4 formalization.

Results

No benchmarks — the findings are verdicts. Every escape route, adjudicated:

Escape routeVerdict
Deterministic certifierThm 1Impossible — sound ∧ complete cannot coexist, even for a 1-bit halting score.
Searcher reading the generator’s codeThm 2Still impossible — a result beyond Rice’s theorem.
Randomized gate, 2/3–1/3 errorProp 1Derandomizes via Kőnig’s lemma → collapses to Thm 1.
Hidden gateChanges who can attack, not that failure inputs exist.
Stateful gateDefeated state-by-state (Thm 1 at every reachable state).
Multi-agent pipelineApp. ACompiles to one program; diagonal survives via query access, verdict feedback, transcript leakage.
Bounded eval + finite budgetThm 3Recovered — but only relative to J T, and it drifts across rounds (Prop 2).
Proof-carrying updatesGödel-machine styleSound yet necessarily conservative — misses infinitely many genuine improvements.

Evidence is constructive proofs plus the Lean 4 formalization; the one escape left open is grounding decisions outside the loop (human oversight sits outside the model). Interactive evaluation inherits guarantees only under finite actions, rational computable dynamics, and per-step compute caps (Prop 3).

Strengths

What the paper firmly establishes

  • Folklore, now a theorem — exact assumptions for when joint soundness + completeness must fail, and what survives.
  • Thm 2 beats Rice — even search, holding the generator’s source code, cannot be sound and weakly complete.
  • Prop 2 isolates a second, orthogonal failure — proxy drift with no adversary — and yields a usable rule: fixed per-round budget + periodic re-evaluation with a larger one.
  • Lean 4 formalization of Thms 1–3, with unusually candid “what is new vs. classical” scoping.

Limitations

Where the guarantee runs out

  • Worst-case only — the proposer compute needed for the diagonal is unbounded and unmeasured.
  • Thm 3 is relative to J T and does not compose across rounds; drift growth under adaptive proposers is open.
  • No experiments — ties to reward models, SWE-bench suites, and AI-Scientist reviewers are interpretive, not measured.
  • Deterministic, extensional model — stochastic LLM agents fit only through Prop 3’s strong finiteness assumptions.

Questions for further exploration

Open threads the paper itself points at

  • How fast can proxy drift grow once the proposer adapts to the re-evaluation schedule — is there a tight bound in the budget growth rate?
  • Can finite-context LLM proposers empirically learn “flip-on-accept” behavior without explicit self-reference — and at what compute cost?
  • Which intermediate restrictions (stronger gates, decidable update languages, non-conditioning proposers) restore which single guarantee — and can proof-carrying conservatism be quantified?
  • Can human-in-the-loop oversight be modeled with quantified overhead, turning the escape hatch into a design space rather than an exception?
  • Do deployed automated-research pipelines already drift this way — and can “idle-then-correct just past the budget” serve as a drift signature?
