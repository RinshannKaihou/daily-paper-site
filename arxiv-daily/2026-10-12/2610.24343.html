Mosaic NVFP4 stacks the best components of prior FP4 recipes to cut the BF16 train-loss gap below 0.81%.

What Matters for NVFP4 Training? A Comparative Study of Low-Precision Pretraining Recipes ↗

Anjulie Agrusa, Andrei Panferov, Elizabeth Wei, Keith Wyss, et al. (10 authors · NVIDIA × IST Austria × Red Hat AI)

low-precision training NVFP4 quantization LLM pretraining MoE ICML '26 workshop (AdaptFM)

Authors include the teams behind Quartet II/MS-EDEN and Four Over Six themselves — deep expertise, mild evaluator conflict of interest.

Key metric · relative train-loss gap to BF16

<0.81%

Nemotron 3 Nano 30B-A3B MoE · 1T tokens — a 0.36 pp improvement over the NVIDIA '25 recipe, and the smallest FP4-to-BF16 loss gap reported at this scale. The 8B dense model lands at <1.2% (+0.24 pp).

Why this matters

Tells practitioners which FP4 training components actually matter at real scale — and hands them a reusable recipe with the smallest known BF16 loss gap.

系统回答了真实规模（8B 稠密 + 30B-A3B MoE、1T token）下 NVFP4 配方中哪些组件真正必要，并给出当前与 BF16 loss 差距最小的可复用配方。

Critical edge

The efficiency story is missing its punchline: FP4's entire motivation is throughput and energy, yet the paper reports no wall-clock, throughput, or energy numbers for Mosaic — only "compatible with efficient kernels."

Methodology · one stack, four stacked updates

All NVFP4 variants run in a common training stack on Blackwell FP4 tensor cores, on two hybrid Mamba-Transformer backbones trained to 1T tokens: dense Nemotron-H 8B, and MoE Nemotron 3 Nano 30B-A3B (128 routed + 2 shared experts, 3.2B active). Run-to-run noise is calibrated from three repeats (relative loss range 0.027–0.064%).

  1. NVIDIA '25 recipe — the cumulative-stacking baseline (2D weight quant, stochastic rounding)
  2. 1D weight quantization with backward re-quantization · TetraJet line
  3. Dim-128 tiled Random Hadamard Transforms on both backward GEMMs, sign vectors resampled throughout training
  4. MS-EDEN unbiased gradient estimation + Four Over Six adaptive scales on W.T / X.T · Quartet II / Cook et al.
  5. Four Over Six extended to forward tensors W and X
  6. Mosaic NVFP4 — last 15% of layers kept in BF16

Quantization spans the three GEMMs — Y=X·W (fwd), DGRAD=G·W.T, WGRAD=G.T·X.T — each tensor's treatment specified independently.

  • Largest single stacking gain: replacing stochastic rounding with MS-EDEN + Four Over Six on backward tensors — unbiasedness at lower quantization-error cost.
  • Full quantization diverges past 300B tokens on the 8B model; the last 15% of layers in BF16 is non-negotiable at this scale.
  • Rankings invert before ~150B tokens — weak recipes can look better early; short ablations can't predict long-horizon accuracy.

Strengths

  • First same-stack, same-hardware comparison of the NVFP4 recipe wave at real scale (8B dense + 30B-A3B MoE, 1T tokens).
  • Every component's contribution measured against BF16; final recipe is concrete and adoptable.
  • Two genuinely useful negative results: full quantization still diverges at scale; de-biasing belongs on gradients, not all backward tensors.
  • The early-training ranking inversion is a widely applicable methodological warning for low-precision ablations.
  • Clean hygiene: three-run noise calibration, train/val consistency, headline numbers shown to be conservative.

Limitations

  • No throughput, wall-clock, or energy measurements — FP4's core motivation is never quantified, and MS-EDEN/Four Over Six overhead is unreported.
  • Downstream evaluation is thin; most deltas inside noise, and the noise reference rests on only three runs.
  • Only NVIDIA '25 run end-to-end to 1T tokens; concurrent recipes (TetraJet-v2, Quartet II) never get full-horizon runs.
  • Findings tied to hybrid Mamba-Transformer backbones at ≤30B params / ≤1T tokens; pure-Transformer and post-training transfer unverified.
  • Cumulative stacking, not factorial — interactions underexplored; Mosaic itself has no seed repeats. 8B MMLU-Pro −7.1% is a nontrivial regression.

Questions for further exploration

  1. What is the actual end-to-end throughput and energy cost of Mosaic vs BF16 and vs NVIDIA '25 on Blackwell — does MS-EDEN's extra rotation/rescaling eat the FP4 speedup?
  2. Does the "last 15% of layers in BF16" requirement transfer to pure Transformers, and can per-layer sensitivity scoring find a smaller high-precision set?
  3. Why do recipe rankings invert before ~150B tokens — is there a predictable early signature (gradient-noise scale, outlier channels)?
  4. Does pretraining in FP4 change the inference-quantization robustness of the final weights?
