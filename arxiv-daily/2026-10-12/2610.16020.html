Theory · Post-Training Quantization · arXiv 2610.16020 · Lean-formalized

Compensating inherited quantization error is provably safe for linear networks — and provably unsafe for ReLU nets and bounded codebooks.

A two-scalar-layer counterexample refutes QEP's published universal guarantee (Theorem B.5) outright: output error ε + ε² vs ε² for all 0 < ε < ½ — a first-order failure the original proof convention cannot absorb.

Why This Matters

Sets the safety boundary for the whole GPTQ / QEP / Qronos family of layer-wise compensation — and shows calibration-data gains can reverse on new text.

Directly informs strength selection and calibration protocol design in quantization pipelines.

Critical Edge

The theory–practice gap is fundamental: every theorem holds the calibration input fixed, while the largest observed losses are on held-out inputs — exactly the regime the theory says nothing about.

Credibility

Single author (Tianhao Guo, Shanxi University) — but claims are verifiable, not trust-based: Lean 4 + Mathlib formalization of the core counterexamples, two independent numerical implementations, pre-registered protocols, released code & rational-arithmetic witnesses, disclosed AI assistance, conservative tone ("we propose no new quantizer").

Methodology

A ladder of analysis, rung by rung

Two schemes compared layer by layer: BASE (uncorrected RTN, Ŵ = W + E) vs. QEP-style compensation Ŵ*(α) = W + α·WδX̂ᵀĤ⁻¹, strength α ∈ [0,1] fixed in advance; δ = inherited input error, Ĥ = calibration Gram matrix.
  1. 1

    One layer, one input §3 · Prop 1

    Exact identity ∥R_α∥² − ∥R₀∥² = −α(2−α)d + 2αc: compensation helps iff 2c ≤ (2−α)d — the new rounding must not nearly cancel the inherited error. Subtractive dither on an unbounded grid supplies the needed conditions.

  2. 2

    Linear nets, any depth §4 · Thm 8

    Propagate error covariance + activation second moment along each scheme's own trajectory; supermartingale-style induction ⇒ compensation never worse. The exact gain splits into direct error removal plus reduced excitation of later rounding noise — invisible to any per-layer view.

  3. 3

    ReLU networks §5 · Thm 15

    Two independent obstructions: noise flips activation states; projection P fails to commute with activation mask D. Excess error bounded by commutator norms under stable activations.

  4. 4

    Shared perturbations §6 · Thm 17/19

    Two scalar layers with E = (ε, −ε) refute QEP's Theorem B.5 and strength monotonicity; the exact proof gaps (invalid triangle-inequality step, trajectory substitution) are isolated.

  5. 5

    Real codebooks §7 · Thm 22

    A 3-layer scalar ReLU chain with codebook {0,1,2,3}: output domination fails even with unique exact minimizers, interior targets, no overload.

  6. 6

    Verification §8–10

    Double Monte Carlo implementations, pre-registered layer-wise diagnostics on 135M/160M models, 8B strength-selection experiments, and a Lean 4 + Mathlib formalization of the core results.

Strengths

What holds up

  • Settles a previously open question with matching positive and negative results, plus one legible criterion — compensation helps iff the new perturbation doesn't cancel the inherited error.
  • The refutation is surgical: constructive, minimal, Lean-formalized — and fair, preserving QEP's still-valid bounds while invalidating only the false comparisons.
  • Rare methodological hygiene: pre-registered protocols, two independent implementations, rational-arithmetic witnesses, ten deliberately-false Lean controls.
  • Practically valuable negative result: network-loss strength selection overfits; fixed α = ½ is a robust, essentially cost-free default; a {½, 1} grid keeps 98.2–99.6% of a ten-point grid's gain.
  • Complete scoping: every hypothesis and failure mode stated before use — nothing overclaimed.
Limitations

Where it stops short

  • No generalization theory: all guarantees fix the calibration input — while held-out inputs are exactly where the biggest losses appear.
  • Idealized positive theorem: linear nets + subtractive dither on an unbounded grid — a quantizer no deployed system uses; dither's decoder-side and accuracy costs were never measured.
  • Conditional ReLU bound: stable activations not established in trained networks; uniform-dither case left open.
  • Experiments outside the theory: ridge-regularized corrections aren't covered by any theorem; 8B study is small (2 models, 2–3 draws, 2 possibly-seen texts, no speed numbers).
  • Tiny counterexamples: 2–3 layers, scalar or 2×2; no search for failures in large structured networks; c/d never measured on the 8B models.
Open Questions

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does 2c ≤ (2−α)d hold along each scheme's own trajectory in 8B-class models at 2–3 bits, where the published QEP regressions actually occur?
  2. Can Theorem 15 tolerate a small probability of activation flips — and can stable activations be certified from teacher pre-activation margins?
  3. What actually causes the held-out degradation — fitting-data size, selection size, ridge shrinkage, or cross-layer interaction — and would block-level calibration shrink the overestimate?
  4. Does dithered rounding with fixed feedback (GPTQ-style) yield an end-to-end guarantee on a trained model, and at what realized perplexity cost?
  5. Since 9–18% of corrected weights leave the original row range, would combining compensation with learned clipping / AWQ-style scale search eliminate the overload failure mode?
  6. Is the closed-form 10·log₁₀ L dB depth gain observable in deep real networks, or do bottlenecks and row-space mismatch dominate?
