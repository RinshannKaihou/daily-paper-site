> **一句话总结：** UCoE 提出免训练的"验证器-框架共生演化"方法：把验证器表示成可执行的打分程序（多准则打分器 + 聚合器 + 诊断器），在"响应对比对"（只破坏单一属性的 base/edited 响应对）上按**最弱对比族上的判别力（driving capacity，最大化最小值准则）**选程序；部署时它诊断草稿的单一弱点、引导一次局部修复、决定返回哪个版本、并门控哪些修复可以写入 harness 经验，任务流检查点上再用已完成任务的诊断播种新候选、只有在不回退任何族的前提下才替换当前验证器——在 6 个开放式基准上以 Qwen3-8B/Qwen3.8-27B 平均拿到 59.00%/57.19% 的胜率（比直接生成高 9.00/7.19 个百分点），3 个可验证基准均值从 36.06% 升至 38.48%。

> **TL;DR:** Training-free self-improvement on open-ended tasks needs a model-based verifier, but a wrong verifier makes the served response worse AND persists misleading experience, while a frozen verifier goes stale. UCoE (Oregon State + Penn State + AG2 AI) evolves an executable verifier program (criterion scorers Φ + aggregator A + diagnosis D) alongside the agent's harness state with all model parameters fixed: candidate programs are ranked by "driving capacity" — discrimination on their WEAKEST family of response contrasts (base vs single-property-degraded responses, a maximin objective with a covariance/worst-case-mixture justification) — then the selected verifier diagnoses one weakness per draft, guides one repair, gates which repairs enter procedural memory, and is itself re-tested and replaced at stream checkpoints only under strict no-regression update criteria. Against matched direct generation (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B judge, tie-adjusted, 50 = parity), UCoE averages 59.00% with Qwen3-8B vs ~49-51% for the best baseline per benchmark (+9.00 pts over parity; best baseline avg only 49.26%), and on three verifiable benchmarks (IFBench/Hard-MATH/BigCodeBench-Hard) raises the mean from 36.06% to 38.48% while being the only method that preserves Pass@1 on BigCodeBench-Hard (21.62%, where every other feedback baseline drops below direct generation). Ablations on Arena-Hard attribute most of the gain to current-task repair (58.30% → 50.80% without it); the method also transfers to GPT-5.4 actors and a Codex agent harness on SpreadsheetBench-2 (22.7% vs 17.3% exact match), at 2.2-2.8× the listed-price cost of simpler feedback baselines ($0.0142/task).

## 核心贡献

- **首个把验证器本身纳入演化的免训练自改进框架**：在模型参数完全冻结的前提下，让可执行验证器程序（准则打分器集合 Φ、聚合函数 A、诊断过程 D）与 agent 的 harness 状态（有界过程记忆）共同演化——此前免训练方法（Self-Refine、Reflexion、ACE 等）都把反馈/选择机制视为固定不变的。
- **提出 driving capacity（驱动容量）作为验证器选择准则**：候选验证器按其在**最弱对比族**上的判别力排名（maximin），而非平均准确率——强属性上的高分无法掩盖盲区；理论上给出协方差解释（Price 定理）与最坏混合分布下的鲁棒性保证（Theorem 1：最不利混合把全部质量压在最弱族上），并用 Prop 2 把选择收益分解为"机会 q × 能力 J_mix"。
- **响应对比（response contrasts）作为验证器的行为测试床**：从校准响应出发，一次只破坏一个属性（遗漏必需内容、无依据断言、无关材料三类通用族 + 关系挖掘得到的基准特定族），构造已知偏好方向的 (y+, y−) 对，使所有候选验证器在同一受控基准上可比。
- **保守的验证器更新准则**：检查点上新候选必须（1）在选择划分上严格提升最弱族准确率、（2）在与当前验证器的 held-out 对比中赢多输少、（3）在所有已有族上不超出回退容差，否则保留当前验证器——避免了演化过程中的性能回退。
- **九个基准上的实证 + 机理解剖**：6 个开放式基准全部 12 个设置第一；3 个可验证基准均值提升且不损害任何单项；消融、成本分析、跨骨干迁移（GPT-5.4）、Codex agent harness 内的真实任务流验证齐备；代码开源。

## Key Contributions

- First training-free self-improvement framework that adapts the **feedback process itself**: an executable verifier program V = (Φ, A, D) — criterion scorers, reward aggregator, and a diagnosis procedure that turns the weakest participating criterion into repair feedback — co-evolves with the agent's harness state while all model parameters stay fixed. Prior training-free work (Self-Refine, Reflexion, ExpeL, ACE, Recursive Harness Self-Improvement) takes the feedback/selection mechanism as given.
- **Driving capacity**: a verifier's discrimination on its weakest contrast family (one quarter of the minimum centered accuracy J_j = 2p_j − 1). Selecting by this maximin objective prevents strength on easy properties from masking a blind spot. The paper gives it a covariance interpretation (selection covariance of the Price equation flavor) and proves that maximizing signed driving capacity maximizes the worst-case selection probability over every mixture of the tested families (Theorem 1), plus a decomposition showing selection gain = opportunity q (how often the pair truly differs in adequacy) × selector ability J_mix (Prop 2).
- **Response contrasts** as a common behavioral test for verifiers: calibration responses are edited one property at a time to form (y+, y−) pairs with a declared preference, covering three general families (omitted required content, unsupported claims, irrelevant material) plus benchmark-specific families mined by blinded relation discovery (a relation is kept only if it recurs in ≥ 3 development prompts and is expressible as one binary criterion with one edit).
- **Conservative co-evolution rules**: at scheduled checkpoints, new candidates are synthesized from the current verifier plus unlabeled diagnostics of completed tasks (acceptance rates, repeatedly weakest criteria, harness growth); a replacement must strictly improve worst-family accuracy on the selection split, win more held-out contrasts than it loses, and stay within a fixed regression tolerance on every existing family.
- **Comprehensive evidence**: state-of-the-art among compared feedback methods on all 12 open-ended settings (6 benchmarks × 2 actor sizes), +2.42 points mean on three verifiable benchmarks with no per-benchmark loss, full ablations, cost analysis, cross-backbone transfer (Qwen3-14B, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.4-mini), a Codex agent-harness study, mechanism diagnostics, and released code (github.com/tianyi0216/Unified-Co-Evolution).

## Methodology

UCoE keeps a fixed actor F_θ0 processing a task stream x1..xT. Every method follows the same per-task lifecycle: draft y0 = F(x; H_t), at most one verifier-guided revision yR, one of the two is served, and only then is the harness state updated H_{t+1} — so feedback affects the current response while task-derived state affects only later tasks. Scoring is prequential (Eq. 1): each response is produced from state inherited from earlier tasks, never from final-evaluator feedback.

**Verifier as a program.** V = (Φ, A, D): Φ maps (task, response) to a vector of criterion scores, A aggregates them into a scalar reward, and D converts the weakest participating criterion into a repair instruction (target criterion, localized evidence, instruction). Semantic criteria are scored by a fixed verifier model (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B), which also proposes and refines programs; the interpreter and the verifier model never change — only Φ and A evolve.

**Relation discovery and synthesis.** On a disjoint development set, the actor and a larger frozen comparison model answer the same prompts; the verifier model reads each pair blinded and in random order, and recurring differences become general criteria plus targeted edits later reused for contrast construction. Candidates may add up to two criteria of their own; malformed programs are filtered by a lightweight validity check.

**Contrast construction and driving capacity.** Each contrast starts from a calibration response and applies one targeted edit that breaks exactly one property. A verifier induces a strict-margin selector h_V (y+ must beat y− by more than ε). Family accuracy is J_j = 2p_j − 1 (0 = chance); signed driving capacity is (1/4)·min_j J_j — the minimum selection covariance over families — and Theorem 1 shows the least-favorable family mixture makes success probability 1/2 + 2·C, so maximizing the signed value maximizes worst-case selection. Contrasts are split into a selection split (ranking candidates) and a held-out split (challenger vs incumbent).

**Revision, selection, and harness gating.** The deployed verifier diagnoses the draft (c_t, w_t, u_t), the actor performs one local repair preserving unrelated content, and the revision is served only if its reward exceeds the draft's by margin ε. Only accepted repairs may write into the harness state — in the main experiments a bounded procedural memory yielding at most one generalized trigger-action procedure per accepted revision (with retrieval, deduplication, and eviction rules in App. D.2).

**Verifier evolution.** At checkpoints, candidates synthesized from completed-task diagnostics re-enter maximin selection with the current verifier in the pool; adoption requires the strict update criteria above and takes effect only on the next task. App. C.3 formalizes the criteria; Prop 3 bounds how far the empirical maximizer can fall short of the best in-pool candidate.

**Experimental setup.** Six open-ended benchmarks: Arena-Hard, WildBench, WritingPrompts, WritingBench, MultiChallenge, RefineBench (Free-Form split); three verifiable: IFBench, Hard-MATH (drawn from MATH-500 + AIME 2022-2025), BigCodeBench-Hard. Actors Qwen3-8B and Qwen3.8-27B; verifier model Qwen3.6-35B-A3B; final judge Qwen3.5-397B-A17B scoring both presentation orders with ties given half credit (50 = parity). Baselines — Direct Generation, LLM as a Judge, RaR (flat rubrics), DREAM (Reward-DAG), SpyRL (self-verifiable task transformation) — are all adapted to the same fixed-model, one-revision protocol with equal-capacity procedural memories.

## Results & Highlights

- **Open-ended preference (primary endpoint, Table 1):** UCoE averages 59.00% tie-adjusted preference vs matched direct generation with Qwen3-8B (+9.00 pts over the 50% parity line) and 57.19% with Qwen3.8-27B (+7.19 pts), winning all 12 benchmark×actor settings; the best baseline on any given benchmark averages only 49.26% (8B) and 51.60% (27B), and no baseline exceeds parity on WildBench at 8B (best: LLM-as-Judge at 46.34%, i.e. below parity). Per-benchmark at 8B: Arena-Hard 58.30 vs 51.20 (RaR), WritingPrompts 62.79 vs 50.54 (RaR), WildBench 57.42 vs 46.34, WritingBench 56.90 vs 52.60 (DREAM), MultiChallenge 57.42 vs 53.78 (SpyRL), RefineBench 61.15 vs 51.26 (RaR).
- **Verifiable tasks (Table 2, Qwen3-8B):** the three-benchmark mean rises from 36.06% (direct generation) to 38.48% (+2.42 pts): IFBench 30.00 → 34.67 (+4.67), Hard-MATH 56.54 → 59.16 (+2.62, though DREAM edges UCoE at 59.42), and BigCodeBench-Hard Pass@1 preserved exactly at 21.62 — UCoE is the only feedback method that does not drop below direct generation on code (every other baseline falls to 16.89-18.92).
- **Ablations (Arena-Hard, Qwen3-8B, Table 3):** removing current-task repair (verifier only updates harness state) costs the most, 58.30% → 50.80% (−7.50 pts, back to parity); random initial verifier 56.70%, fixed (never-updated) verifier 56.60%, no memory updates 56.70% — so verifier-guided repair dominates but each evolutionary component adds 1.6-1.7 points on top.
- **Native benchmark metrics (Table 8):** UCoE has the highest overall scaled mean at both sizes — 44.73 vs 43.42 for the next method at 8B (+1.31) and 69.87 vs 68.58 at 27B — with the largest gains on Arena-Hard style-adjusted score: 7.0 → 10.2 (8B) and 51.6 → 60.9 (27B); however the native metrics do not give a uniform lead (rubric baselines score slightly higher on WritingBench Critic; LLM-as-Judge leads MultiChallenge at 8B; SpyRL edges RefineBench at 27B by 0.01).
- **Agent-harness transfer (Fig. 3):** inside a Codex coding-agent harness on SpreadsheetBench-2 (75-task stream, GPT-5.6-luna actor, GPT-5.6-sol verifier model, checkpoints after tasks 34 and 67), UCoE reaches 22.7% exact-match accuracy vs 17.3% for both Direct Generation and LLM-as-Judge (+5.4 pts), with gains concentrated in financial-model and debugging categories.
- **Cross-backbone transfer (Table 9):** with Qwen3-14B, GPT-5.4, and GPT-5.4-mini actors, UCoE is the most preferred method on Arena-Hard for all three (56.70 / 59.10 / 59.60) and leads WildBench and RefineBench for GPT-5.4-mini (62.26 / 55.22), while every baseline falls below parity with the Qwen3-14B actor; MultiChallenge stays near parity for GPT actors (51.17 / 50.98) and is the one setting where other methods match or beat UCoE.
- **Mechanism diagnostics (Fig. 4):** repair targets adapt to task type (Correctness gets 78.9% of Arena-Hard repairs, Requirements 42.5% on WritingBench, Coherence 36.5% on RefineBench, Emotion 53.0% on WritingPrompts); two verifier updates in WritingPrompts raised signed driving capacity from −0.0625 and −0.0208 to 0.25 and lifted selection gain on frozen natural pairs by 10.16 and 4.69 points; of 94 Arena-Hard proposals accepted by the updated verifier, external judgments favor 73 over the draft vs 7 worse and 14 ties; and of 639 procedure retrievals, 76 reuse a procedure after ≥ 10 intervening tasks, with served revisions beating drafts 54-5 (11 ties) on the 70 externally judged retrieval tasks.
- **Cost (Fig. 5):** UCoE averages $0.0142 per served task at list prices vs $0.0050-0.0063 for the simpler baselines (2.2-2.8× more expensive); SpyRL at $0.0160 costs more than UCoE on five of six benchmarks while being less preferred on all six; most of UCoE's cost is the verifier model.
- **Significance:** with pointwise 95% bootstrap intervals, UCoE's interval lies above parity in 11 of 12 open-ended settings (MultiChallenge at 27B touches parity at 49.80), whereas baseline intervals include parity in most settings.

## Authors & Credibility

Nine authors from Oregon State University (Tianyi Xu, Joe Nguyen, Huazheng Wang), Pennsylvania State University (Yaolun Zhang, Zhenwen Shao, Qingyun Wu), Johnson & Johnson (Shengyu Dai, Saba Moeinizade), and AG2 AI (Yiran Wu, Qingyun Wu, Huazheng Wang); the first three contribute equally, with correspondence to Xu and Wang. The group has a credible track record in LLM agents and test-time adaptation (Qingyun Wu and Huazheng Wang are behind the AutoGen/AG2 line of agentic frameworks), which fits the agent-harness framing. The paper reads as a careful systems+theory contribution: the reproducibility statement points to a full algorithm (Alg. 1), proofs (App. C), hyperparameters and splits (App. D), and code is public at github.com/tianyi0216/Unified-Co-Evolution. The evaluation protocol is unusually disciplined — prequential scoring, matched one-revision budgets for all baselines, both presentation orders for the judge, McNemar tests with Holm correction, a cost analysis, and an independent-critic audit of WritingBench revisions with Critic-7B. One caveat on provenance: two of the compared methods (DREAM/DREAM-RL and CARE) are cited as "Anonymous, under review", so some baselines are contemporaneous unpublished work rather than established reference points.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuine and well-scoped.** The core move — evolving the verifier program together with the harness state, selected by worst-family (maximin) discrimination on constructed contrasts — is distinct from rubric-evolution RL training (DR-Tulu, EvoRubric) and from Reward-DAG evolution (DREAM-RL), and the driving-capacity objective with its covariance/worst-case-mixture justification is a real theoretical contribution, not decoration. That said, the ingredients (contrast sets à la Gardner et al. 2020, checklist perturbations, executable reward code à la Eureka) are recombinations; the novelty is the assembly and the selection criterion.
- **The headline metric is author-chosen.** The +9.00/+7.19-point margins are on tie-adjusted preference against *matched direct generation* judged by a single judge (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B) — from the same model family as the actors. Under the benchmarks' own native metrics the gains shrink to +1.31 points of overall scaled mean at 8B (43.42 → 44.73) and are not uniform (LLM-as-Judge leads MultiChallenge at 8B; SpyRL edges RefineBench at 27B by 0.01). The Critic-7B audit and the verifiable-task results mitigate the single-judge concern, but a same-family judge preferring the outputs of same-family actors remains a plausible bias channel.
- **Baselines are deliberately weakened.** All feedback baselines were "adapted to the same fixed-model, one-revision protocol … without reproducing the original parameter-training pipelines." RaR (Rubrics-as-Rewards) is an RL training method; stripping its training loop and giving it one revision and a small procedural memory tests a strawman version of it. The comparison is fair for the *training-free* claim, but the paper cannot (and does not claim to) show superiority over training-based rubric-reward pipelines, and DREAM edges UCoE on Hard-MATH (59.42 vs 59.16), hinting the gap is not universal.
- **Contrast validity is assumed, not established.** The declared preference of each (y+, y−) pair rests on the assumption that the single edit breaks exactly one property — the paper itself concedes (App. A) that passing the contrasts "does not guarantee reliable discrimination on natural revisions, on interacting errors, or on task properties that no family covers." Real failure modes interact; a verifier optimized on isolated single-property edits can be systematically blind to compound errors, and Prop 2's opportunity term q is unmeasurable at deployment.
- **Coupled-risk design.** The same program diagnoses, selects the served response, AND gates what enters long-term memory — the paper honestly flags that one mistaken judgment can both serve a worse response and store a misleading procedure that persists across the stream (the ethics statement repeats this). The no-regression update criteria bound verifier drift but not the harness-state contamination path; the Fig. 4(c) audit (73/94 accepted proposals judged better) is reassuring but post hoc and only on 94 Arena-Hard proposals.
- **Rigor and reproducibility are above average** — prequential protocol, both-order judging, bootstrap intervals, McNemar+Holm for native metrics, cost accounting, sensitivity study, and code release. **Significance** is real but bounded: on verifiable tasks the mean gain is +2.42 points with BigCodeBench-Hard merely *matched* (not improved) and Hard-MATH lost to DREAM; the strongest, most consistent gains are on model-judged open-ended tasks where the verifier was effectively co-designed with the evaluation regime. The 2.2-2.8× listed-price cost premium is disclosed but nontrivial.

## Strengths

- Identifies and attacks a real gap: training-free self-improvement methods fix the verifier while everything around it adapts — UCoE closes that loop with a coherent, safe-by-construction update rule.
- The driving-capacity maximin objective is principled (covariance meaning + worst-case-mixture optimality) and directly answers the "aggregate scores hide blind spots" failure mode of rubric methods.
- Evaluation breadth is excellent: 9 benchmarks, 2 main actors plus 3 transfer actors including closed GPT models, a real Codex agent harness with a different harness-state instantiation, native metrics alongside preference, statistical intervals, cost analysis, and a mechanism study that actually explains WHERE gains come from (repair targets shifting by benchmark; procedure reuse after ≥ 10-task lags winning 54-5).
- Conservative evolution design: candidates must beat the incumbent on the same contrasts with no per-family regression, and any failed comparison retains the incumbent — drift is structurally resisted rather than hoped away.
- Code, full algorithm, proofs, and hyperparameters are all released; the paper reports its own weak spots (MultiChallenge, cost, DREAM's Hard-MATH edge) instead of hiding them.

## Limitations

- Contrast families cover only declared, single-property local edits; discrimination on natural revisions, interacting errors, or uncovered properties is not guaranteed (acknowledged in App. A) — the central validity assumption is unverifiable at deployment.
- The verifier concentrates three coupled powers (diagnosis, response selection, memory gating), so a single wrong judgment can both degrade the current response and poison persistent experience; there are no independent checks or rollback rules in the deployed system.
- Open-ended evaluation rests on one model judge from the same family as the actors; native-metric gains are much smaller than preference gains, and MultiChallenge is a consistent weak spot (near parity for GPT actors; LLM-as-Judge leads at 8B).
- 2.2-2.8× the listed-price cost of simpler feedback baselines ($0.0142/task, mostly verifier-model scoring), limiting practicality at scale.
- The protocol allows only one revision per task, so nothing is known about behavior under deeper revision budgets or longer streams; the verifiable-task gains are modest (+2.42 mean) with code execution merely preserved, not improved.
- Baselines are reimplemented under a training-free, one-revision protocol, so results do not speak to the original training-based pipelines of RaR or rubric-evolution methods.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of UCoE's preference gain survives under human evaluation or judges from a different model family than the actors, given that the single judge (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B) shares a family with the Qwen actors and the native-metric gains are ~7× smaller than the preference margins?
- Can contrast construction be extended to interacting/compound edits (two properties broken jointly) so that driving capacity measures discrimination on the failure modes real drafts actually exhibit, rather than isolated single-property degradations?
- Since Prop 2 decomposes selection gain into opportunity q × ability J_mix, can q be estimated online from the actor's own draft-revision pairs to predict — before deploying a verifier — whether a task stream offers enough mixed-adequacy pairs to pay for the 2.2-2.8× cost premium?
- Does the coupled-risk failure (one bad judgment poisoning procedural memory) occur measurably in adversarial or distribution-shifted streams, and would decoupling the memory gate from the response selector (e.g., an independent acceptance check) remove it at acceptable cost?
- How do gains scale with revision budget (>1 repair per task), longer task streams (more checkpoints), and stronger verifier models — and is the MultiChallenge/multi-turn weakness a fundamental limit of single-property contrast testing on conversational tasks?
