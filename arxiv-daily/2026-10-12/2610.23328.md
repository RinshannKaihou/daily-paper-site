> **一句话总结：** 本文证明把优化后的算子顺序交回 PyTorch Inductor 重新生成代码是不可靠的（CPU 上 54 个配置中 22 个偏离预测峰值、9 个反而更差），转而直接对已生成的程序逐语句剖析并建立精确内存模型、只置换语句顺序，在 CPU 上逐字节精确实现预测峰值且输出逐位一致；配套的"陷阱"下界证书以 O(n²) 次最小割证明 94% 的 held-out 图及全部 16 个微控制器级模型已达最优调度，并给出选择性重计算的复杂度边界（任意固定 cap 下强 NP 完全），在 ReDimNet 上以 ≤0.34% 额外 MACs 换取 5.2–6.1% 峰值内存下降。

> **TL;DR:** For real-time inference on small devices, operator order is the only memory lever that is free in time — but deploying a better order by handing it back to PyTorch Inductor and regenerating code is unreliable: across 54 CPU configurations the regenerated program misses its predicted peak in 22 and is worse than stock in 9 (once by 30.6% on a T5 encoder), and on GPU it is worse in 19 of 50 configs and silently changes gradients. The authors instead profile the generated program statement-by-statement, build an exact "root model" of its memory behaviour, and permute the program's own statements: on CPU this realizes the predicted peak to the byte with bitwise-identical outputs/gradients in all 78 training and 22 inference runs. New forward/reverse "trap" lower bounds (each O(n²) min-cuts, both dominating the classical event-cut bound) certify the found order optimal for 115/122 (94%) held-out wrapper graphs and all 16 microcontroller-class models in under a second — revealing that most inference graphs are already optimally scheduled (the exception: branching networks, +10.0% saving on DeepLabV3; compiled training saves up to 7.60%). Selective recomputation with per-operator caps is proven strongly NP-complete for every fixed cap even at treewidth two (exactly knapsack on series–parallel graphs), is useless on public tiny models whose peak is one operator, but on ReDimNet speaker networks (B0–B6) lowers the peak by 5.2–6.1% at 0.02–0.34% extra MACs with bitwise-identical outputs.

## 核心贡献

- **量化了"重新生成代码"部署方式的不可靠性：** 在 PyTorch Inductor（2.14.1）中，把优化后的顺序映射回调度节点并重新编译，54 个 CPU 训练配置中有 22 个实际峰值偏离预测、9 个比原始程序更差（最差 −30.6%，T5 encoder @ seq 512）；GPU 上 50 个配置中 19 个更差，且重编译内核的舍入方式不同导致梯度数值发生改变。
- **提出"固定 wrapper 实现"（fixed-wrapper realization）：** 对 Inductor 生成的程序做一次带剖析的暖运行，从其自身语句及读写效应构建精确的 root 内存模型，然后只置换语句顺序（内核、参数、缓冲区复用全部不动）。定理 1 证明在六条件契约（含"分配序列与历史无关"的 frame 条件）下，实现的峰值逐字节等于模型预测、且结果逐位一致；CPU 上 78 个训练 + 22 个推理运行全部验证为零预测误差。
- **给出可独立验证的最优性证书：** 前向与反向"陷阱"下界 F 与 R，各用 O(n²) 次最小割计算，均支配经典 event-cut 界 G 且二者不可比较；在 122 个 held-out wrapper 图上证明 115 个（94%）已最优，16 个微控制器级模型全部最优，中位耗时 0.004 s（CP-SAT 基线中位 0.02 s、3 个超时且证书不可独立复核）。
- **绘制了重排的实际收益地图：** 大多数推理图（22 个中 20 个，含 ResNet/MobileNetV2/BERT/GPT-2/Llama prefill 至 2048 token）已是最优；分支结构是例外——DeepLabV3-ResNet50 @ 512×512 峰值降 10.0%；编译训练最多省 7.60%（Transformer encoder–decoder），读写效应的精确建模比搜索本身更关键（把所有访问当写会使 encoder 的 4.6% 收益完全消失）。
- **刻画了带 per-operator cap 的选择性重计算的复杂度边界：** 任意固定一致 cap k≥1 下峰值可行性是强 NP 完全的（即使 treewidth ≤ 2）；cap=2、出度 ≤2 的 series–parallel 图上最小额外工作量精确等于 0–1 背包；有界 treewidth 上存在精确算法（典型序列归约），但原型在 6 顶点、K=2、B=8 的 treewidth-2 图上就超过 3×10⁶ 个状态，"是关于可解性的陈述，而非工具"。
- **给出重计算的适用判据并用 ReDimNet 验证：** 公开微控制器模型（含 MLPerf Tiny 四个参考网络）的峰值均由单个算子决定，张量级重排与重计算都无能为力；而 ReDimNet（B0–B6，VoxCeleb2 公开检查点）每阶段都晚用到 stem 输出，重算一次 stem 即降低峰值 5.2–6.1%（额外 MACs 0.02–0.34%），输出逐位一致，且新旧顺序均被证书证明最优。

## Key Contributions

- **A measurement that regeneration is unreliable.** The standard way to deploy a better order — force it in Inductor's reordering pass and recompile — misses its own predicted peak in 22 of 54 compiled CPU training configurations, is *worse* than the stock program in 9 (worst case −30.6%, T5 encoder at sequence length 512), and on GPU is worse in 19 of 50 configurations while also changing gradient numerics (regenerated kernels round differently; the encoder–decoder wrapper's 559 backward statements become 554).
- **Exact realization on the generated program.** Instead of regenerating, they profile one warm step of the stock-compiled wrappers, build an exact root model (units = kernel/library statements, allocations merged as late as possible, transients folded into a per-unit envelope s(v), liveness via allocation epochs so views/tuples are attributed exactly), derive RAW/WAR/WAW ordering edges from pointer const-ness (C++), parameter roles (`in_ptr`/`out_ptr`/`in_out_ptr`, Triton) and `out=` arguments (library calls), then emit the *same* statements in the new order. Theorem 1: under a six-condition contract the realized peak equals the modeled peak and results are preserved bitwise. Verified: 0 B prediction error in all 78 training and 22 inference CPU runs, bitwise-identical outputs and gradients.
- **"Trap" lower bounds and certificates.** A forward bound F (every order exits a proper ideal through some first operation) and a reverse bound R (symmetric argument on last operations), each computable with O(n²) minimum-cut calls, each dominating the classical event-cut bound G, and incomparable with each other (F beats R in 98 random instances, loses in 11; worst ratio 1.23 random, 1.50 adversarial). A certificate (stuck ideal + one flow/cut pair per frontier operation) is independently checkable without the solver. On 800 random DAGs (≤14 vertices) max(F,R) equals the exact optimum in 773 cases vs 671 for G.
- **A map of where scheduling pays.** Certificates prove the stock order already optimal for 20 of 22 server-scale inference graphs (ResNet, MobileNetV2, EfficientNet, DenseNet, Inception, Swin, ViT, BERT, GPT-2, Llama prefill up to 2048 tokens) and all 16 microcontroller-class models (up to 134 operators). Reordering pays where parallel branches overlap a large feature map: DeepLabV3-ResNet50 at 512×512 saves 10.0%. Compiled training saves up to 7.60% (Transformer enc–dec d64), median ~1.3–2.5%.
- **Complexity limits of selective recomputation.** Peak feasibility with uniform cap k is strongly NP-complete for every fixed k≥1 already at treewidth ≤2 (via a reset-gadget reduction from weighted one-shot scheduling on pumpkins); minimum extra work under cap 2 on out-degree-≤2 series–parallel graphs is *exactly* 0–1 knapsack (so the peak/work frontier can have exponentially many non-dominated points); an exact f(t,K,B)·poly(n,L) algorithm exists on bounded treewidth using typical-sequence reductions, but its dependence on the numeric budget B cannot be made polynomial unless P=NP, and the prototype is unusable in practice.
- **When recomputation helps, demonstrated.** On public tiny models recomputation of whole tensors cannot help because the peak equals inputs + one operator's output (a trivial lower bound; the capped solver with cap 2 finds nothing feasible). On the seven public ReDimNet speaker-embedding backbones (VoxCeleb2 checkpoints), whose model order is already certified optimal, recomputing the cheap stem once for its late uses lowers the peak by 5.2–6.1% at 0.02–0.34% extra MACs with bitwise-identical outputs, and the transformed graph is itself certified optimal.

## Methodology

The paper sits at the intersection of ML compilation, scheduling theory, and parameterized complexity.

1. **Root model of a compiled program.** A one-shot schedule executes each operation of a DAG once; operation v allocates persistent buffers ("roots", total a(v)) plus a transient envelope s(v), and executing v after ideal S costs L(S) + a(v) + s(v). Liveness L is submodular over the lattice of ideals, which is what makes min-cut machinery applicable. Multi-output calls, views and external inputs are expressed as roots with several consumers.
2. **From statements to units.** The model is compiled once with stock Inductor (PyTorch 2.14.1); each generated wrapper runs one warm step under per-statement profiler scopes, recording the storage address and size bound to every name after each statement. Storages are identified by allocation epoch, not address. Kernel/library calls become units; pure-allocation statements join the first unit that touches the buffer (allocation as late as possible); aliases, tuple projections and `del` statements are not units but are placed during emission; storages born and freed inside one statement are transients.
3. **Read/write effects and dependencies.** For generated C++ kernels, pointer const-ness is read from the emitted signature; for Triton kernels, from parameter names (`in_ptr`, `out_ptr`, `in_out_ptr`); for library calls, the `out=` argument and the first argument of mutating operators are writes; unrecognized calls are serialized against everything. Per-storage RAW/WAR/WAW edges leave readers of the same buffer generation free to reorder — this precision, not the search, is where the savings live.
4. **Emission and guarantees.** Given a unit order, the original statements are each emitted exactly once; allocations go immediately before their unit, aliases/assertions/dels as early as dependencies allow; nothing is synthesized, only input-rebinding renames. Theorem 1's contract (C1–C6, notably C5: each unit's allocation-event sequence is independent of surrounding live memory) is *not trusted* — every emitted program is re-profiled and its measured peak compared with the prediction (this harness caught two of the authors' own bugs during development).
5. **Search and certification.** An exact capacity search (DFS over ideals with forced non-increasing steps and min-cut packet closure, bisecting between the trap lower bound and the stock peak) handles models with up to 259 units in milliseconds. The trap bounds F and R are computed with O(n²) minimum cuts; a certificate is a stuck ideal plus flow/cut pairs, replayable by an independent verifier. Baseline: CP-SAT on an exact cumulative model.
6. **Recomputation theory.** Caps k_v as in Moccasin; one-shot certificates project onto once-computed sets to prove recomputation *necessary* (knapsack-cover constraints on recomputed bytes); strong NP-completeness via reset gadgets; a treewidth-based exact algorithm records complete compute/delete event words (length ≤ 2K(t+1)) plus typical-sequence reductions of private memory profiles (length ≤ 2B+1).
7. **Experimental setup.** PyTorch 2.14.1 + Inductor; CPU FP32 single-thread (bfloat16 inference repeated); NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti, CUDA 12.6. Stock baseline = Inductor with the peak-memory reordering pass and in-place buffer reuse enabled. Four study parts: 22 server-scale inference configs, 16 microcontroller-class models (MLPerf Tiny references, TC-ResNet keyword spotters, MobileNets, MNASNet, ShuffleNet, SqueezeNet, EfficientNet-B0, GoogLeNet, tiny U-Net; BN folded, int8 activations, TFLite-Micro-style greedy arena planner), 50 compiled-training configs of 16 architectures, and a GPU replication. Every run measures stock, normalized, and reordered programs twice and compares outputs and all gradients bitwise.

## Results & Highlights

- **Regeneration misses its own prediction:** on CPU, 22 of 54 training configurations miss the predicted peak; where a saving exists, regeneration realizes only 0.94% on average vs 1.97% for fixed-wrapper realization; 9 configurations end up *worse* than stock, once by −30.6% (T5-small encoder, b1 s512: 153,120,776 B → regeneration loses; fixed wrapper saves 2.05%). On GPU, regeneration is worse than stock in 19 of 50 configurations and changes gradients.
- **Exactness on CPU:** fixed-wrapper realization has 0 B prediction error in all 78 training runs and all 22 inference runs, with bitwise-identical outputs and gradients; e.g., Transformer enc–dec d64 drops from 1,073,928 B to 992,264 B (−7.60%), TransformerEnc 2L d256 s512 from 16,813,320 B to 16,026,888 B (−4.68%), Swin-T b2 from 220,170,192 B to 213,530,552 B (−3.02%).
- **Inference gains are concentrated:** only 1 of 22 server-scale inference configurations improves — DeepLabV3-ResNet50 at 512×512, −10.0% (the atrous spatial pyramid's five parallel branches over a large feature map); certificates prove the other 20 (incl. BERT, GPT-2, Llama prefill to 2048 tokens) already optimal. All 16 microcontroller-class models are certified optimal in model order, with every executed schedule reproducing the modeled peak exactly.
- **Certificates:** on 122 held-out wrapper graphs, max(F,R) certifies optimality in 115 (94%) — forward alone 99, reverse alone 112 — in median 0.004 s / max 0.25 s; the CP-SAT baseline answers 91/94 infeasibility queries with median 0.02 s, max 66 s, and 3 timeouts. On 800 random DAGs with exact optima, max(F,R) is exact in 773 vs 671 for the event-cut bound G. Open gaps: 0.36–1.50% on DistilBERT/BERT/ViT-B backward graphs and one inference case (RegNetY-400MF, 13% gap).
- **Selective recomputation on ReDimNet (Table 1, 2 s input, fp32):** peaks fall from 7.28→6.85 MB (B0, −6.0%), 10.49→9.85 (B1, −6.1%), 16.78→15.91 (B2/B3, −5.2%), 33.56→31.76 MB (B4–B6, −5.4%) at +0.02–0.34% MACs, outputs bitwise identical; the capped solver with cap 2 finds *no* feasible reduction on any of the 16 public tiny models because their peak equals a single operator's footprint.
- **Effects precision beats search:** treating every storage access as a write (conservative modelling) erases the encoder's 4.6%, the large-sequence encoder's 4.7% and DistilBERT's 2.3% savings entirely, and cuts the enc–dec from 7.6% to 6.2%. Disabling Inductor's own memory pass leaves the stock peak unchanged in 6 of 8 configs and raises it by only 1.3%/1.9% in the others.
- **GPU:** fixed-wrapper realization lowers the allocator-reported peak in 25 of 50 configs (median 1.3%, up to 7.4%: encdec-L2-d64 −7.39%) but raises it in 8 (up to +1.5%) because the CUDA caching allocator's history-dependent block rounding breaks contract condition C5 — the block-level prediction is exact in only 17 of 49. With a measured guard (keep stock unless the peak improves) no configuration regresses and the mean saving is 0.92%. In all 27 deterministic-stock configurations the reordered program is bitwise identical; in the other 23 (cuDNN/attention atomics) it differs from stock by no more than stock differs from itself.
- **Cost is essentially zero:** median reordered-to-stock time ratio 0.997 (CPU, within [0.959, 1.045]; 0.998 on GPU); profiling + model building + certification add one extra step and under a second per graph. Bfloat16 inference is exact in 6/6 configs; pretrained torchvision/HF checkpoints give identical peaks and schedules in 6/6 (pretrained DeepLabV3 still saves 10.0%).

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors (Ivan Fomin, Ivan Koryabkin, Boris Sheludko) are at Yandex, Moscow — an industry lab with strong systems-ML incentives (on-device inference at scale), which fits the paper's deployment-first mindset (guards, bitwise checks, the honest note that internal production on-device models gained up to 15% arena reduction at ~1% extra MACs but that all paper claims rest on public models). The paper is an arXiv preprint (v1, 16 Oct 2026, cs.LG/cs.DS/cs.PF) and the authors candidly state it is not peer-reviewed. Credibility signals are unusually strong: every theorem is paired with a mechanical check (realization re-profiled, certificates independently replayable, bitwise output/gradient comparison on every run), the development harness is reported to have caught two of their own bugs, negative results (most graphs already optimal; recomputation useless on public tiny models) are presented as first-class findings, and a generative-AI usage statement discloses that AI assisted with translation, code and figures under line-by-line author review. The cited related work (Inductor, TFLite Micro, Checkmate, Moccasin, Rockmate, MCUNetV2, pebbling complexity) is accurate and appropriately positioned.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core combination is genuinely new — not another scheduling heuristic (the search is an off-the-shelf exact capacity search), but (i) the measurement that regeneration silently breaks predicted savings in a mainstream compiler, (ii) statement-level permutation of the *generated* program with a proven realization contract, and (iii) polynomial lower-bound certificates that say when to stop. The trap bounds' domination over event cuts and their O(n²) min-cut computation are claimed as new, which a bounded literature review supports. The recomputation complexity results (strong NP-completeness at every fixed cap on treewidth-two DAGs; exact knapsack equivalence on series–parallel graphs) cleanly delimit the design space.
- **Rigor:** High. Exactness is verified to the byte rather than statistically; outputs and gradients are compared bitwise; the certificate verifier re-derives everything from the graph without importing the solver; CP-SAT provides an external baseline for both correctness and speed. Statistical claims (medians, ratios with intervals) are appropriately framed.
- **Reproducibility:** Moderate-to-good. All numbers rest on public models and checkpoints (VoxCeleb2 ReDimNet, MLPerf Tiny, torchvision/HF), precise software versions (PyTorch 2.14.1, CUDA 12.6) and per-configuration tables in the appendix; but no code release is mentioned, the harness depends on fragile internals of Inductor's generated wrappers, and the exact profiler instrumentation would need re-implementation.
- **Significance:** The practical takeaway is double-edged and the paper says so honestly: most inference graphs are already optimal, so the *certificate* (telling you to stop) is arguably the most deployable contribution; realized savings are 1–3% typical in training, 10% only on branching segmentation networks, 5–6% on one architecture family (ReDimNet) via recomputation. For latency-critical TinyML these margins can still matter.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) The GPU — where most inference actually runs — breaks the exactness guarantee by construction (caching allocator violates C5), and the fallback guard cuts the mean saving to 0.92%, with the block-level prediction exact in only 17 of 49 configs; a simulated-allocator search is left as future work. (2) The regeneration-unreliability measurement may partially reflect immaturity of Inductor's reordering-pass interface (forcing an order via scheduler nodes) rather than a fundamental obstacle; a future compiler version could close part of this gap. (3) Theorem 5's bounded-treewidth algorithm is admitted to be "a statement about tractability, not a tool" (prototype exceeds 3×10⁶ states on six-vertex width-two graphs). (4) The tiny-model study evaluates an operator-graph simulation with a greedy arena planner, not actual firmware on hardware. (5) The most eye-catching internal number (15% arena reduction) is unverifiable, though correctly quarantined as "context only." (6) Single GPU (one RTX 2080 Ti), library-kernel effects are profiled and re-measured rather than proven, and each model is specific to one input shape. (7) Training runs mostly use random weights (shown equivalent for 6 pretrained configs, but the equivalence check is narrow); 7 of 122 graphs remain uncertified, with a 13% bound gap on RegNetY-400MF.

## Strengths

- Turns a folklore assumption ("a better order on the compiler graph yields a better program") into a quantified, surprising negative result with a concrete failure census (22/54 CPU, 19/50 GPU, −30.6% worst case).
- Realization is *proven* exact under an explicit contract and then *mechanically verified* on every run — a standard of trust rare in memory-optimizer papers, where predictions are usually approximate.
- The certificates change the engineering conversation: instead of chasing marginal heuristic improvements, a deployment engineer learns in <1 s that 94% of held-out graphs (and all 16 tiny models) admit no better order, and effort should go to branching topologies, recomputation, or tiling instead.
- Precise read/write effect modelling (reordering readers of the same buffer generation) is shown to matter more than the search itself — an actionable lesson with an ablation (4.6%→0% when collapsed).
- The complexity section is honest and complete: hardness at every fixed cap, an exact knapsack characterization, and a tractability theorem with a working-but-impractical prototype, all pointing to certified tile-level scheduling as the pragmatic continuation.
- Exceptional experimental hygiene: bitwise gradient checks, twice-measured programs, bfloat16 and pretrained-weight replication, latency neutrality reported with an interval, and internal-production claims clearly quarantined from the public evidence.

## Limitations

- Exactness does not transfer to the CUDA caching allocator (history-dependent block sizes violate the frame condition C5); GPU deployment needs a measured guard, under which the mean saving is 0.92% and block-level prediction is exact in only 17 of 49 configs; expandable segments do not remove the effect.
- Absolute savings are modest: 0% on 20 of 22 server-scale inference graphs (by certified optimality, not search failure), typically 1–3% in compiled training (median 2.54%, max 7.60% on the replication subset), and the 10% DeepLabV3 gain vanishes in the common deployment where torchvision's segmentation head is attached (the extra FCN head raises the peak 10% and makes stock optimal again).
- Recomputation of whole tensors is provably useless on the public tiny models (single-operator peak); further gains there require tensor tiling / patch-based inference, which this paper does not implement.
- The bounded-treewidth exact algorithm is exponential in the budget B (unavoidably, unless P=NP) and its prototype exceeds 3×10⁶ states on trivial graphs — a theory result, not an engineering tool.
- The microcontroller study simulates the runtime (folded BN, int8, greedy arena planner) rather than running TFLite Micro firmware on device; library-kernel effects are profiled and re-measured, not proven; models are shape-specific.
- One hardware configuration per accelerator class (single-thread CPU, one RTX 2080 Ti); multi-stream CUDA, multi-GPU and non-PyTorch compilers are out of scope. Whether OPT/max(F,R) is bounded remains open, and 7 of 122 held-out graphs (incl. RegNetY-400MF with a 13% gap) are left uncertified.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"编译器/系统层的高效推理与内存优化"方向高度相关：它把峰值内存调度从启发式搜索升级为"可证明、可验证"的工程流程（精确实现 + 最优性证书 + 复杂度边界），对 PyTorch Inductor/TinyML 工具链的可靠性度量（重新生成代码反而更差）对任何做 on-device 推理部署或编译器内存优化的人都直接有用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Could the trap bounds be strengthened into a family that closes the remaining 7/122 gaps (notably the 13% RegNetY-400MF gap), and is OPT/max(F,R) bounded by a constant on any natural graph class?
- Can the search be run against a *simulated* CUDA caching allocator (whose behaviour is deterministic for a fixed single-stream request sequence) to restore byte-exact realization on GPU without the measured guard?
- How do the certificates and the exact realization interact with dynamic shapes / CUDA graphs / multi-stream execution, where the wrapper is generated once but allocation histories diverge across invocations?
- Do the ReDimNet-style "cheap value held for a late reuse" patterns generalize to other streaming architectures (e.g., keyword spotters, separable-Conv front-ends), and can a static analyzer detect them without profiling?
- The paper points to certified tile-level scheduling (choosing tile shapes jointly with the schedule under a latency budget, with per-tile halo recomputation as caps) as the natural continuation — what does the certificate structure look like on the tile graph of an early high-resolution layer?
- Since the regeneration unreliability was measured on PyTorch 2.14.1's reordering-pass interface, how much of the gap is attributable to the forcing mechanism vs. codegen itself, and would a compiler-native "preserve my order" API restore faith in regeneration?
