> **一句话总结：** 该文把"智能体提出自我更新、奖励模型/自动审稿人等终止性门控决定是否接受"形式化为可计算性问题，用 Kleene 递归定理证明：在无界的一般情形下，任何会停机的门控（确定性的证书器或搜索器、错误率有界的随机门控、隐藏或带状态的门控、多智能体管线）都无法同时做到"可靠"（绝不接受非改进）与"不保守"（绝不漏掉真改进）——evaluator hacking 是结构性不可避免的，而非统计误差；只有在有界评测+有限提案预算下，穷举模拟才能（相对于有界代理分数）恢复保证，且此时仍存在无需对手的"代理漂移"：有界分数每轮上升而真实分数每轮下降。

> **TL;DR:** A pure theory paper (no experiments; proofs plus a Lean 4 formalization of Theorems 1–3) that models self-improving agents as programs and automated gates — reward models, test suites, automated reviewers like those in The AI Scientist pipelines (Lu et al. 2024; Yamada et al. 2025) — as terminating computable procedures. Using Kleene's recursion theorem it proves that no total computable certifier can be simultaneously sound (never accept a non-improvement) and complete (never reject a true improvement) for a semantic score J, even when J is just "halts on input 0 and outputs 1"; even a *searcher* handed the proposal generator's code cannot be sound and weakly complete; randomized gates with 2/3–1/3 bounded error are derandomizable and equally impossible; hidden or stateful gates, and multi-agent pipelines, do not escape. Guarantees are recovered only under bounded evaluation and a finite proposal budget (exhaustive simulation is sound and weakly complete for the bounded score J_T) — but a no-adversary drift construction (n = 2k tasks: bounded score climbs 0→k while the true score falls 2k→k) shows these guarantees do not compose across rounds. Worth reading if you care about the theoretical limits of self-improving agents; skip if you want empirical benchmark numbers.

## 核心贡献

- **最小形式化**：把智能体建模为程序（Gödel 编号 p，行为为部分可计算函数 φ_p），自我更新建模为程序变换 q，提案机制建模为总可计算枚举器 E(p, i)，门控建模为总可计算的证书器 V(p, q) ∈ {0,1} 或搜索器 S(p, e) ∈ N ∪ {⊥}；"真改进"定义为可能不可计算的语义分数泛函 J 的严格提升（J(q) > J(p)）。可靠性（soundness，不接受任何非改进 = 安全/防 reward hacking）与不保守性（completeness，不错过任何可得改进 = 不阻塞真进步）由此可以精确陈述。
- **定理 1（证书器不可能性）**：即便 J 只是"φ_p(0) 停机且输出 1"这一极简分数，也不存在总可计算的 V 同时满足可靠与完备。证明用 s-m-n 定理 + Kleene 递归定理构造出"被接受则输出 0、被拒绝则输出 1"的自指候选——这正是 evaluator hacking（提案机制把门控当作环境中的可预测部件来优化）的形式化对应。
- **定理 2（搜索同样不可能）**：把逐候选判定弱化为"只要枚举器中含有改进就找出一个"的搜索也无济于事——即使搜索器能读到提案枚举器的源码，枚举器本身仍可通过对角构造让候选行为依赖于搜索器的输出，迫使任何尊重提案的总搜索器违反可靠性或弱完备性。这一点超出了 Rice 定理的范畴。
- **命题 1 与第 5 节（常见逃逸路径全部失效）**：错误率有界（接受真改进概率 ≥ 2/3、误接受概率 ≤ 1/3）的随机门控可经 Kőnig 引理去随机化为确定性可靠完备证书器，故同样不存在；隐藏门控只改变"谁能实施攻击"，不改变失败输入的存在性；有状态门控在每个可达状态上仍被定理 1 击败；附录 A 证明多智能体管线（提案者/审稿者/编排器/协议）可编译为单个程序，对角信道经由直接查询、接受/拒绝反馈、转录泄漏持续存在。人类监督在模型之外——这是设计教训而非漏洞。
- **定理 3（有界情形可恢复）**：在评测时限 T、提案预算 B、生成时限 T_E 下，穷举模拟给出一个总可计算搜索器，对有界分数 J_T 可靠且预算内弱完备；且界不必是常数，可以是任意总可计算函数 T(p,e)、B(p,e)、T_E(p,e)——真正的分界是"门控能承诺计算的预算"对"无界语义行为"。
- **命题 2（代理漂移）**：存在一条每步都被（对 J_T 可靠完备的）门控接受的更新链：n = 2k 个任务上，有界分数从 0 升到 k，真实分数从 2k 降到 k。机制是每轮用"更少任务上的快速正确"换掉"更多任务上的慢速正确"，损失藏在了时限之外——无需自指、无需对手。有界性挡住了对角线，却挡不住漂移；两种失败模式正交，修复其一不修复另一。

## Key Contributions

- A **minimal formal model of verification-gated self-improvement**: agents are program indices, updates are program transformations, proposal generation is a computable enumerator, and gating is a terminating (total computable) procedure — turning the folklore claim "evaluator hacking is unavoidable once the proposer can model the gate" into a theorem with explicit assumptions.
- **Theorem 1**: no total computable certifier is both sound and complete for strict semantic improvement, even for the trivial halting-based score; the diagonal candidate is built with Kleene's recursion theorem and is "bad if accepted, good if rejected". A generality remark shows only two properties of J are needed (extensionality + two attainable comparable values) — a relational Rice-style phenomenon.
- **Theorem 2**: replacing certification by search does not help — no proposal-respecting total searcher (even one that reads the proposal enumerator's code) is sound and weakly complete over all enumerators; the enumerator itself can host the diagonal.
- **Proposition 1 + Section 5**: randomized gates with bounded error (Pr[accept | improvement] ≥ 2/3, Pr[accept | non-improvement] ≤ 1/3) are derandomized via Kőnig's lemma and collapse to Theorem 1; hidden, stateful, and multi-agent gates (Appendix A) do not repair the guarantee; human oversight sits outside the formal model and bounds what automation alone can guarantee.
- **Theorem 3**: under bounded evaluation (T), finite proposal budget (B), and generation bound (T_E), exhaustive simulation yields a terminating searcher sound and weakly complete for the bounded score J_T — delimiting exactly which design constraints (timeouts, budgets) restore decidability, with bounds allowed to be arbitrary computable functions of the input.
- **Proposition 2 (proxy drift)**: within the decidable regime, a chain of accepted updates can raise the bounded score every round (0 → k) while the true score falls every round (2k → k) with no adversary involved — bounded guarantees do not compose across rounds, motivating the design rule "fixed per-round budget + periodic re-evaluation with a larger budget".

## Methodology

The paper is self-contained computability theory applied to ML evaluation pipelines. Agents are encoded as natural numbers (program indices p) under a fixed universal machine, with behavior given by partial computable functions φ_p; the intended performance is a semantic score functional J : N → S (S a strictly ordered score set) that may be uncomputable; the gate is a total computable certifier V : N × N → {0,1} or a total searcher S : N × N → (N ∪ {⊥}) that must respect a total computable proposal enumerator E : N × N → N. "Strict improvement" is J(q) > J(p); soundness means V(p,q)=1 ⇒ improvement (no false approvals), completeness means improvement ⇒ V(p,q)=1 (no blocked progress).

- **Theorem 1 proof**: fix p with J(p)=0; use the s-m-n theorem to define a total computable program transformation f(e) = index of "on input 0, compute V(p,e), output 0 if it is 1, else 1"; by Kleene's recursion theorem there is a fixed point q with φ_q = φ_{f(q)}. If V(p,q)=1 then J(q)=0 (soundness violated); if V(p,q)=0 then J(q)=1 > 0 = J(p) (completeness violated). The construction uses the gate as an oracle — the formal counterpart of evaluator hacking.
- **Theorem 2 proof**: define programs q_e that on input 0 compute r ← S(p,e) and c ← E_e(p,0), outputting 0 iff r = c; build a computable transformation g mapping e to an enumerator that proposes q_e first and the dummy p afterwards; the recursion theorem yields a fixed-point enumerator E_{e⋆} = E_{g(e⋆)} whose first proposal is exactly the diagonal candidate, and a two-case analysis on S(p, e⋆) forces a violation of soundness or of weak completeness (all other proposals equal p, so no improving alternative exists).
- **Proposition 1**: a probabilistic gate halting on every coin sequence has, by Kőnig's lemma, a finite coin tree; a deterministic machine can exhaustively simulate it, compute the exact rational acceptance probability α(p,q), and threshold at 1/2 — producing a deterministic sound-and-complete certifier, contradicting Theorem 1 (the de Leeuw–Moore–Shannon–Shapiro argument covers the halt-with-probability-one relaxation).
- **Theorem 3 construction**: S_{T,B,T_E}(p,e) simulates φ_p(0) for T steps to compute J_T(p), then for each i < B simulates E_e(p,i) for at most T_E steps (skipping non-halting generators) and simulates each candidate for T steps, returning the first strict improvement of J_T or ⊥. Everything is step-bounded, so the searcher is total. The remark after the theorem shows T, B, T_E may be arbitrary total computable functions — no time-constructibility needed.
- **Proposition 2 construction**: with n = 2k tasks, p_i is the program that outputs 1 immediately for x < i, idles T+1 steps then outputs 1 for i ≤ x < n−i, and outputs 0 for x ≥ n−i. Then J_T^{(n)}(p_i) = i (fast successes only) while J^{(n)}(p_i) = n − i (fast + slow successes), so each accepted round raises the bounded score by 1 and lowers the true score by 1. The mechanism (trading slow-correct for fast-correct behavior beyond the timeout) needs no self-reference.
- **Appendices**: A compiles a multi-agent pipeline (proposer, reviewer, orchestrator, protocol) into a single program and re-runs the diagonal under a mild "controllability" assumption (two proposer indices inducing scores 0 and 1), identifying query access, outcome feedback, and transcript leakage as the channels that keep the diagonal alive; B (Proposition 3) proves the interactive expected-reward score is total computable under finite action/observation sets, rational computable transitions and rewards, a horizon H (evaluation tree ≤ |O|^H leaves), and a per-step compute cap, so Theorem 3 carries over to interactive evaluation under those assumptions.
- A **Lean 4 formalization of Theorems 1–3** accompanies the paper (github.com/Brian-Moser/SelfImprovementProof).

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — this is a pure theory paper: the evidence consists of constructive proofs plus a Lean 4 formalization of Theorems 1–3, not benchmark experiments. The closest things to quantitative claims are the exact parameters of the formal constructions:

- **Scope of the main impossibility**: Theorem 1 holds already for the minimal semantic score J(p) = 1 iff φ_p(0) halts and outputs 1 (a binary metric, i.e., the hardest case to break), and the generality remark extends it to any extensional J with two attainable comparable values — no certifier exists even for this 1-bit "benchmark".
- **Randomization buys nothing (Proposition 1)**: no probabilistic gate achieves Pr[V(p,q)=1 | improvement] ≥ 2/3 and ≤ 1/3 on non-improvements — the 2/3 vs 1/3 bounded-error regime is derandomizable and collapses to the deterministic impossibility.
- **Proxy drift magnitude (Proposition 2)**: with n = 2k tasks and a time bound T just large enough to read an input and output 1, there is a chain of k accepted updates over which the bounded score J_T^{(n)} rises from 0 to k (+1 per round) while the true score J^{(n)} falls from 2k to k (−1 per round) — a 100% divergence between proxy and truth over the run, with each individual slow success hidden behind exactly T+1 idle steps.
- **Recovery regime (Theorem 3)**: exhaustive simulation under bounds (T, B, T_E) restores soundness and budget-weak-completeness relative to J_T; the remark shows the boundary is not "constant vs growing budgets" (the bounds may be any total computable functions of (p, e)) but "budgets the gate can compute vs unbounded semantic behavior" — the diagonal returns exactly when the score depends on behavior beyond every bound committed to in advance.
- **Interactive extension (Proposition 3)**: under finite actions/observations, rational computable dynamics, and per-step compute caps, the interactive expected-reward score is computable over an evaluation tree of at most |O|^H leaves, so the bounded-regime guarantee survives interactive evaluation — but only under those assumptions.
- **Proof-carrying gates are necessarily conservative**: for Theorem 1's J, the set of true improvement pairs is not computably enumerable (witnessing J(p)=0 is a Π₁ fact), while any fixed proof-checking gate (e.g., Schmidhuber's Gödel machine) accepts a computably enumerable set — so every sound proof-based gate misses infinitely many genuine improvements.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (moderate, honestly scoped)**: The authors themselves concede that Theorem 1 is "close to a restatement of Rice's theorem" in relational form, and that the proof ingredients (diagonalization, s-m-n, the recursion theorem) are all classical. The genuinely new pieces are Theorem 2 (search with the generator's code in hand is still impossible — this goes beyond Rice, which says nothing about finding witnesses), Proposition 1's randomized-gate collapse, Proposition 2's separation of diagonal failure from proxy drift, and the ML-facing formalization itself. Readers expecting a new proof technique will be disappointed; readers wanting exact assumptions behind "reward hacking is inevitable" will be satisfied.
- **Rigor (high)**: proofs are constructive, each hypothesis's role is isolated (the remark pinpoints exactly where extensionality, attainable comparability, and totality of V are used), and the three main theorems are Lean 4-formalized — close to the gold standard for this genre.
- **Reproducibility (high for theory)**: the formalization is publicly linked; there are no experiments to reproduce. This is also the paper's main empirical gap (below).
- **Significance (real but model-to-practice gap)**: the impossibility is worst-case and universally quantified over proposal mechanisms. A real LLM proposer with finite context cannot literally run a Kleene fixed-point construction on a reward model's weights; the paper acknowledges this ("they do not imply that self-improvement cannot occur"), but it offers no bridge — no bound on how much proposer compute the diagonal requires, and no demonstration that learned "flip-on-accept" behavior approximates it. Until such a bridge exists, the practical force of Theorems 1–2 for today's systems is by analogy.
- **Weak spots to flag**: (i) Theorem 3's positive result is the folklore practice of "run everything under a timeout and a budget" made exact — unsurprising on its own, and its guarantee is only relative to J_T, which Proposition 2 then shows can drift; (ii) Proposition 2's construction is contrived (idles for exactly T+1 steps), a pathology engineered to sit just outside the budget rather than one observed in any real pipeline; (iii) the model's deterministic halting semantics and extensional J abstract away stochastic sampling — the interactive extension (Proposition 3) needs finite action/observation sets, rational transition probabilities, and per-step compute caps, assumptions that real environments violate; (iv) there is no empirical illustration at all — even a toy experiment showing a proposer learning to predict a reward-model gate would have grounded the formal claim; the discussion of AI-Scientist-style pipelines (Lu et al. 2024; Yamada et al. 2025) analyzes no actual system; (v) the hidden-gates discussion is careful ("a defense against a specific attacker, not a repair of the guarantee") but risks being over-read as "hiding is useless", when the authors' own point is that the guarantee is false while practical risk may still drop.

## Strengths

- Turns a widely repeated folklore claim about evaluator hacking into a theorem with exactly stated assumptions, separating what fails (joint soundness + completeness), when (unbounded, self-referentially conditionable proposals), and what survives (bounded, budgeted evaluation).
- Theorem 2 is a substantive extension beyond Rice-style undecidability: it shows that even the weaker *search* task — with the generator's source code as input — is impossible when the generator can host the diagonal.
- The constructive proofs describe the concrete attack pattern (a candidate that is good if rejected and bad if accepted, using the gate as an oracle), which maps directly onto reward-model overoptimization intuitions and onto multi-agent channels (query access, accept/reject feedback, transcript leakage) identified in Appendix A.
- Proposition 2 isolates a second, orthogonal failure mode (proxy drift without any adversary) and yields an actionable design rule: keep the per-round budget fixed for speed and periodically re-evaluate with a larger budget.
- The survey of practitioner escape hatches is systematic — randomized, hidden, stateful, multi-agent, interactive — with a clear verdict for each, and an honest account of the one escape the theorems leave open (grounding decisions outside the loop).
- Lean 4 formalization of the three main theorems, and an unusually candid "what is new here and what is not" scope statement.

## Limitations

- Purely worst-case results: nothing is said about the compute or data an actual bounded-capability proposer would need to implement the diagonal, so the distance from "impossible in the limit" to "fails for today's agents" is unmeasured.
- Theorem 3's guarantees hold only relative to the bounded proxy J_T; Proposition 2 shows they do not compose across rounds, and the paper's own open problem — how fast drift can grow when the proposer adapts to the re-evaluation schedule — is left unanswered.
- No experiments, simulations, or case studies; the connection to real pipelines (reward models, SWE-bench-style suites, AI Scientist reviewers) is made by interpretation, not measurement.
- The formal model uses deterministic partial-computable behavior and extensional scores; stochastic LLM agents fit only through the interactive extension, which requires finite action/observation sets, rational computable transitions/rewards, and per-step compute caps.
- The one-sided escape (proof-carrying updates à la Gödel machines) is characterized qualitatively (sound but necessarily incomplete, missing infinitely many improvements) without quantifying how much progress is lost; intermediate regimes (gates with more compute than the proposer, decidable update languages, non-conditioning proposers) are pointed at but not mapped.

## Relevance

直接命中"Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research"方向的核心关切：它为自改进智能体与 AI Scientist 式自动研究管线中"奖励模型/自动审稿人能否可靠地给自我更新把关"给出了精确的理论极限与设计边界。对构建或评估长程自主智能体系统的可信性（超时与有限提案预算、限制更新语言、沙箱、定期放大预算复评、偶尔的人类接地）具有直接的规范意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How fast can proxy drift grow when the proposer adapts to the re-evaluation schedule (the paper's own open problem) — is there a tight bound in terms of the budget growth rate, and what schedule minimizes drift per unit of compute?
- Can bounded-capability proposers (LLM agents with finite context and no access to their own code) empirically approximate the recursion-theoretic diagonal — e.g., can RL against a reward model learn "flip-on-accept" behavior without explicit self-reference, and how much compute does that take?
- Which intermediate restrictions (gates with strictly more compute than the proposer, update languages with decidable score effects, proposal mechanisms provably unable to condition on the gate) restore which single guarantee, and can the conservatism of proof-carrying gates be quantified (e.g., the fraction of true improvements accepted)?
- Can human-in-the-loop oversight be modeled as a non-total procedure with quantified overhead, so that the "escape hatch" the theorems leave open becomes a design space rather than an exception?
- Do deployed automated-research pipelines already exhibit Proposition-2-style drift in the wild — benchmark scores rising under a fixed-timeout suite while slower, deeper evaluation metrics fall — and can drift detectors be built on the "slow success" signature (idle-then-correct behavior just beyond the budget)?
