AdamW-moment scaling plus output-feedback thresholds make spectral clipping self-calibrating — beating nine baselines at 19B MoE scale.

Why This Matters

A drop-in gradient transform before AdamW buys lower loss and far fewer spikes on huge MoE pretraining runs — for ~2% step-time overhead.

它不改变模型结构，只在 AdamW 之前做基于历史统计的自适应梯度变换，用极小的额外开销（约 2% 步时）同时改善损失与梯度尖峰。

Critical Edge

Every condition is a single seed-48 run — the 1.18×10⁻² headline gap (~0.6% relative) could plausibly be seed noise, with no repeats or error bars.

Methodology — Two Histories Around Soft Spectral Clipping

CAST wraps SPECTRA's soft spectral clipping (SSC) with two per-matrix histories, so the clip is defined in relative coordinates, not raw magnitudes:

1Standardize — Z = G∘S, where S is built from row/column/global means of AdamW's previous bias-corrected second moment (floors ρ=0.01, ε_s=10⁻⁸).
↓
2Soft spectral clip — φ_τ(σ) = σ/√(1+(σ/τ)²); a Frobenius bound gates the transform, then 5 Newton–Schulz iterations on the Gram matrix.
↓
3Restore scale — G_out = S∘Z_out; both AdamW moments update from the restored gradient.
↺ feedback
τAdaptive threshold — EMA of the clipped output's estimated spectral norm (3 power iterations, β_h=0.95); τ_t = 1.3·h_{t−1} uses the previous history, so it cannot chase its own output.
  • Scope: spectral branch hits only 2-D dense-group tensors; MoE experts (18.1B of 19.08B params) and embeddings run a simpler output-norm controller.
  • Engineering: 40 warm-up GlobalGC updates; staged state committed only after successful optimizer steps; FP32 transform inside BF16 training.

Strengths

  • Real, underexplored problem — clipping thresholds are only meaningful relative to gradient scale, which drifts per matrix and over training.
  • Principled design — the threshold derives from the previous EMA (τ_t = 1.3·h_{t−1}), so it cannot self-reference within a step; state commits only after successful updates.
  • Fair comparison — nine baselines spanning global, per-tensor, coordinate-wise, spectral, and signed-power methods, under identical data, tokenizer, LR, and hardware.
  • Unusual transparency — full per-subset loss tables, explicit spike definitions, a gate-fraction diagnostic, and per-step timing distributions.
  • Decisive ablations — fixed τ=0.1 is catastrophic (+4.7×10⁻² train loss), pinning the gain on the adaptive threshold, not the scaling alone.

Limitations

  • Single seed (48), single run — the 1.18–1.26×10⁻² margins are small vs plausible run-to-run variance; no error bars.
  • Short horizon, loss-only — 5k steps; "validation" is token-level LM loss, not downstream accuracy, and CAST loses HumanEval + CMMLU.
  • ~95% of parameters bypass the spectral branch — 18.1B expert params and all embeddings use the simple norm controller.
  • No hyperparameter sensitivity for λ=1.3, β_h=0.95; SSC-cosine < SSC-constant here contradicts SPECTRA, unexplained.
  • Timing may not transfer — measured on Meituan's internal stack (~60%-of-H100 devices); no code released for independent reproduction.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the 1.18×10⁻² edge survive multi-seed replication and 50k–500k-step horizons?
  2. Why does SSC-cosine underperform SSC-constant here when SPECTRA reported the opposite?
  3. What happens if the 18.1B expert matrices join the spectral branch — do factored moments hold for sparsely-activated weights?
  4. How does λ=1.3 trade clipping strength vs convergence, and does the optimum shift with scale, LR, or BF16/FP8?
  5. Could the two-history principle improve Muon-style orthogonalized updates?
  6. Do the MMLU/GSM8K token-loss wins translate to few-shot accuracy — and is the HumanEval/CMMLU loss systematic?
