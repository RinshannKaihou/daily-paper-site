Key metric

+11.28pts

accuracy under σ=0.20 CIM noise, ViT-Tiny / CIFAR-100: 58.86% (NAT) → 70.14% (ABNAT)

vs the stronger NAT+KD baseline: +7.69 pts · attention drift −85.77% (1.272 → 0.181)

Why this matters

A zero-overhead, training-time fix for deploying Transformers on analog CIM hardware — and evidence that supervising internal representations beats output-only supervision under device noise.

⚠ Critical edge

Deployment claims rest entirely on a synthetic Gaussian noise model — no measured PCM/RRAM device data, no ADC nonlinearity, IR drop, or aging effects.

Deploy — noise-robust, inference cost identical to NAT
  • Noise model: W8A8 bit-planes perturbed by relative Gaussian deviation σ (conductance variation) plus additive per-bit-plane read noise.
  • Headwise attention alignment: temperature-scaled KL between teacher and student attention maps over a selected set S (defaults λ_logits=3, λ_attn=50, τ=1).
  • Cost levers: top-k sensitivity heads (ranked once, pre-training) or block sets — the Last4 variant nearly matches full alignment at under half the overhead.

Diagnosis — why output-only training fails

r = 0.9633

Pearson correlation of mean attention drift with accuracy drop (Spearman ρ = 0.9879) — drift is a reliable robustness diagnostic.

Depth-accumulated drift
Per-head KL drift stays low and uniform through blocks 1–8, then explodes in blocks 9–12 — late blocks are the target.
NAT is overconfident on wrong routes
On jointly-correct samples at σ=0.20, NAT is more confident than ABNAT on 88.9% (0.899 vs 0.678) while its route fidelity is far worse: route KL 1.972 vs 0.338, top-10 token overlap 0.178 vs 0.360.

Right answers via corrupted attention routes — output-only supervision masks the damage.

Robustness beyond matched noise

  • Trained at σ=0.15, still +10.86 pts over NAT at σ_infer=0.20.
  • Trained at σ=0.20, still +9.68 pts even at σ_infer=0.01; clean inference keeps 79.29% vs 69.59% (NAT).
  • Hyperparameters plateau at 79.71–80.19% across λ and τ sweeps; only a too-sharp τ_attn=0.125 collapses (67.57%).

Strengths

  • Mechanistic story, not a bolted-on loss: drift–accuracy correlation (r = 0.9633) justifies the method.
  • Compelling diagnosis of NAT's confidence–route inconsistency (0.899 confidence, route KL 1.972).
  • Practical engineering: no hardware change, zero inference overhead, teacher is a frozen twin of the deployed checkpoint.
  • Unusually broad for the venue — vision, autoregressive LM, and vision-language retrieval, all with 30-seed error bars.

Limitations

  • Purely simulated CIM noise; conclusions may shift under measured PCM/RRAM statistics, temporal drift, or ADC/parasitic effects.
  • Evidence centers on ViT-Tiny/CIFAR-100; the LM budget is tiny (262k tokens) and CLIP gains are modest (+0.57 MR).
  • Gains shrink with noise: +2.17 pts at σ=0.05 vs +11.28 at σ=0.20 — benefit concentrated in high-variation regimes.
  • No comparison against post-training calibration or weight-clipping; no wall-clock/energy numbers for the added training cost.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does ABNAT retain its advantage under measured PCM/RRAM device statistics with temporal conductance drift and ADC nonlinearity?
  2. Must the sensitivity-ranked head set S be recomputed if the target σ changes — and does the ranking transfer across checkpoints?
  3. Can attention alignment combine with post-training calibration or clipping to close the remaining gap to clean accuracy (79.29% vs ~87.7%)?
  4. Does preserving teacher attention routes also improve out-of-distribution robustness (adversarial or distribution shift)?
  5. At realistic LM scale, does late-block drift concentration still hold, or does causal masking redistribute the vulnerable heads?
