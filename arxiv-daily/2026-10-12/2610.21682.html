Critical edge

Every number is one run: single model, single math dataset, no seed variance, scored by peak-of-trajectory accuracy — run-to-run noise is the same order as several reported gaps.

01 · Methodology — filter, don’t replay

RDC sits on a standard decoupled RL loop and changes only which responses enter each update — no architecture change, no route substitution.

rollout vLLM · FP8  |  trainer Megatron · BF16  |  objective GRPO + DAPO Clip-Higher (ε 0.2 / 0.27)
model Qwen3-30B-A3B (thinking off)  |  data DAPO-Math-17K  |  TIS C = 2.0  |  async staleness 0.5, sync every 4 updates
  1. 1Roll out response y from µ (vLLM FP8 engine).
  2. 2Teacher-force y through the trainer π (BF16) — routing assignments are already recorded during old-log-prob computation, so overhead is just the distance calc.
  3. 3Score drift: per-layer expert-usage distributions uₗ(e|y); dₗ = TV-distance between engines; D_route(y) = layer mean ∈ [0,1].
  4. 4Bucket responses by length (2K / 4K / 8K / 12K) and rank D_route within each bucket.
  5. 5Filter the top q = 8% out of the mini-batch update; retained responses train on their native routes. Recomputed every update.
  • Why not replay (R3): Props. 1–2 prove substituted routes zero the gradients of natively-selected-but-not-replayed experts — a nonzero gradient bias.
  • Why TIS can’t see it: §3.4 constructs two different expert sets producing identical logits — importance ratios and token-level KL are provably blind to routing changes.

The honest negative: at low staleness (2048/2048) RDC loses to plain TIS — AIME24 78.5 vs 80.0, MATH-500 97.8 vs 98.2 (wins ZebraLogic 73.9 vs 66.1, AIME25 72.4 vs 71.3). The payoff is specifically staleness-shaped.

03 · Strengths

What the paper gets right.

  • Crisp problem decomposition: three named sources of off-policy mismatch, each mapped to system-level, probability-level, or routing-level correction.
  • Best idea — non-identifiability (§3.4): a principled reason probability-space corrections (TIS/CISPO/GSPO…) cannot substitute for routing awareness.
  • Deliberately simple: TV score + top-q filter, no architecture change; routing already recorded, so overhead is negligible; composes orthogonally with TIS.
  • Gains land where theory predicts: staleness ↑ → RDC margin ↑; the losing regime is openly reported.
  • Beyond leaderboards: reward / KL / grad-norm / length dynamics tie RDC to the known async-RL collapse phenomenon.

04 · Limitations

Read the +7.9 with these attached.

  • Single model, single dataset: Qwen3-30B-A3B on DAPO-Math-17K, math/logic benchmarks only; other MoE topologies and dense models untested.
  • No seeds: all conclusions from single runs scored by peak-best accuracy — noise-favoring.
  • Missing ablations: q fixed at 0.08 with no sensitivity study; no RDC-without-TIS arm, so the filter’s standalone effect is never isolated.
  • Coarse score: TV over usage distributions blurs where in a response routing diverged; a token-level mask is untested.
  • Theory-to-evidence gap: replay bias b_replay is never directly measured, nor correlated with D_route(y).
  • Unpriced compute: discarding 8% of rollouts and no wall-clock comparison vs R3’s replay bookkeeping.

05 · Questions for further exploration

  1. How does the benefit scale with expert granularity and routing sparsity (K, E, DeepSeek/Llama-4-style MoEs)? Does it survive shared experts or auxiliary router losses?
  2. Would a token- or segment-level drift mask beat the response-level mask — is the whole response stale, or only a high-drift span?
  3. What is the empirical magnitude of b_replay(θ) during training, and does it correlate with D_route(y) as assumed?
  4. Can q be adaptive (fixed drift mass or KL budget) instead of a flat 8%, and how sensitive are AIME gains to q ∈ [0.02, 0.3]?
  5. Does RDC help under engine-consistent bitwise-on-policy training, isolating routing drift from numerical mismatch?
  6. Do gains transfer to code / general RL, and survive multi-seed mean-final (not peak-best) evaluation?
