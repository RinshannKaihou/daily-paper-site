> **一句话总结：** 这篇 KAIST 的可解释性工作区分了语言模型的"内部判断（judgment）"与"表达方式（verdict）"：对同一输入在不同指令下的隐藏态做配对差分，发现差异集中在一个低维"表达子空间"（中位数 rank 8，SVD 估计、完全不用 benchmark 标签），把该子空间从线性读出方向中投影掉得到"隔离判断"——在四个从未参与估计的 held-out 条件（否定问句、No/False/0 表示正确）下，读出 AUROC 从 0.815 跌到 0.650 而隔离判断保持 0.802，在 ANLI 蕴含和 BeaverTails 安全任务上更把否定问句下的 0.504/0.480 拉回 0.827/0.891，且层级分析表明判断在半深度即可读出、破坏只在后段层由"响应语义改变"的方向引入。

> **TL;DR:** When an instruction changes what "Yes" means (No/False/0 means correct, negated question), a linear readout fitted on the model's final hidden state collapses — on the negated correctness question over 14 model–dataset cells (Qwen2.5-7B/14B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B × GSM8K/MMLU/ARC/TriviaQA) AUROC drops from 0.815 to 0.650, and on ANLI (entailment) and BeaverTails (safety) it falls to 0.504 and 0.480, essentially chance. The paper estimates a low-dimensional "expression subspace" by SVD on paired same-input hidden-state differences across instructions, with no benchmark labels, and projects it out of the readout: the resulting isolated judgment recovers 0.802 on the negated correctness question, 0.827 on ANLI and 0.891 on BeaverTails under negation (+0.32 to +0.41 over the frozen readout, within ~0.01–0.02 of a readout fitted with labels on each held-out condition), holds on five prospectively added models up to Qwen2.5-32B, and depth analysis shows judgment is already readable at mid-depth (0.786 AUROC at 0.5 depth) while the meaning-reversal disruption only enters after 0.75 depth. If you care about truth-direction probing, hallucination detection, or robust internal evals, this cleanly separates "the model knows" from "the model says" — with the honest caveat that a supervised pooled baseline matches its held-out numbers, so the win is label-freeness, not accuracy.

## 核心贡献

- **概念区分：判断 vs 表达。** 把模型对输入的内部评估称为"判断（judgment）"，把按指令要求产出的回答称为"裁定（verdict）"；同一判断可被要求表达为 Yes/No、字母、数字或句子，而裁定失准并不意味着判断消失——论文首次针对"逐输入的判断"系统研究这一分离（区别于 Bürger et al. 2024 研究的通用真值方向）。
- **无标签的低维表达子空间。** 对同一输入在两种指令下的隐藏态做配对差分 h_i(a) − h_i(b)，跨输入堆叠做 SVD，取按 split-half 稳定性选秩的前若干方向（各 cell 中位秩 8，范围 6–9）作为表达子空间 E——整个过程不用任何 benchmark 标签。
- **隔离判断及其迁移性。** 用 (I − EE^T)w 把读出方向在 E 中的分量投影掉，得到隔离判断；它在四个从未参与子空间估计的 held-out 条件（否定问句、No/False/0 表示正确）下保持稳定（平均 AUROC 仅掉 0.024，而读出掉 0.129、裁定掉 0.127），且与"在目标条件上直接拟合"的读出差距中位数 < 0.02。
- **时序定位：判断早于表达。** 层级分析显示三条件（Yes/No、No 表示正确、否定问句）的读出在 0.5 深度几乎一致（0.786/0.781/0.783），到 0.9 深度才分裂（0.813 vs 0.648/0.632）；破坏由"改变响应含义"（否定、映射反转）的方向主导，仅换符号（字母↔数字↔句子）几乎无影响，且 3 个否定对方向即可恢复到与全子空间相差 0.005 以内。
- **裁定改变时判断仍可读。** 把读出拟合到模型自己的 Yes/No 裁定后，在否定问句导致两个裁定不一致的样本上，隔离判断仍以 0.953 AUROC 预测原 Yes/No 裁定——改变的是表达而非判断本身。

## Key Contributions

- **A judgment/verdict separation for per-input judgments.** Formalizes the distinction between the model's internal assessment (judgment) and the instruction-conditioned response (verdict), and asks whether the judgment stays recoverable when the verdict becomes unreliable — a different question from prior work on universal truth directions (Bürger et al., 2024) or hallucination probes.
- **A label-free estimator of the expression subspace.** Paired hidden-state differences for the same input under different instructions (five response formats plus entailment/safety affirmation–negation pairs) are centered and stacked; the leading right singular vectors define E. Rank is chosen by a split-half stability rule (every principal angle |cos| ≥ 0.95), giving median rank 8. No benchmark labels enter the estimate.
- **Isolated judgment = readout projected out of E.** w_iso = (I − EE^T)w recovers reliability under instructions never used for estimation: mean AUROC drop across the four held-out conditions is only 0.024, versus 0.129 for the frozen readout and 0.127 for the model's own verdict.
- **Depth-wise and causal localization.** The readout strengthens through the judgment prompt (0.741 → 0.812 → 0.818 AUROC at pre-question/post-question/pre-verdict positions), and steering along it shifts verdict logits by 2.39 per unit under Yes/No in 14/14 cells (vs ~0.0001–0.005 for random or shuffled-label directions). Judgment is readable at mid-depth; meaning-reversal disruption enters only after 0.75 depth, even though condition identity is decodable at 0.99 AUROC before any response token — so the late disruption is specifically the expression component.
- **Prospective replication.** Gemma-2-9B-IT and five more models (up to Qwen2.5-32B and Gemma-2-27B) were run after the method was frozen, with predictions written before extraction; the isolated judgment stays within 0.02 of condition-fitted readouts in every model.

## Methodology

The study covers three binary judgments: answer correctness, entailment, and safety. For correctness, each model (Qwen2.5-7B/14B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 as the four primary models, fourteen primary model–dataset cells over GSM8K, MMLU, ARC-Challenge, TriviaQA; each cell ~800 questions) first greedily generates a candidate answer, then judges it; entailment uses 1,000 balanced ANLI examples (entailment vs contradiction), safety uses 1,000 balanced human-labeled BeaverTails test examples. The input being judged is held fixed while only the instruction varies.

The readout is a class-mean linear probe: standardized final-layer hidden states, direction = positive-minus-negative mean difference, fitted under the Yes/No condition only (tuned logistic probes were rejected because they transfer worse across formats). Evaluation uses five question-disjoint folds with frozen standardization, AUROC against benchmark labels, and question-clustered bootstrap (10,000 resamples) plus cell resampling for aggregates.

The expression subspace is estimated per cell/fold/wording on training questions from paired differences among five response formats (Yes/No, True/False, A/B, B/A, sentence) plus affirmative/negated pairs from entailment and safety inputs; rank set by the split-half rule (median 8). The four evaluation conditions — the negated correctness question and mappings where No, False, or 0 means correct — never enter the estimate. Each condition uses ten matched instruction wordings differing only in the clause specifying response meaning. Key controls: subspaces estimated only from pairs where the model's verdict is identical under both instructions; LEACE/INLP erasure of condition identity; removing leading principal components; random subspaces of equal rank; cross-wording and cross-instruction-family transfer; and a supervised pooled class-mean fitted on labeled A/B and B/A states as an upper reference.

## Results & Highlights

- **Held-out negated correctness question (mean over 14 cells, 5 wordings):** the frozen Yes/No readout falls from 0.815 to 0.650 AUROC (−0.165), while the isolated judgment reaches 0.802, within 0.003 of the condition-fitted readout (0.805). The model's own verdict is 0.665. Under the reversed mappings No-means-correct and False-means-correct, the readout gets 0.640 and 0.665 versus isolated judgments of 0.781 and 0.787 (condition-fitted: 0.805, 0.803).
- **Entailment and safety under negation (4 models, ANLI / BeaverTails):** the readout collapses to 0.504 AUROC on ANLI and 0.480 on BeaverTails (chance level), while isolation lifts these to 0.827 (+0.323) and 0.891 (+0.411) — matching readouts fitted directly on those conditions (0.827 and 0.881). The model's verdicts under negation are only 0.627 (ANLI) and 0.782 (BeaverTails), and drop to ~0.51 when No/False means positive.
- **Stability across the four held-out conditions:** mean AUROC drop is 0.127 for the verdict and 0.129 for the readout, but only 0.024 for the isolated judgment; the isolated-minus-condition-fitted gap has bootstrap intervals within ±0.012 for the negated question and 0-means-correct.
- **Readout is causally linked to the verdict, not just predictive:** adding the readout direction at 0.75 depth shifts the correct-vs-incorrect response logit difference by 2.39 logits per unit dose under Yes/No (14/14 cells), 1.30 under A/B (14/14), 0.46 under B/A (13/14); shuffled-label directions give 0.001–0.005 and random directions ~0.0001. The readout also climbs from 0.741 AUROC before the judgment question to 0.812 after it and 0.818 immediately before the verdict, in all fourteen cells.
- **Depth separation:** at 0.5 depth the readout is nearly identical across Yes/No / No-means-correct / negated question (0.786 / 0.781 / 0.783 AUROC); at 0.9 depth it is 0.813 vs 0.648 and 0.632, while the isolated judgment stays aligned throughout (0.818 under negation at 0.75 depth vs 0.802 at the final layer). The three negation-pair directions alone recover the negated question and reversed mappings to within 0.005 AUROC of the full rank-8 subspace; the leading direction alone does not.
- **Scale and prospective models:** in Qwen2.5-32B the readout falls to 0.42 AUROC under the negated question while the isolated judgment comes within 0.007 of condition-fitted; across five additional models (7B–32B, run post-hoc with pre-written predictions) the isolated-vs-fitted gap is ≤ 0.02 everywhere. Pure symbol changes (letters, digits, sentences) leave the readout within 0.009 AUROC of format-fitted readouts.
- **When verdicts disagree, the judgment persists:** a readout fitted to the model's own Yes/No verdicts, after isolation, tracks that verdict at 0.953 AUROC even on the subset of examples where the negated prompt produces the opposite verdict (median 0.924 overall vs 0.675 for tracking the negated verdict).

## Authors & Credibility

Chaemin Jang, Donggyu Lee, Jeesoo Kim, Dongman Lee (co-corresponding), and Jihee Kim (co-corresponding) from KAIST. This is an interpretability/methods group rather than a big-lab team; the paper reads as careful, methodology-first academic work. Credibility is strengthened by: prospective registration (Gemma-2-9B-IT and five further models were run after the method was fixed, with predictions written before any states were extracted); question-disjoint five-fold evaluation with frozen standardization; 10,000-resample question-clustered bootstrap intervals reported for all headline gaps; an explicit AI-use statement (generative AI assisted code, figures, and prose; authors verified numbers and text); and a reproducibility statement with code, prompts, and per-cell result files at github.com/jchaemin/isolated-judgment, plus a full protocol appendix specifying wordings, folds, and labels.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core idea — paired same-input differences reveal instruction-related directions — is a clean and somewhat novel application to per-input judgments, distinct from Bürger et al.'s general truth direction vs polarity component. However, the ingredients (difference-in-means probing, SVD on paired differences à la CCS/Burns et al., linear concept erasure à la LEACE/INLP) are all standard; the contribution is the framing and the careful demonstration that ~3 negation directions carry most of the disruption.
- **Rigor:** High. Held-out conditions are never used in estimation, folds are question-disjoint, the mean/median reporting quirk is disclosed (readout under negation is 0.650 mean vs 0.694 median), and the controls are unusually thorough (LEACE/INLP erasure of condition identity fails where isolation succeeds: 0.689–0.695 vs 0.772–0.805; removing leading PCs changes the negated-question readout by −0.087 while removing the subspace changes it by +0.093; random equal-rank subspaces do nothing).
- **The honest caveat that undercuts the headline:** a supervised pooled class-mean using labels from A/B and B/A states achieves similar held-out AUROC (0.771–0.804 across the four held-out conditions) — the authors admit the advantage "is not higher held-out AUROC" but label-freeness. So this is a practical-utility contribution, not a capability jump.
- **Cherry-picking check:** the dramatic collapses (0.504/0.480 on ANLI/BeaverTails) are the frozen readout's worst cases; on correctness the readout only drops to 0.650 mean, and the correlation between the readout's drop and the model's rate of applying the inverted mapping (Spearman 0.62 under negation) means the method's benefit is largest exactly where models are most instruction-confused — a confound worth noting, though isolation still works where verdicts stay consistent.
- **Reproducibility:** Good — public code, full protocol, pre-registered replications. But absolute AUROCs are modest and uneven (GSM8K cells ~0.94–0.97 vs MMLU ~0.72), several ARC cells are "descriptive" due to too few negatives, and two ARC cells were excluded from the primary fourteen, so the headline averages rest on a fairly narrow cell base.
- **Red flags:** none serious. Minor: the "judgment preserved" claim is correlational — steering interventions only show the readout direction shifts verdicts; no intervention establishes that the expression subspace causally controls expression while leaving judgment intact. The paper itself flags this as future work.

## Strengths

- Crisp problem statement (judgment vs expression) that reframes what behavioral failures under prompt-format changes actually mean for evaluation and for internal readouts.
- Fully label-free subspace estimation from paired differences, yet performance within 0.01–0.02 of supervised condition-fitted readouts on conditions never seen during estimation.
- Exceptional control battery: verdict-free estimation, LEACE/INLP baselines, PCA and random-subspace controls, cross-wording and cross-instruction-family transfer, and prospective replication on six additional models.
- The depth analysis (judgment readable at 0.5 depth, disruption after 0.75) plus the rank-3 negation-direction ablation gives a genuinely mechanistic-flavored localization of where expression interferes.
- Transparent statistics: bootstrap intervals on all key gaps, per-cell tables in appendices, disclosed mean-vs-median conventions, and pre-written predictions for held-out models.

## Limitations

- Binary judgments only; single-token or short-sentence verdicts; models from 7B to 32B (27B+ run in 4-bit NF4). Open-ended judgments, long generations, and frontier-scale models are untested.
- The separation is representational, not mechanistic: the authors explicitly state they do not decompose the computation, and no causal intervention separates the causal roles of judgment vs expression.
- The supervised pooled baseline matches the held-out numbers, so the method's value is avoiding labels, not improving accuracy — a weaker selling point for users who can afford labeled states.
- Requires white-box hidden-state access (final-layer and mid-layer activations), so it cannot be applied to API-only models.
- Readout AUROCs remain moderate (~0.78–0.89 on most correctness cells), and GSM8K's near-ceiling ~0.96 values suggest some cells have easy label structure that inflates averages.
- Effect sizes depend on the instruction family; on digit mappings (0 means correct) the readout barely degrades (0.787), so the headline recovery matters mainly for word-negation and question-negation conditions.

## Relevance

对研究 LLM 内部表示与可解释性的方向高度相关：它把"真值方向探测/幻觉检测"类工作推进到"逐输入判断与表达分离"的层面，给出的无标签表达子空间与中层即可读的结论，对构建对指令格式鲁棒的内部评估读出、以及判断模型"答错但是否知道对错"都有直接方法学价值；对关注 prompt 敏感性与评测可靠性的工作也是重要的解释性补充。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the expression subspace generalize to open-ended judgments (e.g., grading free-form essays) and multi-turn settings where the verdict is a long generation rather than a single token?
- Can targeted interventions on the expression subspace (steering along or against its directions) causally dissociate judgment from expression, moving from a representational to a mechanistic claim?
- Is the expression subspace shared across models, or even universal across architectures — and do the three negation directions correspond to identifiable circuits or attention heads?
- How does the judgment/expression separation interact with chain-of-thought: does verbalized reasoning before the verdict shift where in depth the disruption appears?
- Could isolating judgment improve practical pipelines like self-consistency or LLM-as-a-judge, e.g., by reading the isolated judgment instead of the verdict when instructions are negation-heavy?
- Since readout degradation correlates with the model's rate of applying inverted mappings (Spearman 0.62), can instruction-tuning targeted at the expression subspace reduce verdict fragility without touching the judgment?
