Causal Syntax Circuits in CodeLMs: Budget and Probe Decide What Looks Localised

Harsh Kumar · Tanmay Shailesh Joshi · Rahul Maity · Anusa Saha (equal contribution) NeurIPS 2026 · LP4FM Workshop 2026-10-12
mechanistic-interpretability code-LLMs activation-patching circuit-discovery steering
◆Key metric · the budget knob
1/15 → 6/15

Settings with a significant, specific circuit after raising the retained-component budget k from 12 to 40 — same models, same pipeline.

Probe knob: swapping the hand-built aligned suite for XLCoST parallel functions collapses measured bias S from ≈1.6 to <0.012 — two orders of magnitude, 10/10 settings.

◆Why this matters

A rare systematic causal study of code-model language preference — and a budget-and-probe warning that directly binds all circuit-discovery and activation-steering research.

◆Critical edge

The aligned prompt suite manufactures its own decision point — the headline ≈1.6 separation is not the size of the bias a developer meets.

01

Methodology

55 settings · 10 models × 3 languages × 2 suites × 2 budgets

Ten open code models — CodeLlama-7B, DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B/6.7B, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B/14B, each base + instruct — probed via TransformerLens hooks, with components addressed uniformly as (kind, layer, head) triples. The metric ΔM reads probability mass on target-syntax opening tokens (fn, mut, :: for Rust) minus Python tokens (def, self) at a scored position; separation S summarises a Python/target prompt pair. Two prompt suites: 64 structurally aligned function pairs (ADD, SQUARE, GREET, IS_EVEN) with explicit language tags, and 128 natural parallel pairs from XLCoST.

Config: seed 0 · 1× A100-80GB · fp16 (bf16 for Qwen) · code, configs and all 55 result files released — an explicit self-correction of the earlier 15-setting version that concluded “not localisable, never steerable”.

Necessity mean ablation

Ablate each component with suite-averaged activations; rank by ΔM drop; keep top k ∈ {12, 40}.

Sufficiency activation patching

Transplant target-run activations of a candidate set into the clean Python run; test whether ΔM crosses zero.

Minimality greedy extraction

Add components ranked by individual patched shift until 90% (τ = 0.90) of the full-set effect.

Specificity gate random controls

Welch t-test vs 30 size-matched random component sets; Benjamini–Hochberg FDR, qBH < 0.05, per probe grid.

Steering difference-in-means

Add direction d = E[acttarget − actbase] at α ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}; track ΔM, argmax token, generation, control contamination.

  • “Uniform failures” sometimes work: patching flips the output language in 2/45 settings — both Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (base/PHP @ k=40: −0.91 → +0.50; instruct/Rust @ k=12: −0.99 → +0.50) — and steering flips the argmax next token in 9/45 (0/15 at k=12, which is what the earlier version reported).
  • Steering is fragile: 3/9 flips occur at α=1, six need α≥2; at α≥4 behaviour degrades — Qwen2.5-Coder-14B/C# injects 126–128 markers into control prompts that should contain none. The safe window is per-setting (one model tolerates α=8/4/0 across Rust/PHP/C#).
  • No compact transferable circuit: surviving circuits share no structure across models (MLP-mediated, spanning L9 → L43); leave-one-out removal of any component leaves marker accuracy/F1 at 1.000; two CodeXGLUE tasks stay pinned at 0.50 chance under steering — surface syntax, not competence.
  • Away-pointing circuits diagnose the search: all five significant reversed circuits come from settings where greedy extraction returned the full candidate set (median shift −0.04 vs +0.05 for compact circuits).
03

Strengths

  • One unified causal pipeline (ablation → patching → extraction → random-control significance → steering) applied identically across 55 settings — the grid itself is the contribution.
  • Every localisation claim gated on 30 size-matched random controls with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction, a standard most circuit papers skip.
  • Exemplary self-correction: openly quantifies how its own earlier “never steerable” verdict was an artifact of k=12 and the aligned suite.
  • Honest intervention reading — metric crossings vs argmax flips vs control contamination, with per-setting safe windows instead of a general steering claim.
  • Clean negative controls: vocabulary-size confound bounded (ρ = +0.20, p = 0.48); CodeXGLUE pinned at chance; full release of code and all 55 result files.
04

Limitations

  • Single seed (0) for everything, including the 30 random control sets.
  • Only two budgets (12, 40) — the advocated “curve over budgets” is a line segment, not a curve; the instruct grid runs only at k=12.
  • The aligned suite rests on four trivial templates (64 highly templated pairs), so the constructed decision point is narrow.
  • Python-only base; targets Rust/C#/PHP only — JavaScript and the Java/C++ ecosystems are absent; C#/PHP markers overlap other C-family languages.
  • XLCoST runs cover only C# and PHP, leaving the probe-collapse evidence at 10 rather than 15 settings; ΔM reads one hand-chosen vocabulary at a single position.
05

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the budget-sensitivity finding persist under sparse-autoencoder feature circuits or edge attribution patching, where the unit of analysis is finer?
  2. Is the probe collapse a property of naturally occurring code positions — or would injected decision points (e.g. after a docstring) recover a measurable preference?
  3. Why does instruction tuning strengthen separation (+12%) — Python-heavy instruction data, and would a balanced multilingual tune shrink it?
  4. What structurally makes Qwen2.5-Coder-7B steerable when CodeLlama and DeepSeek are not, and does the property survive scale (32B, 110B+)?
  5. Can a steering schedule (per-layer α, ITI-style null-space projection) widen the narrow safe window and avoid control contamination at α≥4?
  6. Can “extracted circuit size ≈ full candidate set” be formalised as an automatic validity flag for greedy circuit discovery in other domains?
