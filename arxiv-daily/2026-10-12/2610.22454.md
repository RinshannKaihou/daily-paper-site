> **一句话总结：** 该论文系统定位了 MoE 模型 prefill 阶段削减 routed experts 所造成的损害：在四个模型家族中的三个里，半数专家带来的 F1 损失主要是"答案形式"损坏（模型仍能找到答案但不再简短作答），且集中在极少数 prompt 边界 token 上（Qwen3-30B-A3B 是答案前的两个模板 token，DeepSeek-V2-Lite 是指令所在的头 8-32 个 token）；据此提出把约 4% 的 full-expert 预算放在任务接口（instruction/question/模板）上即可把 F1 保持在与原模型 1 分以内，而同样预算随机撒在文档里要差约 3 分。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author mechanistic study of MoE prefill expert reduction (K → k′, e.g. 8 → 4 routed experts for prompt tokens only). On HotpotQA (distractor setting, ~1,800-token prompts), uniform 8 → 4 reduction costs Qwen3-30B-A3B 3.3–3.5 F1 points, but the loss is mostly answer *form* (precision −4.87 vs recall −1.09; answers lengthen from 2.33 to 2.49 words) and is made at the two chat-template tokens before the answer: keeping just those two full shrinks the loss from −3.38 to −1.06. Against the attention-sink/super-expert hypothesis, restoring super experts or massive activations recovers at most ~8–25% of the NLL damage while keeping the sensitive positions full recovers +49% (Qwen3) / +22% (DeepSeek). On real marked prompts, an "Interface-Full" rule (~4% of tokens full, 52% of routed-expert slots) stays within one F1 point of the unmodified model (+0.46 [−0.62, +1.57]) and beats scattering the same budget randomly in documents by +3.22/+3.05 points (two prespecified primaries met); but rules keeping the prompt ends (KeepEnds 32+32) are statistically indistinguishable from it, and TTFT drops only ~14% on 11K-token prompts with no saving at 2K. Read it for the localisation methodology and honest prespecified statistics, not for a new speedup method.

## 核心贡献

- **损害定位（What）：** 在四个 MoE 家族（Qwen3-30B-A3B、DeepSeek-V2-Lite、Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B、OLMoE-1B-7B）上，用 token 级/层级/注意力路径干预证明：半数专家削减造成的短答案 QA F1 损失在三个家族中主要是**答案形式**（answer form）损坏——模型仍找到答案（recall 仅 −1.09）但不再简短作答（precision −4.87），GSM8K 上 "####" 结尾标记的遵守率从 97.7% 跌到 84.1%。
- **位置定位（Where）：** Qwen3 的损失几乎全部由答案前的两个模板 token（空 think 块的闭合标签和空行）造成——单独把这两个 token 降到 2 专家即损失 −8.07 F1；DeepSeek-V2-Lite 的损失位于其标准布局的头 8-32 个 token（指令所在处），保持头 32 个 token full 可完全恢复 +8.33 分；Qwen1.5 在最后两个 token，OLMoE 分布在两端。关键不变量是"紧邻答案边界的 token"，而非某个特定模板。
- **机制排查（Why）：** 通过注意力分析、路径阻断和层级修复，证据与"中间层（20-27 层）expert 把'如何作答'的指令写入边界 token、答案 token 再读取"的图景一致；同时用预注册的直接测量排除了 attention sink / super expert / massive activation 被破坏的假说——首 token sink 统计量变化 <4%，恢复 super expert 或 massive activation 最多只挽回约四分之一的损失。
- **预算放置原则（So what）：** 在真实带标记 prompt 上，等量 full-expert 预算放在**任务接口**（system message、instruction、示例、问题、chat 模板，平均约 4% token）能避免损失，随机撒在文档中则几乎无效（两个预注册主比较成立：+3.22 和 +3.05 分）；但在作者测试的所有模板里敏感 token 都位于 prompt 两端，因此"保两端"的免标记规则与"读应用标记"的规则**无法区分**——结论是放置原则而非新选择器。
- **方法论标杆：** 四个批次预注册（primary/secondary 比较与决策规则先写入分析脚本）、成对 bootstrap 区间、Holm 校正、以及 full-budget 代码路径对照和 unmarked-prompt 对照等额外控制，明确标注哪些比较是 post hoc。

## Key Contributions

- **Localisation of what is lost.** Across four MoE families, halving routed experts for prompt tokens mostly damages answer *form* on short-answer QA, not content: with uniform 8 → 4 on Qwen3-30B-A3B (HotpotQA, 2,000 questions), precision falls −4.87 while recall falls only −1.09 and answers lengthen (2.33 → 2.49 words); on GSM8K, only 84.1% of outputs still end with the required "####" marker under 8 → 2 uniform reduction vs 97.7% when the last two prompt tokens stay full.
- **Localisation of where.** In Qwen3 the F1 damage is made at the two chat-template tokens immediately before the answer (the empty-reasoning-block closing tag and following blank line): reducing only those two tokens to 2 experts costs −8.07 F1 while the six tokens before them cost −0.70; keeping them full shrinks the uniform loss from −3.38 to −1.06. In DeepSeek-V2-Lite the damage sits at the first 8–32 tokens where the instruction lives (keeping the first 32 full recovers +8.33 [+5.63, +11.21] F1 vs uniform on 400 questions); Qwen1.5-MoE at its last two tokens (a content/containment loss), OLMoE across both ends. The effect follows the *answer boundary*, not any one template: with a base model ending in "Answer:" it still appears (−5.69), and with reasoning enabled (generated tokens always run full experts) it vanishes (−0.12 [−1.23, +1.01]) — until the same reasoning trace is replayed as a prompt (−10.62).
- **Mechanism ruled down to "instruction carried by boundary tokens", with sinks excluded.** Path blocking shows the first answer token depends on the last two prompt tokens seeing the instruction (+6.21 nats) and documents (+3.35), and the stop token depends on the answer seeing those two tokens (+20.70 nats); layer repair localises to middle bands 20–27 (each leaves 46% of damage). Prespecified direct tests find the first-token sink's attention mass, norm and largest activation change by ≤3.2% under uniform reduction, and restoring super experts (+8% [−11, +24] recovery on Qwen3) or massive activations (+6%) explains far less of the NLL increase than keeping the sensitive positions full (+49% [+30, +65] Qwen3; +22% [+10, +33] DeepSeek).
- **A placement principle from equal-budget, prespecified tests.** On real prompts where the application marks documents, Interface-Full (only marked documents reduced; ~4% of tokens full, 52% of unmodified routed-expert slots) is within one F1 point of the unmodified model (+0.46 [−0.62, +1.57] and +0.76 [−0.49, +2.06]) and beats the negative control with the same budget scattered inside documents by +3.22 [+1.74, +4.73] and +3.05 [+1.47, +4.68] (two of five prespecified primaries met; per-placement +2.33 to +3.88 across seven placements). Because the sensitive tokens always lay at a prompt end in every layout tested (DeepSeek's loss disappears once its instruction moves off the start), mark-reading rules are not distinguished from KeepEnds-style positional rules.
- **Serving reality check.** The saving is length- and model-dependent: median TTFT ratio 0.86 on 10.9K-token LongBench prompts but 1.02 (slightly slower) at ~2K tokens, throughput +1.20–1.22× at concurrency four on Qwen3-30B-A3B, and the same implementation is *slower* on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (0.863–0.932 of unmodified throughput).

## Methodology

The paper studies **prefill expert reduction**: each prompt token keeps only its k′ highest-weight routed experts (K → k′, weights unchanged), generated tokens always run all K, shared experts are never reduced; the last prompt position computes the first answer token's logits, so reducing it also reduces the first decoding step. Unless stated, reduction is to half the experts (8 → 4 for Qwen3-30B-A3B, 6 → 3 for DeepSeek-V2-Lite); single-token isolation experiments use 8 → 2.

The analysis task is HotpotQA (distractor setting): an instruction to give only the short answer, two demonstrations, ten paragraphs and the question (~1,800 prompt tokens), F1-scored with greedy decoding, reasoning disabled. The paper proceeds in three tiers:

1. **Token/span/layer/attention interventions** (HuggingFace transformers with forward hooks on MoE blocks, eager attention; validated to reproduce the vLLM engine's results, e.g. −8.02 vs −8.05 for the same intervention). F1 is decomposed into precision, recall and answer length to separate *form* from *content* losses; spans are reduced or kept full in isolation; attention paths are blocked (hiding one span from another) and single 4-layer bands are restored to full experts ("layer repair").
2. **Mechanism tests against sink hypotheses** in a prespecified 200-question batch: super experts identified per Su et al., massive activations per Sun et al.; conditions scored by the share of the uniform-reduction increase in answer NLL (of the unmodified model's own answer) that they remove — a far less noisy metric than F1 at this scale.
3. **Equal-budget placement batches** in vLLM 0.31 (760-prompt joint batch + three quality-only prespecified batches of ~2,098–2,100 prompts on real marked prompts spanning HotpotQA layouts, six LongBench QA sets, LCC and RepoBench-P code completion). Rules compared: Uniform, KeepEnds (32+32), Template role, Interface-Full, three equal-budget positional controls (ends / random / span), a negative control (same budget placed randomly inside documents ≥32 tokens from either end), and an inverted control (only the interface reduced). Every rule is one vLLM hook on the fused-MoE layer; masks are verified per-prompt against the server's token ids.

Statistics are unusually careful for this genre: differences paired per question with their reference, 95% (sometimes 90%) bootstrap intervals over questions, Holm correction for families of secondary comparisons, and — for four batches — prompts, conditions, primary/secondary comparisons and decision rules written into the analysis script *before* the run (prespecification, no external registry; post-hoc comparisons are labelled). A full-budget control isolates the code path's numerical effect and an unmarked-prompt control isolates the effect of the marks themselves.

## Results & Highlights

- **The F1 loss is mostly form, made at two tokens (Qwen3).** Uniform 8 → 4 on HotpotQA (2,000 questions) costs −3.38 F1; keeping only the last two prompt tokens full shrinks this to −1.06 [−2.10, −0.01], while eight full tokens at the start (−2.78) or at random positions (−3.27) recover little. At 8 → 2, reducing the last eight tokens alone costs −8.02 F1 and the last two alone cost the same (−8.07), with precision falling 10.31 points vs recall 0.53 when only the last eight are reduced — answers double in length.
- **DeepSeek's loss is at the prompt start.** Uniform 6 → 3 costs −8.87 F1 [−10.38, −7.37]; reducing only the first 20 tokens reproduces it (−9.45). A prefix sweep (400 questions, uniform loss 6.79 in that batch) shows keeping the first 1 / 8 / 32 tokens full recovers −0.42 / +3.80 [+1.88, +5.88] / +8.33 [+5.63, +11.21] F1 against uniform. With 32 tokens full at each end, none of the four model families shows a detectable HotpotQA loss (Qwen3 −0.41, DeepSeek +0.95, Qwen1.5 containment +0.05, OLMoE −0.94).
- **Sinks/super experts are not the explanation.** Answer NLL under uniform reduction rises from 0.22 to 1.64 nats (Qwen3) and 1.10 to 2.64 (DeepSeek); hiding the first token (a working sink) raises NLL by 40.6 / 1.1 nats, yet reduction changes its attention/norm/largest activation by ≤3.2%. Restoring super experts removes +8% [−11, +24] of the NLL increase (Qwen3) and +1% [−4, +6] (DeepSeek), versus +49% [+30, +65] and +22% [+10, +33] for keeping the sensitive positions full — 20–43 points more than any restoration.
- **Placement beats scattering on real prompts.** Uniform 8 → 4 costs 1.8–3.5 F1 points on Qwen3 across batches; Interface-Full vs unmodified is +0.46 [−0.62, +1.57] (negative-control batch) and +0.76 [−0.49, +2.06] (placement batch); Interface-Full minus the equal-budget negative control is +3.22 [+1.74, +4.73] and +3.05 [+1.47, +4.68] (both prespecified primaries met the ≥2-point / half-the-gain rule). On DeepSeek the negative control is 7.92–8.09 F1 points below Interface-Full, entirely via answer length (containment is if anything higher).
- **Caveats surfaced by the paper's own controls.** KeepEnds (32+32) is within ±1.1 points (90% intervals) of Interface-Full in the quality-only batches — marks and positions are not distinguished; on 2,000 long-system HotpotQA questions (700-token system prompt) KeepEnds loses −1.55 [−2.50, −0.60] while Interface-Full stays close; Interface-Full itself is 1.6–2.4 points below the unmodified model on question-first LongBench and code completion. TTFT ratio is 0.86 [0.86, 0.87] on ~11K-token prompts (uniform: 0.85) but 1.02 at ~2K tokens, and throughput at concurrency four rises only 1.20–1.22×.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Zhiyuan Wang, listed as Independent Researcher (email at sjtu.edu.cn); arXiv preprint (cs.CL), no peer review, no institutional affiliation or code release mentioned in the main text. The work is unusually methodologically careful for a solo preprint: four prespecified batches with decision rules written into analysis scripts before runs, paired bootstrap intervals, Holm correction, a "sources of evidence" appendix (Table 7) mapping every headline claim to its model/engine/GPU/mask, and explicit labels for post-hoc comparisons. Experiments span single rented GPUs (RTX PRO 6000, H100, H200, A100), vLLM 0.10.2/0.31 and HuggingFace pipelines, with a validation that the HF path reproduces the engine (−8.02 vs −8.05 F1 for the same intervention). Compute scale is modest but adequate (200–6,000 questions per condition, models up to 30B plus a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B serving check). The self-critical Limitations section and the honest reporting of undecided/negative primaries (2 of 5 met) increase confidence; the absence of code or an external registry reduces it.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: good, incremental.** The observation that prefill expert pruning hurts answer form is anticipated by SlimWise (cited as noting accuracy can hide pruning-induced generation-length changes); the paper's real contribution is the precise token-level localisation and the clean exclusion of sink/super-expert explanations, which is new. It explicitly frames itself as a localisation study ("a placement principle rather than a new selector"), which is honest.
- **Rigor: high on statistics, mixed on mechanism.** Prespecification, equal-budget controls, the full-budget code-path control and the unmarked-prompt control are better practice than most published serving papers. But several mechanism probes are uninterpretable: partial repairs are non-monotone (three repairs *increase* damage above 100%), and the key clean-keys-and-values condition that would separate the writer from the reader of the boundary channel fails outright in Qwen3 (NLL rises to 6.68, far above uniform's 1.64) — the authors correctly refuse to interpret it, but that leaves the mechanism account "consistent with, not uniquely identified by" the evidence, as they concede.
- **Reproducibility: moderate.** No code release stated; single-author work on rented GPUs; results depend on exact chat templates and vLLM versions (vLLM 0.10.2 vs 0.31 batches give different loss sizes for the same rule — e.g. KeepEnds on long-system prompts: −3.51 vs −1.55/−1.03), and outputs do not reproduce exactly across GPU types (95.7–96.5% vs a 99% merge threshold). Batch-to-batch loss sizes vary (uniform costs −3.51, −2.15, −1.83 on Qwen3 across three batches), which the paper flags but which complicates external replication.
- **Significance: narrow but real.** The headline localisation is established on one task paradigm — short-answer QA with reasoning *off* and ~1,800-token prompts — with F1, a length-sensitive metric, as the primary measure; the "form loss" framing partially rests on that metric choice (mitigated by containment, GSM8K marker rates, and NLL analyses). The practical payoff is modest: TTFT improves only on long prompts (14% at 11K, nothing at 2K, unexplained and contrary to the multiply-add model's prediction of +41% at 2K), and the same implementation is *slower* on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.
- **Red flags / honest weaknesses worth noting.** (1) Only aggressive reduction settings (half experts, or 8 → 2) were tested — milder pruning known to suffice (Chen et al.: two thirds of chosen experts) is out of scope, so the damage estimates may overstate what practical systems would incur. (2) The central practical claim "marks beat positions" is directly *not supported* by the paper's own data: the Interface-Full vs KeepEnds/equal-budget-ends comparisons are all undecided, and the one prespecified test designed to separate them (DeepSeek, instruction mid-document) was uninformative because that layout showed no loss to recover. (3) Placement variance (two random placements on identical prompts/GPU differ by 1.8 points) is as large as several reported effect sizes. (4) All sensitive layouts happened to place sensitive tokens at prompt ends — a confound the paper handles honestly but which limits generalisation to tool-use/multi-turn layouts where the interface is not at an end (Interface-Full already loses 1.6–2.4 points there, unlocalised). (5) Latency from one request at a time without prefix caching on single GPUs; models above 35B untested.

## Strengths

- A clean three-tier experimental design (what is lost → where → why) that connects an interpretabilities-style token-level analysis all the way to a serving-level rule on real marked prompts in vLLM.
- Statistical hygiene well above the norm: prespecified primaries with decision rules, paired bootstrap intervals, Holm correction, negative/inverted/full-budget/unmarked controls, and an appendix table attributing every claim to its exact batch, engine and GPU.
- Genuine falsification work: the sink/super-expert/massive-activation hypotheses — the obvious explanations given prior work — are tested directly and excluded with prespecified rules, rather than being gestured at.
- The form-vs-content decomposition (precision/recall/answer length, containment, GSM8K "####" marker rate) is a simple, reusable diagnostic that other pruning papers should copy.
- Unusual candour: negative, undecided and post-hoc results are reported with the same prominence as confirmatory ones, and the abstract itself states that only two of five prespecified primaries were met.

## Limitations

- Task and metric narrowness: short-answer QA (mainly HotpotQA distractor) with reasoning disabled; F1 is length-sensitive and the central "form loss" partly depends on this; GSM8K, code completion and reasoning-on runs are checks only. In answer NLL the boundary positions account for only about half (Qwen3) or a fifth (DeepSeek) of the damage.
- Only halving (and 8 → 2) tested — no milder reduction rates; models only up to 30B parameters (plus one serving check on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B).
- The mechanism is traced only in Qwen3, on unmarked templates, with interventions that do not identify it uniquely and whose partial repairs are non-monotone; the last position's own computation of the first answer token is not separated from what later tokens read.
- Direct sink tests use 200 questions and layer/head averages that could hide single-head changes; Qwen3's sink-test rows rest on NLL alone.
- Equal-budget evidence comes from Qwen3 and DeepSeek-V2-Lite only; placement variance (~1.8 points between placements) is comparable to headline effects and not included in the intervals; the amount of budget was never varied.
- Interface-Full leaves an unlocated residual loss of up to 2.4 points on question-first, tool-output and code layouts; a fixed 32+32 rule loses form on long system prompts.
- Serving results are from single GPUs, one request at a time, without prefix caching; the absence of any TTFT saving at 2K tokens (despite the multiply-add model predicting ~+41% throughput) and the Qwen3.6 slowdown are explicitly unexplained.

## Relevance

该论文直接服务于 MoE 高效推理/服务这一研究方向：它为 prefill 阶段 expert 剪枝（含 ExFold、SlimWise、MC-MoE 一类方法）提供了损失机理的精确定位——少数边界 token 携带"如何作答"的指令——并给出可落地的放置原则（保住任务接口/两端 token），对长上下文 RAG 与 agentic 场景的 MoE 部署具有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the boundary-token localisation survive milder reduction (e.g. keeping 2/3 of experts), larger models, or reasoning-enabled workloads where generated tokens always run full experts — and does the effect reappear when reasoning traces are cached/replayed as prompts?
- Can the "instruction carried by middle-layer experts into boundary tokens" account be uniquely identified — e.g. with causal scrubbing or activation patching that avoids the non-monotone mixed-state failures the clean-keys-and-values patch hit in Qwen3?
- Where does Interface-Full's residual 1.6–2.4-point loss on question-first, tool-output and code-completion layouts come from, and does a budget on gold-paragraph-style content tokens (which the base-model interventions suggest matter more than distractors) close it without gold labels?
- Is the negative control's failure purely positional, or do document-internal full tokens actively harm by creating inconsistent expert-width states across positions — and how does the answer change when the budget amount, not just placement, is varied?
- Does the form-loss diagnostic (precision/recall split + instruction-marker compliance) transfer to other serving-time perturbations — quantization, KV-cache compression, speculative decoding — as a cheap early-warning signal?
