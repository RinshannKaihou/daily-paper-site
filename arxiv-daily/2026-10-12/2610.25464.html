Key metric — the causal null

0/ 236

steering configurations — probe, CAA, or directly-optimized vectors across heads, layers, positions, strengths and schedules — consistently beat the unsteered baseline, over 68,096 paired generations. Best case flips 5 of 256 problems.

Why this matters

A causal stress-test of the linear representation hypothesis: signals interpretability can read from hidden states may not be usable to steer reasoning.

Critical edge

Everything rests on one 3M-parameter, SFT-trained model and one synthetic arithmetic task — the null may reflect scale or training regime, not reasoning itself.

Methodology

Decodability study followed by a causal intervention study — both tractable at every token position.

01

ChainSearch testbed

Countdown-style arithmetic search (6 numbers → target 100–999) with explicit BACK (restore an earlier state), SUCCESS, and EXPR tokens — correctness becomes exactly labelable at every position. Traces from a modified A* with human-inspired, deliberately non-admissible heuristics: 1.02M SFT + 6.9M pretraining traces, target-disjoint splits to stop near-duplicate leakage.

02

Subject model

A 3M-parameter decoder-only transformer trained from scratch — strong enough to reason (71.12%), weak enough to fail 29% of the time, supplying balanced positive/negative probing data.

03

Four-axis direction search

Label (state reachability vs final-answer correctness) × semantic token position × construction (linear probe / CAA mean-difference / directly optimized vector) × component (22 heads, residual stream, MLP output, final hidden state), summarized by five stability metrics.

04

Paired causal steering

Each steered generation paired with an unsteered run of the same problem; configurations selected on validation, staged evaluation escalating 256 → 512 → 16k traces. Outcomes: next-state reachability and final-answer correctness.

Results

Beyond the headline null — what the 68k-generation sweep actually showed.

Decodability is strong

~0.94 AUROC decoding final-answer correctness by end of generation on the 16,114-trace held-out split — across attention heads, residual streams, MLP outputs, and the final hidden state. Not driven by heavy-backtracking traces.

Geometry & prior work

  • Cross-label agreement is only moderate: reachability vs correctness directions align at cosine ~0.5 (≈60°) even at the best attention heads; CAA is the most stable construction.
  • Reproduction finding: 56.7% of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B’s AIME 2025 traces exceed the 10,000-token cap used by Zhang et al. (2025) — over half the evidence for “reasoning models know when they’re right” was never examined.

Strengths

  • Exact ground truth: reachability labels at every state turn “do models know when they’re right?” into a fully controlled experiment.
  • Direct Optimization control is the smartest move — it refutes the cheap excuse that probe/CAA vectors were never trained to be causal.
  • Bidirectional reporting of improved and worsened counts across 236 configurations makes the null hard to blame on under-searching.
  • Target-disjoint splits deftly kill the near-duplicate-trace leakage that plagues synthetic-task evaluation.
  • The critique of prior work is backed by an own reproduction number, not hand-waving.

Limitations

  • Single 3M SFT model, single synthetic task — no evidence yet the pattern transfers to billion-parameter RL-trained reasoners on GSM8K/AIME-style tasks.
  • The 0.94 AUROC signal conflates self-monitoring with problem difficulty; no experiment separates the two.
  • No nonlinear baseline — the suggested explanation (linear geometry is the wrong shape) remains conjecture.
  • Headline flip counts rest on the smaller staged splits (256/512 problems); escalation to 16k only happens for promising conditions.
  • A*-shaped backtracking distribution; no ablation over data-generation strategy.

Questions for further exploration

  • Does decodable-but-not-steerable replicate at 1B+ scale on RL-trained reasoning models, or is it an artifact of 3M-parameter SFT?
  • Would a nonlinear intervention — SAE feature, MLP controller, low-rank subspace — succeed where single-vector steering fails?
  • Can the ~0.94 AUROC signal be decomposed into problem difficulty vs on-track-ness?
  • If correctness directions can’t steer generation, are they still useful non-causally — as backtracking triggers or early-exit verification signals?
  • Does Direct Optimization fail from teacher-forced training vs free-running evaluation — would rollout-optimized vectors recover causal control?
