> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一个"元评测"框架，用单调性、非随机性、排名一致性三个元指标系统性比较 8 个评测设置维度（shot 数、prompt 聚合、任务表述、归一化、指标等），在 26 个单语 2B 模型（4 种语言、80 个任务）和 3 个更大规模 LLM 上发现：答案概率（answer probability）配字符归一化与 5-shot 提示是最稳的选择（2B 上 AnsProb 以 93% 胜率碾压 accuracy 的 6%），而任务表述和指标的最佳选择会随模型规模改变；两个"都合理"的评测设置能让同一批模型的排名几乎完全颠倒（Kendall's τ = −0.6）。

> **TL;DR:** A meta-evaluation study from the University of Oslo asking which benchmarking setup extracts the most reliable signal from *base* (pretraining) LLMs. Across 26 monolingual 2B models (11 checkpoints each, 2B–100B training tokens) evaluated on 80 tasks in Norwegian, Finnish, French, and Spanish (NorEval, FIN-bench-v2, FrenchBench, SpanishBench) — plus three larger LLMs (OLMo 3 7B, Apertus 8B, Prelude 9B) — the authors score every combination of 8 setup aspects (1,488 classification setups) on monotonicity, non-randomness, and ranking consistency. Headline findings: answer probability beats accuracy as a metric (93% vs 6% pairwise win rate at 2B scale), 5-shot beats 0-shot (70% win rate), and cloze formulation beats multiple-choice at small scale (76% vs 20%) — but accuracy (6% → 37%) and MCF (20% → 38%) become far more competitive at ≥7B scale. Two equally defensible setups can rank the same six Norwegian 2B models in nearly reverse order (Kendall's τ = −0.6), flipping which pretraining corpus looks best.

## 核心贡献

- **提出并落地了一个"元评测"框架**：将单调性（分数随训练推进上升）、非随机性（与随机基线的间隔除以噪声）、排名一致性（相邻 checkpoint 间模型排名的 Kendall's τ 稳定性）三个已有信号准则重新用作**规范性元指标**，用于系统性选择评测设置而非只用于选任务。
- **系统覆盖 8 个评测维度**：shot 数（0/5-shot）、prompt 聚合（单 prompt / 多 prompt 取均值 / 取最大）、分数重标定（baseline / z-score / min-max / 无）、跨任务聚合（任务级均值 / 类别级均值）、任务表述（cloze / 多选 MCF / hybrid）、对数概率归一化（无 / 字符长度 / PMI）、指标（accuracy / CondProb / AnsProb）、解码（贪心 / 随机采样）。
- **大规模受控实验**：26 个共享架构与训练配置、仅预训练数据不同的单语 2B 模型（挪威语、芬兰语、法语、西班牙语；原生网页语料 FineWeb2、HPLT 2.0/3.0、MADLAD-400 与机器翻译语料 Tower-9B/72B、OPUS-MT），每模型 11 个 checkpoint，共约 11,000 GPU 小时；另用三个更大 LLM（OLMo 3 7B、Apertus 8B、Prelude 9B，约 7,000 GPU 小时）验证规模泛化。
- **扩展了 NorEval 基准**：为 14 个挪威语多选任务补充了 CF/MCF/hybrid 三种表述、字符/PMI 归一化、CondProb/AnsProb 概率指标与随机采样，使挪威语成为唯一可研究全部 8 个维度的语言。
- **给出分层实践配方**：高影响选择（AnsProb 指标、字符归一化、5-shot、小模型用 CF 表述）、低影响选择（多 prompt 优于单 prompt，但 max/mean 聚合差别小；任务级 vs 类别级聚合差别小）、依赖上下文的选择（重标定、解码策略），并展示"逐维选最优"拼出的设置在 264 个跨语言共享设置中排第 6（总分 0.93 vs 最优完整设置的 0.95）。

## Key Contributions

- **A meta-evaluation framework for choosing evaluation setups**: repurposes three existing signal criteria — monotonicity (Spearman ρ between training tokens and score), non-randomness (gap between best final score and random baseline in units of checkpoint noise), and ranking consistency (Kendall's τ between adjacent-checkpoint model rankings) — as normative meta-measures for selecting *how* to evaluate, not just *what* tasks to include.
- **Systematic coverage of 8 setup aspects**: shots (0/5-shot), prompt aggregation (single / multi-mean / multi-max), score rescaling (baseline / z-score / min-max / none), cross-task aggregation (task-level mean / category mean), task formulation (cloze CF / multiple-choice MCF / hybrid), log-probability normalization (none / character-length / PMI), metric (accuracy / conditional answer probability / answer probability), and decoding (greedy / random sampling).
- **A controlled model grid at scale**: 26 monolingual 2B-parameter models sharing architecture and training recipe but differing only in pretraining corpus (native web: FineWeb2, HPLT 2.0, HPLT 3.0, MADLAD-400; machine-translated: Tower-9B, Tower-72B, OPUS-MT), 11 checkpoints each from 2B to 100B tokens, evaluated on 80 tasks in 4 languages (~11,000 GPU hours), plus validation on three larger multilingual LLMs (OLMo 3 7B, Apertus 8B, Prelude 9B; ~7,000 GPU hours).
- **Benchmark extension**: NorEval's 14 multiple-choice tasks extended with CF/MCF/hybrid formulations, character/PMI normalizations, CondProb/AnsProb metrics, and temperature-1 random sampling — making Norwegian the only language where all 8 aspects can be studied.
- **A tiered, actionable recipe**: high-impact choices (AnsProb + character normalization + 5-shot + CF for small models), low-impact choices (any multi-prompt aggregation, either task aggregation), and context-dependent choices (rescaling, decoding) that should be re-assessed as scale grows; the recipe-assembled setup scores 0.93 vs 0.95 for the jointly-best complete setup (rank 6 of 264), showing per-aspect selection costs almost nothing.

## Methodology

The paper enumerates 1,488 classification setups (and 96 generative setups for Norwegian) as combinations of options across the 8 aspects, runs every setup over all checkpoints of all models, and scores each setup with three meta-measures:

1. **Monotonicity**: per model, the Spearman rank correlation ρ between training-token count and aggregate score across its 11 checkpoints, averaged over models — a good setup's scores should rise with training.
2. **Non-randomness**: (max final score − score of a synthetic random model) / noise, where noise is each model's sample standard deviation over its last K = 5 checkpoints, following FineWeb2's definition. Unit-free, so comparable across rescalings.
3. **Ranking consistency**: average Kendall's τ between model rankings at adjacent checkpoints within token-aligned training windows (half the smallest budget; first window ignored).

The three meta-measures are converted to percentile ranks and averaged into an overall 0–1 score per setup and language. Three complementary decision procedures are used: the **best complete setup** (joint argmax, captures aspect interactions), **option ranking** (mean overall score of all setups containing an option, i.e., its average effect across all combinations of other aspects), and **per-option win rate** (paired comparisons of setups differing in exactly one aspect, ties counting half). Choices are then tiered by cross-method and cross-language agreement.

Experimental substrate: for each language, an established benchmark is used — NorEval (Norwegian, 35 tasks, 17 MC + 18 generative, extended as above), FIN-bench-v2 (Finnish, 14 tasks, CF/MCF variants for 10 MC tasks), FrenchBench + Global-MMLU + INCLUDE (French, 16 tasks), SpanishBench/IberoBench multi-prompt version (Spanish, 16 tasks). Evaluations use LM Evaluation Harness with a vLLM backend on GH200 GPUs, fixed seeds for few-shot example selection. Scale generalization is tested on intermediate checkpoints of OLMo 3 7B (10 checkpoints, up to 6T tokens), Apertus 8B (14, up to 15T), and Prelude 9B (45, up to 8T).

## Results & Highlights

- **Evaluation setup choice can invert model rankings.** On NorEval classification tasks (Figure 1), two defensible setups — both 5-shot with max-over-prompts — rank six Norwegian 2B models largely in *reverse* order at the end of training (Kendall's τ = −0.6): MADLAD-400 ranks highest under setup A (baseline rescaling, cloze formulation, accuracy) but lowest under setup B (z-score rescaling, MCF, answer probability), while machine-translated corpora (OPUS, Tower-9B) jump from bottom to top.
- **Answer probability is by far the most informative metric at 2B scale**: AnsProb wins 93% of pairwise comparisons pooled across meta-measures (monotonicity 92%, non-randomness 95%, ranking consistency 92%), vs CondProb at 63% and accuracy at just 6% (9% monotonicity) — discrete accuracy inflates variance while giving almost no early-training signal. At ≥7B scale AnsProb's win rate drops to 63% while accuracy's rises to 37% (monotonicity 9% → 53%).
- **5-shot prompting beats 0-shot with a 70% overall win rate** (84% on monotonicity) for the 2B models, and remains the unanimous choice of all three selection methods in 4/4 languages at both scales.
- **Cloze formulation dominates at small scale (76% win rate vs 20% for MCF and 51% for hybrid)**, consistent with small base models not having learned the multiple-choice format; at larger scale CF falls to 61% while MCF improves to 38% (ranking consistency 33% → 71%, monotonicity 2% → 30%), and for Norwegian the larger models' methods select the hybrid formulation.
- **Character-length normalization wins in every language except Spanish** (55% overall win rate, driven mainly by non-randomness), while PMI shows the highest non-randomness (66%) but the lowest monotonicity (18%); at larger scale PMI's non-randomness advantage collapses (66% → 17%).
- **Multiple prompts beat a single prompt, but max vs mean aggregation barely matters**: single-prompt win rate is only 37% (2B) / 35% (larger), while multi-max (58%/59%) vs multi-mean (53%/52%) differ mainly in which meta-measure they favor — max gives higher non-randomness (80% of 672 pairs), mean higher monotonicity (58%). The authors show part of max's advantage is *mechanical*: at the first checkpoint (2B tokens), max-accuracy already exceeds mean-accuracy by 3.0 points on average (vs a 5.3-point gap at the end of training).
- **Rescaling is the most context-dependent choice**: no language's three selection methods agree on baseline rescaling; at larger scale z-score gains (39% → 49% win rate) and appears in the best complete setup, while win rates slightly favor no rescaling (56%) over baseline (54%).
- **Cross-scale transfer is only moderate**: overall setup scores correlate between 2B and larger models at Spearman ρ = 0.66 (Finnish), 0.41 (Spanish), 0.32 (French), 0.30 (Norwegian) — high agreement at the top of the ranking, substantial reordering in the middle.
- **The tiered recipe is nearly free**: assembling the per-aspect best options (5-shot, multi-max, baseline rescaling, task-level mean, CF, AnsProb, char normalization) yields a setup ranked 6th of 264 cross-language setups (overall 0.93) vs 0.95 for the best complete setup, which differs only in using min-max rescaling.
- **For Norwegian generative tasks, random sampling beats greedy decoding** (option-ranking score 0.56 vs 0.45; 64% win rate), though the best complete setup there uses greedy.

## Authors & Credibility

All five authors are from the Department of Informatics at the University of Oslo (Shenbin Qian, David Samuel, Jindřich Helcl, Andrey Kutuzov, Stephan Oepen), a group deeply embedded in the European multilingual-LLM ecosystem: they are contributors to NorEval, FIN-bench-v2, HPLT 3.0, and the OpenEuroLLM project (which funded the work under EU Digital Europe grant 101195233, with a Marie Skłodowska-Curie fellowship and LUMI supercomputer access). This is a credible team for a multilingual evaluation methodology study, and the infrastructure (26 freshly trained 2B models with identical recipes, ~18,000 total GPU hours) is far beyond what a typical methodology paper invests. The work is a careful formalization and generalization of practices informally used in FineWeb2/FineTasks and DCLM (monotonicity, non-randomness, ranking consistency), and extends the OLMES standard's spirit from English to multilingual base-model evaluation. An ethics statement and an explicit AI-use disclosure are included.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Moderate but genuinely useful. The individual meta-measures are borrowed (FineTasks, FineWeb2, Madaan et al. 2025), as are the setup aspects (OLMES standardized several of them). The contribution is the *joint, systematic* comparison of complete setups across 8 aspects with three agreement-based decision procedures — nobody had done this at this scale, and the Kendall's τ = −0.6 reversal experiment is a compelling motivation. It is more of a rigorous empirical meta-study than a methodological breakthrough.
- **Rigor**: Strong within its scope — controlled model grid, three complementary selection methods, an honest Appendix E dissecting *where and why* the meta-measures disagree, and an explicit demonstration that multi-max's non-randomness advantage is partly a mechanical artifact of taking a maximum (3.0-point inflation at 2B tokens). However, the meta-measures are equal-weighted percentile means; the authors acknowledge that Pareto-optimal or weighted combinations could select different setups. There is also a mild circularity risk in z-score/min-max rescaling: the normalization statistics (mean, sd, min, max) are computed over the very model pool being compared, so adding a model can change everyone's scores.
- **Reproducibility**: The evaluation stack is standard (LM Evaluation Harness + vLLM, fixed seeds), and the benchmarks are public; the authors state they extended NorEval with formulations/normalizations. But reproducing the core grid requires retraining 26 2B models (~11,000 GH200 GPU-hours) — practically only feasible for well-funded labs, and the larger-scale validation models (OLMo 3, Apertus, Prelude) are third-party checkpoints the authors do not control.
- **Significance**: The tiered recipe is immediately actionable for anyone running pretraining-time proxy evaluations (the scaling-law-driven data-selection scenario in §1). The scale-dependence finding — accuracy and MCF becoming viable only above chance-level performance — is a concrete, non-obvious guideline.
- **Red flags**: (1) The headline formulation and decoding conclusions rest on thin coverage: formulation variants exist only for Finnish (10 tasks) and Norwegian (14 tasks), French/Spanish have none, and decoding is tested only on Norwegian 2B models — so "CF preferred" is effectively a two-benchmark result that could partly reflect NorEval/FIN-bench task properties, which the authors themselves concede. (2) The larger-scale evidence uses exactly three models with different architectures, training data, and token budgets; ranking consistency there rests on very few comparisons, and the authors correctly label scale-dependent differences "suggestive rather than conclusive" — a reader should not treat "MCF catches up at 7B" as established. (3) The paper evaluates whether setups give *clean signals*, not whether they measure capabilities that matter downstream; a setup could ace all three meta-measures while tracking a narrow capability, an admitted limitation that somewhat undercuts the normative framing of the "recipe".

## Strengths

- Exceptionally controlled experimental design: 26 architecturally identical 2B models differing only in pretraining corpus, each with 11 checkpoints — the cleanest possible substrate for studying evaluation signal quality during early pretraining.
- Three complementary, well-formalized decision procedures (best complete setup, option ranking, per-option win rate), with the tiered recipe derived from their *agreement*, which makes recommendations robust to any single method's quirks.
- Intellectual honesty: dedicated analysis of meta-measure disagreement, quantification of the mechanical max-aggregation artifact, and explicit downgrading of the underpowered scale comparisons.
- Practical payoff: a concrete tiered checklist (always use 5-shot + multiple prompts + AnsProb + char normalization for small base models; re-assess formulation/metric/rescaling as scale grows), with near-zero loss (0.93 vs 0.95) for the cheap per-aspect assembly.
- Real multilingual breadth (Norwegian, Finnish, French, Spanish, including lower-resource Nordic languages) and a substantial benchmark extension of NorEval that the community can reuse.

## Limitations

- No ground truth for "best" evaluation setup exists; monotonicity, non-randomness, and ranking consistency are proxies that a narrow or downstream-irrelevant capability could still satisfy.
- Uneven aspect coverage across languages: formulation only in Finnish/Norwegian, CondProb and generative-task decoding only in Norwegian (2B only), PMI accuracy only partially for Spanish (5 of 11 tasks) — several headline comparisons are narrower than the four-language framing suggests.
- The larger-scale validation rests on three heterogeneous LLMs (OLMo 3 7B, Apertus 8B, Prelude 9B), confounding scale with architecture, data, and token budget; ranking-consistency estimates there are based on few comparisons.
- Equal-weight combination of the three meta-measures is a simplification; alternatives (weighting, minimum thresholds, Pareto front) could pick different setups, especially where the measures disagree (e.g., prompt aggregation).
- Findings are specific to *early pretraining* of base models (2B–100B tokens, often near chance-level performance); transfer to mid/late pretraining, fine-tuning, or instruction-tuned model evaluation is untested.
- Very high compute cost (~18,000 GPU hours) makes independent replication of the model grid impractical for most groups.

## Relevance

对于需要在大规模预训练过程中做数据/配置决策的团队，这篇论文直接回答了"中间 checkpoint 的 proxy 评测该怎么做才可信"的问题，其分层配方（AnsProb + 字符归一化 + 5-shot + 多 prompt）可以立即落地到任何 base 模型的训练监控流水线中；对关注多语言低资源语言（挪威语、芬兰语）评测基础设施的工作也有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the tiered recipe hold for mid/late pretraining (models well above chance), continued pretraining, or post-training stages (SFT/RLHF), where accuracy and MCF are already competitive?
- Can the meta-measures be validated against *downstream* outcomes — e.g., do setups with high monotonicity/ranking consistency at 2B scale better predict final-model performance on held-out tasks or human preference?
- How do the recommendations transfer to English-centric evaluation (lm-eval-harness/OLMES-style pipelines), to non-European and truly low-resource languages, and to agentic or long-context benchmarks?
- Is there a principled way to weight the three meta-measures (or add Heineman et al.'s signal-to-noise ratio) instead of equal percentile averaging, especially for aspects where they disagree?
- Does the circularity of computing z-score/min-max rescaling statistics over the evaluated model pool bias rankings when the pool is small or heterogeneous, and would leave-one-model-out normalization change the context-dependent tier?
- Could the Kendall's τ = −0.6 ranking-reversal phenomenon be predicted a priori from setup properties (e.g., rescaling choice interacting with metric chance level), allowing practitioners to flag unreliable comparisons without running the full grid?
