> **一句话总结：** 在带精确模拟器的 in-context Mealy 机执行任务上，本文把"过度思考（overthinking）"拆解为计算、保持、复用三种独立性质：looped transformer 训练出口处 100% 正确的答案到 2 倍深度只剩 22%，但把监督位置从"仅最后一位"改成"每例随机采样一位"即可保持 99.8%；换向量实验证明计算本身精确（237/237 对完全跟随反事实目标），而冻结实验表明答案可读时其状态尚不可复用（准确率从 100% 跌至 4–6%，需再等 2–4 个 loop 才能冻结）。

> **TL;DR:** Using in-context Mealy-machine execution (where a simulator gives exact per-position targets and counterfactuals), this paper decomposes "overthinking" in looped transformers into three separable properties. Models trained at loop 32 with FINAL-ONLY supervision are 100% correct at the trained exit but keep only 22.3% at loop 64; switching to one uniformly sampled supervised position per example (SAMPLED-PREFIX, same budget) retains 99.8%. Interchange interventions show the computation itself is exact — swapping one frontier hidden vector redirects all 24 later positions to the source input's exact counterfactual continuation in 237/237 eligible pairs per model — yet freezing each position's state at the loop its answer first becomes readable (what per-position halting would do) collapses endpoint accuracy from 100% to 4.1–6.3%, and even Bansal et al. (2022)'s released anti-overthinking maze network solves 0/32 mazes under that freeze vs 32/32 four iterations later. Retention and reuse are distinct, and neither is implied by correct computation.

## 核心贡献

- **把"过度思考"拆成三种可分别测量的性质**：答案从未被算出（计算失败）、算出后没有被保持（保持失败）、携带答案的状态无法被后续计算复用（复用失败）。用带精确模拟器的 in-context Mealy 机任务使三者可被分别干预和评分。
- **保持性由训练要求决定，且只需一个随机监督位置**：仅在最后一位施加损失（FINAL-ONLY）的模型在训练出口 100% 正确、但 2 倍深度处只剩 22.3%；每例只随机采样一个位置监督（SAMPLED-PREFIX，与 FINAL-ONLY 同为每例一个监督点）即可保持 99.8%。截断梯度的 progressive 训练在此设定下反而有害。
- **证明计算本身是精确的、但答案是瞬态的**：在答案首次出现的 frontier loop 上，把一个 128 维隐向量换成另一输入的对应向量，后续 24 个位置完全精确地跟随源输入的反事实执行（10 个模型、每个 237/237 对）。答案在算出后很快褪色。
- **"可读"早于"可复用"**：把每个位置在其答案首次可读的 loop 处冻结（即按位置停机 / Universal Transformer 式 halt-and-copy 的做法），即使是保持性近乎完美的模型，端点准确率也从 100% 跌到 4–6%；推迟 2–4 个 loop 冻结则无害。对 Bansal et al. (2022) 已发布的反过度思考迷宫网络得到同样结论。
- **方法学贡献**：为每个位置、每个 loop、每个反事实提供精确符号目标，使"在答案首次出现的时刻测试状态"成为可操作的实验范式。

## Key Contributions

- **Decomposes overthinking into three measurable properties** — failed computation, failed retention, and a state that later computation cannot reuse — using in-context Mealy-machine execution where a simulator supplies the correct state/output at every position and exact counterfactual continuations for hidden-state interventions.
- **Shows retention is set by what training asks for, and one random supervised position suffices**: FINAL-ONLY models are 100% correct at the trained exit (loop 32) but keep only 22.3% at loop 64; SAMPLED-PREFIX — supervising one uniformly drawn position per example, the same per-example budget — retains 99.8% at loop 64 and 96.6% at loop 96. Randomizing the training exit (Jitter-8 vs Slack-4) raises loop-64 accuracy from 30.0% to 85.6% at matched expected loop budget, while gradient truncation (an adaptation of progressive training) impedes learning outright.
- **Proves the computation is exact but its answers are transient**: at the frontier (position k correct at loop k), swapping one full hidden vector for another input's vector redirects every later position (9–32) to the source's exact simulator-computed counterfactual in 237/237 eligible pairs, in each of ten FINAL-ONLY models — one vector steers 24 further steps of exact symbolic execution — yet those answers fade above the diagonal in clean runs too.
- **Shows answers become readable before their states become reusable**: freezing each position's hidden vector at the loop its answer first appears (what per-position halting does) drops endpoint accuracy from 100% to 4.1% (SAMPLED-PREFIX) and 6.3% (MATCHED-RANDOM-EXIT), while caching the answers outside the model keeps all 32 correct; a 2–4 loop capture delay restores accuracy. The released recurrent maze network of Bansal et al. (2022) shows the same pattern (0/32 mazes frozen at arrival, 32/32 four iterations later).
- **Methodological template**: exact symbolic targets for every position, loop, and counterfactual, plus matched-budget training controls, let a state be tested at precisely the moment its answer first appears.

## Methodology

The task is deterministic Mealy-machine execution in context: each example presents a transition table (as tokens) plus a length-H action sequence; at each action position the model must predict the machine's current state q_k and output y_k. A simulator generates behaviorally-minimal machines (4/6/8 states, 2/4 input symbols, 2 outputs) and provides correct targets at every position, including exact counterfactual continuations after any state change — so interventions are scored against a unique correct answer, not by whether the output moved.

The model is a single shared two-layer pre-norm transformer block (width 128, 4 heads, FFN width 512, no positional or loop-depth embeddings) applied for R loops with X^r = B_θ(X^{r-1}) + E(x); training reads out state/output heads only after the final loop (the exit). Main models use R = H (one loop per input symbol, H = 32). Three objectives differ only in which positions are supervised at the exit: FINAL-ONLY (last position), ALL-PREFIX (all H positions), and SAMPLED-PREFIX (one uniformly sampled position per example — same per-example budget as FINAL-ONLY, expected loss equal to ALL-PREFIX). Additional controls vary the exit distribution (Slack-4 fixed R = H+4 vs Jitter-8 R = H+U, U uniform on {0..8}) and gradient truncation (PROGRESSIVE vs MATCHED-RANDOM-EXIT at identical supervised exits). Training: 132,000 AdamW steps, batch 256, 35-stage curriculum to H = 32; evaluation on 512 traces (16 machines × 32 traces) per model and length.

Three intervention families, all on the recurrent trajectory: (1) interchange swaps — replace the 128-d hidden vector of position c, loop r with the source run's vector, where the two inputs share the machine, symbol assignment, and table presentation and differ only in actions up to c; success = every later state/output matches the simulator's counterfactual; (2) resets to a position's own earlier vector; (3) freezing — capture each position k's hidden vector at loop k + d and write it back after every later block, emulating Universal-Transformer-style halting, with an external answer cache as the control that differs only in whether the answer or its state is kept. The frontier τ(k) — first loop where position k's marginal accuracy reaches .99 — is measured to be k = the diagonal, so each answer can be tested exactly when it first appears.

## Results & Highlights

- **Retention collapse under FINAL-ONLY, fixed by sampling one position**: on length-32 test inputs, FINAL-ONLY endpoint accuracy (state AND output at the last action) falls from 100% at loop 32 to 60.81% at loop 33 and 22.33% at loop 64, with whole-trajectory exactness 0% at both loops; ALL-PREFIX reaches 94.27% at loop 64, and SAMPLED-PREFIX — with the same single supervised position per example — reaches 99.80% (loop 64) and 96.61% (loop 96) endpoint, with whole-trajectory exactness 96.03% / 74.09% vs ALL-PREFIX's 74.28% / 67.45%.
- **Exit randomization beats exit slack at matched budget**: drawing the exit as Jitter-8 (R = H+U, U ∈ {0..8}) instead of fixed Slack-4 (R = H+4) raises loop-64 endpoint accuracy from 30.0% to 85.6% at the same expected loop budget.
- **Gradient truncation hurts in this recipe**: 2 of 3 PROGRESSIVE seeds never exceed 60% H32 endpoint accuracy; the third reaches 100% at loops 40–48 but falls to 26.6% by loop 96, while the MATCHED-RANDOM-EXIT control (identical supervised exits, full gradients) learns in all 3 seeds and keeps 97.9% at loop 64 — though only 29.6% whole-trajectory exactness vs 96.0% for SAMPLED-PREFIX.
- **Computation is exact at the frontier**: swapping one hidden vector at position 8 / loop 8 (τ(8) = 8 in every model) makes every state and output at positions 9–32, read at loop k, match the source's simulator counterfactual — 237 of 237 eligible pairs, in each of ten FINAL-ONLY models; self-swaps change nothing.
- **Readable ≠ reusable**: freezing each position at loop k drops endpoint accuracy from 100% to 4.1% (SAMPLED-PREFIX) and 6.3% (MATCHED-RANDOM-EXIT); across all 12 frozen models at most 2% of examples keep all 32 positions correct, while the answer cache keeps 100%. Errors start exactly one step downstream (position 3, reading position 1's stale loop-2 vector, falls to 68% accuracy in FINAL-ONLY models); freezing only positions 1–8 still caps FINAL-ONLY endpoint accuracy at 11.9%.
- **Reuse needs a 2–4 loop delay**: with a two-loop capture delay SAMPLED-PREFIX recovers to 99.8% endpoint accuracy; MATCHED-RANDOM-EXIT needs four loops. Bansal et al. (2022)'s released maze network (solves all 32 9×9 mazes at every iteration from 25 to 120) solves 0/32 mazes when each pixel is frozen at its answer's arrival, 28/32 at +2 iterations, 32/32 at +4, and 32/32 with answer caching.
- **Retention does not buy length extrapolation**: on the H64/loop-96 evaluation, SAMPLED-PREFIX scores 82.68% endpoint vs 93.03% for MATCHED-RANDOM-EXIT — the best-retaining objective extrapolates worse.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the conceptual move — separating compute/retain/reuse and testing a state exactly at the loop its answer first appears — is genuinely new and enabled by the simulator-provided counterfactual targets; the ingredients (overthinking, interchange interventions, frontiers, variable exits) are all borrowed and the related-work section says so plainly.
- **Rigor**: strong within its scope — matched-budget controls, exact symbolic scoring, self-swap controls, patch scans, per-seed reporting. Two caveats the authors themselves surface: the PROGRESSIVE comparison is an adaptation to FSMs, not the original recipe, so "truncation impedes learning" should not be read as a refutation of Bansal et al.; and expected-loop matching does not match measured FLOPs (backward work differs between progressive and matched-random-exit).
- **Reproducibility**: appendices specify machine generation, seeds, streams, pairing protocols, and checkpoint hashes; the online data stream is reproducible from generator state. Machine generation is procedural and cheap. The main external artifact dependency (the released maze checkpoint) is public.
- **Significance**: relevant to anyone building recurrent-depth / latent-reasoning models (Huginn-style) with per-token halting or KV-caching of "converged" tokens: the paper's evidence says a token's state is not safe to freeze when its output stabilizes — it needs a few more iterations. That is an actionable, architecture-level caveat. However, everything rests on a synthetic FSM suite with a 2-layer width-128 block and no positional/loop embeddings; no LLM-scale or real-data evidence is offered.
- **Red flags / weaknesses to keep in mind**: (1) evaluation uses 16 machines × 32 traces — only 16 sampled machines per length, and accepted transition tables are dependent draws, with no guarantee of structural disjointness from training machines; (2) the "state not reusable" finding may partly reflect timing information carried in the evolving state (the authors admit their tests cannot separate "content not yet consolidated" from "later positions read timing/order cues that freezing also freezes"); (3) SAMPLED-PREFIX still needs intermediate labels at inference-relevant positions, so it is not a label-free fix for production recurrent LMs; (4) the maze check is one checkpoint and 32 mazes per size; (5) whole-trajectory exactness of 0% for FINAL-ONLY at its own trained exit shows the headline "100% correct" is endpoint-only — worth remembering when quoting the 22.3% collapse.

## Strengths

- A clean conceptual decomposition (compute / retain / reuse) with an experimental design that actually separates the three, rather than tracking one accuracy-over-loops curve.
- The interchange result (237/237, ten models) is a remarkably crisp demonstration that frontier states are causally sufficient for exact symbolic continuation — and simultaneously that answers fade, which is a subtle and well-told contrast.
- Matched-budget and matched-exit controls (Jitter-8 vs Slack-4; PROGRESSIVE vs MATCHED-RANDOM-EXIT) isolate exit distribution from gradient truncation — rare discipline in this literature.
- The freezing-vs-caching contrast isolates precisely the assumption behind per-position halting and KV-reuse in recurrent models, and is validated on an external, previously-published checkpoint.
- Unusually transparent limitations section, including non-independence of overlapping checkpoints and what the interventions cannot identify.

## Limitations

- One synthetic task family (Mealy machines up to 8 states) and one principal architecture (2-layer, width-128 shared block, no positional/loop embeddings); the only external check is a single maze checkpoint. No evidence yet that the 2–4 loop reuse delay holds for pretrained recurrent LMs at scale.
- Interventions replace full post-block vectors; they establish sufficiency, not a unique encoding, and cannot distinguish lost content from frozen timing signals (authors state this explicitly).
- SAMPLED-PREFIX requires intermediate supervision — it changes what labels training needs, which limits transfer to settings without per-position simulators.
- Original comparisons use six paired seeds, new controls three; budget controls match expected loops rather than measured FLOPs; finite-window retention does not establish hidden-state convergence or uniform length extrapolation.
- Downstream-reset mechanisms were not tested on the new training controls or stochastic-budget models, so the retention gains do not establish the same downstream mechanism across objectives.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接触及循环深度（recurrent-depth / latent reasoning）模型中的测试时计算、按位置停机与 KV 复用等核心设计假设，对理解 looped transformer 的机制可解释性和长程状态跟踪有参考价值；如果你关注 test-time compute 的扩展、循环架构训练目标设计或对 overthinking 的机制分析，本文提供了一个可控性极强的实验范式和明确的"何时可以冻结状态"的负面证据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the 2–4 loop gap between readability and reusability scale with model width/depth, horizon, or task complexity, or is it a constant of the architecture family — and what physically changes in the hidden vector during those loops?
- Can an unsupervised signal (e.g., key-value stability, attention-pattern convergence, or Jacobian/feature-map criteria as in Wang & Reid 2026) predict when a state becomes reusable, replacing the simulator-provided frontier τ(k) = k?
- Would adding loop-depth or positional embeddings eliminate the freeze fragility by moving timing information out of the evolving state, or does the fragility persist because the content itself is still being consolidated?
- Do SAMPLED-PREFIX-style objectives transfer to pretrained recurrent-depth LMs (e.g., Huginn-style models) where per-position intermediate labels are unavailable — for instance via self-generated readouts at random depths?
- Why does MATCHED-RANDOM-EXIT both retain answers at H=32 (97.9% at loop 64) and extrapolate better to H=64 (93.03% vs 82.68% for SAMPLED-PREFIX) despite worse whole-trajectory exactness — is retention mechanistically orthogonal to length generalization?
