# Épi: Towards a Persistent, Self-Improving Agent for Autonomous Research

> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 Épi，一个能跨模型会话持续开展研究的自主智能体：通过层级记忆、版本化分支与独立评审在"固定验证规则 + 增益下限"的框架下演化研究方法（权重不变），产出了120项可验证的纪录结果——包括将 n-queens 常数的区间收窄约84倍、用精确对偶证书证明 Cohn–Elkies 线性规划界在维度10和11不可能取到紧界、并给出 Tuza 猜想一个新情形的计算机辅助证明；其轻量配置 Épi-turbo 在 FrontierOR 的30个运筹编码任务上以64%的模型成本取得比 Codex 高5.4%的总分（同一底层模型 GPT-6-Astra）。

> **TL;DR:** Épi is a persistent autonomous-research agent that sustains campaigns across model sessions via hierarchical memory, versioned branching, and independent review, all evaluated by a fixed "ruler" (submission format, feasibility, objective, normalization, gain floor) frozen before search. It claims 120 verifiable record results (20 numerical bounds across 12 families, 100 constructions across 8 families), headlined by an ~84x narrower interval for the n-queens constant vs Nobel et al. (2023) and an exact-certified proof that the Cohn–Elkies LP bound cannot be sharp in dimensions 10 and 11, plus a computer-assisted proof of Tuza's conjecture for split graphs with three neighborhood types. The lightweight Épi-turbo config beats the Codex agent on 30 FrontierOR tasks 53.96 vs 51.21 total score (+5.4%) at 64% of the model cost — though the gain comes almost entirely from the speed term, and Codex actually finds better raw objective values on more instances.

## 核心贡献

- 提出一个跨会话持久的自主研究智能体架构：研究状态（代码、运行中实验、中间结果、决策理由）存储在模型上下文之外，实验跨越会话边界持续运行，后续会话通过交接笔记恢复调查
- 设计三套自我改进机制：层级记忆（短期调查上下文 + 长期的"经验教训"与"死胡同"记录，可检索）、版本化分支与回溯（每个备选方法保留实现与证据，可回退到任意历史提交）、由不同模型执行的独立评审（评审者身份被记录，与隔离验证器分工）
- 提出"固定标尺 + 增益下限"验证协议：提交格式、可行性条件、目标、归一化、比较值与最小有意义改进 δ 在搜索开始前固定，只有 V(x)=1 且 Δ(x)≥δ 才算纪录，从机制上排除奖励作弊与精度噪声
- 产出120项纪录结果：12个族群的20项数值界纪录与8个族群的100项构造纪录（97项为几何堆积），外加 Tuza 猜想（τ△ ≤ 2ν△）对"8顶点团部 + 至多3种三角活跃邻域类型"分裂图族的计算机辅助证明（560个剩余档案、18,444个重数盒子，独立检查器验证）
- 轻量配置 Épi-turbo 将同一研究流程扩展到算法编码：在 FrontierOR 的30个新任务上比 Codex 高5.4%总分、仅用64%模型成本，且所有构造与证书在 GitHub 公开可查

## Key Contributions

- A persistent autonomous-research agent architecture: research state (code, running jobs, intermediate results, decision rationale) lives outside the model context; experiments keep running across session boundaries and later sessions resume via handover notes
- Three self-improvement mechanisms with model weights held fixed: hierarchical memory (short-term context vs long-term lessons and documented dead ends), versioned branches with backtracking (each alternative keeps its implementation, tests, and evidence; abandoned branches are retained with reasons), and independent review by a different model whose identity is recorded, separated from the isolated verifier
- A fixed verification protocol ("ruler"): submission format, feasibility conditions, objective, normalization, comparison value, and a positive gain floor δ are frozen before search; a record requires V(x)=1 and Δ(x)≥δ, with exact arithmetic or outward rounding required for rigorous bounds — structurally excluding reward hacking and reporting-precision artifacts
- 120 record-setting results (references as of 2026-09-16): 20 numerical bound records across 12 families and 100 construction records across 8 families, including exact dual certificates that the Cohn–Elkies sphere-packing LP bound cannot be sharp in dimensions 10 and 11, plus a computer-assisted proof of Tuza's conjecture for split graphs with an 8-vertex clique part and at most three triangle-active neighborhood types (560 remaining profiles, 18,444 multiplicity boxes, independently checked)
- Épi-turbo, a lightweight configuration for algorithmic coding (continuous sessions, per-call budget reservation, review disabled), scoring 5.4% higher than the Codex agent on 30 FrontierOR tasks at 64% of its model cost on the same underlying model — with all constructions and certificates publicly released

## Methodology

Épi operates on research tasks that have a reference result, an explicit budget, and checkable outputs. A human curator supplies the question and the "ruler" — the evaluation rule defining submission format, feasibility conditions, objective, normalization, and comparison value — plus a gain floor δ (the smallest improvement counted as meaningful, derived from the objective's granularity). A candidate is accepted only if the verifier confirms feasibility (V(x)=1) and the improvement over the reference meets the floor (Δ(x)≥δ). For rigorous numerical bounds, certificates must use exact arithmetic or outward rounding that controls numerical error; for theorems, the agent must supply both finite checked witnesses and a mathematical reduction explaining why the checks establish the full claim. A complete investigation is a "campaign" that may span many model sessions; the environment evolves between campaigns (a new record becomes the next reference), but within a campaign every branch is scored by the same frozen ruler. The agent's self-improvement loop has three pillars: (1) branches/backtracking — methods are developed on versioned branches storing implementation, tests, and notes, so the agent can compare alternatives under the common ruler and return to any saved commit; (2) hierarchical memory — short-term records support the current investigation while long-term lessons and dead ends are retrieved for new tasks and updated with supporting conditions and evidence, with handover notes at session boundaries keeping the working context bounded; (3) independent review — a different model receives the score trajectory, attempted approaches, retrieved findings, baseline, and resource use, and assesses whether the evidence justifies the agent's interpretation of progress. Execution runs on "Breadboard," which provides isolated experimental environments, long-running jobs across sessions, persistent storage, and isolated verifier execution. All reported campaigns keep model weights fixed (OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6-Astra, run over a few days with per-problem budgets). Épi-turbo adapts this to short algorithm-design tasks: one continuous session when possible, per-call cost reservation with a reserved end-of-campaign buffer, a stop rule when further sessions stop improving the best verified score, and independent review / extra validation turned off because their cost exceeded their benefit on short tasks. On FrontierOR, the agent writes standalone solvers (no external optimization solver calls) scored on held-out instances by a quality term (gap to reference value) plus a speed term that only counts when within 1% of the reference value.

## Results & Highlights

- **n-queens constant:** the rigorous interval for the asymptotic constant α shrinks from [1.944000752, 1.944001082] (Nobel et al. 2023, width 3.30×10⁻⁷) to [1.9440010223327, 1.9440010262694] (width 3.94×10⁻⁹) — an ~84x narrowing, via separate rational feasibility witnesses at resolutions 65,536 (lower) and 4,096 (upper)
- **Cohn–Elkies separation (dims 10 and 11):** new dual lower bounds via Li's discrete reduction — in d=10, 0.05643385 vs the packing upper bound 0.05623600 (Cohn et al. 2022), a 0.35% separation; in d=11, 0.05773180 vs 0.05664550, a 1.92% separation (earlier dual bounds were only 1/24 in both dimensions) — proving the Cohn–Elkies LP bound cannot be sharp in these dimensions
- **Spencer discrepancy constant:** an explicit 8×8 matrix with entries in {−1,0,1} has discrepancy exactly 5 (verified by exhaustively checking all 256 sign assignments), raising the record lower bound from 7/√17 ≈ 1.697749 to 5/√8 ≈ 1.767767
- **Quartic Feigenbaum attractor dimension:** the rigorous interval width drops from 7.80×10⁻³ to 2.13×10⁻⁹ — more than a 3.6-million-fold narrowing (full-domain interval proof)
- **waterund36 (MINLPLib):** the global lower bound improves from 655.544171 to 662.807028, within ~10⁻⁵ of the known feasible value (~662.807) — closing more than 99.999% of the MINLPLib optimality gap and certifying near-optimality of the existing network
- **Other numerical records:** Klarner's polyomino constant upper bound 4.5238 → 4.29569 (recurrence certificate over 1,186 neighborhood types); MAX-4-CUT hardness ratio 195581/198140 ≈ 0.987085 → 84931/86101 ≈ 0.986411; all five Bh[g] coefficients improved (e.g., h=4: 71.49 → 70.290; h=7: 21294.74 → 21149.19); dimer entropy Z³ 0.452130 → 0.452010
- **Packing constructions (97 of the 100):** e.g., 34 unit pentagons in an equilateral triangle: side length 12.98141 → 12.97564 (−0.0445%) vs Friedman's Packing Center reference; 74 circles in a semicircle: radius +0.0140%
- **Tuza's conjecture (τ△ ≤ 2ν△):** proven for split graphs with K₈ clique part and ≤3 triangle-active neighborhood types with arbitrary multiplicities — Zeng (2026) covered 2 types; of 872 three-type profiles, 35 are nested and 277 satisfy Zeng's max-cut criterion, and Épi built exact cover/packing certificates for the remaining 560 using 18,444 multiplicity boxes, extended to arbitrary multiplicities by Zeng's truncation lemma (counted separately from the 120 records)
- **Épi-turbo vs Codex on 30 FrontierOR tasks (same model GPT-6-Astra, $20 budget/task, held-out instances):** total score 53.96 vs 51.21 (+5.4%), higher score on 18 vs 12 tasks, mean model cost $7.30 vs $11.35 per task (64%), median research time 22 vs 37 min, median held-out runtime 2.9 s vs 9.3 s; on the 13 hard-split tasks the total is 22.33 vs 20.30 (+10%). The gain is speed-term-driven (mean speed 0.784 vs 0.675; mean quality 1.030 vs 1.032 — Codex is marginally ahead on quality)

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** the individual mechanisms (shared research state à la Kosmos/ScienceFlow, tree search à la AI Scientist-v2, memory à la MemoHarness, code-search-evaluation à la FunSearch/AlphaEvolve) are syntheses of known ideas; the genuinely new parts are the frozen-ruler-plus-gain-floor verification protocol and the breadth of independently checkable records produced under one architecture.
- **Missing baseline — the biggest rigor gap:** on the mathematical campaigns there is no agent-vs-agent comparison; every "record" is measured against static literature/registry references, never against FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, or AI Scientist-v2 on the same tasks and budgets. One therefore cannot tell how much of the output is due to Épi's architecture versus simply giving a frontier model (GPT-5.6 Sol / GPT-6-Astra) a few days of compute and a verifier.
- **Épi-turbo's headline needs unpacking:** the +5.4% total-score win over Codex is almost entirely the speed term (0.784 vs 0.675) — on raw solution quality, Codex finds the better objective value on 61 of 119 comparable held-out instances versus 18 for Épi-turbo (40 ties), Épi-turbo is >1% worse than Codex on 29 instances vs 10 in the other direction, and Épi-turbo produced 1 infeasible solution vs 0. The authors themselves state they ran one run per task per system and "do not report statistical significance" — honest, but it means the 5.4% delta carries unknown variance on n=30 tasks.
- **Anti-reward-hacking safeguards disabled where they matter:** independent review and extra validation are switched off in Épi-turbo, justified only as "cost larger than benefit in our tests" with no supporting data — precisely the mechanisms the design section motivates via reward-hacking literature.
- **Headline-number inflation risk:** the 120-record count aggregates 97 incremental packing refinements, some as small as a 0.0021% radius gain, alongside deep results (n-queens, Feigenbaum, Cohn–Elkies separation, Tuza); the count mixes magnitudes. Records are frozen against references as of 2026-09-16 in evolving registries.
- **Reproducibility:** total compute cost for the math campaigns is not reported ("a few days," budgets "set per problem"), and the models are proprietary; the agent code itself does not appear to be released (only result artifacts). The ruler, tolerances, gain floor, and task/reference curation are all manual human work, and the paper does not quantify that curation cost or how it scales.
- **No ablations:** nothing isolates the contribution of hierarchical memory vs branching vs independent review to any record.

## Strengths

- Verification-first methodology: the ruler and gain floor are fixed before search, feasibility tolerances are explicit, and rigorous bounds require exact arithmetic or controlled outward rounding — the strongest part of the design, directly aimed at reward hacking and unverifiable claims
- Every headline result ships with a checkable artifact (dual vectors, rational witnesses, the explicit 8×8 Spencer matrix, the weighted MAX-4-CUT gadget, Tuza certificates), and independent checkers verify the largest computations
- Real breadth under one protocol: 12 numerical families, 8 construction families, a theorem extension, and an algorithmic-coding benchmark — including structurally meaningful results (the Cohn–Elkies non-sharpness in d=10, 11 is a limitation theorem about a method, not just a better number)
- Unusually honest reporting: single-run disclosure, acknowledgment that some FrontierOR reference values are weak, and retention of archived comparison values so improvements stay traceable as registries update
- A clear human-understanding agenda: branches, dead ends, and review assessments are retained so researchers can inspect why a method was abandoned or a conclusion reached — rare among autonomous-discovery systems

## Limitations

- Model weights are fixed, so "self-improving" means method-level adaptation (memory, branching, review), not learning in the parameter sense — the term invites conflation with test-time training systems like TTT-Discover
- No comparison against other autonomous-research agents on the mathematical tasks, and single-run, n=30 evaluation on FrontierOR with no significance testing
- The Épi-turbo advantage is speed-dominated; on solution quality alone it loses to Codex on the majority of comparable instances and produced the only infeasible solution
- Independent review and extra validation are disabled in Épi-turbo without quantitative justification
- Compute budgets and total costs for the mathematical campaigns are undisclosed; relies on proprietary frontier models, limiting replication
- Heavy dependence on expert human curation (rulers, references, gain floors) that is not costed; record references are a snapshot of fast-moving registries
- The Tuza result still covers only |C|=8 with ≤3 neighborhood types — the 1981 conjecture remains open in general

## Relevance

与自主智能体和自我改进 agent 的研究方向高度相关：Épi 展示了不更新模型权重、仅靠持久化记忆、分支探索与独立评审即可跨会话持续改进研究方法的完整设计，其"固定验证规则 + 增益下限"协议对构建可验证、抗奖励作弊的长程智能体系统有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of Épi's record output survives when the frontier model is swapped for an open-weight mid-tier model — i.e., what fraction of the gains is architecture vs raw model capability?
- Would FunSearch/AlphaEvolve-style baselines reproduce the same packing and bound records under equal budgets, making an agent-vs-agent leaderboard possible for these tasks?
- Can the ruler (feasibility conventions, normalization, gain floor) be auto-generated and cross-checked safely, reducing the uncosted human-curation bottleneck?
- Does independent review measurably reduce reward hacking or wasted exploration in long campaigns — the question Épi-turbo's cost/benefit judgment leaves unanswered?
- Do the Tuza certificate techniques (profile enumeration + multiplicity boxes + truncation lemma) extend to four neighborhood types or clique parts larger than 8?
- What is the per-record compute/energy accounting, and do the memory/branch/review mechanisms show compounding value across successive campaigns?
