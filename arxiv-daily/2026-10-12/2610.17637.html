Critical Edge

STAGE 3 Extend & allocate Layer-wise law (L₂−L₁)pγ+L₁ places high-precision blocks; a GEMM-cost model then solves the hardware-aware compute-optimal precision (Eq. 5).
  • ρN/ρD rescale effective parameters/tokens per quantization scheme; ϵf/ϵb are additive, scale-independent error floors.

Compute-optimal prediction: with persistent error fixed while A/B terms shrink, optimal precision increases with compute budget — contradicting Kumar et al. (2025)'s budget-independent optimum. The optimum switches to all-MXFP8 forward ≈1020 FLOPs and both passes ≈5×1022 FLOPs (λMXFP8=1.8, λNVFP4=3).

03Assessment — read it with both eyes

Strengths

  • Theory-guided, not curve-fit — every term has an identified source (SGD noise, weight oscillation, gradient bias); ablations confirm necessity.
  • 375-run full-factorial grid with shared init and data order — quantization is the only varying factor.
  • External validation on the published Quartet grid guards against self-consistent overfitting.
  • Actionable rules: high precision → forward pass and first/last blocks first; expect optimal precision to rise with scale.

Limitations

  • Validated only to 910M params / 35.8B tokens; all 1020+ FLOPs conclusions are extrapolation.
  • Quantitative comparison is against the Quartet law only — no head-to-head with Kumar et al. QPT or Panferov et al.
  • No code, no run data, unnamed training corpus.
  • Cost model is matmul-only with fixed speedups (λ=1.8 from one NVIDIA kernel); not stress-tested on real hardware.
  • Theory assumes convexity and deterministic quantization — far from the LLM regime it is applied to.

04Open Questions for Further Exploration

Does "optimal precision increases with budget" survive at 10B+ parameters, or does the additive-error model break down in overtrained regimes?
Can the layer-wise exponent γ be predicted a priori from cheap proxies (per-layer gradient norm, Fisher sensitivity) instead of fitted per strategy?
Do QAT-style techniques (loss-aware scaling, gradient compensation, learned codetypes) shrink the ϵf/ϵb floors?
How sensitive are the allocations to realistic heterogeneous speedups — per-layer shapes, non-GEMM overheads, distributed communication?
Do optimizer corrections (momentum, second moments, proximal operators) reduce the persistent error in practice?
