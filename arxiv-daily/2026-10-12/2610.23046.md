> **一句话总结：** SpikeQ 发现 SNN 量化精度损失由「被翻转的脉冲比例」而非 MSE 误差决定（Spearman 相关 −0.99），据此提出脉冲引导的 PTQ 框架：权重逐层模拟下游神经元选层、膜电位采用阈值锚定的 pre-fire 网格，使 2-bit 膜电位下所有现有方法崩溃而 SpikeQ 保持大部分精度，4-bit 权重+膜电位在 7 个基准上最多掉 2.59 个点，内存缩小 7.8–7.9×、能耗降低 6.4–6.9×。

> **TL;DR:** SpikeQ argues that SNN accuracy loss from quantization tracks the fraction of flipped spikes (Spearman −0.99) rather than MSE (−0.77), and rebuilds PTQ around this: weight levels are chosen by simulating downstream neurons to minimize spike errors, and a threshold-anchored reset-aware grid quantizes the membrane potential *before* firing (matching real neuromorphic chips like Loihi/Speck). With 2-bit pre-fire membrane potentials every existing grid collapses (e.g., PTQ4SNN-M gets 13.98% vs SpikeQ's 61.39% on ImageNet with SEW-ResNet-18), while W4V4 models stay within 2.59 points of full precision across all 7 benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet, VOC 2012, ADE20K, COCO), shrinking memory 7.8–7.9× and energy 6.4–6.9×. The dramatic wins are concentrated at extreme low bit (W2/V2); at 4-bit weight-only it is roughly at parity with GPTQ/SBC (+0.1–0.3 pts), and 2-bit joint quantization still collapses on complex tasks (SEW-ResNet-18 W2V2: 15.85% vs 62.89% FP).

## 核心贡献

- **揭示正确的高层认识：** 通过误差注入实验证明，等 MSE 的量化误差造成的精度损失可以差异巨大，而翻转相同比例脉冲的误差造成的损失相同——精度与「翻转脉冲比例」的 Spearman 相关为 −0.99，远强于与 MSE 的 −0.77。这直接否定了沿用 ANN PTQ 的数值误差最小化目标。
- **脉冲引导的权重量化：** 对每一层，在已量化的输入脉冲上模拟下游 LIF 神经元，直接以脉冲误差为目标（脉冲计数损失 Lcount + 边际损失 Lmargin + 总数平衡项 Lpop）离散搜索每个权重的量化层，同时参照全精度网络和局部（全精度权重、已量化输入）两个基准，无需梯度微调。
- **面向真实硬件的 pre-fire 膜电位量化：** 指出现有方法量化 reset 后的状态，而 Loihi/Speck 等神经形态芯片实际是在固定位宽下存储膜电位并在点火前与阈值比较。SpikeQ 用**阈值锚定**（θ 置于两个量化层的正中间成为舍入边界）保证舍入永不翻转当前时步的脉冲，配合**reset 感知的层分配**（硬 reset 下只留一个高于 θ 的层、软 reset 下让 θ 为整数步长使 V−θ 落在网格上），使 reset 后状态无需再次舍入（附有证明）。
- **全面实验验证：** 在分类（CIFAR-10/100、ImageNet×2）、分割（VOC 2012、ADE20K）、检测（COCO）共 7 个基准上，2-bit pre-fire 膜电位下所有现有网格全部崩溃，SpikeQ 独自保持大部分精度；W4V4 距全精度最多 2.59 点，内存 7.8–7.9× 缩减、能耗 6.4–6.9× 降低。

## Key Contributions

- **A corrective insight with strong evidence:** injected-error experiments (PreAct-ResNet-18, CIFAR-100) show equal-MSE errors cause wildly different accuracy losses (constant vs zero-sum input-current errors; errors pushed across θ vs not), whereas all four error curves merge into one when plotted against the fraction of flipped spikes (Spearman −0.99 vs −0.77 for MSE). Spikes, not numerical error, are the right optimization target for SNN quantization.
- **Spike-guided weight quantization:** for each layer, SpikeQ captures the already-quantized input spikes, simulates the downstream LIF neurons, and searches each weight's quantization level against a composite objective — Lcount (per-neuron spike-count error against both the full-precision network and a "local" reference that applies FP weights to the changed input), Lmargin (sums |Vt − θ| over wrong spikes, to keep making progress when the count loss is flat), and Lpop (keeps the layer's total spike count balanced). The discrete search (Algorithm 1) picks each weight from its four nearest levels, largest-current inputs first, models undecided weights as random rounding errors, and runs refinement sweeps with a 25% held-out calibration split that freezes overfit channels.
- **Pre-fire membrane quantization matched to real hardware:** existing methods quantize the post-reset state Ut, but neuromorphic chips (Loihi, Speck) store the membrane potential at a fixed bit width and compare it with θ *before* firing. SpikeQ's **threshold anchoring** places θ exactly on a rounding boundary (so 1[Qv(V) ≥ θ] = 1[V ≥ θ] for any step size), and its **reset-aware level allocation** (one level above θ under hard reset; θ an integer number of steps under soft reset, with proof in Sec. B) guarantees the post-reset state is itself a grid level. Grids are calibrated by counting flipped spikes, not membrane MSE.
- **Broad evaluation across three tasks:** 7 benchmarks spanning classification, segmentation, and detection with both CNN-style and spiking-transformer SNNs; joint W4V4 quantization stays within 2.59 points of full precision everywhere while shrinking memory 7.8–7.9× and energy 6.4–6.9×.

## Methodology

SNNs transmit information only through spikes: a LIF neuron integrates input current, fires when the membrane potential crosses threshold θ, and resets (hard: to 0; soft: subtract θ). A quantization error in weights or membrane potentials reaches later layers *only* through the spikes it flips — now or at later time steps — yet existing PTQ objectives (AdaRound/GPTQ-style current alignment, SBC's membrane alignment) minimize MSE-type numerical errors.

**Weight stage.** For layer ℓ, SpikeQ records the quantized input spikes Sℓ−1, fixes a per-channel weight grid, and minimizes L = Lcount + λLmargin + μLpop (Eq. 4). The double reference (FP network + local recomputation) handles the fact that earlier layers are already quantized. Because L has no useful gradient through spikes, Algorithm 1 searches levels directly: phase one decides weights one *input* at a time (largest contributors first), each choosing among its four nearest levels with undecided weights modeled as uniform random rounding draws (scored over 4 draws); phase two sweeps ±1-level changes, guarding against calibration overfitting with a held-out split and freezing any output channel whose held-out loss rises. μ is adaptively updated to rebalance total spike counts (|μ| < 1 so layer totals never outweigh fixing a neuron's spike). Channels that drive no spiking neurons (e.g., a non-spiking classifier head) fall back to an output-error objective.

**Membrane stage.** SpikeQ targets pre-fire quantization V̂t = Qv(Vt), which is what on-chip storage forces. The grid Gv places n of the K levels below θ with θ exactly midway (Eq. 5), making θ a rounding boundary so rounding cannot flip the current spike. Reset-aware step selection: hard reset uses Δv = θ/(m + ½) with exactly one level above θ; soft reset sets Δv = θ/m for an integer m so subtracting θ maps levels to levels. Candidate (m, n) grids are compared by the number of flipped spikes they cause under simulation with earlier layers already quantized (Eq. 7) — not by membrane MSE, which is dominated by far-from-threshold errors that flip nothing.

**Setup.** T = 4 time steps throughout. Classification: PreAct-ResNet-18 (soft reset) on CIFAR-10/100, SEW-ResNet-18 and SDT-8-384 on ImageNet. Segmentation: 55M SDT-V2 + spiking FPN on VOC 2012 and ADE20K. Detection: EMS-YOLO (ResNet-34 backbone) on COCO. Weights calibrated on 1,024 unlabeled images, membrane grids on 256; ρ = 0.5 at W2, 0.25 at W3/W4; λ = 1; at most 6 sweeps; A100 GPUs. Weight baselines: RTN, FlowQ, GPTQ, SBC, GPTAQ, BRECQ, PTQ4SNN. Membrane baselines: MinMax, MSE, FlowQ-M, SQUAT, PTQ4SNN-M (+MPBA). All baselines are additionally evaluated under the pre-fire protocol, and SpikeQ under post-reset, so the comparison is two-sided. Energy is modeled as EC = Ecompute + Ememory + Eneuron with per-operation energies (e.g., 32-bit synaptic add 0.9 pJ, weight read 0.0985 pJ/bit).

## Results & Highlights

- **2-bit weights, SDT-8-384 on ImageNet:** SpikeQ reaches **45.76% Top-1 vs 22.21% for PTQ4SNN** (the strongest baseline) — more than double (+23.55 pts); on COCO W2 detection it gets **21.30 AP vs 14.82 for BRECQ** (+6.48 AP), and on ADE20K segmentation **29.32 mIoU vs 19.53 for SBC** (+9.79 mIoU). It is best on 6 of 7 benchmarks at W2.
- **2-bit pre-fire membrane potentials: total baseline collapse.** On SEW-ResNet-18/ImageNet V2, SpikeQ keeps **61.39% Top-1 while MinMax gets 0.15%, SQUAT 4.46%, PTQ4SNN-M 13.98%, and +MPBA 15.83%** (FP = 62.89%). On CIFAR-100 PreAct-ResNet-18 V2, SpikeQ retains **68.60% vs FP 73.78** (−5.18 pts) while every baseline drops to ≤1.00%. This holds on all seven benchmarks.
- **Joint W4V4:** stays within **2.59 points of full precision on all 7 benchmarks** (e.g., CIFAR-10 92.41 vs 92.60; SDT-8-384 ImageNet 70.49 vs 72.04; VOC 70.32 vs 70.86 mIoU; COCO 29.80 vs 31.38 AP), shrinking memory footprint **7.8–7.9×** (e.g., SDT 101 MB → 13.0 MB; ADE20K SDT-V2 723 MB → 91.4 MB) and cutting modeled energy **6.4–6.9×** (e.g., CIFAR-10 1.56 mJ → 0.228 mJ).
- **W4 weight-only is near-parity, not a blowout:** SpikeQ stays within 0.28 points of the best method on every benchmark (CIFAR-10 92.38 vs SBC's 92.39; VOC 70.36 vs GPTQ's 70.64) — consistent with the paper's own analysis that fine 4-bit steps flip few spikes for any method, so the advantage concentrates at extreme low bit.
- **Ablations confirm both mechanisms matter:** removing threshold anchoring collapses pre-fire V2 everywhere (−8.04 pts on CIFAR-10, −47.36 on SEW-ResNet-18, −50.61 on SDT, −50.93 mIoU on VOC); removing the margin term costs up to 5.29 pts at W2 (SDT); replacing the flipped-spike grid criterion with MSE in post-reset calibration costs up to 40.22 pts (SEW-ResNet-18: 8.07% vs 48.29%). The population term matters most on segmentation (~3.2 mIoU).
- **Energy decomposition:** weight reads dominate full-precision EC, so energy falls 6.4–6.9× at W4V4 and 8.7–10.8× at W2V2 almost entirely via weight quantization; membrane bit width touches only the small neuron term (CIFAR-10: EC 1,560 µJ FP → 1,554 µJ at V4 alone, but 228 µJ at W4V4).

## Authors & Credibility

Kai Sun, Peibo Duan (co-corresponding), and Levin Kuhlmann (co-corresponding) are at Monash University; Zhanglu Yan is at NUS; Yongsheng Huang at Northeastern University; Guowei Zhang at Monash. The work is firmly in the SNN-efficiency literature and cites the right lineages: neuromorphic hardware (Loihi [Davies et al.], Speck [Yao et al., Nature Communications 2024]), ANN PTQ methods honestly ported as baselines (AdaRound-lineage BRECQ by Yuhang Li, GPTQ by Frantar et al., GPTAQ ICML 2025), and direct SNN competitors (SBC/SQUAT/PTQ4SNN/FlowQ, including ASP-DAC 2026 work). The experimental protocol is unusually careful for a PTQ paper: baselines are re-evaluated under the pre-fire protocol they were not designed for *and* SpikeQ under post-reset, BRECQ/PTQ4SNN/SpikeQ share the same clipping search so "they differ only in the selected levels," COCO results disable TF32, and the reset-aware grid claim is backed by an explicit proof (Sec. B). The negative controls (injected-error Spearman analysis; accuracy-vs-flipped-spikes curves where baselines fall on the same trend line) are a credible form of mechanism validation.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: solid and well-motivated, not just incremental.** The "spikes, not MSE" reframing is demonstrated rather than asserted, and the pre-fire vs post-reset distinction is a genuinely hardware-grounded observation (Loihi/Speck compare the stored potential with θ). The threshold-anchored grid is elegant and provably flip-free at the current step. That said, the components are assembled from known ideas — output-error/second-order calibration (GPTQ, SBC), threshold-centered levels (SQUAT already concentrates levels near θ) — and the contribution is the objective and grid geometry, not a new learning principle.
- **Rigor: high on evaluation, with one real omission.** The paper never reports calibration cost (wall-clock, memory, or energy of the search itself) for SpikeQ vs GPTQ/BRECQ/PTQ4SNN, even though its search simulates downstream neurons over all T steps, over four nearest levels, over several sweeps, plus refinement sweeps and held-out re-evaluation. BRECQ/PTQ4SNN's 20k reconstruction iterations are mentioned as setup, but no runtime comparison is given — for a PTQ paper whose selling point is practicality, this is a conspicuous gap. Calibration-seed variance is also mostly unexamined: a single seed is used everywhere, with only one number (CIFAR-10 W2: 90.52 ± 0.19 over 3 seeds) reported.
- **Honesty about where it wins: mostly good.** The paper openly states that at W2 on CIFAR-10 "the four strongest lie within 0.19 points" and that gains grow with task complexity; it also concedes W2V2 collapses on complex tasks (SEW-ResNet-18: 15.85% vs 62.89% FP). But the abstract-level framing ("outperforms existing PTQ methods") leans on the V2 regime where *all* baselines score near zero — a denominator effect that inflates headline deltas (e.g., 61.39 vs 0.15). The 2-bit pre-fire collapse of baselines is an important finding about protocol mismatch, but readers should note those baselines were designed for post-reset quantization and were never intended for that operating point (to the authors' credit, they also show SpikeQ wins under post-reset).
- **Reproducibility: good.** Hyperparameters are fully specified (ρ per bit width, λ = 1, 4 draws, ≤6 sweeps, 1,024/256 calibration images, 25% held-out), models are public architectures (PreAct/SEW ResNet-18, SDT, SDT-V2, EMS-YOLO), and the appendix details the energy model with per-operation pJ constants. No code release is mentioned in the main text, which limits independent verification of the discrete search.
- **Red flags / weaknesses to keep in mind:** (1) all experiments use T = 4 time steps — error accumulation over longer horizons, where flipped spikes cascade across more steps, is untested; (2) the energy results are from an analytical model, not measured on actual Loihi/Speck hardware; (3) ADE20K at W4V4 (34.12 vs 34.91 FP) and COCO at W2 (21.30 vs 31.38) show the method does not rescue dense-prediction tasks at low bit, and unused-slot redistribution (mixed precision beyond PTQ4SNN's MPBA) is left unexplored; (4) no QAT comparison at all (defensible for practicality, but SQUAT-style QAT numbers would contextualize the accuracy ceiling).

## Strengths

- A crisp, falsifiable empirical insight (flipped spikes predict accuracy, Spearman −0.99) that directly implies the right PTQ objective — and negative controls that show existing methods lie on the same flipped-spike trend line.
- Hardware-faithful problem formulation: pre-fire membrane quantization is what Loihi/Speck actually do, and the threshold-anchored, reset-aware grid comes with a correctness proof rather than a heuristic argument.
- Two-sided protocol: baselines evaluated under pre-fire, SpikeQ under post-reset; shared clipping search with BRECQ/PTQ4SNN isolates the contribution of the level-selection rule.
- Comprehensive task coverage (7 benchmarks, 3 task types, CNN and transformer SNNs, hard and soft reset) with consistent ablations that cleanly attribute the gains to anchoring, the margin term, and the spike-based grid criterion.
- Honest reporting of regime boundaries: gains concentrate at W2/V2 and on complex tasks; 2-bit joint quantization is explicitly recommended only for simple tasks under tight memory budgets.

## Limitations

- No calibration-cost (wall-clock/GPU-hours) comparison against the reconstruction- and second-order baselines, despite a search that requires neuron simulation over time steps and multiple sweeps; PTQ practicality claims would be stronger with these numbers.
- Single-seed weight calibration for nearly all results (one 3-seed variance figure); a per-task sensitivity analysis is missing.
- All experiments at T = 4; longer time horizons — where the paper's own mechanism (flipped spikes propagating across steps) should matter most — are untested.
- Energy numbers come from an analytical per-operation model, not on-chip measurements; firing-rate changes from quantization (e.g., 20.55% → 19.50% on CIFAR-10 W2V2) modestly flatter the energy ratios.
- W2V2 is not viable on complex benchmarks (SEW-ResNet-18 15.85% vs 62.89), and dense prediction (ADE20K/COCO) degrades earlier than classification; no mixed-precision extension of SpikeQ's own allocation is attempted.
- No code availability stated; the discrete search has several moving parts (phase ordering, ε draws, sweep count) that would benefit from release.

## Relevance

对关注高效模型部署与神经形态计算的用户高度相关：本文将 ANN PTQ 的成熟思路（GPTQ/BRECQ 一脉）推广到 SNN，并给出「以脉冲而非数值误差为目标」的通用量化原则，与低比特推理、边缘部署和能效优化的研究兴趣直接契合。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is SpikeQ's calibration wall-clock and energy cost relative to GPTQ/BRECQ/PTQ4SNN, and how does it scale with T, network depth, and firing rate?
- Does the flipped-spike objective remain the right proxy at longer time horizons (T = 8–16) or on recurrent/temporal tasks (neuromorphic datasets like DVS-Gesture, N-Caltech101), where flipped spikes cascade further?
- Can threshold anchoring be combined with mixed-precision or bit-allocation search (à la PTQ4SNN's MPBA) to rescue W2V2 on complex benchmarks like ADE20K and COCO?
- Does the spike-error objective transfer to quantization-aware training, where surrogate gradients already exist — i.e., is there anything left for MSE-style losses at all?
- How do the energy predictions compare with measurements on actual Speck/Loihi hardware, where pre-fire storage, leakage, and asynchronous scheduling differ from the analytical model?
- Could the double-reference scheme (FP + local) be extended iteratively (re-quantizing earlier layers jointly) instead of greedily layer by layer, reducing error compounding at 2 bits?
