Key metric — certified reversal severity

96.7%

τcert ≈ 0.967 · GPT-2 Medium, layer 23

Gating reverses directions even where value transport strips ≥ 96.7% of squared magnitude — while blind random search over 8,192 directions finds zero switches (search table ↓).

Why this matters

Exact spectral criteria for which perturbation directions attention amplifies or suppresses — direct tooling for model editing, adversarial robustness, and representation steering.

⚠ Critical edge

All evidence is first-order, single-layer, and thin — 2 prompts, final token, 64-dim probes; regime switching is never linked to end-to-end behavior.

reproducibility: pivotal operating point has no published seed / prompt rule; no code

Methodology — distilled

A local, first-order lens on one attention sublayer: δzℓ+1 ≈ J δzℓ, with per-direction growth read off G = J⊤MJ − M — growth vs. shrink depends on the direction (anisotropy). The attention Jacobian splits exactly into three pathways:

Transport-only vs. full dynamics differ by a correction ΔGG containing a query–key cross term — pathways are non-additive: query or key alone may fail while the pair succeeds. λmax(W) ≤ 1 rules switching out entirely. Analytic Jacobians were triple-checked against autodiff and finite differences, on four pretrained families (WikiText-2, one A100).

Joint query–key switching (neither pathway suffices alone) appears in all four families; no universal depth trend in dsw.

  • Relative strength ≠ absolute severity: the Γ-maximal direction (Γ ≈ 1730, τ ≈ 0.003 — transport barely touches it) is far less consequential than the severe direction (Γ ≈ 1.07, τ ≈ 0.967), which flips only via a query–key cross term ≈ 0.242 (gT+Q, gT+K both < 0, gfull = +0.072).
  • Amortized cost: one-time spectral preprocessing ≈ 0.049 s; beats per-direction finite-forward probing beyond ≈ 3,993 directions (detection) / ≈ 1,981 (mechanism).

Strengths

  • Crisp question — "can routing reverse transport contraction?" — answered with exact, checkable mathematics: necessary-and-sufficient criteria, not heuristics.
  • Exact, interpretable three-pathway decomposition; rank ≤ dim Ur is a clean structural insight with an MoE reading of attention.
  • Strength-vs-severity distinction exposes how the leading eigenvalue alone (Γ ≈ 1730 for a barely-touched direction) can overstate a switch.
  • Compelling search demo: blind 0/8192 and oracle τ ≤ 0.072 vs. certified τ ≈ 0.967.
  • Unusually honest scoping — certificates distinguished from estimates; includes a probe-dimension self-critique.

Limitations

  • Purely first-order and local: no multi-layer propagation, no link to end-to-end behavior despite editing/adversarial motivation.
  • Evidence is thin: 2 prompts, final token only, 64-dim probes (own sensitivity study non-converged by dim 256); headline study is one layer of one model.
  • MLP sublayer excluded — "Transformer dynamics" here is half a block; all experiments use the Euclidean metric M = I.
  • No code release; pivotal operating point under-specified — headline numbers (λmax, dsw, τcert) hard to reproduce exactly.
  • No baseline comparison against prior Jacobian/spectral methods on shared tasks.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does switching at one layer propagate through later layers and MLPs, or get washed out — can certified subspaces survive a multi-layer product of growth operators?
  2. Do certified severe-switch directions actually flip model predictions — enabling query-efficient adversarial attacks or more contained model edits?
  3. How do the switching spectrum and mechanism mix shift under non-Euclidean metrics, longer prompts, and non-final token positions?
  4. Is query dominance (79.5–90.2% across four families) an architectural invariant, or an artifact of the query-token protocol and 64-dim probes?
  5. How do switching subspaces relate to rank collapse, attention entropy, or induction heads — would collapse-resistant models show smaller dsw?
