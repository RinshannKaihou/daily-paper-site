> **一句话总结：** 日本 LLM-jp 团队从零训练了 8B 稠密与 32B-A3B MoE 两个"主权"开源模型（共约 12T token，Apache 2.0），通过全流程公开、许可证合规的数据管线（自建 FineWeb-Rescored 与 MMWPM-OSS 替代含 Llama 限制的数据），在日语基准上从上代 llm-jp-3.1-8x13b-instruct4 的 0.307 大幅提升至 0.605，并与 Qwen3-8B（0.608）、gpt-oss-20b（0.604）持平，但在英语与数理推理基准上仍明显落后。

> **TL;DR:** This technical report documents LLM-jp-4, a fully transparent Japanese "sovereign AI" effort: an 8B dense and a 32B-A3B (3.8B active) MoE model trained from scratch on ~12T tokens under strict license-compliance rules (no GPT/Claude/Llama-derived data; FineWeb-Edu and MegaMath-Web-Pro-Max reproduced with Qwen3-32B and gpt-oss-120b instead), then aligned as Harmony-format thinking models via SFT+DPO with no RL. On llm-jp-eval v2.1.3 (Japanese) the MoE model scores 0.605, roughly matching Qwen3-8B (0.608) and gpt-oss-20b (0.604), and beats gpt-oss-20b on MT-Bench JA (7.82 vs 7.33) — but English average (0.650) trails Qwen3-8B (0.714, -6.4 pts), GPQA Diamond EN is 0.520 vs 0.670 for gpt-oss-20b, and AIME 2024/2025 reaches only 0.400/0.300. The paper's real contribution is methodological transparency: fractional-factorial data-mixture experiments, WSD-vs-cosine ablations, and public release of configs, data, and code.

## 核心贡献

- **完整的"主权 AI"开源范式**：发布 8B 稠密（8.59B 参数）与 32B-A3B MoE（32.14B 总参 / 3.83B 激活，128 专家激活 8 个）两个模型，Apache 2.0 许可，训练配置、部分训练/偏好数据、脚本全部公开，明确对齐 OSAID（开源 AI 定义）与日本著作权法。
- **许可证合规的数据重建**：因 Llama/GPT/Claude 的服务条款限制（禁止训练竞品模型、命名要求等），拒绝直接使用 FineWeb-Edu 与 MegaMath-Web-Pro-Max，改用 Qwen3-32B 重标注 45 万文档训练分类器复现 FineWeb-Rescored，用 gpt-oss-120b 复现 MMWPM-OSS（83M 文档 / 67.4B token）。
- **从零构建的多语言分词器**：SentencePiece Unigram、196,608 词表，在 EN/JA/ZH/KO ≈ 0.68/0.18/0.09/0.05 的均衡混合上一次性训练（非逐语言扩词表），保留原始空白与缩进以高效处理代码，数字逐位切分（消融显示提升数学性能）。
- **实验设计严谨的数据配比优化**：预训练 Phase 2 与 mid-training 均采用分数析因设计（Resolution III 2^5-2=8 组、Resolution IV 2^8-4=16 组）做方差分析，以 p≤0.05 且 Cohen's f≥0.4 为采纳标准——只有 MMWPM-OSS 在 Swallow 上达标（f=4.39）。
- **无 RL 的思考模型后训练**：采用 OpenAI Harmony 响应格式（analysis 通道=推理、final 通道=答案），gpt-oss-120b 以低/中/高三种推理强度合成推理链，五维 LLM-as-Judge 全 5 分过滤，SFT（~10.83B token）+ DPO（8B 模型 154,561 对）完成对齐。
- **新日语数学数据集 LLMC Math**：按日本文部科学省最新课程大纲手工编写 2,243 道题，经 gpt-oss-120b 类题扩增至约 15 万题并用 LLM-as-Judge 过滤。

## Key Contributions

- **A sovereign-AI development template**: two models — an 8B dense (8.59B params) and a 32B-A3B MoE (32.14B total / 3.83B active, 128 experts with 8 activated) — trained from scratch on ~12T tokens, released under Apache 2.0 with training configs, (portions of) SFT/DPO data, and scripts all public, explicitly aligned with the Open Source AI Definition (OSAID) and Japanese copyright law.
- **License-clean data reproduction**: rather than using FineWeb-Edu (built with Llama-3-70B-Instruct) or MegaMath-Web-Pro-Max (built with Llama 3.1 70B Instruct), both are reproduced with open models — FineWeb-Rescored via Qwen3-32B annotations of the same 450K FineWeb documents (classifier scoring of all 24.5B docs took 128 H200 GPUs ~1.5 days) and MMWPM-OSS via gpt-oss-120b over all Common Crawl snapshots through 2025.
- **A five-level data release taxonomy** (L1–LZ) governing internal vs. public-model training, retrieval, and redistribution for every subcorpus — a concrete governance mechanism other open efforts could copy.
- **From-scratch multilingual tokenizer**: SentencePiece Unigram with a 196,608-token vocabulary trained once on a balanced EN/JA/ZH/KO mixture (≈0.68/0.18/0.09/0.05), identity normalization (no NFKC), preserved whitespace runs for code, per-digit splitting; SuperBPE-style superword tokens were tested and rejected for degrading downstream performance.
- **Statistically structured mixture experiments**: corpus-mixture decisions made via fractional factorial designs with ANOVA (Resolution III, 2^5−2=8 runs for pre-training Phase 2; Resolution IV, 2^8−4=16 runs for mid-training) with pre-registered adoption criteria (p ≤ 0.05, Cohen's f ≥ 0.4), instead of ad-hoc trial and error.
- **RL-free thinking-model post-training**: OpenAI Harmony response format with analysis/final channels; gpt-oss-120b synthesizes reasoning traces at three effort levels with strict 5-dimension LLM-as-Judge filtering (all scores must be 5), followed by SFT (~10.83B tokens over 2 epochs) and DPO (154,561 pairs for 8B; 144,131 for 32B-A3B) with length-bias-mitigating pair construction (token-length ratio ≤ 1.5).

## Methodology

**Architecture.** The dense model follows a standard LLaMA-2-style Transformer and the MoE follows Qwen3's general design, chosen deliberately for ecosystem compatibility (vLLM, Megatron-LM). Both use GQA (32/8 heads dense; 40/4 MoE), RoPE θ=500,000, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, 65,536 context. The MoE is wide-and-shallow for larger GEMMs and higher GPU throughput; DeepSeek-style init (std 0.006), OLMoE-style router z-loss (1e-3) and load-balancing loss (1e-2), FP32 router logits.

**Pre-training (~10.5T tokens, two phases).** Phase 1 (6.3T tokens) uses LLM-jp Corpus v4 (target ~20T-token pool); Phase 2 (4.2T tokens) swaps in The Stack v2 and adds MMWPM-OSS for math/code emphasis. Sampling weights (0/1/2/4/8) were chosen via 13 heuristic 400B-token proxy runs (7.7B dense); curated corpora (Wikipedia, books, arXiv, math, code) got weight 8, Japanese Common Crawl 4, Chinese/Korean web 1, and FineWeb-Rescored quality tiers 4/1/0. Training used Megatron-LM on the ABCI H200 cluster, bfloat16, WSD learning-rate schedules with 1-sqrt decay branches; a 2T-token proxy study showed 20K-step WSD decay already beats cosine at the 100K-step scale and longer decays keep improving. Of the hyperparameters tested (batch size, β2, softmax precision, RoPE base, document-boundary resets), only reducing global batch size (1024→512) gave a clear gain.

**Mid-training (1.26T tokens).** A 50/50 mix of pre-training corpus and LLM-jp-Corpus-Midtraining-v2 (regenerated Midtraining-v1 + 7 of 8 candidate corpora selected by Resolution-IV factorial ANOVA; Synthetic-Transpilation dropped for negative effects). Context extends 4K→16K→64K at fixed 8.39M tokens/update.

**Post-training.** Harmony-format thinking model. SFT: gpt-oss-120b synthesizes (reasoning, answer) pairs at low/medium/high effort from ~20 prompt sources; three-stage filtering (format compliance, self-referential-question removal, 5-dimension all-5s quality gate); 82,615 packed 64K chunks ≈ 5.41B tokens/epoch × 2. DPO: 4 samples/prompt (T=1.0, top-p 0.95) from the SFT model, gpt-oss-120b as judge; pairs kept when best score >6 and gap ≥2 (helpfulness) or >3 and gap ≥1 (safety), with a ≤1.5 length-ratio filter; β=0.5.

## Results & Highlights

- **llm-jp-eval v2.1.3, Japanese average**: 0.307 (llm-jp-3.1-8x13b-instruct4, previous flagship) → 0.590 (LLM-jp-4-8B) and 0.605 (LLM-jp-4-32B-A3B), roughly doubling the previous generation and matching Qwen3-8B (0.608, +0.003) and gpt-oss-20b (0.604, −0.001).
- **llm-jp-eval v2.1.3, English average**: 0.354 (previous gen) → 0.634 (8B) / 0.650 (32B-A3B), but still 6.4 points behind Qwen3-8B (0.714) and 5.4 points behind gpt-oss-20b (0.704).
- **JMMLU**: llm-jp-4-32b-a3b-thinking reaches 0.820 vs 0.590 for llm-jp-3.1-8x13b-instruct4 (+23.0 points).
- **Japanese GPQA Diamond**: 0.310 (previous gen) → 0.540 (32B-A3B thinking), +23.0 points; but on **GPQA Diamond (EN)** the 32B-A3B scores 0.520, below Qwen3-8B (0.560) and well below gpt-oss-20b (0.670, −15.0 points).
- **AIME 2024/2025**: 0.400/0.300 for the 32B-A3B — a large gain over the previous generation but short of both Qwen3-8B and gpt-oss-20b (exact external numbers not listed in text).
- **LLM-as-Judge (gpt-5.4-2026-03-05 judge, 3-run average)**: llm-jp-4-32b-a3b-thinking (medium effort) beats gpt-oss-20b (medium) on MT-Bench JA 7.82 vs 7.33 (+0.49), AnswerCarefully 3.70 vs 3.55 (+0.15), and llm-jp-instructions 3.61 vs 3.16 (+0.45), while matching it on MT-Bench EN 7.86 vs 7.85 (+0.01).
- **Corpus-level causal findings**: adding MMWPM-OSS was the only Phase-2 modification meeting the pre-registered criteria on the Swallow benchmark (α=0.0079, p=0.025, f=4.39); mid-training factorial analysis showed Synthetic-Code-Review had the largest effect on HumanEval (signed f = 2.82) and Synthetic-Transpilation negative effects across MT-Bench/JHumanEval/HumanEval.
- **HLE scores are low for all evaluated models**, honestly reported as an open problem.

## Authors & Credibility

The work is authored by the LLM-jp consortium (17 named authors plus the team, listed alphabetically with a per-category contribution statement in the appendix), spanning Japanese academic and national-lab collaborators — including researchers associated with NII, Waseda, UTokyo, Kyoto, and the ABCI compute infrastructure. LLM-jp has a track record of prior open model releases (LLM-jp-3/3.1), open evaluation suites (llm-jp-eval, llm-jp-judge), and the publicly maintained llm-jp-corpus series, all of which this paper builds on. The report is unusually forthcoming: it documents failed/negative ablations (superword tokens, β2=0.99, document-boundary resets), discloses reproduction gaps of its reconstructed datasets (MMWPM-OSS: 83M docs/67.4B tokens vs the original 69M/79.4B), and releases configs, scripts, and per-stage data on Hugging Face and GitHub. Credibility is high; the only structural caveat is that model-quality conclusions rest heavily on the consortium's own benchmark suites for Japanese.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the modeling itself is intentionally conventional (LLaMA-2-style dense, Qwen3-style MoE, Harmony format, SFT+DPO) — the novelty lies in the governance and process transparency, which is genuinely among the best-documented open model reports to date. For readers seeking architectural innovation, there is little here.
- **Rigor**: strong on data-mixture methodology (fractional factorial designs with pre-registered statistical criteria are rare in LLM reports), but the Phase-1 mixture search was heuristic (13 ad-hoc configs, no systematic grid), and the Resolution-III design aliases main effects with two-factor interactions — the authors acknowledge this and simply assume interactions are small, which is unverified.
- **Baseline asymmetries**: headline gains ("substantial improvements") are measured against the previous-generation llm-jp-3.1-8x13b-instruct4, a dated and weak comparator; against contemporaneous open-weight models the MoE is only *on par* in Japanese and clearly behind in English (0.650 vs Qwen3-8B's 0.714). The 32B-A3B also carries 32.14B total parameters while being compared to 8B and 20B models — no total-parameter-matched baseline (e.g., a ~30B dense or other MoE) is included, so the "similar scale" framing flatters the active-parameter efficiency story.
- **Evaluation caveats**: the LLM-as-Judge (gpt-5.4) belongs to the same organization as the gpt-oss-20b baseline, and LLM-jp-4's training data were synthesized by gpt-oss-120b in the Harmony format — a shared stylistic prior between judged models and judge family is a plausible bias source the paper does not discuss. JMMLU external baselines are also not reported alongside the 0.820 headline.
- **Missing ablations**: no DPO-vs-no-DPO or thinking-vs-non-thinking ablation on final benchmarks; no RLHF/RLVR comparison despite AIME (0.400/0.300) lagging all baselines — the no-RL choice is stated, not empirically justified. Mid-training's temporary degradation of GSM8K, XWinograd_en, and OpenBookQA is noted but only anecdotally analyzed.
- **Reproducibility**: excellent in principle (configs, scripts, data licenses, ANOVA tables all public), though full reproduction requires ABCI-scale compute; the 12T-token budget is not accessible to most third parties.
- **Significance**: as a template for legally clean, fully documented sovereign LLM development, this is a reference-grade contribution; as a competitive model, it is mid-tier in Japanese and below-frontier in English/reasoning.
- **Red flags**: none serious — no evidence of benchmark gaming; HLE weakness is disclosed rather than hidden.

## Strengths

- Exceptional end-to-end transparency: every major design choice (tokenizer, mixture weights, LR schedule, hyperparameters, post-training filters) is backed by a described experiment and public artifacts.
- License-clean reproduction of two widely used but license-contaminated corpora (FineWeb-Edu → FineWeb-Rescored via Qwen3-32B; MMWPM → MMWPM-OSS via gpt-oss-120b), removing a real adoption barrier for derivative open models.
- Statistically grounded data-mixture selection (fractional factorial + ANOVA with pre-registered thresholds) — a methodological upgrade over the intuition-driven mixing common in peer reports.
- Honest negative-results reporting (rejected superword tokens; hyperparameter changes with no robust gain; corpus modifications that failed criteria; temporary mid-training regressions).
- Strong Japanese conversational results: the 32B-A3B thinking model beats gpt-oss-20b on MT-Bench JA (7.82 vs 7.33), AnswerCarefully (3.70 vs 3.55), and llm-jp-instructions (3.61 vs 3.16).
- Practical usability focus: standard Apache 2.0 license, mainstream architectures, 64K context, released DPO data and chat template.

## Limitations

- English capability clearly trails same-scale open peers (0.650 vs 0.714 for Qwen3-8B on llm-jp-eval EN; GPQA Diamond EN 0.520 vs 0.670 for gpt-oss-20b), and math-competition performance (AIME 2024/2025 = 0.400/0.300) remains below external baselines.
- No reinforcement learning stage — alignment is SFT+DPO only; the reasoning gap on AIME/GPQA may partly reflect this, but the paper offers no experiment to quantify it.
- Mixture optimization for Phase 1 was heuristic (13 candidate configurations) rather than systematic, and the fractional-factorial aliasing assumptions are unverified.
- Total-parameter-unequal comparisons: 32.14B-total MoE vs 8B/20B baselines; FLOP-matched evaluation is absent.
- Heavy dependence on gpt-oss-120b for data synthesis and (same-family) gpt-5.4 for judging introduces an unexamined stylistic-prior concern; LLMC Math excludes figure-based geometry problems, limiting coverage.
- HLE performance is low for all models and the paper provides little analysis; safety evaluation is limited to AnswerCarefully with no red-teaming suite.
- Reproduction requires ABCI-class H200 clusters (~12T tokens per model), so "independently reproducible" is aspirational for most third parties.

## Relevance

该论文展示了一条完全合规、全流程公开的 8B/MoE 模型从零训练路线图（含析因实验设计、WSD 调度、许可证合规数据重建与 SFT+DPO 思考模型对齐），对任何关注开源 LLM 训练方法学、数据治理与可复现性的研究都极具参考价值。其在同规模开源模型上日语领先、英语与推理落后的量化结果，也为多语言模型能力平衡提供了清晰的实证案例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the AIME/GPQA gap is attributable to the absence of RL (RLVR/GRPO) versus data or scale — would adding an RL stage to the 32B-A3B close the gap to gpt-oss-20b?
- Does the judge-family stylistic prior (gpt-5.4 judging a gpt-oss-120b-distilled Harmony-format model) measurably inflate LLM-jp-4's LLM-as-a-Judge scores relative to human or cross-family judges?
- Would a FLOP- or total-parameter-matched comparison (e.g., vs Qwen3-30B-A3B) change the "competitive at similar scale" conclusion?
- Can the Resolution-III/IV fractional factorial methodology for corpus mixtures be replaced by higher-resolution or Bayesian designs to de-alias interactions at the same compute budget?
- How much English capability could be recovered by rebalancing the tokenizer/corpus mixture (0.68 EN share) without sacrificing Japanese parity?
- Do the mid-training regressions on GSM8K/XWinograd_en/OpenBookQA reflect a general forgetting mechanism, and can they be predicted or prevented (e.g., replay weighting) rather than repaired by post-training?
- Is the Japanese-government-curriculum-aligned LLMC Math dataset (150K problems from 2,243 seeds) transferable as a recipe for other languages' sovereign math data?
