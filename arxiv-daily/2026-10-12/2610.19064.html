Replica scaling in DiLoCo is an inner-optimizer noise problem — and whitening nearly erases it.

一句话总结：副本越多、损失越差的根源，是内层优化器输出的伪梯度协方差结构与局部 batch 缩小带来的二阶矩估计误差——白化型内核（SPlus/SOAP）配合按 token 半衰期扩展的 β₂ 规则，把 512M 模型 96 副本的退化压到 +2.1%（AdamW 为 +12%），并让廉价的 IsoLoCo 反超 MuLoCo。

01Methodology — how they showed it

Step 1 · Decompose
Error to DP = mean mismatch + noise
Directional error of the averaged DiLoCo update splits into a bias term and a stochastic term bounded by λ_max(C_agg) (Prop. 1).
Step 2 · Classify cores
Whitening is the desideratum
AdamW preserves correlations; Shampoo only √-conditions; SOAP whitens ideally; SPlus whitens via the sign map (arcsine law); Muon only under rotational invariance.
Step 3 · Scale β₂
A token-half-life rule
With fresh second moments the mean bound grows linearly in R−1; β₂(R) = β₂(R₀)^(R₀/R) keeps it O(1) (Prop. 3).
Step 4 · Validate
DP-referenced sweeps + probes
178M / 512M LLaMA on DCLM, R ∈ {8…96}, ≥10-point HP grids, H=1 control, checkpoint covariance/curvature probes.

Inside the DiLoCo framework: R replicas each run H=30 inner steps on a shrinking B/R tokens, displacements are uniformly averaged, and a Nesterov outer optimizer applies the aggregate. Global batch is fixed per scale (524K tokens at 178M over ~3.55B tokens; 2M tokens at 512M over ~10.25B), so only locality varies.

Mechanistic probes at a shared 178M checkpoint replay the conditional covariance over 64 replicas × 16 paired rollouts, estimate mean-vs-noise along fitted high-noise directions across all 63 hidden matrices, and measure Hessian-vector curvature there — linking each theory quantity to the measured scaling order.

Unwhitened noise concentrates exactly in high-curvature directions — where errors are most costly.

  • 178M · R=64 ordering: SPlus +3.1% < SOAP +4.8% < Shampoo +8.0% < Muon +8.5% < AdamW +11.0%; at R=8 SPlus (2.851) even beats its own DP (2.853).
  • β₂ rule payoff (512M · R=64): changing only β₂ lifts DiLoCo 2.884→2.849 and IsoLoCo 2.734→2.702 — tuned IsoLoCo (AdamW inner) now beats MuLoCo (2.738, Muon inner) at lower cost.
  • H=1 pure-averaging control: damage appears with zero client drift; Muon collapses (+19.6%) while SPlus/SOAP hold — the nonlinear inner update, not drift, dominates.
  • Authors' takeaway: with SOAP/SPlus inner, 50+ replicas cost <3% vs DP; scaling 178M→512M improved replica scaling for every optimizer tested.
Strengths
  • Falsifiable decomposition confirmed by probes in the predicted order (SPlus > SOAP > Muon > AdamW on anisotropy, curvature, scaling).
  • β₂ rule is nearly free, comes with a proven bound, and reverses the IsoLoCo-vs-MuLoCo ordering — a hyperparameter fix beating a costlier design.
  • H=1 control isolates update nonlinearity from multi-step drift, overturning the classical local-SGD narrative for adaptive optimizers.
  • Mechanistic depth: measured anisotropy, mean/noise ratios with paired corrections, and Hessian curvature give a causal story for why whitening helps.
  • Candid appendices about estimator limitations (biased ratios, non-causal cross-method probes).
Limitations
  • Scale ceiling: 178M/512M, 3.55B/10.25B tokens, one dataset (DCLM), one architecture family — the scaling story is a two-point extrapolation.
  • No end-task evaluation: validation loss only; no evidence loss deltas translate to downstream capability.
  • Mean-mismatch analysis exact only at H=1; multi-step case assumes small temporal covariances (asserted, not proven), and noise dominance is measured on selected directions.
  • Muon's H=1 collapse vs H=30 success is left as an untested hypothesis about the outer optimizer.
  • Costs under-specified: SOAP/SPlus keep two Gram factors per matrix; memory, throughput, and communication volume are argued, not measured.

03Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the whitening advantage persist at 1B+ parameters and on downstream benchmarks (MMLU/GPQA-style), or does SPlus's sign-flavored update lose calibration at scale?
  2. What makes Muon robust at H=30 but fragile at H=1 — outer Nesterov momentum, the polar map's interaction with averaging, or a noise structure the covariance probe misses?
  3. Can the β₂ token-half-life rule merge with learning-rate/outer-momentum schedules into one replica-scaling recipe, and does it fix asynchronous DiLoCo variants too?
  4. Is there a whitening core with Muon-level memory cost — low-rank or diagonal-plus-sign hybrid — that avoids storing two Gram factors per matrix?
  5. Would re-weighting the aggregate by local Hessian estimates (beyond isotropization) buy further scaling at 100+ replicas, as the curvature probe suggests?
