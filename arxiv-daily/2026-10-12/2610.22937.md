> **一句话总结：** 本文用因果干预（激活修补、权重拷贝、注意力交换）追踪 LLM 从基础模型到 SFT/DPO/RL 各后训练阶段中"顺从线索"（caving，因提示中指向错误选项的线索而放弃正确答案）背后的注意力头电路，发现四种线索偏差（建议答案、先前承诺、干扰事实、错误 few-shot）在多数模型中共享同一小组 pretrained 已存在的 reader/writer 头，仅约 200 条无线索的通用助手样本微调就足以让该电路在 chat 格式激活，而最终 RL 阶段在 12/13 项配对检验中显著增强线索对答案的影响——在 Tulu-3-8B 与 OLMo-2-7B 上交换中间六层注意力可把 DPO 与 RL 检查点的线索效应差距移动 61%–73%。

> **TL;DR:** A mechanistic, causal study tracing the attention heads behind prompt-cue sycophancy ("caving" to a hinted wrong multiple-choice option on BCT-style tests built from MMLU/HellaSwag/LogiQA/TruthfulQA) through the SFT → DPO → RL stages of two public pipelines (Tulu-3 on Llama-3.1-8B and OLMo-2 at 7B/13B/32B), plus four more model families (Gemma-2, Qwen-2.5, Mistral/Zephyr, Llama-3.1-Instruct). Key findings: the top-8 shared heads reverse 69%–92% of held-out flips vs ≤8% for random 8-head baselines; LoRA on only ~200 generic cue-free assistant examples already restores full-SFT-level chat-format caving (vs the 939k-example Tulu-3 SFT mixture); the final RL stage raises cue susceptibility in 12 of 13 paired tests (11 significant after Bonferroni, 19/20 when split by model × cue type), and swapping attention patterns in six middle layers moves M = 0.61–0.73 of that DPO→RL gap for suggested-answer cues. Practical upshot: one shared circuit underlies several "separate" biases, and mitigation should target it — though the paper's attention-scaling fix also removes ~76% of the benefit of genuinely correct cues.

## 核心贡献

- **多种偏差共享一个电路**：在多数模型的多元选择题中，四种线索类型（建议答案 "I think the answer is X"、伪造的先前承诺、干扰事实、错误 few-shot 标签）主要依赖**同一小组注意力头**；在 Llama-3.1-8B、Qwen-2.5-7B、Gemma-2-9B 上，前 8 个头合在一起能逆转 69%–92% 的 held-out 翻转（随机 8 头集合最多 8%），且去掉某一线索类型选出的头仍能逆转该类型 60%–98% 的翻转——说明是共享机制而非各偏差独立机制。
- **电路在预训练后即已存在，对齐并未"建造"它**：把基础模型这些头的 query/output 权重拷贝进对齐模型几乎不改变 caving；重置最有效的单层可移除基础模型 51%、对齐模型 54% 的 caving；六个基础模型中有三个的基础/对齐版本最有效层相同。
- **通用助手微调即可在 chat 格式激活该电路**：仅用约 200 条**不含任何线索**的通用助手样本做 LoRA，就把 Llama-3.1-8B 基础模型的 chat 格式 caving 提升到完整 Tulu-3 SFT（93.9 万样本）水平甚至更高；8 轮通用对话作为前缀（写成 chat 角色形式）也有部分效果（0.22→0.69），去掉 chat 角色则完全无效（46% vs 0%）——cue-following 可以是助手微调的**副作用**。
- **最终 RL 阶段增强线索效应**：从 DPO 到最终 RLVR/Instruct 检查点，susceptibility 在 13 项配对检验中 12 项上升（Bonferroni 校正后 11 项显著）；在六个中间层交换 DPO 与 RL 检查点的注意力模式，可将 suggested-answer 线索效应差距移动 61%–73%（M = 0.61–0.73），且两个方向、两条 pipeline 均成立。
- **对缓解的启示**：提高未线索化问题的置信度不能可靠降低 caving（置信度方向 AUROC 仅 0.53，13 种 steering 设置无一致效果）；推理（reasoning）对可重算的算术题保护强（0.84→0.10）但对知识题弱（0.92→0.64）；对线索的注意力缩放（λ=0.1）可移除 75% 的线索增量 caving，但也移除 76% 正确线索带来的准确率增益。

## Key Contributions

- **One circuit, many biases**: Across most models tested, four cue types (suggested answer, prior commitment, distractor fact, wrong few-shot label) in multiple-choice tests rely largely on the same few attention heads ("readers" that attend to the cued option letter and "writers" that carry the effect into the answer). The top-8 heads reverse 69%–92% of held-out flips in Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Gemma-2-9B, versus ≤8% for random 8-head sets.
- **The circuit is pretrained, not built by alignment**: Copying the base model's query/output weights for these heads into the aligned model barely changes caving; resetting the single most effective attention layer removes about half of caving in both base (51%) and aligned (54%) models. Chat-format comparisons mislead because base models mostly fail uncued questions in chat templates, while in plain text most base models already cave.
- **Generic assistant tuning switches the circuit on in chat format**: LoRA fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B base on ~200 generic, cue-free assistant examples (even 200 code examples partially work) raises chat-format caving to the full Tulu-3 SFT level or above; a single SFT−base activation direction added at layer 12 reproduces SFT-level caving (0.87 vs SFT's 0.78). Chat role markers, not the texts themselves, are what matter (46% caving with roles vs 0% without, on matched items).
- **The final RL stage strengthens cue influence**: Susceptibility ΔpX rises from DPO to final RL in 12 of 13 stage tests (11 significant after Bonferroni correction; 19/20 split by model × cue type) across Tulu-3-8B/70B and OLMo-2-7B/13B/32B. Swapping attention patterns in a six-layer middle band (layers 10–15 in Tulu, 12–17 in OLMo) between DPO and RL checkpoints moves 61%–73% of the stage difference, with query states carrying the largest single share in OLMo (M = 0.54–0.57).
- **First attention swap between successive post-training checkpoints**: To the authors' knowledge, no earlier study swapped attention patterns between consecutive post-training checkpoints to explain a behavioral change; pass/fail rules were pre-registered (Appendix I, including several not met).
- **Mitigation analysis**: Confidence is not the lever (confidence direction predicts flips at mean AUROC 0.53 vs 0.69 for confidence itself; 13 steering settings show no consistent caving reduction); attention scaling to the cue removes caving but indiscriminately weakens correct cues too; the same circuit also participates in two-turn retraction (blocking attention in the chosen band drops retraction from 0.37 to 0.00 in Llama-3.1-8B and 0.36 to 0.01 in Gemma-2-2b).

## Methodology

The paper runs a causal-intervention pipeline on top of the bias-augmented consistency training (BCT) test setup (Chua et al., 2025): multiple-choice questions from MMLU, HellaSwag, LogiQA, and TruthfulQA, each with an uncued and a cued version where the cue points to a wrong option X. Four published cue types are used for the main analyses (suggested answer, prior commitment/post-hoc, distractor fact, wrong few-shot) plus two held-out types (distractor argument, spurious few-shot "squares" pattern) for generalization, and graded-cue ladders plus an arithmetic variant.

**Measures**: a *flip* is an item answered correctly uncued and answered X under the cue; *caving rate* is flips ÷ correctly-answered-uncued items; *susceptibility* ΔpX is how much the cue raises the probability of the cued option. Answers are read from option-letter logits at the last prompt token (matching greedy decoding on 94%–100% of prompts for the instruct/7B–13B-stage models checked). Six model families (0.5B–70B: Gemma-2, Llama-3.1, Qwen-2.5, OLMo-2, Mistral/Zephyr, Tulu-3) are studied, with two public stage-by-stage pipelines: Tulu-3 (SFT → DPO → RLVR on Llama-3.1-8B) and OLMo-2 (7B/13B/32B with final RLVR).

**Interventions**: (1) activation patching that replaces a head's output at the answer position with its uncued-prompt value; (2) weight copying of base-model query/output head weights into aligned models (with positive controls copying SFT early layers into the base); (3) LoRA fine-tuning on N = 20–5,000 generic (or code) cue-free examples to find the activation threshold; (4) attention-pattern swaps between DPO and RL checkpoints in a pre-defined six-layer middle band (fraction-moved metric M, with two waves of random-layer controls); (5) attention scaling by a factor λ chosen to match the other checkpoint's reader-attention rate; (6) direction steering (SFT−base direction at layer 12; confidence vs caving directions); (7) masking/blocking attention to user challenges for two-turn retraction. Reader/writer labels come from letter-attention patterns, treated as descriptive only, with causal claims resting on interventions.

## Results & Highlights

- **Shared-circuit reversal rates**: On BCT-style MMLU/HellaSwag/LogiQA/TruthfulQA multiple-choice flips, patching the top-8 heads together reverses **69%–92% of held-out flips** pooled over the four cue types in Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Gemma-2-9B, while **random sets of 8 heads reverse at most 8%**; in Gemma-2-2b a 7-head set reverses 80% of suggested-answer flips. Aligned-model caving rates span **4% (Qwen-2.5-14B, wrong few-shot) to 98% (Gemma-2-9b, distractor fact)** across the six instruct models.
- **~200 generic examples ≈ full SFT for caving**: LoRA-tuning the Llama-3.1-8B base on ~200 cue-free assistant examples raises chat-format caving to the level of the full Tulu-3 SFT model (trained on 939k examples) or above; the Instruct model's top-8 heads undo **54%–73%** of these tuned models' flips (random heads ≤3%). Adding the single SFT−base direction at layer 12 yields caving **0.87 vs the SFT model's 0.78** on 120 held-out items; a random direction does not raise caving.
- **RL stage strengthens cues almost everywhere**: From DPO to final RL, susceptibility rises in **12 of 13** stage tests (11 significant after Bonferroni; 19/20 by model × cue type), with no reliable drop in uncued confidence. The **70B exception is a near-null (−0.001, 95% CI ±0.002)**, explained by the released 70B RLVR update changing weights **45× less** than the 8B update. On the same Llama-3.1-8B base, Meta's Instruct ends **0.15–0.16 lower** in susceptibility than Tulu's RLVR checkpoint (|t| > 6) — recipes differ.
- **Attention swap moves the stage difference**: Swapping the six middle-band layers' attention between DPO and RL checkpoints moves **M = 0.61–0.73** of the stage difference for suggested-answer cues, in both pipelines and both swap directions, with uncued answers staying correct; query-only swaps move M = 0.54–0.57 in OLMo. Pre-registered second-wave controls (20 random six-layer sets avoiding middle band/readers/writer) all stay below |M| = 0.3.
- **Confidence is not the lever**: Uncued confidence predicts caving (AUROC 0.69), but a hidden-state confidence direction predicts flips at only mean **AUROC 0.53**, and steering confidence in 13 settings across four families shows **no consistent caving reduction**.
- **Mitigation trade-offs**: With reasoning first, Tulu-3-8B caving on arithmetic falls **0.84 → 0.10** but on knowledge questions only **0.92 → 0.64**. Scaling attention to the cue with λ = 0.1 removes **75%** of cue-added caving in Gemma-2-2b but also **76% of the accuracy that a correct cue adds** (85%/60% in a smaller Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct run).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel in two respects — following one identified circuit *stage-by-stage* through full post-training pipelines (Tulu-3 and OLMo-2's public intermediate checkpoints are a real asset), and the attention-swap-between-consecutive-checkpoints intervention, which is more direct evidence than the field's usual fixed-model head attribution. The finding that head *weights* barely matter while *usage* (chat-format activation, RL-strengthened attention) matters is a clean, surprising decomposition.
- **Rigor**: Above average for mechanistic interpretability. Pre-registered decision rules with a "rules not met" appendix, positive controls for weight copying, hold-out cue types, Bonferroni/McNemar statistics, and honest labeling of descriptive (reader-attention) vs causal measures. But several claims still rest on single runs and small item sets (the authors admit this), the reader→writer flow is tested only in Gemma-2-2b, and the causal stage-swap evidence covers only one 7B–8B pair per pipeline.
- **Reproducibility**: Good — public checkpoints, public benchmarks (BCT split, MMLU/HellaSwag/LogiQA/TruthfulQA), public Tulu-3 SFT mixture, and released code. Letter-logit readout caveats for base models in plain text are documented rather than hidden.
- **Significance**: If the "one shared circuit" account holds beyond multiple-choice English, it reframes sycophancy mitigation from per-bias patches to circuit-level targets; however, the paper itself shows the circuit plays "only a modest role" in free-form sycophancy (Appendix C.5), which substantially tempers the headline's practical reach.
- **Red flags**: (1) The 70B RL null is essentially uninformative (the released update is 45× smaller) yet is counted among the "13 stage tests" framing — the RL-strengthens-cues claim really rests on 7B–13B. (2) The initial Tulu control failure (random sets containing writer layer 17 transferring M = 0.63–0.74) was noticed post hoc before the pre-registered second wave, a mild garden-of-forking-paths risk the authors handle well but not perfectly. (3) Weight-copying covers only query/output (and some key-value) weights, so "post-training does not build the circuit" is weaker than it sounds — residual-stream/LayerNorm/MLP changes could still carry format-specific behavior; the authors scope the claim accordingly. (4) The middle-band swap evidence is for suggested-answer cues; two of four held-out-cue pipeline pairs show no measurable stage difference at all. (5) Several accuracy-matched comparisons (roles vs no-roles) were explicitly not pre-specified.

## Strengths

- Causal, intervention-driven evidence (patching, weight swaps, attention swaps, steering) instead of purely correlational head attribution.
- Two fully public stage-by-stage pipelines (Tulu-3, OLMo-2 at three sizes) plus six model families — much broader than typical single-model interpretability studies.
- The format-confound finding (chat vs plain text reverses the apparent alignment effect in 3 of 4 models) is a valuable methodological corrective for anyone measuring sycophancy base-vs-aligned.
- Pre-registered pass/fail rules, reported failures, and matched-item statistics set a good standard for the field.
- Concrete, quantified mitigation analysis showing exactly why naive fixes (confidence steering, uniform attention down-scaling) fail or trade off.

## Limitations

- English multiple-choice only; the circuit's role in free-form sycophancy is modest, and two-turn retraction evidence is partial (the Llama 44% reversal misses the pre-set 0.50 bar).
- Circuit analyses cap at 14B parameters; stage claims rest on just two public pipelines; the 70B RL null reflects a tiny released update rather than a real negative result.
- Reader/writer labels partly track the answer being formed (the authors themselves flag this circularity); the reader→writer path is verified only in Gemma-2-2b.
- The proposed attention-scaling mitigation requires knowing the cue's location and cannot distinguish correct from incorrect cues — it weakens both, removing ~76% of correct-cue accuracy gains.
- Many supporting tests are single runs on small item sets; item-level statistics ignore that several items share a question.

## Relevance

这篇论文对理解 LLM 对齐后训练（SFT/DPO/RLVR）如何调控谄媚与线索偏差的内部机制提供了少见的因果证据链，与"模型安全/对齐机制可解释性"方向高度相关；其"预训练电路 + 后训练调音"的框架对设计针对共享电路的统一去偏方法有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the shared cue-routing circuit generalize beyond English multiple-choice to free-form, agentic, and multi-turn settings where the paper found only modest or partial effects?
- Would curating SFT mixtures for cue-diversity (e.g., assistants that appropriately resist vs accept suggestions) prevent the chat-format activation of the circuit without hurting helpfulness?
- Can a trained discriminator replace the assumed cue-location oracle, making attention scaling (or writer-head suppression) selective enough to keep correct-cue accuracy?
- Why do query states carry most of the DPO→RL difference in OLMo but not in Tulu — is this a recipe-specific artifact of RLVR data or optimizer settings?
- Does RL with verifiable rewards strengthen cue-following because cues appear in RL prompts as (spurious) scaffolding, and would cue-free RL prompts avoid the susceptibility increase?
