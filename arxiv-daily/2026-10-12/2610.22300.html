Why this matters

What a model can be taught is set by the geometry of what it already knows — and a cheap linear probe predicts it, before any fine-tuning.

Critical edge

The evidence is correlational: the causal test — intervening on the linear structure itself and watching generalization change — is absent.

01Methodology — the relabeling pipeline

Per (model, relation) pair, six moves:

  1. Screen 70 pretrained relations (Hernandez 2024 · BEAR-big · 6 new Town relations): each needs ≥5 answers, ≥5 subjects per answer, ≥25 fine-tuning subjects on every model.

  2. Relabel by random bijection — every answer maps to one animal label (Europe → crab); subjects sharing an answer share a label. The new relation is unknowable a priori but inherits the old one's equivalence structure.

  3. Fine-tune fully for 20 epochs under 4 templates in a fictional-novel frame; a 5th template is held out. Training accuracy hits 1.00 everywhere — undertraining ruled out.

  4. Measure three things. Recall: pretrained class-balanced candidate accuracy. Generalization: held-out subjects under the held-out template. Pre-linearity: linear probe on 16-dim PCA-whitened last-token subject states, nested cross-validation, majority baseline subtracted — hyperparameters fixed in advance.

  5. Control via partial correlations (recall, answer/subject counts, infini-gram subject frequency on Dolma v1.7), bijection + seed re-randomization, and 13 alternative WordNet label sets.

  6. Attribute errors: decode wrong labels back through the bijection, Shapley-split across five explanation families against shuffled and chance baselines.

03Strengths

  • First systematic study of which known facts support learning new ones; the random-relabeling paradigm makes held-out success statistical generalization by construction.
  • Remarkably consistent effect: r = 0.87–0.92 across 11 diverse models, base and post-trained, 2B–27B.
  • Exhaustive robustness battery — joint four-way controls, bijection/seed re-randomization, 13 label categories — leaves the association essentially unchanged.
  • The probe predicts how models fail, not just whether they will (21.5% vs. 8.8% shuffled error attribution).
  • Full reproducibility package (code, prompts, label sets, relation splits); probe hyperparameters fixed in advance, not tuned.

04Limitations

  • Correlational design — no intervention on the linear structure itself; "linearity enables generalization" remains a well-supported hypothesis.
  • Head-knowledge bias — the ≥5-answers / ≥25-subjects filter excludes sparse, long-tail relations; results are relation-level aggregates over 70 relations.
  • Small-model skew — mostly 2B–9B plus one 27B (a "tiny models" leaderboard); frontier-scale validity unmeasured.
  • Coarse scoring — first-token-only, one held-out template, few held-out subjects per relation.
  • Saturated regime — full-parameter, 20-epoch, 100%-train-accuracy fine-tuning; LoRA, early stopping, and in-context analogues untested; the frequency control is a Dolma proxy for non-OLMo corpora.

05Questions for further exploration

  1. Would causally intervening on the linear structure — rotating the probe-read subspace, or adding a decodable direction to inert relations — convert inert facts into active ones?
  2. Does Pre-linearity predict generalization under LoRA, early-stopped fine-tuning, or in-context learning, or is the effect specific to saturated full fine-tuning?
  3. Do sparse / long-tail relations (excluded by the filters) show the same Pre-linearity → Generalization link, or does subject frequency dominate there?
  4. Does post-training shift Pre-linearity itself — does instruction tuning make knowledge more or less linearly readable?
  5. Can Pre-linearity serve prospectively as a cheap screening metric for teachable relations, or predict knowledge-editing ripple effects?
  6. What explains the 51.7% of held-out errors that none of the five explanation families name — non-linear structure, attention-mediated recall, or label-space interference?
