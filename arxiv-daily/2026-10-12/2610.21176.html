Key Finding / Mechanistic Interpretability

LLMs find the N-back target but misread it — working-memory failure is interference at the readout, not imprecise attention.

Language Models Share a Readout Bottleneck in Working Memory

Why This Matters

Models often hold the right answer internally but fail to use it — for long-context recall, fixing the readout beats sharpening attention.

⚠ Critical Edge

The headline suppression intervention is an oracle — it requires already knowing which position is the target: a causal test of mechanism, not a deployable fix.

§How they adjudicated it

  1. TaskLetter N-back: 24-letter sequences, 4 lures each; models answer “m”/“−” by log-prob preference. Threshold calibrated on 200 sequences, frozen for 1,000 test sequences. Sensitivity = d′.
  2. LocalizeActivation patching isolates task-carrying attention heads — the dominant head alone recovers 16–73% of the answer difference at N=2.
  3. Decompose errorsOn misses: was attention on the target line? Did head outputs already encode the wrong answer? On lure false alarms: which line got the attention?
  4. ProbeLogistic-regression probes on the residual stream at nine relative depths decode match-vs-lure, scored by AUC.
  5. InterveneSharpen top-3 heads vs. suppress distractor letter-identity content (19-D subspace, half-strength, every layer, target spared) vs. five matched controls; paired bootstrap 95% CIs, 2,000 resamples.
  6. Theory check720 minimal one-layer transformers trained from scratch, tested against Propositions 1–4 on what softmax attention can and cannot compute.

15 pretrained models — Pythia (70M–6.9B), GPT-2 (124M–1.5B), Qwen2.5 (0.5B–14B), N = 2–4 — yield 43 analyzable model × load cells after excluding two low-baseline cells.

§Strengths

  • Opposite-prediction logic between the two accounts, executed across 15 models, 3 families, and a 200× size range — the strongest “same mechanism” evidence available without training new models.
  • Three independent evidence types: correlational (error-resolved attention, probes), causal (five interventions with matched controls), and theoretical (propositions verified on 720 minimal models).
  • Unusual honesty: the author revises their own earlier precision account and fairly quantifies the competitor's intervention (+0.11 vs +0.60) under held-out conditions.
  • Attention sharpness is already near-optimal — a concrete, counterintuitive negative result for anyone hoping to fix retrieval by temperature tuning.

§Limitations

  • One synthetic paradigm — 24-item letter N-back with correct answers shown line-by-line; no evidence yet for real-language working-memory demands.
  • All mechanistic analyses stop at 14B parameters, far below frontier scale; circuit-level claims may not extrapolate.
  • The letter-identity subspace and target-sparing suppression are experiment-internal constructs — no method offered for a model to run the fix on its own.
  • “Linearly decodable” is equated with “available to the readout” by assumption; no amnesic-probing causal confirmation of the probe directions.
  • Half-strength removal beats full removal, and the theory does not explain why — the dose-response of suppression is unmodeled.

§Questions for further exploration

  • Can target-sparing suppression be approximated without oracle knowledge — e.g., sparing only the position the model's own attention most strongly selects?
  • Does the readout bottleneck persist at 30B–70B+ scale, where the task circuit is fully distributed?
  • Does the locate-vs-read dissociation appear in realistic loads — long-document QA, multi-constraint instructions, distant coreference — where lures are semantic rather than lexical?
  • Why does half-strength interference removal beat full removal, and is the optimum predictable from the Proposition 1 model?
  • A selective state-space model (Mamba-style) also declines with N — what is the recurrent architecture's equivalent of the readout bottleneck?
  • Could fine-tuning only the final readout map turn “decodable but unused” information into decisions, without touching the circuit?

Credibility: single-author work (Dongyu Gong, Yale) that partly overturns the author's own earlier precision account (Gong & Zhang, 2024); replicates the competing interference intervention of Xiong et al. (COLM 2026) under held-out configuration; paired-bootstrap 95% CIs, Wilcoxon tests, and five distinct control interventions. arXiv v1 (15 Oct 2026), not yet peer-reviewed; no code release mentioned in the main text.
