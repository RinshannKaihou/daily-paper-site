Task-Phase-Conditioned Expert Routing Is Not a Reliable Proxy for Quantization Sensitivity in MoE FFN Tensors ↗

Yifan Liu (MCS student, UIUC) — single-author preprint, no peer review ·2026-10-12 ·arXiv:2610.20826 (v1, cs.LG) ·model: Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B in ik_llama.cpp

Key metric

z ≈ 4.3SE worse

Score-guided quantization decisively degrades vs the uniform baseline at ~2.7 bpw (mean KLD 0.0343 → 0.0382). A depth-matched random-layer control lands within 1 SE of every score-guided allocation.

Why this matters

For local MoE deployment below ~3 bpw: raise the global quantization floor — smarter layer reallocation cannot rescue tail quality.

Direct practical warning for anyone building routing- or activation-guided mixed-precision quantizers.

Critical edge

Routing was profiled under ~4 bpw surrogate quantization with no BF16 agreement check — the input signal may be perturbed by the very noise under study.

Also: one model, one domain (code), and no ground-truth sensitivity reference — so only this proxy is shown to fail.

01Methodology — routing → score → allocation

  1. 1
    Curate & sample. 186 coding prompts (HumanEval-X · HumanEval-Rust · LiveCodeBench Lite) → 30 stratified by source, language, difficulty for profiling.
  2. 2
    Capture routing. ik_llama.cpp patch records per-token top-k expert picks + gate probabilities at every MoE block; surrogate capture (Q8_0 router over ~4 bpw experts) for non-BF16 hardware. Three capture bugs found and fixed.
  3. 3
    Split phases. prompt (prefill) / reasoning (to first </think>) / response; token counts differ ~15× (8.5K / 128K / 27K) → z-score within each phase.
  4. 4
    Score layers. 4 signals — routing entropy, expert-usage CV, cross-phase Jaccard, top-1 gate prob — max-aggregated across phases.
  5. 5
    Allocate byte-neutrally. Downgrade the "fragile" pool to fund rung-by-rung upgrades of the "robust" pool; parity vs uniform enforced twice (projected + measured bytes), SHA-1 dedupe cache.
  6. 6
    Tune & evaluate. Coordinate descent over 3 free weights (≤18 quantizations); score mean KLD vs a Q8_0 reference on a held-out ~331K-token The Stack v3 corpus (ctx 512, 21 languages).

Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B · 39 profiled layers (blk.0–38; blk.39 held) · 256 routed experts · regimes: flat ~4 bpw (iq3_ks…iq4_k), steep ~2.7 bpw (iq2_ks…iq3_k)

02Results — no win in either regime

Across both quality regimes, every score-guided allocation loses or ties the uniform baseline — and the loss lives in the KLD tail, not the bulk.

  • Tail injection, not bulk damage: median KLD 0.0016 → ≤0.0019, max stays 26.4–27.5 (mean ≈ 20× median); the whole mean loss is p99 tail mass. PPL is a wash (~2.00 flat regime).
  • Zero-sum ledger: upgrading 17 layers one rung (+1.25 bpw each) forces 19 of 39 layers to the IQ2 floor (−1.12 bpw each).
  • The phase signal is real but useless: prompt routing is sharper (median norm-entropy 0.863 vs 0.907 / 0.912; expert coverage 0.89 vs ≈1.00) — yet uncorrelated with quantization cost.
  • Slice-bias disclosure: a 12K-token validation slice mis-estimated KLD by ~24% (0.019325 vs 0.014725) and scrambled rankings — all finals on the full corpus.

◈Mechanism — why reallocation loses below the Pareto elbow

The IQ2 floor tier's quality cliff is charged per tensor, not per layer: swapping which layers take the floor does not change the cost, which arrives as p99 KLD inflation (0.437 → 0.483–0.495) on every reallocated candidate — score-guided and random alike — while the median barely moves.

↳ Takeaway: below the elbow, fixed-budget reallocation cannot help — only raising the global floor (more budget) mitigates catastrophic tail injection.

03Strengths

  • Reusable routing-capture patch for ik_llama.cpp — per-token top-k + gate probabilities at every MoE block; the three capture bugs and fixes are documented, and surrogate capture works where BF16 cannot run.
  • Textbook hygiene: byte-exact budget parity (projected + measured), held-out eval disjoint from profiling data, contamination filters, seed-frozen matched random control, replication across 3 weight vectors and 2 seeds.
  • Honest, mechanistic negative result — explains why reallocation loses rather than hiding it, and discloses that its best point came from flipping the allocation direction… and still losing.
  • Public-service warning on validation-slice bias: ~24% KLD mis-estimate and non-transferable rankings on a 12K-token slice.

04Limitations

  • One model, one domain: Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B (hybrid linear-attention MoE) on code only; the planned GLM-5.3 transfer was never executed.
  • Possibly circular input: routing profiled at ~4 bpw with no BF16 routing-agreement check — the signal may be perturbed before scoring even starts.
  • No sensitivity ground truth (no single-layer ablation, no Hessian correlation) — the ceiling of a good signal at the same budget is unknown.
  • Tiny search space: 3 free weights, ≤18 quantizations, layer-level ±1–2 rungs; expert-level allocation — where GEMQ/MoPEQ operate — untested.
  • Narrow evaluation: KLD/PPL/top-1 at context 512 on a custom corpus; no downstream pass@1; nothing released as code or data.

05Questions for further exploration

  1. Would direct-sensitivity signals (Hessian trace, single-layer ablation) fail the same way at the same granularity and budget — is the flaw in the proxy or in layer-level reallocation itself?
  2. Does expert-level precision allocation guided by phase-conditioned routing change the outcome, especially in the steep regime where the floor-cliff dominates?
  3. How much does surrogate capture perturb routing — would a BF16 vs ~4 bpw agreement measurement show the input was degraded before scoring?
  4. Does KLD tail injection (p99 0.437 → ~0.49, unchanged median) translate into measurable downstream failures (HumanEval/LiveCodeBench pass@1), or is it invisible at task level?
  5. Do the results hold beyond code — the reasoning/roleplay/chat/trivia prompt sets were built but never used — and for dense-attention or larger MoE models?
