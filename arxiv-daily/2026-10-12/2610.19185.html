01

Methodology — how setups were judged

  • 26 monolingual 2B models — identical architecture & recipe, only the pretraining corpus differs (Norwegian · Finnish · French · Spanish; native-web vs machine-translated corpora)
  • × 11 checkpoints each, 2B → 100B training tokens
  • → 80 tasks: NorEval (extended), FIN-bench-v2, FrenchBench, SpanishBench
  • → 1,488 classification setups — every combination of 8 setup aspects
  • → scale check on OLMo 3 7B · Apertus 8B · Prelude 9B
① Monotonicity
Spearman ρ between training tokens and score — a good setup's scores rise with training.
② Non-randomness
(best final score − random baseline) ÷ checkpoint noise; unit-free, comparable across rescalings.
③ Ranking consistency
Kendall's τ between model rankings at adjacent checkpoints within token-aligned windows.

Meta-measures become percentile ranks averaged into a 0–1 score per setup. Three decision procedures — best complete setup, option ranking, per-option win rate — and recommendations are tiered by cross-method, cross-language agreement.

02

Results — beyond the headline

pairwise win rates unless noted
  • Setups flip winners: MADLAD-400 ranks highest under setup A (baseline · cloze · accuracy) but lowest under setup B (z-score · MCF · AnsProb); machine-translated corpora jump bottom→top.
  • Cloze beats multiple-choice at 2B — small base models haven't learned the MC format; at ≥7B, MCF's ranking consistency jumps 33% → 71%.
  • 5-shot beats 0-shot (70% win, 84% on monotonicity) — the only choice unanimous in all 4 languages at both scales.
  • Char-length normalization wins everywhere except Spanish (55%); PMI's non-randomness edge collapses at scale (66% → 17%).
  • Max vs mean prompt aggregation barely matters — part of max's edge is mechanical (a max is ≥ a mean by construction).
  • Cross-scale transfer is moderate: setup scores correlate at ρ = 0.30–0.66 between 2B and ≥7B; random sampling beats greedy on Norwegian generative tasks (64%).

04

Strengths

  • Exceptionally controlled grid — 26 architecturally identical 2B models differing only in corpus, 11 checkpoints each: the cleanest substrate for studying evaluation-signal quality.
  • Three complementary decision procedures; the recipe is derived from their agreement, robust to any single method's quirks.
  • Intellectual honesty: quantifies the mechanical max-aggregation artifact, explicitly downgrades underpowered scale claims.
  • Immediately actionable tiered checklist at near-zero cost (0.93 vs 0.95).
  • Real multilingual breadth, including Norwegian and Finnish, plus a reusable NorEval extension.
05

Limitations

  • No ground truth for "best" setup — the three meta-measures are proxies a downstream-irrelevant capability could still satisfy.
  • Uneven coverage: formulation only in Finnish/Norwegian, decoding only Norwegian 2B — several headline comparisons are narrower than the four-language framing suggests.
  • Scale evidence rests on 3 heterogeneous LLMs — scale confounded with architecture, data, and token budget.
  • Equal-weight percentile averaging may pick different setups than weighting or Pareto-front alternatives.
  • Findings specific to early pretraining (2B–100B tokens, often near chance); transfer to post-training untested.
  • ~18,000 GPU hours — independent replication of the grid impractical for most labs.
06

Questions for further exploration

  • Does the recipe hold for mid/late pretraining, SFT/RLHF — where accuracy and MCF are already competitive?
  • Do high-monotonicity setups at 2B better predict final downstream performance or human preference?
  • How do recommendations transfer to English-centric (OLMES-style) pipelines, non-European languages, agentic or long-context benchmarks?
  • Is there a principled weighting of the three meta-measures instead of equal percentile averaging?
  • Does z-score/min-max circularity — statistics computed over the compared model pool — bias small-pool rankings?
  • Can the τ = −0.6 ranking reversal be predicted a priori from setup properties, without running the full grid?
