Why this matters

Faithfulness-reward RAG training is exposed to copying hacks; the diagnostic — ask what a reward pays without its intended behavior — generalizes to any proxy metric.

Critical edge

The copy test is purely lexical (5-word verbatim overlap): a close paraphrase counts as “authored” and earns full credit — the no-hack guarantee is empirical, not structural.

01 · Methodology — ASC in four moves

Two surgical changes to GRPO. No new model, no critic, no penalty weights to tune.

  1. Why copying pays. A sentence copied from the passages is supported almost by construction — authored sentences are unsupported 2.3–4.4× as often as copied ones (3.7× on Qwen3.5-9B), so the reward slope R = s_a + ρ(s_c − s_a) rewards every copied sentence.
  2. Sentence credit (Eq. 3). A sentence earns credit only if it is authored (copy share < τ = 0.5, via ≥5-word verbatim matches), non-vacuous (the checker rejects it given the question alone), and supported — with graded credit φ(p) = max(0, 1 − ln p / ln κ): zero at threshold, one at certainty.
  3. Sentence-level advantage (Eq. 5). Each credit is standardized over all M sentences sampled for the question (μ, σ shared across the G answers); every token takes its sentence's advantage. Proposition 1: copied, vacuous or unsupported sentences provably rank last and are never reinforced.
  4. Cost. One extra question-only checker call per authored sentence.

The copy penalty's 0.92 mean error rate is bought with 82.3% vacuous share and 79.7% refusals; its EM is 20.22.

  • Only method across all 20 settings to cut authored errors while writing more supported sentences — median −38% Err, +46% Supp; held-out corpora cut deeper (38%) than in-domain (32%).
  • Blind human annotators prefer ASC over base 22 vs 12 (of 75 pairs), over GRPO+KL 28 vs 14; on 250 ELI5 questions, authored-unsupported falls 33.2% → 24.8% (p = 0.01).
  • Two third-family LLM judges prefer no baseline over base — but rate ASC on par (51.0 / 49.1%) and above every baseline head-to-head (56.4% vs GRPO+KL; 64.6 / 68.9% vs copy penalty).
  • Robustness: gains confirmed in 20/20 settings by MiniCheck-Flan-T5, 19/20 by AlignScore; copy share stays below base even at τ = 0.4, and only 9.2% of ASC's authored sentences sit just under the copy threshold (base: 11.3%).

03 · Strengths

  • Pinpoints a real, under-examined failure mode of faithfulness RL, with a three-line mathematical account of why copying pays.
  • Minimal, surgical fix: two changes to GRPO plus a provable no-shortcut-earns-advantage guarantee (Proposition 1).
  • Exemplary evaluation hygiene: cross-family held-out judges, three held-out corpora, blind human annotation with significance tests, anti-gaming checks on the copy threshold.
  • Finding 3 explains why answer-level GRPO cannot fix the hack (72.0% of advantage-pushed sentences were copied), not merely that it fails.
  • Complete ablations — copy test, vacuity test, graded credit, sentence baseline each measurably contribute.

04 · Limitations

  • Lexical copy test only: paraphrases count as authored — no semantic-copy detection is tested, so paraphrase-copying remains possible.
  • Everything downstream inherits the reward checker's blind spots (Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B), like every compared method.
  • Scale: models up to 9B, two training corpora; baselines trained once each — headline comparisons are not seed-matched.
  • Rewards grounded writing, not abstention: ASC refuses less than base even where the passages cannot answer and refusing is right.
  • For applications that genuinely want verbatim quotation, plain faithfulness rewards already deliver it — ASC's target is narrower.

05 · Questions for further exploration

  1. Would semantic (embedding- or NLI-based) copy detection change ASC's behavior — and can a policy be trained to paraphrase-copy past the 5-word test?
  2. Does the authored / vacuity / support credit transfer to other GRPO variants (DAPO-style clipping, GiGPO step baselines) or process rewards over reasoning steps?
  3. Can a joint objective keep the error-rate gains while preserving grounded refusal on unanswerable questions?
  4. Do the findings hold at 30–70B scale with stronger open checkers, where base copy share may differ substantially?
  5. How sensitive is the ASC-vs-baseline gap under adversarial or domain-shifted passages where all checkers are unreliable?

Credibility: training checker never used for headline evaluation; error rates judged by held-out Gemma-4-31B, content by MiniCheck-Flan-T5, robustness by AlignScore; blind human annotation; code via anonymous.4open.science (under review).
