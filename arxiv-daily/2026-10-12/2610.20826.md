> **一句话总结：** 本文构建了一条完整的 MoE 路由行为采集与分析管线（在 ik_llama.cpp 中对 Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B 按 prompt/推理/回复三阶段逐层捕获专家路由），用熵、变异系数、跨阶段 Jaccard 与 top-1 专家强度组成复合敏感度分数来指导逐层混合精度量化，结果是一个诚实且设计严谨的阴性结论：在任何质量档位下该分数都无法击败均匀量化基线（~4 bpw 档统计中性 z≈0.94，~2.7 bpw 档决定性变差 z>4 SE），且随机选层控制组与分数引导组差距在 1 个标准误之内——路由统计并非量化敏感度的可靠代理。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author negative-result paper that tests whether phase-conditioned (prompt/CoT/response) expert-routing statistics can guide budget-neutral layer-wise mixed-precision quantization of the routed-expert FFN tensors in Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B. Using a routing-capture patch to ik_llama.cpp, a 4-signal composite score (routing entropy, CV, cross-phase Jaccard, top-1 gate probability), and coordinate descent over the score weights, the method is evaluated by KLD vs a Q8_0 reference on a ~331K-token held-out The Stack v3 code corpus. On the flat ~4 bpw regime, the best of 8 candidates reaches KLD 0.015276 vs 0.014725 for the uniform IQ4_KS/IQ3_K baseline (statistically neutral, z≈+0.94); on the steep ~2.7 bpw regime all candidates lose decisively (KLD 0.038142–0.038249 vs 0.034321 for uniform, z>4 SE), with 99th-percentile KLD inflating from 0.437 to 0.487–0.492. A depth/budget-matched random-layer-selection control (KLD 0.037728–0.037808) performs within 1 SE of the score-guided allocations, showing the composite score carries no selection signal over random — the loss is structural, caused by mandatory IQ2-floor downgrades injecting KLD tail mass, not by a bad heuristic.

## 核心贡献

- **路由行为采集管线**：修改 ik_llama.cpp，逐 token 捕获每个 MoE 层的 top-k 专家选择与门控概率，并按 prompt（prefill）、推理（首个 `</think>` 之前）、响应三阶段做分割统计；该管线适用于标准 top-k MoE 架构，且通过"代理路由捕获"（Q8_0 高精度路由器 + 量化专家）在无法运行 BF16 的硬件上也能采集。
- **复合敏感度分数与坐标下降**：将四个逐层信号——路由熵（决断性）、专家使用变异系数（集中度）、跨阶段 Jaccard（阶段脆弱性）、top-1 门控概率（置信度）——在各阶段内 z-score 归一化、跨阶段取最大值聚合，再用仅调 3 个自由权重的坐标下降（最多 18 个候选量化）优化逐层比特分配，且全部候选在构造上与均匀基线字节级预算中性（干跑投影 + 量化后实测双重校验，SHA-1 去重缓存）。
- **严谨的阴性结果**：在 ~4 bpw（平坦区）与 ~2.7 bpw（陡峭区）两个质量档位下，重分配均无法击败均匀基线；平坦区最佳候选仅 +0.94 SE（统计中性），陡峭区决定性变差（z≈4.3 SE），且该结论在三个独立权重向量上复现。
- **随机选层控制实验**：在深度、档位与预算严格匹配的前提下随机分配量化档，其 KLD（0.037728/0.037808）落在分数引导组（0.03814–0.03825）的 1 个标准误之内甚至略优——证明复合分数相对随机选择没有任何选择信号。
- **失效机理分析**：陡峭区的损失是结构性的——预算中性重分配要求 19/39 层强制降到 IQ2 底档才能为 17 层升级买单，而底档的代价按张量（而非按层）以 KLD 尾部注入的形式收取（p99 从 0.437 升至 0.483–0.495），与选哪些层无关；均值 KLD（0.034）约为中位数（0.0016）的 20 倍，均值被尾部主导。
- **方法学透明度**：作者主动披露 12K token 验证切片与全量 331K 语料的 KLD 不一致（切片 0.019325 vs 全量 0.014725，偏低约 24%），最终全部结果以全量语料报告。

## Key Contributions

- **A routing-capture and profiling pipeline** for ik_llama.cpp that records per-token top-k expert selections and gate probabilities at every MoE block, segmented into prompt / CoT-reasoning / response phases via the prefill boundary and the first `</think>` token; the "surrogate routing capture" trick (Q8_0 router deciding top-k over quantized IQ4_KS/IQ3_K experts) makes profiling feasible on hardware that cannot run full BF16.
- **A composite sensitivity score** over four per-layer signals — routing decisiveness (1 − normalized entropy), concentration (coefficient of variation of expert usage), phase-fragility (1 − min cross-phase Jaccard overlap), and top-1 expert strength — z-scored within each phase (token counts differ ~15x: 8.5k/128k/26.7k), max-aggregated across phases, with weights tuned by a cache-aware, resumable 3-axis coordinate descent (at most 18 distinct quantizations; the score is consumed only via its layer ranking, so one weight is anchored at 1).
- **A budget-neutral allocation mechanism** that ranks layers by composite percentile, downgrades the fragile pool to fund rung-by-rung upgrades of the robust pool, and enforces byte-exact budget parity against the uniform baseline with both a pre-quantize dry-run projection gate and a post-quantize measured-byte gate; identical tier maps collapse to one SHA-1 cache entry so no allocation is quantized twice.
- **A clean negative result with controls**: across the flat ~4 bpw and steep ~2.7 bpw regimes, no score-guided allocation beats its uniform baseline, and a seed-frozen random-selection control at matched depth, tiers, and budget lands within ~1 SE of the score-guided candidates — establishing that phase-conditioned routing statistics carry no allocation signal over random layer choice.
- **A mechanistic explanation of the loss**: below the Pareto elbow, any budget-neutral reallocation must push ~half the layers to the IQ2 floor tier; the floor tier's quality cliff is a per-tensor property that injects 99th-percentile KLD mass (0.437 → 0.483–0.495) regardless of which layers carry it, so the only real mitigation is raising the global floor (more budget), not smarter reallocation.

## Methodology

The pipeline runs end-to-end in ik_llama.cpp on Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B (40 layers plus an MTP head; layers blk.0–38 receive dynamic tiers, terminal blk.39 is held at baseline; 39 layers profiled, 256 routed experts). A pool of 186 coding prompts is curated from HumanEval-X (Python, C++, Java, JS, Go), HumanEval-Rust, and LiveCodeBench Lite, from which 30 prompts are stratified-sampled across source, language, and difficulty for profiling. Routing is captured under a surrogate quantization (~4 bpw experts, Q8_0 router/attention/SSM) — the argument being that the Q8_0 router still decides which top-k experts fire, keeping the signal faithful; the authors patched three capture bugs (per-request IDs for prompt boundaries, disabling graph reuse, and raising batch/ubatch above the max prompt length to stop silent corruption).

Phase segmentation splits each generation into prompt (prefill), reasoning (up to and including the first `</think>`), and response, with a server-side reasoning budget of 8192 tokens and 32768 max tokens per request; all 30 prompts emitted the close delimiter. Because segment token counts differ ~15x (8,508 prompt / 128,287 reasoning / 26,721 response positions), each signal is z-scored within its own segment before max-aggregation, which the authors use to flag layers that are extreme in any phase.

The allocation step assigns each profiled layer a (down tier, gate/up tier) pair from an IQK ladder within ±1–2 rungs of the regime baseline — flat regime (~4 bpw): iq3_ks…iq4_k with IQ4_KS down / IQ3_K gate-up bases; steep regime (~2.7 bpw): iq2_ks…iq3_k with IQ3_KS down / IQ2_KL gate-up bases. A budget-fit algorithm downgrades the low-ranked "fragile" pool to build a byte surplus and upgrades the high-ranked "robust" pool one rung at a time, with `max_step` and `stay_frac` knobs controlling aggressiveness; byte-neutrality versus the uniform baseline is enforced twice (projected and measured). Coordinate descent then tunes the three free weight ratios (objective: minimum mean KLD) over two rounds with adaptive multiplier grids, deduplicated by tier-map hash; notably, the best measured point came from flipping the allocation direction (giving more precision to robust/uniform layers rather than fragile/distinctive ones), and it still failed to beat uniform.

Evaluation uses a single `llama-perplexity` run with `--kl-divergence` against a Q8_0 reference (justified as a BF16 proxy by prior Qwen3 quantization studies), yielding mean KLD, PPL, top-1 token agreement, and the KLD tail (p95/p99/median/max) on a ~331K-token corpus (~647 chunks at context 512) streamed from The Stack v3 across 21 languages, restricted to permissively-licensed, post-June-2024, non-vendored code, and held out from the HumanEval-X/LiveCodeBench profiling data. The authors also disclose that an initial 12K-token validation slice mis-estimated full-corpus KLD by ~24% and did not preserve candidate rankings, so all reported numbers use the full corpus.

## Results & Highlights

- **Flat regime (~4 bpw), full 331K-token The Stack v3 corpus: no win.** Uniform baseline (IQ4_KS down / IQ3_K gate-up) reaches mean KLD 0.014725 ± 0.000430 with 97.5% top-1 agreement; the best of 8 coordinate-descent candidates (C1) reaches 0.015276 ± 0.000400 (97.4% top-1), i.e., statistically neutral at z ≈ +0.94, and all 8 candidates score slightly worse than uniform (z = +0.94 to +1.79). PPL is a wash at ~2.00 across all candidates.
- **Steep regime (~2.7 bpw): decisive loss.** Uniform baseline reaches mean KLD 0.034321 ± 0.000608 (top-1 95.8%, KLD p99 0.437), while all three score-guided candidates (cv 0.5/1.5/3.0) land at KLD 0.038142–0.038249 (top-1 95.6%, KLD p99 0.487–0.492) — a combined z ≈ 4.3 SE degradation, replicated across three independent weight vectors.
- **Random-selection control: the score adds nothing over random.** At matched depth, tiers, and budget, random layer selection (seeds 42/1337) achieves KLD 0.037728 and 0.037808 (p99 0.483/0.495, top-1 95.7/95.6%) — within ~1 SE of, and marginally better than, the score-guided 0.03814–0.03825, showing the composite score carries no selection signal.
- **The loss is tail-injection, not bulk degradation.** In the steep regime the baseline KLD distribution is extremely heavy-tailed (median 0.0016, p95 0.119, max 26.4 — the mean 0.034 is ~20x the median); reallocation moves the median only from 0.0016 to at most 0.0019 and the max stays 26.4–27.5, but p99 rises from 0.437 to 0.483–0.495 on every reallocated candidate (score-guided and random alike), accounting for the 0.0034–0.0039 mean-KLD loss.
- **Structural accounting of the zero-sum constraint.** In the steep regime, upgrading 17 layers by one rung (+1.25 bpw/layer) forced downgrading 19 of 39 layers to the IQ2 floor (−1.12 bpw/layer); because the IQ2 cliff toll is charged per tensor rather than per layer, swapping which layers take the floor does not change the cost — implying that below the Pareto elbow, fixed-budget reallocation cannot help and only raising the global floor (more budget) mitigates catastrophic tail injection.
- **Routing statistics themselves show marked phase asymmetry** (30 prompts, 39 layers): prompt-phase routing is less diffuse (median normalized entropy 0.863 vs 0.907 reasoning / 0.912 response) and leaves some experts never selected (coverage as low as 0.89 vs 0.996–1.00 in reasoning/response) — the phase signal the authors hoped to exploit is real, but it does not correlate with quantization cost.
- **Validation-slice bias quantified**: the uniform IQ3_K baseline's KLD was 0.019325 on a 12K-token slice but 0.014725 on the full 331K-token corpus (~24% lower), and candidate rankings did not transfer — all final results are reported on the full corpus.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author work by Yifan Liu, an MCS (master's) student at UIUC — not an established quantization research lab, and this is an arXiv preprint (v1, cs.LG, October 2026) with no peer review. Credibility is nonetheless above average for the venue: the paper cites and positions itself precisely against the relevant MoE quantization literature (MxMoE ICML'25, GEMQ ICML'26, MoPEQ, EAQuant, MoEQuant) and dense-model sensitivity methods (HAWQ, GPTQ/OPTQ, imatrix); the experimental hygiene is careful (budget-neutrality enforced by two byte gates, a held-out evaluation corpus disjoint from the profiling prompts, contamination filters on The Stack v3, identical perplexity flags across reference/baseline/candidates, a seed-frozen random control, replication across three weight vectors, and a disclosed validation-slice bias); and full launch configurations, the coordinate-descent log, and the dedupe-cache schema are included as appendices. The honest framing ("We do not describe a method that beats existing quantization types") and the explicit limitations section (Section 5.3) further support trustworthiness, though the negative claim rests entirely on one model and one domain.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The routing-capture pipeline for ik_llama.cpp and the phase-conditioned (prompt/CoT/response) framing are genuinely new — no prior MoE quantization work conditions its signal on task phase. But the headline contribution is a negative result confirming, at the layer level, what MoPEQ already hinted at from expert activation frequency: behavioral usage statistics do not predict quantization sensitivity.
- **Rigor**: Strong within its scope — budget-neutrality is byte-verified twice, the random control is depth/tier/budget-matched, the steep-regime loss replicates across three weight vectors and two random seeds, and the validation-slice bias (12K-token slice KLD 0.019325 vs full-corpus 0.014725) is disclosed rather than hidden. The coordinate descent is, however, tiny: 3 free weight ratios, at most 18 quantizations, 8 final flat-regime candidates — the weight space searched is so small that "routing statistics fail" partly means "these four hand-chosen signals under a max-aggregation with these ladders fail."
- **Reproducibility**: Good on paper — open engine (ik_llama.cpp), named quant tiers with bpw values (IQ2_KS 2.1875 … IQ4_K 4.5), exact launch flags, and the cache schema — but nothing is released as code or data, the 30 profiling prompts are only described (not listed), and the composite-score implementation details (e.g., epsilon, percentile splits for the fragile/robust pools) live in prose.
- **Significance**: Useful as a caution for practitioners building routing/activation-guided mixed-precision quantizers, and the per-tensor floor-cliff + KLD-tail-injection mechanism is a valuable empirical observation for anyone quantizing MoE FFNs below ~3 bpw. The constructive takeaway (raise the global floor instead of reallocating below the elbow) is modest but actionable.
- **Red flags**: (1) Single model (Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B, a hybrid linear-attention MoE) and single domain (code) — the planned GLM-5.3 transfer was never executed, so generalization is untested; (2) the surrogate routing capture measures routing under ~4 bpw quantization, and the authors admit there is **no BF16 routing-agreement check**, so the input signal itself may be perturbed by the very noise being studied — a partial circularity; (3) there is **no direct-sensitivity ground truth** (no single-layer ablation, no Hessian-trace correlation), so the paper cannot bound what a *good* signal would achieve — it can only conclude this particular proxy achieves nothing; (4) the evaluation is KLD/PPL/top-1 agreement only, with no downstream task accuracy (e.g., HumanEval pass@1 under the quantized models), and the ~331K-token custom Stack v3 corpus is not a standard benchmark; (5) layer-level granularity means the failure does not rule out expert-level allocation, which the authors themselves flag as future work; (6) evaluation at context 512 only, so long-context behavior under these quantizations is unmeasured.

## Strengths

- A carefully engineered, openly described routing-capture pipeline (with the three capture bugs and their fixes documented) that is reusable for standard top-k MoE architectures and on hardware that cannot run BF16.
- Textbook experimental hygiene: byte-verified budget neutrality, held-out evaluation data disjoint from profiling data, contamination filtering, a matched random-selection control, replication across weight vectors and seeds, and SHA-1 dedupe caching that makes the search resumable and never re-quantizes the same allocation.
- The negative result is reported honestly and mechanistically — the paper explains *why* reallocation loses (per-tensor IQ2 floor cliff charging tail KLD regardless of layer choice) rather than hiding the failure, and even discloses that its best measured point came from flipping the allocation direction and still losing.
- The methodological note on validation-slice bias (12K-token slice vs 331K-token full corpus, ~24% KLD discrepancy and non-transferable rankings) is a genuinely useful warning for anyone doing cheap GPU-side model selection on small eval slices.

## Limitations

- Single model (Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B, hybrid linear-attention MoE), single domain (code for both profiling and evaluation), and 30 profiling prompts whose sufficiency was never validated; datasets for reasoning, roleplay, chat, and trivia were built but unused.
- Routing was profiled under surrogate quantization with no BF16 routing-agreement comparison, so the proxy's input signal may itself be quantization-perturbed.
- No ground truth for sensitivity (no single-layer ablation, no Hessian correlation), so the ceiling of a *direct* sensitivity signal at the same budget is unknown.
- The search space is small: only layer-level allocation within ±1–2 ladder rungs, three free score weights, at most 18 quantizations; expert-level granularity — arguably the more natural MoE axis, and where GEMQ/MoPEQ operate — is untested.
- Evaluation is KLD/PPL/top-1 only at context 512 on a custom Stack v3 corpus; no downstream coding accuracy is reported, and the terminal layer (blk.39) was excluded from allocation, which the authors note could have influenced results slightly.

## 相关性

本文与本地部署大模型高效推理的研究兴趣高度相关：它直接研究了 MoE 模型（Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B）在 llama.cpp 生态下的混合精度量化分配问题，并用严谨的对照实验否定了"路由行为可指导量化比特分配"这一直观假设，对设计 MoE 量化与压缩方法（尤其是超低比特场景下应提高全局底档而非重分配）具有直接的实践警示意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the negative result persist with direct-sensitivity signals (Hessian trace, single-layer ablation) at the *same* layer granularity and budget — i.e., is the failure in the proxy or in layer-level reallocation itself?
- Does expert-level (rather than layer-level) precision allocation guided by phase-conditioned routing statistics change the outcome, especially in the steep regime where the floor-tier cliff dominates?
- How much does surrogate capture perturb routing? A BF16 vs ~4 bpw routing-agreement measurement would quantify whether the input signal was degraded before the score was even computed.
- Does the KLD-tail-injection mechanism (p99 0.437 → ~0.49 with unchanged median) translate into measurable downstream failures such as HumanEval/LiveCodeBench pass@1 drops, or is the tail invisible at the task level?
- Do the results hold for non-code domains (the authors built but never used reasoning/roleplay/chat/trivia prompt sets) and for dense-attention or much larger MoE models like the planned GLM-5.3 transfer?
