> **一句话总结：** 提出 CTXGAP 基准,用同一组执行轨迹衍生的"任务状态探针"对比测量编码智能体的报告可恢复性:在 37 个 SWE-bench Verified 任务、4,799 个探针上,直接询问智能体能答对 93.6%、读完整执行上下文能恢复 92.7%,但只读累计报告仅能恢复 52.0%(差距 40.7 个百分点),即使修复已通过隐藏测试,最终检查点仍残留 34.8 个百分点差距,且"提及但细节不足"(而非完全未提及)是最大缺失类别。

> **TL;DR:** A Tencent measurement study introducing CTXGAP, a paired-probe benchmark that asks identical task-state questions from three views of the same coding-agent run: on 37 SWE-bench Verified tasks (4,799 valid probes), report-only readers recover 52.0% of task state vs 92.7% for full-context readers — a 40.7-point gap (95% CI [37.3, 43.8]) — and the gap persists at 34.8 points even at the final checkpoints of 30 hidden-test-verified fixes (+28/30 tasks positive). Full terminal scrollback only lifts recovery to 73.6%, and context-informed report rewrites at matched length add just ~8.3 points, so simply reading more terminal output or rewriting reports does not close the deficit; the largest failure category (55-59% of weighted gap mass, per two human annotators) is states that are mentioned but without enough supporting detail to answer questions about them.

## 核心贡献

- **定义了一个新的评测维度:任务状态可恢复性(task-state recoverability)。** 区分了三件常被混为一谈的事:任务是否成功、智能体被问及时能否回答、以及监督者仅凭例行报告能否恢复关键状态;CTXGAP 只测第三个维度,不依赖监督者翻阅完整执行日志。
- **提出配对视图测量方法(paired-view measurement)。** 从录制的执行轨迹检查点抽取事实原子(atom)并生成探针(问题+参考答案),让同一个观察者模型分别在"仅累计报告 U"与"完整执行上下文 T"下回答完全相同的问题,用类别感知加权与按 issue 聚类的 bootstrap 置信区间报告 Gap_view = Acc(T) − Acc(U);另设演员分叉(actor fork)让智能体本人在隔离检查点上答题作为辅助对照。
- **给出该差距的系统性实证:差距大、持久、且与监督相关。** 主样本 40.7 个百分点的差距在修复成功的任务上(34.8 点)、被独立标注为"监督相关"的状态上(35.3–36.4 点)、以及换用三种 harness 与独立 DeepSeek 执行/测量链时均复现。
- **诊断了缺失的"解剖结构"。** 双人标注表明最大类别是"提及但细节不足"(加权差距质量的 55.4%/59.2%),而"完全未提及"仅占 42.1%/33.7%;仅添加一句提及(mention-only)对恢复率无可检测提升(Δ = −0.002),必须补充可支撑答案的证据才行。
- **评估了干预的上界。** 在报告总长度匹配的条件下,基于完整上下文改写的"自然式"报告将恢复率提高 8.29 点,但"定向式"改写相对自然式无额外优势(+0.20 点,CI 含零)——说明差距难以靠写作技巧廉价弥补。

## Key Contributions

- **A new evaluation target — report-based task-state recoverability.** The paper cleanly separates task success, on-demand answerability, and supervisory visibility, arguing that issue-resolution benchmarks (SWE-bench) measure outcomes but not how much task state actually reaches a supervisor through routine reports.
- **A paired-view measurement instrument (CTXGAP).** Trajectory-grounded factual atoms (with task-state categories such as failure/progress/risk/decision and extractor-assigned importance levels) are turned into probes; the same observer model answers identical probes under the report-only view (U) versus the full recorded execution context (T), with a separate judge assigning full/partial/no credit, category-aware probe weighting, and issue-cluster bootstrap CIs. A validity filter retains only probes answerable from full context but not from the task description alone (4,799/4,855).
- **Robust empirical evidence of a large, persistent gap.** Across 37 SWE-bench Verified tasks: Acc(U) = 52.0% vs Acc(T) = 92.7% (gap 40.7 pts); the gap survives at final checkpoints of 30 hidden-test-verified fixes (34.8 pts, positive in 28/30 tasks), within model-assessed supervision-relevant states (35.3/36.4 pts), across three harnesses (Claude Code, Pi, DSH: 39.0–49.0 pts), an independent DeepSeek executor (52.7 pts), and a fully independent DeepSeek atom-to-grade measurement chain (39.8 pts).
- **An anatomy of what is missing.** Two human annotators (mention κ = .632, sufficiency κ = .638 on 195 stratified atoms) find "mentioned but insufficiently specified" is the largest category (55.4%/59.2% of weighted gap mass), and a controlled mention-only intervention yields no recovery gain — mention is not recoverability.
- **Intervention upper bounds under matched budgets.** Full terminal scrollback improves recovery to only 73.6% (still 19.1 pts below full context); context-informed natural rewrites at matched total length add +8.29 pts over original reports, with no detectable advantage for targeted rewrites (+0.20 pts, CI [−1.48, +1.78]).

## Methodology

The study builds on SWE-bench Verified: a 76-task inventory yields a 37-task headline subset (37 unique issues, 37 trajectories, 148 checkpoints; 23 development / 14 held-out tasks, split by issue ID). Each session follows a fixed protocol — investigate without edits, implement the fix, test and report, then assess residual risk — with trajectory prefixes and workspace snapshots recorded at each checkpoint, and hidden tests run against restored snapshots to label outcomes.

Three information views are defined at each checkpoint k: **U** (accumulated user instructions + agent reports, mean 6,564 characters), **V** (full terminal scrollback, mean 25,477 characters), and **T_full** (complete recorded execution context: tool interactions, narration, and reasoning). An 11-step "reading ladder" interpolates between U and T_full. The primary pipeline model (HY4-preview) extracts factual **atoms** from the recorded checkpoint, each with a task-state category and an importance level (level 3 = absence could change a user's judgment, e.g., failures, assumptions, residual risks; level 2 = helps explain progress). For each sampled eligible atom (importance 2–3), a probe (question + reference answer) is generated; a validity filter keeps probes whose answers are at least partly recoverable from full context but not fully recoverable from the task description alone. An observer answers each probe strictly from its assigned view; an independent judge scores full/partial/no credit against the reference; and accuracies are category-weighted (inverse-probability weighting over the atom population). The headline statistic is Gap_view = Acc(T) − Acc(U); the auxiliary Gap_actor = Acc(A) − Acc(U) uses an isolated checkpoint fork where the acting agent itself answers questions without tool calls.

Validity is tested extensively: reference-paraphrase audits (300 stratified probes change the gap by −.010), alternative grading rubrics (+1.1 pp), dual human–machine rating audits, a cross-generator/cross-observer factorial swapping HY4-preview and DeepSeek as probe generators and observers (all four cells positive, .345–.400), cross-harness replication (Claude Code, Pi, DSH on 12 shared issues), independent DeepSeek execution (DeepSeek-v4-flash under fixed DSH), and a fully independent DeepSeek atom-extraction-to-grading chain on the frozen 37 trajectories. Two reporting interventions are evaluated: matched-total-length rewrites (natural vs targeted, both generated from the full context) and added-summary appendices under matched added-text budgets, plus an exploratory simulated-supervision study where a model supervisor issues next-step instructions with or without a newly supplied state atom.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main recoverability gap (37 SWE-bench Verified tasks, 4,799 valid probes):** report-only readers recover 52.0% of task-state probes vs 92.7% for full-context readers — a 40.7-point Gap_view (95% CI [37.3, 43.8]); the actor itself answers 93.6% correctly when queried at isolated checkpoint forks, confirming the deficit is in the reporting channel, not the agent's knowledge.
- **Gap persists after verified success:** at the final checkpoints of the 30 tasks fixed per hidden tests (1,172 probes), reports recover 57.05% vs 91.85% from full context — a residual 34.8-point gap [28.2, 41.3], positive in 28/30 tasks.
- **Terminal visibility helps but does not close the gap:** on the identical 4,799 probes, full terminal scrollback (V, mean 25,477 chars) recovers 73.6% vs 52.0% for accumulated reports (U, 6,564 chars) and 92.7% for full context — scrollback closes 53.0% of the report–context gap yet still trails full context by 19.1 points; a structured summary alone (3,015 chars) recovers only 45.2%.
- **Anatomy of the missing information (two human annotators, 195 stratified atoms):** mentioned-but-insufficient accounts for 55.4%/59.2% of weighted gap mass vs 42.1%/33.7% not-mentioned and only 2.5%/3.7% sufficient-but-unrecovered; a mention-only intervention over 99 human-screened candidates yields Δ = −0.002 (CI [−.027, +.025]) — no gain — while adding reference-guided evidence improves recovery by +0.189 (CI [+.137, +.241]) over mention-only.
- **Supervision-relevant states retain the gap:** on probes independently labeled decision-relevant (86.6% agreement between HY4-preview and DeepSeek labelers, κ = .637), Gap_view = 35.3 [26.6, 43.5] under HY4 labels and 36.4 [.259, .463] under DeepSeek labels (jointly relevant subset: 36.1, with Acc(U) = .553 vs Acc(T) = .914).
- **Reporting interventions (matched total length, 129 paired units, 4,175 probes):** context-informed natural rewrites lift recovery from 67.16% to 75.45% (+8.29 pts [+.5.72, +10.42]) and targeted rewrites to 75.66% (+8.50 pts), but targeted beats natural by only +0.20 pts (CI [−1.48, +1.78]) — no detectable targeting advantage.
- **Cross-harness and cross-model replication (12 shared SWE-bench Verified issues):** report/context accuracies are Claude Code .525/.932 (gap .407), Pi .550/.940 (.390), DSH .429/.919 (.490); an independently executing DeepSeek-v4-flash agent shows a 52.7-point gap [46.0, 60.9] vs 52.2 for HY4-preview, and a fully independent DeepSeek measurement chain measures 39.8 [34.8, 45.6] vs the frozen HY4 40.7-point result.
- **Dynamics:** unrecovered atom counts grow monotonically across checkpoints 1→4 (9.4, 19.0, 21.0, 22.3), while the normalized actor gap is non-monotonic (peak .472 at checkpoint 2, .359 at checkpoint 4). In simulated supervision, adding a previously unrecoverable state atom changes the model supervisor's next-step instructions 18.9 points more often than adding already-known state (CI [+10.2, +28.0]).
- **Concrete failure exemplar:** in an archived Django trajectory the agent discovered that with certain custom user models a password change followed by access from another device triggers HTTP 500 instead of a normal logout; its reports never conveyed this condition — probes scored U: 0/3 vs T_full: 3/3.

## Authors & Credibility

The work is by Guoyang Xu, Rui Zhang, Xiuyu Wu, and Hao Chen of Tencent (one author with a USTC affiliation email), posted as arXiv 2610.20003 (cs.AI, v2 dated 15 Oct 2026, 36 pages with extensive appendices). Credibility signals are unusually strong for a measurement paper: benchmark and code are released (github.com/Kaguraaaaaa/Ctxgap); uncertainty is quantified with task/issue-cluster bootstrap CIs throughout; two independent human annotators audit mention/sufficiency with reported κ (.632/.638); and the headline claim is replicated across three harnesses, an independent executor model, and an end-to-end independent measurement pipeline. The related-work section accurately positions the contribution against SWE-bench-style outcome evaluation, QA-based summary evaluation (QAEval, FActScore), and prior coding-agent report analyses. A notable caveat on transparency: the primary pipeline model is referred to only as "HY4-preview" without public identification, and full trajectory texts (including reasoning traces) are not yet released pending privacy review, with only an anonymous numerical supplement for replication.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (good):** Reframing agent evaluation around the report-mediated supervision channel — distinct from success rate, on-demand answerability, or action-mention coverage — is a genuine contribution, and the paired-probe design (identical questions, same observer, different views) is a clean operationalization of it.
- **Rigor (good with caveats):** The statistical hygiene is above average (issue-cluster bootstrap, pre-specified held-out splits by issue ID, paraphrase and rubric audits, κ-reported human annotation). However, the primary pipeline is single-family: HY4-preview extracts atoms, generates probes, observes, and grades — a setup that structurally favors execution-grounded detail (e.g., exact script names like fake_psql.py) that report views lack by construction. The cross-model checks mitigate this, but the paper concedes the two chains "sample different states," so the DeepSeek replication validates the sign and rough magnitude, not the identical probe set.
- **Construct validity:** "Supervision-relevant" is model-assessed (HY4-preview and DeepSeek labels), not validated against human supervisors; the paper says so explicitly ("importance is not a human-validated measure of actionability"). The simulated-supervision result shows more frequent instruction changes when new state is added, but only limited evidence of fact-specific uptake — a honest but weak link to actual oversight outcomes.
- **Cohort and sampling quirks:** The 37-task headline subset is explicitly non-random, Python-only, drawn from SWE-bench Verified, and underrepresents the harder issues of the 76-task inventory; probes also oversample importance-3 atoms relative to their natural share, which plausibly inflates the headline gap relative to what a naturally occurring supervisor question stream would reveal. Prompt-version mixing (v3.0 for 23 tasks, v3.2 for 14, original v3.0 source unrecoverable) forced audit-by-logged-requests workarounds — disclosed, but it weakens exact-replication claims.
- **Interpretation of interventions:** The ~8.3-point rewrite gain is a counterfactual upper bound produced with access to the full execution context, not an autonomous reporting policy, and it recovers only about a fifth of the 40.7-point gap; the added-text experiments use realized-length tolerances (10%/15%) with modest targeted-over-natural advantages (+2.39/+2.52 pts) that sit in tension with the null targeted–natural result under total-length matching.
- **Red flags (minor):** actor-fork capability is harness-dependent (used only as an auxiliary estimate); the scrollback comparison is a point estimate without reading-view intervals in the main table; and the headline 40.7-point number risks being quoted without its "selected analysis population" caveat (the validity filter itself shifts Gap_view by only +0.56 pp, which the paper checks — to its credit).

## Strengths

- Clean paired-view design that isolates the reporting channel while holding questions, observer, and grading fixed.
- Exceptional robustness battery: cross-harness (Claude Code/Pi/DSH), cross-executor (DeepSeek-v4-flash), cross-measurement-chain (independent DeepSeek atom-to-grade), cross-generator/observer factorial, and paraphrase/rubric audits.
- Human-grounded anatomy (dual annotators with κ) that converts "agents underreport" into an actionable diagnosis: mention-without-evidence is the dominant failure mode.
- Honest framing of interventions as offline upper bounds rather than deployable policies, and explicit separation of Gap_view from the auxiliary actor metric.
- Public benchmark and code, enabling future reporting-policy research on a concrete evaluation target.

## Limitations

- No human supervisors: decision relevance and instruction-change effects are model-assessed; no behavioral study with end users was conducted (stated in the paper's ethics section).
- Single-family primary pipeline (HY4-preview) for extraction, probing, observation, and grading, with the anonymized model identity limiting independent scrutiny.
- Non-random, Python-only, SWE-bench-Verified-only cohort that underrepresents difficult issues; transfer to other domains (web agents, data analysis, long-horizon tasks) is untested.
- The matched-length rewrite result is explicitly not an autonomous reporting effect (rewrites were built from full context), and the 19 additional checkpoint units needed for a full 148-checkpoint sensitivity analysis were never observed.
- Data release is partial: reasoning-bearing trajectory texts are withheld pending review; an anonymous numerical supplement supports statistical but not textual replication; prompt-version provenance (v3.0 vs v3.2) is only partially reconstructible.
- The headline gap conflates deliberate reporting policies (e.g., omitting noise) with lossy communication; the paper measures recoverability, not intent, and cannot distinguish the two.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the gap shrink for models explicitly trained or prompted with "report everything relevant" policies, and what is the length/verbosity cost frontier for recoverability?
- Would a human supervisor asking adaptive follow-up questions (rather than fixed probes) close more of the gap than any static report rewrite, i.e., how much of the deficit is recoverable through interaction?
- How does the gap behave outside Python SWE-bench Verified — e.g., web agents, data-analysis agents, or tasks with longer horizons and more heterogeneous tool surfaces?
- Can the "mentioned but insufficient" category be predicted online (before a supervisor asks), enabling agents to targetedly expand only high-risk report entries?
- Since HY4-preview both generates and answers probes, would a fully third-party probe set (human-written from execution logs) reproduce the 40.7-point magnitude, or is part of the gap an artifact of probe style favoring context-resident detail?
- Do reporting interventions that improve recoverability also improve downstream outcomes (fewer faulty merges, faster human review), which the current offline design cannot establish?
