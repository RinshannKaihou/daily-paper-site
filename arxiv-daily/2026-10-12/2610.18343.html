Lightweight but Linguistically Fair? Quantisation Robustness across Bavarian, German, and English

Why this matters

Answers whether compression is unfair to low-resource languages — and provides a reusable test template (aligned items, per-item flip analysis, blind generation judging) for any dialect deployment.

Critical edge

The headline calibration swing (−3.16 → +0.12 pts) rests on just four models at a single seed, and the free-form verdict comes from one LLM judge with no human validation of Bavarian.

Methodology

How it was tested

Content held fixed; only the language variety varies.

  1. 1BAVGROUND, aligned: 206 source questions realised identically in Bavarian, Standard German, English → 618 MC instances (240 general-knowledge GEN + 378 regionally grounded GRD).
  2. 256 configurations: 14 open-weight 7B–10B instruct models × {BF16, GPTQ-8, GPTQ-4, AWQ-4}, group size 128, embeddings and head untouched; each vs its own BF16.
  3. 3Calibration forensics: ~250k balanced tokens (translated Alpaca/Dolly/OpenHermes); ablations swap calibration language (EN/DE/Bavarian-only) and source (Wikimedia).
  4. 4Scoring: length-normalised option log-likelihood; Δ vs BF16 plus dialect-gap amplification A = ΔBAR − Δcomparator; 5,000-replicate clustered bootstrap, pre-declared ±2-pt equivalence region.
  5. 5Instability & generation: margin-decile analysis of prediction flips; blinded pairwise LLM judge (gpt-5.6-luna, ties allowed, 20% order-reversed) on all 618 items; GH200 memory & throughput.

Because all three language versions share source items, cross-language contrasts are paired rather than across independent benchmarks — something MMLU, MGSM or FLORES cannot offer. The model panel spans four linguistic profiles: Bavarian-adapted (GENBA-10B), German-focused (LeoLM, LLaMmlein), European multilingual (EuroLLM, Teuken, Salamandra, Occiglot, Mistral), and general multilingual (Llama 3.1, Qwen2.5, Gemma 2, Aya Expanse, Granite 3.0, OLMo 2).

Highest BF16-margin decile: only 1.6% / 1.3% flips under GPTQ-4 / AWQ-4 — pre-quantisation uncertainty, not language, predicts instability. Free-form: n ≈ 3,047–3,049 judgements across five models; BF16 preferred in 45.7% of AWQ-4 comparisons.

Per-model spread is huge

Best 4-bit: Qwen2.5-7B GPTQ-4 +2.3 (63.1→65.4); Gemma-2-9B AWQ-4 +3.2. Worst: GENBA-10B GPTQ-4 −6.6 (57.6→51.0, EN −11.2); Granite-3.0-8B AWQ-4 −5.7. Linguistic specialisation does not protect; generic Llama-3.1-8B gains +0.5.

The fairness verdict

BF16 baselines: Bavarian 56.9% < German 60.7% < English 62.4%, yet dialect-gap amplification is −1.4 (GPTQ-4) vs +1.0 (AWQ-4) — opposite signs, neither escaping the ±2-pt band. Harder GRD subset loses the same as GEN.

Calibration flips the gap's sign

GPTQ-4 BAR–DE contrast: −3.16 pts under German-only calibration vs +0.12 under Bavarian-only. AWQ-4 stays positive (+1.82…+4.25) under all four regimes; swapping in Wikimedia text shifts AWQ-4 by −4.63.

Memory vs quality

11 configs halve peak GPU memory for ≤1 MC-pt loss. Standout Mistral-7B GPTQ-4: 19.2→7.9 GB (−58.9%), MC 66.8→67.2 — yet still −3.9 net free-form (−5.1 in Bavarian).

Strengths

What the study does right

  • Aligned three-way design (identical 206 items in BAR/DE/EN) cleanly separates language-variety effects from content effects — sharper than any prior multilingual quantisation study.
  • Unusually honest reporting: per-model gains printed next to losses, pre-registered equivalence margins, ±1/±3 sensitivity checks, excluded and invalid runs disclosed.
  • Mechanistic insight: margin-decile analysis explains why aggregate deltas look small — quantisation perturbs decisions the model was already unsure about.
  • Deployment warning: the MC-vs-free-form mismatch (near-equal MC loss, −17.6 preference) shows fixed-choice benchmarks under-detect generation degradation.
  • Real hardware numbers (GH200) backing a concrete quality–memory frontier.

Limitations

Where the ground is thinner

  • One dialect, one benchmark, one domain: 206 Bavarian cultural-knowledge items; no transfer claim to other scripts, tasks, or more distant dialects; Bavarian's internal variation unmodelled.
  • Calibration ablation is narrow: 4 models × 1 seed (source comparison: 3 seeds × 4 models) — treat calibration effects as provisional.
  • Single proprietary judge (gpt-5.6-luna); no human ratings of Bavarian naturalness or dialect appropriateness.
  • Method slice is thin: only weight-only GPTQ/AWQ at group size 128 — no FP8, SmoothQuant, activation quantisation, or QAT.
  • Scale and serving specificity: 7B–10B models, single GH200, batch-size-1; behaviour at 30B–70B untested.

Questions for further exploration

Open threads

  1. Do calibration-language effects replicate beyond 4 models × 1 seed — and is there a cheap proxy (tokeniser coverage, per-language calibration perplexity) that predicts a protective mix?
  2. Are margin-concentrated flips specific to multiple-choice scoring, or does 4-bit likewise perturb low-confidence regions of free-form generation (e.g. token-level entropy)?
  3. Would activation quantisation (SmoothQuant) or FP8 show the same dialect–standard asymmetries, given language-dependent activation outliers?
  4. Does 4-bit instability and calibration sensitivity shrink at 30B–70B scale, as aggregate-robustness literature suggests?
  5. What mechanism drives AWQ-4's −17.6 free-form loss vs GPTQ-4's −6.4 — activation-based channel rescaling meeting morphologically rich dialect tokens?
