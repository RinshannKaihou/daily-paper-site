Why this matters

A complete engineering blueprint for local agents: fixed-memory architecture choice, frontier distillation, near-lossless 4-bit quantization, and speculative decoding on consumer GPUs.

把大模型能力压进消费级设备的完整工程范本，对本地 Agent 系统设计与评测协议都有直接参考价值。

⚠ Critical edge

For an on-device paper, the on-device claim is never measured — no tokens/sec, latency, or DFlash speedup numbers appear anywhere; only "runs at practical speeds on a single RTX 5090."

Methodology — distilled

Muse Spark (frontier teacher) → logit distill → soup → mid-train ×2 → SFT → on-policy distill → multi-objective RL → 4-bit + DFlash

01Architecture

29.6B dense GQA transformer (32Q / 2KV heads, [3×Local+Global] 2048-token sliding window, SwiGLU) + frozen 1.8B ViT-G/14 Perception Encoder; interleaved text+image, 128K+ context. Dense chosen over MoE: fixed-32GB scaling ladders showed 2–2.5× better compute efficiency.

02Pre-training

Logit co-distillation from Muse Spark — top-256 teacher logits, KL-only for 95% of training, cross-entropy for the final 5% to correct top-k truncation bias; checkpoint souping; mid-training (domain infusion, then context 32K→128K with agentic data).

03Post-training

SFT → on-policy distillation (teacher distributional targets on student rollouts) → multi-objective RL over reward models, verifiers, and task success. Four reasoning strengths (low→xhigh) trained via subgroup length penalties + routing, switched by a single system-prompt line.

04Serving

EvoPress mixed-precision 4-bit profile (19.65 GB) for 32GB GPUs; Q4KM for 24GB; DFlash 5-layer block-diffusion speculative drafter (block size 16) for fast decoding.

Strengths

  • Genuinely useful open release — full-precision weights, 4-bit variants for both 32 GB and 24 GB targets, and the DFlash speculation head under Apache 2.0; few labs ship the drafter too.
  • Unusually careful quantization chapter — ΔNLL-based selection validated on 15 downstream tasks; 19.65 GB profile within 0.1 pt of BF16.
  • Transparent per-benchmark appendix — harnesses, judge models (gpt-oss-120b, Claude Sonnet 4.6), run counts, and self-reported vs internally-run flags.
  • Dense-vs-MoE decided by experiment — an actual scaling-ladder under the 32 GB memory constraint, not by fashion.
  • Evaluation breadth — multilingual agentic testing (10 languages) and a token-efficiency analysis addressing real deployment cost.

Limitations

  • No on-device measurements — zero tokens/sec, latency, or speculative-decoding speedup numbers; the headline use case is supported only qualitatively.
  • Not uniformly SOTA — trails Qwen3.6-27B on OSWorld (−9.7), TerminalBench (−9.0), GDPval-AA (−188), SkillsBench, MMMU-Pro; trails Gemma4 on GPQA, HLE, and all translation benchmarks.
  • Privacy cuts both ways — CIMemories violations 26.4 vs Gemma4's 12.1 despite contextual-integrity training; PrivacyLens leakage non-monotonic across strengths (59.6→49.2→59.6), unanalyzed.
  • Reproducibility gap — training depends on proprietary Muse Spark teacher and internal pipelines; only deployment/fine-tuning is reproducible.
  • Missing ablations — on-policy-distillation gains, souping, and routing asserted without controlled numbers; video input unoptimized; multilingual multimodal unevaluated.

Questions for further exploration

  1. What tokens/sec and end-to-end latency does EvoPress + DFlash actually achieve on RTX 5090, Apple Silicon, or 24 GB laptops — and how large is the speculative-decoding speedup?
  2. Why does it trail Qwen3.6-27B specifically on GUI computer use (OSWorld −9.7) and terminal tasks while winning API/tool benchmarks like MCPAtlas — data mix, perception, or action space?
  3. Can contextual-integrity training push CIMemories violations below Gemma4's 12.1 without sacrificing coverage (64.8 vs 53.0) — and why does PrivacyLens swing non-monotonically?
  4. How much of the agentic gain comes from on-policy distillation versus RL alone, given the improvement is asserted but never quantified?
  5. The dense-vs-MoE ladder used 550 TPP (dense) vs 2,800/6,000 TPP (MoE) — would a differently allocated budget flip the conclusion at other memory ceilings (16 GB, 64 GB)?
