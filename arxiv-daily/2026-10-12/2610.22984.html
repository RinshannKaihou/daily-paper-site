Methodology § How it was measured

CFOE — Cold-start Failure-Only Evolution. A frozen backbone πθ runs an online task stream with an evolving external state St (memory bank, skill document, or harness guidance). St starts empty and updates only on failed trajectories; success labels, verifier verdicts, rewards, and expert solutions are hidden from both agent and updater.

  • Repair happens; transfer doesn't. Recovery on failed source tasks is real (3.5–16.8%) yet held-out curves stay flat or fall — robust across frameworks, backbones, environments, and the 800-task run.
  • Correctness alone doesn't fix it. Pruning to only manually verified lessons improves source recovery everywhere, but stays non-transferable — right lessons remain bound to their source trajectories.
  • What's missing, ranked: Success ≫ Mix > Fail for held-out gains; verifier Feedback and resumable reflection (Resume) help only modestly; scaling reflection 5 → 20 turns ≈ no change.

Strengths § What it does well

▲ Points in favor

  • Unusually broad, controlled coverage — 3 paradigms × 10 backbones × 4 environments; conclusions hold on every axis, making the gap hard to dismiss.
  • The verified-experience control (§4.2) is the sharpest experiment — it cleanly separates "wrong lessons" from "right lessons that still don't transfer."
  • Four-mode error taxonomy (false success attribution, misattribution, distorted consolidation, harmful guidance) with annotated examples — immediately actionable for memory/skill-system builders.
  • Honest scoping — reports its exceptions and includes a candid Limitations section rather than overclaiming.

Limitations § Where it's bounded

▼ Points against

  • Single rollout per held-out task on sets as small as 27 tasks, with no variance estimates — small-set noise could masquerade as decline.
  • Text-only interactive benchmarks — no multimodal or multi-agent settings; deployment distributions may differ.
  • Long-horizon evidence is thin — the 800-task run covers only ALFWorld and 3 backbones; unbounded evolution untested.
  • Annotation is partial — manual audit covers 4 model–framework pairs, not the full grid; error rates are extrapolated.
  • CFOE is deliberately pessimistic — it forbids even cheap retry/self-consistency signals, so the measured gap is an upper bound on failure-only learning, not a deployment forecast.

Open Questions § Where next

  1. Would cheap forbidden signals — self-consistency votes, unit-style re-execution, a second model as critic — cut the 53.2% misattribution rate without any verifier access?
  2. Why does skill evolution transfer only for Qwen3.5-27B on WebShop: the validation gate, the skill-document representation, or the domain's homogeneity?
  3. Can experience be stored with explicit applicability conditions — typed rules with preconditions instead of free text — to attack the verified-but-bound finding?
  4. How few successful trajectories mixed into a failure-dense stream recover most of the Success condition's transfer gains?
  5. Can an online "is this lesson safe to consolidate" classifier predict downstream harm from the four error modes?
