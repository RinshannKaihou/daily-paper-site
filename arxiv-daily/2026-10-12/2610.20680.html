Diminishing Returns of Data in Sparse Autoencoders: Functional Behavior does not Guarantee Feature Reproducibility ↗

Sambit Ghosh · Madelyn Mathai · R. Venkatesh Babu · Chirag Agarwal  — IBM Research · Indian Institute of Science · University of Virginia

sparse-autoencoders mechanistic-interpretability data-efficiency feature-reproducibility SAEBench

Key metric · strict feature reproducibility

2.20% → 28.86%

Shared features (τ ≥ 0.7, encoder and decoder matched) for BatchTopK on Gemma-2-2B, from 5M → 50M training tokens.

At those same budgets the SAE already looks done: CE loss-recovered 0.980 (5M) vs 0.987 (500M reference).

Why this matters

Cuts SAE training data 10–100× when only functional quality matters — and ships a reusable one-to-one matching protocol for feature-level reproducibility checks.

按目标匹配预算的量化依据；一对一特征匹配协议可直接复用于自己 SAE 实验的复现性检验。

Critical edge · read with care

Every headline table is a single training run — no seed replicates, no error bars — and the paper's own citation shows SAE seed variance is large: part of the 2.20% → 28.86% gap may be seed noise, not data scarcity.

  • Grid. 4 architectures (ReLU, BatchTopK, JumpReLU, Matryoshka BatchTopK) × 3 LLMs (Gemma-2-2B layer 12, Pythia-160M layer 6, Qwen3-8B layer 17) × 16K / 65K dictionary widths, all in SAEBench-standard configurations.
  • Two axes. Quantity — 5M / 25M / 50M tokens vs a 500M-token reference with training config held fixed. Selection — random vs farthest-point sampling (FPS) over identical activation pools.
  • Feature matching. One-to-one Hungarian matching (Paulo & Belrose protocol), encoder and decoder matched independently by cosine; a latent counts as shared only if both pick the same reference latent and both cosines ≥ τ = 0.7.
Theorem 1 · why the gap exists Statistical consistency of subsampled training does not imply one-to-one feature recovery — that further requires optimization consistency and feature identifiability. Corollary 2: encoder/decoder assignment agreement can be high while directional alignment stays weak — exactly the empirical pattern.

Strengths

  • Answers a real default-practice question with a broad, well-controlled grid (4 architectures × 3 LLMs × widths) rather than a single anecdote.
  • Theory matched to empirics — Corollary 2 predicts the observed counterpart-agreement vs shared-feature gap; rare discipline in interpretability empirics.
  • FPS gains explained, not asserted — a distributional characterization (norm shift, KL divergence, mean-direction cosine) shows FPS silently reshapes training data while random sampling is provably neutral.
  • Actionable — match budget to objective; run an explicit reproducibility check before any feature-level claim.

Limitations

  • Single-seed results — seed variance is a known large effect in SAE feature learning, so absolute shared-feature percentages carry unquantified uncertainty.
  • Anchored to a 500M-token reference — billion-token frontier regimes and models beyond 8B are untested; “diminishing returns” holds only within this range.
  • Uneven metric coverage — RAVEL and unlearning run on Gemma only (SAEBench limitation).
  • Budgeting confound — coupling between gradient-step count and unique activations across budgets is not spelled out; functional metrics beyond reconstruction are non-monotonic and not fully explained.

Questions for further exploration

  • How much of the low shared-feature rate is seed variance, not data scarcity? Multi-seed 500M references would separate the two.
  • Does strict reproducibility keep climbing toward billion-token budgets, or plateau — and at what budget does BatchTopK reach 50% shared features?
  • Can the FPS AutoInterp gain be reproduced by simply clipping or upweighting high-norm activations, decoupling diversity from norm shift?
  • Do Matryoshka / JumpReLU sparsity types show the same functional-vs-feature divergence, or is the BatchTopK gap representative?
  • Would curriculum or importance-sampling strategies dominate both RS and FPS on all metrics simultaneously?
  • Do functional plateaus transfer to frontier-scale models (70B+), where activation distributions may differ qualitatively?
