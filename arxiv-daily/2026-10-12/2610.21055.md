> **一句话总结：** Athanor（ByteDance + Duke + UChicago + Virginia Tech）构建了一个开放的全自主后训练研究（AutoPostTrain）系统——四个 Claude Opus 5.5 智能体各自独占一个 8-GPU 节点、只用公开数据、不调用任何教师模型、无人给研究建议，在 104 小时内通过"SFT → 带 1/4 贪心 rollout 的 GRPO → 跨智能体权重合并与 task-vector 外推 → 自蒸馏 RFT"的已知方法重组，把 Qwen3-4B-Base 从 15.9 提升到 67.3（六任务开发套件）、从 9.2 提升到 48.2（智能体从未见过的 held-out 孪生套件），与官方 Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 的 66.5 / 47.9 持平或略胜，总成本约 $11.8K。

> **TL;DR:** Athanor is an open, agent-native environment in which a team of four Claude Opus 5.5 agents (each with its own 8×80GB-GPU node, public data only, no teacher-model calls, no human research advice) autonomously runs the full post-training research loop — building data mixtures, writing/modifying training code (GRPO on verl), tuning hyperparameters, analyzing eval traces — through four verbs (`athanor job/eval/data/artifact`) backed by frozen evaluation, decontamination, managed async jobs, and a persistent lineage/notes record. In a 104-hour, ~$11.8K campaign they take Qwen3-4B-Base from 15.9 → 67.3 on a six-task dev suite (MathArena-style math contests, ZebraLogic, LiveCodeBench, MMLU-Pro, IFEval, ALFWorld) and 9.2 → 48.2 on a sealed held-out twin suite (Nov-2025 contests, other ZebraLogic half, LCB-2025, SuperGPQA, IFBench, other ALFWorld types), versus 66.5 / 47.9 for the official Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 baseline under the same frozen evaluator — closing 97% of the base→Instruct held-out gap (the default starter code with no research closes only 30%). The claimed first: an agent team autonomously post-training a raw base into one cross-domain generalist matching the vendor's instruct release, confirmed on held-out tasks. The cost: the recipe is pure recombination of known methods (one agent's "decode ¼ of GRPO rollouts greedily to kill repetition loops" is the most novel single move), parity with Instruct-2507 is statistically "indistinguishable" rather than a win, ALFWorld gains offset lagging math/logic, and the vendor's reasoning releases (Thinking-2507: 80.5 dev / 63.0 held-out) remain far ahead.

## 核心贡献

- **Athanor 系统本身**：一个开放的 agent-native 后训练研究环境，把研究拆成原子操作（启动作业、评估 checkpoint、引入数据、保存/提交产物），环境负责执行、测量、去污染与记录，智能体保留全部研究决策权；刻意不内置任何人类设计的研究脚手架（"苦涩教训"式设计）。
- **三项设计原则**：(1) 研究决策与执行分离——智能体写代码做决策，环境跑作业并恢复故障；(2) 研究状态外置——journal、checkpoint lineage、完整 transcript、workspace 版本历史全部持久化，会话可随时恢复或替换；(3) 保持研究回路高效——作业异步后台运行，结果一出来就推给智能体，GPU 几乎不等智能体（<1% GPU 时长空等）。
- **首次智能体团队自主后训练达到官方 instruct 水平**：104 小时、约 $11.8K，四个 Claude Opus 5.5 把 Qwen3-4B-Base 后训练成跨领域通才模型，开发套件 67.3 vs Instruct-2507 的 66.5，held-out 48.2 vs 47.9，且在智能体从未见过的 held-out 孪生套件上确认（此前同类工作要么每基准训练一个专家、要么允许调用教师模型）。
- **基于完整记录的四条科学发现**（500 个作业、255 次全套评估、174 个 checkpoint 的谱系、全部 transcript）：(1) 重组——通往提交模型的每一步都是已知方法，智能体贡献的是组合与修正；(2) 团队进步——想法与 checkpoint 数小时内跨智能体流动，21 小时后每个新最优都融合了多名智能体的训练；(3) 配方收敛——四份相同的智能体在第一小时内就都押注同一个公开推理语料（AM-Thinking-v1-Distilled 占各自首批混合数据的 76–87%），之后尝试的替代谱系得分更低被放弃；(4) 数据感不均衡——能发现格式/长度/可评分性/难度问题，却几乎不核查答案本身的正确性。
- **对"评估选择偏差"的诚实度量**：智能体自己发现单次评估会高估最优候选（23 个复评候选中 19 个首种子偏高，符号检验 p=0.003，平均高 0.4 分），因此结果以多种子均值报告——这是自主研究系统里少见的方法论自觉。

## Key Contributions

- **An open agent-native AutoPostTrain environment**: Athanor exposes research as four composable verbs (`athanor job`, `eval`, `data`, `artifact`) while the environment supplies frozen evaluation, managed asynchronous GPU jobs, decontaminated data pools, a persistent research record (journal + checkpoint lineage + transcripts + workspace history), and a held-out suite the agents never see. No human-designed research scaffolding is imposed — earlier Athanor versions that prescribed recipe forms and per-experiment approvals constrained strong agents more than they helped.
- **A clean, adversarially-honest problem setting**: one generalist checkpoint must improve six capabilities at once, from a raw base, with no teacher-model calls, scored on a sealed held-out twin suite — strictly harder than prior benchmarks (PostTrainBench, Locus, RSI-Master) that train per-benchmark specialists or allow external model APIs.
- **First instruct-level autonomous post-training by an agent team**: 104 hours, ~$11,800 total ($10,800 GPU rental at $3.49/GPU-h for 3,086 GPU-hours + $1,074 agent inference, 9%), Qwen3-4B-Base 15.9 → 67.3 dev / 9.2 → 48.2 held-out vs Instruct-2507's 66.5 / 47.9.
- **Trajectory-level science from the full record**: the paper reconstructs the winning lineage step by step — SFT on ~300–600K rows → 49.7; GRPO with a quarter of rollouts decoded greedily → 57.3 (best GRPO 61.7); cross-agent weight averaging plus task-vector extrapolation ×1.2–1.3 → 65.5; rejection-sampling self-distillation → 66.1 — and quantifies team coordination (57 checkpoint fetches, 160 shared-note versions, first teammate fetch 56 seconds after the hour-15.8 "notice" that the team's submission counts).
- **Four findings on how agents do research**: recombination of known methods; team progress after the notice (every new best after hour 21 combines multiple agents' training; 139 of 287 lineage links pass through a teammate's checkpoint); early recipe convergence on one distilled corpus (of 447 pool datasets the agents inspected 43 and used 27; two alternative data lineages were tried and dropped); and uneven data sense (format/length/scorability checks yes, answer-correctness verification rarely — one agent planned a filter for ground-truth answers that "look like noise" and never ran it).

## Methodology

Athanor implements the AutoPostTrain loop (policy over recipes → execution → evaluation → shared history) as two concurrent loops over one persistent record. Each researcher agent runs Claude Opus 5.5 inside Claude Code in a sandbox on its own 8-GPU node (80GB HBM3 each), with read-only web access and the `athanor` CLI. The environment side runs jobs asynchronously, pushes results into sessions, decontaminates every incoming public dataset against the benchmarks, stores checkpoints with full lineage, and refuses researchers access to the evaluator and the held-out suite. Starter code (which agents may edit or replace) covers dataset building, SFT, GRPO, interactive RL, on-policy distillation, puzzle generation, ALFWorld trajectory replay, and merging, on verl and mergekit. Three roles are separated by enforcement, not convention: researchers (all research decisions, no held-out access), environment (no research decisions), and a human operator (sets the goal, recovers nodes, scores held-out; gives no research advice and every operator command is logged).

The campaign: four agents, one sentence of goal ("raise the dev-suite score of a model post-trained from Qwen3-4B-Base as high as possible" — no target, no method), 104-hour research window. The dev suite has one task per capability: math contests (early 2025, MathArena), ZebraLogic (one half), LiveCodeBench (2024), MMLU-Pro, IFEval, and four ALFWorld task types; each held-out twin measures the same capability on different material (Nov 2025 contests, the other ZebraLogic half, LCB 2025, SuperGPQA, IFBench, the two unseen ALFWorld types). Two protocols are reported: the campaign protocol (≤16,384 output tokens, greedy decoding except math) and a post-campaign final measurement (≤32,768 tokens, Qwen's recommended thinking-mode sampling for every model). The submission rule was fixed before the start: each agent submits one checkpoint, the team's submission is the highest dev-scored of the four, and the reported number is a multi-seed mean (the agents themselves discovered single-evaluation winner's curse and began re-evaluating their best candidates on extra seeds). Analysis draws on the complete record: 500 jobs, 255 full evaluations scoring 174 checkpoints, 57 checkpoint fetches, 3,218 tool calls (97% Bash), 8,243 model calls with 4.5B cached input tokens.

## Results & Highlights

- **Dev suite, final measurement**: team submission 67.3 vs Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507's 66.5 and base's 15.9; held-out suite 48.2 vs 47.9 and 9.2 — paired 95% CIs of the differences (−0.8 to +2.4 dev, −2.0 to +2.6 held-out) contain zero, i.e. statistically indistinguishable from the vendor's instruct release, though still far below Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 (80.5 dev / 63.0 held-out).
- **Held-out generalization**: under the campaign protocol the submission scores 46.7 ± 1.3 (2 seeds) vs 48.0 ± 1.4 for Instruct-2507 and 8.7 for the base — closing 97% of the base→Instruct gap, where the no-research default starter (36.4 dev) closes only 30% (20.4 held-out).
- **Lopsided task profile**: the submission beats Instruct-2507 on multi-step action (ALFWorld 98.9 vs 82.4 dev, 78.8 vs 65.9 held-out) and code (47.1 vs 43.9 dev, 34.9 vs 31.4 held-out) but loses on logic (ZebraLogic 63.4 vs 80.4 dev, 63.7 vs 81.0 held-out) and math (37.2 vs 42.9 dev, 29.7 vs 42.2 held-out); without the ALFWorld gain it would trail Instruct-2507 by 3.7 (dev) and 4.2 (held-out) points.
- **Recipe-stage gains on the dev suite**: SFT on ~300–600K-row mixtures (76–87% AM-Thinking-v1-Distilled) reaches 49.7; Agent 3's GRPO variant with ¼ of rollouts decoded greedily — added because greedy decoding fell into repetition loops that sampled-rollout GRPO never touched — drops validation loops from 86 to 18 and lifts 49.7 → 57.3 (best GRPO checkpoint 61.7); merging three agents' models and extrapolating the task vector 1.2–1.3× reaches 65.5; two rounds of self-distillation (sample 4 answers, keep the shortest correct) reach the 66.1 ± 0.8 submission (3 seeds, vs 66.4 for Instruct-2507).
- **Cost and efficiency**: total ~$11.8K (3,086 GPU-hours at $3.49/GPU-h = $10.8K, plus $1,074 agent inference); the score frontier reaches ~50 after $1.1K and ~60 after $2.4K, but the last 6 points to 66.1 cost ~$8K. Jobs hold the GPUs 93% of available hours (40% GRPO, 22% evaluation, 17% self-distillation sampling, 14% SFT); agents reason only 3.6% of session time and wait on jobs 87% (median agent turn 4 s; median GRPO job 1.6 h; full evaluation 26 min).
- **Long-horizon reliability**: the platform reclaimed 3 of 4 nodes mid-campaign (hours 46, 70, 94; 11–69-minute outages; 23 GPU-hours of work lost), each session resumed from its externalized record; only 26 of 500 jobs failed, and 41 of 2,611 athanor commands errored (mostly wrong arguments).
- **Evaluation-noise honesty**: for 23 re-evaluated best candidates, the first seed ran higher than the later-seed mean in 19 cases (sign test p = 0.003; average inflation +0.4, max +1.1) — justifying the multi-seed submission protocol.

## Authors & Credibility

A ByteDance-led collaboration with Duke University, the University of Chicago, and Virginia Tech; the project lead (Yufa Zhou, Duke/ByteDance) and corresponding authors include the Athanor system builders (Neiwen Ling, Zijun Wei at ByteDance) and a Duke statistics faculty member (Anru R. Zhang). The team has direct access to the required infrastructure (32 GPUs for 4+ days) and to frontier-model APIs, which explains why this experiment has not appeared elsewhere. Credibility is high: the evaluation methodology is unusually careful for an agent paper (sealed held-out twin suite, frozen evaluator, pre-registered submission rule, multi-seed means, paired confidence intervals, decontamination, winner's-curse quantification, verbatim agent notes as evidence, and an explicit statement that Instruct-2507 scores no higher under its own recommended sampling: 66.4/47.5). The claims are mostly scoped correctly ("to our knowledge, the first..."), and the limitation that the public training data (AM-Thinking-v1-Distilled) was itself distilled from stronger models is disclosed rather than hidden.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The system and setting are genuinely new — a sealed held-out generalist suite plus no-teacher-model constraints go beyond PostTrainBench/Locus/RSI-Master. But the *recipe* is, by the authors' own finding, pure recombination: SFT, GRPO, merging, and RFT were all in the starter code or standard practice; the single most creative move (decoding ¼ of GRPO rollouts greedily so repetition loops receive negative advantage) is a targeted fix to a diagnosis the agents made from eval traces. The paper is honest about this, and its real contribution is the demonstration plus the record, not a new training method.
- **Rigor**: Strong on measurement (multi-seed, paired CIs, selection-bias sign test at p=0.003, held-out twins). Two soft spots: (a) the headline "matches or surpasses" the vendor instruct model is a mean-level statement — every paired CI contains zero, so "statistically indistinguishable" is the accurate reading; (b) the ALFWorld advantage that carries the mean comes from fine-tuning on public expert trajectories for the *same environment family* whose dev twin is in the suite (the two held-out task types did transfer, which mitigates but does not eliminate the concern), while math (29.7 vs 42.2 held-out) and logic (63.7 vs 81.0) lag badly — and the logic gap survives the 32K-token rerun (63.0 vs 79.5), so it is not just truncation.
- **Missing controls**: there is no human-recipe control at matched budget (e.g., a Tülu-3-style open pipeline run on the same $12K and 32 GPUs), so "agents do it as well as a human team would" is asserted by comparison to the vendor's instruct release rather than demonstrated; the only ablation is the default starter (36.4), and no experiment isolates how much each environment service (async jobs, shared record, team sharing) contributed — the paper notes a no-sharing control was never run.
- **Reproducibility**: the system is open and the record (transcripts, lineage, every scored answer) supports full audit, but the result depends on one proprietary, moving target (Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code), ~$12K of compute, and exactly one campaign with one 4B base — no campaign-to-campaign variance exists, and the four "replicates" (the four submissions) share data, notes, and checkpoints, so they are not independent.
- **Significance**: as an existence proof for autonomous post-training research and as instrumentation (autoresearch scaling curves, coordination metrics, convergence measurements), it is significant; as evidence of agent research *creativity*, it shows the opposite — the clearest observed limit is idea diversity, with all four identical agents locking onto one corpus within an hour and 76–87% of every first mixture being the same distilled dataset.
- **Red flags**: "no teacher model" is true only at the API level — the dominant training corpus was itself distilled from a stronger model by its publishers (the authors disclose this); "cross-domain generalist" rests on a six-task suite whose plain mean lets a near-saturated ALFWorld (98.9) mask a 42-point logic deficit; and the $12K price tag per campaign, while cheap for the result, is not obviously cheap for the marginal 6 points that consumed ~$8K of it.

## Strengths

- Evaluation hygiene that most autonomous-research papers lack: frozen evaluator outside the sandbox, decontaminated data, a held-out suite the agents did not know existed, pre-fixed submission rule, multi-seed reporting, and a quantified winner's-curse correction.
- The system design is well-motivated and evidenced: separating decisions from execution keeps GPUs busy 93% of the time while agents wait 87% of theirs; externalized state survived three mid-campaign node losses with only 23 GPU-hours lost.
- The trajectory analysis is the paper's scientific core — lineage reconstruction of the winning recipe, the 56-seconds-after-notice first checkpoint fetch, the 139/287 cross-agent lineage links, and verbatim agent notes (Trace 1's greedy-loop diagnosis) make the findings checkable rather than anecdotal.
- Unusual cost transparency: itemized GPU/agent-inference accounting, per-phase GPU utilization (including 155 GPU-hours below 10% utilization inside GRPO steps waiting on the longest rollout), and a capability-vs-dollars curve.
- Honest framing of what was not achieved (vendor reasoning releases remain ahead) and of the agents' weaknesses (data-correctness blind spot, planned-but-never-run noise filter).

## Limitations

- One campaign, one 4B base model, one agent model, four *identical* agents — no independent repeats, no variance estimate, no evidence the found recipe transfers to larger or different-family models (authors' own admission).
- The team reaches the instruct release, not the reasoning releases (hybrid Qwen3-4B 73.3/50.6; Thinking-2507 80.5/63.0), with the biggest residual gaps exactly on math, logic, and code.
- Parity with Instruct-2507 is statistically indistinguishable rather than a win, and is profile-dependent: exclude ALFWorld and the submission trails by 3.7–4.2 points.
- The training data is dominated by one public corpus already distilled from a stronger model, so the result bounds "agents + public distilled data," not "agents from the base model alone."
- Idea diversity is the observed bottleneck: convergence on one recipe within the first hour, no controlled data-source comparisons ever run, and both diversification attempts dropped after single rounds — the paper identifies but does not solve this.
- Agents rarely verified whether their training data's answers were correct (format/length/scorability yes, truth no), which likely contributed to the persistent math/logic deficits.

## Relevance

直接命中"AI 自主做 AI 研究 / 递归自我改进"这一方向：它给出了今天可测量的 RSI 弱形式（前沿智能体自主后训练基座模型）的首个通才级结果，其 Agent 环境设计（研究决策与执行分离、状态外置、异步作业）与诚实评估协议（held-out 孪生套件、多种子、去污染）对任何想构建自动化训练/实验系统的工程实践都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a heterogeneous team (different agent models, or explicitly assigned different data priors) break the one-corpus convergence and raise the ceiling, or does the shared verifier inevitably collapse the search?
- Does Agent 3's "quarter-greedy rollouts" GRPO fix generalize beyond this evaluation protocol (greedy-scored benchmarks), or is it reward-hacking the evaluator's decoding scheme?
- What is the autoresearch scaling law: with 10× the budget, does the frontier keep climbing (new methods) or plateau (idea-basin lock-in), and does a 30B base change the agents' effective recipe space?
- How much of the result survives if answer-correctness verification of training data is forced into the loop — would the math/logic gaps close, or is the residual gap a property of the distilled corpus?
- What would a human-designed open post-training recipe (Tülu-3-style) achieve on the same 32 GPUs and $12K — i.e., are the agents actually at human-team parity, or only at vendor-instruct parity?
- Can the sealed-suite protocol survive agents that construct their own evaluation suites, as the authors propose for the broader loop, without reintroducing contamination or evaluator-gaming?
