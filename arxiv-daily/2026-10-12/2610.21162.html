Critical edge

The headline speedups are the least controlled: 1.05–1.17× vs pooled MFFD mixes input-path changes; the clean isolated gain on Alpaca (+0.4% ±1.4) is statistically zero; and the biggest gains (1.20–1.34×) come from a withheld internal workload.

01 · Methodology

Batch Cost Lookahead (BCL) — a scheduler that lives entirely inside each rank's data loader

No model changes, no collectives, no runtime modifications — only bounded reordering of records each rank was going to read anyway. Every record is emitted exactly once, always in packs of exactly B.

stage window W = K·B records → sort by cost, descending → LPT: assign to least-loaded non-full pack → heavy↔light swap refinement → emit packs of exactly B

Each worker stages a bounded window of W = K·B records from its own shard stream and repartitions it into K packs of exactly B records with balanced estimated cost, then refills. The objective is a cardinality-constrained makespan (decision version contains 3-PARTITION); the heuristic is deliberately classical — Graham-style LPT initialization plus deterministic heavy–light exchanges that strictly reduce max load while never changing pack cardinality. Packs are shuffled within the window before emission, so no global length curriculum is imposed.

cost proxy c(ℓ) = α·ℓ̄ + β·ℓ̄², ℓ̄ = min(ℓ, ℓmax) complexity O(W log W + W·K), host-side K = 32 in all experiments no GPU collectives added
When local scheduling provably fails (η / δ diagnostics)
ReadingVerdict
η = m/C̄ ≥ 1 One record alone ≥ mean pack cost. No schedule can fix it — cap, split, or re-represent the record.
δ > 1 Rank-window means diverge. Local permutations cannot move work across ranks — reshard or coordinate (Libra, Hydraulis).
ρ ≈ 1 Little headroom left. Stop tuning the scheduler; change the workload.

Order-statistics scaling: ρBCL ≈ 1 + aR·CV / √(K·B) vs ≈ 1 + aR·CV / √B under random assignment — predicting diminishing returns in K and growing straggler opportunity as rank count R rises.

  • Provenance discipline: checksummed simulation arrays, pinned dataset splits, per-step token audits, ≤1.5% cross-campaign drift bound.

03 · Strengths

  • Asks the question other balancers blur: exactly how much straggler imbalance is removable with zero coordination — with theory (η, δ, CV scaling) that predicts both the gains and the failure modes, confirmed by the rank-count trend (1.197×→1.343× as ranks go 8→32).
  • Deployment-friendly contract: preserves record-count cadence, collectives, and the optimizer's view; host-side work only; a drop-in loader change that complements sequence packing and FlashAttention rather than competing.
  • Exceptionally honest reporting: every comparison footnoted with what it does and doesn't isolate; classical attributions (LPT 1969, MFFD 1985, 3-PARTITION) kept visible.
  • Actionable failure map: Table 1 turns (η, δ, ρ) readings into interventions — raise K / cap or split / reshard or coordinate / stop.

04 · Limitations

  • Cannot cap or split records: a single record with η≥1 bounds the achievable balance; p99 capping must be decided before scheduling, and its training-quality cost is unmeasured.
  • Cannot move work across ranks: δ>1 divergent shards need resharding or coordinated systems — out of BCL's scope by construction.
  • Cost proxy uncalibrated: capped αℓ+βℓ² is policy, not inference; FlashAttention-3's true cost is far from quadratic, and no calibration against measured step times is reported.
  • Evidence gaps: biggest speedups internal-only; public runs use a 1B model at B=8 with three repeats; no NeMo training-quality eval, no exact mid-stream restart, no head-to-head vs Libra / Hydraulis / WLB-LLM.
  • Larger K costs staging: more host-side records, delayed first pack, farther reordering — no measurements of staging memory or startup latency.

05 · Questions for further exploration

  1. Can (α, β) be calibrated online from measured per-step times per length bucket, instead of static policy, given FlashAttention-3's deviation from quadratic cost?
  2. Would a lightweight periodic cross-rank window-mean exchange (a BCL × Libra hybrid) recover the δ>1 remainder — and at what communication and coupling cost?
  3. What are the convergence and final-quality effects of bounded window reordering at large K on long pretraining runs, where curriculum-like length effects are likelier?
  4. Does BCL transfer to RL post-training in NeMo-RL, where rollout lengths are highly variable and generated online rather than read from fixed shards?
  5. Can η/δ be computed cheaply online as a scheduler trigger — automatically switching from "increase K" to "request reshard" as the rank-divergence index drifts up?
