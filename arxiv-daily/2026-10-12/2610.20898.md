> **一句话总结：** 这篇理论+实证分析论文解释了"为何免标签的多数投票奖励（majority vote）能逼近可验证奖励（gold）的 RL 训练效果"：作者论证正确的分析单位是批次级梯度更新而非单个 prompt——在 GRPO 批次（B=256, n=16）下，一个 prompt 自身的梯度只占其输出变化的不到 1%，多数投票的标签错误在跨 prompt 方向上几乎不相干（与随机奖励零假设同水平），因此经衰减校正后 MV 更新与 gold 更新的余弦 c∞=1（在 5 个模型上成立）；随机翻转高达 30% 的 gold 标签不改变更新方向，而同样错误率但按共享规则（长度）翻转则使 c∞ 跌到 0.287 甚至 −0.129——决定自生成标签能否替代验证的是"错误相干性"而非"错误率"，但该等价性是局部的：在 MATH-500 上 gold 训练最终仍以 maj@64 0.845 领先 MV 的 0.772（基线 0.747）。

> **TL;DR:** Why does RL from label-free majority-vote rewards recover most of the gain of verifiable (gold) rewards in LLM reasoning? This paper's answer: analyze the batch update, not the prompt. Using per-response gradient sketches (count sketches, K=2^16) that let them evaluate ANY reward on identical samples, the authors show on five models (Qwen3-4B-Base, Qwen3-1.7B-Base, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) that the majority-vote batch update agrees with the gold update as closely as two independent gold updates agree with each other, with a disattenuated cosine c∞ of 1.00-1.01 within 95% CI on all six arms. The mechanism: a prompt's own gradient accounts for only 0.09-0.83% of the change in its own output at practical batch sizes, and MV label errors are as incoherent across prompts as a Gaussian random-reward null, so they cancel as ~sqrt(B) while the coherent gold signal grows as B (crossover B*≈37-50, well below the B=256 used). The decisive control: randomly flipping up to 30% of gold labels leaves c∞≈1, while flipping the SAME 20% by a shared length rule crashes c∞ to 0.287 (and to −0.129 at 31%) — error coherence, not error rate, governs misdirection. Rewards that favor a "kind" of response everywhere (random vote 0.961, self-certainty vote 0.975, length-controlled INTUITOR 0.969, GRM-Llama3.2-3B reward model 0.982, pure length −0.415) fall measasurably short of 1. The equivalence is local: on MATH-500, a 20-step gold run reaches maj@64 0.845 vs 0.772 for MV (base 0.747), and doubling MV's batch to 512 does not close the gap. Best read as a criterion for when self-generated labels can replace verification, with unusually strong measurement methodology and an unusually honest limitations section.

## 核心贡献

- **重新定义分析单位：从 prompt 级到批次级。** 传统解释认为多数投票（MV）只能返回模型的众数答案、无法新增正确性信息；作者指出这一论证用了错误的单位——RL 在共享参数上对多样 prompt 批次求和出一个更新，在实用批次大小下单个 prompt 的自身梯度只解释其输出变化的 0.09%–0.83%，因此"错误标签"只有在其方向跨 prompt 相干时才有害。
- **把 MV 更新分解为收缩（shrinkage β）与误导（misdirection E⊥）。** 只有正交分量改变更新方向；理论上证明（命题 1、2 及推论 1）：不相干错误随 √εB 增长而 gold 信号随 B 增长，故任何错误率 ε 下误导都会在 B≫1/ρg 后消失；独立翻转 ε<1/2 的标签在期望上仅把更新缩放为 (1−2ε)·Ggold。
- **梯度层面的直接测量。** 用逐响应梯度 sketch（count sketch, K=2^16）在同一批样本上重加权即可计算任意奖励的更新，余弦与 fp32 精确梯度误差 <0.004；在 5 个模型、两个家族上，MV 与 gold 的分裂半批余弦达到 gold 自身一致性的天花板（差距 <0.02），衰减校正后的 c∞=1（点估计 1.00–1.01）。
- **"错误结构而非错误率"的对照实验。** 相同 20% 错误率下，独立随机翻转 c∞=1.011，而按共享长度规则翻转 c∞=0.287（31% 时为 −0.129，方向反转）；凡是"在每个 prompt 上都偏好某类回答"的奖励（随机投票、自信度投票、INTUITOR、学习型奖励模型、纯长度奖励）c∞ 均低于 1。
- **训练全程追踪与诚实的边界划定。** 沿 26 步 MV 训练轨迹，错误始终停留在随机零假设水平（step 20 时误差/信号比从 0.43 降至 0.24）；但等价是局部的——held-out MATH-500 上 gold 最终仍领先，且加倍批次无法弥合，说明逐步等价不等于轨迹等价。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes the puzzle of self-generated rewards as a batch-level question.** The standard prompt-level account (MV returns the modal answer and cannot add correctness information) cannot explain observed cross-problem information transfer; the authors show the batch update is the right unit because a prompt's own gradient contributes <1% of the change in that prompt's output at B=64–256.
- **A clean decomposition and theory.** G_mv = (1−β)·G_gold + E⊥ separates shrinkage (absorbed like a smaller learning rate / by Adam's second-moment normalization) from misdirection (the only direction-changing part); Propositions 1–2 show incoherent errors decay as 1/√(Bρg) relative to signal for ANY error rate ε, while coherent errors leave a batch-size-independent floor; Corollary 1 proves independent flips at rate ε<1/2 shrink the expected update to exactly (1−2ε)·G_gold.
- **Per-response gradient sketches as a measurement instrument.** Count sketches (K=2^16) of ∇log π at the sampling policy make the GRPO update linear in advantages, so every reward (MV, gold, flips, self-certainty, INTUITOR, reward model, length) is evaluated on literally identical samples — an apples-to-apples comparison that prior prompt-level analyses could not make.
- **Disattenuated-cosine benchmark done right.** Since cos=1 is the wrong yardstick (even two gold updates on disjoint halves only reach 0.37–0.84), they use split-half gold-vs-gold agreement as the ceiling and Spearman's correction for attenuation (c∞ = X/√(YZ)) to estimate the cosine between expected updates; MV hits the ceiling on every model.
- **Error-structure-vs-error-rate controls.** Matched-error-rate experiments (random vs shared-rule flips) plus a zoo of real label-free rewards isolate coherence ρe as the operative variable — MV is the exception among votes because it differs from gold ONLY where the majority is wrong, and there its errors are prompt-specific.
- **Trajectory-level honesty.** They follow gold and MV training runs seeing identical prompts, show the incoherence property survives training, quantify that per-step errors nonetheless accumulate slightly faster than a random walk (exponent 0.594 vs 0.540 random-sign reference, p=0.005), and show gold still ends ahead on held-out majority accuracy.

## Methodology

The paper combines theory, measurement, and training runs. **Setup:** GRPO training (verl 0.8.0) of Qwen3-4B-Base on DeepScaleR (29k problems), B=256 prompts/step, n=16 responses/prompt, 26 steps, lr 1e-6 constant, no KL or entropy regularization; gold and MV runs share the same initialization, data order, and prompts, differing only in the reward. Gradient analyses at initialization add Qwen3-1.7B-Base, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Evaluation is on MATH-500 and its 134 level-5 problems with 64 samples/problem at temperature 1.0. **Instrumentation:** for every response they store the gradient of log π (compressed to K=2^16 by count sketch); since the batch update G(r) = Σ A_ij ∇log π is linear in advantages, any reward is a reweighting of the same sketches — cosine values agree with exact fp32 gradients to within 0.004. **Estimator:** split the prompt groups into disjoint halves; X = cos(G_mv^A, G_gold^B), Y = cos(G_gold^A, G_gold^B) (the ceiling), Z = cos(G_mv^A, G_mv^B); the disattenuated c∞ = X/√(YZ) estimates the cosine between expected MV and gold updates, reported only when Z ≥ 0.3, with bootstrap over prompt groups. **Decomposition:** write G_mv = (1−β)G_gold + E⊥ with E⊥ ⊥ G_gold; β is shrinkage, R⊥ = ‖E⊥‖/‖G_gold‖ is misdirection. **Theory:** Proposition 1 gives the growth exponent of a sum of vectors via mean pairwise alignment ρ̄ (1/2 for incoherent, →1 for coherent, crossover B*≈1/ρ̄ — the gradient noise scale of McCandlish et al. 2018); Proposition 2 bounds R⊥² and shows the ρe=0 case vanishes with B while ρe>0 leaves a floor. **Controls:** independent label flips at ε∈{0.05,...,0.30} vs shared-rule flips (longest-correct/shortest-incorrect) at matched rates; reward zoo (random vote, self-certainty vote, INTUITOR length-controlled, GRM-Llama3.2-3B reward model, pure length, Gaussian random reward null); frozen-base-model labels (Appendix C); same-prompt replication across checkpoints; growth-exponent fits and random-sign references for cross-step error accumulation.

## Results & Highlights

- **MV matches the gold self-consistency ceiling on every model (Table 1).** Split-half gold-vs-gold cosine is only 0.371–0.842 (the ceiling any label source can reach); MV-vs-gold sits within 0.02 of it on 4 of 6 arms (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Base: 0.647 vs 0.647; Qwen3-1.7B-Base: 0.834 vs 0.842), and after disattenuation c∞ = 1.006–1.013 with 95% CIs covering 1 on all six arms across two model families, base and instruction-tuned, 1.7B–8B parameters.
- **A prompt's own gradient is negligible in its own output change.** At B=64–128, the prompt's own gradient accounts for 0.09–0.38% of the change in its output on the Qwen3 models and 0.83% on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct at B=64; correspondingly, on the 76 wrong-majority prompts of the first training batch, the MV batch update still increases the expected gold reward on 82% of them (to first order), with the prompt's own mislabeled term contributing only +0.007 to the alignment cosine vs +0.075 from the other prompts (almost exactly gold's +0.074).
- **Error structure, not error rate (Table 3, Qwen3-1.7B-Base, N=636 groups).** Independent flips of gold labels leave c∞ at 1 up to ε=0.30 (1.036); at a matched 20% error rate, shared-rule (length) flips give c∞ = 0.287 vs 1.011 for random flips, and at 31% the shared-rule update points AWAY from gold (−0.129); a pure length reward reaches −0.415. Real label-free rewards that prefer a response kind on every prompt all sit below 1: random vote 0.961, self-certainty vote 0.975, length-controlled INTUITOR 0.969, GRM-Llama3.2-3B reward model 0.982 — while MV itself is 0.997 with only 7.5% of responses mislabeled.
- **The property survives training, and errors shrink relative to signal.** Along the 26-step MV run, MV errors stay at the random-reward null at steps 10 and 20; the error/signal ratio ‖E‖/‖G_gold‖ falls from 0.43 at initialization to 0.24 at step 20, where c∞ = 1.000 [0.972, 1.030] and the decomposition gives β = 0.020 (98% of the gold component delivered) with a 14° rotation; on identical fixed prompts, misdirection halves (0.480 → 0.239) while shrinkage stays flat (0.019 → 0.020).
- **But the equivalence is local — gold ends ahead on held-out accuracy.** On MATH-500 at step 20, maj@64 rises from 0.747 (base) to 0.772 with MV vs 0.845 with gold (+0.026 vs +0.098 paired over 500 problems); on the 134 level-5 problems at step 10 (2 seeds/reward), gold leads MV in maj@1 0.344 vs 0.320 and maj@64 0.590 vs 0.541, though not in pass@64 (0.855 vs 0.862); doubling the MV batch to 512 prompts does NOT close the gap (step-10 maj@64 0.545 vs 0.582, CI [−0.007, +0.086]).
- **MV training repairs wrong majorities on held-out problems through the shared update.** Of the 66 level-5 problems where the base model has a wrong majority, 13 (19.7%) acquire a correct majority by step 20 of the MV run — about 3x the resampling flip rate — while breakage runs at 4.4% (below resampling); maj@1/maj@4 rise by 17–19 points, maj@16 by +0.100 and maj@64 by +0.082, and dropping the 3 problems that overlap DeepScaleR leaves the repair rate inside its interval.

## Authors & Credibility

Chengsong Huang and Jiaxin Huang are at Washington University in St. Louis, Dylan P. Wilson at Dartmouth College. The senior author (Jiaxin Huang) has an extensive track record in self-improving and label-free LLM training (e.g., "Large Language Models Can Self-Improve", EMNLP 2023; R-Zero, ICLR 2026; Parallel-R1), and the first author has multiple papers on test-time scaling and self-calibration — this is a group with deep firsthand experience in exactly the self-generated-reward methods being analyzed, which shows in the careful choice of baselines (TTRL, INTUITOR, self-certainty) and honest citations of failure modes. The paper carries an unusually strong reproducibility statement: release of gradient-sketching code, the count-sketch implementation, all estimator scripts, launcher/config files for every run, plus the per-response gradient sketches, gold/MV label sets, full generations, and a dated experiment log (code: github.com/dylan518/wrong-votes-right-gradients). The empirical hygiene is notable — identical samples across rewards, bootstrap CIs on essentially every number, seed-replication for training claims, and explicit reporting of unexplained anomalies (step 10) rather than hiding them.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuinely high on mechanism, not another reward variant.** The batch-level framing, the shrinkage/misdirection decomposition, and the measurement instrument (per-response gradient sketches enabling any-reward-on-identical-samples comparison) are new to the label-free-RL debate; the closest prior art (Rolnick et al.'s label-noise robustness, Chatterjee's coherent gradients, McCandlish's gradient noise scale) is correctly credited and extended to the two-reward-source comparison.
- **Rigor: strong at the update level, thinner at the trajectory level.** The training-dynamics claims rest on ONE model (Qwen3-4B-Base) with only two seeds per reward — and the paper itself concedes that at step 10 gold's advantage over MV is weaker than the spread between seeds (Table 8), so the headline "gold ends ahead" gap (0.845 vs 0.772 maj@64) is a two-seed result on a 20-step horizon. The gradient-level c∞=1 results do cover five models, but the authors admit Qwen models benefit even from rewards unrelated to correctness (the spurious-reward literature), so family-level confounding for the full-training story is real.
- **Reproducibility: excellent.** Sketches, labels, generations, configs, and logs promised under open license; estimators are exactly specified (c∞ = X/√(YZ), Z≥0.3 threshold, bootstrap over prompt groups). One caveat: the disattenuated estimator is a ratio of estimates and can exceed 1 by sampling error — the authors disclose this and clip at 1 in figures, but some intervals (e.g., 1.072–1.073 upper bounds on Llama arms) are wide enough that "1 within error" is doing modest work.
- **Significance: the criterion is the contribution, and it is actionable.** "Error coherence, not error rate" tells practitioners WHEN label-free RL should work (residual errors sparse and prompt-specific; on-policy labels; fresh diverse prompts) and when it must fail (shared misconceptions, revisited prompts, rewards that prefer a response kind everywhere). The negative control space (length-rule flips, pure length reward at −0.415) makes the criterion falsifiable rather than anecdotal.
- **Red flags and data quirks (all self-disclosed, which is creditable):** step 10 is an outlier in every measure and the authors state "We do not have an explanation for it"; the cross-step error-coherence analysis covers only steps 3–11 and already shows error accumulation significantly faster than a random walk (exponent 0.594 vs 0.540, p=0.005), a small shared error component that slightly undercuts the pure-incoherence story; answer parsing degrades during training (parseable fraction 0.84 → 0.75 from step 8 to 20); the MV run's entropy rises sharply beyond step 15, a regime left unanalyzed; evaluation is confined to MATH-500 and its level-5 subset (no code, no out-of-domain benchmarks); one verbatim plus two near-duplicate level-5 problems were found in DeepScaleR (dropping them leaves conclusions intact); and cosine analysis uses raw gradients, whereas Adam's preconditioner could reweight the directions being compared.

## Strengths

- The core question (why do label-free rewards track verifiable RL?) is important, timely, and answered with a mechanism rather than another empirical curve.
- Methodological elegance: per-response gradient sketches make every reward comparable on identical samples — this instrument alone will be reusable by others.
- The matched-error-rate control (random vs shared-rule flips at identical ε) is a textbook clean dissection of confounded variables (rate vs structure).
- Theory (growth exponents, crossover B*, flip corollary) is simple, testable, and confirmed by two independent estimators (pairwise cosines and growth-exponent fits agree: ρ̄≈0.020 vs 0.022 measured; B*≈50 vs 37).
- Exceptional honesty: local-vs-trajectory distinction, unexplained step-10 anomaly, above-random-walk error accumulation, and contamination checks are all surfaced in the main text, not buried.

## Limitations

- Full training runs use a single model (Qwen3-4B-Base), a single dataset (DeepScaleR), a short horizon (26 steps, analyses mostly at steps ≤20), and no KL/entropy regularization; the entropy blow-up after step 15 of the MV run is unexamined.
- Held-out evaluation is MATH-500 only — one benchmark, one domain; no code generation, no other reasoning suites, so the "gold ends ahead by ~7 points maj@64" gap could look different elsewhere.
- The equivalence claim is about raw-gradient cosines; Adam's preconditioning, which the actual training uses, could reweight the misdirection component in ways the analysis does not capture.
- c∞ intervals are wide on some arms (Llama models), and the estimator can exceed 1 by construction; a few arms fall below the Z≥0.3 reportability threshold (both step-10 runs).
- Cross-step error coherence is measured over only nine steps (t=3..11) and already shows a small shared component (cos(E_t,E_s)=0.030 vs 0.001 null) growing faster than a random walk — the long-run behavior that would decide trajectory-level divergence is extrapolated, not measured.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接回应了"无标签 RLVR 训练为何有效、何时会失效"这一核心问题：它给出的可操作判据——错误相干性而非错误率决定自生成标签能否替代验证、on-policy 标签与新鲜多样 prompt 是关键——对设计 majority-vote / 自信度 / 熵类自生成奖励的推理 RL 训练管线（以及判断何时必须引入验证器）具有直接指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the "gold ends ahead" gap (0.845 vs 0.772 maj@64 at step 20) widen, close, or invert over hundreds of steps — and is the divergence driven by the small shared error component (cos(E_t,E_s)=0.030) accumulating as T rather than √T?
- How do Adam's preconditioner and group-std advantage normalization interact with the misdirection component in practice — does second-moment normalization amplify directions the raw-cosine analysis treats as negligible?
- Does the error-coherence criterion predict failure in domains with widely shared misconceptions (e.g., commonsense QA, code with common buggy patterns) or under prompt-revisiting curricula, as the theory predicts?
- Would mixing a small fraction of verified labels into MV (to break coherent error directions) recover the gold trajectory at a fraction of the verification cost?
- Can the per-response gradient-sketch instrument be scaled to larger models and longer runs cheaply enough to become a standard diagnostic for any proposed self-generated reward before committing to a full training run?
