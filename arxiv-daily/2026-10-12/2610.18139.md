> **一句话总结：** 本文把 GPTQ 式因果误差反馈的系数矩阵当作显式设计变量,在"量化噪声各向同性"假设下建立了一个迹(trace)优化框架:证明旋转+反馈联合设计的全局最优值为 n·det(H)^{1/n} 并用几何均值分解构造出达到该界的解;实验表明融合下游 Jacobian 敏感度的反馈设计能稳定降低量化后网络输出失真(合成网络上较 GPTQ 式基线降 14–18%),但也诚实地揭示迹最优并不等于有限量化级数下的实际性能提升。

> **TL;DR:** This paper reformulates the causal error-feedback coefficients behind GPTQ-style quantization as explicit design variables of a trace minimization problem, recovering LDLQ feedback as a special case, adding Jacobian-aware (downstream-sensitive) metrics, and proving a closed-form joint rotation-feedback optimum n·det(H)^{1/n} attained via the geometric mean decomposition. On synthetic Gaussian networks it cuts mean test output distortion by 13.88–17.55% vs the α=0 GPTQ-style baseline across 2–16 quantization levels; on MNIST/CIFAR-10 MLPs at M=2 it drops logit MSE from 8.61 (RTN) to 1.64; on Llama-2-7B at 8 levels, objective-aware RHT+EFQ reaches 8.70 perplexity vs 11.31 for local (covariance-only) EFQ on WikiText-2. Notably, the paper's own headline theory (the trace-optimal joint design) does NOT win in practice — randomized Hadamard + EFQ beats it on all three metrics in 16/16 LM comparisons — because the trace objective ignores finite-grid scale effects, a gap the authors document honestly.

## 核心贡献

- **反馈系数即设计变量**:将量化器写成 ŵ = w + Gq(G 为单位下三角阵)的因果反馈形式,在"瞬时量化误差 q 服从各向同性二阶矩 E[qqᵀ] = σ²I"的假设下,把反馈设计归结为可解的迹问题 min tr(GᵀHG);通过 KKT 条件证明最优解恰为 H⁻¹ 的 LDL 分解的 L 因子——这从第一性原理重新推导出了 GPTQ/LDLQ 的反馈结构。
- **下游敏感的反馈设计**:用网络的输出 Jacobian J_{ℓ,t} 构造输入坐标度量 H_Jac(公式 8/10),以插值系数 α 在"层内局部失真"与"下游误差传播"之间权衡(f = tr(Gᵀ(C̄ + αs·H_Jac)G)),使反馈设计能感知量化误差经后续层传播的放大效应。
- **旋转-反馈联合设计的全局最优(Proposition 1)**:对任意正定度量 H,联合优化正交旋转 R 与单位下三角反馈 G 的迹目标最小值为 n·det(H)^{1/n},并利用几何均值分解(GMD)构造出精确达到该下界的 (R*, G*) 对。
- **诚实的"理论-实践鸿沟"分析**:Appendix C 用实验证明联合设计精确达到迹下界(trace ratio = 1.00000),但其有限级数量化后的实际误差反而更大(M=2 时 MNIST 实测度量误差 347.73 vs 协方差反馈的 98.61)——指出迹最优未考虑旋转改变 min-max 量化格距 Δ(R) 与反馈饱和等有限级效应。
- **QAT 中直接训练反馈系数**:用直通估计器(STE)对 G 的自由元素直接针对硬量化网络损失做梯度训练,可与权重联合训练;在合成回归与 Shakespeare 字符级 nanoGPT 上均验证可行。

## Key Contributions

- **Feedback coefficients as design variables.** The causal error-feedback quantizer ŵ = w + Gq (G unit lower-triangular) is treated as an explicit design object. Under an isotropic second-moment model E[qqᵀ] = σ²I for instantaneous quantization errors, the design reduces to the strictly convex trace problem min tr(GᵀHG), whose KKT solution G* = L from the LDL factorization of H⁻¹ exactly recovers fixed-order GPTQ/LDLQ feedback as a special case.
- **Jacobian-aware (downstream-sensitive) metrics.** A row-isotropic input-coordinate metric H_Jac built from output Jacobians J_{ℓ,t} is combined with the input covariance as tr(Gᵀ(C̄ + αs·H_Jac)G), letting α trade local layer distortion against downstream error propagation through subsequent layers — a tractable surrogate for network-output error (Eq. 1–2, 8–10).
- **Closed-form joint rotation-feedback optimum.** Proposition 1 proves that min over orthogonal R and unit lower-triangular G of tr(GᵀRHRᵀG) equals n·det(H)^{1/n} for any H ≻ 0, and constructs an attaining pair (R* = Qᵀ, G* = √η·T⁻ᵀ) from the geometric mean decomposition H^{1/2} = QT Vᵀ — rotation equalizes feedback-adjusted sensitivities while feedback removes their triangular coupling.
- **An honest characterization of the theory-practice gap.** A dedicated diagnostic (Appendix C) shows the joint design attains the blockwise trace bound to numerical precision (ratio 1.00000), yet realized finite-level metric error is ~3.5x WORSE than covariance feedback at M=2 (347.73 vs 98.61 on MNIST), because the fixed-statistics trace objective ignores rotation-dependent grid scale Δ(R) and feedback-induced saturation — a carefully documented negative result.
- **Direct loss-driven feedback tuning (QAT).** Free entries of G are trained against the hard-quantized network loss via a straight-through backward rule, either with frozen weights or jointly with them; validated on synthetic regression and a character-level Shakespeare nanoGPT.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in three layers. **(1) Causal feedback framework (Sec. 3).** Quantization proceeds coordinate-by-coordinate: the quantizer input is b_i = w_i + Σ_{j<i} g_ij q_j, so past instantaneous errors are fed forward with coefficients g_ij, giving ŵ = w + Gq. Under Assumption 1 (q isotropic, zero-mean, fixed variance), the expected layer distortion σ²·tr(GᵀHG) leads to the trace design problem; its solution via KKT/LDL analysis reproduces GPTQ/LDLQ-style covariance feedback when H = input covariance C. The framework generalizes to any metric f(G; Θ), and the authors instantiate it with the Jacobian metric H_Jac = (1/N)Σ_t ‖J_{ℓ,t}e_r‖² x_{ℓ,t}x_{ℓ,t}ᵀ, mixing C̄ and H_Jac with a per-layer tradeoff α selected on held-out calibration data. **(2) Joint rotation-feedback theory (Sec. 4).** With a rotated representation ŵ = RᵀGq, the trace objective becomes tr(GᵀRHRᵀG). For fixed R the optimum is the reverse-LDL factor G* = U_R⁻ᵀ with value tr(D_R); since det(D_R) = det(H), the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives the lower bound n·det(H)^{1/n}, attained constructively via the geometric mean decomposition (Proposition 1). Section 4.1 then analyzes why this fixed-statistics optimum need not translate to finite-level quantization: the min-max grid step Δ(R) = (max(Rw) − min(Rw))/(M−1) grows with the coordinate range the rotation creates, and feedback can push quantizer inputs outside the fitted interval (saturation), both unmodeled by the trace objective. **(3) Experiments (Sec. 5).** (a) 100 random 5-layer Gaussian networks sweeping α; (b) PTQ of trained 5-layer tanh MLPs on MNIST/CIFAR-10 (block size 32, M ∈ {2,4,8,16}, 100 checkpoints) comparing RTN, covariance EFQ, RHT+EFQ, objective-aware EFQ, and joint design; (c) PTQ of GPT-2 (8064 scored positions) and Llama-2-7B/13B, Qwen3-8B/14B calibrated on 128 WikiText-2 sequences of length 512 (seed 7), reporting perplexity / next-token accuracy / final-logit MSE at M ∈ {8,16}; (d) QAT with straight-through gradient on a 4→6→3 synthetic regression network (100 paired trials) and a 2-block nanoGPT on Shakespeare (10 seeds), tuning G alone or jointly with weights.

## Results & Highlights

- **Synthetic networks (Table 1):** with per-network α selection on calibration data, the Jacobian-aware feedback reduces mean test output distortion by **13.88%–17.55%** relative to the α=0 GPTQ-style baseline across M ∈ {2,4,8,12,16} (e.g., M=4: 0.1125 vs 0.1365), winning on ≥72/100 independently generated networks for every M (win/tie/loss up to 75/20/5).
- **MNIST MLPs (Table 2):** objective-aware EFQ achieves the lowest mean test logit MSE at every resolution; at M=2 it reaches **1.637 vs 8.607 for RTN and 1.696 for covariance-based EFQ**, with accuracy drop 4.83 pp vs RTN's 32.57 pp; at M=8 logit MSE is 0.01335 vs RTN's 0.05698. CIFAR-10 M=2 shows the same ordering (1.816 vs RTN 5.124), though the edge over covariance EFQ narrows at large M (M=16: 0.003951 vs 0.004025).
- **Language models (Table 3):** on Llama-2-7B at M=8 levels, objective-aware RHT+EFQ reaches **8.704 perplexity / 53.73% next-token accuracy / 1.739 logit MSE vs 11.312 / 50.22% / 3.989 for local (covariance) EFQ**; on Qwen3-8B at M=8, 16.118 PPL / 47.08% acc vs 20.227 / 43.52% for local EFQ. Across the four larger models, objective-aware design improves final-logit MSE and next-token accuracy in **all 24 matched comparisons** and perplexity in 21/24, and objective-aware RHT+EFQ is best on all three metrics in every setting.
- **Joint trace optimum loses in practice:** RHT+EFQ outperforms the trace-optimal joint rotation-feedback design on **all three metrics in 16/16 comparisons** on the four larger models (e.g., Llama-2-7B M=16: 7.431 vs 7.563 PPL); Appendix C confirms joint design attains its trace bound exactly (ratio 1.00000) yet has ~3.5x worse realized metric error at M=2 on MNIST (347.73 vs 98.61) and test logit MSE 9.22 vs 1.70.
- **QAT (Tables 4–5):** feedback-only tuning on the synthetic 4→6→3 network cuts M=2 test MSE from 0.486 (covariance-feedback PTQ) to **0.286**, and joint QAT beats weight-only QAT at all three levels (M=2: 0.2452 vs 0.2546). On Shakespeare char-LM at M=2, feedback QAT reaches 13.074 held-out perplexity vs 13.629 (local PTQ) and 18.282 (RTN); joint QAT (12.528) edges weight-only QAT (12.561), though continued full-precision training (12.374) still beats all quantized arms.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Solid but incremental on the empirical side — the trace framework largely re-derives known structures (the α=0 solution IS GPTQ/LDLQ feedback, which the authors themselves note via recent Babai-algorithm equivalence papers [5, 8]). The genuinely new pieces are (a) the Jacobian-metric interpolation knob α and (b) Proposition 1's closed-form joint optimum, whose main empirical contribution turns out to be demonstrating that the optimum does not matter at finite levels.
- **Rigor:** The theory is clean and fully proved; the experimental methodology is careful (paired seeds, held-out α selection, 100-instance averages, separate calibration/test protocols). Refreshingly, the paper includes its own negative-result diagnostic (Appendix C) rather than hiding the joint design's failure.
- **Reproducibility:** Reasonable — models (GPT-2, Llama-2, Qwen3) and datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, WikiText-2, Shakespeare) are public, calibration details are given (128 length-512 WikiText-2 sequences, seed 7), but no code release is mentioned and several hyperparameters (per-layer scale s_ℓ, damping λ, block size 32, α candidate grids) would need to be re-tuned from the appendix.
- **Significance:** The Jacobian-aware feedback gains on real LLMs are real but modest (e.g., Llama-2-7B M=16: 7.431 vs 7.450 PPL for local EFQ — a 0.3% relative gain; the big jumps are vs their own RTN/local-EFQ baselines, not vs SOTA). The headline LM numbers (7.4 PPL at M=16 for Llama-2-7B) are in a regime where strong baselines barely degrade, so practical impact over GPTQ/AWQ/QuIP# is undemonstrated.
- **Red flags / weaknesses to note:** (1) **No head-to-head comparison against official GPTQ, AWQ, QuIP#, QuaRot, or SpinQuant implementations** — "Local EFQ" is an internal reimplementation, so the LLM results position the method only within the paper's own framework, not against the SOTA the related-work section names. (2) **No 2–4 bit LM results**: LM experiments stop at M=8/16 (3–4 bit, uniform grids with group-wise fixed alphabets), while the competitive frontier of LLM PTQ targets 2–4 bit with non-uniform/optimal rounding; the α sweep is also skipped for larger models in favor of validation-selected α from a small candidate set. (3) **Assumption 1 (isotropic, independent quantization errors with common fixed variance)** is idealized, and the paper's own Section 4.1/Appendix C shows exactly where it breaks — the mismatch is honestly analyzed but never quantified beyond the diagnostic. (4) GPT-2 rows use a different evaluation protocol (8064 scored positions) than the Llama/Qwen rows, making cross-model reading of Table 3 hazardous — the authors flag this, but it still fragments the evidence. (5) The blockwise restriction (blocks of 32) means Proposition 1's bound is not the full-coordinate bound n·det(H)^{1/n} that the main text emphasizes; the effective guarantee is Σ_b n_b·det(H_bb)^{1/n_b} (Appendix B.3), a caveat easy to miss. (6) QAT experiments are tiny (2-block, width-64 nanoGPT; 200+100 training steps), so the scalability of trainable feedback to real pretrained LLMs is entirely open.

## Strengths

- Unifies GPTQ/LDLQ-style feedback, Jacobian-aware design, and rotation-based quantization under one trace formulation with a precise optimality characterization (Proposition 1) and constructive attainer.
- The Jacobian-aware metric is a simple, plug-in way to make layer-wise feedback downstream-sensitive, and it delivers consistent gains across synthetic networks, MLPs, and five language models (24/24 matched improvements in logit MSE and accuracy on the larger LMs).
- Exemplary scientific honesty: the paper explicitly documents that its own theoretical optimum fails to deliver finite-level gains, explains the mechanism (rotation-dependent grid scale, saturation), and includes the diagnostic numbers.
- The QAT extension (trainable feedback coefficients via straight-through gradients) is a genuinely fresh angle — feedback design by gradient descent rather than closed-form surrogates.
- Clean positioning of a crowded related-work landscape via the criterion-mapping table (Table 6), including precise statements of how QEP/GuidedQuant/YAQA/Qronos criteria embed in the framework.

## Limitations

- The isotropic fixed-variance error model (Assumption 1) decouples the design from the actual quantizer behavior; the paper shows this decoupling is consequential (trace-optimal joint design loses to RHT+EFQ in 16/16 LM comparisons), leaving the core theoretical contribution as a benchmark rather than an algorithmic win.
- No comparison against externally published numbers or official implementations of GPTQ/AWQ/QuIP#/QuaRot; "Local EFQ" is the de facto baseline, so competitive positioning at the 2–4 bit frontier where those methods operate is unknown.
- LM evaluation covers only uniform grids at M ∈ {8,16} on WikiText-2-style perplexity; no downstream task accuracy, no extreme low-bit regime, no group-size/axis ablations beyond block size 32.
- Gains over covariance-based EFQ shrink as M grows (e.g., MNIST M=16: 0.002856 vs 0.002940 logit MSE), so the practical value is concentrated at very low bit widths where the method is least tested on LLMs.
- QAT evidence is limited to toy-scale models; whether learned feedback survives at billion-parameter scale, with larger training budgets, and in combination with rotation methods remains unexamined.
- The blockwise trace bound (Appendix B.3) is weaker than the headline full-coordinate bound; block size effects on the bound and on performance are not ablated.

## Relevance

这篇论文与高效模型压缩/后训练量化(PTQ)方向高度相关:它把 GPTQ 类误差反馈几何化、理论化,并提供了可插拔的下游 Jacobian 敏感度度量与可训练反馈系数两条实用改进路线,对关心 LLM 低比特推理与量化理论(旋转、噪声成形、迹界)的工作都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the trace formulation be augmented with a scale-aware term (e.g., predicting Δ(R)² per block) so that the joint rotation-feedback optimum also wins under finite-level quantization, closing the gap exposed in Appendix C?
- How does Jacobian-aware feedback interact with state-of-the-art low-bit pipelines (QuIP#'s LDLQ + Hadamard, SpinQuant's learned rotations) at 2–3 bits — complementary or redundant?
- Does trainable feedback G (QAT) scale beyond 2-block nanoGPT to 7B-class models, and does it recover the accuracy that PTQ variants lose, at acceptable calibration-compute cost?
- The Jacobian metric is first-order; would a second-order (curvature-aware, YAQA-style) downstream metric change the α sweep conclusions, especially the finding that fixed positive α often hurts?
- Since upstream-quantized activations can be folded into Θ_ℓ (Appendix A), how much extra gain does sequential (already-quantized-input-aware) feedback design yield over the independent layer-wise scheme used in the LM experiments?
