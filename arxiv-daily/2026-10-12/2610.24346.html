LLM-quantization LLM-agents counterfactual-evaluation tool-use evaluation-methodology
Key metric
−4.57 pp

Net gain of action-only BF16 fallback on the pre-registered 34-task telecom holdout — 90% CI [−7.17, −1.96], entirely below zero; damages 9.77% vs rescues 5.21%.

Why this matters

Gives precision-routing research its correct yardstick — signed task-level net benefit with same-precision controls — and warns that outcome flips don’t prove quantization is the culprit.

⚠ Critical edge

The registered holdout categories had no “negative” class — so a 90% CI entirely below zero is officially read as merely “inconclusive”, softening the paper’s own headline negative.

Methodology

The four-branch fork

The core instrument is a fork: before decision t of the quantized agent’s trajectory, the full state (history, budgets, replayed environment) is frozen and restored. Four complete executions run from identical prompt tokens, crossing who supplies the current action × who continues. A fallback’s value is the signed count of tasks rescued minus tasks spoiled — one intervention per task–block.

G = Y_BQ − Y_QQ · H = Y_QB − Y_QQ · F = Y_BB − Y_QQ

Three state sources feed forks: first-divergent, ordinary (hash-selected), and common states. Setup: τ²-bench text mode (85 retail+airline dev tasks × 20 blocks; 34-task telecom holdout), Qwen3-4B/8B with simulated W4 quant (GPTQ/AWQ-style, group 128), INT8-KV plus five lower-bit stress settings; deterministic vLLM serving, no-op replay audits on every state (924/924 dev, 1202/1202 holdout). Same-precision references: a second W4 checkpoint Q′ and a key-order reversal control. Code: github.com/W4ng77/cloq.

  • Finding changes ≠ finding benefit: the token-margin trigger captures 27 turnovers at the 20% rate vs 14.30 random (p = .0002), yet its net benefit is unresolved after Holm correction at every rate.
  • Rescues don’t transfer across modes: ~3.96% of W4 divergent states are joint-only rescues; ~4.80% are rescues undone by BF16 continuation.
  • The “upgrade” isn’t always ahead: end-to-end, 4B GPTQ-W4 scored 40.0% vs 35.8% for its own BF16 (+4.2 pp [−2.4, +10.5]).
  • Scope checks: BFCL pooled +0.1 pp; ALFWorld 8B −3.7 pp [−9.7, +1.9]; Mistral-Small-24B unresolved in every mode.
Strengths

What it gets right

  • The correct estimand — signed net task benefit of the intervention that actually runs; neither KL-divergence nor end-to-end success measures it.
  • Reusable controls — the Q′ checkpoint and key-reversal references reproduce “fallback changes outcomes” with zero extra bits; a direct caution for precision-routing work.
  • Exceptional hygiene — hash-registered holdout, no-op replay audits, deterministic serving, permutation references, dated post-hoc amendments.
  • Honest negatives — refuses to claim a BF16 advantage its intervals cannot support.
Limitations

Where it stops short

  • Simulated quantization only — no deployed low-bit kernels, no latency, no fallback-cost accounting (a takeover spends many BF16 decisions).
  • Narrow crossed coverage — Qwen3-4B/8B on τ²-bench; Llama-3.1-8B dropped at its gate; positives appear only in 3-block exploratory stress cells.
  • One greedy user simulator — under a second simulator only 7/39 text-flip labels kept direction.
  • Confounded holdout — the −4.57 pp estimate mixes domain shift with task shift; cause openly admitted unknown.
Open questions

For further exploration

  1. Would the −4.57 pp action-only estimate survive separating domain shift from task shift?
  2. Do conclusions transfer to real deployed low-bit kernels, where latency and cost interact with mode choice?
  3. Can a learned router be trained against the signed net-benefit target and beat the margin trigger’s unresolved value?
  4. Why does BF16-action + quantized-continuation fail where BB succeeds — a “handoff tax” on non-native trajectories?
  5. Is the ~4% joint-rescue / ~4.8% undone structure stable across scale (14B/32B/70B) and families?
  6. Should key-reversal and second-checkpoint references become standard controls in quantized-agent benchmarks?
