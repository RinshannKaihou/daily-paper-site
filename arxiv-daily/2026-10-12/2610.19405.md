> **一句话总结：** 本文在 8×H200 上用 Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct + vLLM 系统地刻画了张量并行（TP）与流水线并行（PP）的首 token 延迟（TTFT）—逐 token 延迟（ITL）权衡：解码为主的工作负载和宽松 SLO 下 TP 占优，预填充为主、紧 TTFT SLO 和长上下文场景下 PP 占优，并用 goodput（吞吐×SLO 达成率）给出了随工作负载/SLO/上下文长度变化的并行度选择框架。

> **TL;DR:** A systematic empirical study of LLM inference parallelism (TP vs PP vs hybrids) on 8× NVIDIA H200 GPUs with Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct served by vLLM v0.15.0. Using TTFT-ITL Pareto frontiers over fixed-total-length workloads (Lseq=1200, batch 128), the authors show a systematic transition from TP-dominant optima for decode-heavy workloads to PP-dominant optima for prefill-heavy ones; under a goodput objective (throughput × SLO-attainment, throughput spanning ~9K–12K tok/s across configs), tight TTFT SLOs of 5–10 s favor PP8 while relaxed 20–25 s SLOs shift the optimum to TP8; and as context length scales toward ~30K tokens, PP-dominant configurations overtake TP because their SLO attainment decays orders-of-magnitude more slowly (e.g., TP8's attainment collapses toward 10⁻² under a 2 s SLO while PP8 retains far higher compliance). No new system is built — this is a characterization framework intended to guide adaptive parallelism selection.

## 核心贡献

- 提出**并行度工作区域（parallelism operating region）**概念：对固定总序列长度的工作负载族（Lin+Lout=Lseq），在 (TTFT, ITL) 二维空间构造每种并行配置的可行集与 Pareto 前沿，把“哪种并行最好”从静态问题改造为随工作负载组成变化的工作点选择问题。
- 给出**相位级延迟分解模型**：T_q^(p) = τ（计算）+ η（通信/同步）+ γ（调度开销），解释了为何 TP 在解码阶段（每次迭代计算量小、集合通信在关键路径上）吃亏而在预填充阶段受益方向相反、PP 恰好互补。
- 实验揭示**系统性的 TP→PP 转移规律**：工作负载从解码为主转向预填充为主时，Pareto 最优配置依次经历 TP8 → 混合（TP4PP2/TP2PP4）→ PP8；输出长度（OSL）增大主要改善 ITL 且对 TP 收益最大、对 PP 几乎无改善。
- 形式化**SLO 感知的并行度选择**：goodput G_p = λ_p·F_p(T_s|w)（吞吐 × TTFT 达成率），证明紧 TTFT 约束下 PP 用高达成率弥补低吞吐而胜出，约束放宽后（F→1，goodput→原始吞吐）最优解逐步移回 TP；并给出两配置优劣切换的解析条件（公式 14/16）。
- **长上下文可扩展性分析**：固定输出长度、递增输入长度（Lseq 至 ~30K），证明 PP 主导配置的 SLO 达成率随序列长度衰减远慢于 TP，从而在长上下文与紧 SLO 体制下取得更高 goodput——长序列可扩展性由"吞吐衰减 + 达成率衰减速率"共同决定。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the notion of a **parallelism operating region**: for a workload family with fixed total sequence length (Lin + Lout = Lseq), constructs TTFT-ITL feasible sets and Pareto frontiers per parallelism configuration p = (N_TP, N_PP) on N GPUs, reframing parallelism choice as a workload-dependent operating point rather than a static deployment decision.
- A **phase-level latency model** T_q^(p) = τ_q + η_q + γ_q (computation + communication/synchronization + scheduling overhead) explains the complementary behavior: TP slashes per-GPU compute but pays collective communication that dominates small decode steps, while PP avoids fine-grained collectives (good for compute-heavy prefill) but suffers pipeline bubbles and stage-propagation costs in decode.
- Empirically demonstrates a **systematic TP→hybrid→PP transition** of the Pareto-optimal configuration as workloads shift from decode-heavy to prefill-heavy, plus a non-uniform OSL effect: longer outputs lower measured ITL mainly for TP-dominant configurations (better amortization of decode overheads), leaving TTFT essentially unchanged.
- Formalizes **SLO-aware parallelism selection** via goodput G_p(w, T_s) = λ_p(w)·F_p(T_s|w), where F_p is the empirical TTFT CDF; derives the crossover condition (F_pi/F_pj = λ_pj/λ_pi) and shows the optimum moves from PP8 under stringent TTFT SLOs to TP8 as the SLO relaxes and goodput converges to raw throughput.
- Extends the analysis to **long-context scalability**: sweeping Lseq up to ~30K with fixed Lout=1000, C=128 shows PP-dominant configurations degrade far more gracefully because their SLO-attainment F_p(T_s|Lseq) decays much more slowly than TP8's, so they eventually deliver higher goodput in the long-context regime, especially under tight (2 s) TTFT SLOs.

## Methodology

The paper is a measurement-and-modeling study, not a new system. All experiments run Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (BF16) on 8× NVIDIA H200 (141 GB) with vLLM v0.15.0, batch size 128, GPU memory utilization 0.8, and chunked prefill enabled. Four parallelism configurations with N_TP·N_PP = 8 are compared: TP8=(8,1), TP4PP2=(4,2), TP2PP4=(2,4), PP8=(1,8).

1. **Latency decomposition and Pareto analysis (Sec. 2):** Each configuration's per-phase latency is decomposed into computation, communication/synchronization, and scheduling overheads. Workloads are parameterized as w = (L_in, L_out, C). To isolate workload *composition* from workload *scale*, the total sequence length is fixed (Lseq = L_in + L_out = 1200) while the input/output split varies; each configuration's (TTFT_avg, ITL_avg) points define a feasible set, from which a Pareto frontier (non-dominated configurations per Eq. 9-10) is extracted. A second experiment varies L_out ∈ {200, 400, 800} at fixed L_in = 1000 to isolate the output-length effect.
2. **SLO-aware selection (Sec. 3):** Goodput is defined as G_p(w, T_s) = λ_p(w)·F_p(T_s|w), the product of request throughput and the fraction of requests meeting the TTFT target T_s (empirical CDF of measured TTFT). The optimal configuration solves p* = argmax_p G_p. Experiments fix w = (1200, 2800, 256) (Lseq = 4000) and sweep T_s ∈ {5, 10, 20, 25 s}, plotting each configuration in the (F_p, λ_p) plane and as goodput-vs-SLO curves; the limit T_s → ∞ analytically collapses goodput to raw throughput.
3. **Long-context scalability (Sec. 4):** With L_out = 1000 and concurrency C = 128 fixed, L_in is increased so Lseq sweeps from ~0 to ~30K. The paper tracks both components of G_p(Lseq, T_s) = λ_p(Lseq)·F_p(T_s|Lseq): throughput degradation and the decay rate of SLO attainment, under both relaxed (T_s = 5 s) and tight (T_s = 2 s) TTFT constraints.

## Results & Highlights

- **TTFT-ITL frontier (Fig. 1; Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, 8×H200, vLLM v0.15.0, batch 128, Lseq=1200):** TP8 occupies the low-ITL/high-TTFT end and PP8 the low-TTFT/high-ITL end of the frontier (frontier spans roughly 0–12 s TTFT and 0–50 ms ITL, values read from figure axes). Concretely, PP8 achieves substantially lower TTFT than TP8 on prefill-heavy compositions while TP8 achieves substantially lower ITL on decode-heavy ones — no configuration dominates, and the Pareto-optimal point shifts TP8 → TP4PP2/TP2PP4 → PP8 as L_in/L_out grows.
- **Goodput crossover (Figs. 4-5; workload w=(1200, 2800, 256), Lseq=4000):** Raw throughput λ_p spans ~9K to ~12K tok/s across the four configurations (TP8 highest, PP8 lowest, approximate figure-axis reads). Under stringent TTFT SLOs T_s = 5 s and 10 s, PP8 delivers the *highest goodput* — its much larger SLO-attainment F_pp8(T_s) overcomes its ~3K tok/s throughput deficit versus TP8 (crossover condition F_PP8/F_TP8 > λ_TP8/λ_PP8). At T_s = 20–25 s the optimum shifts to the hybrids and ultimately to TP8, matching the analytic limit G_p → λ_p.
- **OSL effect is configuration-dependent (Fig. 2; w=(1000, L_out, 256), L_out ∈ {200, 400, 800}):** Raising output length from 200 to 800 tokens leaves TTFT essentially unchanged (TTFT is prefill-dominated) but lowers measured ITL (ITL axis range ~20–70 ms), with the largest improvement for TP8 and almost no improvement for PP8 — pipeline synchronization costs recur every decode iteration and cannot be amortized over more tokens, whereas TP amortizes per-request decode overheads well.
- **Long-context scaling (Figs. 6-7; w=(L_in, 1000, 128), Lseq swept to ~30K):** Goodput (log scale ~10² to ~10⁴ tok/s) shows TP-dominant configurations winning at short Lseq but PP-dominant configurations overtaking as Lseq grows, under both T_s = 5 s and T_s = 2 s. The mechanism is SLO-attainment decay: TP8's F_p(T_s|Lseq) collapses orders of magnitude faster, dropping toward ~10⁻² under the tight 2 s SLO, while PP8/TP2PP4 retain substantially higher attainment over a wider sequence-length range.
- Across all three perspectives the same summary holds: the optimal parallelism is jointly determined by workload composition (decode- vs prefill-heavy), SLO tightness, and context length — PP trades throughput for latency compliance; TP trades compliance for capacity.

## Authors & Credibility

Both authors — Javad Mirzaei (corresponding) and Jeebak Mitra — are in the Office of the CTO at Dell Technologies (Canada), which gives them direct access to production-grade GPU hardware (8× H200) and a plausible institutional interest in serving-system guidance for enterprise LLM deployment. The paper cites and builds on the canonical serving literature (Sarathi-Serve, DistServe, PagedAttention/vLLM, Etalon, Medha) as well as very recent 2025-2026 work on adaptive parallelism (Shift Parallelism, ReMP, FLYING SERVING, Nitsum) and context parallelism. Notably, reference [35] is the authors' own prior paper ("Characterizing parallelism strategies in LLM inference: Fundamental compute-communication trade-offs," arXiv:2610.05305), which this work extends from compute-communication characterization toward SLO-aware selection. The framing and math (dominance, feasible sets, goodput decomposition) are careful, and the experimental controls (fixing Lseq to isolate composition) are thoughtful.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The individual ingredients are not new — TTFT/ITL decomposition, goodput, and the observation that "no single parallelism is universally optimal" all appear in prior work (DistServe, Sarathi-Serve, FLYING SERVING, and the authors' own arXiv:2610.05305). The contribution is the *unification*: a clean Pareto-frontier formalization of the operating region plus the crossover condition of Eq. (14/16). It is a solid characterization paper, not a new technique or system.
- **Rigor:** The controlled design (fixed Lseq, fixed N_TP·N_PP=8) is commendable, but all quantitative results are presented only as figures — there are no result tables, no error bars, no repetition counts, and several headline magnitudes (e.g., "~9K–12K tok/s", "10⁻² attainment") are axis reads rather than reported numbers. This weakens exact reproducibility.
- **External validity — the biggest weakness:** Everything rests on *one* model (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct), *one* hardware topology (8× H200, single node, NVLink-class interconnect), *one* engine (vLLM v0.15.0), and only 4 of the many possible configurations. The TP/PP trade-off is known to be highly sensitive to interconnect (multi-host PCIe/InfiniBand would punish TP far more), model size (a 70B model needs ≥16 GPUs and different TP degrees), and scheduler internals. None of these sensitivities is probed.
- **Missing ablations/baselines:** Data parallelism, context parallelism (extensively discussed in related work but never measured), expert parallelism/MoE, and prefill-decode disaggregation are all absent from the evaluation. The SLO formulation uses only TTFT (footnote 6 acknowledges this); ITL-SLO and multi-SLO cases — precisely where the TP-ITL advantage should matter — are not treated in the selection framework.
- **Workload realism:** All workloads are synthetic uniform-length requests at fixed concurrency (closed-loop style); no trace-driven arrival processes (e.g., ShareGPT/production traces), no mixed-length populations. "Long context" tops out at ~30K tokens — far from the 128K–1M regime where CP and KV management actually bind.
- **Overclaiming risk:** The abstract's "fundamentally changes" language is acceptable for the measured regime, but the single-setup evidence means deployment guidance beyond 8×H200/14B is extrapolation. The paper is honest that it builds "a foundation for future adaptive serving systems" rather than delivering one.
- **Red flags:** None serious — no benchmark cherry-picking apparent, and the related-work coverage is unusually thorough. Main concern is scope generality, plus the risk of double-counting relative to the authors' companion paper [35].

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated formalization: the TTFT-ITL feasible set / Pareto frontier gives a precise, reusable language for "operating regions" of parallelism, and fixing Lseq to isolate prefill/decode composition from total workload scale is an elegant experimental control.
- The goodput decomposition G = λ·F with the explicit crossover inequality (F_pi/F_pj = λ_pj/λ_pi) turns an intuitive trade-off into a checkable selection rule that practitioners can apply directly.
- The long-context section identifies a genuinely useful insight: long-sequence scalability is governed by the *rate* of SLO-attainment decay, not just throughput decay — explaining *why* PP wins as contexts grow.
- Consistent story across three independent perspectives (latency frontier, SLO-goodput, context scaling) that all converge on the same PP↔TP transition, which lends the conclusions internal coherence.
- Thorough, current related-work treatment spanning latency-oriented serving, SLO-aware resource allocation, and long-context/context-parallelism literature.

## Limitations

- Single model (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct), single node (8× H200), single engine (vLLM v0.15.0), and only the four N_TP·N_PP=8 configurations — no DP, CP, EP, multi-node, or larger/smaller models; generalization to other setups is untested.
- No exact numbers, tables, variance, or repetition statistics; all magnitudes must be read off log-scale figures, and the measured quantities depend on vLLM-internal scheduling defaults.
- Synthetic uniform workloads with fixed concurrency; no open-loop arrivals, no trace-driven request-length mixtures, no burstiness — production behavior may shift the crossovers.
- TTFT-only SLO in the selection formulation; ITL or end-to-end SLOs and heterogeneous multi-SLO workloads are left unaddressed despite being the motivating scenario in the related-work discussion.
- Context scaling stops at ~30K tokens; the regime where context parallelism and KV-cache capacity actually dominate (100K+) is only discussed, not measured.
- No adaptive controller or system artifact is delivered — the "dynamic parallelism selection" the framework motivates remains future work.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 推理效率与分布式 serving 系统的研究者，这篇论文提供了一个可直接落地的并行度选择框架：何时选 TP、何时选 PP/混合，可依据工作负载组成、TTFT SLO 松紧和上下文长度按图索骥，与 cs.PF/cs.DC 方向的推理系统优化兴趣高度契合。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the reported crossovers (PP8 optimal at T_s ≤ 10 s; TP8 optimal at T_s ≥ ~25 s) shift with model size, e.g., 7B vs 32B vs 70B on 8/16 GPUs, or with multi-node interconnects where TP's collective communication is far more expensive?
- How does context parallelism (CP) change the frontier? The paper predicts CP matters beyond ~30K tokens but never measures it — does a TP+CP or PP+CP configuration dominate the pure TP/PP frontier in the 128K+ regime?
- Can the goodput-based selection rule be made an online controller: given observed (L_in, L_out, C) statistics and an SLO, how quickly and at what reconfiguration cost (cf. ReMP / FLYING SERVING) can a serving system track the moving optimum p*(w, T_s)?
- Does the PP-favorable conclusion survive prefill-decode disaggregation (DistServe-style), where prefill and decode can each pick their own parallelism instead of sharing one p = (N_TP, N_PP)?
- How do ITL-side and end-to-end SLOs change the selection? Since TP8 dominates the ITL distribution, a two-sided (TTFT, ITL) goodput formulation might favor hybrids more often than the TTFT-only analysis suggests.
- What happens under realistic, bursty trace-driven arrivals with mixed request lengths — does the fixed-concurrency Pareto picture still predict the goodput ranking observed under an open arrival process?
