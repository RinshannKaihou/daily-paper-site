> **一句话总结：** 该论文提出"推理收缩"（reasoning contraction）概念——将多步推理压缩为一步的记忆复用（思维模板、技能库、智能体记忆、缓存），并证明 ExpeL/ACE 式的成功/失败计数器是错配的变化检测器（部分失效时检测延迟按误报视界的幂次增长，甚至永不退役），进而用似然比 CUSUM + 对效用下注的 anytime-valid 证书替代之：在自建的 KQA-S TREAM 多跳基准上（Qwen3-8B 准确率 0.379 → 0.648、LLM 调用少 21%），计数器比证书多送出 7.0–13.5 倍的陈旧错误答案（代码任务上 35–73 倍），并给出安全收缩的覆盖率极限定理与 Zipf 需求下的标度律。

> **TL;DR:** The paper names "reasoning contraction" (compiling a verified multi-step derivation into a one-step memory) and studies when such memories can be trusted after facts change. It proves that the success/failure vote counters used by ExpeL- and ACE-style systems are badly tuned CUSUM change detectors — under partial staleness (failure rate 0.05→0.25) a symmetric counter's detection delay grows as A^0.373 instead of ln A, and unclipped counters may never retire a stale memory — and replaces them with a per-memory likelihood-ratio CUSUM plus an anytime-valid betting certificate on paired utility vs live reasoning. On their KQA-S TREAM benchmark (multi-hop questions over controlled KB edits, 5 open LLMs), the certificate serves 7.0–13.5× fewer stale answers than ExpeL/ACE-style counters (35–73× fewer on 521 EvalPlus code tasks), cuts bad folds from 111–595 to under 1 per stream, and on Qwen3-8B raises accuracy from 0.379 (live) to 0.648 (+26.9 pts) with 21% fewer LLM calls; a certification-load theorem (Theorem 3) shows every δ-safe memory must leave a Zipf-scaling share κs·Θ^(1−1/s) of queries to multi-step reasoning.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"推理收缩"（reasoning contraction）统一概念**：思维模板、推理/工作流记忆、技能库、行为手册、答案缓存等系统都在做同一件事——把 m≥2 步的验证推导编译成一步的记忆。论文将记忆的"信任"与"退役"两个风险决策统一建模为受变化检测与认证极限约束的序贯检验，给出"形成候选 → 影子模式认证 → 折叠（一步作答）→ 监控 → 退役 → 重启"的生命周期。
- **定理 1（计数器相图）**：证明所有截断式计分规则（ExpeL/ACE/Hebbian 类）本质上是对某个"隐含变化"的 CUSUM 检测器；对称 ±1 投票隐含检验的是 q0 对 1−q0，在部分失效（如失效率 0.05→0.25）下证据漂移方向错误，检测延迟按误报视界 A 的幂次（A^0.373）增长而非 ln A；未截断计数器（ExpeL/ACE 实际实现）以概率 0.50（ACE）/0.89（ExpeL）永不退役部分失效的记忆。推论 2 给出修复：按每条记忆自己的似然比设权重（此时一次失败约抵 7 次成功而非 1 次）。
- **定理 3（安全收缩的极限）**：定义认证负载 Θ = ν·ln(1/δ)/(p·KL)（变化率 ν、风险 δ、审计率 p、单次验证信息量 KL），证明任何 δ-安全策略最坏情况下必须把 Fπ(Θ) 比例的查询留给多步推理，重启式 SPRT 可达该极限（2,520 组设置下比值 0.945–1.025）；Zipf 需求下覆盖缺口服从标度律 κs·Θ^(1−1/s)——热门问题可收缩、冷门问题在可信任前就已变化。
- **定理 6（对赌证书）**：不估计 live 准确率（证明 plug-in SPRT 的假折叠概率可超标至 0.34），而是对"影子评估中记忆相对 live 推理的配对效用差"做 testing-by-betting（B-PAC 式逆倾向加权 e-过程），财富达到 1/δ 才折叠；对模板的第 j 次检验用水平 δj = 6δ/(π²j²) 分配总预算，每次通知后重启，大编辑时直接作废重成。
- **跨三域实证**：在自建 KQA-S TREAM（KQA Pro 知识库 + 受控事实编辑，5 个开源模型）、Wikidata 1992–2016 时间限定符自然编辑时间线和 521 个 EvalPlus 代码任务上验证：计数器比证书多送 7.0–13.5×（多跳）/ 35–73×（代码）陈旧答案，且证书在 5,779 个风险 epoch 中 0 次虚假认证（δ=0.05）。

## Key Contributions

- **The concept of reasoning contraction**: a unifying lens showing that thought templates, skill libraries, agent memories, and caches all compile a verified m≥2-step derivation into a one-step contracted memory; trust and retirement are sequential tests (Figure 1a lifecycle: form → certify in shadow mode → fold → monitor → retire → restart).
- **Theorem 1 (phase diagram of counters)**: every clipped score rule is exactly Page's CUSUM for an implied change; the symmetric ±1 rule implicitly tests failure rate q0 against 1−q0, so under partial staleness its detection delay grows as a power A^0.373 of the false-alarm horizon (failure rate 0.05→0.25) instead of ln A, and unclipped counters (actual ExpeL/ACE code) never retire a partially stale memory with probability ≥ 0.50 / 0.89. Corollary 2 fixes the learning rates: weigh each memory's failures by its own likelihood ratio (one failure ≈ 7 successes at q0=0.05→q1=0.25).
- **Theorem 3 (certification-load limit)**: with Θ = ν ln(1/δ)/(p·KL), no δ-safe policy can answer less than a share Fπ(Θ) of queries with multi-step reasoning, and a restarted SPRT achieves it (measured/limit ratio 0.945–1.025 across 2,520 settings); under Zipf demand the gap follows the scaling law κs·Θ^(1−1/s) — popular templates contract, rare ones change before they can be trusted.
- **Theorem 6 (betting certificate)**: plug-in SPRTs that estimate live accuracy are provably not level δ (false-fold probability 0.34/0.25/0.08 with 20/100/1000 calibration runs); instead, bet on the paired utility of each shadow evaluation against live reasoning on the same query (inverse-propensity-weighted e-process), fold when wealth reaches 1/δ — δ-safe whatever the live accuracy, with matching optimality (rate ln(1/δ)/(p·KLinf)).
- **Cross-domain evidence** that counters are unsafe in practice: KQA-S TREAM (multi-hop QA over controlled KB edits, 5 open LLMs), Wikidata natural temporal edits, and EvalPlus code tasks — 7.0–13.5× (multi-hop) and 35–73× (code) more stale answers than the certificate, 0 false certifications in 5,779 at-risk epochs at δ=0.05.

## Methodology

The paper formalizes a memory system as follows. A stream of queries instantiates templates (canonical derivation skeletons: KB relation paths, lemma chains, tool-call sequences); template i arrives with probability πi and costs mi ≥ 2 LLM calls if answered live. A macro is a one-step operator materialized from a verified trace (e.g., an anchor-to-answer table). With error price L, folding pays iff the macro's reliability exceeds the break-even r* = a − (m−1)/L, where a is live accuracy. Audits (fraction p of shadow/folded queries) reveal exact verifier outcomes Y ∈ {0,1}.

Three technical components replace vote counting:

1. **Retirement = LR-CUSUM (Section 3)**. A score rule Sn = max{0, Sn−1 + f(Yn)} is exactly a CUSUM for the change P0 → e^(θ0 f)P0 and no other; the symmetric ExpeL/Hebbian rule therefore tests the wrong alternative. Theorem 1 gives the three-regime phase diagram (positive drift: D ≍ ln A; zero drift: D ≍ (ln A)²; negative drift: D ≍ A^γ with γ<1 — the new regime), and Corollary 2 sets the per-memory learning rates to the Bernoulli likelihood ratio ρ(q0,q1), making each memory's detector optimal for its own q0→q1 change.
2. **Certification = betting certificate (Section 5)**. Each shadow evaluation yields a paired utility Zt = Ut/B ≥ −1 (steps saved plus L× correctness gained vs live reasoning as control). Predictable bets on inverse-propensity-weighted Zt form an e-process; folding when wealth ≥ 1/δ controls false certification at δ by Ville's inequality without any live-accuracy estimate. The j-th test for a template runs at level δj = 6δ/(π²j²) so all restarts together spend ≤ δ.
3. **Restart and invalidate (deployment)**. Certificates restart at every notice (an edit that changes a gold answer routed to the template); substantial edits additionally invalidate and re-form folded macros, with the LR-CUSUM as the safety net for unannounced edits.

Experiments replay policies on identical 200k-query Zipf streams (s=1.2, 10 seeds × 3 edit sequences, 10% random audits) with five open models (Qwen3-8B/4B, Granite-3.3-8B, Mistral-7B, OLMo-3-7B) on KQA-S TREAM (300 templates of 2–4 hops, 60 instances each, three sequences of 24 fact edits); plus a 1992–2016 Wikidata temporal-qualifier timeline and 521 EvalPlus tasks where memories go stale under requirement revisions and re-enacted Python API changes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Certified contraction improves accuracy and cost on KQA-S TREAM (Qwen3-8B, Table 3)**: ours raises accuracy from 0.379 (live reasoning) to 0.648 (+26.9 points) with 21% fewer LLM calls (2.56 vs 3.26 calls/query); compiled plans reach 0.668. Across all five models the certificate adds 14–33 accuracy points over live reasoning (Table 6).
- **Counters serve 7.0–13.5× more stale answers on every model (Table 2)**: on KQA-S TREAM with no edit notices, ExpeL-style counters serve 967 stale wrong answers per 10^4 queries on Qwen3-8B (ACE-style: 946) vs 72 for the certificate given the same information — and 0 with notices. On EvalPlus code tasks the ratio grows to 35–73× per query (38–86× in LLM-call accounting).
- **Detection delay follows the phase diagram (Figure 2)**: of 156 stale episodes, ExpeL-style counters leave 50% (ACE-style 47%) still in service at stream end vs 2% for the LR-CUSUM; restricted mean delay is 4,069 / 3,949 uses vs 67 — about 60× faster. On the real macro of Figure 1a, counters had served 2,427 stale answers by stream end vs 29 for the certificate.
- **The LR weighting matters exactly as predicted**: at failure rate 0.05→0.25, +7/−1 scores detect in 41 and 54 uses at false-alarm horizons A ≈ 1.6×10^5 and 3×10^6 — 5.7× and 13× faster than the symmetric rule; per-memory weights need 1.3–5.9× fewer uses than the best tested fixed global weight (significant on three of five models); on 217 real partial-staleness episodes the best tested weight is 5–7 vs the symmetric rule's 1.2–1.5× extra delay.
- **The certificate is empirically δ-safe (Figure 3)**: 0 false certifications in 5,779 at-risk epochs at δ=0.05 (answers scored alone), staying >10× below δ up to 0.5; bad folds (trusting a below-break-even candidate) drop from 111–595 per stream for counters to under 1 (0.0–0.6) for ours.
- **Invalidation needs the detector when notices are missed**: with 80%/50%/0% of edits announced, invalidation alone serves 125/325/810 stale answers per 10^4 queries vs 13/28/66 for the full system — 9–12× fewer. Uncertified reuse (fold-once, cache-style) serves 1,290 stale answers per 10^4 queries; certification cuts this 20×, invalidation to zero.
- **The limit law is matched**: the restarted SPRT's coverage gap lies at 0.945–1.025× the predicted Fπ(Θ) across 2,520 settings, with fitted log-log slopes within 0.008 of the Zipf exponent 1−1/s (Figure 1c).
- **On natural Wikidata edits, safety's price is visible**: counters make 149–207 bad folds per stream vs 0.1 for ours and leave 84% of stale memories in service vs 37%, yet counters are 3.5 points *more* accurate because staleness is mild (median 3.3% of a template's answers change per year) and folding still pays — an honest boundary of the method.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are Jianghong Zhou, Hao Rong, Chen Lin, and Daoan Zhang, affiliated with ByteDance and Scale AI. The paper is theory-heavy and unusually careful: two theorems with proofs in appendices, exact dynamic-programming verification of the coverage-gap law over 2,520 settings, Kaplan–Meier curves with 95% template bootstrap bands, paired bootstrap intervals for model comparisons, and a fully replayed experimental design (all policies see identical queries, edits, and audit coins; every LLM call recorded once per model with selective re-runs). Code, edit logs, prompts, and seeds are stated to be released (MIT-licensed code; edit logs under CC BY-SA 4.0). The paper discloses that AI assistants drafted proofs, code, and text and that "independent AI referee agents checked them," with the authors taking responsibility. The negative result about plug-in SPRTs and the honest Wikidata finding (counters win on accuracy under mild staleness) increase confidence in the authors' even-handedness.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The unification (templates/skills/memories/caches as one phenomenon), the negative-drift power-law regime of the CUSUM phase diagram (correcting Wu & Yang 2016, which states an infinite delay — the paper gives a counterexample), and the per-memory certification lifecycle built from anytime-valid betting (extending B-PAC's single global threshold) are genuinely new; the logarithmic regime and CUSUM machinery are classical.
- **Rigor**: Strong. Theorems state exact applicability conditions (i.i.d. outcomes before/after one change, known audit probabilities, verifier-exact outcomes), the limit is verified numerically, and safety claims are measured directly (0/5,779 false certifications). However, the KQA-S TREAM staleness is nearly total (mean q1 = 0.75–0.90), which flatters detectors; partial staleness is covered only by 217 real episodes plus the Wikidata timeline, with wide intervals acknowledged (17–81 stale episodes per model).
- **Reproducibility**: Good in principle (open models run locally, replayed streams, released code/edit logs), though the benchmark itself (300 templates, LLM-verbalized questions, synthetic edit sequences) is bespoke and built by the authors, so independent verification requires their release.
- **Weak baselines caveat**: the ExpeL/ACE baselines are idealized transplants — "we idealize their LLM-issued votes as one vote per verified use" — the real systems use LLM judges whose votes may correlate differently with correctness; the 7–13.5× headline could shift for actual ExpeL/ACE implementations. Also missing: an ablation against a *global* LR-tuned counter at the empirically best fixed k (they test fixed ratios, but the winner varies by model), and no comparison to semantic-cache staleness mechanisms beyond a re-implemented vCache-style baseline (30 stale answers/10^4 at δc=0.02, accuracy 0.377 — actually worse than live).
- **Overclaiming check**: The abstract's "35–73× more stale answers" on code is per-query accounting and per-candidate certification (no notices); the headline is carefully scoped, and the Wikidata section honestly reports the regime where counters win accuracy. One subtlety the paper itself flags: the J20 loss metric nearly hides the safety problem (counters' J20 ≈ ours because they fold 28–76% of queries one-step vs ours' 20–53%), so the practical stakes depend on whether stale answers matter more than throughput in deployment.
- **Red flags**: none serious. Scope is limited to exact-verifier domains (KB execution, unit tests) with models ≤ 8B; per-epoch safety requires notices covering every change of the paired-utility null (including changes to live reasoning itself), which real edit logs may not provide — the paper acknowledges this (Propositions 24–25) and leans on the detector as fallback.

## Strengths

- A single clean conceptual frame (contraction as a sequential test) that converts an engineering folklore problem ("when should an agent trust its memory?") into precise detection/certification theory with actionable rules.
- The diagnosis is constructive: the same math that condemns symmetric counters (Theorem 1, Remark 2) yields the fix (Corollary 2 LR weights, Theorem 6 betting certificate) — one failure ≈ 7 successes, not 1.
- The certification-load limit (Theorem 3) gives practitioners a budget formula: change rate, risk tolerance, audit rate, and verifier informativeness jointly price how much reasoning any safe system can skip, with a Zipf scaling law that explains *which* memories are worth certifying.
- Evaluation is unusually adversarial to the authors' own method: replaying identical streams for all policies, counting stale answers explicitly (a metric most memory benchmarks lack), testing missed notices, natural edit timelines, and a code domain with a second staleness source (Python API drift), and reporting the regime (mild Wikidata staleness) where the proposed method loses accuracy.
- Safety guarantees are distribution-free over live accuracy and error correlations, and empirically validated (0 false certifications in 5,779 at-risk epochs at δ=0.05).

## Limitations

- Requires an exact verifier (exact match, execution, unit test); noisy or LLM-judge verifiers — the norm for open-ended text memories — are left to future work.
- Evidence is from five open models of at most 8B parameters and three domains, one of which (KQA-S TREAM) is the authors' own benchmark with synthetic, nearly-total edits; generalization to frontier-scale models and messier edit streams is untested.
- Per-epoch δ-safety depends on change notices that cover every change of the certificate's null (including changes in live reasoning); without an edit log, only per-decision (not per-epoch) safety survives via detector-triggered restarts.
- Certification has real costs that the paper quantifies but deployers must accept: shadow-mode audits add 17% LLM calls on code; zero-utility ties (84–86% of audited pairs in LLM-call accounting) halve one-step coverage (0.61 → 0.32); under mild staleness (Wikidata) invalidation costs 1.9 accuracy points vs restart-only.
- The ExpeL/ACE baselines are idealized vote-per-audit transplants rather than the released systems end-to-end, so the headline multiples are about the retention *rule*, not the full systems.

## Relevance

论文直击 LLM 智能体记忆/技能库/缓存系统的核心安全痛点——事实变化后的"静默错误"，与智能体记忆、经验学习和推理效率方向高度契合；其"似然比加权 + anytime-valid 认证 + 通知重启"的方案和"认证负载"预算公式，对任何构建自改进智能体或缓存层的工作都有直接的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the guarantees degrade with noisy or LLM-judge verifiers, where audit outcomes are no longer exact Bernoulli draws — can a bet built for noisy paired labels keep the false-certification rate below δ in practice?
- Would frontier-scale models (with lower live error rates and hence smaller KL between healthy and stale behavior) shrink the certification load enough to make contraction near-free, or does their lower q0 make partial staleness harder to detect?
- Can the per-epoch notice requirement be relaxed with the memory's own detector as primary (restart-only), trading a quantified amount of per-epoch safety for no dependence on edit logs?
- Does the Zipf scaling law κs·Θ^(1−1/s) predict coverage on real production traffic (e.g., an actual agent's query logs), where demand may not be Zipf and edits arrive with delays?
- How does the framework extend to memories whose outputs feed downstream reasoning rather than directly answering — does a stale intermediate macro propagate differently than a stale answer cache?
