Why this matters

Turns peak-memory scheduling from heuristic guesswork into a provable, verifiable step in the PyTorch / TinyML toolchain — with an honest map of where reordering helps.

Critical edge

Exactness breaks on GPU by construction: the CUDA caching allocator violates the frame condition; under a measured guard the mean saving is 0.92%, with block-level prediction exact in only 17 of 49 configs.

Source one-liner · 一句话总结

本文证明把优化后的算子顺序交回 PyTorch Inductor 重新生成代码是不可靠的（CPU 上 54 个配置中 22 个偏离预测峰值、9 个反而更差），转而直接对已生成的程序逐语句剖析并建立精确内存模型、只置换语句顺序，在 CPU 上逐字节精确实现预测峰值且输出逐位一致；配套的“陷阱”下界证书以 O(n²) 次最小割证明 94% 的 held-out 图及全部 16 个微控制器级模型已达最优调度，并给出选择性重计算的复杂度边界（任意固定 cap 下强 NP 完全），在 ReDimNet 上以 ≤0.34% 额外 MACs 换取 5.2–6.1% 峰值内存下降。

01Methodology

  1. Profile the compiled programone warm step of the stock Inductor build — per-statement storage addresses & sizes; storages identified by allocation epoch.
  2. Build the exact “root” memory modelunits are kernel / library calls; pure allocations merge as late as possible; transients fold into a per-unit envelope s(v).
  3. Derive ordering edgesper-storage RAW / WAR / WAW from pointer const-ness (C++), parameter roles (Triton), out= args — readers of one buffer generation stay reorderable.
  4. Search exactlyDFS over ideals, bisecting between the trap lower bound and the stock peak; milliseconds for graphs up to 259 units.
  5. Emit the same statementsonly reordered — kernels, parameters and buffer reuse untouched; nothing is synthesized, only input-rebinding renames.
  6. Re-profile & certifythe realized peak must equal the prediction (Theorem 1); forward / reverse trap bounds F and R, each O(n²) min-cuts, certify optimality.

Why this works: liveness is submodular over the lattice of ideals, which is what makes min-cut machinery applicable. The six-condition contract (notably C5: allocation sequences independent of surrounding live memory) is never trusted — every emitted program is re-profiled, a harness that caught two of the authors’ own bugs. Certificates (a stuck ideal plus one flow/cut pair per frontier operation) replay without the solver.

  • Certificates: max(F,R) proves 115/122 (94%) held-out wrapper graphs optimal in median 0.004 s (max 0.25 s); each is independently replayable — the CP-SAT baseline suffers 3 timeouts.
  • Precision beats search: treating every storage access as a write erases the encoder’s 4.6% saving entirely and cuts enc–dec from 7.6% to 6.2%.
  • GPU, guarded: improves 25/50 configs (median 1.3%, up to −7.39%); keep-stock guard removes all regressions, mean −0.92%; bitwise-identical in all 27 deterministic-stock configs.
  • Cost ≈ zero: reordered/stock runtime ratio 0.997 (CPU) / 0.998 (GPU); modeling + certification add under a second per graph.
  • Recomputation limits: strongly NP-complete at every fixed cap ≥1 even at treewidth ≤2; exactly 0–1 knapsack on series–parallel graphs; useless on public tiny models (single-operator peak).

03Strengths

  • Quantified negative result: regeneration in a mainstream compiler misses its own prediction in 22/54 CPU configs and is worse than stock in 9 (worst −30.6%).
  • Proven, then mechanically verified: realization exact under an explicit six-condition contract, re-profiled on every single run.
  • Certificates change the conversation: 94% of held-out graphs (and all 16 MCU models) admit no better order — learned in under a second.
  • Actionable ablation: read/write-effect precision matters more than the search itself (4.6% → 0% when collapsed).
  • Exceptional hygiene: bitwise gradient checks, pretrained & bfloat16 replication, internal-production claims quarantined from public evidence.

04Limitations

  • No exactness on GPU: the CUDA caching allocator violates the frame condition; guarded mean saving 0.92%, block-level prediction exact in only 17/49 configs.
  • Modest absolute savings: 0% on 20/22 server inference graphs (by proof, not failure); compiled training typically 1–3%.
  • The headline 10% is fragile: DeepLabV3’s −10.0% vanishes once torchvision’s segmentation head is attached — stock becomes optimal again.
  • Recomputation dead-end on tiny models: whole-tensor recomputation provably useless where the peak is one operator; gains need tiling, not implemented.
  • Simulation, not firmware: the MCU study uses a greedy arena planner, not on-device TFLite Micro; no code release mentioned.
  • Narrow hardware: one RTX 2080 Ti, single-thread CPU; 7/122 graphs left uncertified (13% gap on RegNetY-400MF).

05Questions for further exploration

  1. Can the trap bounds be strengthened to close the remaining 7/122 gaps (notably the 13% RegNetY-400MF gap) — and is OPT / max(F,R) bounded by a constant on any natural graph class?
  2. Would searching against a simulated CUDA caching allocator restore byte-exact realization on GPU without the measured guard?
  3. How do certificates and exact realization interact with dynamic shapes, CUDA graphs, and multi-stream execution?
  4. Do ReDimNet-style “cheap value held for late reuse” patterns generalize to other streaming front-ends — and can a static analyzer detect them without profiling?
  5. What does the certificate structure look like on tile graphs, where tile shape and schedule are chosen jointly under a latency budget?
  6. Is the regeneration gap due to the forcing mechanism or codegen itself — would a compiler-native “preserve my order” API restore faith in regeneration?
