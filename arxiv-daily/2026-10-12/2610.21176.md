> **一句话总结：** 通过在 15 个预训练 LLM（Pythia/GPT-2/Qwen2.5，70M–14B）和从零训练的极简 Transformer 上做注意头定位、线性探针与因果干预，本文证明 LLM 在 N-back 任务上的工作记忆瓶颈不在"找不到目标项"（注意头在多数错误试次上仍落在目标位置，探针能近乎完美解码匹配发生在目标还是诱饵位置，中位 AUC 1.00/0.98），而在"读取被干扰"——抑制非目标项的字母身份内容使 43 个模型×负载条件中 42 个的灵敏度 d′ 上升（中位 +0.60，最高 +1.78），而锐化注意力几乎无效（中位 +0.05），支持人类工作记忆的干扰理论而非 softmax 精度理论。

> **TL;DR:** Why do LLMs fail the N-back working-memory task like humans do? Across 15 pretrained models (Pythia 70M–6.9B, GPT-2 124M–1.5B, Qwen2.5 0.5B–14B), this paper dissociates two candidate mechanisms — the "precision" account (softmax attention cannot focus on the item N back) versus the "interference" account (attention finds the target but competing items' content corrupts the decision). The verdict favors interference: on the synthetic letter N-back benchmark, task-carrying attention heads stay on the target on most miss trials (off-target on only 16%/7%/8% at N=2/3/4), linear probes decode match-vs-lure from the residual stream at median AUC 1.00 (N=2) versus the model's own decisions at 0.63, and causally, suppressing distractor letter-identity content raises sensitivity d′ in 42/43 model-by-N cells (median +0.60) while sharpening attention yields only +0.05. In Qwen2.5-14B, half-strength suppression lifts d′ from 1.60 to 3.38 at 3-back — better than the unmodified model does at 2-back. The precision limit does bind in a one-layer minimal transformer, but adding a second layer removes it. The practical upshot: for improving model working memory, separating item content from decisions beats sharpening attention.

## 核心贡献

- **正面证伪/证实两大机制的对照实验设计**：在同一套 N-back 范式下把"精度账户"（softmax 注意力无法聚焦 N 之前的项，权重溢出到相邻项）与"干扰账户"（注意力选中目标但竞争项内容污染决策）的直接对立预测（错误试次上注意力在哪、信息是否存在、哪种修复有效）在 15 个预训练 LLM（三个家族、200 倍参数跨度）、43 个模型×负载条件上逐一检验，得出一致的干扰账户结论。
- **"模型知道答案却答错"的探针证据**：在残差流上训练的线性探针能近乎完美区分匹配发生在目标位置还是诱饵位置（N=2/3 中位 AUC 1.00/0.98），而模型自身的"m 对 -"偏好仅 0.63/0.58；探针甚至能将模型自己的诱饵虚报中位 95%（N=2）、86%（N=3）正确判为诱饵——可线性解码的位置信息存在但未被决策使用，瓶颈在读出而非定位。
- **因果干预的干净分离**：抑制每个非目标项在 19 维字母身份子空间中一半的分量（逐层、逐行、保留目标项）使 43 个条件中 42 个 d′ 上升（中位 +0.60，最高 +1.78），而把 top-3 注意头温度锐化（τ=0.67）仅 +0.05；对照组齐全——随机同维子空间（−0.03）、随机 10 头（+0.00）、删目标字母（−1.54）——证明效应特异性。
- **规模规律：实现分散化、瓶颈不变**：消融 top-10 任务头在大模型中代价更小（Spearman ρ=0.74，从最小 Pythia 的约 −1.6 降到 Qwen2.5-14B 的 −0.21），而去干扰收益随规模增大（ρ=0.57）——任务电路随规模重排，读出干扰瓶颈保持不变。
- **理论闭环（极简 Transformer）**：4 个命题 + 推论证明单层无残差模型中读出只能检验注意力质量分布（精确定位但无内容响应的注意力不携带任何信号）、softmax 分散率界，以及两层 + 残差流即可让第一层拷贝 N 之前的内容、第二层直接比较两字母从而解除精度限制；720 个从零训练的模型实证验证（位置固定的注意力混合 d′≤±0.07；注意力对匹配/非匹配的内容响应变化与 d′ 相关 ρ=0.62；位置边际 δP 与 d′ 无关 ρ=−0.05）。

## Key Contributions

- **A direct adjudication between two mechanistic accounts** of the human-like working-memory signature in LLMs (declining performance with N, lure-concentrated false alarms): the precision account (softmax attention spills onto the item's neighbors) versus the interference account (target is located but misread under competing content), tested with opposite, checkable predictions in 15 pretrained LLMs from three families spanning a 200-fold size range.
- **"The model knows where the match was"**: linear probes on the residual stream separate matches from lures at median AUC 1.00 (N=2) and 0.98 (N=3) while the model's own match preference reaches only 0.63/0.58, and the probe correctly classifies a median 95%/86% of the model's own lure false alarms as lures — the position information is computed and linearly present but largely unused by the decision, locating the bottleneck in readout.
- **Causal dissociation with tight controls**: removing half of each distractor's letter-identity content (in the 19-D letter subspace, at every layer, sparing the target) improves d′ in 42 of 43 model-by-N cells (median +0.60, up to +1.78), whereas sharpening the top-3 heads (τ=0.67) moves d′ by only +0.05; random-subspace removal (−0.03), random-head ablation (+0.00), and target-letter removal (−1.54) controls establish specificity.
- **Scaling dissociates implementation from bottleneck**: ablating the ten identified heads costs less as models grow (Spearman ρ=0.74, p=0.002; from ≈−1.6 in small Pythia to −0.21 in Qwen2.5-14B — the circuit distributes), while removing interference helps more (ρ=0.57, p=0.03) — the readout-interference bottleneck is scale-invariant.
- **Exact analysis in minimal transformers**: Propositions 1–4 plus corollaries show (i) a one-layer no-residual transformer's readout is necessarily a test of attention mass, so precise location without content response carries zero signal (verified: fixed position-only attention gives d′ within ±0.07 of zero across 720 trained models); (ii) softmax dispersion does bound one layer, but sigmoid or top-k attention flattens the decline and a second layer with a residual stream removes the limit entirely.
- **A caution for representation-similarity research**: the large gap between what probes decode and what decisions use implies that comparing systems by decodable content alone can mislead; interventions on candidate content are needed to establish behavioral relevance.

## Methodology

The task is letter N-back: sequences of 24 letters drawn from 20 consonants, each containing exactly four lures (letters repeating the query at N±1 or another non-target distance). Models see a one-sentence instruction plus three solved examples, then the test sequence line by line (letter + correct answer "m"/"-"), and the model's preference is the log-probability difference between "m" and "-"; a yes/no threshold is calibrated on 200 sequences and applied unchanged to 1,000 test sequences per model and N. Performance is signal-detection sensitivity d′, with hit and false-alarm rates reported separately for lures and other non-matches.

Fifteen pretrained models are tested: Pythia (7 sizes, 70M–6.9B), GPT-2 (4 sizes, 124M–1.5B), Qwen2.5 (4 sizes, 0.5B–14B). Task-carrying heads are localized by activation patching (copying a single head's output between two sequences that differ only in the target letter; the dominant head alone recovers 16–73% of the answer difference at N=2). Error trials are then decomposed: for misses, whether attention was on the target line and whether the heads' outputs already pointed to the wrong answer; for lure false alarms, whether attention went to the lure's line. A linear probe (logistic regression) is trained on correct trials to decode match-vs-lure from the residual stream at nine relative depths, scored by AUC.

The causal interventions (all 15 models, N=2–4, recalibrated thresholds, paired bootstrap with 95% CIs over 2,000 resamples; two cells with baseline d′<0.3 excluded, leaving 43): (1) sharpening — dividing the top-3 heads' attention scores by temperature τ<1; (2) distractor suppression — removing half of each earlier item's component in the 19-dimensional letter-identity subspace (spanned by mean per-letter activations), at every layer and over each item's whole line, sparing only the target; (3) controls — random-subspace removal, top-10 head ablation (replacing outputs with calibration averages), random-head ablation, target-letter removal, and a held-out replication of Xiong et al.'s single-direction intervention.

Minimal transformers (one attention layer, no residual stream) are trained from scratch on N-back at each N with 50 seeds (300 models, plus 420 architectural/data variants). Their attention scores decompose into a content term and a position term; Propositions 2–4 and Corollaries 1–2 formalize what such a model can and cannot compute, and the trained models are tested against these predictions (attention-mass substitution, content-term removal, position-margin δ_P analysis, normalization swaps to sigmoid/top-k/scalable-softmax, and adding a second layer).

## Results & Highlights

- **Human-like load effect across all 15 models**: median d′ falls from 2.57 at N=1 (range 1.25–4.28) to 0.91 at N=4 and 0.52 at N=6 on the letter N-back task; the largest model, Qwen2.5-14B, reaches 4.28 at N=1 but only 1.15 at N=6 — scale helps when the task is easy, much less when it is hard. At N=2, models false-alarm to a median 79% of lures at N±1 versus 4% of other non-matches.
- **Attention stays on target during misses**: task heads are off-target on only 16%, 7% and 8% of missed matches at N=2, 3, 4; on 41–57% of misses the heads' outputs already encoded the wrong answer despite on-target attention, and on 27–43% the correct answer was present in the heads but lost in later layers. On lure false alarms, 91–96% show a neighboring (lure) line receiving more attention — expected for content-based search, where the query's letter literally is at the lure.
- **Probes vastly outperform the model's own decisions on match-vs-lure**: model preference AUC is 0.63 (N=2) / 0.58 (N=3) versus probe AUC 1.00 / 0.98 at the best depth; the probe classifies a median 95% (N=2) and 86% (N=3) of the model's lure false alarms as lures. In Qwen2.5-14B the model's AUC is 0.81/0.64, the probe's 1.00/0.997, and the probe catches 99.8%/95% of its lure false alarms.
- **Removing interference beats sharpening attention (Fig. 4a)**: half-strength distractor-content suppression raises d′ in 42 of 43 model-by-N cells, median +0.60 (up to +1.78; median +0.44 at N=4), dropping false alarms from ~0.2–0.3 to ~0.1 while sparing hit rates. Sharpening the top-3 heads changes d′ by a median +0.05 (τ=0.67; range −0.30 to +0.30). Controls: random subspace −0.03, random heads +0.00, top-10 head ablation −0.93 (42/43 cells negative), removing the target's letter −1.54. The Xiong et al. single-direction intervention replicated with held-out configuration gives only +0.11 — about a fifth of the subspace-suppression gain.
- **Scaling dissociation**: head ablation cost shrinks with size (Spearman ρ=0.74, p=0.002; from ≈−1.6 in the smallest Pythia models to −0.21 in Qwen2.5-14B) while distractor-suppression benefit grows (ρ=0.57, p=0.03). In Qwen2.5-14B specifically, half-strength suppression raises d′ from 2.42→2.88 at N=2, 1.60→3.38 at N=3, and 1.29→2.05 at N=4 (all bootstrap CIs ≥0.40 above zero) — with suppression, the model does 3-back better than it does 2-back unaided.
- **Minimal transformers localize the precision limit**: a one-layer no-residual model declines from d′ 2.67 to 2.15 over N=1–6, but replacing softmax with sigmoid or top-k attention flattens the decline (scalable softmax does not), and two layers plus a residual stream remove it entirely. Across 300 such models, performance tracks how much target attention differs between match and non-match trials (Spearman ρ=0.62), while the position margin δ_P predicts neither d′ (ρ=−0.05) nor lure false alarms (ρ=−0.03) — and forcing fixed position-only attention collapses d′ to within ±0.07 of zero.
- **Robustness checks**: the best-fitting k-back rule differs from the instructed N in only 14 of 90 cases (all N≥4 small models), ruling out task drift; a chat format in Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B–7B shows the same decline (d′ 1.8–2.0 at N=1, 0.4–0.7 at N=6); and a selective state-space model (Mamba-style) trained from scratch also declines with N, so the limit is not softmax-specific.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author work by Dongyu Gong (Yale University), building directly on their prior publications: the ChatGPT working-memory empirical study (AAAI 2024) and the workshop paper proposing the precision account for minimal transformers (Gong & Zhang, 2024) — notably, the author is here partly overturning their own earlier account with stronger evidence, which is a credibility signal. The paper engages carefully with the competing interference account (Xiong et al., COLM 2026) and replicates that group's intervention under held-out configuration. Methodological hygiene is high: paired bootstrap 95% CIs (2,000 resamples), Wilcoxon tests, Spearman correlations with p-values, calibration/test sequence separation, and five distinct control interventions. This is an arXiv preprint (v1, 15 Oct 2026), not yet peer-reviewed, but the statistical and experimental rigor is well above typical preprint standards for interpretability work.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: High. The precision-vs-interference dissociation had not been tested against each other; the error-trial-resolved attention analysis, the match-vs-lure probe, and the exact treatment of minimal transformers together form a genuinely new kind of evidence. The four propositions connecting attention mass, softmax dispersion, and depth are a real theoretical contribution, not decoration.
- **Rigor**: Strong overall — pre-registered-style calibration on 200 sequences applied unchanged to 1,000 test sequences, bootstrap CIs on every intervention, and the full battery of controls (random subspace, random heads, target-letter removal). One soft spot: Proposition 1 is a post-hoc linear model of the readout, not derived from trained weights, and the authors admit it "does not explain why half-strength removal beats full removal" — the optimal suppression strength is left uncharacterized, and full removal over-suppresses in Qwen2.5-7B without a mechanistic story.
- **Reproducibility**: Good in principle (Pythia, GPT-2, Qwen2.5 are all public; the N-back stimulus generation is described precisely enough to re-implement), but no code release is mentioned in the main text, and details like the activation-patching head-selection criterion live in appendices that were only partially checkable here.
- **Significance**: The dissociation "decodable ≠ used" is an important caution for the representation-similarity literature (CKA/RSA), and the practical hint — separating item content from decisions beats sharpening attention — is actionable for long-context recall work. However, the leap from synthetic letter N-back to "working memory in LLMs" broadly should not be overstated: this is one 24-item, 20-consonant paradigm with answers shown correctly line by line.
- **Red flags**: (1) The headline distractor-suppression intervention is an oracle — it requires already knowing which position is the target, so it is a causal test of mechanism, not a deployable fix; the paper says so, but the framing "improves performance" could mislead a skimmer. (2) The mechanistic claims are capped at 14B parameters, and the authors themselves note the head-level account may not transfer beyond, since identified heads matter less as models grow. (3) In the GPT-2 family at N=3, part of the probe-decodable information is lost by the last layer (probe AUC 0.84–0.89), slightly complicating the clean "information is present but unused" story. (4) GPT-2 Large had to be excluded from the attention-locating analysis because its top heads attend to none of the three relevant lines — a family-specific anomaly that gets one sentence.

## Strengths

- Clean two-account, opposite-predictions experimental logic executed across 15 models, 3 families, and a 200-fold size range — the cross-family consistency is the strongest form of the "same mechanism" claim available without training new models.
- Combines three independent evidence types: correlational (error-resolved attention, probes), causal (five interventions with matched controls), and theoretical (propositions verified on 720 trained minimal models).
- Unusual honesty: the author explicitly revises their own earlier precision account, quantifies the competitor's intervention under fair held-out conditions (+0.11 vs +0.60), and states what the theory does not explain.
- The finding that attention sharpness is already near-optimal (flattening all heads beats sharpening in 19 of 30 cells) is a concrete, counterintuitive, practically useful negative result for anyone hoping to fix retrieval by temperature tuning.

## Limitations

- Single synthetic task (letter N-back, 24 items, line-by-line format with correct previous answers shown); no evidence yet that the readout-interference bottleneck governs real-language working-memory demands like multi-step reasoning or coreference over long documents.
- All mechanistic analyses stop at 14B parameters — far below frontier scale — and the head-distribution trend suggests circuit-level claims may not extrapolate.
- The letter-identity subspace (19-D) and the target-sparing suppression are experiment-internal constructs; no method is offered for a model to implement the fix on its own.
- The linear-probe argument equates "linearly decodable" with "available to a downstream readout," which is an assumption; amnesic-probing-style causal confirmation of the probe directions is not performed.
- Proposition 1's failure to explain the half-removal > full-removal ordering leaves the dose-response of interference suppression unmodeled.

## Relevance

这项工作用因果干预与线性探针把"模型内部信息存在但决策不用"这一可解释性核心问题做了教科书级演示，并给出与人类工作记忆干扰理论的机制对应——对关注 LLM 认知能力边界、注意力机制分析与上下文信息读取瓶颈（长上下文召回失败）的研究都直接相关。其"抑制干扰内容优于锐化注意力"的结论对改进模型上下文利用也有实践启发。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the target-sparing distractor suppression be approximated without oracle knowledge of the target — e.g., by suppressing all items' letter content except the position the model's own attention most strongly selects, and would that still improve d′?
- Does the readout-interference bottleneck extend beyond 14B (30B–70B+ models) where the task circuit is fully distributed, and does the letter-subspace structure remain as low-dimensional?
- Does the same locating-versus-reading dissociation appear in realistic working-memory loads — long-document question answering, multi-constraint instruction following, or coreference at distance — where "lures" are semantically rather than lexically defined?
- Why does half-strength interference removal beat full removal, and is the optimal suppression strength predictable from the relative magnitudes of the lure bias β and the distractor variance σ_D² in the Proposition 1 model?
- Since a selective state-space model also declines with N, what is the recurrent architecture's equivalent of the readout bottleneck, and does content suppression help it too?
- Could the gap between probe AUC and behavioral AUC be closed by fine-tuning only a downstream readout (the final linear map), turning "decodable but unused" information into decisions without touching the circuit?
