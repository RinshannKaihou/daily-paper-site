01 — Methodology

A cross-stack systems study in four instrumented layers, each isolating its own lever.

  1. ROCm library optimizationOrigami replaces offline GEMM autotuning with a closed-form model — per-level cache-hit rates, issue-rate bound, wave quantization — predicting tile configs in microseconds; integrated into hipBLASLt + tritonBLAS. RCCL collectives co-designed against fabric simulation (LL64 protocol, P2P batching).
  2. Microarchitecture-aware kernel tuningFive AMD-specific Triton techniques (XCD-aware swizzle, waves_per_eu, MFMA nonkdim=16, num_stages≈2, GeLU fast math); the multi-chiplet atomic bottleneck fixed with shared-memory tree reduction; principles encoded as agentic skills on KernelEvolve with automatic numerical-equivalence rejection.
  3. Model–system co-designExploit 288GB HBM (1.6× B200): larger batches, activation checkpointing off, embeddings moved back into HBM, FSDP ZeRO-3 → HSDP.
  4. Production evaluationTen DLRMs (8–128 GPUs, ~0.1–3.6T params), equal GPU count and matched batch vs B200, with per-kernel-category trace attribution, SDC containment, and power telemetry.

Kernel-level wins

  • Origami GEMM selection: 93% of exhaustive autotuning, zero warmup, µs per decision
  • Jagged flash attention bwd: 2.3× (32→14 ms) — from 2× slower to 15–20% faster than B200, +25% end-to-end
  • HSTU fwd 3.22× (45.0→144.8 TFLOP/s); GDPA fwd 2.5× (58.4→145.9)
  • Bounds-check kernel: tree reduction 4.26× at 10% violations → 1.75× faster than B200 (naive atomics were >10× slower)
  • RCCL: from 2× slower than NCCL to within 10–15%; AllToAll latency −40% at 128 GPUs
  • Co-design levers: batch 2/4/6× → +26/46/51%; no-AC 1.3B GPT 96.4 vs 71.5 ex/s; total 1.5× vs ported baseline, +0.11% NE at 20 layers

Where MI350X still trails

  • GEMM latency 1.3–1.7× worse (TF32 emulated as 3 BF16 GEMMs; hipBLAS immaturity)
  • Transformer Inductor kernels still ~30% slower
  • ~20% of production kernels fail the agentic optimization workflow

03 — Strengths

  • Production scale no academic evaluation matches: 3.6T-param DLRMs, tens of thousands of GPUs, SDC quarantine & observability as first-class citizens
  • Origami is genuinely elegant: µs-cost closed-form model, chiplet-hierarchy-aware, kills the offline-autotuning database problem — shipped in hipBLASLt and tritonBLAS
  • Honest step-by-step attribution of every optimization — usable directly as a practitioner's manual
  • Agentic tuning section is concrete: equivalence-gated validation, skills port to MTIA (~10× speedups)
  • Volunteers its most damaging facts (GEMM gap, TF32 emulation) rather than burying them

04 — Limitations

  • GEMM is the structural weakness: 1.3–1.7× higher latency than B200
  • Atomic-contention fixes are per-kernel engineering; multi-chiplet atomics >10× slower under high contention before rewriting
  • No LLM-training analysis despite >2,000-GPU LLM jobs already running — precisely the regime where B200's bandwidth edge should dominate
  • Un-reproducible headline: anonymized models A–J, no public benchmark anchor (no MLPerf), no variance, no TCO numbers
  • Power asymmetry (1000W vs 750W) plus different cooling paths muddies the "same-generation flagship" framing

05 — Questions for further exploration

  1. Does DLRM parity extrapolate to LLM pretraining, where per-GPU scale-up bandwidth (538 vs 900 GB/s) matters most?
  2. Can Origami close 93% → ~100% with lightweight online profiling, and handle fully dynamic shapes at runtime?
  3. What TCO model actually backs Meta's provisioning decision — and does it survive B200 at its 1000W liquid-cooled design point?
  4. Does sustained operation near the 1.36×-TBP excursion envelope accelerate hardware wear-out or raise SDC rates?
  5. Why do ~20% of kernels fail the agentic workflow — are failures concentrated in patterns that motivate new CDNA 5 ISA support?
  6. Do the co-design levers (batch scaling, AC removal, ZeRO-3→HSDP) compose additively on LLMs, or does bandwidth contention bound the gains?
