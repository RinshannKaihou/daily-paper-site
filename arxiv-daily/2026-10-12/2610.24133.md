> **一句话总结：** 借用黑洞"无毛定理"的类比，本文严格刻画了自回归 Transformer 对 prompt 的遗忘：在共享同一段续写文本的条件下，两个不同 prompt 造成的隐状态偏移（prompt offset）近似按幂律 t^−α 衰减，作者证明对深度为 L、prompt 长为 P 的 pre-LN softmax Transformer 该偏移的上界为 O(P(log t)^{L−1}/t)，机制是注意力份额随上下文增长的几何稀释加上"接力"（relay，续写 token 传递从 prompt 读到的信息）只能增加对数因子；测量发现训练使遗忘变快（Pythia/Qwen 训练后深层 α≈0.55–0.7，而未训练时仅 0.2–0.5），训练在头 10 亿 token 内（步 128–512 之间）就抑制了 relay，且早于 attention sink 的形成；用 log t logits 偏置补偿稀释可以去除甚至逆转遗忘（必要性），而轻微 s=0.5 的补偿仅 +2.6% 困惑度就把 Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct 的"全法语回答"比例从 31% 提到 94%，但等均值常数偏置效果相同（形状不重要），且代价是指令复述率从 19% 升至 56–69%。

> **TL;DR:** This single-author theory+measurement paper asks how fast a decoder-only transformer forgets its prompt: with two prompts sharing the same teacher-forced continuation (WikiText-103 passages, 100 real-corpus pairs per axis from WikiText/MRPC/WMT14), the hidden-state "prompt offset" decays as a power law t^−α, and the paper proves it is O(P(log t)^{L−1}/t) for finite-depth pre-LN softmax transformers (bounded logits + non-degenerate pre-norm activations), with an exactly solvable linear-relay model showing the bound is tight. Empirically, training *accelerates* forgetting — on real-corpus pairs, Pythia-160m's layer-11 α rises from 0.36 (untrained) to 0.66 (trained), vs the untrained-relay baseline of near-zero decay — because training suppresses relay within the first ~1B tokens (layer-11 α jumps 0.41→0.68 between steps 128 and 512), before attention sinks form. A causal logit-bias that cancels dilution reverses the decay (per-head compensation drives α negative at +140–376% perplexity), while a mild s=0.5 prompt boost trades α_L11 0.65→0.48 for +2.6% perplexity and lifts Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct's fully-French adherence from 31% to 94% — but a constant boost of equal mean strength does just as well, so the log t shape itself earns nothing, and instruction echoing rises from 19% to 56–69%.

## 核心贡献

- **定义并测量"prompt 印记"（imprint）**：在共享续写文本（teacher-forcing）的条件下，把两个不同 prompt 造成的隐状态差定义为 prompt offset，把其在续写间共享的部分定义为信号 S(t)，并证明噪声指数 β≈0，因此一个指数 α 即可概括 prompt 被遗忘的速率；在 Pythia-160m/410m/1b 与 Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B 上测量，训练后深层 α≈0.55–0.7，未训练时 α≈0.2–0.5 且随深度递减。
- **条件无毛定理（Theorem 1）**：对有限深度 L、prompt 长度 P 的 pre-LN softmax 因果注意力 Transformer（logit 有界，如 rotary 位置编码；pre-norm 激活远离零），offset 满足 δ_L(t) ≤ C_L·P(L+ln t)^{L−1}/t；推论给出在给定已生成文本的条件下，两个 prompt 的下一 token 分布的 TV 距离以同样速率消失。
- **机制分解与严格性闭环**：把注意力写入分解为"直接路径"（query 读 prompt）与"接力路径"（query 读已携带 prompt 信息的续写 token），该分解在每一层、每个训练阶段都能在 0.06 内复现测得的 α；可精确求解的线性 relay 模型（均匀注意力 + 每层增益）证明对数幂 (log t)^{l−1} 不可改进，并正确预言未训练网络局部指数随 t 上升、随深度下降。
- **训练动力学定序**：遗忘在步 128–512（约 0.3–10 亿 token）之间出现，源于 relay 开始泄漏，早于 attention sink 的形成（sink 质量在步 1000 前处处 ≤0.003），也早于第一层变为近偏置；由此排除了 sink 是遗忘初始原因的解释。
- **因果验证与实用权衡**：给 prompt 加 γ·log((q+1)/P) 的 logit 偏置以抵消稀释，可在初始化与训练后去除甚至逆转遗忘（说明稀释是必要的），未训练网络与可解模型说明稀释也是充分的；轻微补偿（s=0.5）以 +2.6% 困惑度显著提升指令遵循，但等均值常数偏置同样有效，且伴随指令复述等副作用——重要的只是"更多注意力"，不是补偿的形状。

## Key Contributions

- **Defines and measures the prompt imprint.** The "prompt offset" — the hidden-state difference between two runs that share a teacher-forced continuation but differ in the prompt — decays approximately as t^−α; the cross-continuation noise exponent β is ≈0 (except untrained Qwen), so a single exponent α summarizes forgetting. Measured across Pythia-160m/410m/1b (8 training checkpoints for 160m) and Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B: trained deep layers α≈0.55–0.7, untrained 0.2–0.5 decreasing with depth — training makes models forget *faster*.
- **A conditional no-hair theorem (Theorem 1).** For finite-depth pre-LN softmax decoders with full causal attention, bounded attention logits (e.g., rotary encodings) and non-degenerate pre-norm activations, the offset is O(P(log t)^{L−1}/t) for prompt length P and depth L. Corollary 1: conditional on the text generated so far, the TV distance between the two prompts' next-token distributions vanishes at the same rate — the "no-hair" statement.
- **A tight, exactly solvable mechanism.** The attention write is decomposed into a direct path (queries reading the prompt) and a relay path (queries reading continuation tokens that already absorbed prompt information); the decomposition reproduces the measured α within 0.06 at every layer and checkpoint. A linear relay model (uniform attention, per-layer gains) is solved exactly via symmetric polynomials, showing the (log t)^{l−1} factor cannot be lowered and matching untrained networks (layer-1 fit recovers P̂=28.8 vs actual mean prompt length 25.7 tokens, R²=0.9999).
- **Training-dynamics ordering.** Following the same 400 prompt pairs through Pythia-160m training, forgetting sets in between steps 128 and 512 (layer-11 α: 0.41→0.52→0.68) as relay starts to leak, while layer 1 stays in the untrained regime until step 1000 and attention-sink mass stays ≤0.003 until step 1000 (0.089 only at the end) — forgetting precedes both recency bias and sink formation.
- **Causal test and a costed application.** Adding a γ·log((q+1)/P) logit bias on the prompt cancels dilution: at initialization and step 512 it removes forgetting (α→0.05–0.13 per-head), and after training per-head compensation overshoots to α<0 — but at +140–376% perplexity. A mild s=0.5 uniform boost lowers α_L11 from 0.65 to 0.48 for +2.6% perplexity and raises instruction adherence sharply, yet a constant boost with the same mean bias does equally well, with instruction echoing rising from 19% to 56–69% — extra attention helps, the log t shape does not.

## Methodology

The core measurement device is **teacher-forcing under a shared continuation**: both prompts are followed by the *same* human-written continuation (WikiText-103 passages), so any hidden-state difference is attributable to the prompt alone. The **prompt offset** Δ_l(t) = h_l(x′∘c,t) − h_l(x∘c,t) is window-averaged over 16-token windows at octave-spaced times (te = 16…1024 for Pythia, 2048 for Qwen; windows labelled by centre, which removes an up-to-0.19 local exponent bias of end-labelling). The **signal** S_l(t) is the part of the offset common to all continuations, estimated with an unbiased pairwise estimator (8 continuations per real-corpus pair, 16 per hand-written scene) that corrects the sampling bias of the naive plug-in; the **noise** N_l(t) is the spread of hidden states across continuations. Exponents α, β come from least-squares slopes on log-log axes with 95% bootstrap intervals, plus local exponents between consecutive octaves to detect non-power-law structure.

Two prompt sets are used: **20 hand-written scenes** with five variants each (one-word detail change, paraphrase, genre rewrite, translation) × 16 WikiText-103 continuations, and **100 real-corpus pairs per axis** (WikiText one-word swaps, MRPC paraphrases, WMT14 En–Fr translations, unrelated WikiText sentences) — testing the prediction that the *size* of the prompt difference sets the offset's amplitude, not its decay rate. A shared prepended `<|endoftext|>` token controls for prompt-specific attention sinks where needed.

The **theory** covers sequential and parallel pre-LN blocks, LayerNorm/RMSNorm, grouped-query attention and rotary/no positional encodings. Three lemmas drive the proof: bounded logits (B), Lipschitz normalization away from zero (Assumption 1, checked empirically: r/√d = 0.11–0.49 on the measured sequences, 0.012 in Qwen's first post-attention norm), and the **dilution lemma** — a query at distance i pays at most P·e^{2B}/i attention to the prompt. A one-layer recursion (same-position offset + relay average + direct O(P/i) term) unrolls to Theorem 1. The **linear relay model** (uniform attention, per-layer gains g_l) is solved exactly with symmetric polynomials, giving parameter-free layer-1/layer-2 predictions that untrained Pythia satisfies and untrained Qwen does not. The **path decomposition** splits each attention write into direct (prompt keys) and relay (continuation keys) contributions exactly; the **causal intervention** adds γ·log((q+1)/P) to prompt logits, uniformly or per-head-calibrated. Behavioural tests use free generation: a hashed-unigram logistic probe for what survives in text, and single-/multi-turn instruction-adherence tests on Qwen2.5-Instruct models with a placebo (same-strength boost on the continuation start). All computations ran in float32 on a single Apple-silicon laptop; the measurement toolkit is promised for release.

## Results & Highlights

- **Training accelerates forgetting nearly two-fold in deep layers.** On real-corpus WikiText-103 pairs (100 pairs/axis, unbiased signal), Pythia-160m layer-11 α rises from 0.36 at initialization to 0.66 after training (143k steps); Pythia-1b layer-15 goes 0.33 → 0.58; Qwen2.5-0.5B layer-23 goes 0.18 → 0.58. In layer 1, trained models sit at the uniform-attention dilution rate α ≈ 0.87–1.05 (vs 0.73–0.82 untrained), so beyond the first layers trained models forget at α≈0.55–0.7 while untrained ones decay at α≈0.2–0.5 — the counterintuitive headline.
- **The exponent is content-agnostic; only the amplitude depends on the difference.** Two completely unrelated WikiText sentences are forgotten at the same rate as a one-word substitution (Pythia-160m layer 11: α = 0.70 vs 0.69 trained, 0.35 vs 0.37 untrained). After training the axes separate only mildly by axis type, with paraphrase forgotten most slowly (0.52 in Pythia-1b's last layer).
- **Relay carries the long tail at initialization and leaks early in training.** The exact direct/relay decomposition reproduces the measured α within 0.06 at every layer and checkpoint. At initialization, relay accounts for 73–97% of the late offset (rising with depth) with α_relay = 0.01–0.18, while the direct path decays at α_direct ≈ 0.68–0.70 in every layer (the layer-1 uniform-head value); after training, relay share drops to 0.41–0.56. Attention weights alone are a misleading proxy throughout — they miss relay early and are dominated by sinks late.
- **Forgetting appears between training steps 128 and 512, before attention sinks.** On the same 400 pairs, layer-11 α jumps 0.41 (step 128) → 0.52 (256) → 0.68 (512); layer 1 keeps its untrained value 0.72–0.73 until step 1000 and reaches 0.96 only at step 4000. Attention mass on token 0 stays ≤0.003 in every measured layer up to step 1000 and reaches 0.089 (layer 7) only at the end of training — the onset of forgetting precedes sink formation by several billion tokens.
- **Cancelling dilution removes or reverses forgetting.** Per-head compensation drives α to 0.05–0.13 at steps 0 and 512, and to negative values (−0.12/−0.10/−0.08 in layers 1–3, i.e., the prompt's influence *grows*) after training — at +140–376% perplexity, confirming dilution is necessary. A practical mild boost (s=0.5 uniform) trades α_L11 0.65 → 0.48 for +2.6% perplexity; at matched retention (α_L11 ≈ 0.5) the prompt boost costs +2.6% vs +10.3% for the equal-strength placebo.
- **Instruction adherence improves, but the log t shape earns nothing.** With system prompt "answer only in French" (2048 tokens), Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct's fully-French share rises 31% → 94% at s=0.5, and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct 84% → 94–100%; in 8-turn conversations, s=0.25 lifts conversations-fully-in-French from 78% to 91% — but a constant boost with the same mean bias achieves 88%, and the paper's own verdict is that with only 32 conversations per condition these are trends, not significant effects. Side effect: in forced-length single-turn generation the share of outputs whose second half discusses the instruction rises from 19% to 56–69% (1.5B).
- **What survives is carried by the generated text, not the hidden state.** In free generation, a unigram-histogram probe decodes the prompt's *language* at 0.84 accuracy (Pythia-160m) and 0.81 (Qwen2.5-0.5B) at t=512 (null 95th percentile 0.58–0.63), while detail/paraphrase/genre fall to chance — and the teacher-forced offset for the language axis still decays (α = 0.54–0.65 in trained Pythia layers 3–11), so language persists through the exterior channel of the trajectory itself, exactly the no-hair analogy.
- **The solvable model fits untrained Pythia almost exactly.** Fitting the exact linear-relay solution layer by layer, layer 1 alone recovers P̂ = 28.8 against a true mean prompt length of 25.7 tokens (R² = 0.9999); all fifteen Pythia-1b curves are reproduced with R² ≥ 0.992, with gains g_l ∝ l^−1.4 in layers 4–15 and a symmetry check (ĝ1 = ĝ2 = 0.66) robust across six optimizer restarts.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is authored solely by Shuyang Xiang, listed as an independent researcher (no institutional affiliation), October 2026, cs.AI, 37 pages. It is a preprint without evident peer review. Credibility signals are nevertheless unusually strong for a single-author work: the empirical methodology is scrupulous (unbiased estimator for late-time signal, centre-labelled windows to avoid a documented up-to-0.19 exponent bias, placebo-controlled interventions, shared-first-token controls for sinks, bootstrap intervals reported per table, and three explicit alternative explanations tested and excluded for the Qwen late slowdown); the theory is stated with explicit scope and honest caveats (Remark 1 concedes the constant C_L is huge and the bound is "informative only once t ≫ P e^{Δ}", which for trained deep layers lies beyond any measured window); and negative results are reported plainly (attention weights mislead; the log t compensation shape confers no advantage). All experiments ran on one Apple-silicon laptop in float32, consistent with the modest model sizes (≤1.5B). Prior work is cited accurately and positioned precisely (instruction drift Li et al. 2024, attention sinks Xiao et al. 2024, over-squashing Barbero et al. 2024, Scalable-Softmax Nakanishi 2025, generation limits Meyer et al. 2026).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: high.** The prompt-offset-under-shared-continuation quantity, the conditional no-hair framing, and the direct/relay decomposition with a matching exactly solvable model form a coherent package I have not seen elsewhere; the training-dynamics ordering (forgetting precedes sinks) is a genuinely new empirical finding. The black-hole analogy is decorative but the math (dilution lemma + relay recursion) stands on its own.
- **Rigor: good but the theorem is weaker than the abstract sounds.** Theorem 1 bounds only the *asymptotic shape*: the constant grows exponentially with depth, involves e^{2B}, and — by the paper's own Remark 1 — after training the logit spread Δ saturates float32 in deep layers (e^Δ > 10^7), so the bound "is not informative at any accessible t" for trained models. The fitted exponents are therefore never compared with the theorem, only with the linear relay model, which is a *different*, stronger claim than "the theory predicts the measurements". Assumption 1 (non-degenerate pre-norms) is verified empirically with a margin of only r/√d = 0.012 in Qwen's first post-attention block, and only on the measured sequences.
- **Reproducibility: promising but unverified.** The toolkit and exact prompt pairs are "will be released" — not yet available at the time of writing. The hand-written scenes are fully printed in Appendix C and all constants of the proofs are given, which is above-average transparency; but a single-author, laptop-scale study with no public code at v1 means the headline numbers cannot yet be independently checked.
- **Significance: real but bounded.** Scale stops at Pythia-1b and Qwen2.5-1.5B — pretraining-only models, mostly ≤1.5B parameters, English prompts, WikiText continuations, one temperature. The scope also excludes much of the modern long-context machinery (ALiBi, sliding-window attention, Scalable-Softmax, linear attention are all outside Lemma 1(a)'s bounded-logit condition — the paper correctly notes its analysis predicts Scalable-Softmax *retains* prompt information longer, but does not test this). Extrapolating α ≈ 0.55–0.7 to instruction-tuned frontier models is plausible but unestablished; the paper itself flags instruction tuning as an open question.
- **Red flags and soft spots.** (a) The behavioural "application" is largely a negative result dressed as a positive one: the equal-mean constant boost matches the log t compensation on every metric (multi-turn French share 0.94 vs 0.96, fully-French conversations 88% vs 91%), samples are tiny (32 conversations/condition; the Section 8 probe uses only 3 scenes), and the 19%→56–69% instruction-echo side effect is severe. (b) The trained deep-layer power law is corpus-dependent: α drops by 0.09–0.18 (Pythia-1b, layers 8–15) when continuations are shuffled sentences, so "forgetting rate is a property of the model" holds only on coherent text. (c) Teacher-forcing is a measurement device, not the deployment failure mode; the bridge from α to real instruction drift is argued by mechanism, not measured beyond the small Qwen-Instruct tests. (d) The untrained Qwen first blocks violate the linear-relay predictions (fitted k = 2.9, 4.5 vs predicted 1), and the late-time Qwen slowdown is explained by an untied-to-rotary-frequencies oscillation — loose ends the author honestly leaves open.

## Strengths

- A single quantitative law (power-law offset decay with one exponent, since β≈0) unifies theory, measurement, training dynamics, and intervention — rare coherence between proof and experiment.
- The exactly solvable linear relay model closes the theory-experiment loop tightly (layer-1 fit recovers prompt length P̂=28.8 vs 25.7 true; R²≥0.992 across fifteen layers) and proves the logarithmic factors in the bound are real, not proof artefacts.
- The causal intervention (dilution compensation, with per-head calibration and a placebo control) elevates the dilution story from correlation to necessity, and untrained networks supply the sufficiency direction.
- Methodological hygiene well above the interpretability norm: unbiased late-time estimator, centre-labelled octave windows with a quantified labelling bias, sink-controlled prompt pairs, three excluded alternative explanations for the Qwen anomaly, and confidence intervals on essentially every number.
- Genuinely useful negative results: attention weights are an unreliable proxy for prompt-information flow, and the log t shape of compensation is worth no more than a constant boost — both stated without hedging.

## Limitations

- Theorem 1 constrains only the asymptotic shape; its constant is exponentially large in depth and, for trained networks with sinks (e^Δ > 10^7), the bound bites only beyond any measured window — the theorem does not predict the fitted exponents.
- Assumption 1 is empirical, not derived, with a thin margin (0.012) in Qwen's first block; scope excludes ALiBi, sliding-window attention, Scalable-Softmax, and non-softmax attention.
- Model scale stops at 1.5B parameters, pretrained-only; no instruction-tuned, RLHF'd, or frontier-scale confirmation. All prompts English (French/Chinese variants only in preliminary runs); one sampling temperature; continuations exclusively WikiText.
- The trained decay's shape depends on continuation structure (shuffled sentences shift deep-layer α by up to 0.18), and trained Qwen shows an unexplained late-time oscillation around the 1/t law whose period is not tied to rotary frequencies.
- Behavioural tests are small (32 conversations per condition — trends, not significance; 3 scenes for the exterior probe), and the useful compensation knob carries a large instruction-echo side effect (19%→56–69%) plus perplexity costs; per-head calibration is impractical (+140–376% PPL).
- The toolkit and prompt pairs were not yet public at v1, and the paper is single-author without peer review.

## Relevance

如果研究方向涵盖 Transformer 理论、注意力机制与长上下文行为，本文直接相关：它给出 prompt 信息随生成长度衰减的精确幂律刻画与可解模型，解释了系统指令漂移/多轮对话中约束失效的几何根源（注意力稀释 + relay 被训练抑制），并提示基于 logit 偏置的缓解手段及其代价——对理解与改善 LLM 长对话中的指令遵循有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the fitted exponents (trained deep layers α≈0.55–0.7) persist at 7B–70B scale, and does the depth-flat profile survive in models with much greater depth or grouped-query/MLA variants?
- How does instruction tuning and RLHF change relay suppression — does a model trained on dialogue forget its system prompt at a measurably different α than a pretrained-only model?
- The analysis predicts Scalable-Softmax (s·log n logit scaling) retains prompt information longer because it breaks the bounded-logit condition — can this be measured directly with the same prompt-offset toolkit, and at what perplexity cost?
- Can the layer-1 attention oscillation around 1/t in trained Qwen (log-slopes −0.94 to −1.38) be tied quantitatively to rotary frequency bases, and does it appear in RoPE models with extended/interpolated contexts?
- Is there a training objective or architectural change (beyond inference-time logit bias) that preserves relay or slows dilution without the instruction-echo pathology and perplexity cost observed here?
- Does the conditional no-hair picture extend to KV-cache compression: since the prompt's interior imprint decays as t^−α, can early prompt tokens' caches be safely evicted or quantized more aggressively than the t^−1 law implies?
