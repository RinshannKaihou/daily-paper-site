> **一句话总结：** 本文指出医学分割模型量化后 Dice 几乎不变但不确定性图、校准与失效检测会严重退化（如 MosMedPlus/ResNet50 上 INT4 使不确定性图相关性从 1.000 跌至 0.119），并提出 SGAV-MP——一种"打分引导 + 整模型可靠性验证"的混合精度后训练量化框架，在 2D 达 3.59×–7.8×、3D 达 4.73×–7.82× 压缩的同时保持 FP32 级 Dice 与高不确定性一致性。

> **TL;DR:** The paper formalizes why post-training quantization can keep Dice stable while destroying calibration and uncertainty maps (a 2-class logit-perturbation proposition: the argmax label survives but entropy rises as the logit gap shrinks), and proposes SGAV-MP, a greedy mixed-precision PTQ method that only accepts a layer's precision drop after the FULL compressed model passes aggregate reliability constraints (Dice, ECE, uncertainty-map rank correlation, AURC) on a calibration set. On PROMISE12/ResNet50 it achieves 7.29x compression with uncertainty correlation 0.9697 vs 0.3240 for uniform INT4 at similar compression (7.91x); on MosMedPlus/ResNet50 it keeps Dice 0.7848 (FP32: 0.7850) with rho_U 0.9392 at 5.94x, where INT4 collapses to Dice 0.6507 / rho_U 0.1192. TensorRT deployment runs (RTX 6000 Ada) are reported separately: SGAV-MP's runtime configuration is honest but modest (1.58x on AMOS22/UNet3D vs MedPTQ's 3.90x) since its mixed INT4/INT2 policies are storage-compression only.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示并形式化"可靠性鸿沟"**：通过一个两类别 logit 扰动的命题证明——量化缩小 logit 间距后 argmax 硬标签可以不变，而预测熵（不确定性）必然改变；即 Dice 稳定 ≠ 不确定性稳定。实证上，INT4 在 PROMISE12/EfficientNet-B0 上 Dice 仅从 0.8892 降至 0.882，但不确定性图相关性从 1.000 崩塌到 0.428，ECE 从 0.0982 恶化到 0.112。
- **提出 SGAV-MP 框架**：将可靠性保持压缩形式化为约束优化（Dice ≥ FP32−ε_D、ECE ≤ FP32+ε_C、不确定性图秩相关 ρ_U ≥ τ_U、AURC ≤ FP32+ε_R），用"压缩收益 / (可靠性风险 + 连接感知风险)"的优先分数做贪心候选排序，但**每一步接受前都对完整压缩模型在校准集上做整体验证**，不依赖层间可靠性可加性假设。
- **全面的可靠性评估协议**：在 2D（PROMISE12 前列腺 MRI、MosMedPlus 新冠肺部 CT；5 种编码器骨干）与 3D（AMOS22、BraTS、BTCV 上的 SegResNet）上，同时报告分割（Dice）、压缩（压缩比）、校准（ECE/Brier）、不确定性保持（熵图秩相关 ρ_U）与失效检测（AURC、error-AUROC）指标，与 INT8/INT4、MCKP、Hessian Greedy、AdaRound、EfficientQ2/4 等基线对比。
- **区分"存储压缩"与"运行时部署"**：明确混合 INT4/INT2 策略只是存储压缩策略，另行报告 TensorRT 后端实测（延迟、峰值显存、上下文显存），避免把存储压缩比冒充运行时加速。

## Key Contributions

- **Identify and formalize the reliability gap**: a two-class logit proposition (quantized logits (a−δ, δ) with 0<δ<a/2 preserve the argmax label yet increase entropy) proves hard-mask stability and uncertainty stability are mathematically distinct requirements; empirically, INT4 on MosMedPlus/ResNet50 keeps Brier at 0.730 vs FP32's 0.412 and drops uncertainty-map correlation from 1.000 to 0.119.
- **SGAV-MP method**: reliability-constrained mixed-precision PTQ. Candidate actions (layer, precision ∈ {FP32, FP16, INT8, INT4, INT2}) are ranked by a benefit-risk score — compression benefit U = N_ℓ(32 − r_b) parameters saved, empirical single-layer reliability impact, and a connection-aware risk term (normalized graph degree + skip/multi-scale-fusion flag) — then Pareto-filtered, but an action is accepted only after the complete candidate policy passes all four aggregate constraints (Dice, ECE, rho_U, AURC) on a held-out calibration split.
- **Cross-cutting evaluation**: five 2D encoder backbones (ResNet18/50, MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0, DenseNet121) on PROMISE12 and MosMedPlus, plus 3D SegResNet on AMOS22, BraTS, and BTCV, against uniform-precision, PTQ (AdaRound), constrained-assignment (MCKP), sensitivity-guided (Hessian Greedy), and medical PTQ (EfficientQ2/4) baselines, with uncertainty-error AUROC as an extra diagnostic.
- **Honest deployment accounting**: backend-supported TensorRT FP32/FP16/INT8 measurements (RTX 6000 Ada, TensorRT 10.3) reported separately from storage compression; mixed INT4/INT2 policies claimed only as storage compression unless the backend supports them.

## Methodology

SGAV-MP (Score-Guided Aggregate-Validated Mixed Precision) is a post-training framework that takes an already-trained FP32 segmenter, a labeled calibration set disjoint from the test split, and a compression objective, and outputs a per-layer precision policy. The problem is posed as maximizing compression benefit F(A) = J(q_∅) − J(q_A) subject to aggregate reliability damage R(A) ≤ τ_rel, where the constraints require Dice(f_q) ≥ Dice(f_FP) − ε_D, ECE(f_q) ≤ ECE(f_FP) + ε_C, rho(U_q, U_FP) ≥ τ_U (rank correlation between predictive-entropy maps), and AURC(f_q) ≤ AURC(f_FP) + ε_R.

Because the search space is exponential in the number of quantizable layers, SGAV-MP proceeds greedily: (1) evaluate the FP32 reference metrics on the calibration set; (2) for every candidate action a = (layer ℓ, precision b), estimate benefit U (parameter-count based when model size is the objective: N_ℓ(32 − r_b)) and risk I, in the main implementation via the empirical four-term reliability degradation D_rel of a policy where only layer ℓ is quantized (Hessian/Fisher and activation-perturbation terms are supported but disabled with η_H = η_A = 0 to save compute); (3) remove Pareto-dominated actions in the utility-risk plane; (4) iteratively propose the highest-scoring action S = U / (I + λ_M·M + ε), where M is a connection-aware architectural risk combining normalized graph degree and a skip/multi-scale-fusion indicator, evaluate the FULL candidate mixed-precision model on the calibration set, and accept it only if all four aggregate constraints hold — otherwise the action is rejected permanently. Crucially, layer-wise scores only rank proposals; acceptance is decided by full-model evaluation, so the method does not assume reliability impacts are additive. By default, action scoring uses 10% of the calibration data (sweeps to 100% in the supplement). The paper additionally separates storage compression from runtime: mixed INT4/INT2 policies are storage-only claims, and separate TensorRT FP32/FP16/INT8 deployment measurements (latency, peak/context memory, compression ratio) are provided for representative 2D and 3D settings.

## Results & Highlights

- **PROMISE12 (prostate MRI) / ResNet50, Table 2**: SGAV-MP reaches Dice 0.8858 (FP32: 0.8857) and uncertainty-map correlation rho_U 0.9697 at 7.29x compression, whereas uniform INT4 at similar compression (7.91x) collapses rho_U to 0.3240 and EfficientQ4 reaches rho_U 0.7282 at 7.91x — i.e., +0.646 rho_U over INT4 and +0.242 over EfficientQ4 at comparable compression ratios.
- **MosMedPlus (COVID-19 lung CT) / ResNet50, Table 3**: SGAV-MP keeps Dice 0.7848 (FP32: 0.7850, delta −0.0002) with rho_U 0.9392 at 5.94x compression, while uniform INT4 drops Dice to 0.6507 (−0.134 vs FP32), ECE from 0.2064 to 0.3653, Brier from 0.4123 to 0.7304, and rho_U to 0.1192; EfficientQ4's rho_U is 0.5303 on the same setting.
- **3D SegResNet, Table 4**: on AMOS22 SGAV-MP achieves 7.72x compression with Dice 0.7717 (FP32: 0.7778) and rho_U 0.9857, and actually IMPROVES calibration vs FP32 (ECE 0.0478 vs 0.0527; Brier 0.1679 vs 0.1755); on BraTS it gets 7.82x compression with Dice 0.7293 (FP32: 0.7334) and rho_U 0.9755, where uniform INT4 falls to Dice 0.6458 and rho_U 0.5348.
- **Compression–uncertainty Pareto frontier (Figure 1)**: on MosMedPlus/ResNet50, varying the uncertainty tolerance yields policies from 4.89x compression with rho_U 0.970 up to 5.91–5.92x with rho_U ≈ 0.94–0.95; on DenseNet121, 4.86x with rho_U 0.968 to 6.23–6.25x with rho_U ≈ 0.947.
- **Ablation (Table 5, PROMISE12/ResNet50)**: full SGAV-MP achieves 7.29x / rho_U 0.9697 vs 7.17x / 0.9545 for the UQ-only score and 7.16x / 0.9500 without the connection-aware term; Dice+UQ+risk (no ECE term) matches 7.29x compression but at lower rho_U 0.9531 — the uncertainty term drives most of the compression gain while the full score gives the best reliability point.
- **Cross-dataset calibration (Table 6)**: calibrated on PROMISE12 and evaluated on MosMedPlus, Dice stays at 0.779–0.791, rho_U at 0.942–0.968, and compression at 4.06–5.73x across all five backbones — limited overfitting to the calibration split.
- **Backend deployment (Tables 7–8, TensorRT on RTX 6000 Ada)**: on AMOS22/UNet3D, MedPTQ gives the largest runtime compression and lowest latency (3.90x, 0.843 ms) while TensorRT-compatible SGAV-MP gives 1.58x / 1.580 ms with the best rho_U 0.9972 and Dice 0.7274; in 2D, SGAV-MP deployment policies are simultaneously smaller-context and faster than INT8 on MosMedPlus/EfficientNet-B0 (0.791 ms and 34.72 MB context vs 0.903 ms / 37.13 MB) with higher rho_U (0.9833 vs 0.9600) at 2.25x vs 3.81x weight compression.
- **Search cost**: accepted 3D SGAV-MP policies need build times of ~8,875 s (AMOS22) to ~28,252.6 s (BraTS) and 3.4–6.0 GB peak GPU memory; 2D ResNet50 builds take 2,450 s / ~19.9 GB (PROMISE12).

## Authors & Credibility

All four authors — Sagar Kumar, Shailja Sharma, Tirthajit Baruah, and Punit Rathore — are affiliated with the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, a top-tier Indian research institution; Punit Rathore (the senior author) works on efficient/deployable deep learning for medical imaging. The paper is an arXiv eess.IV preprint (v1, 18 Oct 2026; cross-listed to cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG), apparently not yet peer-reviewed. Credibility signals are strong: the evaluation spans five backbones and five datasets across 2D MRI/CT and 3D volumes; baseline implementations are run from released code (EfficientQ2/4) under a shared calibration/test protocol; negative or inconvenient results (MedPTQ beating SGAV-MP on runtime latency; INT4 remaining a strong baseline on AMOS22) are reported rather than hidden; and calibration/test splits are kept strictly disjoint with final metrics on held-out test data. The mathematical core (Proposition 1) is simple but correct and directly motivates the design.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core observation — hard-label accuracy can survive quantization while softmax-derived uncertainty degrades — follows almost immediately from how softmax responds to logit-gap shrinkage, and is plausibly known informally in the UQ community; the paper's real contribution is formalizing it (Prop. 1) and operationalizing a compression method around full-model reliability validation rather than per-layer sensitivity proxies. The greedy-validate loop is engineering-sound but algorithmically conservative (rejection is permanent — no backtracking when a later action combination would have fit).
- **Rigor**: The constraints are enforced on the calibration set, and the authors honestly note this "does not guarantee reliability on unseen test data"; the cross-dataset table (PROMISE12→MosMedPlus) is a useful but mild robustness check, and no true OOD/failure-mode analysis is included despite the motivation invoking OOD anatomy and domain shift. The default risk estimator is purely empirical (η_H = η_A = 0), so the Hessian/activation generalization (Eq. 21) is presented but never actually exercised in the experiments.
- **Reproducibility**: Datasets are public and splits are stated (e.g., PROMISE12 1031/147/295, BraTS 1134/162/325), but key thresholds (ε_D, ε_C, ε_R, τ_U, α weights) are not enumerated in the main text, many results (five-backbone grids, BTCV, calibration-size sweeps) are deferred to a supplementary not included here, and build costs are substantial (BraTS ≈ 7.9 hours per policy), which will deter reproduction. MedPTQ is discussed as the closest medical PTQ baseline but not directly compared in 3D SegResNet because of code/export/backend incompatibilities — the TensorRT check swaps to UNet3D instead, weakening the deployability story for the architecture actually studied.
- **Significance**: Preserving an FP32 model's uncertainty structure is only as good as that structure — on MosMedPlus the FP32 reference itself is poorly calibrated (ECE 0.2064, Brier 0.4123), so rho_U ≈ 0.94 partially amounts to faithfully reproducing a miscalibrated model. Meanwhile Table 3 shows EfficientQ2 achieves LOWER ECE (0.1569) while collapsing Dice to 0.6765 and AURC to 0.02215 — a caution the paper could have used to argue for multi-metric reporting more forcefully.
- **Red flags**: The headline "7.29x compression" is a storage metric, not deployment speedup — the TensorRT tables show the runtime-legal SGAV-MP policy compressing only 1.58x on AMOS22 (vs MedPTQ's 3.90x) and 2.25–2.53x in 2D (below uniform INT8's 3.8–4.1x); the abstract's framing is honest about this but a skimming reader could over-claim. BTCV has only 6 test volumes, and the 2D models are ImageNet-style encoders rather than the U-Net/nnU-Net architectures that actually dominate medical segmentation.

## Strengths

- Identifies a genuine, safety-relevant evaluation blind spot (uncertainty/calibration under compression) and backs it with both a clean theoretical proposition and striking empirical collapses (rho_U 1.000 → 0.119 on MosMedPlus INT4).
- The aggregate full-model validation gate is the right design: it sidesteps the false assumption that layer-wise reliability damages add up, which is exactly where Hessian Greedy and score-only baselines fail (rho_U 0.4547 and 0.2549 at ~7.9x).
- Comprehensive, multi-axis evaluation: 2D + 3D, MRI + CT, five backbones, five datasets, six reliability/efficiency metric families, plus a real TensorRT hardware study rather than synthetic FLOP counts.
- Refreshing honesty: separates storage compression from runtime speedup, reports MedPTQ winning on latency, shows INT4 remaining competitive on AMOS22 (rho_U 0.9121), and reports build-time/memory costs of its own search.
- The ablation cleanly attributes gains (UQ term drives compression; connection term adds rho_U 0.9500 → 0.9697 at equal 7.16→7.29x), and the Pareto analysis reframes precision choice as a tunable reliability–compression frontier.

## Limitations

- No automatic runtime speedup: mixed INT4/INT2 policies are storage-only on current backends, and the TensorRT-compatible configuration often compresses LESS than uniform INT8 (2.25x vs 3.81x on MosMedPlus weights) — its deployment edge is confined to lower context memory and slightly better latency in 2D.
- High search cost: per-policy build times from ~510 s (MosMedPlus 2D) to ~28,252 s (BraTS 3D) with up to ~19.9 GB peak GPU memory in 2D, plus one single-layer evaluation per candidate action during scoring.
- Depends on the FP32 model's reliability quality and on labeled calibration data representative of deployment; the paper acknowledges sensitivity to calibration representativeness but only tests one cross-dataset shift.
- Scope gaps acknowledged by the authors: no foundation-style segmenters (VISTA3D, MedSAM), no mixed INT4/INT2 backend execution, and MedPTQ/SegResNet deployment paths unverified; the paper itself adds that broader modalities are untested.
- Several quantitative details (threshold values, full five-backbone tables, BTCV, calibration-size sweeps, repeated-run stability) live only in a supplementary material not present in the main PDF.

## Relevance

对关注高效部署与模型压缩的研究方向而言，这篇论文直接回应了"压缩后模型除了精度还该保住什么"的问题：它把不确定性量化、校准与失效检测纳入后训练量化的验收标准，与医疗影像等安全敏感场景的可信 AI 部署高度契合。其"整体验证优先于层间启发式"的思想对任何混合精度压缩流水线都有借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would SGAV-MP policies transfer under real distribution shift (different scanners, sites, or contrast protocols), where the FP32 reference's own calibration may drift — can the constraints be made target-aware rather than calibration-set-fixed?
- Can the permanent-rejection greedy loop be improved with backtracking or lookahead, and would enabling the Hessian/Fisher risk terms (currently η_H = η_A = 0) cut the number of expensive full-model validations enough to reduce the ~7.9-hour BraTS build?
- How does the reliability-preservation argument extend to foundation-style segmenters (VISTA3D, MedSAM-style promptable models) and to other uncertainty sources (MC dropout, deep ensembles, probabilistic U-Net) whose maps SGAV-MP could be asked to preserve instead of predictive entropy?
- Since rho_U only measures agreement with the FP32 model, could the framework instead target an absolute reliability budget (e.g., recalibration post-quantization) so a poorly calibrated FP32 teacher does not get faithfully reproduced?
- What are realistic INT4 kernel paths (e.g., weight-only INT4 with FP16 compute, or upcoming TensorRT support) under which the 7x-class storage policies could translate into genuine latency wins, and how do the reliability constraints behave at that boundary?
