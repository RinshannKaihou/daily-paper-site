Why this matters

Diffusion-training defaults went unexamined; these recipes transfer to any Diffusion Transformer run, and the variance-reduction lens extends to LLM pretraining.

Critical edge

All evidence comes from one 675M model on class-conditional ImageNet at half the standard batch — “large diffusion models” is extrapolated, with no text-to-image or 1B+ validation.

01

Methodology

one lens, three strategies

Constant-step SGD keeps a non-vanishing noise floor — E‖∇f‖² ≤ 2(f₀−f*)/(ηT) + Lησ² — and EMA, LR decay, and Schedule-Free are three different ways to cut it:

EMA

average the noisy trajectory

Stationary variance shrinks, but the constant-step bias survives — Lemma 2.2 builds a strongly convex case where the EMA limit is provably suboptimal.

LR decay

shrink step and noise together

Vanishing stationarity (Lemma 2.3) — at the price of freezing further optimization progress.

Schedule-Free

average while stepping

O(t⁻¹) on the quadratic plus a nonconvex stationarity guarantee (Lemma 2.4): evaluate the Polyak-style average while the primal keeps large steps.

  • Arena — SiT-XL/2 (~675M) + REPA, flow matching, ImageNet-1K 256², global batch 128, up to 800k steps; model/data/objective/eval frozen, only the optimizer changes.
  • Decay probe — independent 40k-step cosine branches (1e-4 → 1e-6) off five matched late checkpoints of one shared trajectory.
  • Evaluation — three levels: checkpoint trace variance · fixed-eval loss (same data/noise/classes/timesteps) · FID/sFID/IS over 3 sampling seeds.
  • LR-scale control — plain AdamW at the same 4e-4 diverges (NaNs after ~218.7k steps); Schedule-Free stays stable.
  • Theory provenance — lemmas largely assembled from known results (Ghadimi & Lan; Bottou et al.; Chae & Kim); the original piece is the EMA-bias counterexample.

04

Strengths

why to trust it
  • A falsifiable central question — “is EMA uniquely suited?” — tested in a genuinely modern recipe (SiT + REPA + flow matching), not a toy DDPM.
  • Same-trajectory decay branches, three-seed FID means, and a fixed-evaluation protocol strip sampling noise out of the comparison.
  • The AdamW@4e-4 NaN control directly kills the “it’s just a bigger learning rate” confound.
  • Transfer experiments probe the claim’s boundary — including the instructive negative result on guidance-side Schedule-Free.
  • Unusually candid reporting: timing-sensitive decay, non-uniform sFID, and mixed cross-reward results are all self-flagged.
05

Limitations

where it stops
  • One architecture (~675M), one dataset (class-conditional ImageNet-256), one training seed per recipe; no text-to-image or billion-parameter validation.
  • Global batch 128 is half the standard REPA recipe (256) — a disclosed compute constraint; absolute FIDs are softer than published REPA numbers.
  • Learning rates are optimizer-specific (1e-4 vs 4e-4) with no intermediate sweep — the attribution rests on two points per curve.
  • Decay is tested only as late 40k branches; full-trajectory WSD/cosine schedules are never compared.
  • Schedule-Free carries unaccounted costs: extra optimizer state to maintain and checkpoint, plus retuning in new regimes.
  • Most comparisons use the noisier FID-5K (FID-50K only at 800k); ReFL at 20× LR degrades orthogonal perceptual metrics with no guardrail provided.
06

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the advantage persist at production scale — 1B+ DiTs, text-to-image latent training, batch 512–2048?
  2. Would a full-trajectory WSD/cosine decay beat Schedule-Free, or is the timing sensitivity an artifact of the branch protocol?
  3. Can Schedule-Free and a brief late decay + EMA be composed to dominate either alone?
  4. Is there a principled way to set the Schedule-Free base LR without a sweep — e.g. from its observed stability margin?
  5. In ReFL, can a KL or perceptual regularizer recover the 20× cross-reward collapse while keeping the on-reward gain?
  6. Does the EMA-bias lemma predict anything measurable in LLM pretraining — decay standard, EMA rare — the mirror image of the diffusion convention?
