Complete or Sparse: A Tale of Two Identifiabilities

Junze Zhou, David Klindt — Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory · 2026-10-12

nonlinear-ICA identifiability mechanistic-interpretability sparse-coding representation-learning

Key metric — weight decay is the lever

10/10

runs at axis-aligned MCC ≥ 0.90 · vs 3/10 without

Adding weight decay 0.03 lifts axis-alignment success from 3/10 to 10/10 of runs (median MCC 0.987 vs 0.878) and cuts time-to-threshold from 640,000 to 133,500 updates.

Why this matters

Gives interpretability research its missing theory: when monosemantic neurons emerge — and why sparse autoencoders recover features from superposition.

Critical edge

The bridge to real systems is the weakest link: the only biological test (macaque IT) is non-significant in both directions (r = 0.158, p = 0.0986), and all controlled evidence is toy-scale.

§1The regime map — four theorems, one table

The paper's own summary (Sec. 4.4): capacity ratio m/n′ + causal direction of the data-generating process ⇒ which representation regime is optimal.

RegimeConditionsProvably optimal representationThm
Complete — m ≥ n′ = rank(W*) either DGP Linear mixture h(z) = Az + ν(z) — polysemanticity is optimal, not an artifact 4.1
Undercomplete — m < n′ either DGP Generically nonlinear; linear encoders provably suboptimal — claimed first identifiability result for undercomplete nonlinear ICA 4.2
Generative (Y → Z), exact capacity m = n = n′ non-negative activation + energy constraint (∥h∥² / weight decay) Scaled permutation h(z) = DPσz — one neuron per latent ("grandmother cell", monosemanticity) 4.3
Discriminative (Z → Y), k-sparse latents spark(A*) > 2k Polysemantic but SAE-recoverable: N(A) = N(A*), latents recovered by sparse decoding — why SAEs work 4.4

Cross-system prediction — "more disentangled units should have counterparts in other systems" — tested via MIS–MMU correlations on 4 ImageNet vision models, 3 LLMs, and 2 macaque IT cortices.

§2Methodology, distilled

Theory first; purpose-built synthetic and real-activation validation second.

z ∈ Rn─g→ x─f→ h ∈ Rm─U→ ŷ

Encoder trained with cross-entropy against the true posterior softmax(W*z); the effective latent dimension n′ = rank(W*) is the pivot.

  • Two causal DGPs. Generative Y→Z (class-conditioned exponential latents, MNIST-style) vs discriminative Z→Y (labels only see the compressed measurement A*z) — same loss, different identifiability regimes.
  • Proof spine. A Log-Ratio Identity turns zero KL into logit-difference equalities; a linearity-from-inner-products lemma forces the encoder affine; non-negative-orthant isometry arguments pin scaled permutations; a spark argument yields compressed-sensing recovery.
  • Validation. n = 5–16 latents, MLP encoders, 10 seeds each; plus real-activation MIS–MMU correlations (ImageNet models, GPT-2 / Pythia-70M / TinyLlama, 58 + 110 macaque IT neurons).

§4Strengths

  • One economical framework (cross-entropy + m/n′ + causal direction) provably unifies nonlinear ICA, compressed sensing, and the superposition debate — with a legible regime table.
  • Every theorem paired with a purpose-built experiment that discriminates the new prediction from prior work's.
  • Unusually honest ablations: exactly which assumptions are load-bearing (non-negative support, exponential-like marginals) vs technical conveniences.
  • Actionable finding: weight decay 0.03 → 10/10 axis alignment, ~5× faster convergence.
  • Code, seeds, and hyperparameters fully released; intuition boxes after each theorem.

§5Limitations

  • All guarantees are population-level under a global-optimum assumption; SGD reaches the axis-aligned solution slowly and seed-dependently.
  • Real-data validation is correlational, uses the authors' own MIS proxy, and the only biological evidence is non-significant at α = 0.05 in both directions.
  • Generative result needs exact capacity + non-negativity + constant conditional entropy + energy constraint — a conjunction real systems are unlikely to satisfy.
  • Toy scale only (5–16 latents, ≤ 100 classes); no test where n′ is estimated, none on real SAE training pipelines.
  • Thm 4.2 gives no bound on surviving linear structure; spark-2k on the unknown A* is unverifiable outside simulation.

§6Questions for further exploration

  1. Relax exact capacity (m = n = n′) to overcomplete bases — continuous degradation or collapse to superposition?
  2. Does spark(A) hold for real SAE training on frontier-LLM activations — and can spark violations diagnose untrustworthy reconstructions?
  3. Interaction with heavy-tailed label priors and self-supervised (contrastive) objectives rather than supervised cross-entropy?
  4. Does weight decay measurably raise the fraction of monosemantic units at LLM-pretraining scale?
  5. A finite-sample Theorem 4.2, bounding minimum nonlinearity by the gap n′ − m?
  6. Approximately k-sparse latents: does dictionary-learning MCC degrade gracefully; can approximate-spark replace exact spark-2k?
