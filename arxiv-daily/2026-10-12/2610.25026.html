One Head Away from Language Confusion: Single Attention Heads Across Model Families ↗
Jiwon Jeong, Anjelo Jann Laroza, Chaewoon Yoon, Arjun Pillai, et al. (7 authors)  ·  2026-10-12  ·  12 checkpoints · 7 model families · 1B–7B
mechanistic-interpretability language-confusion attention-heads multilingual-LLM ablation-study
Key metric
51.5%

of 2,500 FLORES-200 continuations leave the prompt's language when head L27H13 (Qwen2.5-3B) is zeroed — the best other head in the same layer moves 1.4%. Range across the 10 affected models: 8.0–51.5%.

Why this matters

A causal, cross-family mechanism for language confusion: tiny single-head interventions flip behavior at scale while loss barely moves — direct evidence of how fragile multilingual LLMs are inside.

Critical edge

Heads were chosen by their largest observed ablation effect — favoring upward noise — and FLORES swap rates were measured on the same prompts used to estimate the patched language means; post-hoc exclusion criteria removed disconfirming cases.

  • Subjects: 12 instruction-tuned checkpoints across Qwen2.5/Qwen3, Gemma-3, OLMo-2/3, Llama-3.2, SmolLM3 (1B–7B), plus base checkpoints and OLMo-2's Base→SFT→DPO→final sequence.
  • Probes: 2,500 FLORES-200 continuations (En/Fr/De/Es/It, greedy, ≤40 tokens) and LCB chat (800 non-English monolingual + 200 English controls + 1,196 crosslingual).
  • Interventions: zero & mean ablation of the head-output slice, name-token attention masking, value patching with same-/other-language means vs a random-direction control of matched norm, and full output replacement with language-conditioned means.
  • Damage guardrail: candidate heads must keep ΔNLL ≤ 1 and ≥90% English retention — separating language failure from general generation damage. Exact McNemar tests; several control readings fixed in code before running.

  • Decoupled from loss: Qwen2.5-7B L19H1 costs only +0.026 nats yet flips 20% of continuations (441/500 departures → Chinese).
  • Values encode language: patching the language-name values with another language fails 90 replies (vs 4–8 for matched random directions, p < 10⁻¹⁰).
  • Steering asymmetry: injecting a donor language redirects 84.4–99.4% of replies (5 of 7 models); restoring the requested language beats controls only in the two Gemma models (0.118 → 0.560).
  • Post-training amplifies: OLMo-2-1B L12H8 effect grows 0.005 (Base) → 0.028 (SFT) → 0.071 (DPO) → 0.080 (final).

Strengths 优点

  • Breadth, not a case study — every head of 12 checkpoints screened across seven families.
  • Rare methodological hygiene — pre-specified exact McNemar tests, controls fixed in code before running, post-hoc decisions openly disclosed.
  • The random-direction control cleanly separates "the name's values encode language identity" from "any perturbation this size breaks the head."
  • C→W metric + paired bootstrap CIs make effect sizes interpretable without crediting accidental fixes.
  • Quantified specificity — +0.026 nats vs 20% language flips is exactly the dissociation evidence the field needs.

Limitations 局限

  • Latin-script only (5 European languages); Qwen2.5-1.5B already fails Japanese/Russian in the 14-language extension.
  • 1B–7B only; effects smaller in most ≥4B models, and the size-vs-family confound is unresolved.
  • One head per model, never sets — no measurement of redundancy or hydra-effect self-repair.
  • All confusion is intervention-induced; natural confusion may route through different circuits.
  • Mechanism rests on two models, small n (51 passing replies for Gemma-3-1B); bf16 nondeterminism undermines the two largest models' small effects.
  • Thin repair story — requested-language steering works only where baselines are floor-level.

Questions for further exploration 待探索问题

  1. Does the single-head pattern survive non-Latin scripts (Chinese, Arabic, Korean) in the FLORES screen?
  2. Do naturally occurring language-confusion failures route through these same heads — testable by patching the head back to clean on production confusions?
  3. How much function is distributed? Ablating pairs/triples of top-k heads would quantify redundancy and self-repair.
  4. Does instruction-tuning amplification (OLMo-2: 0.005 → 0.080) come from chat templates teaching heads to read language names, or preference data rewarding adherence?
  5. Since zeroing L19H1 costs only +0.026 nats, could head-level regularization during post-training reduce confusion without quality loss?
  6. Do "explicit request" heads (L8H25, L13H19) and "implicit continuation" heads form one circuit — and where do they read the reply language when no language is named?
