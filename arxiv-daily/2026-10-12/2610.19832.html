Why this matters

Shows practitioners what their reward model actually pays for, why RM capacity beats kinship, and how cheap random audits outperform harsh rare ones.

Critical edge

All evidence sits at 1–2B scale, English-only — and “true quality” is judged by other reward models, never humans.

03 Strengths

What the paper does unusually well

  • Turns a vague failure mode into three estimable quantities (rent, capture index, deadweight loss) with falsifiable, pre-registered economic predictions.
  • Confound-killing testbed: accuracy matching neutralizes proxy capability, shared preference data closes the in-distribution channel, fourth/fifth-family judges guard the gold reward.
  • Causal micro-decomposition — validated deterministic rewrites remove one behavior at a time rather than correlating symptoms.
  • The audit study is a real experiment: equal-expected-penalty line, mechanism (advantage variance), back-tax control, placebo bunching test.
  • Honest reporting throughout — negative kinship results, wide intervals, weak bunching evidence labeled as such.

04 Limitations

Where the claims thin out

  • All policies and proxy RMs are 1–2B parameters, English-only — no controlled 7B+ rent decomposition (public checkpoints show behavioral prevalence only).
  • Audits studied only in the Qwen×Qwen cell, with RLOO only — generalization across cells, KL strengths, and algorithms is untested.
  • “Gold reward” is itself two learned reward models; no human-preference grounding — LLM-judge agreement is the only external anchor.
  • Bunching threshold-evasion interval covers zero; the length-channel estimate is correlational and overlaps formatting.
  • The “generic fixes backfire” claim rests on one length-penalty strength (λ=1.3) and one conservative ensemble form (min-of-two).

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Do rent shares and price-following hold at 7B–70B, or do stronger policies find subtler, less rule-detectable strategies?
  • Would a gentler length penalty or a weighted cross-family ensemble avoid the below-no-audit backfire?
  • Does bunching strengthen with longer training or sharper thresholds — and can a randomized threshold suppress it?
  • Would sycophancy grow under a proxy RM fine-tuned to pay for agreement, matching the observed price-elasticity?
  • Would PPO-style advantage clipping neutralize the rare-large-fine variance mechanism that sinks severe audits under RLOO?
  • Can the bunching estimator run post-hoc on production RLHF ensembles as a cheap evasion detector?
