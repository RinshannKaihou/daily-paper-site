> **一句话总结：** PN-Mix 通过对 RoPE 通道对做 query–key pair-norm（范数乘积）显著性分析，在 prefill 阶段一次性选出重要通道对并赋予 3-bit、其余 1-bit（平均仅 2.26 bit），在 GSM8K/HumanEval/AIME 等基准上以更少比特追平甚至超过 Kitty、ChanMix 等 2.32–2.39 bit 的混合精度基线，并可稳定工作到 1.5 bit。

> **TL;DR:** PN-Mix is a mixed-precision key-cache quantizer for RoPE-based decoder LLMs that ranks RoPE channel pairs by query–key pair-norm products (an attention-logit-sensitivity proxy computed once at prefill) and gives salient pairs 3 bits vs 1 bit for the rest. At 2.26 average key bits it matches or beats Kitty (2.39 bits) and ChanMix (2.32 bits) — e.g., 77.21 vs Kitty's 74.43 average accuracy on Qwen3-8B over GSM8K/MATH-Algebra/HumanEval/GPQA-Diamond (+2.78 pts, within 0.64 of FP16), and 48.33 vs Kitty's 42.00 on AIME24 with R1-Llama-8B (+6.33, equal to FP16) — while degrading gracefully to 1.5 bits where baselines collapse, and enabling 32K-token generation at batch 32 on one A100-40GB (8x FP16's feasible length, 2.37x FP16's peak throughput).

## 核心贡献

- **Query 感知的 RoPE 通道对显著性准则**：把 post-RoPE key 量化引起的 attention logit 扰动在通道对层面分解，证明 logit 误差由 query–key pair-norm 乘积 ∥q_i^t∥·∥k_i^s∥ 加权，从而提出 σ_{h,i} = E[∥q∥]·E[∥k∥] 作为可分离的显著性分数——不同于 ChanMix/Kitty 只用 key 统计（range / magnitude）。
- **Prefill 一次选定、全程复用**：显著性通道对仅在 prefill 阶段计算一次（GQA 下对共享 KV head 的 query head 取平均），以 B×L×H^kv×(d/2) 的二值掩码存储，解码期间不再重算——兼顾输入自适应性（无需离线校准）与低开销（无需像 Kitty 那样逐量化组维护通道索引）。
- **低比特稳定的量化方案**：sink token（前 32 个）与残差缓冲（N=128）保持 FP16；1-bit 组使用中点校正；value cache 按 token 分组量化。两个工作点：PN-Mix(1)（r=50%, G=128, 2.26 bit）与 PN-Mix(2)（r=40%, G=64, 2.29 bit）。
- **完整的实证验证**：在 4 个通用模型（8B–32B）× 4 个基准 + 3 个推理模型 × AIME24/25 上与 KIVI、PolarQuant、ChanMix、Kitty 对比，并给出 1.5–3.0 bit 的 Pareto 前沿与 A100-40GB 上的端到端效率（Triton kernel）。

## Key Contributions

- **A query-aware RoPE channel-pair saliency criterion** derived from attention-logit perturbation analysis: quantization error in channel pair i perturbs the logit by a term scaled by the query–key pair-norm product, so σ_{h,i} = E_t[∥q_i^t∥] · E_s[∥k_i^s∥] (a separable approximation of the causal-joint score) is used to rank pairs, unlike prior key-only channel criteria (ChanMix's min–max range, Kitty's average magnitude).
- **Prefill-only, input-adaptive selection**: salient pairs are computed once from prefill Q̃/K̃ states and reused for the entire decoding phase — no offline calibration (unlike ChanMix) and no per-group saliency re-estimation or index storage during decoding (unlike Kitty). Table 1 shows the separable score correlates with the causal-joint oracle at Spearman 0.990–0.996 with 95.3–96.2% top-10% selection overlap.
- **A practical online quantization scheme**: sink tokens (Ns=32) and residual buffer (N=128) stay FP16; salient pairs get bH=3 bits, the rest bL=1 bit with a bin-midpoint correction for 1-bit groups; values use per-token group quantization. Two operating points at 2.26 / 2.29 average key bits.
- **Empirically validated robustness**: best non-FP16 mean accuracy in 5 of 6 model–AIME settings, within 1 point of FP16 on all four standard-benchmark models, graceful degradation down to 1.5 bits, and 8x longer feasible generation length than FP16 on a 40GB A100.

## Methodology

1. **Preliminary analysis (Section 4).** First, quantizing only K̃ to 2.25 bits hurts far more than quantizing only V (AIME, R1-Llama-8B and Qwen3-8B, up to 32K output tokens), confirming the key cache is the component to refine; sink-token FP16 protection helps both. Second, decomposing the post-RoPE attention logit Q̃_t·K̃_s = Σ_i ∥q_i^t∥∥k_i^s∥cos(φ_i) and its quantization-induced perturbation (Eq. 4) shows the error term per channel pair is scaled by the query–key pair-norm product; since the true perturbation terms depend on the reconstructed key, the pair-norm product is used as an available-before-quantization proxy. Under an H3(50%)L1 simulation (3 bits for top-50% pairs, 1 bit rest, ~2.25 effective bits, GQA states from R1-Qwen-7B/AIME25 and R1-Llama-8B/LiveCodeBench v5), the Q̃K̃ pair-norm criterion achieves the lowest attention-distribution KL divergence among all tested criteria (K̃ range, K̃ magnitude, K̃ pair norm). Third, prefill-estimated saliency is shown to be predictive (DKL close to the full-sequence oracle; highest Jaccard overlap with decoding-time salient pairs, stable over long generation) and selective (97–99% of total importance concentrated in the top-50% of pairs).
2. **PN-Mix (Section 5).** During prefill, compute σ_{h,i} per layer/KV head/pair, averaging the query side over query heads sharing the KV head (GQA); select top-r% pairs into set S_h. During decoding, sink tokens and the residual buffer are FP16; when the buffer fills, keys are quantized with per-channel asymmetric groups (pairs in S_h at bH bits, others at bL bits, 1-bit groups use the midpoint correction s=(max−min)/2, z=¾min+¼max); values use per-token asymmetric quantization at bV bits. Main configuration: bH=3, bL=1, Ns=32, N=128; PN-Mix(1) = r 50%, G 128 (2.26 bits); PN-Mix(2) = r 40%, G 64 (2.29 bits).
3. **Evaluation (Section 6).** lm-evaluation-harness accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B/32B, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (GSM8K 8-shot, MATH-Algebra 4-shot, HumanEval, GPQA-Diamond 5-shot; 5 runs, mean±std) and on reasoning models (R1-Llama-8B, Qwen3-8B, R1-Qwen-7B) for AIME24/25 (10 responses/question, 32K token limit, temperature 0.6). Baselines at matched 2.25–2.4 key bits: KIVI (uniform 2-bit), PolarQuant, ChanMix (offline profiles; reproduced with validated procedure where no official profile exists), Kitty (per-group dynamic selection). Efficiency measured on an A100-SXM4-40GB with a custom Triton kernel, including a bit-rate Pareto sweep at 3.0/2.5/2.25/2.0/1.5 bits.

## Results & Highlights

- **Standard benchmarks (Table 2, values in FP16)**: PN-Mix(1) at 2.26 key bits stays within 1 point of FP16 average on all four models, while uniform 2-bit KIVI drops 5.0–9.7 points. On Qwen3-8B, PN-Mix(1) averages **77.21 vs Kitty 74.43 (at 2.39 bits, +2.78 pts) and ChanMix† 74.88 (+2.33 pts)**, vs FP16 77.85 (−0.64); KIVI falls to 68.16. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, PN-Mix(1) averages **53.08 vs Kitty 51.86 (+1.22 pts)** despite 0.13 fewer bits (FP16: 54.04; KIVI: 45.35). On Qwen3-32B, PN-Mix(1) reaches **78.06 vs Kitty 75.84 (+2.22 pts)**, vs FP16 78.58.
- **AIME on reasoning models (Table 3, values FP16)**: with R1-Llama-8B on AIME24, PN-Mix(1) scores **48.33 — identical to FP16 — vs Kitty 42.00 (+6.33 pts), ChanMix 28.00 (+20.33), KIVI 28.67 (+19.66), PolarQuant 0.00**. With Qwen3-8B on AIME24, PN-Mix(2) hits **76.00 vs Kitty 70.67 (+5.33 pts)**, FP16 76.33. The pattern holds at 2-bit values (bV=2), e.g., R1-Llama-8B AIME25: PN-Mix(2) 33.00 vs Kitty 30.00. The one loss: R1-Qwen-7B AIME25, where Kitty's 38.66 edges PN-Mix(1)'s 37.33 — the only one of six model–AIME settings where a baseline wins.
- **Ablations (Table 4, Qwen3-8B, FP16 values)**: removing the 1-bit midpoint correction drops AIME24 from **76.00 to 72.00 (−4.0 pts)** and removing sink-token protection to **69.67 (−6.33 pts)**; replacing the Q̃K̃ pair-norm with key-only criteria lowers AIME24 to 70.67 (K̃ range), 73.00 (K̃ magnitude), or 74.67 (K̃ pair norm); an online re-selection variant is worse (72.33) *and* needs per-group index storage, justifying prefill-only selection.
- **Pareto frontier (Figure 3)**: on AIME24 (Qwen3-8B and R1-Llama-8B), PN-Mix and Kitty are both close to FP16 at 2.5–3.0 bits, but **PN-Mix degrades gracefully down to 1.5 bits while baselines show sharp drops below 2.25 bits**.
- **Efficiency on A100-SXM4-40GB (Qwen3-8B, Triton kernel, bH=3/bL=1/bV=2)**: PN-Mix supports **32K-token generation at batch 32 (8x FP16, which OOMs beyond ~4K at B=32) and batch 110 at 8K tokens (5.5x FP16)**, reaching **2.37x FP16's maximum per-GPU throughput**; at B=32/P=141/Lgen=32K only PN-Mix fits (37.12 GB) while FP16 and Kitty both OOM. Prefill overhead is negligible — TTFT within **12 ms of Kitty at P=16K** — and PN-Mix cuts decode time by **4.0–6.5%** across B=1 settings (8.8% reduction in marginal per-step latency growth on the 32K-token long-CoT trajectory).
- **Saliency analysis**: the separable pair-norm score matches the causal-joint oracle at Spearman 0.996 (R1-Llama-8B/LiveCodeBench v5) and 0.990 (R1-Qwen-7B/AIME25) with 96.2/95.3% top-10% overlap; 97–99% of importance mass sits in the top-50% of channel pairs.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: good, incremental.** The core idea — score RoPE channel *pairs* by query–key pair-norm products instead of key-only single-channel statistics — is a clean, well-motivated step beyond ChanMix/Kitty, and the logit-perturbation decomposition (Eq. 3–4) gives it a principled footing. But it is an evolution of the same mixed-precision channel-saliency paradigm rather than a new paradigm; the machinery (asymmetric group quant, residual buffer, sink protection, 1-bit midpoint correction from Jie et al. 2025) is assembled from prior work.
- **Rigor: mostly solid, with caveats.** Bit budgets are matched across baselines and variance is reported; however, ChanMix on Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B, and R1-Qwen-7B runs on *reproduced* profiles (validated, but still not the official artifacts), and AIME24/25 have only 30 questions each — the ±5–7 point standard deviations mean several "wins" (e.g., 76.00 vs 70.67) are within roughly one standard deviation of each other, a caveat the text does not dwell on.
- **Baseline cherry-picking risk: modest but present.** PolarQuant collapses to 0–9% on Qwen-family models — likely an incompatibility of its polar-coordinate scheme with this pipeline rather than a fair fight, inflating the "uniform precision fails" narrative. Conversely, GPQA-Diamond on Llama-3.1-8B sits near chance (~26% vs 25% random), so that column adds noise more than signal for the 8B model.
- **Efficiency comparison asymmetry.** In Table 5, Kitty is run at its original (bH,bL)=(4,2), r=25% setting (~2.5 key bits) while PN-Mix uses 3/1 at 2.26 bits plus 2-bit values — the 4.0–6.5% decode-time saving partially reflects the lower bit-width being dequantized, not only kernel efficiency; the matched-budget accuracy tables and the efficiency tables are thus not the same operating point for Kitty.
- **Reproducibility: reasonable.** Configurations (r, G, bH, bL, Ns, N), sweep details (Appendix I), kernel design (Appendix G), and profile-reproduction validation (Appendix H) are documented; no public code release for PN-Mix itself is stated in the paper, which tempers reproducibility.
- **Significance: real for long-output serving.** Sub-2.25-bit robustness plus a batch-32/32K-token feasible region on a 40GB card addresses a genuine deployment pain point; the prefill-only selection trick (with Appendix L showing saliency stability over long, multi-topic generation) is the most practically valuable insight.
- **Red flags: none serious.** Claims are mostly measured ("comparable or better"); the honest reporting of the R1-Qwen-7B/AIME25 loss to Kitty and of the online-reselection ablation losing to prefill-only is a good sign.

## Strengths

- Principled saliency criterion grounded in an explicit attention-logit perturbation analysis at the RoPE channel-pair level, then verified by simulation (DKL) before end-task evaluation.
- Prefill-once selection eliminates both offline calibration and per-group decoding-time index management — a clean systems contribution with measured near-zero TTFT overhead (≤12 ms at P=16K).
- Strong empirical coverage: four model families from 7B to 32B, standard reasoning + code + AIME long-CoT, value cache at both FP16 and 2-bit, plus a 1.5–3.0-bit Pareto sweep and honest variance reporting.
- Graceful sub-2.25-bit behavior (down to 1.5 bits) where all compared baselines break down — the most distinctive capability result.
- Concrete deployment-level wins: 8x feasible generation length, 5.5x feasible batch, 2.37x peak throughput vs FP16 on a 40GB A100, with only PN-Mix fitting the B=32/Lgen=32K operating point.

## Limitations

- Applicability is restricted to RoPE-based decoder-only LLMs — the pair structure is definitional; models using other positional encodings (ALiBi, NoPE) or encoder-decoder architectures need adaptation.
- Efficiency numbers come from a single A100-SXM4-40GB with the authors' own Triton kernels; wall-clock gains depend on hardware/kernel maturity, and no multi-GPU or PagedAttention-style serving-stack integration is evaluated.
- AIME's 30-question format yields high variance (±5–7 pts), so several baseline gaps are statistically soft; the paper does not report significance tests.
- The value cache is treated with a simple uniform scheme — the mixed-precision machinery is key-only, leaving potential accuracy on the table for full-KV ultra-low-bit regimes.
- Kitty's efficiency configuration differs in bit budget from the accuracy comparisons, so latency/memory comparisons mix operating points.
- No evaluation on non-reasoning long-context tasks (e.g., long-document QA/summarization), where prefill-prompt saliency might transfer differently than on short math prompts.

## Relevance

面向长输出推理的 KV cache 混合精度量化，正对应高效 LLM 推理与模型压缩方向：在 2.26 bit 键缓存预算下保持 AIME/长思维链精度并扩展 40GB 显卡上的可行服务区间，对推理系统与量化方法研究都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would pair-norm saliency transfer to non-RoPE or partially-RoPE models (e.g., interleaved/partial rotary variants, ALiBi models), possibly by defining pseudo-pairs via the positional kernel's coupling structure?
- How does prefill-only selection behave when the prompt is very short (e.g., single-question AIME, P≈141) or highly off-domain from the generated chain of thought — is there a prompt-length threshold where prefill statistics stop predicting decoding-time saliency?
- Can the Q̃K̃ pair-norm criterion be combined with token-level or layer-level mixed precision (e.g., retrieval-head-aware budgets) for compounding savings below 1.5 bits?
- What is the interaction with prefix caching / shared-prefix serving, where one prefill mask is reused across many requests?
- Does the 8.8% per-step latency-growth reduction hold under production serving stacks (vLLM/SGLang with paged KV), and how much headroom remains with INT4/FP8 weight-cache co-quantization?
- Since 97–99% of importance sits in the top-50% of pairs, is there an adaptive per-head r that pushes the average precision below 2.26 bits at equal accuracy?
