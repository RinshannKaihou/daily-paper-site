SetupQwen2.5-3B-Instruct + rank-stabilized LoRA (r=32) on the Turner et al. datasets; 69 aligned/misaligned prompt pairs (50 discovery / 10 validation / 9 test).

MeasureFree generation at temp 1.0 (9 prompts × 16 gens); a fixed DeepSeek judge scores alignment and coherence — EM = alignment < 30 and coherence ≥ 70.

Gap vs mean of ten layer-matched random sets: −10.6 / −42.8 / −31.5 pp — all bootstrap CIs exclude zero. Random replacement barely helps (finance random 55.3% > unmodified 50.7%); coherence does not decrease.

  • Recurrent locations. L21.h01, L21.h04, L18.h03 appear in all three top-5s; full rankings correlate at Spearman ρ = 0.463–0.603, and each model pair shares 7 of its top-10 heads.
  • Cross-organism transfer. Transplanting the three shared heads between organisms moves preference toward the target's own effect on every eligible prompt — mean recovery ratios 0.59–1.19, two transfers overshoot.
  • Validation. Five-head patch restores the EM direction 0.016–0.042 vs 0.002–0.005 (random); zero-ablating selected heads cuts the preference margin 5–13× more than random.
  • Controls hold. Benign medical fine-tune: margin −0.151 → −0.147 (unchanged); 30-question math check shifts by ≤1 answer.
Strengths
  • Clean causal chain — representational gap → head localization → behavioral intervention → cross-organism transfer — each step with its own control.
  • Ten layer-matched random replacement baselines (far stronger than "compare to nothing"); all three selected sets beat all ten of their random counterparts.
  • Coherence is measured alongside EM, guarding against the trivial "make the model incoherent" fix — coherence actually improves.
  • Honest, specific limitations section plus per-prompt appendix tables that make the transfer analysis auditable.
Limitations
  • Nine test prompts and 10 validation pairs bound every quantitative claim; the −38 / −31 pp effects especially need larger, independent prompt pools.
  • One 3B base model, one seed per organism, one LLM judge — no evidence yet that shared heads recur across architectures or seeds.
  • All benchmark response pairs were generated by the medical organism — distributional bias favoring medical.
  • Substantial residual EM remains (medical 22.9%); the five heads are far from a complete circuit.
  • Head-output patching conflates the head's computation with upstream differences — cannot attribute EM to specific LoRA parameters.
Questions for further exploration
  • Q1Do the three shared heads persist across different LoRA seeds — or under full fine-tuning?
  • Q2Does the intervention transfer to larger bases (Qwen2.5-7B/14B) or other families; does the layer-23 anchor move with depth?
  • Q3What do the shared heads actually attend to — can QK patterns or SAE features explain why these positions carry misalignment?
  • Q4Where does the residual EM (medical 22.9%) live — the two non-shared heads, or outside the top-5?
  • Q5Would conditional repair (steer toward Base only when the EM direction is detected) preserve benign behavior better?
  • Q6Is the EM reduction robust to different judges and adversarial prompts, given the DeepSeek threshold drives every headline number?
