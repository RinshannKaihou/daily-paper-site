> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"steering Hessian"（行为损失对转向向量的二阶导数矩阵），发现 CAA 转向向量中仅占 1–5% 能量的高曲率"尖锐分量"是抗转向（anti-steering）和行为反转的根源；将其一行式移除后（cosine > 0.974、范数损失 < 3%），在 Qwen2.5-7B/Llama-3.1-8B/Mistral-7B 全部 25 个生成条件下超越标准 CAA，并把 Qwen 在 AdvBench 上的拒绝率从 CAA 恶化后的 36% 恢复到 98%（未转向基线为 78%）。

> **TL;DR:** Why does contrastive activation addition (CAA) steering flip behavior on 30–50% of inputs? This NeurIPS 2026 paper answers geometrically: the behavioral loss is highly anisotropic in activation space (steering-Hessian condition numbers 50–3,727), and the sharp top eigenvectors carry only 1–5% of CAA's squared norm yet drive most of its misbehavior during autoregressive generation. Subtracting that sharp component ("sharp removal", a one-line post-processing fix with cosine > 0.974 to the original and curvature reduced by 1.7–12x, median 4x) beats standard CAA in 25/25 open-ended generation conditions across Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B — most dramatically on AdvBench refusal, where CAA actively degrades safety (Qwen 78% → 36%) and sharp removal restores it to 98%. A jailbreak experiment further shows sharp directions act as noise, not signal: they disrupt coherent control under either steering polarity.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 steering Hessian（转向海森矩阵）**：定义为行为损失（首个生成位置对目标 token 的交叉熵）对转向向量 v 的二阶导数 H_steer = ∇²_v L(v)，刻画模型行为对转向向量各方向微小扰动的敏感度；其顶部特征向量（"尖锐方向"）扰动会引起剧烈、不稳定、依赖输入的行为摆动，底部特征向量（"平坦方向"）则行为稳健。
- **理论解释抗转向现象**：Proposition 1 给出 Gauss-Newton 分解——尖锐方向的作用由模型下游 Jacobian 主导（而非行为概念本身），故随输入剧烈变化导致 anti-steering；Proposition 2 证明移除尖锐分量的代价仅为 O(√ε)（ε 为尖锐能量占比），而方向曲率上界从 |λ₁| 降至 |λ_{k+1}|。
- **尖锐移除（sharp removal）一行式修正**：v_cleaned = v_CAA − P_Sk v_CAA，无需重训练、推理零开销（Hessian 估计每层仅需单张 A6000 约 40 秒、全流程 < 2 分钟）；作为后处理 wrapper 可与任意转向向量组合（已在 Arditi et al. 拒绝方向上验证）。
- **五个系统性实证发现（36 个 token 级条件 + 25 个生成条件）**：曲率谱强各向异性；沿尖锐方向转向在全部 36 个条件反转预期行为（anti-steer 率 53–100%）；CAA 中 1–5% 的尖锐能量驱动了生成时绝大部分分布偏移；移除后 25/25 生成条件全面超越 CAA；尖锐方向是噪声而非信号——无论转向极性如何都破坏行为控制的连贯性。
- **安全审计价值**：模型自身曲率结构即可暴露破坏安全对齐的方向（无需对抗优化）；尖锐能量分数 ε(v) 可作为任意转向向量的质量/安全性审计指标。

## Key Contributions

- **Steering Hessian framework**: defines the curvature of the behavioral loss (next-token cross-entropy toward a target token, e.g., "Great" for sentiment, "I" for refusal) with respect to the steering vector itself — not model weights — and decomposes activation space into a sharp subspace (top-|λ| eigenvectors: erratic, input-dependent behavioral swings) and a flat subspace (robust directions).
- **Two propositions ground the method**: Prop 1 (Gauss-Newton decomposition) explains *why* sharp directions are catastrophic — their logit effect is governed by the downstream Jacobian J_ℓ, which varies erratically across inputs, producing anti-steering; Prop 2 proves sharp removal approximately preserves the vector (cosine √(1−ε) > 0.974, norm loss < 3% for ε ≤ 0.05) while bounding its directional curvature by |λ_{k+1}| — a curvature reduction κ_gap of 1.7–12x (median 4x over 36 conditions).
- **Practical one-line correction**: v_cleaned = v_CAA − P_Sk v_CAA estimated via Lanczos (m = 40 iterations, n = 12 prompts, finite-difference HVPs, δ = 0.01) in ~40 s/layer on one A6000, with zero inference-time overhead. It is a post-processing wrapper that composes with other steering vectors (demonstrated on the Arditi et al. refusal direction, cosine 0.01–0.20 to CAA).
- **Five cross-cutting findings** on Qwen2.5-7B / Llama-3.1-8B / Mistral-7B-v0.3 across sentiment, sycophancy, toxicity, and refusal: anisotropic spectra everywhere (κ = 50–3,727); sharp projections reverse behavior in 36/36 conditions; the sharp component (1–5% of squared norm) drives a disproportionate share of the steered-vs-unsteered KL gap during generation; sharp removal wins 25/25 generation conditions over CAA; and a jailbreak experiment shows sharp directions disrupt coherent control under both polarities.
- **Safety-audit metric**: the sharp energy fraction ε(v) predicts steering-vector quality across construction methods, and the model's own curvature exposes directions that reverse safety alignment without any adversarial optimization.

## Methodology

The paper studies a frozen LLM f_θ whose layer-ℓ hidden state h_ℓ(x) is perturbed to h_ℓ(x) + αv. The behavioral loss L(x, v, α) is the cross-entropy of a per-task target token at the first generated position (target tokens listed in Appendix A; a multi-token contrastive variant has cosine 0.61 to the single-token decomposition, so the sharp/flat split is robust to this choice). The steering Hessian H_steer = ∇²_v E_x[L] is never formed explicitly at d ∈ {3584, 4096}; instead its top/bottom eigenpairs are estimated with m = 40 Lanczos iterations (full reorthogonalization), where Hessian-vector products use central finite differences (δ = 0.01) averaged over n = 12 estimation prompts — about 40 seconds per layer on a single A6000.

The default steering vector is CAA: the mean activation difference over 20 positive/negative behavioral exemplar pairs (Eq. 3). Sharp removal then subtracts the projection of v_CAA onto the top-k = 15 sharp eigenvectors (Eq. 6). Proposition 1 decomposes H_steer = α²·E[J_ℓᵀ ∇²_z ℓ_CE J_ℓ] + downstream-curvature term: the positive-semidefinite Gauss-Newton term dominates, so sharp directions are exactly those the downstream network amplifies most into logit changes — a property of the computation graph, explaining input-dependent swings. Proposition 2 bounds the token-level cost of removal as O(√ε) and caps the cleaned vector's directional curvature at |λ_{k+1}|. Note the formal bounds hold at α = 1.0; generation-level gains at larger α are validated empirically (Fig. 2) but not bounded.

Evaluation spans 3 models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) × 4 tasks (sentiment on neutral prompts, sycophancy/misconception disagreement, toxicity on RealToxicityPrompts, refusal on AdvBench) × 3 layers (early/mid/late) = 36 token-level conditions. Token-level metric: Δ = P(target) − P(anti-target) swept over α ∈ [0.25, 5.0], with anti-steer rate = fraction of inputs with Δ < 0. Generation-level: 50 greedy 80–150-token completions per condition scored by task classifiers (RoBERTa for sentiment, BART-MNLI for sycophancy, AdvBench keyword detection with NLI tiebreaker for refusal; GPT-4o-mini as judge in the jailbreak experiment), comparing CAA against v_sharp, v_flat, v_random (dimensionality control), and v_cleaned. Controls include generic-PCA subspaces, sign-flipped CAA, a k-sweep over {1,3,5,10,15,25}, XSTest over-refusal, and an N = 200 robustness check.

## Results & Highlights

- **Sharp removal restores safety where CAA actively breaks it (AdvBench refusal, generation-level)**: on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, CAA *degrades* refusal from a 78% unsteered baseline down to 36–44%, while sharp removal raises it to 88–98% — above baseline. On Llama-3.1-8B, CAA drops refusal from 62% to 24–52%; sharp removal restores 74–76%. On Mistral-7B (12% keyword-matching baseline), sharp removal reaches 36–62%.
- **25/25 generation wins over CAA**: sharp removal outperforms standard CAA in all 25 open-ended generation conditions (3 tasks × 3 model families × multiple layers). Largest sentiment gain: Mistral L13 RoBERTa sentiment score 0.67 (CAA) → 0.97 (sharp removal).
- **Sharp directions reverse behavior in every token-level condition tested**: projecting CAA onto the sharp subspace flips the intended behavior in 36/36 conditions, with anti-steer rates of 53–100%; on Llama refusal, v_sharp hits a 100% anti-steer rate at every tested layer with peak wrong-direction shift |Δ| = 0.48. Meanwhile the flat projection v_flat achieves the lowest anti-steer rate in 11 of 12 model-task combos at the best layer — including 0% anti-steer on Qwen sentiment L21 across all 1,000 evaluations — vs 60–86% anti-steer for random directions.
- **A tiny energy fraction drives the generation gap**: the sharp component is only 1–5% of CAA's squared norm (ε ≤ 0.05, cosine > 0.974, norm loss < 3%), yet on Llama refusal L15 CAA and v_sharp produce nearly identical per-position KL trajectories vs the unsteered model (~19 nats; sharp = 2.6% of squared norm there), while sharp removal cuts the KL divergence by 3–4x. Directional curvature drops by κ_gap = 1.7–12x (median 4x) across 36 conditions; condition numbers of H_steer range 50–3,727 (e.g., Llama sycophancy L16: κ = 3,727).
- **Sign-flipping CAA is not enough**: on the 4 inverted refusal conditions, v_cleaned beats the sign-flipped −v_CAA by 23–38 percentage points (e.g., Qwen L10 refusal: 98% vs 68%), and in 8 of 10 sycophancy conditions — the retained sharp component disrupts generation regardless of sign.
- **Jailbreak experiment (negative steering, α = 3.0, GPT-4o-mini judge)**: −v_cleaned is the *most effective* jailbreak vector (non-refusal 80% vs 68% for −v_CAA on Qwen; 40% vs 33% on Llama), while −v_sharp is the *least* (2% full compliance with 40% incoherent partials on Qwen; 100% refusal on Llama) — sharp directions act as noise that destroys coherent behavioral control in either polarity.
- **Robustness and cost**: the k-sweep shows Qwen refusal is k-robust (k = 1 alone lifts refusal 44% → 90%, plateau by k = 5) while Llama sycophancy peaks at k = 5 (honesty 0.765) and over-removal at k = 25 drops below CAA (0.698), motivating k = 10–15; the whole pipeline runs in < 2 minutes per layer on one A6000 and adds zero inference overhead.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuinely novel angle. Anti-steering and coefficient sensitivity were documented empirically (Tan et al. 2024; Braun et al. 2025), and sharpness-aware minimization is established in *weight* space (SAM, Foret et al. 2021) — this paper is the first to bring curvature analysis to the *activation-space* steering vector, where the object of interest is a single d-dimensional vector rather than billions of weights, and to turn the diagnosis into a deployable one-line fix. The observation that the model's own Hessian top eigenvectors jailbreak safety alignment without adversarial optimization is a distinctive safety finding.
- **Rigor**: strong for the core claims — pre-registered-style exact counts, disjoint train/estimation/eval splits, and a genuinely good control battery: generic-PCA subspaces (mean principal-angle cosine 0.077 to Hessian subspaces, i.e., near-orthogonal — high variance ≠ high curvature), random-direction controls, sign-flipped CAA, k-sensitivity sweep, XSTest over-refusal check, LLM-judge cross-validation, and an N = 200 robustness rerun.
- **Reproducibility**: high. Stdlib-friendly setup with concrete hyperparameters (m = 40 Lanczos, n = 12 prompts, δ = 0.01, k = 15, α = 1.0 for estimation), open models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3), public datasets (RealToxicityPrompts, AdvBench, XSTest), per-task target tokens in Appendix A, and pseudocode in Appendix C. The < 2 min/layer compute cost makes replication cheap.
- **Weaknesses worth flagging**: (1) The headline "25/25" compares only against vanilla CAA (and sign-flipped CAA) — there is no head-to-head against the many existing robust-steering improvements the paper itself cites (conceptors, SAE-guided features, gradient-optimized or preference-optimized vectors), so "outperforms standard CAA" is a weaker claim than "outperforms the state of the art". (2) Generation evaluation is thin per condition: 50 greedy completions scored by automatic classifiers, and the Mistral refusal numbers rest on keyword+NLI matching that the authors themselves show undercounts (LLM judge: 52% vs 12% keyword baseline) — they keep the cruder metric "for cross-model consistency", which flatters the 36–62% Mistral headline less than a judge would, to be fair, but the mismatch is a real caveat. (3) Toxicity appears in the 36 token-level conditions but is absent from the 25 generation conditions, so the toxicity axis has no open-ended-text evidence. (4) The theory is local: the quadratic approximation and Prop 2's bounds hold at α = 1.0, while the generation wins are demonstrated at larger α — the authors acknowledge this gap explicitly. (5) The Hessian is estimated from only 12 prompts under a fixed distribution; distribution-shift transfer of the sharp subspace is untested (stated limitation), and scale is limited to 7–8B instruct models requiring white-box access. (6) The jailbreak claim rests on 2 models × 1 layer × 1 α value with a GPT-4o-mini judge.
- **Significance**: for the interpretability/steering subfield this is a substantive contribution — it converts a folk-known nuisance (CAA fragility) into a measurable geometric quantity with a cheap fix and a safety-audit metric (ε). It does not overturn steering methods generally, and the claim is properly scoped as a wrapper that composes with, rather than replaces, existing vectors.

## Strengths

- A clean central insight with theory (Gauss-Newton decomposition) that mechanistically explains anti-steering, behavioral inversion, and coefficient sensitivity in one framework.
- The fix is nearly free: one line of post-processing, ~40 s/layer one-time cost, zero inference overhead, and it is model-agnostic and method-agnostic (composes with CAA, refusal directions, and in principle SAE-feature or optimization-based vectors).
- Exceptionally systematic empirics: every claim is tied to an exact condition count, with honest controls (PCA, random, sign-flip, k-sweep, over-refusal, LLM-judge, larger-N reruns) rather than cherry-picked anecdotes.
- Directly actionable safety result: CAA can silently *weaken* refusal alignment (Qwen 78% → 36% on AdvBench) — a warning for anyone deploying steering for safety — and ε(v) offers a pre-deployment audit metric.
- The paper is transparent about its own metric weaknesses, polarity asymmetries, and scope boundaries, and provides proofs plus a dedicated limitations section.

## Limitations

- The steering Hessian is a local quadratic approximation at (v_CAA, α = 1.0); higher-order terms may dominate at large α, and the formal guarantees do not cover the empirically successful high-α generation regime.
- Only three 7–8B instruction-tuned models and four behavioral axes are tested; scaling behavior, base (non-instruct) models, and black-box settings without activation access remain open.
- The sharp/flat subspaces are estimated from a fixed 12-prompt distribution; whether they transfer across distribution shifts is unaddressed.
- Generation evaluation uses 50 greedy completions per condition with task-specific automatic classifiers; Mistral's refusal baseline is 12% by keyword matching vs 52% by LLM judge, so cross-model refusal comparisons carry metric noise.
- No comparison against contemporary robust-steering methods (conceptors, SAE-guided, preference-optimized vectors) in the generation benchmarks — only against (sign-flipped) CAA and, for composition, the Arditi refusal direction.
- Toxicity is evaluated only at the token level; the default k = 15 is justified post-hoc from the k-sweep, and the jailbreak study covers a single layer and α per model.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do sharp/flat subspaces remain stable across prompt-distribution shifts, domains, and languages — i.e., can one Hessian estimate per layer be reused in deployment, or must it be refreshed per distribution?
- How does the sharp energy fraction ε(v) scale with model size (30B–70B+), with base vs instruction-tuned checkpoints, and across more layers — does the 1–5% energy fraction and the 3–4x KL reduction persist?
- How does sharp removal interact with stronger steering baselines (conceptors, SAE-guided feature steering, preference-optimized vectors) — does it compose into a consistent further gain, or do those methods already avoid the sharp subspace implicitly?
- Can the O(√ε) bound of Proposition 2 be extended to finite α > 1, or replaced by a higher-order expansion that covers the empirically observed generation-time regime?
- Is the sharp subspace related to known "glitch" or adversarial directions in the literature (e.g., refusal-direction geometry of Arditi et al.), and can ε(v) be computed cheaply enough to serve as a runtime guard for steering-based safety filters?
- The paper shows −v_cleaned is the strongest jailbreak among tested vectors — does removing sharp directions inadvertently create a cleaner attack surface, and does that imply deployable mitigation (monitoring ε of observed activation perturbations)?
