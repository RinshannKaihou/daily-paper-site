  • Curvature of the steering vector, not the weights: the behavioral loss is next-token cross-entropy toward a task target token (“Great” for sentiment, “I” for refusal).
  • Prop 1 (Gauss–Newton): sharp top eigenvectors are what the downstream Jacobian amplifies most — a property of the computation graph, so behavior swings input by input (anti-steering).
  • Prop 2: removal costs only O(√ε) while the cleaned vector’s directional curvature is capped at |λk+1|.
  • Post-processing wrapper: composes with other steering vectors (verified on the Arditi et al. refusal direction).
  • Eval grid: 3 models × 4 tasks × 3 layers = 36 token-level conditions + 25 generation conditions, with PCA, random, sign-flip, and k-sweep controls.

  • Sharp alone reverses behavior: projecting CAA onto the sharp subspace flips the intended behavior in 36/36 token conditions (anti-steer 53–100%; Llama refusal: 100% at every layer).
  • Sign-flipping isn’t enough: vcleaned beats −vCAA by 23–38 points (Qwen L10 refusal: 98% vs 68%) — the retained sharp component disrupts either polarity.
  • Jailbreak probe (α=3, GPT-4o-mini judge): −vcleaned is the strongest jailbreak (80% non-refusal, Qwen) while −vsharp is the weakest (2% compliance, 40% incoherent) — sharp directions act as noise, not signal.
  • Cheap: k=1 alone lifts Qwen refusal 44%→90%; full pipeline < 2 min/layer. Largest sentiment gain: Mistral L13, 0.67 → 0.97.

Strengths what holds up

  • One Gauss–Newton framework mechanistically explains anti-steering, behavioral inversion, and coefficient sensitivity together.
  • The fix is nearly free: one line of post-processing, ~40 s/layer one-time, zero inference overhead — model- and method-agnostic.
  • Exceptionally systematic empirics: exact condition counts (36/36, 25/25, 11/12) with honest controls — PCA, random, sign-flip, k-sweep, over-refusal, LLM-judge, N=200 rerun.
  • Actionable safety result: CAA can silently weaken refusal alignment; the sharp energy fraction ε(v) is a pre-deployment audit metric.
  • Transparent about its own metric weaknesses and scope boundaries; full proofs included.

Limitations what to watch

  • Theory is local: quadratic bounds hold at α=1.0, while generation wins are demonstrated at larger α (gap acknowledged by authors).
  • Only three 7–8B instruct models and four behavioral axes; scaling, base models, and black-box settings untested.
  • Sharp/flat subspaces estimated from a fixed 12-prompt distribution; transfer across distribution shift is unaddressed.
  • Thin per-condition generation eval (50 greedy completions); Mistral refusal carries metric noise (12% keyword vs 52% judge).
  • No head-to-head with contemporary robust-steering methods (conceptors, SAE-guided, preference-optimized); toxicity is token-level only; jailbreak study covers one layer and one α.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Do sharp/flat subspaces survive prompt-distribution, domain, and language shifts — can one Hessian estimate per layer be reused in deployment?
  2. How does ε(v) scale with model size (30B–70B+), base vs instruct checkpoints, and more layers?
  3. Does sharp removal compose with stronger baselines (conceptors, SAE-guided, preference-optimized vectors), or do those already avoid the sharp subspace?
  4. Can the O(√ε) bound extend to α > 1, covering the generation-time regime?
  5. Is the sharp subspace related to known “glitch” / refusal-direction geometry — and is ε(v) cheap enough as a runtime guard?
  6. −vcleaned is the strongest jailbreak tested: does sharp removal inadvertently create a cleaner attack surface to monitor?
