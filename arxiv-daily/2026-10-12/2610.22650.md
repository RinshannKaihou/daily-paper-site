> **一句话总结：** 作者从零预训练 1.2B Llama-3 模型并在预训练/CPT/SFT 三个阶段连续插值 AdamW 与 Muon 更新比例，发现所谓"换优化器代价"基本不存在——最终性能几乎完全由各阶段优化器的独立边际效应解释（SFT 阶段 Muon 比例越高越好），匹配优化器只会降低 SFT 后的遗忘（预训练验证损失变化 +0.49 vs 失配 +0.73）却不提升基准成绩，且 CPT 中的表面切换代价在学习率逐设置调优后消失。

> **TL;DR:** By pretraining 1.2B Llama-3 models at 1×/8× Chinchilla (24B/193B tokens) with per-step random mixtures of AdamW and Muon updates (p ∈ {0, 0.1, …, 1.0}) and decomposing the PT×SFT score grid into marginal + interaction effects, the paper shows optimizer-switching cost is negligible: interaction residuals (−0.6 to +0.3) are smaller than the SFT-optimizer main effect (−0.9 to +0.7) and comparable to seed noise on the six-benchmark suite (MMLU, BBH, GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP). Muon-SFT beats AdamW-SFT on average by +1.4 points (17.2 vs 15.8) on AdamW-pretrained 1.2B models at 1× Chinchilla, and wins 4 of 5 open-weight settings up to Qwen3-14B (e.g. Llama2-7B: 41.19 vs 38.21) — so post-training optimizer choice can be made independently of the pretraining optimizer; only forgetting (not final performance) favors matching.

## 核心贡献

- **将"优化器失配代价"从二值问题变为连续可控变量**：在预训练、继续预训练（CPT）和 SFT 三个阶段，以每步概率 p ∈ {0.0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 1.0} 独立抽取 Muon 或 AdamW 更新，构造出可平滑退火的失配程度，从而能把"切换代价"与两个优化器各自的独立效应分离开。
- **用双因素方差分解（行效应 + 列效应 + 交互残差）量化失配**：SFT 阶段 PT×SFT 交互残差仅 −0.6 到 +0.3，小于 SFT 优化器主效应（−0.9 到 +0.7），与三种子噪声同量级——即没有可靠的"换优化器性能惩罚"。
- **区分了两种代价**：匹配优化器确实显著降低 SFT 后对预训练分布的遗忘（AdamW-PT/AdamW-SFT 遗忘 +0.49 vs AdamW-PT/Muon-SFT +0.73，按 C4-EN 验证损失变化计），但这并不转化为下游基准的最终收益。
- **揭示 CPT 中的表面切换代价其实是超参错配**：固定学习率 5e-5 时匹配优化器出现有利残差，逐设置调优学习率后该模式完全消失。
- **在开源模型上验证规模外推**：对 Llama2-7B、Qwen2.5-7B、Qwen3-4B/8B/14B（全部 AdamW 预训练）做任务通用 SFT，Muon 在 5 个设置中的 4 个取得更高基准均值，说明 Muon 是可行的后训练优化器。

## Key Contributions

- Reframes optimizer mismatch from a binary switch to a continuous dial: at every optimization step a Muon update is selected with probability p ∈ {0.0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 1.0} and an AdamW update otherwise, applied independently across pretraining, continued pretraining (CPT), and SFT stages of 1.2B Llama-3 models trained from scratch at 1× and 8× Chinchilla scales.
- Provides a clean decomposition methodology: the PT×SFT benchmark grid is decomposed as X_ij = μ + α_i (PT effect) + β_j (SFT effect) + γ_ij (interaction), with update RMS matched between optimizers (0.2·√(max(m,n)) scaling vs AdamW's ≈0.22 expected RMS with β1=0.9) so a single base learning rate is shared across mixtures.
- Shows the "switching penalty" reported in prior work (Qu et al. 2026; Liu et al. 2026) does not survive controls: SFT interaction residuals span only −0.6 to +0.3 versus a −0.9 to +0.7 SFT main effect, and the CPT matched-optimizer advantage at a fixed 5e-5 learning rate vanishes once the learning rate is tuned per setting.
- Separates the *forgetting cost* from the *performance cost*: matched optimizers do reduce forgetting of the pretraining distribution during SFT (measured as change in held-out pretraining validation loss), but this yields no final benchmark benefit.
- Validates at scale on open weights: task-general SFT (Tulu-3 mixture) of AdamW-pretrained Llama2-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Qwen3-4B/8B/14B shows Muon is competitive with (and slightly better in 4 of 5 cases than) AdamW.

## Methodology

The authors pretrain 1.2B-parameter Llama-3 architecture models (32 layers, hidden dim 1536, 24 heads, ~1.21B non-embedding params, Llama-3 tokenizer, vocab 128,256) from scratch on the Wen et al. (2026) data mixture (DCLM-baseline + StarCoderData + ProofPile-2) at 1× Chinchilla (24.16B tokens, 23,042 steps) and 8× Chinchilla (193.29B tokens, 184,336 steps), adopting that work's tuned hyperparameters (e.g. peak LR 2e-3 for both optimizers, Muon with 0.2 RMS update scaling, 5 Newton–Schulz iterations).

The central design is the **mixed optimizer**: at each step, a Muon update is drawn with probability p, else AdamW. Because AdamW's expected update RMS with β1=0.9 is √((1−β1)/(1+β1)) ≈ 0.22, matching Muon's 0.2·√(max(m,n)) scaling keeps update magnitudes comparable, admitting one base LR within and across mixtures. Seven pretraining mixtures are trained, and each is then post-trained with the same seven mixtures, giving a 7×7 grid for SFT (Tulu-3 mixture, 2 epochs, 3 seeds per cell) and for CPT (same pretraining data distribution). Learning rates are swept separately per cell (SFT best LRs land in {4e-4, 8e-4}; CPT in {5e-5, 1e-4}), selected by held-out validation loss.

Each grid is analyzed by a two-way ANOVA-style decomposition into row (PT optimizer), column (post-training optimizer), and interaction residuals, so mismatch effects are isolated from each optimizer's independent quality. Evaluation covers six benchmarks: MMLU (0-shot CoT), BBH (3-shot CoT), GSM8K (5-shot EM), MATH (4-shot CoT), HumanEval (Pass@1), MBPP (Pass@1). Forgetting is quantified as the increase in pretraining held-out validation loss after SFT. Finally, the same SFT protocol (best LR swept per model, e.g. 2e-5 for Qwen2.5-7B, 5e-5 for Llama2-7B) is applied to open-weight AdamW-pretrained models: Llama2-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-4B/8B/14B.

## Results & Highlights

- **Interaction (mismatch) effects are noise-level during SFT**: on the six-benchmark average for 1× Chinchilla 1.2B models, the matched corners show residuals of −0.2 (AdamW-PT/AdamW-SFT) and +0.2 (Muon-PT/Muon-SFT), with all diagonal interactions in [−0.6, +0.3] — smaller than the SFT-optimizer main effect (−0.9 to +0.7) and comparable to the three-seed noise in Table 7.
- **Muon-SFT helps regardless of pretraining optimizer (1× Chinchilla)**: switching the SFT optimizer from AdamW to Muon raises the benchmark average from 15.8±0.4 to 17.2±0.6 (+1.4) on AdamW-PT models and from 17.0±0.3 to 18.8±0.2 (+1.8) on Muon-PT models; per-benchmark, GSM8K (5-shot EM) climbs from 16.7±1.0 to 20.7±0.8 (+4.0 pts) on AdamW-PT models.
- **The Muon-SFT advantage shrinks with pretraining data on AdamW-PT models**: at 8× Chinchilla the AdamW-PT average gap nearly vanishes (22.0±0.4 AdamW-SFT vs 21.9±0.4 Muon-SFT, −0.1), while on Muon-PT models Muon-SFT still wins 24.7±0.3 → 27.6±0.2 (+2.9), with GSM8K 30.8±1.0 → 33.8±1.8 (+3.0) and MMLU 29.3±1.2 → 32.4±0.3 (+3.1).
- **Matching matters for forgetting, not for benchmarks**: post-SFT increase in pretraining validation loss (C4-EN, LR 4e-4) is +0.49±0.01 for AdamW-PT/AdamW-SFT versus +0.73±0.02 for the mismatched AdamW-PT/Muon-SFT, and only +0.38±0.00 for Muon-PT/Muon-SFT — yet Muon-PT/Muon-SFT is simultaneously the best final-benchmark cell (18.8 average).
- **CPT switching cost is a learning-rate artifact**: at a fixed LR of 5e-5 matched CPT optimizers show favorable residuals, but after per-setting LR tuning (Table 5) the diagonal preference disappears entirely (e.g. AdamW-PT/CPT-AdamW final loss 2.6592 vs Muon-PT/CPT-Muon 2.6099, monotone in Muon proportion with no interaction pattern).
- **Open-weight models confirm competitiveness**: Muon-SFT beats AdamW-SFT on benchmark average in 4 of 5 settings — Llama2-7B 41.19±0.33 vs 38.21±1.36 (+2.98), Qwen2.5-7B 68.42±0.41 vs 67.76±0.90 (+0.66), Qwen3-4B 71.24±0.30 vs 70.67±0.39 (+0.57), Qwen3-14B 78.40±0.24 vs 78.34±0.02 (+0.06) — with only Qwen3-8B going the other way (73.71±0.73 vs 74.14±0.33, −0.43); all gaps except Llama2-7B are small relative to run-to-run variation.
- **Muon is also the better pretrainer here**: C4-EN validation loss after pretraining is 2.8947 (Muon) vs 2.9426 (AdamW) at 1× Chinchilla and 2.7375 vs 2.8011 at 8×.

First author Xinyu Zhao (MIT) completed the work during an internship at the MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab, with Davis Wertheimer, Ashutosh Sharma, and Rameswar Panda (all MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab) and Paul Pu Liang (MIT). The group has a strong track record in LLM analysis; the experimental design explicitly follows Wen et al. (ICLR 2026, "Fantastic pretraining optimizers and where to find them") for architecture and hyperparameters and Liu et al. (2025, Moonshot, "Muon is scalable for LLM training") for Muon's RMS-matched update scaling, which anchors the setup in community-standard recipes rather than ad-hoc choices. The paper directly engages two prior works claiming an optimizer-switching penalty (Qu et al., ICML 2026; Liu et al., 2026) and explains the discrepancy. Code is stated as released. Precomputing 7 pretraining mixtures at up to 193B tokens plus a 7×7 SFT grid with 3 seeds each is substantial compute for an academic study.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The continuous p-mixture design (interpolating update distributions per step, borrowed from HTMuon-style interleaving) plus the additive row/column/interaction decomposition is a genuinely elegant way to isolate mismatch, and the negative result usefully contradicts Qu et al. (2026). The forgetting-versus-performance distinction is the paper's sharpest conceptual contribution.
- **Rigor**: Three seeds per cell, per-cell learning-rate selection by held-out validation loss, RMS-matched update scales, and full hyperparameter tables in the appendix are above the norm. However, no formal statistical test (e.g. significance of interaction terms against seed variance) is reported — magnitudes are compared to noise informally — and the additive decomposition assumes no higher-order structure. Non-LR hyperparameters (weight decay 0.2 vs 0.1, momentum, β2) are inherited rather than swept, so "optimizer identity" is confounded with its accompanying hyperparameter recipe to some degree.
- **Weak spots in the evidence**: the controlled 7×7 grid exists only at 1.2B / 1× Chinchilla; at 8× Chinchilla only the four pure corner settings were pretrained (mixed mixtures are "—" in Table 3), so the no-interaction claim at higher data ratios rests on corners only. The open-weight section covers only two model families, all AdamW-pretrained — the "Muon-pretrained at scale, then post-train" direction is untested beyond 1.2B. Llama2-7B (4096-token context, 2023-era) is an aging baseline. Absolute benchmark averages at 1.2B are low (15.8–18.8), where floor effects could mask interactions.
- **Internal counter-evidence is acknowledged but worth flagging**: MMLU at 1× actually *drops* with Muon-SFT on AdamW-PT models (23.8±0.5 → 22.7±3.1, −1.1, with 3.1 std), and Qwen3-8B reverses direction — so "more Muon in SFT is generally better" is a trend, not a law.
- **Reproducibility**: strong — standard open data (DCLM/StarCoderData/ProofPile-2, Tulu-3 SFT), open-weight models, full hyperparameter tables (LR sweeps in Tables 4–6), pseudocode for both optimizers, and released code.
- **Significance**: practically relevant for post-training teams (Muon is a drop-in SFT optimizer for AdamW-pretrained checkpoints), though the scope is SFT/CPT only — no RLHF/DPO/preference stages — and 14B is far from frontier scale.
- **Red flags**: minor — "Our code is released here" with a placeholder-looking link in the preprint; the abstract's "generally improving" quietly omits the MMLU regression and the 8× AdamW-PT washout.

## Strengths

- The per-step probabilistic mixture of two optimizers with matched update RMS is a clean instrument for studying mismatch that future optimizer papers can reuse.
- Two-way decomposition of the score grid cleanly separates "which optimizer is better" from "does matching matter" — the question prior work conflated.
- Per-setting learning-rate tuning is exactly the right control, and it overturns the paper's own fixed-LR observation (the CPT diagonal effect), which is a model of self-correcting experimental hygiene.
- Honest reporting: full per-benchmark tables with standard deviations, negative results (Qwen3-8B, MMLU at 1×) included rather than buried, and forgetting reported as a real (if narrow) benefit of matching.
- Scale triangulation: 1.2B from-scratch pretraining at two Chinchilla ratios plus five open-weight models up to 14B.

## Limitations

- Controlled mixed-optimizer grids are limited to 1.2B-parameter models; only pure AdamW/Muon corners exist at 8× Chinchilla, and nothing tests >14B, MoE architectures, or Muon-pretrained open checkpoints.
- Only SFT and CPT are studied; RLHF/DPO/on-policy RL post-training stages — where implicit-bias arguments (Qu et al. 2026) may matter more — are out of scope.
- Benchmark suite is six standard tasks; MMLU shows a small Muon-SFT regression at 1×, and the tiny absolute averages at 1.2B leave little headroom to detect interactions.
- Optimizer identity is bundled with fixed auxiliary hyperparameters (different weight decay for AdamW vs Muon, AdamW parameter group for embeddings/head under Muon), so some "optimizer effects" may partly be recipe effects.
- Forgetting is measured only as pretraining validation-loss increase, not as degradation on pretraining-era downstream tasks.

## Relevance

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the no-mismatch-penalty conclusion hold for RLHF/DPO/on-policy RL post-training, where the implicit bias of the optimizer interacts with preference optimization more strongly than with SFT?
- Would the CPT interaction pattern stay flat at 70B+ scale or for MoE models, where Muon's spectral updates behave differently on routed expert matrices?
- Can the reduced forgetting from matched optimizers be achieved more cheaply — e.g. by lower LR or KL anchors during SFT — decoupling forgetting control from optimizer choice entirely?
- Is the MMLU dip under Muon-SFT at 1× Chinchilla (23.8 → 22.7) a real knowledge-degradation effect or seed noise, and does it persist with more evals (MMLU-Pro, GPQA)?
- How do the findings transfer to Muon-pretrained open checkpoints (e.g. Kimi/Moonshot models) post-trained with AdamW — the reverse direction left untested here?
- Could the per-step mixed-update construction itself be optimized (e.g. annealing p over training) as a practical training recipe, given that intermediate mixtures pretrain to lower C4-EN loss than pure AdamW?
