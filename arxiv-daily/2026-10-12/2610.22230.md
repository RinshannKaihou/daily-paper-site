> **一句话总结：** Meta 联合 AMD 发表首个超大规模厂商将 AMD GPU（MI350X，288GB HBM、CDNA4 8-XCC 芯片化架构）用于生产级 DLRM 训练的系统性报告：通过三层协同优化——利用 1.6 倍 HBM 容量做硬件-模型协同设计（更大 batch、去激活重计算、嵌入表驻留 HBM，带来 20% 训练加速 + >0.1% 归一化熵提升）、微秒级解析式 GEMM 选核模型 Origami（对穷举 autotuning 达 93% 选择效率并扩展到多芯片封装）、以及把调优原则编码为 agentic 工作流（对长尾 kernel 自动优化，成功率 >80%、开发时间降一个数量级）——在 10 个生产推荐模型（8–128 GPU、参数量 1 亿–3.6 万亿）上以相同 GPU 数与 batch size 取得对高度优化的 NVIDIA B200 基线 101.2% 的几何平均吞吐（对比直接移植基线最高 1.5 倍端到端提升），但 GEMM（尤其 TF32 模拟）仍落后 1.3–1.7 倍延迟，且功耗口径为 1000W 对 750W。

> **TL;DR:** The first hyperscaler report on productionizing AMD GPUs for large-scale training: Meta (with AMD) optimizes MI350X for DLRM training across the whole stack — exploiting its 1.6x HBM capacity (288GB vs 180GB) via model co-design (bigger batches, no activation checkpointing, embeddings in HBM: +20% training speedup and >0.1% normalized-entropy quality gain), replacing offline GEMM autotuning with the Origami analytical model (93% selection efficiency vs exhaustive tuning, extended to 8-XCD chiplets), and encoding kernel-tuning know-how into agentic workflows (>80% success rate on production Triton kernels, e.g., jagged flash attention 2.3x faster, 32ms→14ms, flipping MI350X from 2x slower to 15–20% faster than B200). On ten production recommendation models (8–128 GPUs, 100M–3.6T params) at matched batch size, MI350X reaches 101.2% of B200's geometric-mean examples/sec throughput (per-model range 82%–130%), and up to 1.5x over the directly-ported baseline — but GEMM latency remains 1.3–1.7x worse (emulated TF32 via 3 BF16 GEMMs, hipBLAS immaturity), transformer Inductor kernels are still 30% slower, and the comparison runs MI350X at 1000W vs B200 capped at 750W on the same air-cooled platform.

## 核心贡献

- **首个超大规模厂商的 AMD 生产级训练报告：** 在数万张 MI350X 上支撑生产 DLRM 训练（此前 AMD GPU 在 Meta 仅用于推理——数十万张规模）；论文覆盖 ROCm 库、kernel 调优、模型协同设计、可靠性/可观测性的完整生产化路径，所有优化已上游到开源 PyTorch、Triton、ROCm。
- **硬件-模型协同设计（利用 1.6× HBM 容量）：** 不做直接移植，而是扩 batch（朋友推荐模型 2×/4×/6× batch 分别 +26%/+46%/+51% 吞吐）、关闭激活重计算（1.3B GPT 模型无 AC 达 96.4 ex/s，B200 被迫用激进 AC 仅 71.5 ex/s，1.35× 差距纯由内存导致）、嵌入表从主机内存迁回 HBM（feed 排序模型 >10% 加速）并放宽并行策略（1K-GPU 广告基础模型 HSDP +6%）；整体协同设计 + kernel 优化合计 1.5× 端到端加速、20% 训练提速与 >0.1% NE 质量提升（16/20 层注意力分别 +0.08%/+0.11% NE）。
- **Origami 解析式选核模型：** 用闭式 roofline 类模型（矩阵指令延迟、逐级缓存命中率、issue-rate 界、流水线与 wave 量化尾效应）在微秒内预测最优 GEMM tile 配置，替代不可扩展的离线 autotuning（DLRM 每模型数千种矩阵形状）；对穷举调优达 93% 平均选择效率，并首次扩展到多芯片封装（8 XCD 的 L2 感知 workgroup swizzle 与 XCD 级层次化 tiling），已集成进 hipBLASLt 与 tritonBLAS 实现零预热运行时选核。
- **面向 AMD 微架构的 kernel 调优 + agentic 工作流：** 系统化五类 Triton 技巧（XCD 感知 PID swizzle、waves_per_eu 占用提示、matrix_instr_nonkdim=16、无 TMA 下浅流水 num_stages≈2、GeLU 快速数学）；针对多芯片原子操作瓶颈（高竞争下比 B200 慢 10× 以上）提出共享内存树归约与寄存器缓存（bounds-check kernel 在 10% 违例率下 4.26× 加速且反超 B200 1.75×；TBE 反向 1.87–3.32× 加速）；把这些原则编码为 KernelEvolve 之上的 agentic 技能，对生产 Triton kernel 成功率 >80%、开发时间降一个数量级，且技能可迁移至 MTIA/TPU/Trainium。
- **生产级评估与运营体系：** 10 个生产模型（8–128 GPU）等 batch 对比下 MI350X/B200 几何平均吞吐 101.2%（82%–130% 区间）；跨厂商数值一致性验证（训练损失差 <0.3%、NE 差 ~1e-4）；SDC（静默数据损坏）检测（checksum-and-replay + 全对投票隔离）比厂商现场健康工具多抓 2× 以上；OCP 功耗 excursion（50ms 窗口 1.36× TBP）带来 3–6% 端到端增益。

## Key Contributions

- **First hyperscaler report of production-scale training on AMD GPUs.** Prior Meta AMD deployment was inference-only (hundreds of thousands of GPUs; >60% of production model types AMD-compatible by footprint). This paper documents the full productionization of MI350X for DLRM training on tens of thousands of GPUs, with all optimizations upstreamed to open-source PyTorch, Triton, and ROCm.
- **Hardware-model co-design exploiting MI350X's 1.6x HBM capacity (288GB vs B200's 180GB).** Rather than porting models unchanged: increase batch sizes (friend-rec model: +26%/+46%/+51% throughput at 2x/4x/6x batch), disable activation checkpointing (1.3B GPT: 96.4 ex/s without AC vs B200's 71.5 ex/s with aggressive AC — a 1.35x gap attributable purely to memory), move embeddings from host to HBM (>10% speedup on a feed ranking model), and relax sharding (HSDP +6% on a 1K-GPU Ads foundation model for 750MB/GPU). Combined co-design plus kernel work: 1.5x total speedup, 20% training speedup and >0.1% normalized-entropy (NE) quality improvement (8→16 attention layers: +0.08% NE; →20 layers: +0.11% NE — a gain that normally takes months of model iteration).
- **Origami: an analytical GEMM kernel-selection model.** Replaces hipBLASLt's offline autotuning (impractical when DLRMs execute thousands of unique GEMM shapes per model) with a closed-form model spanning matrix-instruction latency, cascading cache-hit prediction per memory level (Eq. 3), an issue-rate bound, pipelined tile latency (Eq. 5), and wave-quantization tail effects — predicting optimal tile configs in microseconds with 93% average selection efficiency vs exhaustively autotuned kernels. It is the first such model extended to multi-chiplet GPUs (XCD-aware workgroup swizzling, XCD-scope L2 tiling), and is integrated into hipBLASLt and tritonBLAS for zero-warmup runtime selection.
- **Microarchitecture-aware kernel tuning encoded as agentic workflows.** Five AMD-specific Triton techniques (XCD-aware PID swizzle, waves_per_eu occupancy hints, matrix_instr_nonkdim=16 MFMA tiles, shallow num_stages≈2 pipelines absent TMA, sigmoid-based GeLU fast math). For the multi-chiplet atomic bottleneck (over 10x slower than B200 under high contention), hierarchical tree reduction and register caching: the bounds-check kernel gets 4.26x speedup at 10% violation rate and beats B200 by 1.75x; TBE backward gets 1.87–3.32x. Codifying these as skills on Meta's KernelEvolve achieves >80% success across dozens of production Triton kernels with order-of-magnitude less development time; the jagged-flash-attention case study turned a 2x slowdown into 15–20% faster than B200 (+25% end-to-end throughput). Skills transfer to MTIA (~10x, up to 65x on reduce-style kernels).
- **Production evaluation and operational machinery.** Cross-vendor numerical parity (training-loss difference within 0.3%, NE within ~1e-4 at multi-thousand-GPU scale); SDC containment via checksum-and-replay and all-pairs voting that catches >2x the SDC of the vendor's field-health tool; power-excursion boosting within OCP EDP limits (1.36x TBP over 50ms) worth 3–6% end-to-end.

## Methodology

The paper is a cross-stack systems study organized in four layers, each with its own measurement methodology:

1. **Roofline-driven ROCm library optimization (Section III).** hipBLASLt GEMM kernels gain Stream-K partitioning, space-filling-curve workgroup mapping (co-locating adjacent tiles on the same XCD to preserve private-L2 locality), and finer-grained runtime dispatch via Origami. Origami models a GEMM C=A×B as tiled execution: compute latency L_compute = N_MI · L_MI per tile; memory traffic cascaded through L2/LLC/HBM with a workgroup-sharing hit-rate model H = 1 − (M_C·M_T + N_C·N_T)/(M_C·N_C·(M_T+N_T)); an issue-rate bound for load/store throughput; and pipelined per-tile latency L_tile = L_prefetch + (K/K_T)·max(L_compute, L_mem) + L_store, with total latency L_total = ω·L_tile where ω captures wave quantization (Fig. 4 tail effect). All candidates are evaluated in microseconds. A separate OCP power-excursion study uses a synthetic microbenchmark sweeping GEMM duty ratio and kernel duration against the platform's 1.36x-TBP/50ms envelope. RCCL collectives were co-designed against a theoretical projection model from memory-subsystem/fabric/NIC simulations: collective-specific QP scaling, an LL64 protocol (56B payload + 8B flags in one 64B cache-line-atomic packet, replacing LL128's split stores), Direct AllGather, P2P batching, and warp-per-GPU communication with 512-thread blocks (46% VGPR reduction, doubled occupancy). rocSOLVER's Cuppen divide-and-conquer eigensolver (the bottleneck of distributed Shampoo) was reworked for precise CU allocation.

2. **Kernel tuning with agentic workflows (Section IV).** Two flagship Triton kernels were optimized incrementally with measured per-step attribution: GDPA (fused GeLU-attention + FFN, central to Kunlun/Interformer) and HSTU (hierarchical sequential transduction for generative recommendation), plus CUDA kernels (bounds check, GIS, TBE) whose atomics were replaced by shared-memory tree reduction (converting ~10^6 global atomics to ~10^3 per batch) and register caching. The agentic workflow runs three phases — benchmark & profile (rocprofv3, Proton), match & fix against a skill library, generate & validate with automatic numerical-equivalence rejection — built on KernelEvolve. PyTorch Inductor heuristics were re-derived for AMD (tl.range pipelining, native intrinsics, raised VGPR spill threshold 16→32, expanded autotuning with non-square tiles), evaluated on ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, and NanoGPT.

3. **Model-system co-design (Section V).** Controlled experiments isolating each HBM-exploiting lever: batch-size scaling curves with peak-memory tracking (Fig. 14/15a), activation-budget-vs-throughput tradeoffs on a 1.3B GPT model, FSDP ZeRO-3→HSDP migration at 1K-GPU scale, embedding placement moves, and model-quality scaling (attention-layer depth 8→16→20 with NE deltas).

4. **End-to-end production evaluation (Section VI).** Ten production models (A–J; 8 to 128 GPUs; ~100M to ~3.6T parameters; sequence lengths 256–24,600) trained on both MI350X and B200 fleets at equal GPU count and matched batch size, reporting examples/second and the geometric mean. A kernel-category trace analysis (GEMM / Triton Inductor / CUDA embedding / elementwise / collectives / hand-written Triton) attributes per-model gaps, with an explicit caveat about multi-stream overlap masking collective latency. Reliability, observability, and power management are reported as first-class operational metrics.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline parity (Table III, ten production DLRMs, 8–128 GPUs, matched batch size):** MI350X/B200 geometric-mean throughput = **101.2%**, with per-model spread from **82% (Model A, 8 GPU, TF32-dominated)** to **130% (Model J, 24 GPU)**; models B and C reach **114%** and **121%**. With MI350X additionally allowed larger batches, Model D goes 98%→102%, Model E 94%→110%, Model F 92%→95%.
- **Origami selection efficiency:** **93% average selection efficiency relative to exhaustively autotuned GEMM kernels** across datatypes and production matrix shapes, with prediction completing in microseconds (nanoseconds per candidate) — replacing lookup tables that cannot cover the thousands of unique GEMM shapes per DLRM.
- **vs the directly-ported baseline:** the full optimization stack delivers **up to 1.5x end-to-end training throughput** (abstract); the co-design package alone (batch increase + AC removal + embedding placement) yields **1.26x**, and combined kernel + co-design reaches 1.5x on a feed ranking model.
- **GDPA Triton kernel (GEM configs, batch=2048, d_model=256, seq=500, sparsity=0.68):** forward latency **1.47ms → 0.59ms (−60.0%)** and throughput **58.4 → 145.9 TFLOP/s (2.5x)** after GeLU fast math + waves_per_eu=3 + MFMA nonkdim=16 + PID swizzle; backward **3.67ms → 2.17ms (−40.9%)**; the optimized kernel beats B200's best Triton configuration on MI350X.
- **HSTU Triton kernel (sparsity 0.95):** forward **3.22x speedup, 45.0 → 144.8 TFLOP/s**; backward **1.21x (60.2 → 72.6 TFLOP/s)**; averaged over sequence lengths 256–4096, MI350X is **1.22x faster than B200 forward and 1.80x backward (1.69x combined)**, widening at longer sequences.
- **Atomic-heavy CUDA kernels:** on the bounds-check kernel (30 tables, 13.8M indices), MI350X forward latency degrades **8.5x** from 0%→20% violation rate with naive atomics (B200 degrades 3.3x); with tree reduction, **4.26x speedup at 10% violations (2.1ms vs 9.0ms)** and 4.34x at 20%, ending **1.75x faster than B200**. TBE backward delivers **1.87–3.32x** speedup on MI350X (FP16 2.5–3.3x, FP32 1.9–2.8x across 12 production configs) and is **1.35–1.96x faster than B200** on uniform (α=1.0) distributions, comparable (0.83–1.16x) on Zipf (α=1.15).
- **Agentic kernel optimization:** jagged flash attention backward was **2x slower than B200**; the agent applied register-accumulated dK/dV with a single tl.store, achieving **2.3x (32ms → 14ms)**, flipping to **15–20% faster than B200** and **+25% end-to-end training throughput**; across dozens of production Triton kernels the success rate exceeds **80%** with ~10x lower development time.
- **RCCL collectives:** narrowed from **2x slower than NCCL to within 10–15%** of NCCL performance; LL64 gives **2x AllGather/ReduceScatter and 1.2–1.4x AllReduce**; P2P batching cut AllToAll latency **40% at 128-GPU scale**; occupancy work gave **+63% ReduceScatter and +48% AllReduce**. Measured AllToAll reaches 93.7–99.6% of theoretical bound for 1GiB messages (64–128 GPU groups).
- **Power-excursion boost:** up to **1.33x** GEMM speedup on synthetic duty-ratio sweeps (50ms window, 1.36x TBP platform limit, strictly within OCP EDP), translating to **3–6% end-to-end** gains in production DLRM training.
- **Co-design quality and throughput:** 1.3B GPT with no activation checkpointing on MI350X: **96.4 examples/s vs 71.5 on B200 (1.35x)**, where B200 needs AC budget ≤0.35 just to fit; friend-rec model **+26%/+46%/+51%** at 2x/4x/6x batch; social ranking model at 2x batch beats the B200 same-batch baseline by **12–15%** (B200 OOMs at 2x batch); attention depth 8→16→20 layers gives **+0.08%/+0.11% NE**.
- **Inductor kernels:** ResNet-50 reduction kernels **−11.7% latency, reaching 1.16x of B200**; DenseNet-121 total kernel time **−4.2%** (pointwise +9.2%); NanoGPT **−3.4%** — but transformer Inductor kernels remain **~30% slower** than B200.
- **Remaining gap:** GEMM operations consistently favor B200 with **1.3x–1.7x higher MI350X latency**; MI350X can be **>10x slower than B200 on atomic-heavy kernels at high contention** (before tree-reduction rewriting). Cross-vendor numerical parity: training loss within **0.3%**, NE within **~1e-4**.

## Authors & Credibility

A 62-author joint Meta–AMD team led by Haoyu Zhang (Meta) and Arshad Rahman (AMD), spanning kernel/compiler specialists (CK Luk, Muhammad Osama, Ryan Swann — the tritonBLAS authors), ROCm library owners, and Meta's infrastructure leadership (Chunqiang Tang, co-author of Zion/TorchRec/RecShard lines of work; Dan Rabinovitsj, who announced Meta's AMD inference deployment). The scale of the author list and the production data (tens of thousands of GPUs, ten shipping models, multi-thousand-GPU jobs, reliability telemetry) is credible in a way academic evaluations cannot match, and the claims are internally consistent (e.g., per-model throughput matches the kernel-category breakdown, and the Limitations paragraph volunteers the GEMM/TF32 weakness). Contributions are stated as upstreamed to PyTorch, Triton, and ROCm, which is publicly checkable in principle. Incentive caveat: Meta and AMD both benefit from a favorable AMD-training narrative (vendor diversification is an explicit strategic goal), so the workload suite and comparison conditions deserve scrutiny — though the matched-batch, equal-GPU-count protocol and the strawman disclaimer for B200 ("received equal or greater optimization") are fair-minded choices.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel as a systems report — this is the first hyperscaler account of production *training* (not inference) on AMD GPUs, and Origami's chiplet-aware analytical selection (93% of exhaustive autotuning with zero warmup) is a real contribution beyond prior lookup-table autotuning in hipBLASLt/Tensile. The atomic-contention analysis on multi-chiplet caches and the tree-reduction fix are well-grounded microarchitecture work. Individually, however, several ingredients are known (Stream-K, swizzling, tree reduction of atomics, power excursions per OCP EDP, KernelEvolve itself is cited [47]); the paper's value is synthesis at production scale, not algorithmic breakthrough.
- **Rigor:** Strong methodology for a production study — matched batch sizes and GPU counts, per-kernel-category trace attribution with an explicit caveat about multi-stream overlap masking collectives, incremental per-technique attribution in Figs. 9–10, and numerical-parity checks (loss within 0.3%, NE within ~1e-4) that rule out silent divergence. Weaknesses: no variance/error bars on any throughput number; the with-co-design (larger-batch) numbers are given for only 3 of the 10 models; and Table III's models are anonymized (A–J) with only coarse parameter counts, so nothing is independently reproducible.
- **Reproducibility:** Low-to-moderate. Library work (Origami, hipBLASLt, RCCL, rocSOLVER, Inductor changes) is upstreamed and in principle verifiable; but the headline evaluation (proprietary DLRMs, private fleets, production traces) cannot be replicated, and no public benchmark (e.g., MLPerf DLRM-v3, which the paper itself references as the external standard) is run as an anchor. Academic groups cannot reproduce the SDC/telemetry claims at all.
- **Significance:** High for the systems/infrastructure community — a concrete, quantified playbook for second-vendor GPU adoption, with reusable principles (co-design to the memory hierarchy, analytical selection, agentic tuning) demonstrated to transfer to MTIA. The 101.2% parity claim, if it holds for LLMs, would materially change accelerator economics.
- **Red flags:** (1) **Asymmetric power envelope** — MI350X runs at 1000W while B200 is capped at 750W on the same air-cooled platform; per-watt B200 clearly wins, and the paper's counter that power is a small share of capital-dominated TCO is asserted without any TCO numbers. (2) **Workload selection favors the challenger** — DLRMs are exactly the workload class that rewards HBM capacity (huge embeddings) and tolerates GEMM weakness; B200's scale-up bandwidth edge (900 vs 538 GB/s, NVSwitch vs xGMI mesh) matters most for LLM training, which is deferred to "future work" after only a >2,000-GPU teaser. (3) **Geomean masks heterogeneity** — parity is carried by models B/C/J (114/121/130%) while A sits at 82% (TF32 emulation) and F/G at 92%; any workload resembling Model A sees a real regression. (4) The abstract's "up to 1.5x" is versus their own directly-ported baseline, not versus B200 — a reader skimming the abstract could conflate the two. (5) The equal-batch kernel analysis "intentionally underutilizes the MI350X," which is disclosed but means the kernel-level Fig. 16 story and the end-to-end Table III story are not measured under identical conditions.

## Strengths

- Unmatched scale and realism: production DLRMs up to ~3.6T parameters and 128 GPUs, tens of thousands of GPUs in fleet, with reliability (SDC quarantine catching >2x the vendor tool's SDC) and observability treated as first-class citizens rather than afterthoughts.
- Origami is an elegant piece of engineering: a microseconds-cost closed-form model that handles the chiplet hierarchy (XCD-scope L2 tiles, swizzled workgroup IDs) and achieves 93% of exhaustive tuning — a clean kill of the offline-autotuning database problem, integrated into both hipBLASLt and tritonBLAS.
- Honest incremental attribution: every optimization claim (GDPA's four techniques, Inductor's per-category gains, batch-scaling curves) is decomposed step-by-step, making the paper usable as a manual for practitioners.
- The agentic-workflow section is unusually concrete about failure containment (automatic numerical-equivalence rejection) and about generalization (skills ported to MTIA with ~10x speedups), avoiding the "LLM magic" hand-waving common in agentic-tuning papers.
- The limitations section volunteers the two most damaging facts (GEMM 1.3–1.7x latency gap; TF32 emulation) instead of burying them.

## Limitations

- GEMM remains the structural weakness: 1.3–1.7x higher latency than B200, driven by emulated TF32 (three BF16 GEMMs) and hipBLAS immaturity on irregular matrix shapes; transformer Inductor kernels are still ~30% slower, indicating substantial optimization headroom remains.
- The atomic bottleneck on multi-chiplet GPUs (>10x slowdown under contention) is real and must be patched kernel-by-kernel — tree reduction and register caching are effective but per-kernel engineering (manual or agentic), and ~20% of production kernels fail the agentic workflow's >80% success rate.
- No LLM-training analysis despite MI350X fleets (>2,000 GPUs) already running LLM jobs; the B200 scale-up-bandwidth advantage is precisely where MI350X would be expected to struggle, so parity for DLRMs may not extrapolate.
- Evaluation is proprietary and un-reproducible: anonymized models A–J, no public benchmark anchor (no MLPerf run), no statistical variance, no TCO quantification behind the "MI350X's lower TCO drives provisioning" claim.
- Power asymmetry (1000W vs 750W deployed limits) plus different cooling paths complicates the "same-generation flagship" framing; per-watt and per-dollar comparisons are promised via TCO but never delivered numerically.

## Relevance

对关注异构算力、GPU 体系结构与大规模训练基础设施的读者高度相关：这是首个超大规模厂商公开的 AMD GPU 生产级训练全栈优化报告，其中的解析式选核模型（Origami）、多芯片封装原子操作优化、agentic kernel 调优工作流以及"按内存层级重塑模型配置"的协同设计原则，可直接迁移到自建集群的多供应商策略与性价比评估；对做推荐系统训练系统（TorchRec/FSDP/2D 并行）的工程师也是一份不可多得的生产实践手册。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does MI350X vs B200 parity hold for LLM pretraining, where per-GPU scale-up bandwidth (538 vs 900 GB/s) and NVSwitch's all-to-all fabric should matter far more than HBM capacity — does the deferred LLM analysis confirm the >2,000-GPU teaser?
- Can Origami's 93% selection efficiency be closed to ~100% with lightweight online profiling hybrids, and how does it behave on fully dynamic shapes (e.g., jagged/variable sequence lengths) at runtime?
- What is the actual TCO model (capex, power, reliability, operational overhead) behind Meta's provisioning decision — and how does the answer change when B200 is run at its 1000W HGX design point with liquid cooling instead of the 750W air-cooled cap used here?
- Does sustained operation near the 1.36x-TBP excursion envelope accelerate hardware wear-out or raise SDC rates, and is the 3–6% end-to-end excursion gain stable across duty-cycle drift in production?
- Why does the agentic kernel workflow fail on the ~20% of kernels, and are those failures concentrated in specific pattern classes (e.g., atomics that cannot be tree-reduced, cross-XCD reductions) that would motivate new hardware or ISA support in CDNA 5?
- Do the co-design levers (batch scaling, AC removal, ZeRO-3→HSDP) compose additively on LLMs as they did on the feed ranking model (1.26x combined), or does memory-bandwidth contention bound the gains?
