Why This Matters
A concrete, mechanistically transparent inductive bias — dynamic divisive gain control — that lets standard RNNs learn genuine continuous representations and low-dimensional dynamics without explicit factorization.

01

How the RDNN works

A continuous-time stochastic system with two coupled non-negative populations: a principal excitatory state R divided element-wise by an auxiliary inhibitory pool G that supplies dynamic gain control.

The inhibitory pool G tracks R via Wf(R), proportionally scaling the denominator. This stabilizes explosive positive feedback while preserving relative activation patterns — the algebraic heart of the paper's claim.

All weights are constrained positive (Softplus / sigmoid parameterization) so the network respects Dale's law as a positive dynamical system. Decoding is linear: y_t = W_out R_t + b_out.

Two tasks isolate different memory regimes: Angular Velocity Integration (input-driven updating onto a ring) and Memory-guided Saccade (autonomous maintenance through a zero-input delay).

τ_R dR = (−R + (Jf(R) + I(t)) ⊘ (η + G)) dt + σ_R dW_R τ_G dG = (−G + Wf(R)) dt + σ_G dW_G
⊘ = element-wise (shunting) division · f(·) = ReLU or Tanh
Euler–Maruyama discretization · Adam, 100 epochs, batch 64
Gaussian state noise σ=0.1 injected per step · 5 seeds
Analyses: exact-Jacobian fixed-point classification (ϵ=10⁻³), Floquet limit-cycle, complex-eigenvalue spectra, uniform-flow norms, 99%-energy effective rank.

Subtractive Ablation

Replaces the divisive gate with R ← max(0, R + α(Jf(R)+I−G)) to isolate division's role.

Explicit Low-Rank Variant

Factorizes J=J₁J₂, W=W₁W₂ with bottleneck ranks r ∈ {8, 16, 24, 32}.

Cross-Architecture Tests

ORGaNICs, Neural ODE, Autoformer, and a 2-D torus (Double Angular Velocity Integration, T²).

Strengths & Limitations, side by side

+ Strengths

  • Clean, minimal model that isolates one biophysical operation (dynamic division); every claim is supported by multiple converging dynamical-systems analyses.
  • BPTT gradient-attenuation derivation and the higher-rank convergence anomaly are mechanistically illuminating and transferable to low-rank RNN design.
  • Honest, extensive appendices (A–P, 46 pages): ORGaNICs, Neural ODE, Autoformer, T² torus, parameter counts, long-horizon trade-offs, candid subtractive-vs-divisive ablation.
  • ~6× effective-rank compression and ~90% manifold reliability with no explicit low-rank bottleneck — while cheaper (4H² FLOPs) than every baseline.

− Limitations

  • Toy tasks only. 1-D ring / 2-D torus with short horizons — no navigation, robotics, or large-scale engineering benchmarks.
  • Correlation, not proof. The gradient-attenuation → low-rank link is empirical; "does not formally minimize a global rank metric."
  • BPTT is biologically implausible. Whether Hebbian or predictive-coding rules reproduce the same topology is left open.
  • DN studied in isolation. No synergy with subtractive thresholding, synaptic scaling, or other homeostatic mechanisms.
  • Long-horizon drift. The flat manifold suffers asymptotic random-walk drift; subtractive discretization may actually win at very long timescales.
  • Awaiting neural data. Predicted eigenvalue spectra and rank claims await empirical validation with large-scale population recordings.
04

Open questions for further work

  1. Does the rank self-compression and normal-hyperbolicity persist on higher-dimensional engineering tasks — 2-D path integration for robotics, continuous control, or spatiotemporal forecasting beyond ring/torus topologies?
  2. Can biologically-plausible learning rules (Hebbian, predictive-coding, feedback alignment) reproduce the same spectral gap and low-rank structure — or is the effect an artifact of gradient-based optimization?
  3. How does combining divisive normalization with subtractive thresholding (mimicking cortical coexistence) shape the short-horizon fidelity vs. long-horizon drift trade-off surfaced in Appendix N?
  4. Would end-to-end-trained explicit low-rank RNNs (à la Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic 2018) close the effective-rank gap with the RDNN — weakening the claim that division is uniquely responsible for low-dimensionality?
  5. Can the activity-dependent gradient-scaling mechanism be ported as a normalization-style module into transformers or modern SSMs (e.g., Mamba) to encourage low-dimensional continuous representations there?
