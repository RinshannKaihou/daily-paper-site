Analyzing Speech Condition Effects in Dysarthric ASR: A Layer-wise Probing Study

Darwin Jelestin Muthu·Navya Gupta·Wei Lin Tay·Zhengchen Zhang·Daniel Wang Zhengkui·Rong Tong

Singapore Institute of Technology · CDSD corpus · Whisper-small · OmniVoice TTS · Accepted to APSIPA ASC 2026

Key metric · CER on dysarthric speech

71.26% CER

Single-layer LoRA at encoder layer 7 — within a 3.5% rel. of full 12-layer adaptation.

Full encoder LoRA: 68.85% CER · Layers 5–8 subset: 70.56% (2.48% rel.) · Baseline (no adapt): 105.08% CER

Why this matters

A reusable "probe → layer-select → LoRA" loop, showing middle-layer adapters are the most parameter-efficient targets for low-resource Mandarin dysarthric ASR.

Critical edge

Single backbone (Whisper-small) and single corpus (CDSD); the layer-hierarchy claims are not validated on UASpeech/TORGO or on larger Whisper variants.

01 · The map

A task-dependent hierarchy inside Whisper's encoder

Frozen Whisper-small · 12 encoder blocks

Activation intensity per probe, layer by layer · darker = stronger signal

Boundary (Probe A, F1) Phoneme identity (Probe B, PER) Difficulty (Probe C, Macro-F1) · LoRA target

Boundary Weak at every layer for dysarthric speech — conditions are sharply separated and stay flat through the encoder. No layer recovers strong boundary structure for atypical speech.

Identity Matures in the middle, plateaus after layer 6. Tone-sensitive PER stays higher than tone-agnostic — Mandarin tone is a persistent error source.

Difficulty Coded deepest — Macro-F1 near chance through early layers, climbs steeply beyond layer 5, peaks at layer 12.

Divergence d–u representational gap grows with depth — disordered articulation distorts high-level, not low-level acoustic, representations.

02 · Methodology

Transcript-matched contrasts, three probes, probe-guided LoRA

Framework

Three conditions, one transcript

For each dysarthric utterance x_d, two synthetic counterparts share the identical lexical content:

  • d — original dysarthric
  • r — speaker-conditioned zero-shot TTS (OmniVoice), preserving speaker & atypical acoustics
  • u — unconditioned TTS, neutral speaker

This isolates pathology-induced articulatory distortion from speaker identity and synthesis artifacts.

Probes · 0–12

Three complementary probes

  • A · Boundary — frame-level linear probe, 4-frame context, F1 (MFA labels @ 20 ms)
  • B · Phoneme — CTC probe (bottleneck 128, residual conv), tone-sensitive & tone-agnostic PER
  • C · Difficulty — linear softmax over mean-pooled states, Macro-F1 (5-fold CV; tiers from Whisper-large CER)

Probing restricted to the encoder — motor-speech disorders degrade acoustic-articulatory realization.

PEFT bridge

Probe-guided LoRA selection

  • Adapters on q/k/v/out_proj of selected layers
  • r=64, α=128, dropout 0.1
  • AdamW, lr 3e-5, batch 128, 6 epochs, cosine
  • Sweep: single-layer (1–12), subsets (1–4, 5–8, 9–12), full

Dataset: CDSD · 133 h · 44 speakers · ~55,850 utt · speaker-disjoint 70/15/15.

A 68-point gap, isolated

Identical transcripts: dysarthric 105.08% vs unconditioned TTS 36.63% CER — acoustic-phonetic factors alone account for a massive share of recognition failure.

The 9–12 trap

Adapting layers 9–12 steadily worsens dysarthric CER (rising to 80.28% at layer 12) — the opposite of TTS, whose best layer is 9.

Layer 6 identity plateau

PER drops fast through early-to-middle layers and plateaus after layer 6; tone gap is largest for dysarthric speech.

Difficulty lives at layer 12

Macro-F1 is near chance early, spikes past layer 5, peaks at layer 12 — recognition difficulty is encoded deepest.

04 · Two-sided read

Strengths & limitations, side by side

Strengths what lands

  • Clean three-condition control. Transcript held invariant — isolates articulation from speaker/synthesis.
  • Three granularities. Boundary / phoneme / difficulty probes give a richer profile than single-task probing.
  • Closed the loop. Probing insights drive a concrete PEFT recipe — analysis → adaptation, end-to-end.
  • Honest framing. TTS contrasts explicitly called "approximations"; absolute CER acknowledged as non-deployable.
  • Reproducible detail. Probe hyperparameters, splits, MFA toolchain, LoRA config all named.

Limitations what's thin

  • One backbone, one corpus. Whisper-small + CDSD only; no UASpeech/TORGO or larger Whisper.
  • No PEFT baselines. AdaLoRA, self-training, Perceiver-prompt cited but not compared.
  • TTS confound. d vs TTS cannot fully separate articulation from synthesis artifacts.
  • Absolute CER ≥ 69%. Analysis contribution, not a deployable system.
  • Model-specific difficulty target. Tiers from Whisper-large CER, not clinical intelligibility scores.
  • No per-tone breakdown. Tone reported only as sensitive vs agnostic aggregates.
05 · Open threads

Questions for further exploration

Does the hierarchy (boundary weak / identity mid / difficulty deep) replicate on English corpora (UASpeech, TORGO) and on Whisper-medium / large-v3?
How do middle-layer LoRA gains compare to AdaLoRA, self-training, and Perceiver-prompt under matched parameter budgets on CDSD?
Would boundary-targeted auxiliary losses (strengthening the weak boundary signal everywhere) combine productively with middle-layer LoRA?
Is the d–r divergence profile sensitive to the zero-shot TTS engine — would a different TTS, or voice-conversion reference, change it?
Can the difficulty probe be re-grounded against clinical severity scores (intelligibility ratings) instead of Whisper-large CER?
Does 9–12 counterproductivity persist if adapters go on feed-forward (not self-attention), or if the decoder is jointly adapted?
