> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 CoKL（Correctness-Conditioned KL Regularization），把 RL 后训练中的 KL 约束从完整输出分布缩小到"经过验证的正确响应"的条件分布上，从理论上证明它能把总正确率与正确响应内的相对分布解耦（不像 full-policy KL 会产生严格的最优正确率缺口），在 Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B 上的 Math→Chat 连续后训练中 Overall 得分分别比最强基线高 +2.37 / +2.42 / +1.40。

> **TL;DR:** CoKL replaces full-policy KL regularization in RL-based LLM post-training with a forward-KL constraint applied only to the correctness-conditioned response distribution (verified-correct responses only). It provably decouples total correctness probability from the relative allocation among correct responses, avoiding the strict optimal correctness gap that full forward/reverse KL induces under an imperfect reference policy. On the Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B Math→Chat continual post-training benchmark (Nemotron-Math-v2 → WildChat; evaluated on MATH-Val, MATH500, OlympiadBench, WildChat-Val), CoKL beats the strongest baseline (Correct-only Forward-KL / Full Forward-KL) by +2.37, +2.42, and +1.40 Overall points, while GRPO-without-KL collapses to 50.00 and Full Reverse-KL on Qwen3-4B goes to -130.93 due to catastrophic forgetting.

## 核心贡献

- 提出"按正确性条件化的 KL 正则化"这一更精细的能力保持原则：约束只在 verifier 判定为正确的响应子集上进行，不再锚定错误响应或总正确率。
- 把该原则实例化为 CoKL 方法（基于 forward KL），给出有限样本分组训练目标，并集成进 GRPO（通过 reference-correct buffer + 联合目标 L_total = L_GRPO + β·L_CoKL）。
- 给出总体层级（population-level）理论分析：Theorem 1 证明 CoKL 把总正确率 Z_π 与正确响应条件分布解耦；Theorem 2 证明 full-policy forward/reverse KL 在参考策略不完美时会强制产生严格最优正确率缺口 Z*（严格介于 q 和 1 之间且随 β 单调下降），而 CoKL 的全局最优可以直接达到 Z=1。
- 在可控多解 RL 环境（contextual bandit）和 Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B 的连续后训练（Math→Chat）两种设定下验证，证明 CoKL 在能力保持与新任务适应之间取得了更优的折中。

## Key Contributions

- A more precise preservation principle for LLM post-training that narrows the KL constraint from the full response distribution to the conditional distribution over verified-correct responses, leaving incorrect responses and total correctness mass unconstrained.
- An instantiation called CoKL using forward KL divergence, with a practical finite-group surrogate (on-policy and off-policy with sequence-level clipped importance correction), integrated into GRPO via a reference-correct buffer and the joint objective L_total = L_GRPO + β·L_CoKL.
- A population-level theoretical analysis showing that CoKL decouples total correctness probability from the correctness-conditioned distribution (Theorem 1), whereas full-policy forward and reverse KL induce a strict optimal correctness gap Z* strictly between q and 1 that shrinks toward q as β grows (Theorem 2); CoKL's global optimum reaches Z=1.
- Empirical validation in both a controlled multi-solution RL bandit environment (showing higher correct-mode coverage@64 and smaller conditional KL at matched correctness) and in continual Math→Chat post-training across Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B (highest aggregate Overall score across all three scales).

## Methodology

CoKL starts from the standard decomposition of full-policy forward KL into three terms: a Bernoulli KL over the correctness event, a KL between correctness-conditioned distributions, and a KL between incorrectness-conditioned distributions. The authors argue only the middle term is necessary for preserving useful behaviors; the Bernoulli term conflicts with the post-training reward objective (it pulls Z_θ toward Z_ref) and the incorrect-conditioned term anchors the policy to erroneous modes.

They define CoKL as L_CoKL(x) = D_KL(π+ref(·|x) ∥ π+θ(·|x)), the forward KL between correctness-conditioned reference and current policies. Expanding log π+θ(y|x) = log π_θ(y|x) − log Z_θ(x) yields the equivalent form (up to θ-independent terms): L_CoKL(x) = −E_{y∼π+ref}[log π_θ(y|x)] + log Z_θ(x). The first term is the reference-side anchor (pushes toward reference-supported correct responses), and the second is a normalization-correction term that removes the implicit correctness-mass optimization embedded in naive correct-only forward-KL replay.

For training, they implement an off-policy clipped surrogate using self-normalized empirical weights α_ij (over reference samples) and γ_ij (over current-policy samples with sequence-level importance ratio ρ̃_ij clipped to [1−ε_IS, 1+ε_IS]). The full training objective is L_total = L_GRPO + β·L_CoKL. Reference responses for the regularization set are pre-generated once and filtered to keep only prompts with at least one verified-correct reference response (forming the reference-correct buffer D+); current-policy correction terms come from fresh KL-minibatch rollouts. When a current-policy group has zero correct responses, the surrogate gracefully falls back to the reference-only term, acting as an implicit adaptive gate that strengthens recovery pressure exactly when current correctness is low.

## Results & Highlights

- **Continual Math→Chat post-training (Table 1, dual-anchor normalized Overall score):** CoKL achieves 86.47 / 88.28 / 74.90 on Qwen3-0.6B / 1.7B / 4B respectively, beating the strongest baseline by +2.37 (vs Correct-only Forward-KL 84.10 on 0.6B), +2.42 (vs Correct-only Forward-KL 85.86 on 1.7B), and +1.40 (vs Full Forward-KL 73.50 on 4B).
- **Catastrophic forgetting without KL:** GRPO w/o KL collapses to Overall = 50.00 on all three scales (0% retention of math capabilities, scoring 0.00 on MATH-Val/MATH500/Olympiad Math after chat-stage training), and Full Reverse-KL on Qwen3-4B reaches Overall = −130.93 (MATH-Val −186.44), demonstrating the failure mode CoKL is designed to avoid.
- **Controlled multi-solution bandit (Figures 2–3):** At matched final correctness P_corr ≈ 0.96, CoKL maintains higher correct-mode coverage@64 and substantially lower conditional KL D_KL(π+ref ∥ π+θ) than forward/reverse/JS KL throughout training, directly verifying that it preserves correct-mode diversity while other regularizers sacrifice either correctness or coverage.
- **Ablation on the two loss terms (Table 2, Qwen3-1.7B):** Removing the reference-anchor term1 collapses Overall to 52.34 (severe math forgetting); removing the normalization-correction term2 reduces CoKL to Correct-only Forward-KL (Overall 85.86 vs CoKL's 88.28), confirming both terms are necessary.
- **Batch-level correctness floor ablation (Table 3):** Adding an explicit one-sided correctness-mass constraint (CoKL+L_floor) does not help and slightly hurts — on Qwen3-1.7B Overall drops to 87.71 (vs 88.28) and on Qwen3-4B to 71.64 (vs 74.90), supporting the claim that forgetting acts through drift within the correctness-conditioned distribution rather than proportional loss of correctness mass.
- **KL batch size and β sensitivity (Figure 4, Qwen3-1.7B):** Increasing KL batch from 8 to 16 substantially improves math retention; β=1.0 is optimal (β=10 restricts chat adaptation, β=0.1 weakens math retention).

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from Meituan (Li Wang, Xiaodong Lu, Xiaohan Wang, Jiajun Chai, Wei Lin, Guojun Yin) and Nanyang Technological University (Tianhao Peng). Two equal-contribution first authors and corresponding authors from both institutions. Code is released at https://github.com/Lumina04/CoKL. The work is grounded in formal decomposition of KL divergence with two theorems and full proofs in appendices, and evaluated across three model scales (0.6B/1.7B/4B) plus a controlled bandit environment — a reasonably thorough empirical setup.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. The decomposition of full-policy KL into Bernoulli + correct-conditional + incorrect-conditional components is standard, but the deliberate decision to retain only the middle term, formalized as CoKL with a population-level decoupling theorem, is a clean and well-motivated idea distinct from concurrent verifier-aware methods (UCPO forces uniform allocation over correct responses; Kruszewski et al. filter incorrect responses but preserve relative reference probabilities without conditioning the current policy; DSDR adds trajectory/token-level diversity regularization). CoKL uniquely conditions both policies on correctness and regularizes D_KL(π+ref ∥ π+θ).
- **Rigor:** Strong theoretical grounding (Theorems 1 and 2 with full proofs), plus ablations isolating each loss term and the floor variant. The dual-anchor normalization scheme for combining exact-match math scores with reward-model chat scores is a reasonable methodological choice, though it obscures raw absolute numbers (unnormalized results only in Appendix E.5).
- **Reproducibility:** Code is public. However, reproducing requires generating a reference-correct buffer (G reference rollouts per prompt from π_ref before training), which adds non-trivial rollout cost not borne by simple full-KL baselines that reuse on-policy samples. Hyperparameters (β=1.0, KL batch size 8, ε_IS clipping, 50K math examples, WildChat with Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B-v0.2) are stated.
- **Significance:** Real but moderate scope. The Math→Chat two-stage continual protocol is narrow; broader continual sequences (e.g., code→math→safety→chat) and more recent base models would strengthen claims. The framework is currently tied to binary verifier feedback — the continuous-score thresholding extension is mentioned but not empirically validated.
- **Red flags / weak baselines:** The empirical baseline pool is limited to GRPO-family methods (GRPO w/o KL, Full Reverse-KL, Full Forward-KL, Correct-only Reverse-KL, Correct-only Forward-KL). There is no head-to-head empirical comparison with the most directly related concurrent methods discussed in Related Work — Kruszewski et al. (2025), UCPO (Lochab et al. 2026), or DSDR (Wan et al. 2026) — even though these are the methods CoKL most needs to differentiate from. Full Reverse-KL on Qwen3-4B producing Overall = −130.93 (a negative normalized score) suggests the baseline may be pathological rather than competitive at that scale, somewhat inflating CoKL's relative margin. The reference-correct buffer requires π_ref to produce at least one correct response per prompt; prompts with zero correct reference responses are filtered out, which could bias the regularization set toward easy prompts and limit applicability when the reference policy is weak on the target domain. Evaluation is restricted to models up to 4B parameters; behavior at 7B+ is untested.

## Strengths

- Clean theoretical motivation with explicit decomposition of full-policy KL and a decoupling theorem that crisply distinguishes CoKL from full forward/reverse KL.
- The two-term loss is well-justified by ablation: removing term1 causes catastrophic forgetting (Overall 52.34), removing term2 reduces to Correct-only Forward-KL.
- Consistent improvement across three model scales (0.6B/1.7B/4B), not a single-scale cherry-pick.
- Controlled bandit experiment directly visualizes the mechanism (coverage@64 and conditional KL dynamics) that the LLM experiments can only measure indirectly.
- Honest negative result on the correctness-floor variant, supporting the central claim that mass-coupling is the wrong lever.

## Limitations

- The two-stage Math→Chat continual protocol is a narrow evaluation of "capability retention"; multi-stage sequences and other domain pairs are untested.
- Reference-correct buffer adds rollout cost and requires π_ref to produce correct responses on the regularization prompts, filtering out prompts where the reference is uniformly wrong.
- Binary verifier feedback only; the continuous-score threshold extension is described but not empirically validated.
- No empirical comparison with the closest concurrent methods (Kruszewski et al., UCPO, DSDR), only conceptual contrast.
- Model scale capped at 4B; larger-scale behavior unknown.
- Tied to GRPO; integration with DAPO/GSPO is mentioned conceptually but not demonstrated.

## Relevance

这项工作直接关系到 LLM 强化学习后训练中"如何在不损失已有能力的前提下学习新任务"这一核心问题，对关注 RLVR/GRPO 训练动态、灾难性遗忘、以及连续多阶段后训练（math→chat 等）的研究者具有较高参考价值；其条件化 KL 的思路也可启发其他基于 verifier 信号的正则化设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does CoKL behave on longer continual sequences (e.g., math → code → safety → chat), and does the reference-correct buffer need to be re-generated per stage?
- What happens at 7B–70B scale, where the cost of the reference-correct buffer and the diversity of correct modes both grow substantially?
- Can the continuous-score threshold τ be tuned online rather than fixed, and does this change the trade-off vs binary verification?
- How does CoKL interact with non-GRPO optimizers (DAPO, GSPO), and does the decoupling theorem still translate to practical gains under their different normalization?
- A direct head-to-head empirical comparison with UCPO, DSDR, and Kruszewski et al. would clarify which verifier-aware regularization principle dominates under matched compute.
- Does the implicit adaptive gate (zero-correct fallback) introduce unwanted bias on hard prompts where the current policy rarely produces correct responses early in training?
