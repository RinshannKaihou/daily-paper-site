> **一句话总结：** 本文从确定性轨道的视角研究扩散模型中的伪随机输入,用一个小 MLP 预测轨道下一值,以及用一个将真实图像替换为 IID 随机张量的"扩散探针",证明在统一边缘分布与均值方差校准之后,不同伪随机轨道仍能被网络学习/利用并显著改变 MNIST/CIFAR-10 的训练损失与生成质量,且探针损失与真实数据扩散损失在 IID 归一化后近似服从幂律(MNIST 指数 3.06,CIFAR-10 指数 4.79)。

> **TL;DR:** Treating PRNG outputs as deterministic orbits rather than as interchangeable noise, the authors show on MNIST and CIFAR-10 that after marginal uniformization, Gaussianization, and mean/variance calibration, different pseudorandom orbits still produce diffusion training losses ranging from 0.0049 (L3.7) to 0.0212 (IID) on MNIST and 0.0040 to 0.0300 on CIFAR-10, and generation grades that collapse from IID-level 6 down to blank/grade-0 outputs; both an MLP next-value predictor and a novel "diffusion probe" (real images replaced by online IID tensors) correlate strongly with human generation grades (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.92–0.94 vs. IID baseline), and the IID-normalized probe loss and real-data diffusion loss follow an empirical power law d = p^ρ with non-overlapping exponents ρ_MNIST = 3.06 [2.82, 3.31] vs. ρ_CIFAR = 4.79 [4.39, 5.19].

## 核心贡献

- **将伪随机流重新表述为可学习输入**:把每一步优化的实现损失写作 L̂_t(θ_t; D, R_t),证明梯度直接依赖于具体的伪随机值,从而伪随机轨道不只是实现细节,而是与模型耦合的结构化输入。
- **区分两种"可学习性"**:一般下一值可预测性(MLP 测试 MSE)与目标扩散系统内的可利用性(diffusion probe 损失),并指出二者虽排名高度相关但不等同。
- **提出预筛流程**:剔除具有强内在动力学结构(如 L3.6 的带状交替)与有限精度吸收态(如 Sine-1、L4.0 因浮点舍入坍塌到 0)的轨道,并给出基于临界点 f′(c)=0 推导出的吸收范围 √Δ 量化分析。
- **构建受控的轨道实验框架**:经验秩均匀化 + Box–Muller 高斯化 + 均值方差校准,确保比较的是高阶/联合时间结构而非边缘分布差异。
- **发现经验幂律关系**:在 IID 归一化下,diffusion probe loss 与 real-data diffusion loss 近似满足 d = p^ρ,且 MNIST 与 CIFAR-10 的指数 95% 置信区间不重叠,提示 ρ 依赖于模型 M 与数据 D,而非源本身的普适常数。
- **跨系统对照**:MNIST 分类 MLP 与卷积 VAE 在同样源替换下未观察到稳定的精度下降或生成坍塌,说明伪随机源敏感性是学习系统相关的。

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the realized training objective of diffusion models as jointly dependent on (model, data, concrete pseudorandom stream), so that accessible orbit structure becomes a learnable input via the gradient ∇θ L̂_t(θ_t; D, R_t).
- Operationalizes two distinct notions of orbit learnability: (i) general next-value predictability measured by a 273-parameter MLP on a uniformized orbit, and (ii) target-system exploitability measured by a novel **diffusion probe** that keeps the U-Net, noising, and objective but replaces real images with online IID random tensors.
- Provides a pre-screening procedure that separates strong dynamical structure (Logistic 3.6 band alternation) from finite-precision absorbing states (Sine-1 and Logistic 4.0 collapse to zero), with a quantitative capture-range argument showing that critical points with f′(c)=0 enlarge the rounding-induced capture width from O(Δ) to O(√Δ).
- Establishes a controlled pipeline (empirical-rank uniformization, Box–Muller Gaussianization, mean–variance calibration) so that remaining differences come from higher-order and joint temporal structure rather than marginals.
- Discovers an empirical power law d = p^ρ between IID-normalized probe loss and real-data diffusion loss, with non-overlapping 95% CIs for MNIST (ρ̂ = 3.06) and CIFAR-10 (ρ̂ = 4.79), supporting ρ = ρ(M, D) as a system-dependent calibration rather than a universal source constant.
- Cross-system comparison (classification MLP, convolutional VAE) shows the effect is not universal across model classes — sensitivity depends on where and how often the source enters training/generation.

## Methodology

**Optimization view.** The idealized objective L(θ; D) = E_R[L̂(θ; D, R)] is replaced by the realized per-step loss L̂_t(θ_t; D, R_t). Because the SGD update consumes concrete pseudorandom values, any structure that helps minimize the loss can in principle be exploited by the same gradient-descent process used to learn data regularities.

**Random sources.** Compared are an IID reference (NumPy default) and four deterministic families: Logistic (x_{n+1}=a x_n(1−x_n), a∈{3.6…4.0}), finite-memory Markov-type (M_k, k∈{2,4,8}, plus first-order M1), Sine-1 (x_{n+1}=sin(πx_n) mod 1), and ShiftedSine (SS_a with a∈{1,2,4,8}). All orbits run as continuous streams (1000 burn-in iterations) and feed separate sub-streams for data order, timesteps, training noise, and sampling noise.

**Marginal control.** Each orbit is processed by empirical-rank uniformization on a 100k reference, then Box–Muller Gaussianization, then mean/variance calibration against an independent 200k sample. This controls the 1-D uniform marginal and the Gaussian output's first two moments, leaving higher-order and joint temporal structure as the source of differences.

**Diffusion setup.** MNIST: 60k images, U-Net (64,128,128), 30 epochs, batch 128. CIFAR-10: 50k images, `google/ddpm-cifar10-32` U-Net config (128,256,256,256) without pretrained weights, 30k steps, batch 64 (~38.4 epochs). 1000 forward timesteps, noise-prediction MSE objective, 100-step DDIM sampling with η=1.

**MLP indicator.** A 273-parameter single-hidden-layer MLP (hidden width 8) predicts the next uniformized value from the previous 32; trained with AdamW, batch 4096, up to 5000 steps on 250k windows. Indicator L^MLP_s is the test MSE; IID theoretical floor is 1/12 ≈ 0.0833.

**Diffusion probe.** Same U-Net, noising, and objective as the real experiment, but clean inputs are online IID uniform tensors (no semantic structure). Probe indicator L^probe_s is the mean training loss over the final 5 epochs, measuring how far the diffusion system can push down its objective while continuously consuming the tested orbit as timesteps and noise.

**Generation grading.** Outputs span blank images, textures, partial contours, and clean class-consistent samples; no single automatic metric captures this range, so an ordered 0–6 human grade is used (evaluators blind to source, third-evaluator tie-breaker), serving only as an ordered variable for rank correlation.

## Results & Highlights

- **Strong rank correlation with generation degradation.** On MNIST, MLP test MSE Spearman ρ_s = 0.9150 (exact p = 9.14×10⁻⁵, Pearson r = 0.9487); probe ρ_s = 0.9185 (p = 7.90×10⁻⁵, r = 0.9573). On CIFAR-10, MLP ρ_s = 0.9376 (p = 3.05×10⁻⁵); probe ρ_s = 0.9341 (p = 3.85×10⁻⁶). All four p-values are below 10⁻⁴.
- **Massive loss spread across orbits.** On MNIST, real-data diffusion loss ranges from 0.004906 (L3.7, grade 1) up to 0.021215 (IID, grade 6) — i.e. the worst non-IID orbit reaches a loss ~3.3× *lower* than IID while producing only weak textures. On CIFAR-10, the range is 0.003986 (L3.7) to 0.029989 (IID), a ~7.5× ratio.
- **Counterintuitive loss/quality inversion.** Lower training loss co-occurs with *worse* generation: L3.7 has the lowest training loss on both datasets yet produces blank/texture outputs (MNIST grade 1, CIFAR-10 grade 1), whereas IID has the highest training loss and the best samples (grade 6). This is consistent with the orbit supplying a predictable component the model exploits.
- **Empirical power law with non-overlapping exponents.** After IID normalization d = p^ρ: MNIST ρ̂ = 3.0628, 95% CI [2.8173, 3.3083], R²_log = 0.9730; CIFAR-10 ρ̂ = 4.7873, 95% CI [4.3896, 5.1850], R²_log = 0.9655. CIs do not overlap, empirically supporting ρ = ρ(M, D).
- **Probe and MLP are highly correlated with each other.** Spearman ρ_s = 0.9231 on MNIST and 0.9161 on CIFAR-10 (matched run 1), but local rankings differ — they capture related yet distinct properties.
- **Finite-precision absorbing states documented concretely.** Sine-1 reaches x = 0.5000000021116002, sin(πx) rounds to exactly 1, mod 1 gives 0, and the orbit stays at the zero absorbing state; Logistic 4.0 reaches 0.4999999944571299 at iteration 15070465 with the same fate. These are retained as separate failure cases and excluded from the power-law fit.
- **Cross-system sensitivity is heterogeneous.** A 1-hidden-layer MNIST classifier (20 epochs, 3 runs) shows source-induced accuracy differences that do not form a stable cross-run ranking; a convolutional VAE generates recognizable digits for all 12 sources with only mild blur changes and no posterior collapse. Diffusion models are by far the most sensitive of the three.
- **Pre-screening matters.** Logistic 3.6 (band alternation), Sine-1, and L4.0 (absorbing states) are removed from the continuous correlation analysis and power-law fit; the strong correlations above are computed on the remaining 12 sources.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** is genuinely interesting and under-explored: prior PRNG-in-ML work (Antunes 2025, refs [25,26]) ties generator *statistical* quality to task performance, and prior neural-sequence work (Tao et al. 2025, refs [6,7]) treats the random sequence itself as the target. This paper instead keeps the orbit as an *internal input* to a normal diffusion system and shows the gradient pathway R_t → ∇θ L̂_t → θ_{t+1} by which orbit structure leaks into the model. The diffusion-probe design (replacing real images with online IID tensors while preserving the U-Net) is a clean operational idea.
- **Rigor** is the main concern. The headline correlations rest on a small N (12 screened sources, only ~9 distinct families after excluding Sine/Logistic variants), and the CIFAR-10 power-law exponent comes from a *single matched run* — the authors admit another CIFAR-10 probe run is used only for ranking stability and excluded from the fit. With N=11 fit points and one run, the non-overlapping CIs between MNIST and CIFAR-10 are suggestive but not strong evidence for ρ = ρ(M, D); they could shift substantially with a few more runs or sources.
- **The "human grade" as the macroscopic quality variable** is the weakest measurement link. The authors justify it by arguing no single automatic metric captures the heterogeneous failure modes (blank, texture, partial contour), but a 0–6 ordinal grade from 2–3 evaluators on 1000 generated images cannot resolve order within a grade and is the very quantity the central correlations are computed against. FID/IS/MMD numbers would have strengthened the claim even if used only as supplements.
- **Training budgets are deliberately short** (CIFAR-10 ~38.4 epochs vs. the public ddpm-cifar10-32 recipe). The loss/quality inversion (lower loss → worse images) is striking, but it is plausible that this inversion is a small-budget transient that disappears at convergence; the paper does not run any longer training to test this.
- **The power law is empirical only.** No theoretical origin is offered; the fit passes through (1,1) by construction (IID normalization), so any monotone concave/convex relation will look "power-law-like" on a log-log plot with N=11. The claim ρ = ρ(M, D) is essentially underdetermined with two datapoints (MNIST, CIFAR-10).
- **Reproducibility** is the strongest aspect: public code, fixed seeds, identical initial parameters across sources within a run, separate sub-orbits per random role, and pre-screening rules specified in enough detail to replicate. The pre-screening (especially the absorbing-state analysis with concrete float values like 0.5000000021116002) is reproducible by construction.
- **Significance** is real but narrow: the result that PRNG choice matters for diffusion (but not for a small classifier or VAE) is a useful practical warning for diffusion practitioners, especially as models scale. The paper honestly frames this as a working hypothesis to be tested at scale rather than a proven law.
- **Red flags:** none severe, but the combination of (a) non-top-tier affiliations, (b) ChatGPT-assisted writing, (c) very small source/family sample, and (d) single-run CIFAR-10 power law means the strongest claims should be treated as exploratory. The lower-loss-worse-quality finding, if it survives at scale, would be the most consequential result and deserves a dedicated ablation separating "model exploited orbit" from "model underfit data".

## Strengths

- Clean conceptual reframing: pseudorandom streams as deterministic orbits coupled to a learning system, with the optimization pathway written out explicitly.
- The diffusion probe is a clever, modular tool — it isolates system-level exploitability from raw predictability and supports per-role ablations (data order vs. timestep vs. noise).
- Thorough pre-screening with a real numerical analysis of finite-precision absorbing states (the √Δ vs. Δ capture-range argument and concrete float values).
- Strong, consistent rank correlations across two datasets and two independent indicators.
- Honest scope: cross-system comparison with a classifier and a VAE grounds the claim that sensitivity is system-dependent, not universal.
- Public code and data tables.

## Limitations

- Small sample of source families (mainly Logistic + Markov-type + ShiftedSine); only one history length (32) and one MLP capacity (273 params).
- Short training budgets; the headline loss/quality inversion is not verified at full training duration.
- The CIFAR-10 power-law exponent comes from one matched run.
- The macroscopic quality variable is an ordinal human grade, not a standard image-quality metric; within-grade ordering is unresolvable.
- No theoretical justification for the empirical power law; the IID-normalization anchor at (1,1) makes the log-log fit permissive.
- The probe is more expensive than the MLP and inherits the target U-Net, so it cannot be used to compare sources before the target system is chosen.
- All experiments are on small (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32) datasets; nothing is shown for ImageNet-scale or modern latent diffusion.
- The separate contributions of the five random roles (data order, timestep, training noise, initial sampling noise, stepwise sampling noise) are explicitly deferred — the main experiments use one consistent source configuration end-to-end.

## Relevance

对研究扩散模型训练动力学与噪声设计的方向高度相关:它直接挑战了"训练噪声只是可互换的实现细节"这一隐含假设,并提出 diffusion probe 这一可复用的诊断工具,可用于评估大规模扩散模型对 PRNG 选择的鲁棒性以及推理种子敏感性等问题。对追求稳定可复现训练与采样质量的研究者而言,这是一篇值得关注的探索性工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the lower-training-loss-yet-worse-generation inversion persist when CIFAR-10 is trained to convergence (e.g., the full ddpm-cifar10-32 schedule), or is it a small-budget transient?
- Which random role drives the degradation most — data ordering, diffusion-timestep selection, training noise, initial sampling noise, or stepwise sampling noise? The probe supports per-role ablations but they are not reported.
- Does the empirical power law d = p^ρ survive on a third dataset and on a modern latent/Text-to-Image diffusion architecture, and does ρ scale systematically with model width, depth, or number of random calls?
- Can a deliberately non-IID source with controlled temporal structure *outperform* the operational IID baseline on a specific model (the authors' open question 9)?
- How do the standard PRNGs used in production training (Philox, PCG64, MT19937, Threefry) score on the diffusion probe at current and larger scales, and is there a practical exploitability threshold beyond which a generator becomes unsafe for diffusion training?
- Is the MLP indicator's lack of a clean power-law relation with downstream diffusion loss a fundamental limitation, or an artifact of the single capacity/history length tested?
- Can the human 0–6 grade be replaced by a learned classifier-of-failures or a multi-metric composite that preserves rank while adding within-grade resolution?
