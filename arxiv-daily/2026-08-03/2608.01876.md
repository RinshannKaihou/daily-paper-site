> **一句话总结：** 作为 Transformer Geometry Observatory 系列的第三篇，本文在 ViT-Small/16 + ImageNet-100 训练过程中，通过 Linear Probe Accuracy、Fisher Ratio、类中心距离与 Local PCA Rank 等指标刻画了语义类别的几何演化，发现类表征在训练后期显著变得线性可分、类中心彼此远离、最后一层（Block 12）判别力最强，从而为 Semantic Expansion Hypothesis 提供了实证支撑。

> **TL;DR:** The third Transformer Geometry Observatory (TGO-III) tracks how semantic class geometry evolves while training a ViT-Small/16 on ImageNet-100 for 100 epochs, showing monotonically rising Linear Probe Accuracy, a two-stage Fisher Ratio (dip-then-rise), expanding class centroid distances after ~20 epochs, and a non-monotonic Local PCA Rank (peak in the first quarter of training) — with the strongest class specialization emerging in the final Transformer block (Block 12) rather than uniformly across depth, providing empirical support for the Semantic Expansion / Manifold Expansion Hypotheses over a token-diversification explanation.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 TGO-III: Semantic Geometry Observatory，从语义类级别（而非仅全局协方差）的几何视角分析 ViT 训练过程，补全了 TGO-I（谱几何）和 TGO-II（表征几何）之后的语义维度。
- 构建了一组互补的语义探针工具：Linear Probe Accuracy、Fisher Ratio、Class Centroid Distances、Local Intrinsic Dimensionality 与 Local PCA Rank，用于刻画类别可分性、全局语义组织与局部流形复杂度的协同演化。
- 通过实验为先前 TGO 提出的 Semantic Expansion Hypothesis、Manifold Expansion Hypothesis 与 Transition Zone Hypothesis 提供新的经验证据，将 Effective Rank 爆炸归因于语义方向的组织，而非 token 多样化或纯统计协方差增长。
- 揭示了"全局流形扩张 + 局部语义压缩"的同时性现象：全局表征空间不断扩展，而每个语义类的局部流形在训练后期反而变得更加低维紧凑。
- 细化了 Transition Zone Hypothesis：把第 4–5 层附近的过渡区重新解释为"几何特化的起点"，而非语义表征完全建立的位点——最强判别能力实际集中在网络末端（Block 12）。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces TGO-III, a class-level semantic observatory that complements the global covariance (TGO-I) and global representational (TGO-II) observatories, jointly tracking how individual ImageNet-100 class representations evolve during ViT training.
- Defines a complementary metric suite — Linear Probe Accuracy, Fisher Ratio, pairwise Class Centroid Distances, Local Intrinsic Dimensionality, and Local PCA Rank (τ = 0.95 explained variance threshold) — that together quantify class separability, global semantic arrangement, and per-class local manifold complexity.
- Empirically validates the Semantic Expansion Hypothesis by showing that covariance expansion observed in TGO-I corresponds to directions increasingly allocated to discriminative semantic organization rather than token diversification.
- Identifies a striking co-occurrence: global manifold expansion happens simultaneously with local semantic concentration (per-class Local PCA Rank decreases during the second half of training), so global and local geometry move in opposite directions.
- Refines the Transition Zone Hypothesis, arguing the layer-4/5 transition previously identified marks the onset of geometric specialization, while the most discriminative semantic representations mature much later, peaking at the final Transformer block.

## Methodology

The paper is an empirical analysis study rather than a new architecture. A ViT-Small/16 backbone is trained on ImageNet-100 for 100 epochs on an NVIDIA Quadro RTX 6000. To ensure consistent observability, all measurements are taken on a fixed analysis subset of 1000 validation images throughout training, evaluated at every layer. For each Transformer block, TGO-III extracts the feature representation X_l and computes both the feature covariance C_feat = (1/(N−1))(X − 1μ^T)^T(X − 1μ^T) and the token covariance C_token = (1/(D−1))(X − X̄)^T(X − X̄), noting that both share the same non-zero singular values and differ only in eigenvectors, so feature-space and token-space analyses are two perspectives on the same geometry.

Five complementary observables are computed at each epoch and each layer:

1. **Linear Probe Accuracy** — a linear classifier f(x) = Wx + b is trained on frozen representations and evaluated with standard classification accuracy; measures linear separability of class information already encoded in the representation.
2. **Fisher Ratio** — F = S_B / (S_W + ε), the ratio of between-class scatter S_B = Σ_c N_c ||μ_c − μ||² to within-class scatter S_W = Σ_c Σ_{i:y_i=c} ||x_i − μ_c||²; jointly captures inter-class separation and intra-class compactness.
3. **Class Centroid Distance** — pairwise Euclidean distances D_ij = ||μ_i − μ_j|| between class centroids, summarized as mean D̄ = (2 / (C(C−1))) Σ_{i<j} D_ij; characterizes the global geometric arrangement of semantic classes.
4. **Local PCA Rank** — for each class, PCA is performed on the centered representations; rank is the minimum number of components needed to explain τ = 0.95 cumulative variance; measures per-class intrinsic geometric complexity.
5. **Local Intrinsic Dimensionality** — referenced alongside Local PCA Rank for characterizing local manifold complexity.

These observables are tracked layer-by-layer and epoch-by-epoch, then connected to three previously proposed hypotheses (Semantic Expansion, Manifold Expansion, Transition Zone), with the implementation, checkpoints, and observatory pipelines released publicly via the linked GitHub repository.

## Results & Highlights

Quantitative observations are presented primarily as figures rather than tabulated numbers, so the claims below report the paper's stated trajectories, threshold values, and layer/epoch landmarks (no baseline comparison is possible — this is a single-model mechanistic analysis):

- **Linear Probe Accuracy on ImageNet-100** (ViT-Small/16) rises consistently across training epochs and across depth, with a noticeable acceleration after approximately Transformer block 10, and the maximum probe accuracy achieved at the final CLS representation at Epoch 100 — i.e., the deepest layer is decisively the most linearly separable, not a mid-layer.
- **Layer-wise Fisher Ratio at Epoch 100**: most intermediate blocks show relatively similar values, but the final encoder blocks show a dramatic increase, with Block 12 attaining the highest Fisher Ratio "by a considerable margin" followed by the penultimate block — semantic separation is concentrated near the output, not uniform across depth.
- **Mean Fisher Ratio trajectory**: exhibits a clear two-stage evolution — it gradually decreases during the first half of training, reaches a minimum near the midpoint, then increases steadily and ultimately exceeds its initial value by Epoch 100, indicating early reorganization followed by late-stage discrimination growth.
- **Mean pairwise Class Centroid Distance**: initially decreases during the first few epochs, then increases rapidly and stabilizes after approximately 20 epochs at substantially larger values than at initialization, remaining roughly constant for the remainder of training.
- **Mean Local PCA Rank** (per-class intrinsic dimensionality at τ = 0.95): non-monotonic — increases rapidly during the first quarter of training (local manifolds initially expand), peaks, then decreases steadily until convergence (local manifolds become more compact/concentrated). This is the opposite trajectory to the global metrics.
- **Class-wise Local PCA Rank at convergence**: substantial variability across the 100 ImageNet-100 classes — some classes remain concentrated in low-dimensional subspaces while others occupy considerably richer local manifolds, showing class-dependent geometric complexity.
- All measurements use a fixed 1000-image validation subset, 100 training epochs, ViT-Small/16 backbone on ImageNet-100, with the Local PCA explained-variance threshold fixed at τ = 0.95.

No traditional benchmark-vs-baseline numbers (e.g., top-1 accuracy vs. prior methods) are reported; the paper is a mechanistic training-dynamics study, so the quantitative content above consists of training-trajectory and layer-wise landmarks rather than comparisons to competing models.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Modest. The paper reuses well-established probes (linear probing, Fisher ratio, PCA rank) that are standard in mechanistic interpretability; the contribution is the specific synthesis of these into a "TGO-III" observatory and the framing in terms of the authors' own previously proposed hypotheses. The class-level (vs. global) lens is the genuinely new angle relative to TGO-I/II, but each individual metric is familiar.
- **Rigor**: Mixed. The methodology is clearly specified (fixed 1000-image subset, τ = 0.95 PCA threshold, layer-wise + epoch-wise sweeps) and the results are internally consistent with the authors' hypotheses. However, the paper reports its findings almost entirely through figures with **no tabulated numerical values, no error bars, no confidence intervals, and no statistical tests** — the magnitude of effects (e.g., "highest Fisher Ratio by a considerable margin") is conveyed qualitatively, making precise quantitative comparison or independent verification difficult. A specific weak baseline worth noting: the only architecture studied is ViT-Small/16 — there is no comparison to other ViT sizes, other architectures (e.g., DeiT, Swin, ResNet as a non-Transformer control), or other datasets, so the generality of the Semantic Expansion Hypothesis is untested.
- **Reproducibility**: Plausible but under-specified. The training recipe (optimizer, learning rate schedule, batch size, data augmentation, hyperparameters) is not described in the paper — only the model, dataset, epoch count, and GPU are given. The promised GitHub release of checkpoints and pipelines would substantially mitigate this, but as presented the paper alone is not enough to reproduce the runs.
- **Significance**: The empirical observations are interesting and the unified three-observatory narrative (TGO-I → TGO-II → TGO-III) is a reasonable incremental step toward a mechanistic theory of Transformer learning. However, the "hypotheses" (Semantic Expansion, Manifold Expansion, Transition Zone) are qualitative narratives that the new observations support but cannot falsify in a strong sense — the paper explicitly states TGO-III "does not add to the hypotheses" but rather "provides enough empirical evidence to validate or rule out the previous theories," which overstates what qualitative figure-based evidence can do.
- **Data quirk**: All observations are taken on the same fixed 1000-image subset of ImageNet-100 validation images at every epoch — this is good for consistency but means the reported dynamics are computed on a small subset rather than the full validation set, and the choice of ImageNet-100 (a 100-class subset) rather than full ImageNet-1k limits how representative the class-geometry conclusions are.
- **Red flags**: No major red flags, but readers should be aware that this is a short (4-page) workshop-style paper that is part of a self-citing series (refs [1], [2] are the authors' own prior TGO papers) and the framing leans heavily on validating the authors' own earlier hypotheses; the future-work section re-plans the originally promised six-observatory program, dropping further semantic analysis in favor of computational/gradient/optimization observatories.

## Strengths

- Provides a clean, complementary class-level (semantic) lens that TGO-I and TGO-II lacked, completing a coherent three-paper geometric narrative.
- The co-occurrence of global manifold expansion with local per-class compression (Local PCA Rank decreasing in late training while global centroid distances increase) is a non-obvious and intellectually interesting finding.
- Layer-wise analysis showing semantic discrimination is concentrated at Block 12 rather than uniformly distributed is a concrete, useful observation for interpretability and pruning/early-exit research.
- Public release of checkpoints and observatory code promised, lowering the barrier to extension.
- Clearly written methodology section with explicit covariance formulations and the feature/token covariance duality argument.

## Limitations

- Single architecture (ViT-Small/16), single dataset (ImageNet-100), single seed — generalization is untested.
- No tabulated numbers, error bars, or statistical significance tests; results are communicated only via figures and prose.
- Training hyperparameters (optimizer, LR schedule, batch size, augmentation) are not reported, hindering exact reproduction from the paper alone.
- The three "hypotheses" are qualitative narratives; the paper's claim to "validate or rule out" them overstates the strength of figure-based evidence.
- Heavy self-citation (TGO-I, TGO-II) and a future-work section that rewrites the previously promised roadmap may confuse readers unfamiliar with the series.
- No baseline comparison and no downstream-task benefit demonstrated — this is purely an analysis paper.

## Relevance

For researchers interested in mechanistic interpretability of Vision Transformers, training dynamics, and the geometry of learned representations, this paper offers a useful class-level complement to spectral and representational analyses, and connects directly to intrinsic-dimensionality and CKA/SVCCA-style probing literature. It is most relevant as a reference for designing probes of semantic organization in ViTs and as a data point in the broader effort to build a mechanistic theory of how Transformers learn.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the Semantic Expansion and local-compression findings generalize beyond ViT-Small/16 — e.g., to ViT-Base/Large, DeiT, Swin, or to convolutional architectures as a control?
- Does the Block-12 concentration of Fisher Ratio and probe accuracy hold on full ImageNet-1k or other datasets (CIFAR-100, iNaturalist with many fine-grained classes)?
- What happens to the Local PCA Rank trajectory under different optimizers, learning-rate schedules, and data augmentations — is the non-monotonic peak in the first quarter a robust phenomenon or an artifact of one recipe?
- Can the global-expansion / local-compression co-occurrence be linked quantitatively to the Effective Rank explosion of TGO-I via a predictive relationship rather than a qualitative narrative?
- Would adding statistical tests and confidence intervals over multiple seeds change any of the three hypotheses' empirical support?
- Since the future-work section pivots to computational/gradient/optimization observatories (TGO-IV/V/VI), what concrete experiment would falsify the Semantic Expansion Hypothesis rather than merely corroborate it?
