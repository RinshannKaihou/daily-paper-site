> **一句话总结：** ARM 是一个检测器无关的变点归因"包裹器"，通过对每个坐标取"所有切分点上的秩统计量最大值"（Wilcoxon 位置 + Mood 尺度）来打分，借助 Westfall–Young 联合置换实现精确 FWER 控制、用 Benjamini–Yekutieli / e-BH 在高维下做 FDR 控制，从而在估计的变点处仍能给出带"位置/尺度"类型标签的、有限样本保证的"哪些坐标变了"的认证集合。

> **TL;DR:** ARM wraps any changepoint detector and, by scoring each coordinate with a max-over-splits rank statistic (Wilcoxon location + Mood scale), returns the coordinate set certified to have changed, with selection-proof per-coordinate validity, exact Westfall–Young FWER control, and BY/e-BH FDR control under arbitrary dependence — even at an estimated changepoint. On the selection-effect simulation, naive per-coordinate testing at τ̂ inflates FWER to 0.33 / 0.67 / 0.89 / 0.99 at d = 5 / 10 / 20 / 40 while ARM stays at 0.052–0.108 (nominal α = 0.10); on the five FRED series around the Lehman collapse, ARM certifies all five asset classes as scale changes and correctly excludes three injected controls (asset M = 4.1–6.0 vs. control M = 1.99–2.26 against the WY threshold c = 3.6).

## 核心贡献

- **提出变点归因的形式化框架**：将"检测到变点之后，哪些坐标真正发生了变化"形式化为一个检测器无关的"包裹器"问题，输出坐标级别的认证集合，而非粗粒度的"块"或无保证的"排序"。
- **选择免疫（selection-proof）的打分方式**：用每个坐标在所有候选切分点上的秩统计量最大值 M_j = max_t max{Z_loc, Z_sc} 打分；由于 Z_j(τ̂) ≤ M_j，估计变点的精度（或偏差）只会影响功效、不会破坏有效性。
- **三条有限样本保证**：(1) 任意检测器下的单坐标有效性（Theorem 1）；(2) 通过 Westfall–Young 联合置换的精确 strong FWER 控制（保留坐标间相依），附 distribution-free 的 Holm fallback（Theorem 2）；(3) 任意坐标相依下高维 FDR 控制（Benjamini–Yekutieli 与 e-BH，Theorem 3）。
- **类型标签**：每个被认证的坐标额外携带"位置变化"或"尺度变化"标签（由主导证据通道决定），在仿真中标注近乎完美（位置 1200/1200、尺度 1058/1060）。
- **完整的可复现实验**：所有数字均来自 archived code（`experiments.py`，固定种子），覆盖选择效应、重尾/相依下的有效性、高维 FDR、类型标签精度，以及 2008 年金融危机五个金融序列的真实应用。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes post-detection coordinate attribution as a **detector-agnostic wrapper** that outputs coordinate-level certificates, advancing all four axes (granularity, guarantee, calibration, dependence) simultaneously relative to prior work (Table 1).
- Introduces a **selection-proof scoring rule**: scoring by the max-over-splits rank statistic M_j rather than by Z_j(τ̂) makes the certificate invariant to the manner and accuracy of the changepoint estimate (Theorem 1).
- Establishes **three finite-sample guarantees** from within-coordinate ranks alone: per-coordinate validity under any detector (Thm. 1); exact strong FWER via Westfall–Young joint row permutation with a distribution-free Holm fallback (Thm. 2); FDR under arbitrary coordinate dependence via Benjamini–Yekutieli and e-BH (Thm. 3).
- Adds an explicit **location-vs-scale type label** per certified coordinate (dominant evidence channel), achieving near-perfect label accuracy in simulations.
- Provides a **fully reproducible empirical study** (archived code + outputs, fixed seeds) covering the selection effect of naive testing at τ̂, validity under heavy tails and dependence, high-dimensional FDR, label accuracy, and a 2008-crisis financial application.

## Methodology

ARM (Attribution by Rank Maxima) operates on a window X ∈ ℝ^{n×d} (n time points, d coordinates) with independent rows and arbitrarily dependent columns. A black-box detector returns an estimated changepoint τ̂, which ARM never inspects and tolerates whatever its value. The per-coordinate null is H0_j: the n values of coordinate j are identically distributed (no marginal change).

**Rank channels and max-over-splits statistic.** For coordinate j, ARM computes centred within-column ranks C_ij = R_ij − (n+1)/2 and, for each candidate split t on a grid G, evaluates two evidence channels: a Wilcoxon-type location statistic Z^loc_j(t) and a Mood-type scale statistic Z^sc_j(t). The coordinate's score is the maximum over all splits and both channels: M_j = max_{t∈G} max{Z^loc_j(t), Z^sc_j(t)}; the per-channel maxima M^loc_j and M^sc_j are retained for the type label.

**Selection-proofness.** Because Z_j(τ̂) ≤ M_j for every split, no detector output — accurate or arbitrarily misplaced — can raise a true-null coordinate's statistic above what the maximum already attains. The estimate τ̂ therefore affects only power, never validity.

**Attribution rule.** Coordinate j is attributed when M_j exceeds c_{n,α}, the (1−α) quantile of M under a single null coordinate, computed once by permuting a rank vector (depends only on n and the grid, never on the data).

**Multiple-testing calibration.**
- *FWER:* A single time permutation π applied jointly to all columns (row permutation) preserves cross-coordinate dependence; the Westfall–Young single-step maxT adjusted p-value p̃_j uses the permutation law of max_k M_k(X_π). Under subset pivotality this controls FWER exactly; a fully distribution-free Holm fallback on the per-coordinate p-values is valid with no dependence assumption.
- *FDR (high-d):* Benjamini–Yekutieli on the permutation p-values (harmonic penalty c_d) is the robust default; alternatively, boosted e-values e_j = s^{−1} 1{p_j ≤ s} are fed to e-BH, which controls FDR under arbitrary dependence for every s (s only locates the power boundary).

**Type label.** Each attributed coordinate is labelled "scale" if M^sc_j > M^loc_j, else "location"; the label is descriptive and not part of the error-control statement (a certified label would require closed testing, left to future work).

## Results & Highlights

- **Selection effect (Table 2, Figure 2):** Naive per-coordinate testing at the estimated τ̂ inflates FWER as dimension grows — 0.33 at d=5, 0.67 at d=10, 0.89 at d=20, 0.99 at d=40 (at |τ̂−τ|=0, nominal α=0.10). ARM (WY) holds the nominal level across all 16 cells, FWER in 0.052–0.108, and is flat in detector misplacement |τ̂−τ| ∈ {0,5,10,20} because M_j does not involve τ̂.
- **Validity under heavy tails, dependence, and detector (Table 3):** At d=10 with three changed coordinates (CUSUM detector), WY FWER ranged 0.044–0.088 and Holm FWER 0.036–0.092 across Gaussian, t_3, Cauchy, and Lognormal marginals × independent/equicorrelated/factor dependence (all within MC error of 0.10); BY FDR sat at 0.011–0.023. The true-τ and deliberately-misplaced (τ̂=τ+10) detector columns differed by at most 0.03.
- **High-dimensional FDR (Section 5.4, Figure 3):** At d=200 with 15 changed coordinates, BY reported FDR 0.019 (independent) and 0.017 (equicorrelated ρ=0.4) at power 1.00; e-BH (s=0.004, B=1999) reported FDR 0.043 and 0.048 at power 1.00. A sensitivity sweep showed s ∈ {0.002, 0.004, 0.006} (all below the boundary αk/d = 0.0075) gave power 1.00 at FDR 0.027–0.060, while s=0.008 (just above the boundary) dropped power to 0.66 at FDR 0.082 — FDR remained controlled at every s.
- **Attribution quality vs. baselines (Table 4):** At d=20 with three changed coordinates, ARM's coordinate-level F1 was 0.985–0.987 under strong signal (δ=1.8) and 0.877–0.986 under weak signal (δ=1.1); a WAVE-style ranking AUC stayed above 0.99 and the block test hit rate was 0.890–1.000, but only ARM returned individual coordinates with a finite-sample guarantee.
- **Type-label accuracy (Section 5.5):** Over 300 replications with four location-shift and four scale-shift coordinates, attributed location coordinates were labelled "location" 1200/1200 times and scale coordinates 1058/1060 times (two mislabels). FWER (0.053) and power (1.00) were unchanged across B ∈ {199, 499, 999} permutations, and coarsening the grid from step 1 to step 4 cut cost ~4× while keeping FWER in [0.073, 0.087] at power 1.00.
- **Real data — 2008 regime shift (Section 6, Table 5):** On a 120-day window around Lehman (15 Sep 2008) over five FRED series plus three injected controls, ARM (WY, α=0.10, threshold c=3.6) attributed a scale change to all five asset classes — equities M=6.04, oil 5.56, Treasury 4.50, USD/EUR 4.29, VIX 4.11 — and correctly excluded all three controls (M=1.99–2.26). Conclusions were identical under Holm and BY and unperturbed by the detector's two-day mislocation.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from Beijing University of Technology (School of Mathematics, Statistics and Mechanics), Nanyang Technological University (College of Computing and Data Science), and Sichuan University (College of Electrical Engineering). The corresponding author is Da Chen. The work is positioned squarely in the active post-detection inference literature, directly building on ART (Cui et al., JRSSB 2026), TUNE (Jia et al., 2024), and Ouerfelli et al. (2026), and it carefully distinguishes itself from the WAVE ranking method (Lan et al., Mathematics 2026). The claims are tightly scoped to proven theorems with finite-sample statements, and every reported number is tied to archived code (`arm core.py`, `run experiments.py`, `real data panel.py`) with fixed seeds and public FRED data. The paper is ~14 pages with three formal theorems and full proofs in Appendix A.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-scoped. The core insight — score by M_j = max-over-splits rather than Z_j(τ̂) to obtain selection-proofness — is simple and powerful, and the transposition of the ART/TUNE time-axis mechanism to the coordinate axis is a clean conceptual move. However, the constituent tools (Westfall–Young maxT, Benjamini–Yekutieli, e-BH, Wilcoxon/Mood rank statistics) are all classical; novelty lies in the assembly and the formal guarantee, not in any new component.
- **Rigor:** High. Three theorems with complete proofs in the appendix; honest statement of the subset-pivotality assumption (Theorem 2) and explicit fallback (Holm) when it is in doubt; explicit boundary that pure dependence changes with invariant marginals are out of scope, matching Ouerfelli et al. Remark 8. The selection-effect comparison (Section 5.1) is framed fairly: the naive procedure is the documented double-dipping practice, and ARM implements the exact maximization-over-splits correction rather than an approximate Bonferroni.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong by the paper's own claim — fixed seeds, named scripts, public FRED series (NASDAQCOM, DGS10, DCOILWTICO, DEXUSEU, VIXCLS), MC standard errors reported (~0.02 at 250–300 reps). The reader can in principle regenerate every table.
- **Significance:** Practically meaningful for monitoring/portfolios/sensor arrays where "which coordinates" is the operational question. The 2008 application is a clean demonstration but small (d=8 with three injected controls), so real-world scalability beyond d=200 is not exercised.
- **Red flags / weak points:**
  - The WAVE baseline in Table 4 is the authors' own "WAVE-style" reimplementation (energy distance fusion), not the original WAVE code; it serves as a ranking-AUC comparator only, so the comparison is somewhat asymmetric (WAVE was not designed to certify coordinates).
  - The 2008 financial study has no ground-truth null coordinates among the real assets, so the authors *inject* three control coordinates built from the window's own quiet period. This is disclosed and methodologically defensible, but it means the falsifiability of the FWER claim on real data rests entirely on synthetic injections rather than on genuinely held-out financial nulls.
  - The type label is explicitly *descriptive only* and not error-controlled — a caveat easily missed by a casual reader; a certified label would require closed testing that is left to future work.
  - The high-dimensional FDR experiments (d=200) are still modest by modern "high-dimensional" standards and use Gaussian/equicorrelated designs; no factor-model or network-structured dependence is tested at d=200.
  - Independent rows are assumed; serially dependent rows are acknowledged as out of scope, which limits applicability to raw (un-prewhitened) financial returns in practice.

## Strengths

- A clean, detector-agnostic wrapper that delivers what it advertises: finite-sample, distribution-free error control on the coordinate axis, valid at an *estimated* changepoint.
- The max-over-splits scoring idea elegantly converts a nuisance (unknown/estimated τ) into a non-issue for validity.
- Honest, useful type labels (location vs scale) at near-perfect accuracy in simulations.
- Multiple practical calibration options (WY exact, Holm distribution-free, BY robust default, e-BH for e-value settings) with clear guidance on when to use each.
- Genuinely reproducible setup with archived code and public data.

## Limitations

- Assumes independent rows; serial dependence is only approximately handled by a (future) block-permutation variant.
- Type label is descriptive, not certified; closed testing is open future work.
- Pure dependence changes among null coordinates (invariant marginals) are outside the marginal-attribution model and require a dedicated dependence test.
- Does not exploit known block structure when it exists (the Ouerfelli predefined-block setting is recovered as a special case via within-block aggregation, but ARM does not use such priors).
- Empirical scale tested only up to d=200; behavior at thousands of coordinates under structured dependence is unverified.
- FDR power with e-BH is sensitive to the choice of s relative to sparsity; the rule in Section 3.5 must be followed or power collapses (e.g., power 0.66 at s=0.008 above the boundary).

## Relevance

对于关注变点检测后推断、多坐标归因与多重检验误差控制的研究者，ARM 提供了一个可直接套用在任意检测器之上的"认证层"，其秩统计量 + 联合置换的 distribution-free 思路与作者团队此前的 ART-NN / ARC 工作（共享秩通道）可自然组合成完整的"何时 + 何坐标"后检测工具链，对时间序列监控、金融/传感器归因场景高度相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does ARM behave under serial dependence in rows (e.g., GARCH-filtered financial returns), and what is the empirical size inflation of the i.i.d.-row Westfall–Young procedure before a block-permutation variant is available?
- Can the location/scale type label be promoted to an *error-controlled* statement via closed testing across the two channels without prohibitive cost?
- How does ARM scale and calibrate at d ≫ 200 (e.g., d ~ 10^3–10^4) under factor-model or graph-structured coordinate dependence, where e-BH and BY may diverge sharply in power?
- Can ARM compose cleanly with an online, anytime-valid sequential detector, replacing the permutation reference with e-processes?
- Does pairing ARM with WAVE-style estimation weights (estimation nominates, ARM certifies) materially improve power in sparse-signal regimes over either method alone?
- How sensitive are the 2008 financial conclusions to the window length, the choice of injected controls, and the detector (variance-CUSUM vs. mean-CUSUM vs. kernel-based)?
