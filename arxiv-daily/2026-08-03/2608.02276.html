§ 01 / METHOD

From failure to edit to rerun — a closed loop that updates only the engineer.

A separate 9B model reads a failure packet, writes a validated patch over four lifecycle hooks, and learns from the realized reward change when the frozen target reruns the batch.
STEP 01
Run frozen agent
Collect baseline trajectories & rewards R⁰ across batch B.
STEP 02
Mine failures
Deterministic extractor compacts constraints, action–observation excerpts, outcomes into packet sB.
STEP 03
Engineer writes patch
9B Hθ reads sB once, emits batch-conditioned overlay P over four hooks; invalid → zero reward.
STEP 04
Rerun & reward
Install validated overlay; rerun every task; reward = mean ΔB(P). Only θ updates.
Reward (non-differentiable, observable only post-edit)
ΔB(P) = (1/n) · Σi (RPi − R⁰i)   ·   r(B,P) = ΔB(P) if valid & complete, else 0
Action space · four lifecycle hooks · click to expand
on_initepisode start
Sets up starting context, episode state, skills, or a tool hint before the agent acts.
make_pre_hintpre-decision
Injects a state-conditioned message before the agent decides its next action.
on_before_actionpre-action mediation
Runtime guardrail — may canonicalize, rewrite, block-and-reprompt, or force the pending action. Ablation: removing this costs −3.9 pts (the dominant position on WebShop).
on_post_steppost-feedback recovery
Inspects the returned observation; triggers recovery (e.g. loop breaking) when the trajectory stalls. Ablation: removing this costs −3.3 pts (dominant on ALFWorld).
Cold-start SFT. GPT-5.5 teacher proposes edits; only one executable, complete, non-regressive response per packet is kept → 877 examples (381/248/248). 2 epochs, lr 1e-5, full-param, BF16, 8×H800.
Online GRPO. K=8 candidates per packet; rerun frozen target on full batch; advantages (r−μ)/σ; clipped surrogate εℓ=0.20, εh=0.28; truncated IS max 2.0; temp 0.7, top-p 0.95. No format bonus, no KL term.

§ 03 / ASSESSMENT

Strengths & limitations, side by side.

Distilled from the paper's own Critical Assessment, Strengths, and Limitations sections.
+ Strengths
  • Clean formulation. Isolates harness editing as a distinct learning target — neither model training nor prompt optimization.
  • Outcome-grounded reward. Rerun the frozen target, take realized ΔB(P) — no reward for plausible-but-useless edits.
  • Validated action space. Four hooks, structured effects, zero reward for invalid patches — edits stay executable and safe.
  • Honest comparisons. Reports the Self-Refine rule that hurts all three benchmarks; flags frontier editors straddle zero on held-out tasks.
  • Reproducible. Public code & models, prompt templates (Appx. A), full hyperparameter tables (Appx. B).
  • Broad generalization. 20 unseen targets and 1,270 held-out tasks; positive average gains everywhere.
! Limitations
  • One co-evolution round only. The compelling multi-round story is future work, not demonstrated.
  • Same-batch, transductive reward. No held-out regression penalty during training; no persistent patch memory across batches.
  • Transfer is uneven at the extremes. Llama-3.2-1B (+2.2), Gemma-3-1B (+1.2), Qwen2.5-72B (+1.0) gain the least.
  • Not self-bootstrapping. Cold-start SFT relies on a GPT-5.5 teacher to filter 877 examples.
  • Narrow suite. Three benchmarks, single-agent, text/SQL/Web — no multimodal, no real-world deployment traces.
  • Inference cost unmeasured. Installing and rerunning patched hooks is not bounded.

§ 04 / OPEN

Questions worth chasing next.

Drawn from the paper's own future-work flags and the sharpest gaps in the evidence.
Q1
Does iterating target-SFT ↔ engineer-GRPO for multiple rounds compound, or does the editing signal saturate once the agent stops failing in characteristic ways?
Q2
Can the reward be enriched with a held-out task term and an inference-cost term so patches optimize for transfer and overhead, not just same-batch success?
Q3
Why do very small and very large targets benefit least — failure-mode diversity, action-format mismatch, or something intrinsic to the engineer's prior?
Q4
Would giving frontier editors the same rerun-based filtering Harness-R1 enjoys close the gap, and by how much?
Q5
How does Harness-R1 behave on multimodal or long-horizon deployments where failure modes are rarer and more heterogeneous?
Q6
Can the four-hook space express conditional coordination across hooks (state set in on_init consumed by on_post_step), and does scaling the vocabulary keep helping?
