> **一句话总结：** 提出 SPEE 框架，通过显式经验演化（提取/合并/验证可迁移经验并通过特权引导的 on-policy 自蒸馏 OPSD 内化为参数）与隐式 GRPO 策略优化的两阶段闭环，将瞬时交互经验转化为持久模型能力，在五个数学推理基准上较 base 模型最高提升 6.96 个百分点，且比 GRPO 节省约 28% 训练数据。

> **TL;DR:** SPEE is a unified post-training framework that bridges test-time experience use and training-time RL by first explicitly evolving a global experience pool (extracted from success/failure trajectories, validated by marginal utility) and internalizing it via privilege-guided On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), then running GRPO from that stronger initialization; on five math benchmarks (AIME24/25, GSM8K, MATH500, Minerva Math) across qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B it beats GRPO by +1.16 pp on average and reaches +6.96 pp over base on qwen3-4b, while using ~28% fewer training trajectories than GRPO.

## 核心贡献

- 提出经验中心的自演化视角：用互信息公式 `min I(T;E) - λI(E;C)` 形式化"将瞬时轨迹压缩为可预测未来能力的紧凑经验"这一目标，并据此指出 test-time 方法、RL 与 self-distillation 各自的近似缺陷。
- 设计 SPEE 两阶段闭环：Stage I 显式经验演化（提取、合并、效用过滤、验证的全局经验池）+ 特权引导的 on-policy 自蒸馏（OPSD，teacher 看经验、student 不看，KL 蒸馏）；Stage II 用 GRPO 从蒸馏后的初始化继续做奖励驱动探索。
- 提出持续演化的全局经验池：同时保留成功（正向策略）与失败（避免重复无效路径）轨迹提取的经验条目，用 held-out probe 集上的边际效用 `w(e) = R(q, E∪{e}) - R(q, E)` 做两阶段过滤，缓解单条轨迹导致的事后合理化。
- 实验验证 SPEE 在三个模型规模上稳定优于 GRPO、SDPO、Domain Prompt 等 test-time 和 training-time 基线，并以显著更少的数据达到同等性能。
- 开源代码 https://github.com/rrrsj/SPEE，提供可复现的自演化训练流水线。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates LLM self-evolution from an experience-centered mutual-information view (`min I(T;E) - λI(E;C)`), arguing that test-time prompting, RL, and trajectory-level self-distillation are all imperfect approximations of an underexplored "experience distillation" stage.
- Introduces SPEE, a two-stage post-training pipeline: Stage I explicitly evolves a global experience pool and internalizes it through Privileged On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD); Stage II runs GRPO starting from the distilled checkpoint.
- Designs the continuously evolving global experience pool: extracts transferable items from both successful (reinforce) and failed (avoid) trajectories, merges/filters them via an evolution operator Φ and a two-stage marginal-utility validation on a held-out probe set, mitigating post-hoc rationalization from instance-specific cues.
- Shows OPSD as a teacher/student setup sharing parameters: teacher conditions on `(q, e, y<t)` while student sees only `q`; teacher is detached from gradients and rescores student trajectories token-level via reverse-KL.
- Demonstrates that experience internalization before RL improves exploration: it shifts the policy toward higher-reward regions (fewer all-incorrect groups, more non-zero GRPO advantages) while preserving entropy comparable to the base model.

## Methodology

SPEE operationalizes the experience-distillation objective `πθ'(y|q) ← πθ(y|q,e)` in two coupled stages.

**Stage I — Explicit Experience Evolution.** At round k, for each problem q, the current policy πθk samples G candidate responses, each receiving a binary verifier reward r(q,y). Trajectories are split into success set T+ and failure set T-. An experience extractor Σθk (the same policy under an extraction prompt) turns a subsample of S trajectories into candidate items e_i: positive items encode reusable reasoning patterns; negative items encode recurring failure modes. A candidate set E_new^(k) is merged into a provisional pool via an evolution operator Φ (implemented by the model) that merges overlaps, resolves conflicts, and abstracts instance-specific observations into transferable strategy. Quality is gated by a marginal-utility metric `w(e) = R(q, Ê^(k+1)) - R(q, E^(k))` measured on a held-out probe set Q_pb, with a two-stage filter (small subset, then full probe) for efficiency; only items with w(e) > ε survive. Repeated consolidation yields the path E^(0) → E^(1) → … → E^(M).

After several rounds the refined pool is treated as privileged training-only information. OPSD constructs two branches sharing parameters: the teacher sees `(q, e, y<t)`, the student sees only `q`; the teacher is detached from gradients and rescores on-policy student samples token-by-token. The student is optimized to match the teacher distribution by minimizing reverse-KL `L_OPSD = E_q D_KL(πθ_stu(·|q) ‖ π̄θ_tea(·|q))`.

**Stage II — Implicit Policy Optimization.** GRPO is applied to the distilled policy. For each q, G responses are sampled, group mean μ_G and variance σ_G are computed, and the group-relative advantage is `A_i = (r_i - μ_G) / (σ_G + δ)`. The clipped surrogate objective `J_GRPO` is maximized. Because the policy has been moved toward higher-reward regions, more groups have mixed rewards, producing non-zero advantages and informative gradients; this is the mechanism by which experience distillation improves RL exploration efficiency. The authors note other GRPO variants could replace standard GRPO.

## Results & Highlights

- **Average accuracy across five benchmarks:** SPEE reaches +4.87 / +6.96 / +6.53 percentage points over the base checkpoint on qwen3-1.7B / 4B / 8B respectively (Table 1, averages 30.56 / 36.67 / 38.71 vs base 25.69 / 29.71 / 32.18).
- **vs GRPO baseline:** SPEE beats GRPO at all three scales, with an average improvement of +1.16 pp; e.g. on qwen3-4b SPEE 36.67% vs GRPO 34.75% (Δ +1.92 pp), and on qwen3-8b SPEE 38.71% vs GRPO 37.92% (Δ +0.79 pp).
- **vs SDPO baseline:** on qwen3-8b, SPEE averages 38.71% vs SDPO 35.17%, and notably on AIME24 SPEE 10.60% vs SDPO 3.33%, showing the advantage of evolving abstract experience over distilling instance-specific trajectories.
- **Per-benchmark on qwen3-8b:** GSM8K 93.14 (base 80.38, +12.76 pp); MATH500 53.70 (base 46.85); Minerva Math 24.06 (base 19.30); AIME25 pass@16 12.08 (base 6.87).
- **Data efficiency (Figure 3):** at the same performance level, SPEE requires approximately 28% fewer training trajectories than GRPO, owing to dense Stage-I supervision and more efficient Stage-II sampling.
- **Ablation (Table 2):** removing Stage II drops average from 36.67% → 35.16% (4B) and 38.71% → 36.14% (8B); removing the shared experience pool drops further to 34.13% (4B) and 35.48% (8B), confirming both components — and especially the shared pool — are necessary.
- **Experience-evolution iterations (Figure 4):** sampling accuracy rises from 18.83% at iteration 0 to 22.66% after 4 iterations, a +3.83 pp / ~20.34% relative gain, showing progressive consolidation rather than mere accumulation.
- **Rollout diversity (Figure 2):** Stage I substantially reduces the proportion of problems for which all 8 sampled responses are incorrect, while keeping policy entropy comparable to the base model — avoiding premature collapse.

## Authors & Credibility

- Authors are from the Gaoling School of Artificial Intelligence, Renmin University of China (a strong NLP/ML group) and WeChat AI, Tencent. Corresponding author Xiting Wang is an established researcher on interpretable and self-improving LLMs.
- The work cites and positions itself clearly against recent strong baselines: GRPO/DeepSeekMath (Shao et al. 2024), DAPO (Yu et al. 2026), RL-via-Self-Distillation (Hübotter et al. 2026), and Privileged Information Distillation (Penaloza et al. 2026).
- Source code is publicly released at https://github.com/rrrsj/SPEE, supporting reproducibility of the two-stage pipeline.
- Training data (DAPO-math-17k) and benchmarks (AIME24/25, GSM8K, MATH500, Minerva Math) are standard, publicly available math-reasoning suites, easing comparison.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine conceptual contribution — the mutual-information framing of experience distillation and the explicit two-stage coupling (evolving pool → OPSD → GRPO) is more principled than simply chaining reflection prompts with RL. The "privileged teacher sees experience, detached student learns" OPSD variant is a clean, well-motivated design.
- **Rigor:** Solid empirical protocol (controlled training steps and total sampled rollouts across methods; three model scales; standard benchmarks; per-scale ablations; data-efficiency and entropy analyses). However, the strongest baseline compared is essentially GRPO/SDPO; newer or stronger RL post-training recipes (e.g., DAPO itself, for which the training set is named) are not run head-to-head, and only standard GRPO is used in Stage II even though the authors acknowledge variants might do better.
- **Reproducibility:** Good — public code, public datasets, named model family (Qwen3-base), specific hyperparameters implicit in the equations (G, S, ε, δ, clip ε). Missing details: probe set Q_pb size, exact number of evolution rounds used in main results (stated as "a single evolution round for computational efficiency" in the iteration study), and the experience-extraction prompt.
- **Significance:** Moderate-to-high for the self-evolving-LLM subfield; the experience-pool-as-RL-initialization idea is a useful, transferable recipe. Scope is narrow — only math reasoning with verifier-based binary rewards; generalization to coding, agents, or sparse/ambiguous rewards is asserted but untested.
- **Red flags / data quirks:**
  - AIME24/25 numbers are pass@16 averages while the others are pass@1 — the Δ columns mix metrics, slightly inflating perceived gains on the hardest benchmarks (e.g., SDPO's AIME24=3.33 vs SPEE's 10.60 is a 16-sample metric, not pass@1).
  - Domain Prompt is a weak test-time strawman (single hand-written prompt); its modest gains make parameter-level methods look good but don't really test the strongest prompt/retrieval baselines.
  - The "shared experience pool" ablation removes cross-problem sharing but the paper does not isolate how much of the gain comes from pool evolution versus simply having any distilled supervision.
  - SDPO underperforms Base on qwen3-1.7b (24.51 vs 25.69) and on AIME24 (0.00), suggesting the baseline may be misconfigured or sensitive — worth scrutiny before accepting SPEE's relative margin at face value.
  - Single-evolution-round main setup plus an n=0..4 iteration study on sampling accuracy (not final eval) means the headline of "progressive self-improvement" is supported indirectly rather than by a full multi-round benchmark table.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated unification of test-time experience use and training-time RL through an explicit intermediate "experience distillation" stage, with a coherent information-theoretic justification.
- The evolving global experience pool with marginal-utility filtering is a concrete, reusable mechanism against the well-known post-hoc-rationalization failure mode of trajectory distillation.
- Consistent improvements across three model scales and five benchmarks, with ablations isolating both Stage II (GRPO) and the shared pool.
- Strong data-efficiency story (~28% fewer trajectories than GRPO) and a thoughtful rollout-diversity analysis (entropy preserved, fewer all-incorrect groups) that explains why pre-internalization helps RL.
- Public code release and use of standard, accessible benchmarks.

## Limitations

- Evaluated only on mathematical reasoning with rule-based verifier rewards; no results on code generation, agentic tasks, or tasks with sparse/ambiguous/noisy rewards where experience extraction is hardest.
- Headline experiments use a single experience-evolution round; the multi-round study reports only sampling accuracy, not downstream benchmark accuracy, so the "progressive" claim is under-evidenced at the final-eval level.
- Metric mixing (pass@16 for AIME, pass@1 elsewhere) complicates cross-benchmark comparison of the Δ column.
- Stage II is restricted to standard GRPO despite the authors noting that DAPO or other variants could outperform it; the comparison against DAPO-style training (the very dataset they train on) is missing.
- No analysis of experience-pool drift, noise, or failure cases where evolved experience misleads the policy; the qualitative case study (Figure 5) is a single cherry-picked success.
- Computational cost of the probe-set validation and the model-implemented evolution operator Φ is not reported; the data-efficiency claim does not account for this overhead.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 自演化与强化学习后训练的研究者而言，SPEE 提供了一个可落地的"经验池 + 自蒸馏 + RL"闭环配方，可直接迁移到代码生成、智能体长程任务等可验证奖励场景，并启发如何用互信息视角设计更通用的经验压缩目标。其"先内化经验再做 GRPO"的初始化策略对提升弱基座模型在 RL 阶段的探索效率具有直接工程价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does SPEE behave on tasks with non-binary or noisy rewards (e.g., code execution with partial test pass, agentic environments with delayed rewards), where the success/failure split and marginal-utility filter become ambiguous?
- Does the evolving experience pool suffer from drift or confirmation bias over many rounds, and can the marginal-utility filter detect and prune stale or misleading items at scale?
- What is the wall-clock and token-cost comparison (including probe-set validation and the model-implemented Φ operator) versus vanilla GRPO and DAPO — does the ~28% trajectory saving survive an honest total-cost accounting?
- Would replacing Stage-II standard GRPO with DAPO or a DAPO-style variant narrow or widen SPEE's margin, given the authors trained on the DAPO-math-17k set?
- Can the OPSD teacher/student design be extended to cross-model distillation (stronger external teacher providing privileged experience) without reintroducing the post-hoc-rationalization risk the pool was designed to remove?
