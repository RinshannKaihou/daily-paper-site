> **一句话总结：** FOCUS 识别出 FP4 量化中量化尺度（quantization scale）无需服从硬件离散格式这一被忽视的自由度，通过引入可学习的全精度松弛系数（CRS）和子块粒度独立系数（DGS）将尺度优化从离散空间提升到连续空间，在 MXFP4/NVFP4 两种格式下均取得 SOTA，对 Qwen3-4B NVFP4 恢复率达 98.2% 的 FP16 精度且无推理开销。

> **TL;DR:** FOCUS is a PTQ framework that exploits an overlooked freedom — the FP4 quantization scale is never stored at inference, so it need not obey the discrete hardware format. By introducing a learnable full-precision relaxation coefficient (Coupled-Relaxation Scaling) and sub-block-level coefficients (Dual-Granularity Scaling), it lifts scale optimization into a continuous, gradient-friendly space; on Qwen3-4B under NVFP4 it recovers 98.2% of FP16 zero-shot accuracy (67.05% vs 68.25%), beating RaZeR, 4over6, MR-GPTQ, GPTQ, and RTN with no inference overhead and a 19-minute H20 calibration.

## 核心贡献

- **识别被忽视的优化自由度：** 指出在 MXFP4/NVFP4 中只有反量化尺度（dequantization scale）被存储并部署，而量化尺度（quantization scale）仅在离线量化过程中使用、推理时丢弃，因此后者无需遵守 E8M0/E4M3 离散格式或块大小约束——这一观察开辟了此前未被利用的优化空间。
- **Coupled-Relaxation Scaling (CRS)：** 在量化尺度与反量化尺度之间引入可学习全精度系数 `c_i`（初始化为 1），使 `S_q = S_dq · c_i`，将尺度优化从离散硬件空间提升到连续空间，同时保持耦合结构以避免全解耦带来的训练振荡。
- **Dual-Granularity Scaling (DGS)：** 将每个硬件块（MXFP4 块大小 32、NVFP4 块大小 16）再划分为大小为 8 的子块，每个子块配独立系数，使量化尺度能捕捉块内局部权重分布变化；DGS 在 K=1 时退化为 CRS。
- **端到端尺度学习框架：** 冻结原始权重，仅用 KL-Top 蒸馏损失优化块尺度和松弛系数，使用 STE 反传，部署模型仍为标准 MXFP4/NVFP4 格式，零推理开销，且与变换类方法正交可叠加。
- **更强的量化效率：** 单张 H20 GPU 上 Qwen3-4B 量化仅需 19 分钟，比 MR-GPTQ 的 29 分钟快 34%；Qwen3-8B 快 28%（29 vs 40 分钟）。

## Key Contributions

- Identifies that the FP4 quantization scale is never stored or deployed at inference, so it is free from the discrete hardware-mandated format (E8M0/E4M3) and block-size granularity — an optimization space no prior learnable, gradient-based FP4 method had exploited.
- Proposes **Coupled-Relaxation Scaling (CRS)**, which inserts a learnable full-precision coefficient `c_i` (init = 1) between the two scales so that `S_q = S_dq · c_i`, lifting optimization from a discrete power-of-two grid to a continuous space while keeping the coupling that prevents the training instability seen under full decoupling.
- Proposes **Dual-Granularity Scaling (DGS)**, which subdivides each hardware block into sub-blocks of size 8 (K=4 for MXFP4, K=2 for NVFP4), each with an independent coefficient, capturing intra-block weight variation that a single per-block coefficient cannot.
- Builds an end-to-end PTQ framework that freezes weights, optimizes only scales and coefficients with a KL-Top distillation loss via STE, and produces a deployed model that is bit-for-bit standard MXFP4/NVFP4 with no inference overhead and orthogonality to Hadamard-transform methods.
- Demonstrates consistent SOTA across 5 LLM families and 7 benchmarks under both FP4 formats, while quantizing 28-34% faster than the previous SOTA MR-GPTQ on a single H20 GPU.

## Methodology

**FP4 preliminaries.** Both NVFP4 and MXFP4 represent weights with E2M1 elements and block-wise scaling. NVFP4 uses block size B=16 with a two-level scheme: a tensor-wide FP32 global scale α and per-block E4M3 scales Δ_i, giving effective scale `S_i = α · Δ_i`. MXFP4 uses block size B=32 with a single E8M0 (power-of-two only) scale per block.

**Scale-learning baseline.** Weights are frozen; only block scales are learnable, kept as FP16 copies initialized by absmax and quantized on-the-fly to the target format during the forward pass (Eqs. 4). STE propagates gradients. The loss is KL-Top distillation against the FP16 teacher. The same quantized scale `S_i` serves both as the quantization scale (dividing weights) and the dequantization scale (multiplying them back).

**Key insight.** The baseline tightly couples the two roles via `S_q = S_dq = Q_fmt(S_fp)`. But the dequantization scale is the only one stored at inference, so it must be hardware-compliant; the quantization scale is discarded and can be optimized free of precision and granularity constraints.

**Coupled-Relaxation Scaling (CRS).** Rather than fully decoupling (which the authors show causes oscillation in the Appendix), CRS keeps the shared structure but relaxes tightness with a continuous coefficient: `S_q = S_dq · c_i`, with `S_dq = Q_fmt(S_fp)`. Starting from `c_i = 1` (baseline), the task loss drives `c_i` to discover better FP4 assignments. The asymmetry is intentional — the goal is the best FP4 weight matrix, not scale equivalence.

**Dual-Granularity Scaling (DGS).** Each hardware block is partitioned into K sub-blocks of size 8 (K=4 for MXFP4, K=2 for NVFP4), each with its own coefficient `c_i^k`. Quantization operates per sub-block while the dequantization scale remains block-shared (Eq. 8). K=1 reduces DGS to CRS.

**Deployment.** After training, all coefficients and quantization scales are discarded; only the FP4 weights `W_fp4` and the hardware-compliant dequantization scales `S_dq` are retained, yielding a standard MXFP4/NVFP4 model with no online transforms. The method is orthogonal to Hadamard-rotation approaches and can be combined with them.

**Training setup.** PyTorch + HuggingFace on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU; AdamW with constant LR (MXFP4: 2e-2 for scales, 5e-2 for coefficients; NVFP4: 5e-3 and 1e-3); 1 epoch, batch size 32, 1,248 WikiText2 calibration samples at seq len 2,048; KL-Top loss with k=1000. Weights and activations of all linear layers are quantized to FP4.

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen3-4B NVFP4 recovery rate:** FOCUS recovers **98.2%** of FP16 zero-shot average accuracy (67.05% vs FP16 68.25%), vs RaZeR 96.3% (65.71%), 4over6 94.9% (64.74%), and MR-GPTQ 95.9% (65.46%).
- **Qwen3-8B NVFP4:** FOCUS reaches **71.37%** average zero-shot accuracy, recovering **99.8%** of the FP16 baseline (71.54%); the best competing method RaZeR achieves only **70.54%** — a 0.83-point gap.
- **Qwen3-8B WikiText2 perplexity:** FOCUS attains **9.76 (MXFP4)** and **9.47 (NVFP4)**, essentially matching the FP16 baseline of **9.73**, while MR-GPTQ under MXFP4 sits at 11.45 — FOCUS reduces perplexity by 1.5–2.5 points over MR-GPTQ on Qwen models.
- **LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct GSM8K-CoT (MXFP4):** FOCUS **73.31%** vs MR-GPTQ 72.40% (+0.9) and FP16 85.67%; on MMLU-CoT FOCUS **65.53%** vs MR-GPTQ 63.39% (**+2.1 points**).
- **LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct reasoning (MXFP4):** FOCUS leads with **64.14%** GSM8K and **59.09%** MMLU, surpassing MR-GPTQ by roughly **4 points** on both.
- **Quantization cost (Table 6):** On one H20 GPU, FOCUS quantizes Qwen3-4B in **19 min** vs MR-GPTQ **29 min** (34% faster), and Qwen3-8B in **29 min** vs **40 min** (28% faster); peak GPU memory is higher (77 GB vs 45 GB on Qwen3-4B).
- **Ablation (Table 4, Qwen3-4B):** Adding CRS lifts NVFP4 average accuracy from the scale-learning baseline's 65.92% to 66.91% (+0.99%); adding DGS on top reaches 67.05%. Under MXFP4 the full method improves the baseline by +0.96%.
- **Sub-block size study (Table 5):** For MXFP4, halving the sub-block size from 32 (= CRS) to 16 lifts average accuracy from 64.81% to 65.65%; for NVFP4, size 8 and 4 perform similarly (67.05%), so size 8 is chosen for both formats.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine but incremental):** The central observation — that the quantization scale is never stored and so need not be hardware-compliant — is correct and underused, but it is conceptually adjacent to the authors' own SOAR (Bao et al. 2026), which already performs "decoupled scale search" for NVFP4, and to MR-GPTQ's MSE-based scale search. The CRS trick of `S_q = S_dq · c` is essentially a reparameterization that turns a discrete search into a continuous one; the engineering contribution (end-to-end gradient learning of FP4 scales) is the more novel part.
- **Rigor (mixed):** Ablations in Tables 4–5 properly isolate CRS and DGS and study sub-block size. However, the paper does **not directly compare against SOAR** (same group, NVFP4, decoupled scale search) in the results tables — it is only cited — leaving the strongest conceptual competitor unbenchmarked. The NVFP4 DGS benefit is structurally thin: block size 16 split into K=2 sub-blocks of 8 means only one extra coefficient per block, and Table 5 shows NVFP4 K=2 vs K=4 give identical 67.05%.
- **Reproducibility (good):** Hyperparameters, LR schedule, calibration data, optimizer, batch size, and the public GitHub commitment are all stated; single H20 GPU keeps the bar accessible. The higher peak memory (77 GB) is honestly reported.
- **Significance (moderate-to-high):** Recovering 98.2%/99.8% of FP16 on Qwen3-4B/8B NVFP4 with zero inference overhead is a meaningful datapoint for practical FP4 deployment, and the 28–34% calibration speedup over MR-GPTQ is useful.
- **Red flags / data quirks:**
  - The reasoning results expose a large absolute gap to FP16 that the headline recovery numbers obscure: LLaMA-3.1-8B GSM8K drops from 85.67% (FP16) to 73.31% (FOCUS, MXFP4) — a ~12-point loss — even though FOCUS "wins" the FP4 comparison. Multi-step reasoning is acknowledged as sensitive but the framing still leans on relative wins.
  - Evaluation is capped at 8B parameters; no 30B/70B/100B+ model is tested, so scaling claims are extrapolated.
  - MXFP4 NVFP4 comparison omits format-specific strong baselines (RaZeR/4over6 only compared under NVFP4; no 4over6/RaZeR analog under MXFP4).
  - Table 5 MXFP4 sub-block sizes 16 and 8 yield identical 65.65% Wiki2 (12.98 vs 12.85) — the "8 is best" choice is partly driven by Wiki2/C4 ties, not a clean signal.
  - Authors conflate their own prior SOAR framing with FOCUS without a head-to-head; readers cannot tell whether FOCUS strictly dominates SOAR or trades off against it.

## Strengths

- Exploits a genuinely overlooked asymmetry (quantization scale is free at inference) with a clean, minimal mathematical formulation.
- End-to-end gradient-based FP4 scale learning is a real advance over rule-based per-layer heuristics (MR-GPTQ, 4over6, RaZeR).
- Zero inference overhead — the deployed model is bit-exact standard MXFP4/NVFP4, and orthogonality to Hadamard methods keeps it composable.
- Faster calibration than the previous SOTA while delivering better accuracy.
- Strong, consistent results across Qwen3 and LLaMA-3 families on both FP4 formats and on perplexity, zero-shot, and CoT reasoning benchmarks.

## Limitations

- No evaluation on models larger than 8B; behavior on 70B+ architectures is unverified.
- The reasoning gap to FP16 remains large (e.g., ~12 points on GSM8K for LLaMA-3.1-8B), which the relative-recovery framing can mask.
- Peak GPU memory during calibration is ~1.7× MR-GPTQ (77 vs 45 GB on Qwen3-4B), which constrains who can reproduce.
- DGS yields marginal gains on NVFP4 because the small native block size (16) leaves little room for sub-partitioning beyond K=2.
- No head-to-head comparison with the authors' own SOAR, the closest prior decoupled-scale method for NVFP4.
- Calibration uses only 1,248 WikiText2 samples; sensitivity to calibration set distribution is not studied.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 高效推理与低比特量化（尤其是 FP4/MXFP4/NVFP4 硬件原生格式）的研究者高度相关：FOCUS 提供了一个零推理开销、可叠加变换类方法、且在 Qwen3/LLaMA-3 上接近 FP16 精度的 PTQ 方案，可作为后续 FP4 部署与量化尺度优化的强基线。其"量化尺度无需硬件合规"的洞察也为设计新的低比特量化算法提供了通用思路。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does FOCUS scale to 30B–405B parameter models, where the per-block weight distribution is heavier-tailed and the gains from finer-grained scales may be larger — or smaller?
- What is the head-to-head accuracy/speed trade-off between FOCUS and SOAR (the authors' own decoupled-scale NVFP4 method), which is cited but not benchmarked?
- Can CRS's relaxation coefficient `c_i` be replaced by a low-rank or shared structure to cut the calibration-time memory spike (77 GB on Qwen3-4B) without hurting accuracy?
- Since FOCUS is orthogonal to Hadamard transforms, what is the cumulative accuracy when stacked with QuaRot/MR-GPTQ-style rotations on the hardest benchmarks (long-context, code, math)?
- Why does DGS plateau at sub-block size 8 for NVFP4 (K=2 vs K=4 identical)? Is the gain bound by the small native block size, and would a learned per-element coefficient help further?
- How sensitive are the 98–100% recovery numbers to the 1,248-sample WikiText2 calibration set when evaluated on out-of-distribution tasks (multilingual, code, long-context)?
