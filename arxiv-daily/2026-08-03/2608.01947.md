> **一句话总结：** 本文提出循环除法归一化网络 (RDNN)，将皮层中广泛观察到的除法归一化作为乘性增益控制机制嵌入到双群 (兴奋性 R + 抑制性 G) RNN 中，通过 BPTT 训练后在角速度积分与记忆引导扫视两个连续工作记忆任务上学习到高保真、近似连续吸引子的慢流形，并自发地把有效秩压缩到基线 (GRU/LSTM) 的约 1/6 (H=256 时 ~38 vs ~244)，消融实验进一步证明除法 (而非减法) 抑制对于抵抗时变输入下的流形碎片化是数学上必要的。

> **TL;DR:** The paper proposes the Recurrent Divisive Normalization Network (RDNN), a minimal two-population (excitatory R + divisive inhibitory G) RNN that uses element-wise shunting division to emulate a canonical cortical computation. On Angular Velocity Integration and Memory-guided Saccade tasks, RDNN learns high-fidelity approximate continuous attractors (forming reliable ring manifolds in ~90% of runs vs <50% for GRU/LSTM), self-compresses its 99%-energy effective rank to ~38 vs ~244 for baselines at H=256 (a ~6× reduction), and an ablation shows divisive — not subtractive — inhibition is mathematically essential to prevent manifold shattering under time-varying inputs.

## 核心贡献

- **生物启发的连续记忆架构：** 提出循环除法归一化网络 (RDNN)，将除法归一化作为最小化、代数上可隔离的动态除法模块，克服标准 GRU/LSTM 把连续状态空间碎裂成离散点吸引子的拓扑离散化倾向。
- **突现的低秩动力学：** 从 BPTT 梯度动力学分析中指出，动态除数 (η+G) 对高活动方向产生活动依赖的局部梯度缩放 (梯度衰减)，使 RDNN 在无显式低秩参数化的情况下，经验性地收敛到有效秩远低于基线的低维子空间，且避开了显式低秩分解的非凸优化病态。
- **抑制机制的功能分野 (消融)：** 通过系统的减法 vs 除法消融，界定两类抑制的功能生态位——减法抑制可维持静态 (零输入) 记忆，但乘性除法增益控制在时变外部输入下防止"吸引子破碎"是数学必要的。
- **跨架构与高维泛化：** 在 ORGaNICs、Neural ODE、Autoformer 以及二维环面 (Double Angular Velocity Integration, T²) 任务上的补充实验表明，除法归一化作为典范计算在多种实现下都稳定地促成慢流形，而缺乏该机制的连续时间基线丧失了正常双曲性。

## Key Contributions

- **Biologically-inspired architecture for continuous memory:** Introduces the RDNN, a minimal algebraically-isolated model of dynamic division (excitatory population R divided element-wise by an auxiliary inhibitory pool G) that overcomes the topological discretization inherent in standard GRU/LSTM architectures.
- **Emergent low-rank dynamics without explicit factorization:** Derives the BPTT gradient dynamics showing the dynamic divisor (η+G) acts as an activity-dependent gradient attenuator in highly active regimes; empirically, RDNN self-compresses to sub-linear effective rank without the non-convex optimization pathologies of hard low-rank factorization (which exhibits a higher-rank convergence anomaly).
- **Mechanistic ablation delineating divisive vs. subtractive inhibition:** Demonstrates that while subtractive inhibition suffices for static (zero-input) memory maintenance, multiplicative gain control is mathematically essential to prevent manifold shattering under time-varying external inputs.
- **Generalizability across implementations and higher-dimensional manifolds:** Supplementary experiments on ORGaNICs, Neural ODEs, Autoformer, and a 2-D torus (Double Angular Velocity Integration) task confirm that divisive normalization as a canonical computation consistently facilitates stable slow manifolds across implementations and dimensions.

## Methodology

The RDNN is a continuous-time stochastic dynamical system with two interacting non-negative populations: a principal excitatory population R ∈ ℝ₊^H (the working-memory state) and an auxiliary inhibitory pool G ∈ ℝ₊^H providing dynamic gain control. The dynamics are governed by coupled SDEs:

- τ_R dR = (−R + (Jf(R) + I(t)) ⊘ (η + G)) dt + σ_R dW_R
- τ_G dG = (−G + Wf(R)) dt + σ_G dW_G

where ⊘ denotes element-wise (shunting) division, f(·) is ReLU or Tanh, and J, W, η, τ_R, τ_G are learnable and constrained positive (Softplus/sigmoid parameterization) so the network respects Dale's law as a positive dynamical system. Discretization uses Euler-Maruyama; the inhibitory pool G dynamically tracks R via Wf(R), proportionally scaling the denominator to stabilize explosive positive feedback while preserving relative activation patterns. Decoding is linear (y_t = W_out R_t + b_out).

Training uses Adam, 100 epochs (200 for low-rank variants), batch size 64, gradient clipping at 1.0, Gaussian state noise σ=0.1 injected per step, and online-generated data. Two canonical tasks evaluate (i) input-driven updating (Angular Velocity Integration: integrate a stochastic 1-D angular velocity onto a ring output [sin θ, cos θ]) and (ii) autonomous maintenance (Memory-guided Saccade: hold a cued angle through a zero-input delay, then saccade on Go Cue). Analyses include fixed-point extraction on slow manifolds (32× task-length autonomous relaxation, 256 angular bins), automatic topological classification via Jacobian eigenvalues (stable/marginal/saddle, with tolerance ϵ=10⁻³), limit-cycle Floquet analysis, complex-eigenvalue spectra, uniform (L∞) flow norms, and 99%-energy effective rank of the merged recurrent weight matrix.

A subtractive ablation replaces the divisive gate with R ← max(0, R_t−1 + α_R(Jf(R_t−1) + I_t − G_t−1)), and an explicit low-rank variant factorizes J = J₁J₂, W = W₁W₂ with bottleneck ranks r ∈ {8,16,24,32}.

## Results & Highlights

- **Effective-rank self-compression (Table 1):** At H=256 on Angular Velocity Integration, the RDNN's 99%-energy effective rank is 38.0±6.7 (ReLU) / 35.4±5.3 (Tanh), versus 240.8±0.4 for GRU (ReLU) and 244.0±0.0 for LSTM (Tanh) — a roughly 6× reduction; GRU/LSTM ranks scale linearly with H while RDNN scales sub-linearly.
- **Manifold reliability (Figure A5):** The RDNN forms reliable, topologically correct ring manifolds in ~90% of trained instances, while GRU and LSTM yield reliable manifolds in fewer than 50% of cases.
- **Baseline convergence failure:** Both GRU and LSTM completely failed to learn the Memory-guided Saccade task across all tested learning rates (Figure A4); the RDNN is the only model that converges, with its memory-guided-saccade manifold overwhelmingly dominated by marginal fixed points (uniform flow norm ~10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴) approximating a true continuous attractor.
- **Test NMSE (Table A2, H=128 RDNN):** Angular Velocity Integration reaches −15.6±0.7 dB at the best LR (5×10⁻³) versus −10.2±0.5 dB at LR=10⁻³; Memory-guided Saccade reaches −29.8±1.0 dB versus −21.0±1.1 dB at the worse LR.
- **Explicit low-rank self-compression (Section 5.1):** Regardless of the explicit bottleneck r ∈ {8,16,24,32} applied to an H=128 RDNN, the trained network compresses its effective rank back to ~3.5–4.0 (integration) and ~2.0 (saccade) — far below the unconstrained RDNN's rank and revealing the intrinsically low-dimensional nature of the slow manifold.
- **Higher-rank convergence anomaly (Figure 3B):** Wider bottlenecks (r=32) converge more slowly with massive variance across seeds, while tighter bottlenecks (r=8) converge faster and more stably — a counter-intuitive signature of non-convex matrix-factorization symmetries avoided by the unconstrained RDNN.
- **Divisive vs. subtractive ablation (Figure 4 & Table A1):** Under time-varying inputs (integration task), the Subtractive Network's manifold shatters into discretized stable fixed points and recruits a significantly higher effective rank than the RDNN; under zero-input saccade both mechanisms form equivalent marginal-fixed-point ring attractors, isolating division's role to the input-driven regime.
- **Long-horizon trade-off (Appendix N, Figure A30):** Over 2048 steps the RDNN's flat manifold diffuses (error grows continuously) whereas the subtractive network's discrete basins bound error via an error-correcting "lock-in" effect, exposing a complementary cortical synergy.
- **Higher-dimensional T² torus (Appendix O):** On Double Angular Velocity Integration the RDNN maintains superior NMSE with low-rank limit-cycle-dominated topology, whereas GRU/LSTM discretize the 2-D torus into a grid of stable point attractors.
- **FLOPs/parameters (Table A4):** The RDNN requires 4H² FLOPs/step — cheaper than GRU (6H²), LSTM (8H²), ORGaNICs (12H²), and Neural ODE-RK4 (16H²).

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from the School of System Science and the State Key Laboratory of Cognitive Neuroscience and Learning at Beijing Normal University, and Qiyana (Qiyuan) Laboratory. Dahui Wang (corresponding) has a long track record on divisive-normalization and cortical computation modeling. Zhaotian Gu and Jie Su are equal-contribution first authors. The work is positioned not as a performance benchmark but as a mechanistic computational-neuroscience study that explicitly acknowledges its objective is to investigate how a biophysical constraint shapes learned representations rather than to maximize raw task performance. The paper provides extensive supplementary appendices (A–P, 46 pages total) with full derivations, ablations, ORGaNICs/Neural-ODE/Autoformer comparisons, parameter counts, and long-horizon analyses — a level of rigor consistent with NeurIPS-grade computational neuroscience.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Real and well-scoped. Unlike ORGaNICs (Heeger & Mackey 2019; Rawat et al. 2024), which bundles DN with multiple biophysical components, the RDNN is purposefully minimal to isolate the algebraic role of division. The BPTT gradient-scaling derivation tying divisive normalization to rank self-compression, and the higher-rank convergence anomaly for explicit factorizations, are genuinely new mechanistic insights. However, the core observation that gated RNNs shatter continuous manifolds is largely inherited from Jordan et al. (2021) and Ságodi et al. (2024); the paper's contribution is to explain what inductive bias fixes it.
- **Rigor:** Strong on dynamical-systems analysis (exact-Jacobian fixed-point classification, Floquet analysis, complex-eigenvalue spectra, uniform-flow norms, Lyapunov-style noise propagation). Statistical reporting uses 5 seeds with std; effective-rank and topology claims are corroborated by both merged- and component-matrix analyses (Appendix J). A specific weak point: results are reported in NMSE (dB) in figures but only the H=128 learning-rate table (Table A2) gives numeric values — the main NMSE comparison in Figure 1C is graphical only, making exact deltas against GRU/LSTM hard to extract from the main text.
- **Reproducibility:** Reasonable. Architecture, Softplus/sigmoid parameterization, initialization (negative-mean raw weights to avoid explosive positive products), tasks, hyperparameters, and 5-seed protocol are documented in Appendices B–D. Code is referenced as trainable on CPU in 10–20 minutes with ~800 MB memory, lowering the reproduction bar. However, no public code/data repository URL is provided in the body text I reviewed.
- **Significance:** Conceptually significant for computational neuroscience (a mechanistic answer to why DN is canonical) and a useful inductive-bias blueprint for ML, but the practical ML impact is narrow: validation is restricted to toy 1-D/2-D ring/torus tasks with T≈tens-of-steps horizons, and the authors themselves concede engineering-scale generalization (navigation, robotics) is untested.
- **Red flags / paper-specific concerns:** (1) The "higher-rank convergence anomaly" is partly explained by well-known non-convex matrix-factorization theory (Arora 2019; Li 2019) — the framing as a novel discovery slightly overstates novelty. (2) The central claim that gradient attenuation "empirically aligns with" rank self-compression is hedged ("does not formally minimize a global rank metric") — the link is correlational, not a proof. (3) GRU/LSTM are weak baselines: they are explicitly reported as failing to converge on the saccade task and as shattering on integration, so the most flattering comparisons (effective rank, reliability) are against models that the authors themselves show are structurally mismatched; no modern continuous-time gated or low-rank RNN baseline (e.g., Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic-style low-rank RNN trained end-to-end) is included. (4) The ReLU-LSTM variant is excluded entirely due to untrainability, removing a configuration that might have changed the rank comparison. (5) Long-horizon analysis (Appendix N) quietly concedes the RDNN's flat manifold diffuses over 2000 steps while the subtractive network's discretization bounds error — a honest but important caveat that tempers the "high-fidelity" framing.

## Strengths

- Clean, minimal model that isolates one biophysical operation (dynamic division) and supports every claim with multiple converging dynamical-systems analyses.
- The BPTT gradient-attenuation derivation and the higher-rank convergence anomaly are mechanistically illuminating and transferable insights for low-rank RNN design.
- Honest, extensive appendices including ORGaNICs, Neural ODE, Autoformer, double-integration T² torus, parameter counts, long-horizon trade-offs, and a candid subtractive-vs-divisive ablation.
- Strong inductive bias yields a ~6× effective-rank compression and ~90% manifold reliability without any explicit low-rank bottleneck, while remaining cheaper (4H² FLOPs) than all baselines.

## Limitations

- Validated only on canonical low-dimensional cognitive toy tasks (1-D ring, 2-D torus) with short horizons; no large-scale engineering benchmarks, navigation, or robotics evaluations.
- The gradient-attenuation → low-rank link is empirically correlated, not formally proven as an explicit rank-minimization mechanism.
- BPTT training is acknowledged as biologically implausible; whether Hebbian or predictive-coding rules reproduce the same topologies is left open.
- Divisive normalization is studied in isolation; synergy with subtractive thresholding, synaptic scaling, and other homeostatic mechanisms is unmodeled.
- The flat RDNN manifold suffers asymptotic random-walk drift over long horizons (Appendix N), so subtractive discretization may actually be preferable for very-long-timescale stability — a functional trade-off the paper surfaces but does not resolve.
- Predicted eigenvalue spectra and effective-rank claims await empirical validation with large-scale neural population recordings.

## Relevance

This work is highly relevant if your interests include biologically-plausible recurrent architectures, continuous attractor / slow-manifold dynamics, low-rank RNN theory, or inductive biases that bridge neuroscience and deep learning — it offers a concrete, mechanistically transparent recipe (dynamic divisive gain control) for getting standard RNNs to learn genuine continuous representations and low-dimensional dynamics without explicit factorization.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the rank self-compression and normal-hyperbolicity of the RDNN persist on higher-dimensional engineering tasks (e.g., 2-D path integration for robotics, continuous control, or spatiotemporal forecasting beyond ring/torus topologies)?
- Can biologically-plausible learning rules (Hebbian, predictive-coding, or feedback alignment) reproduce the same spectral gap and low-rank structure that BPTT induces here, or is the effect an artifact of gradient-based optimization?
- How does combining divisive normalization with subtractive thresholding (mimicking cortical coexistence) shape the short-horizon fidelity vs. long-horizon drift trade-off surfaced in Appendix N?
- Would end-to-end-trained explicit low-rank RNNs (à la Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic 2018) close the effective-rank gap with the RDNN, weakening the claim that division is uniquely responsible for low-dimensionality?
- Can the activity-dependent gradient-scaling mechanism be ported as a normalization-style module into transformers or modern SSMs (e.g., Mamba) to encourage low-dimensional continuous representations there?
