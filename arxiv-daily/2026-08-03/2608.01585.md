> **一句话总结：** 该工作用拓扑数据分析方法（持续景观/轮廓 + 能量统计同质性检验）刻画 LLM 嵌入空间的语义结构，在 Llama-3.2-1B 的 LoRA 概念坍缩追踪、OLMo-2-0425-1B 的 16 层与 267 个训练 checkpoint 演化、以及 WMT14 上 8 个翻译模型的三语对（法/德/印-英）对齐三项实验中，证明该方法可敏感捕捉嵌入语义的细微变化。

> **TL;DR:** The paper applies topological data analysis (persistence landscapes/silhouettes + energy-statistic homogeneity tests) to compare LLM embedding spaces against curated baselines (equidistant embeddings, base models, translated-text pairs). It tracks LoRA-induced concept collapse on Llama-3.2-1B (significance retained even for subtle adaptations where Wasserstein/JS distances fail), follows OLMo-2-0425-1B across 16 layers and 267 checkpoints, and ranks 8 translation models on WMT14 French/German/Hindi-English pairs, finding embedding models generally outperform generative ones (e.g., BGE-M3 is the best general pick, MADLAD-400 best for Hindi-English, Qwen3-Embedding-8B best for German-English).

## 核心贡献

- 提出基于拓扑数据分析的语义结构对齐检验框架：将嵌入空间转为对同态变换不变、与维度无关的二维持续景观（landscapes）和轮廓（silhouettes），再用能量统计做正式的同质性假设检验，并用 Benjamini–Yekutieli 校正多重比较，工具开源为 `persiscope`（PyPI / GitHub `tylerashoff/persiscope`）。
- 首次用该方法可追踪地刻画由对抗性 LoRA 适配诱发的「概念坍缩」：随着 LoRA 强度（α/r 比例从 0.125 升至 8）增加，模型嵌入空间系统性趋向等距基线、远离原始基座模型。
- 在 OLMo-2-0425-1B（4T tokens、16 层、hidden 2048、共 267 个 checkpoint）上，可视化跨层与跨训练阶段的语义结构演化：早期层变化剧烈、后期层平滑精炼；中期训练（mid-training）阶段在学习率衰减至 0 时三组「ingredient」模型的语义结构收敛。
- 在 WMT14 的法-英、德-英、印-英三语对上比较 8 个翻译模型（含 4 个嵌入模型 BGE-M3、LaBSE、multilingual-e5-large、Qwen3-Embedding-8B 与 4 个生成式模型 BART、TranslateGemma、MADLAD-400、NLLB-200），证明该框架可基于「意义相同应结构对齐」做模型选择。
- 证明能量统计比 Wasserstein 距离与 Jensen–Shannon 距离更具区分力：在 LoRA 概念坍缩的细微适配中，能量统计仍保持显著性，而曲线相似度指标只在更激进适配下才显著。

## Key Contributions

- A topology-based semantic alignment test that converts high-dimensional embedding spaces into stable, dimension-agnostic 2D persistence landscapes/silhouettes and runs a formal homogeneity test via energy statistics with Benjamini–Yekutieli multiple-comparison correction; released as the open-source `persiscope` package.
- First demonstration that induced concept collapse is *trackable*: as adversarial LoRA strength (α/r from 0.125 to 8) increases on Llama-3.2-1B, the embedding space monotonically drifts toward an equidistant baseline and away from the base model.
- A "discovery"-style layer/checkpoint analysis on OLMo-2-0425-1B (4T training tokens, 16 layers, hidden size 2048, 267 published checkpoints) that visualizes how semantic structure sharpens across layers and stabilizes during mid-training once the learning rate decays to 0.
- A cross-lingual alignment study on WMT14 using 200 translation pairs for each of French-, German-, and Hindi-English, comparing 4 embedding models (BGE-M3, LaBSE, multilingual-e5-large, Qwen3-Embedding-8B) and 4 generative models (BART-large, TranslateGemma-4b, MADLAD-400-3b, NLLB-200-distilled-600m) under a single framework.
- Evidence that energy statistics are more discriminative than Wasserstein / Jensen–Shannon curve distances, retaining statistical significance for the subtlest LoRA adaptations where the curve-similarity metrics do not.

## Methodology

The core procedure (Algorithm in Table 3) is a formal two-sample homogeneity test on embedding spaces:

1. **Embedding generation.** For a fixed set of inputs, collect embeddings from two models (or two layers/checkpoints/languages). For word-level tests, archetype words (`break`, `cut`, `run`, `play` — chosen as the words with the most WordNet synsets) are placed in example sentences from WordNet so that contextual embeddings can be extracted; only words that are *not* subtokenized are kept (358 samples for OLMo, 284 for Llama).
2. **Topological representation.** Subsample, compute persistence diagrams, rotate/rescale (with an extra H₀ rotation), and convert to persistence landscapes (Bubenik et al., 2015) or persistence silhouettes (Chazal et al., 2014). These are stable, connectivity-focused 2D curves invariant to homomorphic transformations and agnostic to embedding dimensionality.
3. **Energy statistic.** Compute the energy statistic T between the two sets of landscape/silhouette curves (energy statistics per You et al., 2022; Szekely & Rizzo, 2004).
4. **Permutation test.** Pool the curves, randomly split B times, recompute T(b), and derive a p-value `p = (1 + #{b : T ≤ T(b)}) / (B + 1)`; reject H₀ (equal semantic structure) at level α.
5. **Multiple-comparison correction** via Benjamini–Yekutieli.

Three application modes are demonstrated: (a) **concept collapse** — LoRA adapters on Llama-3.2-1B trained via prompt engineering to convince the model that every word-of-interest means "to go to an aquarium," with α/r swept from 0.125 to 8, then compared both to an equidistant baseline and to the base model; (b) **training dynamics** — comparing OLMo-2-0425-1B across its 16 layers and a sampled 15+15 subset of the 267 training checkpoints (stage-1 pretraining vs stage-2 mid-training "ingredients"); (c) **cross-lingual alignment** — embedding each side of WMT14 translation pairs with 8 models and comparing the two language embeddings directly (energy statistic of 0 = exact structural alignment). Auxiliary Wasserstein (for landscapes) and Jensen–Shannon (for silhouettes) distances are reported alongside as consistency checks.

## Results & Highlights

- **Concept-collapse sensitivity vs curve distances.** On the Llama-3.2-1B LoRA sweep (α/r = 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8), the energy statistic on landscapes/silhouettes retains statistical significance (star-rated `*`) even for the *subtle* 0.25–0.5 adaptations when comparing to the base model, whereas both the Wasserstein distance and the Jensen–Shannon distance only become significant (`**`/`*`) for the more aggressive α/r ≥ 1 adaptations — directly demonstrating that the topological energy test is more discriminative than standard curve-similarity metrics.
- **Monotonic collapse trajectory.** As LoRA aggression increases, adapted-model embeddings move progressively toward the equidistant baseline (downward trend in Figure 3, all comparisons significant) and progressively away from the base Llama-3.2-1B model (upward trend in Figure 4), confirming the method tracks induced semantic degradation sample-by-sample.
- **Word-of-interest coverage.** After dropping subtokenized words, the concept-collapse study retains 358 word samples for the OLMo model and 284 for the Llama model, with 4 archetype words (`break`, `cut`, `run`, `play`) drawn from WordNet synsets.
- **Layer-wise structure on OLMo-2-0425-1B** (4T training tokens, 16 layers, hidden size 2048). Compared to the final layer, early layers show sharp changes and later layers change smoothly; compared to an equidistant baseline, deeper layers become progressively *more dissimilar* to equidistant embeddings (Figure 6), indicating semantic structure intensifies toward the output.
- **Checkpoint dynamics across 267 checkpoints.** Across the sampled 15 stage-1 + 15 stage-2 (5 per "ingredient") checkpoints, stage-1 pretraining shows a general but inconsistent trend toward the initialized model; stage-2 mid-training shows dissimilar structures while the learning rate is high, with all three "ingredient" curves converging as the learning rate decays to 0 (Figures 7–9), matching Ai2's "model souping" recipe.
- **Cross-lingual alignment on WMT14** (200 translation pairs × 3 language pairs × 8 models). Embedding models generally outperform generative models on structural alignment; BGE-M3 is the best *general* choice, MADLAD-400-3b-mt is best for Hindi-English, and Qwen3-Embedding-8B is strongest on German-English but falls behind even generative models on French-English and Hindi-English alignment (Figures 10–12).
- **Discriminative advantage.** Across the WMT14 results, energy-statistic p-values flag significant differences (`*`) for essentially all model-vs-model comparisons, while Wasserstein/JS distances on landscapes frequently fail to reach significance (`ns`) — recapitulating the higher discriminative power observed in the LoRA experiment.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The application of persistence landscapes/silhouettes plus energy-statistic homogeneity tests to *LLM* embedding spaces (as opposed to general latent-space comparison as in You et al., 2022) is a reasonable extension, but the methodological core is imported wholesale from the companion paper (arXiv:2607.29008) and the dissertation; this paper is essentially a three-experiment demonstration/application note rather than a new method.
- **Rigor.** The use of Benjamini–Yekutieli correction and permutation-based p-values is statistically sound, and the inclusion of two corroborating curve distances (Wasserstein, JSD) is a genuine strength. However, the headline claim that energy statistics are "more discriminative" rests on visual interpretation of star-significance patterns in figures — there is no head-to-head power analysis, no multiple-seed variance reporting, and no formal test of *how much* more discriminative.
- **Missing ablations.** Several design choices are unexamined: the impact of bootstrap count n, permutation count B, subsample size, the choice of archetype words, and the rotation/rescale transformation step. The concept-collapse study deliberately discards subtokenized words, leaving only 358/284 samples — a quirk that limits how representative the "concept space" is, but no sensitivity analysis to this filtering is given.
- **Weakness of the cross-lingual comparison.** The "baseline" for cross-lingual alignment is internal (one language vs the other), and the authors concede WMT14 is old and "likely ingested" by the newer models, which contaminates the alignment comparison as a measure of true cross-lingual capability. There is no BLEU/BLEURT/comet correlation reported to validate that better alignment corresponds to better translation quality.
- **Reproducibility.** Open-source code (persiscope on PyPI) and exclusive use of public Hugging Face models/checkpoints (Llama-3.2-1B, OLMo-2-0425-1B with all 267 checkpoints, WMT14) are strong positives; the LoRA training prompts and α/r grid are fully specified. The main reproducibility gap is the absence of reported random seeds and confidence intervals on the energy-statistic curves themselves (only pointwise CIs on the landscapes are shown).
- **Significance.** As a diagnostic/interpretability tool the work is useful and well-motivated by the saturation of MMLU/HELM/BIG-Bench, but the demonstrated applications are illustrative rather than actionable safety evaluations — no real malicious adaptation is detected in the wild, only a self-induced one.

## Strengths

- Single, principled framework (TDA + energy statistics) that unifies model-vs-baseline, model-vs-model, layer-vs-layer, checkpoint-vs-checkpoint, and language-vs-language comparisons.
- Dimension-agnostic and invariant to homomorphic transformations, so it can compare embedding spaces across model families without re-alignment.
- Genuinely more sensitive than Wasserstein/JSD in the controlled LoRA collapse regime, which is the most compelling empirical finding.
- Excellent reproducibility posture: open-source package, all models/checkpoints/datasets are public.
- Clear, honest writing that positions the method as complementary to (not a replacement for) outcome-based benchmarks.

## Limitations

- Methodological novelty is largely in the companion paper; this paper is application-focused.
- Concept-collapse experiments use only non-subtokenized words (358/284 samples) and self-induced adversarial LoRAs, not naturally occurring misalignment.
- WMT14 is likely contaminated by training data of the evaluated models, weakening cross-lingual claims.
- No correlation with downstream task metrics (e.g., translation quality) to validate that "better alignment" is practically better.
- No power analysis, seed variation, or sensitivity analysis for hyperparameters (bootstrap count, permutation count, subsample size, archetype choice).
- Quantitative results are mostly presented as figure-based significance stars rather than tabulated effect sizes, making precise numerical comparison across models difficult.

## Relevance

该工作属于 LLM 可解释性与评估方向，与对模型嵌入空间结构、对齐性及评测基准的兴趣高度相关：它提供了一个不依赖输出正确性的「语义结构层」诊断工具，可补充 MMLU/HELM 等已饱和的产出型基准，对理解训练动态与跨语言对齐尤其有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does embedding-space alignment energy on translation pairs correlate with established translation quality metrics (BLEU, COMET, BLEURT) across a broader set of language pairs and model scales?
- Can the energy-statistic test detect naturally occurring misalignment (e.g., from real-world RLHF or fine-tuning) rather than only self-induced LoRA concept collapse?
- How sensitive are the results to the bootstrap count n, permutation count B, subsample size, and the choice of archetype words — and is there a principled way to set them?
- Would the layer-wise structure pattern (sharp early, smooth late) generalize to non-transformer or MoE architectures, or to models trained with substantially different data mixes?
- Can the framework provide an *early-warning* signal during training that predicts downstream benchmark saturation or safety regression before it appears in outcome metrics?
