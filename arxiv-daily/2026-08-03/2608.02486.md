> **一句话总结：** 在 27 个 Thompson 民俗母题 × 10 种文化（共 270 个实体）的平行基准上对 18 个开源 LLM 做线性探针、logit lens、激活修补与生成四种测量，发现模型残差流能线性读出文化标签（探针峰值 0.61–0.88），但解码器把小众文化的正确名字坍缩到希腊-罗马主流传统上（生成准确率仅 0.09–0.43），即"偏见在解码端而非表示端"；规模只缩小不闭合该鸿沟，而跨语言查询在语言内相关 0.57、跨语言仅 0.29，双语言集成可零训练成本把恢复率提升 +36% 相对值。

> **TL;DR:** On a parallel cross-cultural grid of 27 Thompson folklore motifs × 10 cultures (270 entities), the authors apply linear probing, logit lens, activation patching, and greedy generation to 18 open-source LLMs (1.2B–34B, 8 families). The residual stream linearly separates culture at 0.61–0.88 (well above a 0.60 name-string baseline), yet free-generation accuracy collapses to 0.09–0.43 — a "DecodingSuppressed" failure mode that dominates 51–76% of cells in every model; scaling shrinks but never closes the gap (Llama 1B→8B: 0.70→0.63), prompt-language gating is the structural cause (r_within=0.57 vs r_cross=0.29), and a zero-cost bilingual ensemble recovers +36% relative over the best single mode.

## 核心贡献

- **定位文化偏见的因果机制到解码端（unembedding），而非残差流表示端。** 这是本文区别于 CultureScope（在表示空间测纠缠）的关键：通过 (探针是否正确 × 生成是否正确) 的 2×2 分解，证明 DecodingSuppressed（探针对、生成错）在 18 个模型的每一个中都是最大单元（51–76%，均值 0.65），而 RepresentationallyFlat（表示都未编码）只占 0.10–0.33（均值 0.18）。
- **构建了带引用锚点的平行跨文化 ground truth：27 个 Thompson 民俗母题 × 10 种文化 = 270 个实体。** 每个 (motif, culture) 单元都有指向原始文本（Eddas、Kalevala、Rig Veda、Pyramid Texts、Kojiki、Enuma Elish 等）或现代学术专著的引用锚点，使探针 vs 生成对比在每个单元都可解释。同时明确排除 4 个结构性缺失单元（日本/芬兰洪水、美索不达米亚盗火、埃及森林精灵）。
- **设计了四种互补的机制性工具并把它们联合解读：** 线性探针 E1（10 类文化标签的残差流可读性）、logit lens E2（gold token 进入 top-k 的深度）、激活修补 E3（替换残差流后 gold-target 概率翻转的因果定位）、生成提取 E4（EN/NL 两模式的贪婪解码）。核心论证策略是"每个工具只回答一个问题，论文的论证来自它们的合取"。
- **提出 within- vs cross-mode 相关性检验作为语言条件解码的结构性诊断。** 在 18 个 chat-template 模型上得到 r_within=0.57 vs r_cross=0.29（差距 0.27，p ≤ 0.01 per model），证明解码器受 prompt 语言门控，并对"LLM 是翻译机"的强解读提出定量反驳。
- **零训练成本的跨语言集成方案：** 因为 EN 和 NL 门控了同一表示中部分不相交的子集，简单取并集（5 NL OR 5 EN paraphrases）即可把单元恢复率提升 +0.08 绝对、+36% 相对，且 same-language 控制实验证明收益来自语言切换而非 paraphrase 数量。

## Key Contributions

- **Mechanistically localizes cultural bias to the unembedding / decoder, not the residual stream.** A per-cell (probe, output) 2×2 decomposition shows DecodingSuppressed (probe correct, output wrong) is the largest cell in every one of the 18 models (51–76%, mean 0.65), while RepresentationallyFlat is consistently small (0.10–0.33, mean 0.18) — direct quantitative pushback on the "LLMs are translation machines" reading.
- **Releases a citation-anchored parallel cross-cultural substrate: 27 Thompson motifs × 10 cultures = 270 entities**, each with a primary-text or monograph citation anchor, four flagged structural absences, and contested-fill adjudication (e.g., Khors vs Dažbog, Mielikki vs Rauni). This makes per-cell probe-vs-generation comparison interpretable.
- **Four complementary mechanistic instruments (E1–E4) read jointly.** Linear probe, logit lens, activation patching, and greedy chat-template generation — each answers exactly one question; the argument is the conjunction: culture is encoded (E1), encoded before decoded (E2), causally bound to output late (E3), still not emitted (E4).
- **Within- vs cross-language correlation test (r_within=0.57 vs r_cross=0.29) as a structural diagnostic of language-conditioned readout**, robust to the language-proficiency confound by construction, and corroborated by tokenizer-fertility not predicting per-cell deltas (r=−0.004) and Tiny-Aya-Global showing the smallest gap (0.004) where a capability account predicts the largest.
- **Zero-cost bilingual ensemble**: EN ∪ NL disjunction lifts cell recovery by +0.08 absolute / +36% relative over the best single mode, with a same-language control confirming the lift comes from language-switching rather than paraphrase count.

## Methodology

**Parallel cultural entity set (§3.1).** A (motif, culture) cell instantiates a Thompson Motif-Index role (e.g., A1141.2 "supreme sky god", A220 "sun god") across 10 cultures (Greek, Roman, Norse, Finnish, Ukrainian, Indian, Egyptian, Chinese, Japanese, Mesopotamian) spanning six language families (Indo-European, Uralic, Afro-Asiatic, Sino-Tibetan, Japonic, Akkadian-Sumerian). 27 motifs × 10 cultures = 270 entities. Each entity carries a romanized canonical name, native-script form, standardized description, and a per-row citation anchor to either a primary text or a modern academic monograph. Four cells are flagged structural absences and excluded from output scoring. Two prompts per entity: a **Contextual** prompt (`"${name} embodies the role of ${description}."` — entity first, culture word never present, so the probe must derive culture from name+role alone) for hidden-state extraction, and a **Chat** prompt (`"In ${culture} mythology, what is the name of the ${description}? Reply with only the proper name..."`) for generation.

**Models (§3.2).** 18 open-source decoder-only LLMs at fp16, ≤35B params, across 8 families: Llama 3.x (1.2B/3.2B/8B), Gemma 4 (E2B/E4B/31B/26B-A4B-MoE), Phi 4 (mini 3.8B / 14B), Qwen 1.x (1.5B/7B), Qwen 3.6 (27B / 35B-A3B-MoE), Yi 1.5 (6B/9B/34B), OLMo 3.1 (32B), Aya (tiny-aya-global 3B). Selection: open-source, decoder-only, ≤35B fp16 in a 4×A100 40GB slot. No further quantization, to avoid confounding cultural recall with quantization-induced degradation.

**Four mechanistic instruments (§3.3):**
- **E1 Linear probing**: per layer, average-pool residual-stream activations over the entity span, fit a 5-fold stratified ridge classifier (α=1.0) to predict the 10-way culture label; report layer-wise accuracy, peak layer, and a label-shuffled chance ceiling.
- **E2 Logit lens**: apply final norm + unembedding to every intermediate hidden state; report the depth at which the gold entity's first sub-token enters top-k (using top-k, not top-1, to reduce tokenization sensitivity). Raw (not tuned) lens for cross-model comparability.
- **E3 Activation patching**: for each cross-cultural prompt pair (Greek→Finnish, etc.), at each layer replace the source prompt's residual with the target's and measure the change in gold-target log-probability. Run at a sparse layer step (every 4th layer) on 5 motifs × 4 culture pairs per model to fit the A100 budget.
- **E4 Output extraction**: greedy decode ≤256 tokens of the chat-template form; score by exact match, substring match, or length-normalized Levenshtein ≥0.8; a positive on any counts as correct. Run in two modes: EN (English, 5 paraphrases) and NL (target-culture native language, 5 paraphrases). Qwen 3.6's `<think>` trace is disabled.

**2×2 decomposition (§3.3).** Combining per-cell E1 (probe right/wrong) and E4 (output right/wrong) yields four cells: **Preserved** (recognized and emitted), **DecodingSuppressed** (recognized but emits wrong name), **SurfaceLuck** (right name without internal disambiguation), **RepresentationallyFlat** (neither).

**Output-format control (§4.2).** An MCQ task whose options are the 10 parallel fillers of the same Thompson role, scored by restricted first-token log-probability (single forward pass, no generation), to separate selection from generation and match the probe's chance level (≈0.10).

## Results & Highlights

- **Decoding flattening is universal (RQ1).** 17 of 18 probes clear the 0.60 char-n-gram surface baseline (peaking 0.61–0.88; one-sided paired bootstrap + McNemar p<0.05 for all 17; sole exception Gemma-4-E2B at +0.011, n.s.). The same models emit the name at only **0.09–0.43**. The smallest gap in the sweep is **0.26 (Gemma-4-31B)**, the largest is **0.70 (Llama-3.2-1B)**. Across the 18-model sweep, **mean Probe=0.79, mean MCQ=0.67, mean EN=0.26, mean Best=0.26, mean Gap=0.53** (Table 2). DecodingSuppressed is the largest cell in every model (51–76%, mean 0.65).
- **The loss localizes at generation, not selection.** With the output format matched to the probe (MCQ control), selection accuracy 0.67 sits far above free generation 0.26, but generation still loses **~41 points**. On Roman cells, **49% of errors land on the same-motif Greek counterpart** (vs ~11% under a uniform error model) — the Greco-Roman collapse is visible even inside a pure selection task.
- **Decoding is uniformly late, encoding varies (RQ2).** The logit lens crosses its 5%-in-top-1 onset in the **last 12% of layers on every single model** (onset range 0.88–0.97, median 0.96). Greek and Roman cross the 5%-in-top-5 threshold first; the other eight cultures cross later by ~1.3 layers on a 32-layer model.
- **Causal localization to the late band (RQ3).** Activation-patch preference-flip rate peaks in the last quarter (peak depth 0.75–1.00, median 0.89; peak rate 0.40–0.95, median 0.75; median lift over baseline +0.55) — the same band as lens onset, at roughly 7× the early-network rate.
- **Scaling shrinks but never closes the gap (RQ4).** Llama 3.x 1.2B→8B: probe peak **0.79→0.88**, output **0.09→0.25**, gap **0.70→0.63**. All five families with multiple sizes show both probe and output rising and the gap shrinking, but none close it. A naive log-parameter extrapolation predicts only ≈+0.10 output gain per decade of parameters — insufficient to close the gap within an order of magnitude.
- **The decoder is gated on prompt language (RQ5).** Across 18 chat-template models: **r_within=0.57 vs r_cross=0.29** (gap 0.27, p ≤ 0.01 per model by permutation). Cross-language queries are ~2× more decoupled than within-language. Tokenizer fertility does not predict per-cell delta (r=−0.004, n=120). **Tiny-Aya-Global (3B, explicit multilingual pretraining) shows the smallest NL−EN gap in the sweep (0.004)** where a capability account predicts the largest.
- **Per-culture EN−NL delta tracks the canon's documentation language**, not script or speaker base: Greek +0.241 and Egyptian +0.177 (English-language scholarship) vs Chinese −0.071 and Finnish −0.058 (in-language curation). Mesopotamian control (both modes in English) bounds pure paraphrase noise at |Δ|≈0.04.
- **Bilingual ensemble**: EN ∪ NL disjunction lifts cell recovery by **+0.08 absolute / +36% relative** over the best single mode at zero training cost; a same-language control confirms the lift comes from language-switching rather than paraphrase count.
- **Robustness**: rescoring all 48,568 generations under three stricter criteria preserves per-model ranking at Pearson ≥ 0.87; per-(model, mode) 95% bootstrap CIs have median half-width 0.046. When a cell is wrong across all 5 paraphrases of a mode, the five wrong answers agree on their first word only **1.1% of the time** — the models explore, they do not converge on a confident substitute.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine. The closest prior work is CultureScope (Yu et al., 2025), which patches everyday cultural-knowledge prompts to score entanglement of low-documented cultures with dominant ones inside the representation. This paper asks the complementary question (representation vs readout failure) on a *parallel* citation-anchored mythology grid with one unambiguous gold entity per cell, which is what makes the per-cell (probe, output) decomposition possible. The within- vs cross-mode correlation test as a language-conditioned readout diagnostic is, to my knowledge, new.
- **Rigor**: High. Pre-registered-style 2×2 decomposition applied uniformly to 18 models; four instruments whose scopes are explicitly non-overlapping; statistical significance via paired bootstrap, McNemar, permutation, sign test; a strictness audit over 48,568 generations; three confound controls for the NL condition (positive control Tiny-Aya-Global, negative control Mesopotamian-English-with-parenthetical, structural within-vs-cross test). The Qwen-3.6-27B chat-template artefact (decodes to generic "Here" at every layer in 320/320 rows) is disclosed and worked around with a bare-prompt estimate, but explicit bare-prompt patching for that model is deferred.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong. Open-source models only, fp16, no quantization; full per-entity predictions, code, dataset card, and citation anchors released. The sparse activation-patching step (every 4th layer, 5 motifs × 4 culture pairs per model) is a disclosed budget compromise — a dense pass might shift peak-depth estimates by a layer or two.
- **Significance**: The mechanism-level finding ("the loss is the unembedding's, not the residual stream's") has direct mitigation implications: it predicts that output-side interventions (retrieval, reranking, instruction tuning on cultural QA, the bilingual ensemble) target the failure locus, while encoder-side continued pretraining on under-represented corpora helps only the smaller RepresentationallyFlat cell (0.10–0.33) and cannot directly affect the dominant DecodingSuppressed cell (0.51–0.76). That is a non-trivial, falsifiable claim for the cultural-bias mitigation literature.
- **Red flags / specific weaknesses**:
  - **NL prompts are LLM-assisted forward-then-back-translations, not native-speaker validated** for 7 of the 9 non-English languages (the two authors are native speakers of one NL language each). Per-culture EN−NL deltas are explicitly scoped as "directionally trustworthy at the gross level but await native-speaker review for absolute calibration" on Greek, Italian, Norwegian Bokmål, Hindi, MSA, Mandarin, Japanese — i.e., on most of the languages that drive the cross-lingual claim.
  - **Substrate is 270 cells, all inference at cell level**; ten cultures deliberately exclude sub-Saharan African, Native American, Polynesian, Australian, Arctic indigenous traditions, so per-culture NL/EN deltas may not generalize, especially to canons documented predominantly in non-Anglocentric sources. The mechanism is claimed to transfer "wherever the knowledge is attested in pretraining and a dominant-culture default exists to collapse onto" — but transfer beyond folklore is explicitly a conjecture, not a result.
  - **Linear ridge probe by design** (chosen for transparency, to avoid MLP over-fitting to surface co-occurrences); an MLP probe would lift absolute probe-peak accuracy but, as the authors note, cannot change the qualitative residual-vs-output ordering — yet this is a self-serving argument that sidesteps whether a stronger probe would erode the gap on smaller models.
  - **Greedy chat-template generation only**; beam or nucleus sampling would shift absolute output numbers (though the per-(model, culture) ranking is robust across the strictness audit).
  - **Open-source models only** (white-box access required for E1–E3); the headline DecodingSuppressed-dominance claim is established directly only for these 18. The NL/EN independence *prediction* generalizes to closed-source frontier models but is not tested on them.
  - The mean-best-of-(NL,EN) sometimes equals mean-EN exactly (Table 2: EN=Best=0.26 across the sweep), so the "Best" column understates the value of NL on the cultures/models where NL wins (Gemma-4-31B, Qwen-3.6-27B, Tiny-Aya-Global); the bilingual-ensemble lift is real but the table presentation obscures it.

## Strengths

- Mechanistic claim with causal (not merely correlational) backing: activation patching localizes the cultural readout to the same late band as the lens onset, making the encode-vs-decode separation mechanistic.
- Disciplined substrate construction with citation anchors, structural-absence flags, and contested-fill adjudication — exactly the parallel-by-construction property that probing-plus-generation needs.
- A genuinely useful, zero-cost mitigation (bilingual ensemble, +36% relative recovery) that falls out of the language-conditioning finding.
- Honest scope: the "representation preserved but decoding suppressed" headline uses only English (EN) prompts and English-rendered activations, so it is unaffected by NL-proficiency confounds; the cross-lingual claim is held in place by three design-derived controls rather than new evaluations.
- Robustness audit (48,568 generations rerated, Pearson ≥ 0.87 ranking preservation; 95% bootstrap CI half-widths 0.046–0.08 against observed differences of 0.34–0.79) is rare in this subfield.

## Limitations

- NL prompts not native-speaker validated for 7/9 non-English languages; directional deltas trustworthy, absolute calibration pending review.
- 270-cell substrate, all inference cell-level; ten cultures exclude sub-Saharan African, Native American, Polynesian, Australian, Arctic indigenous traditions.
- Linear ridge probe by design; raw (untuned) logit lens, so lens onset is a lower bound on readout depth rather than a calibrated probability.
- Greedy chat-template generation only; sparse (every-4th-layer) activation patching on 5 motifs × 4 culture pairs to fit the A100 budget.
- Open-source models only; closed-source frontier models not tested for the headline white-box claim.
- Qwen-3.6 reasoning-mode artefact handled by bare-prompt lens estimate; explicit bare-prompt patching for Qwen-3.6-27B deferred to follow-up.
- All fp16, no quantization; whether post-training quantization hits DecodingSuppressed cells harder than Preserved is left to a separate study (Siniaev et al., 2026).
- Transfer beyond folklore (e.g., culturally variable biomedical misconceptions, emotion concepts) is a conjecture for which the analogous parallel substrates do not yet exist.

## Relevance

与用户关注方向高度契合：本文核心是 **LLM 隐藏状态与可解释性**（线性探针、logit lens、激活修补三种机制可解释性工具在残差流上的应用），同时触及 **LLM 推理评估**（生成一致性、解码端坍缩的失败模式分析）和 **跨语言推理可靠性**（prompt 语言门控、tokenizer fertility 不预测 per-cell delta）。它对"表示几何 vs 读出"的拆解，对任何关注"模型知道什么"和"模型说什么"之间鸿沟的研究都是有用的方法学模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the DecodingSuppressed dominance replicate on **closed-source frontier models** (GPT-5/Claude/Gemini) using only the NL/EN independence prediction, since E1–E3 require white-box access?
- Would an **MLP probe or SAE feature** change the picture on the smallest models where the probe barely clears the 0.60 surface baseline (Gemma-4-E2B at +0.011, n.s.)?
- Does **post-training quantization (4-bit / 8-bit)** hit DecodingSuppressed cells harder than Preserved ones — the paper explicitly flags this as a separate study (Siniaev et al., 2026)?
- Does the late-decoding band (last 12% of layers) coincide with a specific **circuit / attention head set** that activation patching at sparse layer step cannot resolve?
- Does the bilingual-ensemble gain generalize to **task types beyond named-entity fillers** (e.g., open-ended cultural-knowledge QA where the gold is not a single proper name)?
- Would **native-speaker-validated NL prompts** change the per-culture EN−NL ordering, especially on Greek (+0.241) and Egyptian (+0.177) where the LLM-assisted translation risk is highest?
- Can the within-vs-cross correlation gap (0.57 vs 0.29) be used as a **model-agnostic, white-box-free diagnostic** for language-conditioned bias in production?
