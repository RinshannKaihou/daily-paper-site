> **一句话总结：** 论文将现有 LLM-based MLE 智能体的搜索方式归结为"以解为中心"的范式，并提出"信息范式"，在 Iris 系统中实现"询问—修订"循环：通过自适应动作拓扑和认知型动作（epistemic actions）主动探测关键未知，再用跨实验、可修订的带作用域与状态的任务知识来管理证据，在 MLE-Bench 上以 12 小时预算取得 64.9% any-medal 与 39.1% gold-medal，并在四个跨域任务上验证可迁移性。

> **TL;DR:** The paper reframes LLM-based ML-engineering agents' search as a "solution-centric" paradigm and proposes the **information paradigm**, instantiated as **Iris** — an inquiry-revision loop that uses adaptive action topology and epistemic (non-modifying diagnostic) actions to probe decision-critical unknowns, while managing evidence as revisable task-knowledge claims with explicit scope/status; on MLE-Bench (75 Kaggle tasks), Iris reaches **64.9% any-medal at 12h** (vs. 63.1% for the model-matched AIBuildAI baseline at 24h) and **39.1% gold-medal**, and beats Codex / Claude Code on all four cross-domain tasks (τ3-Banking, BrowseComp, HumanEval, GSM8K).

## 核心贡献

- 提出并形式化"信息范式"（information paradigm）：将研究过程围绕一个持续演化的"信息状态" $I_t$ 来组织，而非围绕候选解的树/图/链结构，揭示了既有方法在信息获取与信息管理两方面的根本局限。
- 设计并实现 **Iris**——一个"询问—修订"（inquiry–revision）循环：信息获取侧用从当前状态生成的局部动作拓扑 + 干预型/认知型动作划分；信息管理侧用带证据溯源、作用域与状态（active/qualified/invalidated）的可修订任务知识，并提供原始证据、结构化摘要、任务知识三级多粒度访问。
- 在 MLE-Bench 全量 75 任务上以 12 小时预算取得 64.9% any-medal、39.1% gold-medal，均为对比系统中最优；在 4 个跨域任务（τ3-Banking、BrowseComp、HumanEval、GSM8K）上超过 Codex 与 Claude Code。
- 通过组件消融（去除自适应拓扑/认知动作/信息管理）与骨干网络泛化实验（Claude Opus 4.6 vs. Qwen3.7-Max）以及 Kuzushiji 识别轨迹分析，展示了各组件与"信息范式可跨模型迁移"的证据。

## Key Contributions

- **A new paradigm, not just a new agent.** Names and formalizes the prevailing "solution-centric search" paradigm (tree/graph/chain over candidate solutions, as in AIDE, AIRA, MLE-STAR, MARS, ML-Master 2.0, Gome) and contrasts it with the proposed **information paradigm**, where an evolving information state $I_t = (E_t, S_t, K_t)$ — evidence, structured summaries, and revisable task knowledge — drives solution improvement.
- **Iris: an inquiry–revision loop** with two paired cores. *Inquiry* generates a local action plan $\text{Plan}(D_q, s_t) = (A_t, \prec_t)$ from the current state, with topology that adapts step-by-step rather than being fixed as a tree/graph. Actions are partitioned into **interventional** (modify/evaluate the retained solution $x_t$) and **epistemic** (produce discriminative evidence *without* modifying $x_t$, e.g., error stratification, validation-reliability checks, feature-interaction probing).
- **Revisable task knowledge** $K_t = (C_t, R_t)$: claim records with `claim / scope / E⁺ / E⁻ / status(active|qualified|invalidated)` and support/conflict/association relations; revised via Add / Update / Invalidate. Multi-granularity access serves task knowledge to direction selection, structured summaries to experiment design, and raw evidence (via provenance pointers) to anomaly diagnosis.
- **State-of-the-art on MLE-Bench** at 12h and **cross-domain transfer** to harness engineering (τ3-Banking, BrowseComp) and model post-training (HumanEval, GSM8K) under a matched 6-hour protocol against Codex and Claude Code.

## Methodology

Iris formulates MLE as maximizing a held-out evaluation $J_q$ over research trajectories $\tau$ subject to a wall-clock budget $B$: $\max_\tau J_q(x_{out}(\tau))$ s.t. $T(\tau) \le B$. During research the agent only sees a fixed development evaluator $F_q$; $J_q$ is queried once externally on the final submission.

**Decision state.** At step $t$ the state is $s_t = (x_t, I_t, b_t)$ where $x_t$ is the currently retained valid solution, $b_t$ the remaining budget, and $I_t = (E_t, S_t, K_t)$ the information state (raw evidence, per-action structured summaries, and task knowledge). Five LLM roles (Planner, Executor, Analyst, Summarizer, Knowledge Manager) implement the loop (Algorithm 1).

**Adaptive action topology.** Rather than fixing tree/graph/chain, the Planner emits a *local* plan $(A_t, \prec_t)$ conditioned on $(D_q, s_t)$; actions in the same topological layer run concurrently when resources permit, and the global trajectory topology emerges from successively generated local plans. Only actions schedulable within $b_t$ are emitted.

**Epistemic vs. interventional actions.** $\text{Execute}(a_i; s_t, \omega_i)$ returns $(e_i, \emptyset)$ for epistemic actions (Analyst) and $(e_i, ex_i)$ for interventional actions (Executor). Epistemic actions target a *decision-critical unknown* — a question whose possible answers would lead to qualitatively different next interventions — and are triggered when current evidence cannot distinguish next steps, when progress stalls for unclear reasons, or when an unexpected result must be traced to its source.

**Knowledge revision.** After each action the Summarizer produces $\sigma_i$ (attempt, observation, implication). Cross-experiment synthesis selects evidence $Z_t = \text{SelectEvidence}(E_{t+1}, S_{t+1}, K_t)$ and revises $K_{t+1} = \text{Revise}(K_t, Z_t)$ via Add / Update (which can narrow scope and demote a claim from active → qualified) / Invalidate (contradicted claims). The retained solution updates as $x_{t+1} = \arg\max_{x \in V_t} F_q(x)$ where $V_t$ always contains the previous $x_t$, guaranteeing a valid submission.

**Multi-granularity access.** Each role invocation gets a decision-specific context built from $I_t$: task knowledge for direction selection, structured summaries for experiment design, raw evidence (reached via provenance links $E^+_i, E^-_i$) for anomaly diagnosis. The same loop is reused across domains; each task supplies only its data, evaluator, and initial executable artifact.

## Results & Highlights

- **MLE-Bench (full, 75 Kaggle tasks, 12h budget):** Iris attains **64.9% overall any-medal** (best of compared systems) and **39.1% gold-medal**, with **100.0% valid-submission rate** and **76.0% above-human-median (Med+)**. Per-complexity any-medal: Low **80.3%** (1st), Medium **64.0%** (1st), High **44.4%** (tied 2nd).
- **Direct model-matched comparison vs. AIBuildAI** (both Claude-Opus-4.6): Iris raises any-medal from **63.1% → 64.9%** and gold-medal from **25.8% → 39.1%** while *halving* the budget from 24h to 12h.
- **Next-best 24h systems:** MARS+ (Gemini-3-Pro, 24h) 62.7% any-medal / 33.8% gold; ML-Master 2.0 (DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, 24h) 56.4% / 19.6%; MARS (24h) 56.0% / 31.1%. Iris's 12h result beats all 24h systems on overall any-medal and gold.
- **Cross-domain (matched 6h, single run, held-out tests):** τ3-Banking **30.3%** avg reward (vs. Claude Code 26.3, Codex 20.7, Initial 15.8); BrowseComp **67.7%** accuracy (vs. Codex 57.5, Claude Code 42.8, Initial 38.3); HumanEval **47.0%** pass@1 (vs. Claude Code 41.5, Codex 36.3, Initial 9.0 — from Qwen3-1.7B); GSM8K **68.4%** exact match (vs. Claude Code 54.0, Codex 52.3, Initial 8.5). Improvements over the stronger baseline on each task: **+4.0 to +14.4 percentage points**.
- **Component ablations (15 Small-Data tasks, 6h, Qwen3.7-Max, any-medal):** Full Iris **66.7%**; w/o Adaptive Topology **40.0%** (−26.7); w/o Epistemic Actions **60.0%** (−6.7); w/o Information Management **53.3%** (−13.4, gold drops 20.0% → 13.3%). All variants keep 100.0% valid-submission, confirming ablations affect research effectiveness, not execution.
- **Backbone generality (MLE-Bench Lite, 22 tasks, 12h):** Claude-Opus-4.6 81.8% any-medal / 54.5% gold; Qwen3.7-Max 77.3% any-medal / 36.4% gold — both 100% valid, supporting cross-model transferability of the paradigm (and justifying Qwen3.7-Max for controlled studies).
- **Inquiry-revision case study (kuzushiji-recognition, micro-F1 over (char,location) pairs):** trajectory 0.629 → bronze 0.661 (recall stuck at 0.48) → first epistemic diagnosis finds inference cap of 100 detections/image truncates dense pages, invalidates wrong recall-cause claim K1, lifts F1 to silver **0.817** *without retraining* → second diagnosis finds silent input downscaling (min800/max1333) defeats hi-res training, qualifies K2 to rare classes → reaches gold **0.923**.

## Authors & Credibility

- Lead authors are from **Alibaba Group** (Shaokang Fu, Yulong Tao, Linbo Jin*, Haonan Li, Chengyu Wang, Chengfu Huo), with collaborators from East China Normal University, Zhejiang University, Fudan University, and an independent researcher. Linbo Jin is the corresponding author.
- The evaluation uses the **official, full MLE-Bench** (75 tasks) rather than a curated subset, reports complexity-stratified any-medal/gold/valid/Med+ rates, includes a model-matched comparison (AIBuildAI on the same Claude-Opus-4.6 backbone), and exposes ablations plus a backbone-swap generalization check.
- Numbers are reported as mean ± SEM over three runs for the main MLE-Bench result, with hardware fully specified (23 vCPUs, 234 GB RAM, single NVIDIA H20-3e 144GB GPU per task).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine).** The framing contribution — elevating "information state" to the primary evolving variable and decomposing it into evidence/summaries/revisable-claims with a three-valued status (active/qualified/invalidated) plus epistemic actions that deliberately *do not* modify $x_t$ — is a real conceptual reframing distinct from tree/graph/chain search and from verbal-reflection memory (Reflexion, MARS, Gome).
- **Rigor (mixed).** Headline MLE-Bench numbers are strong and come with mean±SEM over 3 runs, a model-matched AIBuildAI comparison, and per-complexity breakdowns. *However*, the ablations are **single-run per task on only 15 Small-Data (≤60MB) tasks** with a different backbone (Qwen3.7-Max) than the headline system; the paper explicitly states ablations on the 60 Big-Data tasks "require more than six hours per task" and are therefore not reported, leaving the headline 64.9% number itself without a controlled component decomposition. Cross-domain comparisons (Table 2) are also **single-run** with no variance bars.
- **Baselines — a notable asymmetry.** On MLE-Bench, baseline numbers are taken "from the cited papers and official leaderboard" rather than re-run, so budgets, code, and even backbone generations differ (e.g., the only model-matched rival is AIBuildAI; the strongest 24h rival MARS+ uses Gemini-3-Pro). The +1.8pp any-medal margin over AIBuildAI is within plausible run-to-run variance for a single-model comparison even if SEM is small, and the +5.3pp gold margin is the more load-bearing claim.
- **Reproducibility.** The loop, algorithm, and claim schema are described in enough detail to reimplement, but no code release is mentioned in the paper, the role-specific prompts are not shown, and the five roles all run on Claude-Opus-4.6 / Qwen3.7-Max — proprietary models whose exact behavior is not reproducible. Hardware is stringent (one H20-3e GPU per task), which will limit independent reproduction.
- **Significance.** If the information-paradigm framing holds up, it is a useful organizing lens for the crowded MLE-agent literature; the empirical lift is real but modest in absolute any-medal terms, with the more interesting evidence being the ablation deltas and the Kuzushiji trajectory showing epistemic diagnoses redirecting interventions.
- **Red flags / data quirks.** (1) The cross-domain "Initial" baselines are conspicuously low (HumanEval 9.0%, GSM8K 8.5% from Qwen3-1.7B), which inflates the apparent gain but is consistent with starting from a weak base model; the gain over the *stronger* coding agent is the honest metric and is reported. (2) Ablation "w/o Information Management" still scores 53.3% any-medal, i.e., most of Iris's headline effect survives without its central novelty — the marginal contribution of knowledge revision is +13.4pp on a 15-task subset, not on the full 75. (3) The Kuzushiji win rests on diagnosing an *inference-code bug* (detection cap = 100) and a silent input-downscaling bug — impressive, but it is a single hand-picked trajectory, not a systematic count of how often epistemic actions find such bugs.

## Strengths

- Conceptually clean paradigm articulation with a formal decision-state and Algorithm 1 that is implementable.
- The epistemic/interventional action distinction directly addresses a real failure mode of solution-centric search (you cannot "just look" without producing a new candidate).
- Revisable claim records with provenance, scope, and a three-valued status give a concrete, falsifiable mechanism for cross-experiment conflict resolution — beyond verbal reflection or record accumulation.
- Strongest-in-class headline result on full MLE-Bench at half the budget of the closest model-matched rival, plus positive cross-domain transfer to four genuinely different task types.
- Honest about backbone sensitivity: gold-medal rate swings 18pp between Claude-Opus-4.6 and Qwen3.7-Max on MLE-Bench Lite, and the authors use this to justify their ablation backbone choice rather than hiding it.

## Limitations

- Ablations cover only 15 Small-Data tasks at 6h with Qwen3.7-Max; no controlled component decomposition exists for the headline 75-task / 12h / Claude-Opus-4.6 setting, so the contribution of each component to the 64.9% number is inferred, not measured.
- Cross-domain and ablation tables are single-run; variance is unreported, so per-task gains of a few points (e.g., +4.0pp on τ3-Banking over Claude Code) could be within noise.
- Reliance on proprietary frontier models (Claude-Opus-4.6, Qwen3.7-Max, GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8) and heavy compute (one H20-3e per task) limits reproducibility; no code/prompts released.
- MLE-Bench baselines are transcribed from prior papers/leaderboard rather than re-run under a unified protocol, introducing confounds in backbone, budget, and implementation.
- The Kuzushiji trajectory is illustrative but singular; the paper does not report how frequently epistemic actions surface decisive bugs across the full benchmark.
- No statistical significance testing between Iris and the closest rival (AIBuildAI); the +1.8pp any-medal delta is small relative to the budget difference and could benefit from paired per-task analysis.

## Relevance

与"LLM 智能体做长程自主研究/MLE"以及"智能体记忆与知识管理"方向高度相关：Iris 的"信息范式 + 可修订任务知识 + 认知型动作"为自动化科研智能体提供了一个比树/图搜索更具解释性的组织原则，对关注 MLE-Bench、agent memory、self-improving LLM agents 的研究都有直接参考价值。其组件消融与跨域迁移结果也可作为后续工作（如更细粒度的认知动作学习、可验证的知识图谱式记忆）的基线。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of Iris's 64.9% any-medal is attributable to each component on the *full* 75-task set under the *headline* 12h/Claude-Opus-4.6 setting, rather than the 15-task/6h/Qwen ablation subset? A full-budget component decomposition is the obvious missing experiment.
- How frequently do epistemic actions surface decisive, non-obvious bugs (like the kuzushiji detection-cap = 100) versus merely confirming what interventional actions would have found anyway? A systematic taxonomy/count across all 75 tasks would quantify the epistemic-action premium.
- Can the revisable-claim knowledge store be evaluated for *calibration* — e.g., what fraction of "active" claims are later invalidated, and how does claim status correlate with downstream intervention success?
- Since the cross-domain gains over Claude Code are modest on some tasks (+4.0pp on τ3-Banking, single-run), does the advantage persist with paired multi-run evaluation and statistical tests?
- Does the information paradigm compose with cheaper open-weight backbones, or is the claim-revision quality tightly coupled to frontier-model reasoning (the 18pp gold-medal gap between Claude-Opus-4.6 and Qwen3.7-Max hints at coupling)?
- Could the epistemic/interventional partition be *learned* (e.g., via a small value-of-information head) rather than emitted by the Planner, to reduce reliance on prompt engineering?
