> **一句话总结：** 本文揭示 KV cache 压缩中存在"答案-证据鸿沟"——在 AIME、GPQA-Diamond、MedCalc 上，token 蒸发类方法可在保持最终答案准确率的同时显著损坏支持性推理链与扰动鲁棒性，使最终准确率成为不对称诊断指标；而保留覆盖率的 KIVI 量化几乎不受影响（如 SnapKV 在 AIME 上 RWAC/correct 高达 65.5%，KIVI 仅 4.5%）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces an **answer–evidence gap** in KV cache compression for large reasoning models: on AIME24-26, GPQA-Diamond, MedCalc-Bench, and RULER QA, eviction-based compressors (StreamingLLM, SnapKV, HeadKV, AdaKV, PyramidKV, ChunkKV, KNorm, TOVA, LagKV, R-KV) can preserve competitive final-answer accuracy while degrading answer-chain consistency and perturbation faithfulness, whereas coverage-preserving KIVI-2bit quantization stays close to Full-KV (e.g., on AIME, SnapKV keeps 50.0% accuracy but 65.5% RWAC/correct vs. KIVI's 67.4% accuracy and 4.5% RWAC/correct). Accuracy is shown to be an *asymmetric* diagnostic — on AIME26 the Spearman ρ between accuracy and reasoning quality is **−0.95**.

## 核心贡献

- **首次识别"答案-证据鸿沟"这一假阳性失败模式**：在 KV 压缩下，压缩模型可保持正确最终答案，同时支持该答案的推理链（推导、验证、扰动鲁棒性）已失效，使仅用最终准确率评估严重高估压缩质量。
- **提出受控 fixed-trace replay 协议**：先由未压缩模型生成完整推理轨迹，各压缩方法在相同的文本轨迹与提示下重放但使用各自的压缩 KV 状态，从而把"KV 保留能力"与"rollout 动态"解耦，构成 component-level 诊断。
- **三轴评估指标体系**：final accuracy、answer–chain consistency（用 RWAC_total 与 RWAC_correct 度量"答案对但推理链错"的比例）、perturbation faithfulness（在 start/middle/end 位置注入错误答案后的 Fidelity 与 Bias Rate）。
- **大规模系统化评测**：覆盖 11 种代表性压缩方法（10 种 token 蒸发 + KIVI 量化），3 个模型（Qwen3-8B 主模型、DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B、Qwen3-30B-A3B），4 类任务，并以人类专家校验 LLM judge（人-Claude 一致率 94.6%, κ=0.89）。
- **可操作的结论与设计建议**：覆盖保持型量化（KIVI）几乎不受影响，说明失败的本质是"丢失推理 trace 片段"而非"显存降低"本身；建议未来压缩器保留 dependency/provenance/verification 结构而非仅保留看似显著的 token。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies the answer–evidence gap** as a new false-positive failure mode of accuracy-only KV-compression evaluation: eviction-based methods can preserve correct final answers while the visible rationales become invalid, incomplete, or fragile to injected errors.
- **A controlled fixed-trace replay protocol** that fixes the reasoning trace and question cache across methods, compressing only the trace's KV cache and resuming decoding after `</think>`; this isolates KV-retention behavior from rollout dynamics and is validated against end-to-end generation (Tables 2 and 11).
- **A three-axis measurement protocol** — final accuracy, answer–chain consistency (RWAC/total and RWAC/correct via LLM-as-judge), and perturbation faithfulness (Fidelity and Bias Rate after injecting a wrong answer at start/middle/end positions).
- **First comprehensive faithfulness-focused study of KV compression**: 11 compressors spanning recency/sink, attention scoring, head-/layer-wise allocation, semantic span, local utility, redundancy/reasoning-aware, and quantization families × 3 models × 4 task types (AIME24-26, GPQA-Diamond, MedCalc-Bench, RULER QA).
- **Concrete design implication**: the gap is *not* an inevitable consequence of memory reduction — coverage-preserving KIVI-2bit stays near Full-KV across all metrics, so future reasoning compressors should preserve dependency/provenance/verification structure rather than salience-only signals.

## Methodology

**Controlled fixed-trace replay.** For each question, the uncompressed model first generates `<think> r </think>` + answer. The system then prefills `q || <think> r </think>`, protects the question cache, compresses only the cache corresponding to `r`, and resumes decoding right after `</think>`. The post-`</think>` continuation (visible rationale + final answer) is regenerated by each compressor from its own compressed cache, so differences reflect how well each compressed KV preserves *usable information from an already-present trace* rather than reasoning-content availability. This makes the protocol a component-level diagnostic, not a deployment benchmark — the authors add end-to-end scope checks on AIME26 (Table 2) and GPQA-Diamond (Table 11) to confirm the pattern persists in deployment-style generation.

**Three evaluation axes.**
1. *Final-answer accuracy* — whether the predicted answer matches ground truth.
2. *Answer–chain consistency* — an LLM judge (Claude) labels both answer correctness and reasoning validity. RWAC (Reasoning-Wrong, Answer-Correct) counts correct final answers whose reasoning is not fully correct. RWAC/total measures benchmark prevalence; RWAC/correct conditions on answer-correct samples to test whether accuracy-positive outputs are actually valid. Judge validation: human–Claude 94.6% agreement (κ=0.89); Claude–Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite 94.2% (κ=0.88); second human on 50 outputs 96% (κ=0.92).
3. *Perturbation faithfulness* — a wrong answer `z_i ≠ y_i` is injected at start/middle/end positions in the shared trace; Fidelity = E[1[ŷ_p = ŷ_0]] measures answer stability under misleading intervention; BiasRate = E[1[ŷ_p = z_i]] measures directed adoption of the injected error.

**Setup.** Primary model Qwen3-8B (validated on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B and Qwen3-30B-A3B). Default eviction budget = 256 retained tokens (aggressive, to surface differences; budget ablation 512–8192 in §4.5). Datasets: AIME24/25/26 (multi-step math, integer answers), GPQA-Diamond (graduate-level science MCQ), MedCalc-Bench (clinical numerical calculation), RULER QA (32K-token multi-hop retrieval with distractors). 3-seed means reported. Spearman ρ used for rank-correlation analyses.

## Results & Highlights

- **Answer–evidence gap on AIME24-26 (Qwen3-8B)**: Full-KV reaches 67.4% accuracy with only 4.3% RWAC/correct; the coverage-preserving **KIVI-2bit quantization matches Full-KV (67.4% acc, 4.5% RWAC/correct, 90.7 fidelity)**, while eviction methods retain decent accuracy but collapse on chain support — e.g., **SnapKV: 50.0% accuracy but 65.5% RWAC/correct (i.e., ~2/3 of correct answers have broken reasoning chains) and 61.6 fidelity**; R-KV: 49.6% acc / 58.7% RWAC/correct; HeadKV: 48.1% / 58.5%.
- **Accuracy vs reasoning rank anti-correlation (AIME26)**: Spearman ρ between final accuracy and answer-chain consistency is **−0.95** — methods that rank higher on accuracy tend to rank *worse* on chain validity, so an accuracy-based leaderboard would actively select evidence-destroying compressors. (Perturbation fidelity still correlates positively at +0.92 because the injected cue makes answer-relevant tokens salient.)
- **GPQA-Diamond**: LagKV achieves 57.9% accuracy (slightly above Full-KV's 57.7%) but **90.2% RWAC/correct** — almost all of its correct MCQ answers lack a verifiable rationale (guessing amplified). KIVI-2bit: 58.8% acc / 55.5% RWAC/correct.
- **End-to-end AIME26 (Table 2)** confirms the gap under deployment-style generation: Full-KV 56.7% acc / 11.8% RWAC/correct / 91.1 fidelity; **SnapKV degrades to 26.7% / 0.0% / 30.0**; StreamingLLM collapses to 6.7% acc / 50.0% RWAC/correct / 6.7 fidelity.
- **Matched-answer chain degradation on AIME (Table 5)** — conditioned on examples where both Full-KV and the compressor gave the *same correct answer* and Full-KV reasoning was correct: KIVI degrades the chain only **4.2%** of the time, while eviction methods degrade it 47.9%–64.8% (LagKV 47.9%, AdaKV 63.2%, SnapKV 64.8%, HeadKV 60.2%). This isolates compression-induced reasoning corruption from accuracy confounds.
- **Output-level failure signature (Table 6)**: among AIME RWAC cases, SnapKV produces **answer-first rationalization in 94% of cases vs. Full-KV's 58% (Δ=+36)** — i.e., the model reaches the correct answer then constructs a post-hoc unsupported derivation around it.
- **Budget ablation (Fig. 2)**: on AIME, accuracy recovers substantially as the budget grows, but chain consistency stays degraded at *all* budgets (512–8192); on GPQA/MedCalc chain consistency recovers near baseline while a persistent perturbation-faithfulness gap remains.
- **Wrong-chain span replacement (Table 7)** — replacing a middle reasoning block with an in-domain wrong-chain block from another seed still exposes robustness degradation (e.g., AIME StreamingLLM fidelity 28.9 vs Full-KV 94.0; SnapKV 53.0; AdaKV 66.3), confirming the gap is not just answer-cue sensitivity.
- **Position-conditioned perturbation (Fig. 3)**: StreamingLLM (sink+window) is *most* fragile to start-position perturbations because the early injected answer is preserved as an anchor while later corrective reasoning is evicted; SnapKV/AdaKV/HeadKV (attention/head signals) over-preserve late conclusions and are most fragile at the end position.

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors — **Mengting Ai, Jingrui He, Yue Guo** — are from the **University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC**). Jingrui He is a well-established HCI/ML researcher with a long publication record in heterogeneous learning and trustworthy ML. The work exhibits strong methodological discipline: the LLM judge is cross-validated against an independent human auditor *and* a second LLM (Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite) with reported Cohen's κ near 0.9, the fixed-trace protocol is paired with explicit end-to-end scope checks, and matched-answer analyses are added to separate accuracy confounds from chain degradation. Code is publicly released at the linked GitHub. The paper is positioned at the intersection of two active and well-cited literatures (KV compression and CoT faithfulness, e.g., StreamingLLM, SnapKV, HeadKV, KIVI, Lanham et al. 2023, Turpin et al. 2023, Arcuschin et al. 2025), and the framing of "accuracy as an asymmetric diagnostic" is a cleanly articulable conceptual contribution.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel framing — *not* another compressor, but a new evaluation lens for an entire family of methods. The "accuracy is asymmetric" observation and the use of CoT-faithfulness perturbation tests (imported from Lanham/Tutek/Barez) applied to KV compression is a fresh cross-pollination that, to the authors' knowledge and plausibly, is a first.
- **Rigor**: Strong. 3-seed means, LLM judge validated against two humans and a second judge (κ ≈ 0.88–0.92), Spearman rank analyses, matched-answer conditioning, wrong-chain span replacement as a non-answer-cue robustness check, and end-to-end cross-checks beyond the controlled protocol. The fixed-trace design cleanly isolates the claimed variable.
- **Reproducibility**: High. Public code, named models (Qwen3-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B), standard public datasets (AIME, GPQA-Diamond, MedCalc-Bench, RULER), specified budget (256 tokens, ablated to 8192) and seed counts. The perturbation protocol and judge prompts are described (with examples in Appendices B/C).
- **Significance**: Practically important — it directly indicts the dominant evaluation convention in KV-compression leaderboards (final accuracy) and offers concrete, adoptable metrics. The negative Spearman ρ = −0.95 on AIME26 is a striking, citable finding.
- **Red flags / specific weaknesses**:
  - **Aggressive 256-token budget is the headline setting.** This is far below typical deployment budgets and is chosen precisely to surface gaps; at larger budgets (§4.5) chain consistency on GPQA/MedCalc *does* recover near baseline, which somewhat softens the paper's framing for production use. The gap is most catastrophic on AIME even at large budgets, but the paper could be clearer that GPQA/MedCalc users with adequate budgets may see limited impact.
  - **Fixed-trace replay is not deployment.** The authors acknowledge this in Limitations — it isolates retention but does not capture online co-evolution of trajectory and cache, and end-to-end numbers (Table 2) actually show *much larger* accuracy collapse than fixed-trace, so the two regimes can diverge in either direction.
  - **LLM judge for chain validity is itself a noisy instrument.** Even with κ ≈ 0.89, RWAC/correct values like LagKV's 90.2% on GPQA are sensitive to judge thresholds; a small absolute shift in labeling could change rankings among eviction methods.
  - **RULER is treated as a "boundary condition" where accuracy already collapses** — useful as a control, but it means the faithfulness lens adds little there; the gap story is essentially an AIME/GPQA/MedCalc story.
  - **Only one quantization control (KIVI-2bit).** The strong claim that "coverage preservation, not memory reduction, is the cause" rests on a single quantization data point; other quantization methods (e.g., KVQuant, GEAR) are cited but not tested.
  - **No new compression method proposed.** This is a deliberate and defensible choice (the contribution is diagnostic), but readers expecting a fix will not find one — only a design recommendation.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated conceptual contribution ("answer–evidence gap", "accuracy is an asymmetric diagnostic") that reframes how KV compressors should be benchmarked for reasoning models.
- Methodologically careful: fixed-trace protocol isolates the right variable; matched-answer and wrong-chain-span controls rule out the most obvious confounds.
- Comprehensive baseline coverage — 11 methods spanning all major compression families, plus a quantization control that elegantly separates *memory reduction* from *coverage loss*.
- Multi-axis metrics with rigorous judge validation (dual-human + dual-LLM agreement).
- Practical, actionable recommendations for future compressor design (preserve dependency/provenance/verification structure, not salience).
- Public code and reproducible setup.

## Limitations

- The 256-token default budget is far below typical deployment and is chosen to surface gaps; at realistic budgets, the gap on GPQA/MedCalc largely closes for chain consistency.
- Fixed-trace replay is a component-level diagnostic, not a deployment benchmark; end-to-end behavior can diverge (and on AIME26 end-to-end, accuracy collapses harder than fixed-trace suggests).
- Single quantization control (KIVI-2bit) underpowers the "coverage vs. memory" causal claim.
- RWAC depends on an LLM judge whose labeling thresholds can swing the eviction-method rankings.
- No proposed remediation/algorithm — the paper is diagnostic only.
- RULER mostly serves as a control case where accuracy already collapses, so the faithfulness lens contributes little there.

## Relevance

高度相关于"推理时压缩/高效推理 vs. 推理可验证性"的研究主线：直接指出当前 KV 压缩 benchmark 仅用最终准确率的盲点，对评估长 CoT 模型压缩安全性（如 SnapKV、HeadKV、LagKV 在 AIME 类任务上的部署）有直接指导意义，也与 chain-of-thought faithfulness、reasoning trace 压缩（Chain of Draft、sketch reasoning）的研究方向形成对话。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the answer–evidence gap persist for newer *reasoning-aware* compressors (e.g., R-KV, sentence-skip, hierarchical memory routing) at *realistic* deployment budgets (e.g., 2048–8192 tokens), or is it primarily an artifact of aggressive 256-token budgets?
- Can dependency-/provenance-aware compression signals (protecting definitions, intermediate computations, verification steps differently from answer-like tokens) be operationalized into a compressor that closes the gap without re-expanding the cache?
- How does the gap interact with *test-time scaling* (longer traces, best-of-N, verification loops) — does scaling amplify or repair evidence loss?
- Is the RWAC/correct ranking among eviction methods stable under different LLM judges or different labeling thresholds, given the κ≈0.89 ceiling?
- Does the gap appear for other coverage-preserving compression types (KVQuant, GEAR, residualized KV) beyond the single KIVI-2bit data point?
- On end-to-end deployment where accuracy collapses harder than under fixed-trace (Table 2), what is the *net* trustworthiness trade-off — is fixed-trace optimistic or pessimistic relative to real serving?
