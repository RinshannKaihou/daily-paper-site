> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 ChaosProbe，一种确定性的神经混沌（Neurochaos）探针框架，用广义 Lüroth 级数（GLS）斜帐篷映射对 GPT-2、DistilGPT2、BERT-base-uncased、RoBERTa-base 四个模型的冻结输入嵌入矩阵生成 800 维响应指纹，在 Pearson、Spearman 和余弦相似度下 100% 正确恢复了同家族最近邻配对（GPT-2/DistilGPT2 与 BERT/RoBERTa），并通过 200 次 bootstrap 验证了稳定性。

> **TL;DR:** ChaosProbe is a deterministic, neurochaos-inspired framework that probes frozen transformer input-embedding matrices with Generalized Lüroth Series (GLS) skew-tent chaotic trajectories to produce 800-dimensional response fingerprints; on a four-model cohort (GPT-2, DistilGPT2, BERT-base-uncased, RoBERTa-base) and 80 prompts, Pearson, Spearman, and cosine similarity all achieve 1.00 same-family nearest-neighbor accuracy and recover both expected mutual family pairs (GPT-2↔DistilGPT2 Pearson = 0.57; BERT↔RoBERTa Pearson = -0.16 vs. cross-family correlations of -0.69 to -0.40), while Euclidean distance reaches only 0.75 accuracy and recovers one of two pairs.

## 核心贡献

- 提出确定性神经混沌（Neurochaos）探针框架 ChaosProbe，将传统的轨迹描述符方法从面向分类的特征提取扩展为对冻结 transformer 输入嵌入空间的"响应面指纹"刻画。
- 设计了一套固定的 Q–B–ϵ 探针网格（10×5×2 = 100 种配置），对每个 prompt 级嵌入矩阵施加 GLS 斜帐篷混沌映射，提取 Firing Rate 与 Entropy 两个描述符通道，并结合四种表示级度量（均值余弦相似度、线性 CKA、各向异性、归一化有效秩），生成 800 维的模型级响应签名。
- 在四模型概念验证中证明：Pearson、Spearman、余弦相似度均能 100% 恢复 GPT-2/DistilGPT2 与 BERT-base-uncased/RoBERTa-base 的同家族最近邻配对，欧氏距离恢复 3/4 个家族最近邻与 1/2 个互对。
- 引入签名有效性诊断（零方差维度比例、描述符坍塌比例）以及 200 次成对 bootstrap 重采样，证明 Pearson/Spearman 配对在所观察的 80 个 prompt 集合上具有稳定性（200/200 = 1.00）。
- 提供了完整的开源实现、prompt 集与分析脚本（https://github.com/kunal1704/ChaosProbe）。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces ChaosProbe, a deterministic neurochaos-inspired framework that reframes trajectory-descriptor methods from classification-oriented feature extraction to response-surface fingerprinting of frozen transformer input-embedding spaces.
- Formulates a fixed Q–B–ε probing procedure (10×5×2 = 100 configurations) that converts each prompt-level input-embedding matrix into a standardized 800-dimensional model-level response signature (100 configs × 2 descriptor channels × 4 representation-level measures).
- Demonstrates within a four-model proof-of-concept (GPT-2, DistilGPT2, BERT-base-uncased, RoBERTa-base) that the signatures recover the expected same-family nearest-neighbor pairings under Pearson correlation, Spearman correlation, and cosine similarity (each 4/4 = 1.00 family accuracy; 2/2 mutual pairs), while Euclidean distance recovers 3/4 same-family neighbors and 1/2 mutual pairs.
- Adds signature-validity diagnostics (zero-variance dimension fraction and descriptor-collapse fraction) and a 200-iteration paired prompt-bootstrap resampling that confirms the stability of the Pearson/Spearman pairings (200/200 = 1.00 for both within-family dominance and simultaneous mutual-pair recovery).
- Releases the implementation, prompt set, and analysis scripts at https://github.com/kunal1704/ChaosProbe.

## Methodology

ChaosProbe treats a frozen transformer's input-embedding lookup as a geometric object and characterizes it by how it *responds* to a controlled deterministic nonlinear probe, rather than by its raw coordinates. The pipeline is:

1. **Prompt-level embedding matrix.** For model m and prompt p, the model-specific tokenizer produces token IDs, which are looked up in the frozen token-embedding table E to form X_{m,p} ∈ R^{T×d}. Crucially, no positional, segment, or contextual-later-layer information is included — only the static learned embeddings.

2. **Global min–max normalization.** Each X_{m,p} is normalized entry-wise to [0,1] (with a zero guard when min = max), placing every model on the unit interval where the chaotic map operates.

3. **Deterministic GLS skew-tent probing.** For each configuration θ_c = (Q_c, B_c, ε_c) drawn from a fixed grid (Q: 0.06–0.96, 10 values; B: 0.06–0.46, 5 values; ε: {0.06, 0.16}, 2 values → 100 configs), a Generalized Lüroth Series skew-tent chaotic trajectory of length L = 1000 is initialized at A_0 = Q_c. For every normalized embedding coordinate x, the first trajectory index τ_L whose value falls within ε_c of x is recorded; the trajectory *segment preceding* the first match (or the full length-L trajectory if no match) is used for descriptor computation.

4. **Two descriptor channels.** ChaosProbe retains only **Firing Rate** (the fraction of the segment where neural activity exceeds B_c) and **Entropy** (binary Shannon entropy of the thresholded symbolic sequence), computed coordinate-wise to produce descriptor-channel matrices with the same shape as the normalized prompt matrix.

5. **Four representation-level measures.** For each (prompt, config, channel) the framework computes: (i) mean cosine similarity between normalized input rows and descriptor-channel rows; (ii) linear centered kernel alignment (CKA); (iii) anisotropy (mean inter-row cosine similarity); (iv) normalized effective rank (exponential entropy of the normalized singular-value spectrum, bounded in [0,1]).

6. **Model-level response signature.** Responses are averaged over the 80 prompts to form R_m ∈ R^{100×2×4}, then vectorized in a fixed order (Q, then B, then ε; Firing Rate before Entropy; measures in Eq. 10 order) to yield s_m ∈ R^{800}. Signatures are standardized across the M = 4 evaluated models (zero-mean, unit-std; zero-variance dimensions set to zero) before comparison.

7. **Comparison & evaluation.** Models are compared via Pearson correlation, Spearman correlation, cosine similarity, and Euclidean distance on the standardized 800-D signatures. For each metric, each model's nearest neighbor is selected from the other three; family accuracy = fraction of models whose NN belongs to its preassigned family (GPT-style vs. masked-encoder). A pair is "mutually recovered" when both members choose each other. Stability is assessed with 200 paired bootstrap resamples of the prompt set; signature-validity is checked via zero-variance dimension counts and all-zero descriptor-response (collapse) fractions.

**Experimental setup.** Four checkpoints: GPT-2, DistilGPT2 (autoregressive family); BERT-base-uncased, RoBERTa-base (masked-encoder family). A common set of 80 English-language neutral prompts (20 each: factual, reasoning, mathematical, conversational). Tokenization is per-checkpoint; no packing/padding/truncation; special tokens excluded. Prompt answers, continuations, and task accuracy are explicitly *not* evaluated.

## Results & Highlights

- **Family-accuracy under three similarity measures (Table 5).** Pearson correlation, Spearman correlation, and cosine similarity each correctly assign all four models to a same-family nearest neighbor — **family accuracy 1.00 (4/4)** and **both expected mutual family pairs recovered (2/2)**. Euclidean distance reaches only **0.75 (3/4)** and recovers **1/2** mutual pairs (one cross-family assignment).

- **Signature similarity magnitudes (Figure 3).** Under Pearson, GPT-2 ↔ DistilGPT2 = **0.57** (their largest off-diagonal value); BERT-base-uncased ↔ RoBERTa-base = **-0.16**, but their cross-family correlations are even more negative (**-0.69 to -0.40**), so they are still mutual nearest neighbors in relative correlation space. Spearman shows essentially the same pattern (GPT-2↔DistilGPT2 = 0.58; BERT↔RoBERTa = -0.16; cross-family -0.70 to -0.41).

- **Bootstrap stability (Table 6).** Across **200 paired resamples** of the 80 prompts, mean within-family similarity exceeded mean between-family similarity in **200/200 (1.00)** of cases for both Pearson and Spearman, and both expected mutual NN pairs were simultaneously recovered in **200/200 (1.00)** — well above the descriptive 0.80 threshold.

- **Signature validity.** Out of 800 unstandardized dimensions, **60/800 = 0.075** had zero cross-model variance (below the 0.20 threshold). Descriptor-collapse fractions ranged from **0.0500 (RoBERTa-base)** to **0.0544 (GPT-2)**, both well under 0.20 — confirming constant dimensions and all-zero responses do not dominate the fingerprints.

- **Parameter-response variation (Figure 2).** Mean cosine similarity and linear CKA vary non-uniformly across the initial-activity parameter Q (decreasing over the lower-to-intermediate range, then recovering at high Q), demonstrating that the 800-D signature captures parameter-dependent structure rather than an approximately constant response — justifying the full grid over a single probe configuration.

No quantitative results on downstream tasks, contextual hidden states, or model behavior are reported — by design, the study restricts itself to representation-space fingerprinting of frozen input embeddings.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are **Kunal Kumar Pant** (Department of Electronics and Communication Engineering, Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham) and **Nithin Nagaraj** (Complex Systems Programme, National Institute of Advanced Studies [NIAS], IISc Campus, Bengaluru). Nagaraj is one of the originators of the Neurochaos Learning (NL) paradigm (Harikrishnan & Nagaraj, SPCOM 2020; Harikrishnan, Kathpalia & Nagaraj, NeurIPS 2022), so the paper sits squarely within the authors' own research line. The referencing is appropriate (Foundational embedding work: Mikolov, Pennington, Ethayarajh, Biś et al., Kornblith CKA, Roy & Vetterli effective rank; the four model papers; the 2026 Stanford AI Index). The paper is unusually disciplined about scope: it explicitly labels itself a "bounded proof-of-concept," pre-registers family assignments before signature comparison, states interpretation thresholds as descriptive rather than calibrated decision rules, and lists six concrete limitations. Code and prompt set are publicly released (https://github.com/kunal1704/ChaosProbe).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core move — repurposing Neurochaos trajectory descriptors (historically used as classification features) into a *response-surface fingerprint* of frozen embeddings — is a genuine methodological reframe. However, the paper honestly concedes it does not establish that chaotic trajectories are *necessary* for the recovery or superior to matched non-chaotic transformations; a matched random/linear-probe control would have materially strengthened the "neurochaotic" claim. Without that, it is unclear whether the GLS dynamics or simply the (Firing Rate, Entropy, CKA, anisotropy, effective rank) summary is doing the work.

- **Rigor.** Statistical hygiene is above average for a proof-of-concept: pre-registered family labels, standardized signatures, 200 paired bootstrap resamples, and explicit signature-validity checks. But the cohort is the central weakness — **only four checkpoints in two two-model families** (GPT-2/DistilGPT2 share a direct teacher–student relationship [23]; BERT/RoBERTa share masked-encoder lineage). With N = 2 families and a teacher–student pair inside one family, recovering them as "same-family" is close to trivially expected and tells us little about the method's discriminative power on independently trained or distantly related models. The BERT↔RoBERTa Pearson value of **-0.16** (negative!) being celebrated as "mutual nearest neighbor" underscores how coarse the cohort is — the result rests entirely on cross-family correlations being even more negative.

- **Reproducibility.** Strong: deterministic pipeline, fixed Q–B–ε grid, L = 1000 trajectory length, public code, prompt set, and analysis scripts. The main reproducibility caveat is that the *standardized* signature depends on the set of models included in the comparison (the mean/std are estimated across the cohort), so adding a fifth model changes every other model's signature — the authors acknowledge this but it limits cross-paper comparability.

- **Significance.** Modest at present. As a tool for model-family identification it is unvalidated beyond a near-trivial cohort; as a representation-analysis technique it adds a chaotic-dynamics angle to an already crowded CKA / probing / isotropy literature without yet demonstrating an analytic question it answers that existing tools cannot.

- **Red flags.** No fabrication concerns. The main intellectual red flag is the gap between the ambition of the framing (chaos-theoretic "fingerprinting" of LLM embedding spaces) and the smallness of the empirical demonstration; readers should treat the "chaotic" lens as currently aspirational rather than proven necessary. The descriptive thresholds in Table 4 (0.75, 0.20, 0.80) are chosen by the authors and not statistically calibrated, which the paper admits but which reviewers should still weigh.

## Strengths

- Genuinely novel framing: response-surface fingerprinting of *frozen input embeddings* via a deterministic chaotic probe, distinct from CKA, probing classifiers, and raw geometric analyses.
- Maps models with different tokenizers, vocabularies, sequence lengths, and embedding dimensions into a single 800-D signature space without coordinate alignment — a non-trivial design.
- Excellent methodological transparency: pre-registered family labels, descriptive-not-calibrated thresholds, six explicit limitations, and an honest "bounded proof-of-concept" framing.
- Solid stability diagnostics (200 paired bootstraps; zero-variance and collapse-rate checks) that actually support the reported pairings rather than just asserting them.
- Public, deterministic, end-to-end reproducible pipeline with code and prompts released.

## Limitations

- Only **four checkpoints in two two-model families** — the smallest possible non-trivial cohort, with one pair (GPT-2/DistilGPT2) being a direct teacher–student distillation. General model-family identification is not established.
- **No ablation** isolating the contribution of chaos vs. a matched non-chaotic probe, of Firing Rate vs. Entropy, or of the four representation-level measures; only one Q–B–ε grid and one trajectory length (L = 1000) are evaluated.
- Restricted object: only **frozen input-embedding matrices** are analyzed — nothing about contextual hidden states, attention, layer-wise evolution, or behavior after fine-tuning/distillation.
- Restricted prompt scope: **80 neutral English prompts**; no multilingual, long-context, domain-specific, or adversarial inputs, and no out-of-sample prompt-set evaluation.
- The **standardized signature depends on the model set**, hindering cross-cohort comparability; the BERT↔RoBERTa nearest-neighbor signal is negative in absolute terms and only meaningful relative to even-more-negative cross-family values.
- Descriptive thresholds (0.75, 0.20, 0.80) are author-chosen, not calibrated, and no formal hypothesis test is conducted.

## Relevance

对关注 transformer 表示几何、模型相似度与可解释性的研究而言，本文提供了一种区别于 CKA / probing classifier 的"混沌动力学响应指纹"新视角，可作为表示分析工具箱的补充思路；但其目前仅在四模型概念验证上得到验证，对实际大模型家族识别或下游能力评估的实用价值仍待更大规模、独立训练的模型队列上验证。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a matched **non-chaotic probe** (e.g., a fixed random deterministic map, a simple thresholding, or a linear transform producing the same Firing Rate / Entropy / CKA summary) recover the same family structure? If so, the "neurochaotic" contribution is incidental.
- How does ChaosProbe perform on a **larger, independently trained cohort** (e.g., multiple GPT-2 / GPT-Neo / Pythia scales, several BERT/RoBERTa/ALBERT checkpoints, T5 and encoder-decoder families)? Does family accuracy stay at 1.00, or collapse?
- Are the signatures **stable to the standardization set**? What happens to BERT↔RoBERTa's relative-NN status when a fifth masked-encoder model is added?
- Does extending the probe from frozen input embeddings to **contextual hidden states** (per-layer, pre- vs. post-attention) reveal layer-wise signature trajectories that distinguish fine-tuned vs. base, or distilled vs. teacher models?
- How sensitive are the 800-D signatures to **prompt distribution** (multilingual, adversarial, domain-specific) and to alternative Q–B–ε grids, trajectory lengths L, and matching tolerances ε?
- Can the per-config response surface be **compressed** — do a handful of (Q, B, ε) configurations carry most of the discriminative signal, and can those configurations be selected in a principled, model-agnostic way?
