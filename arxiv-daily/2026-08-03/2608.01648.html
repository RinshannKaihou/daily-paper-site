Evaluating Forecasting Techniques for Hardware Errors on a Large-scale HPC System ↗
Kaiyuan Liao · Xiwei Xuan · Tanwi Mallick · Kevin Brown · Christopher D. Carothers · Kwan-Liu Ma | UC Davis · Argonne · RPI | 2026-08-03 | arXiv:2608.01648
Key finding

Model capacity temporal structure governs HPC error forecasting — regular Minor faults yield to LSTM, sparse Critical errors defeat every model.

Key metric — predictability gap
30.29 RMSE% · Minor (LSTM)
vs
1,249 RMSE% · Critical (best)

A ~41× chasm between the most and least predictable error class on the same 7-year Theta log. Even the naive LAST baseline beats every deep model on Critical-error MAE (0.00132 vs 0.00165–0.00192).

n = 2,417 days · 8 models · 10 runs each
Why this matters

Establishes the first empirical feasibility boundary for HPC error forecasting — and a public benchmark for which temporal structures modern deep models can actually capture on engineering logs.

Critical edge

The “sparse errors are unpredictable” verdict is weakened because the strongest current TS models — TimesNet, PatchTST, Time-LLM, TEMPO — were never actually tested.

Also: single decommissioned system (Theta), no significance tests, no operational metric.
01

Methodology

Argonne Theta XC40 · 7-year production logs

Data & pipeline

Hardware error logs from Theta, an 11.7-petaflop Cray XC40 at ALCF — 4,392 Intel Xeon Phi 7230 nodes on an Aries dragonfly network.

Spanning Jun 20, 2017 – Jan 31, 2024 (2,417 daily obs). Pipeline: 70-second redundancy dedup (per Brown et al.), then severity classification into four levels and daily aggregation into four univariate count series.

Minor93.6%correctable / warning
Intermediate3.7%mid-severity
Major1.1%high-severity
Critical1.6%system-level

Models & protocol

One-step-ahead forecasting from a look-back window Lx (deep models standardized to Lx = 14, the biweekly maintenance cycle).

8 models: LAST, Rolling Mean, AR, ARIMA (Lx=100 sliding retrain), HA (period 14), LSTM, TCN, encoder-only Transformer.

Chronological 80/20 split · Adam + MSE · min-max [0,1] · grid-searched statistical baselines · MAE / MSE / RMSE% · 10-run averaging on Quadro RTX 8000.

Feature engineering (Minor series only): day-of-week one-hot, 1st/2nd-order difference, EMA(span=5).

03

Strengths & limitations

side-by-side

Strengths

  • Long (7-year), real, production-grade dataset from a leadership-class HPC system — rare and credible.
  • Honest, well-scoped feasibility framing: explicitly calls forecasting “a targeted tool, not a universal solution,” and reports negative results rather than overselling.
  • Clean protocol: chronological split, 10-run averaging, grid-searched baselines, three metrics, two ablation axes.
  • Practical guidance: optimal Lx per architecture, which features help which families, which classes warrant forecasting at all.
  • Severity-based formulation is a transferable lens for other HPC log datasets.

Limitations

  • Single-system evaluation (Theta only); cross-system generalization to GPU-dominated systems (Frontier, Aurora, Perlmutter) is untested.
  • Modern TS models most likely to challenge the verdict — TimesNet, PatchTST, Time-LLM, TEMPO, CARD — are not evaluated.
  • No significance testing; several “best” results differ from runners-up in the 4th decimal place.
  • 93.6% Minor imbalance means most quantitative conclusions rest on one series.
  • No operational metric (lead time, false-alarm rate) and no point-process / anomaly-detection baseline for burst-dominated errors.
  • No released code or model checkpoints.
04

Open questions for further work

  1. Would modern TS foundation models (TimesNet, PatchTST, Time-LLM, TEMPO, Timer-XL) materially change the predictability of Intermediate/Major/Critical errors — or do burst-dominated dynamics remain a fundamental barrier?
  2. Can point-process (Hawkes), survival analysis, or probabilistic / anomaly-aware frameworks outperform regression forecasting on the sparse series, and at what lead times?
  3. Do the findings transfer to current GPU-dominated systems (Frontier, Aurora, Perlmutter) with different node architectures, topologies (Slingshot vs Aries), and workload mixes?
  4. What operationally meaningful metric — false-alarm rate, lead-time-to-failure, maintenance-cost saving — should replace raw MAE/MSE/RMSE%, and what threshold defines a “useful” prediction?
  5. Why does feature engineering degrade TCN while helping LSTM/Transformer — a capacity/regularization effect of dilated causal convolutions on short auxiliary signals?
  6. Given that naive LAST wins on Critical-error MAE, is one-step-ahead daily forecasting even the right formulation, or should the target be multi-step lead-time prediction of component-level failures?
