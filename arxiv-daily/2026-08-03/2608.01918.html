§ 01

Methodology — three disciplined stages per turn

STAGE 01

Constrained evolution · Generalization Gate

Every edit must clear a global gate. Content: no instance ids, test names, private symbols, or token-conditional branches — only reusable criteria with an applicability condition. Placement: capabilities → middleware/tools/sub-agents; pure guidance → prompt/memory.

HARD-BAN list enforced
STAGE 02

Proactive first-person feedback

The code agent is queried twice per failed task: a blind report (verdict hidden) and a hindsight report (verdict shown, attributes to harness / reasoning / ambiguity / env). Reports are reconciled, grounded against trajectory, then aggregated by component with a confidence score.

blind + hindsight · anti-bias
STAGE 03

Component-wise optimization · R3 merge

Two variants evolve in parallel — structural-only and guidance-only — so updates never interfere. Higher Pass@1 wins; the loser is folded back via R3: Revision keeps independently beneficial loser edits, Recombination merges with winner precedence, Refinement drops overlap.

each turn compounds
Attribution setup: all four agent roles share one base model (GPT-5.4, non-thinking); both automatic methods start from the identical minimal seed H₀ (one shell tool, a short system prompt, nothing else).

§ 03

Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • One-to-one mapping of three named failure modes to three named principles, each with its own ablation row isolating the marginal effect.
  • The Gate's content + placement requirements are concrete and enforceable — the explicit HARD-BAN list is an unusually operationalized constraint.
  • Honest about a weakness: the paper openly shows Proactive Feedback hurts held-out performance without R3, instead of hiding it.
  • Cross-model + per-repository analyses go beyond a single headline number and directly address the generalization claim.
  • 4× iteration efficiency over AHE (5 vs 20 turns) — practically meaningful given expensive benchmark rollouts per turn.

Limitations

  • Single benchmark and single primary base model; transfer to terminal/web/long-horizon agent tasks is untested.
  • 50-task evolution sample is small and no variance / confidence intervals are reported; the +3 Sample edge may not be statistically robust.
  • Non-thinking mode throughout to isolate harness effects — how much of the gap survives extended reasoning is open.
  • Narrow comparison field: only AHE and the seed appear in result tables, despite many cited optimizers.
  • Rule-based Gate bans keywords/symbols; subtler task-specific shortcuts avoiding banned tokens could slip through — no evasion analysis.
  • No per-turn token/dollar cost reported; the 5-vs-20-turn edge may shrink in total compute (two tracks + R3 + feedback queries).
§ 04

Open questions for further work

Q · 01Do the three principles still compound under thinking mode, or does extended reasoning absorb the harness gains?
Q · 02How robust is the rule-based Gate to subtler task-specific shortcuts — latent-feature triggers, paraphrased instance descriptions?
Q · 03How does HarnessCompass compare empirically against ADAS, AFlow, Darwin Gödel Machine, Live-SWE-agent on the same split?
Q · 04What are the per-turn token/dollar costs of the two-track + R3 + feedback loop vs total compute, not just turns?
Q · 05Does it transfer beyond code-repo fixing to terminal workflows, web/tool coordination, long-horizon planning?
Q · 06Would multiple seeds / CIs on the 50-task sample change the +3 edge over AHE — and can blind-vs-hindsight be measured, not asserted?
