> **一句话总结：** RADAR 是一种"评测前"诊断框架，通过为每条评测准则生成定向合成探针、用验证器 LLM 在所有准则上打分并构造有向耦合矩阵，在 HelpSteer2/SummEval/SumPubMed 上以仅 3-5 个探针即可恢复人类跨准则相关性结构（Pearson r ≥ 0.84），远超 RRD 与被动相关性基线，成本不到全量标注的 1%。

> **TL;DR:** RADAR is a preflight diagnostic that detects behavioral coupling between rubric criteria in LLM-as-judge pipelines by generating criterion-conditioned synthetic probes and reading off-diagonal leakage. On HelpSteer2, SummEval, and SumPubMed it recovers human inter-criterion correlation at Pearson r ≥ 0.84 (vs. RRD ≤ 0.570 and synthetic correlation as low as −0.111), needing only 3-5 probes per criterion (~1-2M tokens, <1% of full annotation) to surface directional double-counting before large-scale judging.

## 核心贡献

- 提出并形式化了"准则耦合 (criterion coupling)"这一被既有方法忽视的失败模式：即便两条准则在文本上不重复，LLM 评判仍可能因内部"质量观"坍缩到同一潜在维度，从而在聚合分中悄然双重计入同一信号，扭曲模型发布决策。
- 设计了 RADAR 三阶段流水线（定向干预生成 → 全准则独立打分 → 有向泄漏矩阵），用干预式而非被动共变的方式判断两条准则在评判者行为下是否可分；矩阵的对称性与不对称性把"双向双重计入"与"上下游依赖"区分开。
- 在三个真实人工标注基准（HelpSteer2、SummEval、SumPubMed）上验证 RADAR 的合成耦合估计能恢复人类跨准则相关性，Pearson r ≥ 0.84，且是三种方法中唯一在所有基准上一致达标的方法。
- 给出极低成本可扩展性结论：每准则只需 3-5 个探针、约 1.2-1.9M tokens（不到 HelpSteer2 全量标注 150M tokens 的 1%），相对全量标注节省 380×/130×，为快速迭代的智能体评测提供了可落地的"上线前审计"步骤。

## Key Contributions

- Defines and formalizes **criterion coupling** as a hidden failure mode in rubric-based LLM-as-judge: distinct-looking criteria can collapse to a single latent axis under a judge, silently double-counting one response property in aggregate scores.
- Builds RADAR, a three-stage preflight diagnostic (criterion-conditioned generation → per-criterion independent scoring → directional coupling matrix) that probes separability via intervention rather than passive correlation, and separates symmetric double-counting from asymmetric upstream-downstream dependence.
- Validates RADAR on three human-annotated industry benchmarks (HelpSteer2, SummEval, SumPubMed), showing it is the only method among RADAR / RRD / synthetic correlation to consistently recover the human inter-criterion structure at Pearson r ≥ 0.84 and Spearman ρ ≥ 0.83 across all three.
- Demonstrates cost-stable scalability: 3-5 probes per criterion (≈1.2-1.9M tokens, 0.8-1.3% of the ≈150M-token full HelpSteer2 annotation, 380×/130× savings) match the accuracy of N=20 probes, making continuous rubric auditing feasible amid fast model release cadence.

## Methodology

RADAR takes a rubric R = {C1,…,Cn} (each criterion is a label plus a one-paragraph definition), a generator LLM G, and a verifier LLM-as-judge V, and outputs a directional coupling matrix without needing any human labels.

**Stage 1 — Criterion-conditioned generation.** For each task prompt t, criterion Ci, and direction d ∈ {+, −}, RADAR asks G to produce N responses that "strongly satisfy" or "clearly fail" Ci *without naming any other criterion* (HIGH/LOW for descriptive axes like verbosity). Crucially, only the targeted criterion is named, so any movement on untargeted criteria signals coupling. N=20 probes are drawn per (task, criterion, direction) cell at temperature 0.7.

**Stage 2 — Per-criterion independent scoring.** The verifier V is queried once per criterion on each probe, returning an integer score Si(x) ∈ {0,1,2,3,4} normalized to pi(x) ∈ [0,1]. Separate calls (rather than one prompt listing all criteria) deliberately eliminate within-prompt ordering and self-consistency artifacts, so residual cross-criterion correlation is less likely to be a prompt artifact.

**Stage 3 — Coupling statistics.** The pipeline computes: (i) **SelfEffect(Ci)** = the verifier's own score gap between high/low Ci probes — the intervention legibility that gates reliability (cells with SelfEffect < τ=0.40 are flagged unreliable and excluded); (ii) **Leakage(Ci → Cj)** = GenCoupling(Ci→Cj) normalized by SelfEffect(Ci), i.e. how much moving Ci shifts Cj relative to how strongly Ci itself moved; (iii) **SymCoupling** and **Asymmetry** from the pair's two directed leakages — high symmetric + low asymmetry ⇒ shared latent cluster; high asymmetry ⇒ directional (upstream→downstream) dependence; (iv) per-task mean and std to separate stable rubric-level coupling from distribution-sensitive coupling.

**Generator-verifier grid.** Four contemporary LLMs (GPT-5-mini, GPT-5.5 Reasoning, Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Sonnet 4.6) form a grid, with self-pairings excluded to dodge self-preference bias, giving 12 off-diagonal cells per benchmark (36 total). Per benchmark, T=5 short task prompts are hand-curated from the target domain, independent of the annotated corpus used for validation.

**Baselines.** (a) Synthetic correlation — score unconditioned generations on every criterion and correlate score columns; (b) RRD (Shen et al., 2026) — whitening-based decorrelation applied to an independently sampled score set. Both are passive (no intervention).

## Results & Highlights

- **HelpSteer2 (GPT-5.5-R → Sonnet-4.6 cell):** RADAR reaches Pearson +0.957, Spearman +0.879, MAE 0.075 against human inter-criterion correlations, vs. RRD +0.570 / +0.044 / 0.318 and synthetic correlation +0.671 / +0.714 / 0.258. RADAR is the only method to clear r ≥ 0.84 here while RRD's Spearman collapses to 0.044.
- **SummEval (same cell):** RADAR Pearson +0.842, Spearman +0.943; synthetic correlation is *anti-correlated* at r = −0.111, ρ = −0.500 (MAE 0.581), and RRD only reaches +0.050 / +0.395. This is the most striking failure of the passive baselines.
- **SumPubMed:** RADAR Pearson +0.872, Spearman +0.829, MAE 0.079, while RRD drops to +0.064 / −0.030 / 0.386; synthetic correlation is +0.765 / +0.486 but inconsistent.
- **Headline claim — RADAR is the only method that recovers the human structure on all three benchmarks (Pearson ≥ 0.84, Spearman ≥ 0.83);** RRD never exceeds ρ = 0.40 and synthetic correlation flips sign on SummEval.
- **Probe-budget scaling (HelpSteer2, Table 3):** N=3 costs 1.16M tokens (0.8% of full ≈149.5M-token annotation, 130× savings) at Pearson 0.953 ± 0.011; N=5 costs 1.94M (1.3%, 77× savings) at 0.953 ± 0.009; N=20 costs 7.74M (5.2%) at 0.957. Accuracy is essentially flat, and all three benchmarks stay ≥ 0.84 even at N=1.
- **Concrete diagnostic pairs surfaced (Table 1, means over 12-cell grid):** HelpSteer2 helpfulness/correctness shows Sym=+0.92, Asym=0.21 against human corr +0.94 (bidirectional cluster → independent weighting double-counts); SummEval coherence/fluency Sym=+0.57, Asym=0.16 vs human +0.67 (cluster); HelpSteer2 coherence→correctness leakage 0.78 vs 0.22 reverse (directional); SumPubMed coherence→readability 0.62 vs 0.26 (directional).
- **Reliability gating:** every axis on every benchmark clears the τ=0.40 self-effect threshold (min 0.46; per-benchmark means 0.64-0.78), so every reported leakage comes from a judge-legible intervention; criteria below τ would be flagged as too ill-defined to score.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is a collaboration between Divyansh Singh (Microsoft / University of Florida), Reza Davari (Microsoft, corresponding), and Afra Mashhadi (Microsoft / University of Washington). The authorship sits squarely in the LLM-evaluation tooling space, and the framing — "rubric scores gate model releases, drive iteration, feed regression dashboards" — is consistent with industry practitioner incentives rather than pure academic benchmark chasing. The validation strategy is honest: they do not claim a gold dependency graph exists, treat human inter-criterion correlation only as an imperfect proxy, and explicitly use benchmarks whose annotations are *separate* from the synthetic probes RADAR runs (T=5 hand-curated task prompts per benchmark, drawn independent of the annotated corpus). Citations situate RADAR clearly against G-Eval, HD-Eval, RRD, ARGO, Prometheus, and the Feuer et al. "factor collapse" line, and they re-implement RRD as the strongest baseline rather than strawmanning it.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** is genuine but narrow. The high-level observation that LLM-judge criteria collapse to a few latent factors is already established by Feuer et al. (2025b), and RRD (Shen et al., 2026) already targets rubric redundancy. RADAR's actual contribution is the *intervention-based, preflight* framing plus the directional (asymmetric) leakage decomposition — this is a real methodological delta but is presented as a bigger leap than it is.
- **Rigor of the central comparison is the strongest part of the paper but has a notable weak spot.** The headline Table 2 reports a single representative cell (GPT-5.5-R → Sonnet-4.6), and Table 1 means are over 12 cells, but the paper leans heavily on Pearson r aggregated over only ~6 off-diagonal criterion pairs per benchmark (HelpSteer2 has 5 axes ⇒ 10 pairs; SummEval 4 axes ⇒ 6 pairs; SumPubMed 4 axes ⇒ 6 pairs). Pearson over 6 points is fragile, and the reported ±0.011 std at N=3 does not by itself establish stability of the *ranking* of methods — a permutation or bootstrap-over-pairs test would be more convincing than the probe-subsample bootstrap shown.
- **Reproducibility** is mixed. Probe generation templates and decoding settings are promised in appendices, the four-model grid and τ=0.40 threshold are concrete, and the three benchmarks are public. However, the GPT-5-mini / GPT-5.5 Reasoning / Claude Opus 4.7 / Claude Sonnet 4.6 model names are not yet externally reproducible at the time of writing, and the hand-curation of T=5 task prompts per benchmark is an undocumented, potentially high-leverage human choice — different prompts could move the human-correlation target. There is no ablation over T or over task-prompt selection.
- **Significance** is real for practitioners: a sub-1%-of-annotation-cost preflight that flags which criteria to merge/rewight before a release gate is a useful artifact. The directional vs. symmetric split is a genuinely actionable audit signal that symmetric correlation provably cannot recover (the 0.78 vs 0.22 HelpSteer2 coherence→correctness case).
- **Red flags.** (1) The claim "Pearson stays ≥ 0.84 even at N=1" is mentioned in passing in the discussion but the supporting table (Appendix G) is not shown in the main text — given that N=1 is one probe per (task, criterion, direction), this is the most surprising number in the paper and deserves a main-text result. (2) Coupling is acknowledged to be **conditional on the generator-verifier pair**, not a property of the rubric — meaning a team must re-run RADAR whenever either changes, and the 12-cell aggregation may mask evaluator-specific pathology. The paper is honest about this but it substantially limits the "one-time audit" framing in the abstract. (3) No causal identification: the authors admit leakage could be driven by a confounded third property shifted by the Ci probe rather than Ci itself, which is controlled only by self-effect gating, not by design.

## Strengths

- Intervention-based design is the right tool for separating "judge treats as one dimension" from "merely co-occurs in data" — a real gap that passive correlation and RRD's whitening provably cannot fill.
- The directional / symmetric decomposition gives practitioners distinct, actionable audit hypotheses (merge vs. inspect-hierarchy vs. leave-alone product preference) rather than a single number.
- Cost-accuracy curve is excellent: 0.8% of full annotation tokens for Pearson 0.953 on HelpSteer2 makes the method genuinely cheap to rerun as models and rubrics churn.
- Honest limitations section explicitly conditions results on the generator-verifier pair and disclaims causal identification.
- Validation uses benchmarks whose human annotations are independent of the synthetic probes, so the comparison is not circular.

## Limitations

- Coupling estimates are tied to a specific (generator, verifier) pair and must be refreshed when either changes — not an intrinsic property of the rubric.
- No formal causal identification: a probe targeting Ci may shift a confounding property that moves Cj, controlled only by self-effect gating rather than by design.
- Human inter-criterion correlation is an imperfect reference (mixes dependence, annotator noise, dataset-specific co-occurrence); no benchmark provides a gold dependency graph.
- Main-text headline (Table 2) is a single generator-verifier cell; ranking stability over the full grid is shown only as aggregated means (Table 1) with per-cell breakdowns deferred to an appendix.
- Validation is limited to text-based, single-turn, multi-axis settings (assistant response, news summary, biomedical summary); extension to multimodal, tool-use, safety, or multi-turn agentic evaluation is claimed to be template-only but unproven.
- RADAR deliberately stops at diagnosis — it will not tell a team whether a flagged coupling is redundancy, intended hierarchy, or acceptable product preference; that decision still requires a human in the loop.
- T=5 hand-curated task prompts per benchmark are an undocumented, potentially high-leverage choice; there is no ablation over T or prompt selection.

## Relevance

这项工作直接服务于"如何可信地评估 LLM 与智能体"这一核心问题：它给出了一种在跑大规模评测前、用极低成本自动检测评分准则是否在 LLM-as-judge 下发生行为耦合的方法，对关注评测可靠性、评测偏差与自动评测流水线的读者有较高参考价值，可与既有对 LLM-judge 偏差、奖励模型可解释性的研究形成互补。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How stable are RADAR's coupling matrices across the full 12-cell grid at the *pair* level (not just the aggregated mean)? Are there generator-verifier pairs where RADAR inverts the human ranking?
- Does the N=1 Pearson-≥-0.84 claim hold across all three benchmarks or only HelpSteer2, and does it survive a leave-one-task-prompt-out analysis over the hand-curated T=5 set?
- Can the directional leakage matrix be turned into an automated *repair* (criterion merging, reweighting, hierarchy induction) that measurably reduces aggregate-score distortion on a held-out model-comparison task, rather than stopping at diagnosis?
- When the production judge is swapped (e.g., GPT-5.5-R → Opus 4.7), how often does the set of flagged coupled pairs change, and is there a cheap transfer heuristic to avoid rerunning the full grid?
- Does intervention-based coupling generalize to multi-turn, tool-use, or safety rubrics, where "strongly satisfy / clearly fail Ci in isolation" is genuinely hard to construct — i.e., is self-effect gating going to flag most criteria as unreliable in those settings?
