Self-Certification of Representation Adequacy: Sequential Certification at Minimum Task Loss

Zijie Huang · Independent Researcher · arXiv:2608.02267v1 [cs.AI]

representation-adequacy sequential-testing pure-exploration track-and-stop bayes-risk dual-control self-certification
The headline finding

Verify is worth exactly c*=(Tr/2)(1−TV) —
and every δ-correct self-certification
pays ≥ C(E,f)·log(1/δ),
a bound CTS meets.

A four-layer theory turns "is my compressed representation still adequate?" into a priced decision: a Bayes-risk adequacy label, an exact one-shot TV threshold for buying a verification, and a sequential layer where the cost-weighted characteristic time C(E,f) is solved as a finite linear program. The framework also proves its own boundary — kernel-switching policies escape the constant entirely.

Why this matters

Gives a decision-theoretic answer to whether an agent can detect its own representation/memory adequacy from the inside — bridging representation learning, state aggregation, POMDP belief sufficiency, and active hypothesis testing.

Conceptual foundation, not a deployable method — value is theoretical.

Critical edge

The whole sequential theory is fixed-kernel (M1.2) — Verify-driven switching, Repair and representation dynamics all violate the premise and collapse cost to O(1): the proven constant does not bound the procedures an agent would actually deploy.

— Section 4, NT2; the Repair program is left open.

01 / FRAMEWORK

Four layers, each priced in task loss

A self-contained theory paper (single independent author, no datasets). It stacks a static adequacy label, a one-shot certification impossibility result with an exact buy threshold, a sequential optimal-stopping problem, and an honest boundary layer that demarcates where the analysis breaks.

01 · STATIC
Adequacy label
§2.2 · Thm. 1
Adeq_E(f) = 1[R*_E(U) = R*_E(H)]

Representation f:H→U is adequate iff Bayes risk under the compressed view equals risk under full history — reduced cell-by-cell to no action conflicts within any aliasing cell.

02 · ONE-SHOT
Price of one Verify
§2.3–2.5 · Thm. 3, 6
err(C) ≥ (1−TV)/2
c* = (T_r/2)(1−TV)

Le Cam's two-point method makes internal certification impossible below chance; a perfect external Verify is worth buying iff its price c ≤ c*. Counterexample A falsifies a strong state-closure conjecture.

03 · SEQUENTIAL
Optimal stopping, core
§3 · Thm. 8, 9, Cor. 10
R(π,E_i) ≥ C_i·kl(δ,1−δ)
CTS: lim sup R/log(1/δ) ≤ C_i

Every δ-correct policy pays the lower bound; the Certification Track-and-Stop policy (MLE estimator, plug-in cost-ratio optimizer, C-tracking, GLR stop) matches it to first order. C_i is a finite LP.

04 · BOUNDARY
Where it ends
§4 · NT1, NT2, B1–B3
switching → O(1)
≠ Θ(log(1/δ))

A near-perfect Verify truncates the loss stream: kernel-switching policies beat the fixed-kernel optimum (≥4.17× at δ=10⁻⁴) and escape the proven constant. Repair is left open.

NT2 · the proven constant does not govern switching§4

Fixed-kernel Θ(log 1/δ) vs. kernel-switching O(1)

The single sharpest boundary: a one-shot perfect Verify at price 1.0 truncates the loss stream, so a kernel-switching policy pays exactly 1.0 at every δ while the fixed-kernel formula predicts Θ(log(1/δ)). The gap is unbounded asymptotically.

03 / ASSESSMENT

Strengths & limitations, side by side

Strengths

  • Exceptional honesty. The five-direction audit (§1.4) and Table 6 enumerate which adjacent line each tool comes from and which element of the present setting is uncovered.
  • Full proofs, tracked assumptions. Lower/upper bounds proved in App. A–B; theorems stated under explicit H1–H4 vs H1–H3 sets.
  • Self-falsified conjecture. Counterexample A is reported in the body, not buried — the author records their own earlier "state-closure" conjecture being wrong.
  • Clean mathematical structure. C(E,f) has two equivalent forms (covering LP and max-min characteristic time) with the duality spelled out via Sion's minimax.
  • Reproducible numerics. All instance tables/channels tabulated to four decimals; LP verified by both dual-simplex and grid max-min (within 2.5×10⁻⁴); binary specialization recovers Nitinawarat–Veeravalli exactly.

Limitations

  • Fixed-kernel restriction (M1.2). The proven constant does not govern kernel-switching, Verify-driven, or Repair policies — exactly the procedures an agent would deploy.
  • No empirical evaluation. §5 verifies finite hand-crafted instances only; no dialogue, RL, or learned-embedding experiments connect theory to practice.
  • Asymptotic-only matching. C(E,f) governs only limδ→0; at δ=0.01 CTS pays 1.9–2.5× the constant, with no tight second-order analysis.
  • "No loss feedback" is load-bearing. Every unidentifiability result flows from this ESD modeling choice; with loss feedback (Prop. 5(b)(ii)) the picture changes and concept-drift methods (FLORA) are only acknowledged as orthogonal.
  • Single independent author, no peer review. 108-page self-published tract; correctness of the longer concentration and plug-in stability arguments rests on author-provided proofs.
04 / OPEN

Questions worth chasing

Policy-dependent kernels. Can C(E,f) extend to kernel-switching transcripts, and does a finite constant survive as a dynamic program over posterior-dependent regimes (§4.2)?
The free-ride scale. In boundary B2 (C=0), what is the exact sub-logarithmic scale of R*? The paper shows CTS still pays Ω(√log(1/δ)) and leaves tightness open.
Loss feedback. Can the framework incorporate loss feedback (Prop. 5(b)(ii)) without collapsing to standard online aliasing-regret estimation?
Large/continuous environment classes. How do the LP and CTS behave when K→∞ and Alt(i) becomes a continuum — the covering LP is no longer finite?
Closing the 1.9–2.5× gap. Is there a non-asymptotic mixture-threshold GLR variant (Kaufmann–Koolen 2021 spirit) tight at δ∈[0.01, 0.1]?
Priced Repair. Can the Repair action enter the decision problem when a successful repair changes the alternative sets Alt(i) of all future certifications (§4.3)?
