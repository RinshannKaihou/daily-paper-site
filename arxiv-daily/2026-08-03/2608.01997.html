AOS: Adaptive Optimizer Switching via Training-State Signals for Faster Convergence and Better Generalization

Alok Kumar Pandey · Umang Chaturvedi · Aatish Rana · Gopi Krishna Nedanuri — Qualcomm India, Hyderabad
optimizer-switching adaptive-training gradient-noise-scale convergence image-classification
Key finding

AOS-R switches among AdamW, Lion, and SGD-M via six online training-state signals — winning 6 of 8 CIFAR tasks at 0.80× AdamW's convergence cost.

Key metric
0.80×
mean convergence speedup vs AdamW
Reaches 78% top-1 on CIFAR-100 / WRN-28-10 in 81 epochs vs 109 for AdamW — 26% fewer.
Why this matters

Interpretable six-signal online diagnosis plus state-preserving switching — directly transferable to large-scale pretraining as adaptive optimizer-scheduling infrastructure.

Critical edge

Headline accuracy gains (+0.1 to +0.4 pp) rest on a single seed (42) and are not separated from plausible seed variance — the result's statistical strength is unverified.

01 /

Methodology

Three design pillars: a fixed three-optimizer pool, six online training-state signals evaluated every 200 steps, and state-preserving switching bridged by a 400-step LR ramp.

Optimizer pool

AdamW (noisy early phase, per-coordinate adaptation) · Lion (memory-efficient sign momentum, mid-phase) · SGD-M (flat-minima convergence, late phase).

State-preserving transfer

Momentum buffers map across optimizers at switch points — e.g. SGD-M velocity seeded from AdamW's bias-corrected first moment, scaled by the LR ratio. Naive zero-init costs 0.9 pp.

LR continuity bridge

When the LR ratio r > Rbridge=8, the incoming optimizer's learning rate linearly ramps over 400 steps, preventing loss spikes at boundaries (3.1× larger spike without it).

Six online signals — click to inspect
S1Gradient Noise Scale

Mini-batch split into two micro-batches; high GNS = noisy gradients.

favors → AdamW
Threshold τ_gns = 0.60. High GNS indicates adaptive per-coordinate methods will outperform sign-based or plain momentum.
S2Hutchinson Curvature

m=5 Rademacher probes estimate the Hessian trace.

favors → SGD-M
Threshold τ_tr = 2.0× median. Sharp geometry (high trace) benefits from SGD-M's implicit sharpness regularization.
S3Loss Stagnation

ΔL over a window falls below δstag.

favors → SGD-M
Threshold δ_stag = 1e-4. Stalled descent triggers a switch to exploit SGD-M's flat-minima convergence.
S4Update Stability Ratio

ρ = ‖Δθ‖ / (‖∇L‖ + ε); large = instability.

favors → SGD-M
Threshold τ_ρ = 5.0. Large update-to-gradient ratios signal divergence risk, preferring the calmer SGD-M dynamics.
S5Gradient Stability Index

Ratio of squared EMA of ‖∇L‖ to EMA of ‖∇L‖².

favors → Lion / SGD-M
Threshold τ_gsi = 0.65. Gates Lion and late-phase SGD-M transitions once gradient direction stabilizes.
S6Loss Improvement Ratio

Normalized descent vs a reference baseline.

favors → early SGD-M
Threshold τ_lir = 0.05, gated by t/T ≥ 0.60. Triggers an early SGD-M transition when improvement plateaus late in training.

Anti-thrash: hysteresis (H=3 consecutive evaluations) + minimum dwell Dmin=4,000 steps on CIFAR-100. Empirically S ≤ 4 switches per run.

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Genuinely lightweight: ≤2.5% overhead, no extra forward/backward beyond 5 Hutchinson probes, S≤4 switches.
  • Well-motivated signals: each maps to a distinct pathology (noise, sharpness, stagnation, instability, drift, plateau); ablation confirms independent information.
  • Concrete engineering wins: state transfer (saves 0.9 pp) and LR bridge (3.1× smaller spike) are validated, not assumed.
  • Convergence theory correctly frames cost as additive S·Dmin; S grows sublinearly with T in practice.
  • Shared hyperparameters work across 7 of 8 benchmarks — no per-architecture tuning.

Limitations

  • Single-seed headline: Tables II/III use seed 42 only; +0.1 to +0.4 pp wins sit within plausible seed variance.
  • Narrow scope: only CIFAR-10/100 supervised classification; ImageNet and GLUE are "in progress" and absent.
  • Known failure mode: PyramidNet-110 loses to SGD-M by ~1 pp on both splits — monotonic channel widening breaks the Hutchinson threshold; unfixed.
  • Threshold sensitivity: six hand-set thresholds + Rbridge + Dmin; thrashing mitigated but "not eliminated."
  • AOS-L underspecified: learned MLP's training data, architecture, and reward are not detailed.
  • Theory is descriptive, not predictive — does not explain the wall-clock speedup. No large-model / long-context evaluation.
04 /

Open questions

  1. Do the +0.4 pp mean gains survive multi-seed (≥5 seeds) evaluation, and what is the per-row variance on the single-seed Table II wins?
  2. How does AOS-R behave on ImageNet-scale and transformer/LM pretraining, where the AdamW→Lion→SGD-M phase story and τtr may not transfer?
  3. Can the six hand-tuned thresholds be auto-calibrated per-architecture to fix the PyramidNet-110 failure without manual intervention?
  4. Is the observed phase ordering truly a property of the optimization landscape, or an artifact of CIFAR augmentation / ResNet-family curvature?
  5. How does AOS interact with second-order or sharpness-aware optimizers (SAM, SOAP/Shampoo) added to the pool — does the convergence bound still hold?
  6. What is the full specification and training cost of AOS-L, and does its 0.76× advantage generalize beyond the single ablation setting?
