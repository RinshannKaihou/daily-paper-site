Long-Horizon Autonomous Architecture Research with a Language-Model Agent: A Behavioural Case Study

Aon Safdar · Mohamed Saadeldin — University College Dublin, Ireland [v1, submitted to IEEE]

One LLM ran a 10-week architecture search, hit a saturation wall, and revealed that workflow design — not model capability — is the key lever in long-horizon autonomous research.

A single persistent LLM session holds all five research roles (proposer, implementer, executor, interpreter, archivist) over ~10 weeks / ~2,400 GPU-hours / ~100 hypotheses on a channel-primary vision transformer — pushing CIFAR-10 from 69.67% → 96.59% with only bounded human intervention.

Why this matters

Surfaces failure modes invisible at benchmark timescales — saturation walls, greedy anchoring, cross-scale anti-transfer — and hands back five actionable loop-design levers for autonomous-research pipelines.

Critical edge

N=1, no counterfactual human control, no run-to-run replicate. And the headline +27 pp mostly measures how weak the baseline was — "add an FFN" is a textbook transformer move.

01 Methodology · the loop, not the architecture

Four design principles of the autonomous research loop

The authors study a loop, not an architecture. Four binding rules govern behaviour:

  • 1Single agent, single hypothesis. Exactly one experiment in flight at a time — parallelism is deliberately sacrificed to keep per-hypothesis causal signal clean.
  • 2Commit-or-discard. Beat the champion → commit & it becomes the new baseline; else revert. Later shown to be isomorphic to greedy hill-climbing — the main structural source of incremental bias.
  • 3Structured persistent memory. Each hypothesis logged in a fixed template the agent must read at session start and append before every submission.
  • 4End-of-turn after submission. An external watcher returns control when the Slurm job finishes — token budget stays on reasoning, not polling.

03 Strengths ↔ Limitations · side by side

What is genuinely strong

  • Unusual intellectual honesty — confounds, failure modes and the tool-access confound are enumerated, not buried.
  • Complete, auditable human-intervention record (Table 2) plus a candid cost breakdown; rare in autonomous-agent papers.
  • The workflow-induced vs. LLM-inherent bias decomposition (Table 10) is a frame other groups can operationalise and test.
  • Cross-scale anti-findings (Table 8) and independent rediscoveries (Table 9) are concrete and falsifiable.
  • Five recommended workflow interventions map cleanly onto observed failure modes — genuinely actionable.

Where the evidence is fragile

  • Single agent, single problem, single run. N=1 on every axis; quantitative findings may be trajectory-specific.
  • No counterfactual human control on the same problem and compute — comparisons to human behaviour are indicative only.
  • Tool-access confound: Phase 1→1b bundled literature access, code-edit permission and a brief amendment. The "literature broke the wall" claim cannot isolate a cause.
  • No run-to-run replicates; LLM stochasticity means a different seed could produce a different champion and trace.
  • The ImageNet champion is a +0.26 pp delta with no multi-seed and no 300-epoch validation — fragile evidence.
04 Open questions for further work
  1. Does the three-regime structure generalise beyond architecture search to data-curation or training-recipe pipelines — or is it an artefact of the channel-attention design space?
  2. How much of the Phase-1→1b jump is literature specifically vs. the simultaneous code-edit authorisation? A staged ablation is the obvious next experiment.
  3. Do budgeted moonshots (every k-th hypothesis introduces a new mechanism) actually beat greedy hill-climbing — and at what k?
  4. How sensitive are the greedy-anchoring / post-failure risk-aversion findings to the underlying LLM? Same workflow, several frontier models, would separate capability from workflow effects.
  5. Is the cross-scale sign-flip of mixup / label-smoothing / drop-path a genuine scale effect or a protocol artefact?
  6. How would a matched human researcher do on the same problem, compute and hypothesis budget? The authors flag this as the single most informative missing experiment.
