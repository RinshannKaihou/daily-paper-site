Divergent large language model predictions from convergent representations in ambiguous word pairs

K. Jack Scott · Narun Pat · Veronica Liesaputra  —  University of Otago (Psychology & Computing)

Key

Word senses reconverge geometrically in late layers while next-token predictions diverge maximally there — so last-layer cosine similarity systematically hides what the model actually disambiguates.

Why This Matters
Embedding pipelines used by RAG, semantic search and clustering read from the last layer — exactly where geometry misreports the model's true disambiguation ability.
Critical Edge
Evidence comes from single-token residual-stream activations in base decoder LMs — not the pooled sentence vectors from instruction-tuned embedding models actually used in production. The bridge to applied cosine failure is, in the authors' own words, “suggestive rather than direct.”

01Methodology

Three decoder-only models · 3 orders of magnitude · TransformerLens v2.1.6 · no fine-tuning

Stimuli
Strict homonyms (bank: money / river) and concept-drift polysemes (mouse: rodent / device). 190 + 97 candidate pairs → after validation, 156–167 homonym & 86–94 polyseme pairs for logit lens; 66–86 & 25–36 for patching.
Exp 1 · Logit lens
At every layer: activation distance (1 − cos h), logit distance (1 − cos WUh), and KL divergence of next-token distributions across the two contexts.
Exp 2 · Activation patching
Swap residual-stream activation at the target position A↔B per layer. Measure token overlap (top-10) and swap effectiveness = DKL(Pswap‖Porig) / DKL(Pswap‖Pother).
Exp 3 · Single-layer ablation
Zero one residual layer (protecting foundations 0–1/2/3); track % degradation of all three lens metrics downstream — exposes per-layer criticality.
Sequence-position control
Reordered cat/mouse sentence pairs isolate semantic distance from pure positional/syntactic distance across all 28 Llama layers.
Statistics
Mixed-effects (GPT-2) + non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis on normalized ranks (Llama/Qwen, handling non-finite late-layer KL by dropping, not capping → conservative). Mann-Whitney U pairwise; α = .05.

02Results & Highlights

Same dissociation across 117M → 3B → 32B · homonyms & polysemes

  • Geometry peaks mid, reconverges late; predictions keep diverging. GPT-2 activation distance .069 → .215 → .167 while KL grows 2.08 → 10.70 → 16.45. Replicated on Llama (KL rank .14 → .46 → .82, H=3808.70) and Qwen (.14 → .53 → .89, H=5464.47).
  • Late layers are causally sufficient despite geometric closeness. Qwen2.5-32B patching token overlap .23 → .66 → .95; swap effectiveness climbs to 965–1298 in late layers — bidirectional A↔B, so it is encoding, not denoising.
  • Ablation reveals architecturally divergent routes to the same behaviour. Llama: representations robust (4–14% degradation) but predictions fragile everywhere (95–100%). GPT-2 & Qwen: representational and predictive criticality are coupled, dropping after layer 6 / layer 30.
  • Not a positional artifact. Llama homonym activation distance beats the reordered cat/mouse control at every one of 28 layers (early median .483 vs .113, rrb=.758; all p<.001).
  • Qwen final-layer anomaly. Layer 63: activation distance dips (.50 vs .53) yet KL rank jumps to .92 vs .52 — maximal prediction divergence coincident with representational reconvergence.

03Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Three-model, three-orders-of-magnitude design with two ambiguity types and bidirectional patching.
  • Decouples representational encoding from predictive encoding instead of conflating them.
  • Causal patching evidence, not just correlational geometry.
  • Honest scope — flags the Llama-vs-Qwen puzzle and missing anisotropy correction.
  • Falsifiable, practical advice for the embedding/RAG community.

Limitations

  • Single-token residual activations ≠ pooled embeddings from instruction-tuned models used in production.
  • Standard logit lens, not the tuned lens (Belrose 2023); no direct replication.
  • No anisotropy baseline subtraction, unlike Ma et al. (2025) and Ethayarajh (2019).
  • One model per scale — scale and architecture are confounded.
  • No trained linear probe per layer; linear decodability of late layers still open.
  • Decoder-only, English only; no encoder/encoder-decoder or pragmatic ambiguity test.

04Open Questions

Where the reconvergence-while-divergence pattern goes next

  1. Does the pattern replicate in instruction-tuned or embedding-specialised models with pooled sentence/document vectors — the actual objects in production RAG?
  2. Does a trained linear probe at each layer recover sense identity from late layers, and with what accuracy gap versus middle layers?
  3. What circuit-level features (cf. Ameisen et al. 2025 attribution graphs) drive reconvergence of geometry and simultaneous amplification of prediction?
  4. Does Llama's "robust representations, fragile predictions" survive a controlled scale sweep within the Llama family, or is it tied to data/regularisation?
  5. Do encoder and encoder-decoder models (BERT, T5) show the same dissociation, or does a non-unidirectional objective change late-layer geometry?
  6. Would middle-layer + prediction-derived features measurably beat last-layer cosine on a benchmarked WSD or retrieval task?
