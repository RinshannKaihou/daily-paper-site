RADAR Rubric-Aware Dependency & Redundancy Analysis for LLM-as-Judge Evaluation

Divyansh Singh · Reza Davari · Afra Mashhadi — Microsoft / University of Florida · University of Washington
llm-as-judge rubric-evaluation evaluation-diagnostic criterion-coupling synthetic-probing
◆ Key finding

RADAR detects hidden criterion coupling in LLM-judge rubrics via synthetic intervention probes — recovering the human inter-criterion structure at Pearson r ≥ 0.84 on all three benchmarks, the only method to do so.

+Key metric

↳Why this matters

A sub-1%-of-annotation preflight audit that flags which rubric criteria to merge or reweight — before large-scale judging silently double-counts one signal and distorts release decisions.

!Critical edge

Coupling is conditional on each (generator, verifier) pair, not a property of the rubric. The "one-time audit" framing breaks whenever the judge or generator changes — and the headline table rests on a single grid cell.

01 / METHOD

Intervention, not correlation

STAGE 1

Criterion-conditioned generation

For each task, criterion Ci and direction d ∈ {+,−}, ask generator G for N responses that strongly satisfy or clearly fail Ci — without naming any other criterion. Movement elsewhere signals coupling.

STAGE 2

Per-criterion independent scoring

Verifier V scores each probe on each criterion in separate calls — eliminating within-prompt ordering and self-consistency artifacts. Residual cross-criterion correlation is then behavior, not prompt.

STAGE 3

Directional coupling matrix

Compute SelfEffect, normalized Leakage(Ci→Cj), then SymCoupling and Asymmetry. Symmetric+low-asymmetry ⇒ shared cluster; high asymmetry ⇒ upstream→downstream.

Generator × Verifier grid
Four LLMs — GPT-5-mini, GPT-5.5 Reasoning, Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Sonnet 4.6 — give 12 off-diagonal cells per benchmark (36 total); self-pairings excluded to dodge self-preference.
Reliability gate & baselines
Cells with SelfEffect < τ=0.40 are flagged unreliable and excluded. Baselines: synthetic correlation & RRD (Shen et al. 2026, whitening) — both passive.

04 / CRITIQUE

Strengths & limitations

✓Strengths

  • Intervention beats observation. Probing separability via generation is the right tool to separate "judge treats as one dimension" from "merely co-occurs in data" — a gap RRD's whitening cannot fill.
  • Directional / symmetric split yields distinct audit hypotheses (merge vs. inspect hierarchy vs. leave-alone preference) instead of one number.
  • Cost-accuracy curve is excellent: 0.8% of full annotation tokens for Pearson 0.953 makes RADAR cheap to rerun as rubrics and models churn.
  • Honest limits section conditions results on the generator-verifier pair and disclaims causal identification; validation uses human annotations independent of the synthetic probes.

!Limitations

  • Pair-conditional. Coupling estimates must be refreshed whenever the judge or generator changes — not an intrinsic property of the rubric.
  • No causal identification. A Ci probe may shift a confounding property that moves Cj; controlled only by self-effect gating, not by design.
  • Fragile headline. Pearson over ~6 off-diagonal pairs per benchmark is brittle; main-text Table 2 is a single grid cell, with full-grid per-cell breakdowns deferred to an appendix.
  • Scope. Only text, single-turn, multi-axis. Generalization to multimodal, tool-use, safety, or multi-turn agentic rubrics is claimed template-only, unproven. T=5 hand-curated task prompts per benchmark are an undocumented, high-leverage choice with no ablation.
05 / OPEN

Questions worth asking next

i.

How stable are RADAR's coupling matrices at the pair level across the full 12-cell grid — not just the aggregated mean? Are there generator-verifier pairs where it inverts the human ranking?

ii.

Does the N=1, Pearson ≥ 0.84 claim hold across all three benchmarks or only HelpSteer2 — and does it survive a leave-one-task-prompt-out analysis over the hand-curated T=5 set?

iii.

Can the directional matrix become an automated repair (merging, reweighting, hierarchy induction) that measurably reduces aggregate-score distortion on a held-out model-comparison task, instead of stopping at diagnosis?

iv.

When the production judge is swapped, how often does the set of flagged coupled pairs change — and is there a cheap transfer heuristic to avoid rerunning the full grid?

v.

Does intervention-based coupling generalize to multi-turn, tool-use, or safety rubrics, where "strongly satisfy / clearly fail Ci in isolation" is genuinely hard to construct? Will self-effect gating flag most criteria as unreliable there?
