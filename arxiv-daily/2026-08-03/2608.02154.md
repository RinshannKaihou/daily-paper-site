> **一句话总结：** 提出 DPA —— 一个针对黑盒、恶意 LLM 微调场景的事后数据 IP 侵权审计框架，通过新设计的 JSLS（联合语义-词汇相似度）和 SIDE（自指令数据抽取）算法在医学与法律任务上以 95.62% TPR / 4.38% FPR 可靠识别数据来源，并在释义、知识蒸馏等规避策略下保持鲁棒。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces Distribution Provenance Audit (DPA), a post-hoc, black-box framework that audits whether a suspect LLM was fine-tuned on a proprietary dataset by capturing intrinsic distributional fingerprints via a Joint Semantic-Lexical Similarity (JSLS) metric and a prompt-agnostic Self-Instruct Data Extraction (SIDE) sampler; on MedAlpaca/ChatDoctor/CaseLaw across Pythia-2.8B, Qwen3-4B, and Llama3-8B, DPA attains 95.62% TPR vs 4.38% FPR and remains robust under subset-split, paraphrasing, and knowledge-distillation evasion tactics where a Reference-based MIA baseline collapses (e.g., member score 0.411 vs non-member 0.115 under paraphrasing).

## 核心贡献

- **首个面向黑盒恶意微调的数据 IP 审计框架**：提出 Distribution Provenance Audit (DPA)，将审计范式从易被规避的记录级 MIA 转向分布级对齐检验，仅依赖推理 API 输出即可工作，无需事先植入水印或要求训练方协作。
- **联合语义-词汇相似度 JSLS**：基于词相关性（频率方差稳定化散度）融合 Jensen-Shannon 词汇散度与加权 KL 散度前沿语义相似度，形成严格的逻辑"AND"门控指标，避免 BLEU 的假阴性（释义误判为独立）和 MAUVE 的假阳性（同域独立数据被误判为同源）。
- **自指令数据抽取 SIDE**：通过领域无关的冷启动提示加上嵌入空间扰动 + vec2text 解码的迭代自精炼，从模型隐空间无偏地重建其潜在分布，相比无关提示将抽取偏差从 0.683 降至 0.054，逼近 oracle ID 提示的 0.046。
- **可证审计的形式化**：将审计建模为密码学"可区分博弈"（Distinguishability Game, Exp_DPA），并通过非参数自助法 95% 下置信界假设检验给出统计严格的判定，校准阈值 ω=0.60 / τ=0.50。
- **透明度-隐私悖论的揭示**：论证并实证了 DPA 与数据抽取攻击、分布推断攻击的结构同构性——同一高保真指纹既能审计也能被武器化（如 PII 抽取 F1 在 MedAlpaca 上达 0.59）。

## Key Contributions

- **First intrinsic-fingerprint audit for black-box malicious fine-tuning**: DPA shifts the audit paradigm from fragile record-level Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs) to distributional alignment, requiring only black-box output access — no retroactive watermarking or trainer cooperation.
- **Joint Semantic-Lexical Similarity (JSLS)**: Combines a variance-stabilized word-relevance score with a JS-divergence lexical term and a relevance-weighted KL-frontier semantic term via a gated product kernel, enforcing a strict semantic-AND-lexical dual constraint that resists both paraphrasing (BLEU's failure) and same-domain decoys (MAUVE's failure).
- **Self-Instruct Data Extraction (SIDE)**: A prompt-agnostic extractor with a generic-prompt cold start and iterative embedding-perturbation refinement (using vec2text inversion plus a surrogate LLM) that recovers the model's latent distribution with deviation δ_ext ≈ 0.054, closely matching the infeasible oracle ID baseline (0.046) and far better than unrelated prompts (0.683).
- **Rigorous game-theoretic formulation**: Casts the audit as a cryptographic distinguishability game (Exp_DPA) with a quantifiable DPA Advantage, operationalized through non-parametric bootstrap 95%-LCB hypothesis testing with calibrated thresholds (ω=0.60 separating ground-truth distributions, τ=0.50 for extracted samples).
- **Dual-use / transparency-paradox analysis**: Empirically demonstrates that the same high-fidelity fingerprints enabling auditing can facilitate data extraction, distribution-inference (mixing-proportion reverse-engineering), and optimal auxiliary-dataset synthesis for stronger MIAs.

## Methodology

DPA is grounded in the observation that a *rational* malicious trainer is bound by a **utility constraint**: even when scrubbing verbatim traces (paraphrasing, distillation), the model must internalize the proprietary distribution's intrinsic statistics — the joint dependency between "what is said" (semantic substance) and "how it is said" (lexical form) — to preserve expert-level utility.

**Threat model.** The Trainer (Challenger) secretly samples b∈{0,1}; if b=1, the model is fine-tuned on the proprietary distribution π_own, possibly with provenance-evasion tactics (T1 Subset Split, T2 Paraphrase, T3 Knowledge Distillation); if b=0, only on a statistically independent decoy π_ind. The Auditor (Data Owner) has only black-box query access and a reference dataset D_ref, with zero access to parameters, gradients, or logits. The Auditor wins if it can distinguish the two cases (DPA Advantage).

**From probability to similarity.** Because LLM-induced distributions are implicit and likelihood is intractable, DPA uses Empirical Likelihood (EL) theory to map the intractable posterior Pr[b=1|M] onto a computable similarity Δ(D_ext, D_ref), justified by the fine-tuning alignment between π_M and π_own.

**JSLS metric (Δ_JSLS).** Defined as a kernel-product:
- **Word relevance** r(w) = √f_D(w) · log(f_D(w)/f_L(w)) — a Poisson-variance-stabilized frequency divergence that suppresses shot noise (rare words) and magnitude dominance (common words).
- **Lexical divergence** γ = JS(N^R_0, N^R_1) between robust Gaussians (median/IQR-estimated) over relevance-score distributions.
- **Semantic similarity** m_r = AUC of KL-divergence frontier over K-Means clusters, but with relevance-weighted cluster probabilities that down-weight generic patterns like "the results show".
- **Fusion** Δ_JSLS = m_r / exp(k·γ), a Laplacian-kernel lexical term gating the semantic term, bounded in [0,1] and symmetric.

**SIDE extraction (G_SIDE).** Two phases: (1) cold start with domain-agnostic generic prompts (blank tokens for Q&A, high-frequency conjunctions for completion); (2) iterative self-instruct refinement — sample a seed from current D_ext, perturb its embedding e' = e + α·δ/‖δ‖, decode back via vec2text plus a surrogate LLM for coherence/format, and use s' to query the model. This avoids prompt-induced bias that would otherwise falsely accuse innocent models.

**Decision rule.** Compute s = Δ_JSLS(G_SIDE(M), D_own); reject H₀ (independent source) only when the 95% bootstrap LCB exceeds τ (≤ω, due to information-bottleneck attenuation of extracted fingerprints).

## Results & Highlights

- **Audit reliability (R2):** Averaged over the stable threshold region, DPA achieves **95.62% TPR and 4.38% FPR** on the MedAlpaca / ChatDoctor / CaseLaw benchmarks across Pythia-2.8B, Qwen3-4B-Instruct, and Llama3-8B-Instruct — correctly attributing infringing models and clearing innocent ones.
- **MIA baseline collapse (Table 1):** The Reference-based MIA fails systematically under adversarial tactics, exhibiting *rank inversion* where member D_HCM scores higher than non-member D_GPT — e.g., 0.411 vs 0.115 under Tactic 2 (paraphrasing), 0.198 vs −0.020 under Tactic 3 (distillation) — yielding false "innocent" verdicts in all four settings.
- **JSLS metric validity (Table 4):** JSLS separates proprietary derivatives (Δ_JSLS > 0.77, e.g., 0.990 for ChatDoctor split, 0.811 for MedAlpaca paraphrase) from independent sources (Δ_JSLS < 0.60, e.g., 0.485 for MedAlpaca flashcards, 0.575 for CaseLaw Arizona), enabling a clean ω=0.60 threshold.
- **SIDE extraction fidelity (Table 8):** SIDE achieves δ_ext ≈ **0.054**, comparable to the infeasible oracle in-distribution prompts (0.046) and dramatically better than empty (0.226), tailored (0.193), OOD (0.074), and unrelated (0.683) prompt baselines.
- **Robustness to evasion (Table 5):** Extraction deviation remains low under all three tactics — δ_ext ≈ 0.040 (no tactic), 0.056 (subset), 0.051 (paraphrase), 0.040 (distillation) — confirming fingerprint persistence.
- **Metric-reality anchoring (Fig. 9):** JSLS correlates strongly with MAUVE similarity (Pearson r=0.83, p=0.011) and inversely with MMD divergence (r=−0.88, p=0.0038); it also tracks PII F1 retention, correctly flagging high-PII CaseLaw paraphrase (F1≈0.84) with a high score of 0.770.
- **Production model validation:** On gpt-4.1-nano fine-tuned with 3K MedAlpaca WikiDoc samples for 5 epochs, proprietary references scored Δ_JSLS > 0.76 (95% CI [0.82, 0.90]) while OOD decoys stayed around 0.42.
- **Dual-use privacy risk (Figs. 13-14, Table 9):** SIDE recovers training text with >30% BLEU improvement over unrelated prompts and extracts PII with F1 up to 0.59 (MedAlpaca); JSLS scores exhibit diagonal dominance enabling mixing-proportion inference (e.g., 0.823 for correct 25% Delaware mixture vs 0.647 for pure Arizona), and SIDE-generated references boost an MIA's AUC to 0.72 vs 0.53 for unrelated prompts.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel positioning — most prior data-IP work (dataset watermarking [Qiu et al. 2025], ZKPs [zkLLM 2024]) is *invasive* and assumes trainer cooperation, which fails in the malicious setting; DPA's reframing of provenance as a cryptographic distinguishability game over *intrinsic* distributional fingerprints is a real conceptual advance over MIAs (whose unsoundness for LLM provenance is documented by Zhang et al. 2025 and Duan et al. 2024, both cited).
- **Rigor:** Strong — formal Exp_DPA game, Empirical-Likelihood proxy justification, robust statistics (median/IQR) for parameter estimation, bootstrap 95%-LCB hypothesis testing, and three evasion tactics tested across three models and three domains. The metric-validity analysis (R1) anchors JSLS to MAUVE/MMD/PII with statistical significance tests.
- **Reproducibility:** Good — public datasets (MedAlpaca, ChatDoctor, CaseLaw), named base models (Pythia-2.8B, Qwen3-4B, Llama3-8B), LoRA hyperparameters (r=16, α=32), 7K-sample subsets, 2K extracted / 2K reference samples, public GitHub repo. However, the SIDE pipeline depends on third-party components (vec2text, Sentence-BERT, a "surrogate LLM" defaulting to Qwen3-4B) whose exact versions are not pinned in the main text.
- **Significance:** High practical relevance for the LLM supply-chain copyright problem, with a thoughtful dual-use analysis that reframes the audit/privacy tension rather than ignoring it.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:**
  - The **ω=0.60 / τ=0.50 thresholds are calibrated on the same three datasets** used for evaluation (Table 4 → Section 7.2.3), creating a circularity risk; no held-out domain validates generalizability of the threshold constants.
  - **The decoys are weak by construction**: the paper's own diagnosis (Section 2.2) attributes MIA failure to the "inherent structural simplicity" of synthetic D_GPT, but DPA's same-domain decoys (Arizona vs Delaware, flashcards vs wikidoc) are arguably also distributionally separable — a truly adversarial trainer using a closer decoy (e.g., a different medical-corpus mix) is not tested.
  - The **ChatDoctor δ_ext values are markedly worse** (0.087 mean, 0.139 for ID trn on Pythia) than MedAlpaca (0.016) or CaseLaw (0.027), suggesting SIDE fidelity is dataset-sensitive; the headline 95.62% TPR averages over an unstable region whose bounds are not reported per-dataset.
  - **Comparison with baselines is asymmetric**: MIA is reported as point-estimate scores (Table 1) while DPA is reported with bootstrap CIs; an apples-to-apples comparison of *audit decisions* (with statistical confidence) for the MIA baseline is missing.
  - The **distillation threat (T3)** assumes the auditor compares against the teacher's training data D_base, which presupposes the auditor already knows the distillation pipeline occurred — a non-trivial assumption under the stated black-box model.

## Strengths

- Conceptually clean paradigm shift from record-level to distribution-level auditing, with a well-motivated utility-constraint argument for why fingerprints persist.
- JSLS's gated product kernel elegantly combines the failure modes of BLEU (false negatives) and MAUVE (false positives) into a single bounded metric with theoretical grounding in the kernel trick.
- SIDE is a principled solution to prompt-induced bias, and its near-oracle performance is convincingly demonstrated.
- Honest and substantive treatment of the dual-use tension, with concrete empirical demonstrations of how the same machinery enables PII extraction, distribution inference, and stronger MIAs.
- Public code, diverse model families, and rigorous statistical hypothesis testing rather than point estimates.

## Limitations

- Threshold calibration lacks held-out validation; ω=0.60 may not generalize to other domain pairs.
- DPA assumes measurable distributional differences between proprietary and independent datasets — the authors concede that when these are statistically indistinguishable or when the proprietary fraction is heavily diluted into background data, provenance evidence weakens or becomes non-identifiable.
- Evaluation is limited to two domains (medical, legal) and three English datasets; cross-lingual, multi-modal, and heavily mixed-distribution settings are left to future work.
- The threat model's T3 (distillation) case requires the auditor to know the teacher's training data, which slightly contradicts the strict black-box assumption.
- SIDE's reliance on embedding-inversion models (vec2text) and a surrogate LLM introduces pipeline complexity and potential version sensitivity not fully analyzed.
- The privacy-attack demonstrations are illustrative rather than benchmarked against state-of-the-art attack methods, so the magnitude of dual-use risk may be under- or over-stated.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does DPA perform when the malicious trainer uses a *close* decoy distribution (e.g., a different but same-subdomain medical corpus) rather than the easily-separable synthetic GPT or out-of-state legal data tested here?
- Can the ω=0.60 / τ=0.50 thresholds be validated on held-out domains (e.g., financial, code) without recalibration, or is per-domain calibration required?
- How sensitive is SIDE to the choice and version of the embedding-inversion model (vec2text) and surrogate LLM, and can a single-step decoder replace the two-stage inversion+surrogate pipeline?
- Does differential privacy (DP-SGD) fine-tuning, which the appendix claims DPA resists, actually erase JSLS-detectable fingerprints at realistic ε budgets, or does it merely shift the τ threshold?
- Could the same JSLS+SIDE machinery be inverted into a *defense* — e.g., a fingerprint-erasing fine-tuning procedure that minimizes Δ_JSLS while preserving utility, and would that close the dual-use loop?
- How does DPA extend to multi-modal fine-tuning (vision-language models) where the "lexical form" axis has no direct analogue?
