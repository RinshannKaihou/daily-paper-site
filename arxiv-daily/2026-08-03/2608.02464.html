Methodology

one-class ESN · CUSUM · deterministic layer · repair

An agent episode is a step sequence t = 1…T, each emitting a telemetry vector built from three causal channels: a semantic embedding (deterministic char-3-gram feature hash — no encoder to download), token-uncertainty aggregates, and action metadata. Telemetry evolves across four versions (v1 43-dim → v4 +g content-grounding).

Per channel, a sparse random recurrent reservoir is fixed at init and never trained; only a ridge readout is fitted in closed form on healthy runs. Per-step surprise is the normalized one-step prediction error averaged over K reservoirs; a one-sided CUSUM integrates small persistent shifts so slow drift is still caught. The alarm reads the loudest channel.

Three deterministic checks — total_consistency, required_coverage, tool_contract — need no null, threshold, or calibration, covering classes a one-class monitor cannot structurally score. Flagged episodes roll back to the last fact-gathering step and replay live under each repair rung.

Strengths & Limitations

side-by-side

Strengths

  • Sub-ms, label-free, no-second-LLM monitoring that is causal-by-construction — every quantity at step t depends only on x₁…x_t and fit-time constants.
  • The deterministic verification layer elegantly covers classes (silent aborts, plausible-value corruption, fabrication) that statistical monitors cannot structurally score — needs no null, threshold, or calibration.
  • Intellectual honesty throughout: negative results kept, pre-registration reported as underpowered, the simulator's escalation claim explicitly corrected against a measured judge.
  • Best-in-class artifact availability: SHA-256 manifests, behavior-snapshot tests, pinned container, three public artifacts (GitHub, HuggingFace, demo Space).
  • The "naming the failing check beats handing over the answer" repair finding is counterintuitive and well-isolated: 26 of 55 specific hints contain the correct total, yet value-free located recovers at least as much.

Limitations

  • No cross-deployment transfer. Healthy null must match the exact serving distribution; cross- and within-family transfer both land at chance (AUROC 0.527 vs 0.885 recalibrated).
  • Slow goal drift evades every per-step-surprise monitor tested; only trajectory self-consistency sees it, weakly. The limit is the rate of change, not the class.
  • Content coverage bounded by telemetry completeness: results not passed through the channel are invisible; terse results carry too little mass.
  • Plausible-value corruption (a wrong-but-well-formed number) is undetectable from telemetry by construction and needs an external reference.
  • Fabrication claims limited by base rate: only 2 fabricated inputs in organic data vs a pre-registered minimum of 10 — no detection claim either way.
  • Narrow scope: mock-tool and research-loop tasks; two local model families, one commercial API; wall-clock latency features excluded as machine-specific.

Questions for Further Exploration

open edges
  1. Can the per-deployment healthy-null requirement be relaxed via meta-learning across model families, or is chance-level cross-family transfer (AUROC 0.527) a fundamental property of one-class calibration rather than a data-quantity problem?
  2. How does the deterministic tool_contract check generalize to tools whose return shapes are themselves free-form natural language (open-ended search, code execution), where "any shape the tool can return" is ill-defined?
  3. The located rung beats handing the agent the correct answer — does this generalize to multi-step / compositional tasks where the failing-check name is ambiguous, or is it specific to single-numeric-total tasks?
  4. Could the content-grounding g channel's +0.31 gain replicate on retrieval corruption that does not produce a cleanly separable lexical signal (topically correct but subtly wrong documents)?
  5. What is the adversarial robustness of the ESN+CUSUM monitor against an attacker who shapes tool outputs to stay within the healthy-null distribution? (A tamper_check ships, but headline numbers are non-adversarial.)
  6. The measured gemini-2.5-flash judge (p_detect 0.548) is far weaker than the stipulated 0.90 — how does the monitor+judge stack behave with stronger open-weight judges, and does "fail on different classes" complementarity still hold?
