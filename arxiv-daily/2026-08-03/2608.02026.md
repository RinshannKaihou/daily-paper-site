> **一句话总结：** HPFA 通过对比同一问题的成功/失败轨迹并构建依赖超图，将反事实搜索聚焦于真实分歧点，在 MATH500/GSM8K/KodCode/MBPP/CoQA 五个基准的根因定位成功率上相较 AgentDebug 和 AgenTracer 基线提升 17%–30%+，并用所合成数据 SFT+GRPO 训练的轻量 attributor 在 MATH500 上将推理准确率从 79.4% 提升至 84.8%。

> **TL;DR:** HPFA pairs a successful and a failed rollout for each query, builds a dependency hypergraph over aligned atomic steps, and walks backward to localize the earliest recoverable divergence, reducing counterfactual search cost; on MATH500 the full method raises rollout-verified localization success to 64.6% (vs. 30.9% for AgentDebug and 27.3% for AgenTracer) and yields a +5.4 point accuracy gain (79.4% -> 84.8%) for a trained attributor, with hypergraph gains scaling with trajectory length (e.g., +18.6 points on 18.2-step KodCode traces, -4.5 on 8.2-step GSM8K).

## 核心贡献

- **高效归因数据合成框架 HPFA**：首次将"成对轨迹（成功 vs 失败）+ 依赖超图"结合，把反事实搜索复杂度从 O(轨迹长度) 降为 O(1/p)（p 为单次采样命中成功轨迹的概率），并通过超图沿非线性依赖回溯到上游根因。
- **形式化的根因定义与算法**：给出"最早可修复步"的严格定义（k = min{j : ∃s′_j 使得替换后轨迹成功），并配套 Algorithm 1 的迭代回溯定位算法。
- **两阶段 attributor 训练（SFT+GRPO）**：用 HPFA 合成的 (失败轨迹, 根因步, 修复片段) 三元组做 SFT 模仿学习，再用 GRPO 以"格式奖励 + 反事实 rollout 成功奖励"在线优化，使 attributor 在测试时无需成功参考路径即可定位。
- **跨任务、跨模型的一致性验证**：在数学推理（MATH500、GSM8K）、智能体编程（KodCode、MBPP）及隐私场景（CoQA 去识别）五个基准上系统对比 AgentDebug 和 AgenTracer，并跨 Qwen3-8B/14B、Llama-3.1-8B、Gemma3-4B 验证泛化性。
- **超图构建可靠性量化**：附录 A 用 200 题的跨模型 micro-F1（76.3%–92.2%）和 50 题人工评审（88% 通过率）证明超图构建稳定，并指出语义对齐是主要不确定源。

## Key Contributions

- An **HPFA pipeline** that combines paired success/failure trajectories with a dependency hypergraph, cutting counterfactual search from per-step probing to O(1/p) where p is the single-shot success probability, and walking back along non-linear hyperedges to find upstream root causes.
- A **formal "earliest recoverable step" definition** of root cause (k = min{j : ∃s′_j s.t. 1(R(T_fail, j, s′_j)) = 1}) plus Algorithm 1, an iterative meet-on-T+ backward walk with counterfactual verification.
- A **two-stage attributor training recipe (SFT + GRPO)** whose sequence-level reward combines a format term (parsable diagnosis template) with a rollout term (does the diagnosis enable a correct re-rollout), letting the trained attributor localize errors at test time without any successful reference path.
- **Broad empirical validation** across MATH500, GSM8K, KodCode, MBPP, and a privacy-preserving CoQA slice, plus a cross-backbone study (Qwen3-8B/14B, Gemma3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B) and an out-of-domain transfer test on AIME 2024 and LogiQA 2.0.
- A **hypergraph reliability audit** (Appendix A) reporting per-stage micro-F1 of 76.3%–92.2% against Claude Opus 4.7 / Gemini 3.5 Flash / GPT-5.5 and an 88% strict human pass rate, with semantic-equivalence merging identified as the dominant uncertainty source.

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** A trajectory T = (s_1, …, s_n, y) is correct iff 1(T) = 1. For a failed rollout, the root cause is the earliest step k such that substituting a repaired span s′_k and re-sampling the downstream yields a correct trajectory. This earliest-recoverable convention treats later repairable steps as consequences rather than independent causes.

**Stage 1 — Paired trajectory generation.** For each query, the target LLM/agent is rolled out under a fixed protocol (cap K = 8) until both a success T+ and a failure T- appear; if only one outcome appears the query is dropped, and when several pairs qualify the closest-length pair is kept. This reduces search complexity to 1/p instead of the number of reasoning steps.

**Stage 2 — Dependency hypergraph construction.** Each trace is segmented into atomic steps (one arithmetic move, tool call, or subgoal). Semantic similarity/clustering induces an equivalence ~ over the union of steps so that V = {[s]~}; each trace's prerequisites {s_i}_{i∈P(j)} are encoded as hyperedges, yielding G = (V, E_h). The hypergraph is built by an LLM (Qwen3-8B for math/coding, GPT-4o for privacy) using the template in Appendix D.

**Stage 3 — Iterative root-cause localization (Algorithm 1).** Starting from the success trajectory's output vertex v+_out, walk backward along T+'s dependencies until the first vertex v⋆ that also appears on T- (the "meeting vertex"). Successors of v⋆ on T- that are unaligned with T+ form the candidate set C. Each candidate is counterfactually tested via R(T-, idx(v), s′); a verifying candidate is returned as the root. Otherwise re-anchor at v⋆ and repeat, terminating on repair success, shared-prefix exhaustion, or fallback to the earliest unresolved divergence.

**Attributor training (Section 3.2).** A lightweight model π_φ maps (Q, T_fail) to a structured diagnosis (error step, error type, mistaken content). (1) SFT uses causal LM loss on templated diagnoses harvested from HPFA-verified roots. (2) GRPO (group size 4, 4 groups/batch, lr 2e-6) refines on on-policy failures from the target model, with reward R_i = R^fmt_i + λ R^rollout_i where R^rollout_i = 1(T̃_i) tests whether the diagnosis enables a correct re-rollout. SFT runs 5 epochs, batch 16, AdamW, lr 5e-5, on 4× A100-80GB.

**Test-time reasoning (Algorithm 2).** A single backbone rollout is scored by verbal confidence S(T); if S(T) < τ, the attributor emits a diagnosis and the backbone regenerates the trajectory, iterating up to B repair rounds.

## Results & Highlights

- **Localization success rate (Table 2, 5th refinement step):** HPFA reaches 64.6% on MATH500, 76.9% on GSM8K, 53.7% on CoQA (privacy), 53.4% on KodCode, and 23.0% on MBPP — vs. the stronger of AgentDebug/AgenTracer on each dataset, gains exceed 30 points on MATH500/GSM8K/CoQA/KodCode and 17.2 points on MBPP (e.g., MATH500: 64.6% vs. AgentDebug 30.9% and AgenTracer 27.3%).
- **Hypergraph ablation (Table 3):** removing the graph costs 9.7 points on MATH500 (12.4-step traces), 11.1 on MBPP (14.0 steps), and 18.6 on KodCode (18.2 steps), while GSM8K (only 8.2 steps) actually loses 4.5 points with the graph — establishing that hypergraph value scales with trajectory length.
- **Attributor-driven accuracy (Table 4):** Ours (SFT+GRPO) lifts MATH500 from 79.4% (vanilla) to 84.8%, beating Self-reflection (81.8%) and AgenTracer (82.4%); on KodCode it reaches 46.3% vs. 43.9% vanilla; on MBPP 48.4% vs. 46.5%.
- **Cross-backbone consistency (Table 6):** the same attributor checkpoint raises MATH500 on Qwen3-8B (79.4%→84.8%), Qwen3-14B (81.0%→85.0), and Llama-3.1-8B (46.8%→52.4), but gives essentially no gain on Gemma3-4B (~20–21%) because the base model cannot execute the repaired reasoning.
- **Out-of-domain transfer (Table 5):** an attributor trained only on math achieves correction success 19.3% on MATH500, 14.3% on GSM8K, 22.7% on AIME 2024 (Pass@16), and 6.6% on LogiQA 2.0 — vs. Self-reflection's 11.9 / 7.1 / 9.1 / 1.2, including a 5.5× relative gain on the non-math LogiQA benchmark.
- **Statistical significance (Table 9, McNemar test on 500 MATH500 problems):** net rescues are 12 (p=0.140, n.s.) for Self-reflection, 24 (p=0.015) for Ours-SFT, and 27 (p=0.010) for Ours-SFT+GRPO; the +5.4-point SFT+GRPO gain over vanilla exactly matches 100·δ/500.
- **Cost–accuracy trade-off (Figure 3):** at comparable wall-clock time, HPFA attains higher localization success than both baselines, with accuracy stabilizing from refinement iteration 5 onward across 16 rounds (Figure 2).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine incremental novelty — AgenTracer (Zhang et al., 2025a) already introduced counterfactual-replay-trained attributors and Who&When established the multi-agent attribution framing; HPFA's deltas are (a) paired-trajectory divergence search and (b) the dependency hypergraph. The hypergraph is essentially a learned PRM-style dependency structure, and its measured benefit is uneven (negative on GSM8K), so the core contribution rests more on the combination than on any single component.
- **Rigor:** Reasonable. Localization uses a single shared oracle 1(·) across all methods; the McNemar test (Table 9) and the cost-vs-accuracy curves (Figure 3) add statistical and efficiency depth that attribution papers often skip. However, the attributor-accuracy gains on the saturated GSM8K (92.6→93.0) are within noise, and the paper itself flags this.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong on paper (public code, named GPUs, all hyperparameters, prompt templates in Appendix D) but several undisclosed details matter: the verbal-confidence threshold τ and repair budget B in Algorithm 2 are not numerically specified, the LLM-as-judge used for cross-model hypergraph agreement is not pinned to a model version, and the per-dataset repair-span source s′ used in counterfactual rollouts is under-specified.
- **Significance:** Practically meaningful for long-horizon agentic traces (the 18.6-point KodCode gain and AIME/LogiQA transfer are the strongest results) but modest for short math traces where paired rollout alone already captures most of the gain.
- **Red flags / data quirks:** (1) The privacy "CoQA" slice contains only 108 retained cases after filtering, a small sample whose 53.7% vs. 22.2% AgenTracer delta should be read with caution. (2) The hypergraph is built by the same Qwen3-8B that generates trajectories, so builder errors and reasoner errors are not independent; the 88% human pass rate is on only 50 graphs. (3) Counterfactual "repair spans" s′ are LLM-generated rather than ground-truth edits, so localization success conflates root-cause identification with the model's ability to produce a correct repair — a confound shared with baselines but not dissected. (4) Gemma3-4B's null result (20→21%) is presented as a base-capability ceiling rather than investigated.

## Strengths

- Directly attacks the cost bottleneck of step-wise counterfactual attribution; the O(1/p) paired-rollout reduction is simple and well-motivated by test-time-scaling literature.
- The trajectory-length-vs-hypergraph-gain analysis (Table 3) is an unusually honest characterization of when the method helps and when it hurts.
- Two-stage SFT+GRPO with a rollout-grounded reward is a clean, actionable recipe that other attribution/prm works could adopt.
- Cross-dataset and cross-backbone evaluation, plus an out-of-domain AIME/LogiQA transfer test, go beyond the single-benchmark claims common in this area.
- Public code and explicit disclosure of LLM use in writing.

## Limitations

- Hypergraph benefit is negative on short trajectories (GSM8K -4.5 points), so the method is not uniformly dominant.
- Privacy benchmark is tiny (108 cases) and uses GPT-4o for all LLM components, limiting statistical and model coverage claims.
- Hypergraph construction depends on a single builder LLM (Qwen3-8B) whose errors are correlated with the reasoner's; cross-model agreement is stability, not correctness.
- The attributor provides no gain on Gemma3-4B, suggesting attribution is only useful when the backbone can execute repaired reasoning — a boundary condition the paper notes but does not deeply analyze.
- Algorithm 2 hyperparameters (τ, B) and the LLM judge used for agreement scoring are not numerically reported.
- Only a single counterfactual repair span s′ is tested per candidate step; the method's sensitivity to repair-span quality is not ablated.
- Localization success conflates root-cause identification with repair-span adequacy because s′ is model-generated, not a ground-truth edit.

## Relevance

对研究 LLM 反思式推理与过程级奖励/归因的工作高度相关：HPFA 提供了一种结构化、可扩展的根因标注管线，可作为 PRM、self-reflection 与 agent 调试的数据合成基线，对 agentic coding 与数学推理的失败诊断与修复尤其有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does decoupling the hypergraph builder from the reasoner (e.g., builder = Claude Opus, reasoner = Qwen3-8B) meaningfully change localization accuracy, given the current shared-LLM correlation?
- What is HPFA's sensitivity to the verbal-confidence threshold τ and repair budget B in Algorithm 2, and were these tuned on the test set?
- Can the conflation between root-cause identification and repair-span quality be separated by using oracle (ground-truth) repair spans s′ in the counterfactual test?
- Why does the hypergraph hurt on 8.2-step GSM8K — is it the small candidate set, the failure mode distribution, or noisy semantic-equivalence merging, and could an adaptive "graph-only-when-long" policy recover the loss?
- How does HPFA scale to truly long-horizon agent tasks (hundreds of steps, multi-tool) where the 1/p paired-rollout assumption may break down?
- Can the rollout-grounded GRPO reward be combined with process-reward-model pretraining to further improve attribution calibration?
