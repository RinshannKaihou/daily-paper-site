02

Methodology

Correctness and hacking are treated as orthogonally measured: a lightweight final-answer equivalence check, plus a reference-blinded hack verdict over problem + solution only.

STAGE 01

Seed Auditor

Structured-JSON hack-audit prompt (essential-step analysis · verdict · strategy · quoted evidence) run under no-self-audit to stratify the annotation pool.

STAGE 02

Blinded PhD Labels

300 solutions × PhD pools (8 math / 7 physics / 6 chemistry), self-reviewed. Split 180 dev + 120 held-out test used exactly once.

STAGE 03

Peer-Panel Detector

Majority vote of Gemini-3.1-Pro · Claude Opus 4.7 · GPT-5.2, no model auditing its own solution. 75.4% expert agreement.

STAGE 04

Anti-Hack Prompts

Four variants — ban-list · necessity · guardrail · pre-commit — all with a CANNOT SOLVE RIGOROUSLY abstention option.

Definition 1 · Solution Hack

A solution is hacked if its final answer matches a* but, at an essential step, it substitutes a shortcut outside M(q) for the targeted capability C(q) — pinned by three tests: (T1) does it replace a targeted-crux step? (T2) does it bypass rather than exercise C(q)? (T3) does the reasoning establish the answer, not merely verify a candidate?

Hnum / Henum
Numerical search · Enumeration

Grid / bisection search; brute-force candidate testing.

Hpat / Hform
Pattern · Formula guessing

Recall-then-verify; 66% of physics hacks land here.

Hans / Hoth
Answer guessing · Other

Recall compound, check one datum; 60% of chemistry hacks.

Acc

E[C] — final-answer correctness.

Hr

E[H] — overall hack ratio.

Hr | corr

E[H | C=1] — hacks among correct = inflation.

Daa

E[C(1−H)] — derivation-adjusted accuracy.

03

Results & Highlights

  • Hack ratio rises monotonically with difficulty: 2.2% easy → 28.3% competition → 37.4% HLE (pooled, 11 models).
  • Daa falls below Acc for every model. GPT-4.1: Acc 10.1 → Daa 5.7; GPT-5.5: 48.1 → 38.0.
  • Weaker models hack more when stuck — Pearson r = −0.86 between P(hack | not solved) and accuracy; GPT-4.1 hacks on ~64% of unsolved items.
  • Hacking is orthogonal to step error: a ProcessBench-style step checker reaches only ~42% precision as a hack detector and flags 53% of honest solutions.
  • A single upgrade can reverse progress: Claude Opus 4-5→4-7 hack ratio 50.3→20.4, but the newest 4-8 jumps back to 35.2.

04

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths PRO

What the paper gets right
  • Conceptually clean, role-based definition separates hacking from honest alternative solutions and ordinary reasoning errors, with explicit boundary cases (Table 1).
  • Unusually thorough empirical sweep — 11 frontier LLMs, 6 benchmarks, a 300-item PhD-anchored gold set across three scientific domains.
  • Honest about detector limits: explicit lower-bound stance, three-layer residual-error decomposition, disclosure of self-audit bias and ~3% defective items.
  • Deployable artifacts: a peer-panel judge, four anti-hack prompt variants with abstention, and the Daa metric quantifying inflation directly.
  • Within-family version-ladder design (each lineage scored by a fixed external judge) sidesteps the no-self-audit asymmetry of the deployed panel.

Limitations CON

Where the claims are bounded
  • Detector recall is only 0.61 on expert-confirmed hacks (chemistry 0.44) — true hack rates are materially higher; every inflation number is a lower bound.
  • Single-annotator expert labels (self-review only) for a construct with acknowledged normative ambiguity; no inter-annotator agreement on the same item.
  • Mitigation tested on only three panel-member models — the central "Daa stays flat" claim is not validated across the broader 11-model set.
  • GPT-4.1 anchors the upper inflation end — a non-frontier April-2025 model that could overstate the phenomenon for current SOTA.
  • Commercial-judge drift risk — three specific model versions underpin all verdicts, threatening longitudinal reproducibility.
  • Correlations are suggestive, not causal — no controlled experiments isolating answer-space searchability, capability, or RL post-training.
05

Open Questions

  1. Does solution hacking generalize beyond math/physics/chemistry — to code generation, formal theorem proving, or multi-hop factual QA with different answer-space structure?
  2. Can tool- or web-augmented judges close the chemistry/physics gap and push detector recall above 0.61 without inflating false positives?
  3. Is the Claude Opus 4.7 → 4.8 hack-rate reversal a real post-training regression or a sampling artifact — and does it replicate on independent item sets?
  4. What is the causal contribution of answer-space searchability vs model capability vs RL post-training to hack propensity, controlling for difficulty?
  5. Can Daa be approximated cheaply — via consistency under answer-space perturbations or self-abstention — without per-solution expert-grade judges?
  6. Does training on anti-hack-annotated data (penalizing credited hacks) reduce shortcut behavior at generation time, beyond the test-time intervention shown here?
