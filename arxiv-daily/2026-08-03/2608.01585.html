01 · METHODOLOGY

From embeddings to a homogeneity test

Algorithm (Table 3)

  1. Embed. Collect embeddings for a fixed input set from two models / layers / checkpoints / languages. Archetype words break, cut, run, play in WordNet sentences; drop subtokenized words.
  2. Topological repr. Subsample → persistence diagrams → rotate/rescale → landscapes (Bubenik 2015) or silhouettes (Chazal 2014). Stable, homomorphism-invariant, dimension-agnostic.
  3. Energy statistic T between the two curve sets (Szekely & Rizzo 2004; You 2022).
  4. Permutation test for the p-value.
  5. Benjamini–Yekutieli correction across comparisons.
p = (1 + #{b : T ≤ T(b)}) / (B + 1)

02 · RESULTS

What the topology actually saw

Finding 01 · Discrimination

Energy statistic stays significant where curve distances fail.

On the LoRA sweep, energy on landscapes/silhouettes holds significance (★) at α/r 0.25–0.5; Wasserstein & JSD only flag collapse at α/r ≥ 1.

Finding 02 · Collapse trajectory

Embeddings drift monotonically toward the equidistant baseline.

As LoRA aggression rises, adapted embeddings move toward equidistant (down, Fig. 3) and away from base Llama-3.2-1B (up, Fig. 4) — sample-by-sample trackable semantic degradation.

Coverage: 358 words (OLMo) · 284 (Llama) after dropping subtokenized items.
Finding 03 · Layer dynamics

Early layers shift sharply; deep layers sharpen away from equidistant.

On OLMo-2-0425-1B, deeper layers become progressively more dissimilar to equidistant embeddings (Fig. 6) — semantic structure intensifies toward the output.

Finding 04 · Checkpoint convergence

Three "ingredient" curves converge as LR → 0.

Stage-2 mid-training shows dissimilar structures while learning rate is high; all three ingredient models converge once LR decays to zero — matching Ai2's "model souping" recipe.

Finding 05 · Cross-lingual ranking (WMT14, 200 pairs × 3 lang pairs × 8 models)

Embedding models usually beat generative ones — but the winner is task-specific.

Energy-statistic p-values flag significant differences (★) for essentially every model-vs-model comparison, while Wasserstein/JS on landscapes frequently fail to reach significance (ns) — recapitulating the LoRA finding at scale.

03 · TRADE-OFFS

What it does well · what it doesn't prove

＋ Strengths

  • One framework, five comparisons. Unifies model-vs-baseline, model-vs-model, layer, checkpoint, and language comparisons.
  • Dimension-agnostic. Invariant to homomorphic transformations — compares spaces across model families with no re-alignment.
  • Genuinely more sensitive than Wasserstein/JSD in the controlled LoRA collapse regime.
  • Strong reproducibility. Open-source persiscope, all Hugging Face models/checkpoints public.
  • Honest framing as complementary to outcome benchmarks, not a replacement.

－ Limitations

  • Method novelty lives in the companion paper (2607.29008); this is application-focused.
  • Self-induced collapse only. Non-subtokenized words (358/284 samples); no real-world misalignment detected in the wild.
  • Contaminated benchmark. WMT14 is likely ingested by newer models, weakening cross-lingual claims.
  • No downstream correlation. No BLEU/COMET/BLEURT to confirm "better alignment = better translation."
  • No power analysis or sensitivity sweeps over bootstrap count n, permutation B, subsample size, archetype choice.
  • Figure-star results rather than tabulated effect sizes — hard to compare precisely across models.
04 · OPEN QUESTIONS

Where this could go next

Q1
Does embedding-alignment energy correlate with BLEU / COMET / BLEURT across more language pairs and model scales? Validates "better alignment" against actual translation quality.
Q2
Can the test detect naturally occurring misalignment from real RLHF or fine-tuning — not just self-induced LoRA collapse? Moves the tool from illustration to actionable safety evaluation.
Q3
How sensitive are results to n, B, subsample size, and archetype choice — and is there a principled setting? Closes the missing-ablations gap head-on.
Q4
Does the "sharp early, smooth late" layer pattern generalize to non-transformer or MoE architectures and different data mixes? Tests whether the structural story is transformer-specific.
Q5
Can the framework act as an early-warning signal during training, predicting benchmark saturation or safety regression before outcome metrics show it? The highest-leverage application: structural canary before behavioral fallout.
