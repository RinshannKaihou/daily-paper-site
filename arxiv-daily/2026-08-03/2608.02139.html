§ 01

Methodology — a two-stage closed loop

STAGE I · EXPLICIT

Experience Evolution + OPSD

The policy samples G responses per problem; an extractor turns success (reinforce) and failure (avoid) trajectories into candidate items. An operator Φ merges overlaps and abstracts instance cues.

w(e) = R(q, Ê ∪ {e}) − R(q, Ê)

Items survive only if marginal utility w(e) > ε on a held-out probe set — a two-stage filter that fights post-hoc rationalization.

The refined pool is internalized via Privileged On-Policy Self-Distillation: teacher sees (q, e, y<t), student sees only q; teacher is detached, student minimizes reverse-KL.

STAGE II · IMPLICIT

GRPO from the distilled init

Standard GRPO is applied to the distilled policy. For each q, G responses are sampled; the group-relative advantage is computed:

A_i = (r_i − μ_G) / (σ_G + δ)

Because distillation has moved the policy toward higher-reward regions, more groups have mixed rewards — yielding non-zero advantages and informative gradients. This is the mechanism by which pre-internalization improves RL exploration efficiency.

Note: authors acknowledge DAPO or other GRPO variants could replace standard GRPO here.

§ 03

Strengths & Limitations

+

Strengths

  • Principled unification of test-time experience use and training-time RL through an explicit intermediate "experience distillation" stage, with a coherent information-theoretic justification.
  • Marginal-utility filtering on a held-out probe set is a concrete, reusable mechanism against post-hoc rationalization — the known failure mode of trajectory distillation.
  • Consistent gains across three model scales and five benchmarks, with ablations isolating both Stage II and the shared pool.
  • ~28% data savings vs GRPO and a rollout-diversity analysis (entropy preserved, fewer all-incorrect groups) explaining why pre-internalization helps RL.
  • Public code and standard benchmarks (AIME24/25, GSM8K, MATH500, Minerva Math) for reproducibility.
−

Limitations

  • Math-only. No results on code, agentic tasks, or sparse/noisy rewards where experience extraction is hardest.
  • Single evolution round in headline experiments; the multi-round study reports only sampling accuracy, not final-eval — "progressive" is under-evidenced at the top level.
  • Metric mixing (pass@16 for AIME, pass@1 elsewhere) complicates cross-benchmark comparison of the Δ column.
  • No DAPO head-to-head, despite training on DAPO-math-17k and acknowledging DAPO could outperform standard GRPO in Stage II.
  • No failure analysis of pool drift or misleading evolved experience; the qualitative case study is a single cherry-picked success.
  • Hidden cost: probe-set validation and the model-implemented Φ operator are not accounted for in the data-efficiency claim.
§ 04

Open questions

Q1 · NOISY REWARDS How does SPEE behave on non-binary or noisy rewards (partial test pass, delayed agentic rewards), where the success/failure split and marginal-utility filter become ambiguous?
Q2 · POOL DRIFT Does the evolving pool suffer drift or confirmation bias over many rounds, and can the marginal-utility filter prune stale or misleading items at scale?
Q3 · HONEST COST What is the wall-clock and token-cost comparison (including probe-set validation and Φ) vs vanilla GRPO and DAPO — does the ~28% saving survive an honest total-cost accounting?
Q4 · DAPO IN STAGE II Would replacing standard GRPO with DAPO-style training narrow or widen SPEE's margin, given the authors trained on DAPO-math-17k?
Q5 · CROSS-MODEL TEACHER Can OPSD be extended to a stronger external teacher providing privileged experience, without reintroducing the post-hoc rationalization the pool was designed to remove?
Q6 · GENERALIZATION Beyond math, does the same "evolve → internalize → RL" structure help on tool-use, code, and multi-step agent tasks where verifiers are weaker?
