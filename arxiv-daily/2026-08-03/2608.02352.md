> **一句话总结：** Qwen-CUA 是基于 397B-A17B MoE 的纯原生计算机使用智能体（仅看截图、只发键鼠事件），通过 10 万 vCPU 级云 rollout、约 4 万可验证任务、SAPO 强化学习与迭代训练，在 8 个计算机使用基准上全面超过 Qwen3.7（OSWorld-Verified 86.2 vs 73.3），并与 GPT-5.5、Claude Opus 4.8 竞争，同时把 RedTeamCUA 攻击成功率从 36.6 降到 16.4。

> **TL;DR:** Qwen-CUA is a screenshot-only, native keyboard-and-mouse computer-use agent built on a 397B-A17B mixture-of-experts Qwen backbone, trained with ~100k vCPUs of cloud rollout capacity, ~40k verifiable tasks, and Soft Adaptive Policy Optimization (SAPO) RL over full trajectories. Across 8 computer-use benchmarks it beats Qwen3.7 everywhere — reaching 86.2 vs 73.3 on OSWorld-Verified (and 18.5/48.4 vs 2.5/22.5 binary/partial on OSWorld 2.0) — while staying competitive with GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8, and it cuts indirect prompt-injection attack success on RedTeamCUA from 36.6 to 16.4; scaling the recipe to >1T params (Qwen-CUA-Max) lifts OSWorld-Verified to 87.6 and OSWorld 2.0 to 21.2/53.3.

## 核心贡献

- **纯原生计算机使用接口**：智能体仅以截图为观察、以键鼠事件为动作，不使用 DOM、可访问性树、shell 或任务专属 API，使其接口与人类操作桌面电脑一致，可跨浏览器/桌面/专业软件无缝迁移。
- **长程视觉上下文管理**：将活跃截图历史扩到 20 张，并对更早的截图按"每 10 步折叠一次"的方式压缩为文本占位符，既保留近期视觉证据又使 prompt 前缀在 21–30 步保持稳定，显著提升 KV-cache 复用率。
- **大规模可验证交互基础设施**：基于阿里云 ECS 构建近 10 万 vCPU、可同时承载数万个并发桌面环境的 rollout 集群，使 fresh trajectory 能在多样软件环境中持续生成。
- **约 4 万可验证任务 + 个性化人类轨迹**：合成可控 web mock 环境、环境操作任务、模拟用户交互任务、以及通过"阶段状态链"构造的长程工作流；同时在个性化桌面与专业软件（CAD、Blender 等）中采集人类长轨迹并用模型辅助补全逐步推理。
- **SAPO + 轨迹切片的强化学习**：用 Soft Adaptive Policy Optimization 处理长多模态轨迹，对负向优势采用更快衰减的不对称温度（τpos=1.0, τneg=1.05）；终端奖励通过折叠切片复用于多段上下文有界优化单元。
- **8 基准全面评估 + 缩放验证**：在 OSWorld-Verified、OSWorld 2.0、MyPCBench、MacAgentBench、Gym-Anything、ScienceBoard、WebArena、RedTeamCUA 上系统评测，并将同一配方缩放到 >1T 参数的 Qwen-CUA-Max 取得一致增益。

## Key Contributions

- A screenshot-only native computer-use interface: Qwen-CUA observes only screenshots and emits keyboard/mouse events, deliberately avoiding DOM trees, accessibility metadata, shell access, or task-specific APIs, so the interface is identical to how a person drives a desktop and transfers unchanged across browsers, desktop apps, and professional software.
- A long-horizon visual context manager that expands active history to 20 screenshots and folds older screenshots in blocks of 10 into text placeholders, bounding visual-token growth while keeping the prompt prefix stable across steps 21–30 for improved KV-cache reuse.
- A scaled rollout infrastructure on Alibaba Cloud ECS with access to nearly 100,000 vCPUs and tens of thousands of concurrent isolated desktop environments, decoupling environment execution from GPU-side training.
- Approximately 40,000 verifiable tasks constructed along three axes — environment-operation tasks, simulated-user clarification tasks, and long-horizon workflows built via verifiable phase-state chaining — complemented by human trajectories from personalized desktops and professional software (CAD, Blender), augmented with model-assisted step-level reasoning.
- A computer-use RL recipe combining RLVR with Soft Adaptive Policy Optimization (SAPO) and training-time trajectory slicing that reuses the inference-time fold operator, letting one terminally rewarded episode yield multiple context-bounded optimization units without hand-designed step-level rewards.
- A comprehensive 8-benchmark evaluation plus scaling evidence: the same recipe applied to a >1T-parameter model (Qwen-CUA-Max) yields consistent further gains.

## Methodology

Qwen-CUA is built around a minimal **native computer-use loop**. At each step the agent receives only a screenshot of the current desktop state and emits one action from a keyboard-and-mouse action space (click, drag, type/key, scroll, plus control actions: screenshot, wait, terminate, call user). It deliberately avoids DOM trees, accessibility trees, shell access, and task-specific shortcuts.

**Long-horizon context management** has three parts:
1. *Scaling active visual history* to 20 screenshots per turn.
2. *Chunked screenshot folding*: a folded-prefix boundary advances by 10 screenshots at a time; screenshots behind the boundary become a fixed textual placeholder while their reasoning/actions remain. Because the boundary moves only every 10 steps, steps 21–30 share the same request prefix, improving KV-cache reuse.
3. *Training-time trajectory slicing*: complete episodes are rendered as multiple context-bounded slices by advancing the same fold boundary; each slice inherits the terminal reward and only active model-generated tokens contribute to the loss, aligning training and inference without separately generated summaries.

**Scaling agentic training** spans four axes:
- *Infrastructure*: Alibaba Cloud ECS fleet with ~100k vCPUs, tens of thousands of concurrent environments; environment provisioning, task dispatch, agent–environment interaction, outcome verification, and trajectory logging are all parallelized.
- *Environments*: self-contained mock web services with programmatic state injection/reset, plus broad desktop coverage spanning creative, scientific, engineering, and long-tail professional applications in sandboxed desktops.
- *Verifiable tasks (~40k)*: three classes — environment-operation tasks sampled from feature taxonomies, user-interactive tasks where a simulated user holds bounded knowledge and responds only on clarification, and long-horizon workflows whose phases have verifiable completion states that serialize to seed the next phase (phase-state chaining).
- *Personalized workflows*: human trajectories from personalized desktops and specialized software, augmented with action-grounded chain-of-thought rationales filtered for consistency with observed state and demonstrated behavior.

**Reinforcement learning** uses RLVR: each instance is (task t, reproducible initial state s, executable reward r∈[0,1]) evaluated on the resulting environment state. SAPO normalizes rewards within a group of G=16 trajectories (oversampled Nover=20), assigns each active token the group-relative advantage, and routes the importance ratio through a smooth asymmetric temperature-controlled gate (τpos=1.0, τneg=1.05) so on-policy tokens are nearly unclipped and off-policy tokens decay smoothly — negative-advantage tokens decay faster because they are the more destabilizing direction. Gradients flow only through model-generated reasoning/tool-call tokens.

**Iterative agent training**: each resulting model reveals failed SFT queries and weak domains; teacher rerolls, new human trajectories, and targeted weak-domain data refresh the SFT mixture (each SFT run initializes from the same mid-training checkpoint, not the prior agent). Candidate RL queries are then calibrated via 8 trial rollouts, retaining only tasks where 0 < success-count < 8, concentrating optimization on tasks with useful outcome variation. Training hardware: 512 NVIDIA H200 SXM GPUs across 64 nodes (32 training / 32 rollout), ~61,440 H200 GPU-hours for a full 1,000-update run (~5 days).

## Results & Highlights

Across eight computer-use benchmarks (all under pure screenshot-only native interaction, no DOM/accessibility/shell/API):

- **OSWorld-Verified (360 tasks)**: Qwen-CUA scores **86.2**, beating Qwen3.7 (**73.3**), GPT-5.5 (78.7), and Claude Opus 4.8 (83.4) — a **+12.9 point** gain over Qwen3.7. It averages 14.2 outer steps and 3,605.8 output tokens/task; Claude Opus 4.8 reaches only 83.3 with 21.8K tokens, showing Qwen-CUA's gain is not from more verbose reasoning.
- **OSWorld 2.0 (long-horizon)**: Qwen-CUA reaches **18.5 binary / 48.4 partial** completion, vs Qwen3.7 at **2.5 / 22.5** — a +16.0 / +25.9 improvement. Qwen-CUA-Max raises this to **21.2 / 53.3** (+2.7 / +4.9 over Qwen-CUA).
- **MacAgentBench (676 macOS tasks, 25 apps)**: Qwen-CUA achieves **69.2 Pass@1**, the highest among compared models, vs Qwen3.7 (57.1), GPT-5.5 (66.7), and Opus-4.8 (58.4). Strongest on Multimedia (81.0); weakest on Multi-App (58.6).
- **ScienceBoard (169 tasks)**: Qwen-CUA completes **109/169 (64.50%)**, nearly matching GPT-5.5 (110/169, 65.08%) and Opus-4.8 (113/169, 66.80%), and far above Qwen3.7 (60/169, 35.50%) — a +29.0 point jump.
- **Gym-Anything (1,295 tasks, 97 environments)**: Qwen-CUA scores **46.3** (strict-clean) vs Qwen3.7 (33.1), GPT-5.5 (45.6), and Opus-4.8 (47.3) — a +13.2 point gain over Qwen3.7 and slightly ahead of both frontier competitors.
- **RedTeamCUA (indirect prompt injection)**: Qwen-CUA improves task success to **74.0** (vs Qwen3.7 70.5) and cuts attack success rate (ASR) from **36.6 to 16.4**, a **−20.2 point** reduction, consistent across ownCloud, Rocket.Chat, and Reddit. It approaches GPT-5.5 (75.7 / 15.6); Opus-4.8 remains strongest (80.7 / 0.7).
- **Hybrid Bash ablation (MyPCBench)**: adding a Bash tool shortens average turns from 63.6 to **49.1** for Qwen-CUA (and 69.3→53.4 for Qwen3.7), but perfect-task rate drops from 58.7 to **55.1** (and 51.6→41.8 for Qwen3.7) — indicating the models have not yet learned reliable tool routing.
- **RL dynamics (final iteration, 397B-A17B)**: training-task smoothed score recovers from ~0.49 to ~0.64; cross-domain validation rises from 0.734 pre-RL to a peak of **0.770 at checkpoint 40** (update 800), selected for downstream evaluation.
- **Infrastructure scale**: ~100,000 vCPUs, ~40,000 verifiable tasks, ~61,440 H200 GPU-hours per 1,000-update RL run, up to 2,000 concurrent active environments at >75% utilization.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The individual ingredients are mostly composites of recent prior work — native screenshot-only agents (UI-TARS, OpenCUA), verifiable environment/task synthesis (CUA-Gym, GUI-GENESIS, InfiniteWeb), online multi-turn RL for GUI (UI-TARS-2, EvoCUA-1.5, SCALECUA), and iterative self-improvement (SEAgent, EvoCUA). SAPO is imported from Gao et al. 2025 rather than introduced here. The genuine contribution is engineering integration and scale (397B-A17B MoE, ~100k vCPUs, ~40k tasks, 20-image active history with 10-step block folding), not a new algorithm.
- **Rigor**: Main table compares against Qwen3.7, GPT-5.5, and Claude Opus 4.8 across 8 benchmarks with detailed appendices per benchmark. However, several comparability caveats are underplayed: (a) on OSWorld 2.0 the turn counts are not normalized — GPT-5.5 (83.5 turns) and Opus-4.8 (105.7) can batch multiple actions per turn whereas Qwen-CUA emits one native action per turn (218.9 turns), so the "interaction efficiency" comparison conflates interface packaging with true action efficiency; (b) Qwen3.7 is run in non-thinking mode while Qwen-CUA uses thinking — a setting asymmetry that inflates the headline delta against its own base model; (c) Gym-Anything omits 100 of 197 environments (22 Windows, 7 Android, 71 Linux with setup failures), so the 46.3 score covers only ~49% of the benchmark.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong on surface (public repo, exact SAPO config in Table 3, action schema in Appendix A, slicing algorithm in Appendix B, per-environment Gym-Anything scores in Table 11). But ~100k vCPUs, 512 H200 GPUs, and ~5-day RL runs put the actual recipe far beyond academic reach, and the ~40k verifiable tasks + mock web services are not released as a dataset, so the RL data pipeline is not reproducible.
- **Significance**: High. The OSWorld-Verified 86.2 (beating Opus-4.8's 83.4 and GPT-5.5's 78.7 under native protocol) and the RedTeamCUA ASR drop to 16.4 are meaningful, and the Qwen-CUA-Max scaling result shows the recipe is not yet saturated.
- **Red flags / data quirks**: (i) MacAgentBench includes 12 "clock" tasks that all score 0 from the official evaluator despite manual inspection showing correct interactions — the paper retains the official 0 rather than correcting, which depresses the 69.2 aggregate by an unquantified amount. (ii) RedTeamCUA's Rocket.Chat platform must be read with task success (32.6) because low execution success can mechanically lower ASR without robust rejection. (iii) The Bash-hybrid experiment shows perfect-task rate *drops* when Bash is added — framed as "optimization gap," but it is also evidence that the native-only policy has not learned when *not* to use the GUI. (iv) Figure 4(b)'s iteration slopes are explicitly disclaimed as not controlled convergence, since SFT mixture, domain coverage, and RL tasks all change between iterations.

## Strengths

- Genuinely broad and consistent evaluation across 8 complementary benchmarks (everyday desktop, long-horizon, personalized, scientific, web, macOS, cross-software breadth, and adversarial safety), all under a single pure-native protocol.
- Strong and honest efficiency analysis: token-per-task and turns-per-task are reported separately, and the interface-packaging confound for turn counts is explicitly acknowledged.
- The chunked-folding context manager is a clean, deterministic, training-inference-consistent mechanism that improves KV-cache reuse without an extra summarizer model.
- Detailed appendices (action space, slicing algorithm, full SAPO objective and gradients, RL hyperparameter table, distributed-training topology, per-domain and per-environment results) give uncommon engineering transparency for an industry report.
- Honest framing of hybrid native+Bash results: reports the trade-off (shorter trajectories but lower task success) rather than cherry-picking.

## Limitations

- Pixel-only observation and serialized low-level actions incur substantial latency and interaction cost; visual operation is inefficient when a task could be expressed directly via code, shell, or APIs.
- The Bash-hybrid experiment shows current Qwen models have not learned reliable tool routing — adding Bash *reduced* perfect-task rate despite shortening trajectories.
- Residual safety risk remains: RedTeamCUA ASR is still 16.4, and Rocket.Chat's low task success can mask ASR; the paper explicitly states this is improved resistance, not a deployment-safety guarantee.
- Comparability confounds: turn-count metric is interface-dependent (Qwen-CUA emits one action/turn vs multi-action turns for GPT-5.5/Opus-4.8); Qwen3.7 is run in non-thinking mode vs Qwen-CUA in thinking mode.
- Benchmark coverage gaps: 100 of 197 Gym-Anything environments were omitted due to Windows/Android/Linux setup failures; 12 MacAgentBench clock tasks scored 0 due to evaluator issues despite apparently correct interactions.
- The ~40k verifiable tasks and mock web services are not publicly released, so the RL data pipeline — the core of the recipe — is not reproducible outside Alibaba-scale infrastructure.

## Relevance

与「计算机使用智能体 / GUI Agent / 多模态 LLM 长程决策」方向高度相关：该工作展示了纯视觉原生交互在大规模 RL 训练下的可行性，是后续研究「原生交互 + 工具调用混合智能体」与「Agent 安全对齐」的重要参考。其对长程视觉上下文管理（块折叠 + 训练/推理一致切片）与可验证任务合成的工程经验，对自研 Agent 训练 pipeline 有直接借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the OSWorld-Verified gain over Qwen3.7 survives when Qwen3.7 is also run in thinking mode with an equivalent scaffold, isolating the contribution of the CUA post-training from the inference-setting asymmetry?
- Can the chunked-folding operator be replaced by a learned visual summarizer to recover information lost in the text placeholders, and does it help on tasks where early screenshots carry load-bearing state?
- The Bash-hybrid experiment shows tool routing is the bottleneck — would explicit tool-routing supervision or heterogeneous trajectory collection recover the lost perfect-task rate while keeping the ~22% turn reduction?
- How does SAPO's asymmetric temperature (τneg > τpos) interact with the MoE routing stability at 397B scale, and is the asymmetry still beneficial at the >1T Qwen-CUA-Max scale?
- Given the 100/197 omitted Gym-Anything environments, what is Qwen-CUA's score on the full benchmark once Windows/Android images are provisioned, and does the ranking vs Opus-4.8 (47.3) change?
- The 16.4 residual RedTeamCUA ASR — what failure modes account for it, and does targeted safety RL on indirect-prompt-injection trajectories close the gap to Opus-4.8's 0.7 without sacrificing task success?
