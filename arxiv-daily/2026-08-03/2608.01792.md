> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 ConfBench——首个面向文档关键信息抽取 (KIE) 的置信度校准专用基准，通过 20 条受控降质管线把 75 份真实 FCC 发票扩展为 1,346 个覆盖完整准确率谱系的文档变体（70K+ 实体级评测），在 7 个 VLM 上系统比较 verbalized 与 logprob 两类置信度估计，发现 OCR+Image 一致最优、模型能力是置信度质量的主导因素（Claude Opus 4.6 AUROC=0.84、ECE=0.05 接近完美校准），并提出 ECARB 这一运营节省指标量化人审预算收益。

> **TL;DR:** ConfBench is the first calibration-specific benchmark for VLM-based key information extraction (KIE), built by applying 20 controlled document-degradation pipelines to 75 verified FCC invoices to yield 1,346 variants with 70K+ entity-level evaluations spanning the full accuracy spectrum. Evaluating 7 VLMs (4 proprietary, 3 open-weight) under verbalized (1S-TopK) and log-probability confidence across three input modalities, the authors find that OCR+Image is uniformly the strongest modality, model capability dominates (Claude Opus 4.6 reaches AUROC 0.84 and ECE 0.05 vs. Gemma 3-12B's 0.58 / 0.31), and the proposed ECARB@30% metric shows the best configuration (Opus 4.6, OCR+Image) catches 2.43× more errors than random review while the weakest still yields 1.30×.

## 核心贡献

- **首个面向 IDP/KIE 的校准专用基准 ConfBench**：用 Augraphy 框架配合 20 条受控降质管线（8 个 archetype + 12 个 custom，覆盖 ink/paper/post 三阶段）把 75 份人工核验过的 RealKIE-FCC-Verified 发票扩展为 1,346 个文档变体，把原数据集高度集中在 0.6+ 准确率区的分布补齐到 0.0–1.0 全谱，使低/中准确率区域有足够样本支撑可靠的校准评估。
- **首次系统化评测 7 个 VLM 的置信度质量**：在 4 个闭源 (Claude Opus/Sonnet/Haiku 4.5–4.6, Kimi K2.5, Qwen 3 VL-235B) 与 3 个开源 (Qwen 3.6-27B, Gemma 3-12B, Qwen 3 VL-235B) 模型上跨 verbalized 与 logprob 两类置信度、OCR-only / Image-only / OCR+Image 三种模态建立校准基线。
- **提出 ECARB（Error Capture at Review Budget）运营指标**：把判别性收益（AUROC）转译为人审预算下的实际错误捕获增益，ECARB@b=1 表示与随机抽样持平，>1 表征人审性价比。
- **四条可操作结论**：(i) OCR+Image 一致最优；(ii) Claude 家族内校准随能力单调提升 (Opus > Sonnet > Haiku)，跨家族参数量是弱预测器；(iii) 校准质量跨度极大，从近完美到严重过自信，per-model post-hoc 校准可吸收绝对阈值问题但不改变排序型运营指标；(iv) logprob 中 first-token 聚合一致优于 mean 与 margin。
- **数据集与配套资源开源**（HuggingFace `amazon/ConfBench`），并设计了 tier 划分（High/Moderate/Mild）和完整的 augmentation 配置说明，便于复现与扩展。

## Key Contributions

- **ConfBench, the first calibration-focused KIE benchmark**: applies 20 controlled degradation pipelines (8 Augraphy archetypes + 12 custom) across ink/paper/post phases to 75 manually verified FCC invoices, yielding 1,346 documents that fill out the 0.0–0.6 accuracy region left sparse by the original clean corpus (Figure 1) and enable 70K+ entity-level calibration evaluations.
- **First systematic VLM confidence-calibration study**: evaluates 4 proprietary (Claude Opus 4.6 / Sonnet 4.5 / Haiku 4.5, Kimi K2.5) and 3 open-weight (Qwen 3 VL-235B, Qwen 3.6-27B, Gemma 3-12B) models under verbalized (1S-TopK) and token-level log-probability confidence (first-token / mean-token / margin aggregations) across three input modalities, producing the first empirical baselines for VLM confidence quality on KIE.
- **ECARB metric**: translates the discriminative ability captured by AUROC into operational savings for human-in-the-loop (HITL) deployments; at a 30% review budget, the best config catches 2.43× more errors than random sampling and the weakest still 1.30×.
- **Four practitioner-relevant findings**: (i) OCR+Image uniformly dominates Image-only and OCR-only across all models; (ii) within the Claude family calibration scales monotonically with capability (Opus 4.6 > Sonnet 4.5 > Haiku 4.5), while across families parameter count is a poor predictor (Qwen 3.6-27B at 0.72 AUROC beats the 8×-larger Qwen 3 VL-235B at 0.62); (iii) calibration ranges from near-perfect (Opus 4.6, ECE=0.05 without any post-hoc adjustment) to severely overconfident (Gemma 3-12B, ECE=0.31), and per-model post-hoc correction rescales absolute scores without changing ranking-based metrics; (iv) for logprobs, first-token aggregation consistently beats margin and mean.
- **Public release** of the benchmark (`https://huggingface.co/datasets/amazon/ConfBench`, CC-BY-NC 4.0) with full augmentation configs and tier breakdown to enable systematic study of confidence estimators and calibration methods.

## Methodology

**Benchmark construction.** ConfBench builds on the 75-document RealKIE-FCC-Verified corpus (manually corrected subset of RealKIE). Each FCC invoice contains multiple entity types (invoice number, date, and LineItems with five sub-fields). The authors use the Augraphy document-augmentation framework to apply 20 pipelines (8 archetypes + 12 custom), each combining sequential noise operations across three phases: ink (bleed-through, mottling, low ink, dithering), paper (staining, watermarks, colored paper, moiré, bindings), and post-processing (JPEG compression, shadows, lighting gradients, scanner defects, geometric distortion). Parameters are constrained to be "OCR-safe" (rotation ≤ ±5°, JPEG quality 70–95, brightness 0.7–1.3, blur ≤ 200 px / < 8 iterations, overlay alpha < 0.3). Quality filtration drops documents with > 80% null entities (catastrophic OCR failure), leaving 1,346 documents and ~70K–130K entity-level evaluations (depending on the model). Pipelines are bucketed into three severity tiers (High > 10% accuracy drop, Moderate 5–10%, Mild < 5%).

**Confidence estimation.** Two complementary families are compared.
- *Verbalized (1S-TopK)*: a single LLM call jointly extracts entities and emits the top-4 candidate guesses with continuous probabilities per entity; the highest-probability candidate is selected. Prompted per Tian et al. (2023) with no chain-of-thought. Works for any instruction-following VLM.
- *Logprob*: a single greedy-decoding pass (T=0) emits JSON; confidence is derived from token log-probabilities spanning each entity value. Three aggregations are compared: first-token `exp(ℓ₁)`, mean-token `exp((1/K)Σℓₖ)`, and first-token margin `(p₁−p₂)/(p₁+p₂)`. Restricted to two self-hostable open-weight models (Qwen 3.6-27B, Gemma 3-12B); Qwen 3 VL-235B was too costly to self-host and was evaluated only under verbalized.

**Input modality factorial.** {Verbalized, Logprob} × {OCR-only (Textract text + per-token confidence), OCR+Image, Image-only}, giving six conditions per model.

**Metrics.** Extraction: Weighted Overall Accuracy (WOA, mean of per-field normalized-Levenshtein / tolerance-based similarity) and F1 (binary acceptance). Calibration: AUROC (scale-invariant discrimination), ECE (adaptive quantile binning, M=5), Brier score (proper-scoring sanity check), and the new ECARB@b = (errors caught reviewing top-k lowest-confidence predictions) / ((k/n)·E). ECARB=1 means no lift over random; values are largely budget-stable because they depend only on confidence-induced ranking.

**Control philosophy.** Token budgets are held constant within a model across confidence strategies (40K for Anthropic, 8K for open-weight), so within-model comparisons are fully controlled; cross-model comparisons are only partially controlled because LineItems is variable-length and per-model entity counts differ. The authors therefore explicitly avoid reading small cross-model gaps as strict orderings.

## Results & Highlights

- **Calibration spectrum spans near-perfect to severely overconfident (OCR+Image, Table 3)**: Claude Opus 4.6 achieves AUROC=0.84 and ECE=0.05 (near-perfect reliability without any post-hoc adjustment), whereas Gemma 3-12B (verbalized) scores AUROC=0.58 and ECE=0.31, a 26-point AUROC gap and 6× worse ECE on the same benchmark.
- **Within the Claude family, confidence quality scales monotonically with capability**: Opus 4.6 → Sonnet 4.5 → Haiku 4.5 yield AUROC 0.84 → 0.77 → 0.74 and ECE 0.05 → 0.13 → 0.17. Across families, parameter count is a poor predictor: Qwen 3.6-27B (27B, AUROC 0.72) beats Qwen 3 VL-235B (235B, AUROC 0.62) despite < 1/8 the parameters.
- **ECARB@30% operational lift**: the strongest configuration (Opus 4.6, OCR+Image, verbalized) catches 2.43× more errors than random sampling at a 30% review budget; Sonnet 4.5 reaches 2.24× and Haiku 4.5 1.96×; even the weakest logprob (mean-token) configuration still yields 1.30×.
- **Modality effect**: OCR+Image is uniformly best. The AUROC gap between OCR+Image and Image-only ranges from ~3 points (Opus 4.6) to ~6 points (Gemma 3-12B), so weaker models benefit most from the OCR overlay.
- **Verbalized vs. logprob is model-dependent**: for Qwen 3.6-27B, verbalized AUROC (0.72) beats logprob first-token (0.62) by 10 points; the pattern reverses for Gemma 3-12B (logprob 0.64 > verbalized 0.58), consistent with weaker models being unable to self-assess but still producing informative logits. Among logprob aggregations, first-token consistently dominates margin and mean for both models.
- **Benchmark coverage**: the 20 degradation pipelines fill the 0.0–0.6 accuracy region left empty by the original 75 documents (which all cluster above 0.6), producing the full-spectrum calibration data that existing clean benchmarks (RealKIE, UniKIE-Bench, OmniDocBench) cannot provide. Custom22 (dithering + dot-matrix, –21%) and Archetype9 (letterpress/fax/high geometric distortion, –14%) are the highest-severity tiers.

## Authors & Credibility

All eleven authors are from **Amazon Web Services** (explicitly attributed on the title page), including senior applied scientists (Diego A. Socolovsky, Bob Strahan) and active IDP researchers (Md Mofijul Islam, co-author of the related DocSplit benchmark, arXiv:2602.15958). The work builds directly on AWS's own production-oriented RealKIE-FCC-Verified dataset (Amazon AGI Team, 2024) and uses Amazon Textract for OCR, consistent with a deployment-motivated industry study rather than a synthetic academic exercise. The dataset is publicly released on HuggingFace at `amazon/ConfBench` under CC-BY-NC 4.0, and the paper self-reports an unusual degree of scope-limiting language ("scoped observations rather than general claims") and an explicit limitations section, which strengthens credibility.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: High and well-positioned. The paper correctly identifies a real gap — every prior KIE benchmark it surveys (RealKIE, UniKIE-Bench, OmniDocBench, RVL-CDIP, Tobacco, DocSplit) targets clean, high-quality documents and reports only accuracy/F1; none measures confidence calibration. The Augraphy-based degradation-to-spectrum design is a clean, reproducible way to populate the low-accuracy region calibration evaluation requires. ECARB is a small but genuinely useful operational reframing of AUROC.
- **Rigor**: Mixed but largely honest. Within-model comparisons (modality, confidence strategy) are fully controlled and the token-budget story is transparent. However, **cross-model comparisons are only partially controlled**: Anthropic models get a 40K token budget vs. 8K for open-weight models, and because LineItems is a variable-length nested list the per-model entity counts vary wildly (Qwen 3.6-27B emits ~2× the entities of Qwen 3 VL-235B at the same task). The authors acknowledge this and explicitly tell readers not to read small gaps as strict orderings — an admirable stance — but it still means absolute metric values across families are indicative, not matched.
- **Sample-size red flags the paper itself flags**: two key claims rest on n=2. The verbalized-vs-logprob interaction (§5.2) is established on only Qwen 3.6-27B and Gemma 3-12B, and the MoE observation (§5.3) rests on only Kimi K2.5 and Qwen 3 VL-235B. The authors correctly scope these as observations rather than trends, but readers should not generalize them.
- **Corpus scope**: 75 base FCC invoices is a small, single-domain, single-language, single-layout-family seed. Calibration is a joint property of model × schema × visual structure, so the headline model ranking may not transfer to multi-column, handwritten, or non-English documents. The authors are unusually explicit about this and frame ConfBench as a "seed benchmark," with DocILE as a planned expansion — but the current ranking claims are bounded to degraded business invoices.
- **Augmentation realism**: several of the highest-severity augmentations (bleed-through, letterpress, fax) are explicitly admitted to be implausible for modern single-sided FCC invoices; they exist purely to populate the low-accuracy end. This is a defensible design choice (spectrum coverage over distribution fidelity, as the paper states) but means the absolute ECE/Brier numbers cannot be read as expected production calibration.
- **Missing ablations**: no post-hoc calibration results (temperature scaling, etc.) are reported — by design, the authors frame calibrator benchmarking as future work. But this means a reader cannot judge how much of the ECE gap between Opus 4.6 (0.05) and Gemma 3-12B (0.31) survives a simple temperature fit, which is the operationally relevant question for threshold-based routing. The reliability diagrams in Appendix A.9 are offered as a substitute but not analyzed in the main text.
- **Verbalized extraction-confidence coupling**: 1S-TopK extracts and scores jointly in one call, so WOA differs slightly between verbalized and logprob runs (e.g., Qwen 3.6-27B 0.74 verbalized vs. 0.75 logprob). The authors flag this and point to a decoupled two-stage variant as future work, but it is a confound for the within-model verbalized-vs-logprob comparison.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong on the data side (public HuggingFace dataset, full augmentation configs in Appendix A.3, OCR-safe parameter ranges specified). Weaker on the model side — four of the seven models are proprietary APIs whose behavior can drift between evaluations, and several model identifiers (Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.5, Haiku 4.5, Kimi K2.5, Qwen 3.6-27B) are at the date-of-writing snapshots that may not be re-fetched identically.

## Strengths

- Fills a genuine methodological gap: first calibration-specific KIE benchmark with full-accuracy-spectrum coverage, vs. all prior clean-document accuracy-only benchmarks.
- Clean experimental design: 2 (confidence families) × 3 (modalities) factorial with within-model token-budget control and four complementary calibration metrics (AUROC, ECE, Brier, ECARB).
- ECARB is a well-motivated, deployment-relevant metric that bridges the gap between statistical calibration and HITL economics, and is shown to be largely budget-stable because it depends only on confidence-induced ranking.
- Intellectual honesty is above average: explicit scope-limiting language, a detailed Limitations section, public dataset release, and refusal to over-claim from n=2 observations.
- Concrete practitioner guidelines (shortlist on WOA + AUROC, add post-hoc calibration only for threshold routing, read ECARB at the deployment's own review budget) are directly actionable.

## Limitations

- **Single-domain seed corpus**: 75 manually verified FCC invoices from one layout family, one language, one document type. Findings may not transfer to multi-column, handwritten, non-English, or naturally-degraded production scans.
- **Small n for two headline claims**: verbalized-vs-logprob comparison rests on two open-weight models; MoE-vs-dense observation rests on two MoE models. Both are framed as scoped observations, not trends.
- **Partial cross-model control**: 40K vs. 8K token budgets and variable-length LineItems mean absolute metric values across models are indicative, not strictly matched.
- **No post-hoc calibration experiments**: temperature scaling and similar calibrators are explicitly deferred to future work, leaving the operationally critical "how much does calibration close the gap" question open.
- **Augmentation realism gap**: several high-severity pipelines (bleed-through, letterpress, fax) are implausible for modern FCC invoices and exist only to populate the low-accuracy end; absolute ECE/Brier numbers should not be read as expected production calibration.
- **Verbalized extraction-confidence coupling** in 1S-TopK slightly contaminates the within-model verbalized-vs-logprob WOA comparison; a decoupled two-stage variant is left as future work.
- **Proprietary model drift**: four of seven models are commercial APIs whose behavior can shift between evaluations, complicating long-term reproducibility.
- **Potential deployment risk** the authors themselves flag: a system that treats high confidence as a substitute for review will silently pass through the errors the model is most certain about; calibration can shift under distribution drift, so operators must re-measure ECARB and thresholds on their own data.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to anyone deploying **vision-language models for document intelligence / KIE in production**, because the central question—whether VLM confidence scores are trustworthy enough to route between automation and human review—is the gating concern for IDP ROI. For research agendas in **LLM/VLM confidence calibration, selective prediction, and human-in-the-loop systems**, ConfBench supplies the missing evaluation substrate (and a clean ECARB metric) that prior clean-document benchmarks could not, making it a useful reference both methodologically and as a public dataset to benchmark new calibrators against.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the ECE gap between Opus 4.6 (0.05) and Gemma 3-12B (0.31) survives a simple per-model temperature scaling fitted on a held-out degradation tier, and does a calibrator fitted on the High-severity tier transfer to the Mild tier?
- Does the verbalized-vs-logprob reversal between Qwen 3.6-27B and Gemma 3-12B persist across more open-weight models (Llama 3.x-Vision, Mistral-Doc, Phi-VLM) once token probabilities are exposed, or is it specific to these two architectures?
- When ConfBench is extended to non-invoice domains (DocILE receipts, multi-column forms, handwritten documents) and to non-English corpora, do the modality (OCR+Image dominance) and within-Claude monotonic-scaling findings replicate?
- Can verbalized and logprob signals be ensembled into a single confidence score that strictly dominates both families per model, given they draw on different information?
- How stable are ECARB rankings across review budgets (5%, 30%, 50%) for models whose reliability diagrams cross the y=x diagonal non-monotonically (e.g., Sonnet 4.5, Haiku 4.5)?
- Does naturally-occurring production-pipeline degradation (real scanner artifacts, real capture noise) produce calibration behavior consistent with the synthetic Augraphy tiers, or do the implausible-but-useful pipelines (bleed-through, fax) bias absolute calibration estimates?
