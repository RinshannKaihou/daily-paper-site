> **一句话总结：** 通过 logit lens、activation patching 和单层消融三类实验在 GPT-2-Small (117M)、Llama-3.2-3B、Qwen2.5-32B 三个尺度上追踪词义消歧过程，作者发现歧义词的不同义项在表示空间上"中段最远、末层部分再收敛"，但下一 token 预测的 KL 散度恰在末层最大，且末层表示对输出仍具因果决定力——这从机制上解释了为何末层嵌入余弦相似度不能忠实反映模型实际的消歧能力。

> **TL;DR:** Across GPT-2-Small (117M), Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-32B, the cosine distance between hidden-state representations of an ambiguous word in two contexts peaks in middle layers and then partially reconverges in late layers, while KL divergence between next-token predictions grows monotonically to its maximum in the final layers (e.g., on the homonym stimuli in GPT-2 KL rises early M=2.08 → middle 10.70 → late 16.45, F(2,1115)=1318.39, p<.0001). Activation patching provides causal evidence that these late-layer representations nonetheless determine the output (Qwen late-layer swaps reach token overlap 0.95 and swap effectiveness 965–1298), explaining Capone et al. (2024)'s behavioural/embedding decoupling and warning that late-layer cosine similarity used in semantic search, retrieval and clustering can understate the sense distinctions a model actually encodes.

## 核心贡献

- 通过 logit lens 在每一层同时测量表示几何（激活距离、logit 距离的余弦距离）与预测编码（下一 token 分布的 KL 散度），将"表示编码"与"预测编码"分离，揭示二者在末层呈现反向走势：表示再收敛而预测最大发散。
- 在 GPT-2-Small、Llama-3.2-3B、Qwen2.5-32B 三种跨越三个数量级的解码器模型上重复得到一致结论，并同时覆盖同形异义词（homonyms）与概念漂移多义词（concept-drift polysemes）两类歧义，证明该机制对架构、规模、语义关系均普适，而非模型特异性现象。
- 使用 activation patching 提供直接因果证据：即便末层表示在余弦空间中看似接近，将其在不同上下文之间互换仍能把模型的 top-k 预测几乎完全推向源上下文，证明语义区分确实由这些"看似收敛"的末层表示承载。
- 单层消融揭示不同模型即便最终行为等效，其逐层计算依赖截然不同：GPT-2 与 Qwen 的表示/预测在早-中层共同退化，而 Llama 的表示鲁棒、预测却全网脆弱——为"异途同归"的消歧机制提供了新的描述性证据。
- 控制分析（"cat/mouse" 词序互换对照）排除了序列位置/句法角色变化这一混淆变量，表明观察到的激活分离确实是语义消歧，而非位置编码差异所致。
- 对实践给出了清晰建议：依赖末层余弦相似度的语义检索/聚类/RAG 会系统性低估模型的实际消歧能力，建议结合模型预测信息或改用中层表示。

## Key Contributions

- A layer-by-layer disambiguation-tracking design that decouples representational encoding from predictive encoding, measuring cosine (activation) distance, projected-logit distance, and next-token KL divergence at every layer of three decoder-only models spanning 117M → 3B → 32B parameters.
- Cross-scale and cross-architecture generality: the "middle-layer peak, late-layer reconvergence in representation space vs. monotonic late-layer maximum in predictive divergence" pattern holds for GPT-2-Small, Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-32B, and is qualitatively identical for both homonyms and concept-drift polysemes.
- Causal evidence from activation patching that late-layer representations, despite reduced geometric separation, functionally determine the output in both directions (A↔B), ruling out "loss of information" accounts of the reconvergence.
- A mechanism proposed for Capone et al. (2024)'s observation that models' behavioural similarity judgments correlate only weakly with their own internal embedding similarities: predictive divergence is amplified exactly where representational geometry reconverges.
- A single-layer ablation finding that different architectures reach equivalent disambiguation through qualitatively different layer-wise dependencies (Llama shows robust representations but near-complete predictive collapse across all layers; GPT-2 and Qwen show coupled early/middle-layer criticality), opening questions about what architectural feature distinguishes Llama here.
- A sequence-position control (reordered "cat/mouse" sentence pairs) showing homonym activation distances exceed sequence-order distances at every one of Llama's 28 layers (e.g., early median 0.483 vs 0.113, r_rb = .758), confirming the effect is semantic rather than positional.

## Methodology

**Models.** Three decoder-only transformers spanning three orders of magnitude, accessed via TransformerLens v2.1.6 on PyTorch 2.6.0 with no fine-tuning and default hyperparameters: GPT-2-Small (12 layers, 117M; OpenAI), Llama-3.2-3B (28 layers; Meta), Qwen2.5-32B (64 layers; Alibaba). They differ in normalization (LayerNorm vs RMSNorm), attention (grouped-query in two), positional encoding, activations (SwiGLU vs GELU) and tokenizer (BPE / SentencePiece / tiktoken byte-level BPE).

**Stimuli.** Two ambiguity types — strict homonyms (e.g., "bank" = financial vs river) and concept-drift polysemes (e.g., "mouse" = rodent vs computer device) — sourced from public lists (Grammar.com, Wikipedia, Merriam-Webster, TCK Publishing, etc.). Initial candidate set of 190 homonym and 97 polyseme pairs; sentence contexts generated with Claude Sonnet 4.5 and then hand-edited. After model-specific validation (correct tokenization, distinct next-token predictions with D_KL > 0.5 for the logit-lens set, top-k token overlap < 0.4 and D_KL > 1.0 for the patching set, identical token positions for patching) the validated counts were 156–167 homonym pairs and 86–94 polyseme pairs for the logit-lens experiment, and 66–86 homonym pairs and 25–36 polyseme pairs for the patching experiment (Table 1).

**Experiment 1 — Logit lens.** Extract hidden state h_{l,t} at the target word position from layer l's residual stream, project through the unembedding matrix W_U, softmax to obtain layer-specific next-token distributions. Per layer compute: (a) activation distance = 1 − cosine similarity between raw hidden states; (b) logit distance = 1 − cosine similarity between projected logit vectors; (c) KL divergence between the two contexts' next-token distributions with ε = 1e-10 smoothing.

**Experiment 2 — Activation patching.** Bidirectionally swap the residual-stream activation at the target word position between Context A and Context B at each layer, then let the model finish its forward pass. Quantify: token overlap (fraction of top-10 predicted tokens matching the source context) and swap effectiveness = D_KL(P_swap‖P_original) / D_KL(P_swap‖P_other-original), capturing how completely predictions shift toward the source. Summary statistics computed in log10 space as geometric means ± 1.96 SEM.

**Experiment 3 — Single-layer ablation.** Zero the residual-stream activation at one layer while protecting foundation layers 0–1, 0–2, or 0–3, and measure percentage degradation of the three logit-lens metrics across all subsequent layers.

**Sequence-position control.** Compare Llama homonym activation distances to those of matching tokens appearing in reordered sentence pairs (e.g., "The cat chased the mouse" vs "The mouse chased the cat") at all 28 layers.

**Statistics.** Linear mixed-effects models (metric ~ layer_group + (1|stimulus_id)) for GPT-2; non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis with normalized ranks for Llama/Qwen because KL values became non-finite in late layers; Mann-Whitney U for pre-defined pairwise comparisons; layers grouped early/middle/late as GPT-2 0–3/4–8/9–11, Llama 0–6/7–16/17–27, Qwen 0–14/15–48/49–63; α = 0.05, via statsmodels 0.14.4 and scipy 1.15.2.

## Results & Highlights

- **Representational geometry peaks in the middle and reconverges in late layers; predictive divergence keeps growing.** On GPT-2-Small homonyms, activation distance followed early M = 0.069 → middle 0.215 → late 0.167 (F(2,2003) = 696.98, p < .001), while KL divergence grew monotonically early 2.08 → middle 10.70 → late 16.45 (F(2,1115) = 1318.39, p < .0001). The same dissociation was replicated on Llama-3.2-3B (activation distance medians 0.48 → 0.55 → 0.48; KL normalized-rank 0.14 → 0.46 → 0.82, H = 3808.70, p < .0001) and on Qwen2.5-32B (activation distance 0.38 → 0.63 → 0.42, H = 3844.57, p < .001; KL rank 0.14 → 0.53 → 0.89, H = 5464.47, p < .001). Concept-drift polysemes showed qualitatively identical trajectories in every model.
- **Late-layer representations are causally sufficient despite their geometric similarity.** In Qwen2.5-32B, activation patching raised token overlap from early A→B 0.23 → middle 0.66 → late 0.95 (H = 636.35, p < .001), and median swap effectiveness climbed to 965–1298 in late layers for homonyms (H = 720.54 / 736.78, p < .001). GPT-2 showed a similar escalation in swap effectiveness from early 0.11–0.14 through middle 22–25 to late 820–1033, and Llama reached 95%+ late-layer token overlap with swap effectiveness 621–677 (homonyms). Bidirectional A↔B symmetry indicates genuine encoding rather than noise reduction.
- **Single-layer ablation uncovers architecturally divergent routes to the same behaviour.** Llama-3.2-3B showed near-complete KL-divergence degradation across the whole network (early 100%, middle 100%, late 95%; H = 6892.78 homonyms) while representational-distance degradation stayed low (4.1%–14.3%) — coarse representations are robust but precise predictions are fragile everywhere. In contrast, GPT-2 and Qwen degraded together: GPT-2 fell steeply past layer 6 (activation-distance degradation ~100% → ~10–11.4% late), and Qwen's degradation dropped precipitously after layer 30 (activation distance 100% → ~4.6–5.3%, KL 100% → ~10% late; H = 8074.62 / 8625.90, p < .0001).
- **Effects are not explained by sequence position or syntactic role.** In Llama-3.2-3B, homonym activation distances exceeded the reordered-sentence control at every individual layer (early median 0.483 vs 0.113, U = 1,287,007.5, r_rb = .758; middle 0.546 vs 0.223, r_rb = .901; late 0.482 vs 0.190, r_rb = .883; all p < .001), confirming the separation is semantic rather than a positional artifact.
- **A notable Qwen anomaly.** Qwen2.5-32B showed a peculiar sharp rise in activation distance in the final layer (layer 63: 0.50 vs others 0.53, p = 0.28), possibly reflecting transformations related to output projection, while KL ranks at that layer jumped to 0.92 vs 0.52 (U = 1,183,155, p < .0001) — maximal prediction divergence with representational reconvergence.

## Authors & Credibility

K. Jack Scott (corresponding author) and Narun Pat are in the Department of Psychology, University of Otago; Veronica Liesaputra is in the School of Computing, University of Otago. The work spans psychology (lexical ambiguity, human-similarity-rating literature) and computer science (mechanistic interpretability), an appropriate combination for the question. The paper is a substantial 41-page arXiv submission with complete stimulus counts, statistical tests, effect sizes, foundation-size controls, a sequence-position control, and links to reproducible code at https://github.com/scoki211/Divergent_LLM_Predictions_Convergent_Reps_amb_words. The methodology builds cleanly on established interpretability tools (logit lens, activation patching) and existing literature (Ethayarajh 2019; Capone et al. 2024; Ma et al. 2025; Rivière, Beatty-Martínez, and Trott 2024; Skean et al. 2025) and is explicit about which prior results it extends rather than claims to invent.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine but incremental. The "non-monotonic layer-wise separation of homonym senses" was already established by Ma et al. (2025) and Rivière et al. (2024) using anisotropy-corrected angular measures; the paper's specific contributions are (a) adding a predictive-encoding axis (KL divergence) next to the representational one, (b) the causal activation-patching test for late layers, and (c) the concept-drift polyseme stimulus set. The central "reconvergence + divergence" dissociation is the truly new claim, and the proposed mechanism for Capone et al. (2024)'s decoupling is plausible but post-hoc.
- **Rigor.** Statistical reporting is unusually thorough — separate mixed-effects and non-parametric pipelines per model with stated convergence failure modes, normalized ranks with explicit handling of non-finite late-layer KL values that were dropped rather than capped (making the late-layer increase conservative), multiplicative CI computed in log space, and a sequence-position control that is rarely seen in this kind of work. The authors honestly flag that they did not statistically test between-model differences because depth and metric scale differ.
- **Reproducibility.** High. Pre-registered-style detail on stimulus selection sources, validation thresholds (D_KL > 0.5 / > 1.0, top-k overlap < 0.4), library versions, and a public GitHub repo. Stimulus lists promised in supplementary materials. The use of TransformerLens is standard.
- **Significance.** Practically relevant for the RAG / semantic-search / clustering community, which routinely extracts from penultimate or final layers: the paper offers a concrete reason to also consider middle-layer representations and prediction-derived features. However, the authors are careful to scope their claims to base decoder LMs and explicitly state they do not evaluate a retrieval or WSD system, so the downstream implications are framed as directions rather than demonstrated results.
- **Red flags / weak points.** (1) Stimuli are single-token residual-stream activations in base decoder models, while production embedding systems use pooled sentence/document vectors from fine-tuned embedding models — the bridge to applied cosine-similarity failure is "suggestive rather than direct," in the authors' own words. (2) The standard logit lens was used instead of the tuned lens (Belrose et al. 2023) without direct replication, justified by a 2026 Gurnee et al. argument that the tuned lens bypasses unverbalised intermediate computation; reasonable but the brittleness concern is acknowledged. (3) Cosine distance is reported without an anisotropy baseline subtraction (unlike Ma et al. and Ethayarajh 2019); the authors argue the decoder anisotropy profile runs opposite to their separation trajectory so raw distances if anything understate mid-layer separation, but a direct anisotropy-corrected replication would strengthen the geometric claims. (4) The Llama-specific "robust representations, fragile predictions" ablation profile is presented descriptively without an architectural explanation, and Llama and Qwen — which share RMSNorm, GQA, RoPE and SwiGLU — group apart, undermining a clean architecture-driven account. (5) Cross-architecture comparisons cannot separate scale from architecture; only one model per scale is tested. (6) No probe-based test of linear decodability at each layer is run, leaving open whether the distinction is linearly readable from late-layer representations.

## Strengths

- Three-model, three-orders-of-magnitude design with two ambiguity types and bidirectional patching gives unusually broad evidence for a single interpretability paper.
- Decouples representational encoding from predictive encoding instead of conflating them under a single "geometry" measure.
- Provides causal (patching) evidence rather than relying solely on correlational geometric observations.
- Honest scope and limitations section, including the Llama-vs-Qwen puzzle they cannot explain and the absence of anisotropy correction.
- Practical, falsifiable recommendations for the embedding/RAG community (combine with predictions, use middle-layer features) that follow directly from the findings.

## Limitations

- Single-token residual-stream activations in base decoder models do not match how pooled embeddings from instruction-tuned or embedding-specialized models are used in production RAG/search; the bridge to applied cosine-similarity failure is suggestive rather than direct.
- Standard logit lens used rather than tuned lens; no replication with the latter.
- Cosine distance reported without an anisotropy baseline, complicating direct comparison with Ma et al. (2025) and Ethayarajh (2019).
- Cross-architecture comparison conflates scale and architecture (one model per scale).
- No trained linear probe at each layer to test decodability directly; the question of whether the distinction is linearly separable in late layers remains open.
- Decoder-only architectures only; no test of encoder or encoder-decoder models, syntactic/referential/pragmatic ambiguity, or languages other than English.
- The Llama ablation anomaly (robust representations, fragile predictions) is reported descriptively without a clean architectural explanation.
- Does not evaluate an actual retrieval or WSD system end-to-end, so downstream recommendations are framed as directions.

## Relevance

本文对关注大模型可解释性、表示几何与下游语义任务（检索/RAG/聚类）之间关系的研究者高度相关：它从机制角度揭示了"末层嵌入余弦相似度不能忠实地反映模型的消歧能力"这一关键事实，并提供具体可行的改进方向（结合预测信息或中层表示）。对于从事嵌入模型评估、语义检索系统设计、以及词义消歧机制研究的工作尤其值得参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the reconvergence-while-divergence pattern replicate in instruction-tuned or embedding-specialized models with pooled sentence/document vectors, the actual objects used in production RAG pipelines?
- Does training a linear probe at each layer recover sense identity from late-layer representations, and if so with what accuracy gap relative to middle layers?
- What circuit-level features (cf. Ameisen et al. 2025 attribution graphs) drive the reconvergence of representational separation and the simultaneous amplification of predictive divergence?
- Does the Llama-specific "robust representations but fragile predictions" ablation profile survive a controlled scale sweep within the Llama family alone, or is it tied to training data / regularization rather than architecture?
- Do encoder and encoder-decoder models (BERT, T5) show the same dissociation, or does the absence of a unidirectional next-token objective change the late-layer geometry?
- Can an anisotropy-corrected replication reproduce the present geometric trajectory and quantitatively compare it against Ma et al. (2025)'s angular disambiguation scores?
- Extending beyond English homonyms: does the pattern hold for morphologically richer or languages-without-strict-homonym structures?
- Would combining middle-layer embeddings with prediction-derived features measurably outperform standard last-layer cosine similarity on a benchmarked WSD or retrieval task?
