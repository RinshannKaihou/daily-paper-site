Pseudorandom Streams within Diffusion Models Act as Learnable Inputs That Affect Generation Quality

Deng Shengzhi · Ye Chenqi · Guo Yanze
Harbin Institute of Technology · Northeastern University
diffusion-models pseudorandom-number-generation training-dynamics learnability empirical-power-law
⟶ Key Finding

Pseudorandom streams are not interchangeable noise — their orbit structure is a learnable input that shifts diffusion training loss and generation quality on MNIST & CIFAR-10.

Key Metric
ρ ≈ 0.94
Spearman rank correlation between diffusion probe loss and human generation grade, across 12 screened PRNG sources. All four correlations exceed 0.91, all p < 10⁻⁴.
Why This Matters

It challenges the assumption that training noise is merely an interchangeable implementation detail — and offers the diffusion probe as a reusable diagnostic for PRNG robustness and seed sensitivity.

Critical Edge

Headline correlations rest on N=12 sources (~9 distinct families) and a single CIFAR-10 matched run; the striking loss/quality inversion is not verified at convergence, only at deliberately short training budgets.

01

Methodology

Step 01
Random sources sampled as deterministic orbits — Logistic, Markov-type, Sine, ShiftedSine + IID baseline.
Step 02
Marginal control: empirical-rank uniformization → Box–Muller Gaussianization → mean/variance calibration.
Step 03
Diffusion U-Net trained on MNIST & CIFAR-10; two indicators computed: 273-param MLP + diffusion probe.
Step 04
Generation graded on 0–6 ordinal scale (blind human evaluators); rank-correlated against indicators.
  • Optimization view The idealized objective L(θ; D) = E_R[L̂(θ; D, R)] is replaced by the realized per-step loss L̂_t(θ_t; D, R_t) — SGD consumes concrete pseudorandom values, so any useful orbit structure can in principle be exploited.
  • Diffusion probe (novel) Same U-Net, noising, and objective, but clean inputs are replaced by online IID uniform tensors. Isolates system-level exploitability of an orbit from raw predictability — a clean, modular diagnostic.
  • MLP indicator 273-parameter single-hidden-layer MLP (width 8) predicts next uniformized value from previous 32. IID theoretical floor 1/12 ≈ 0.0833.
  • Pre-screening Sources with strong intrinsic dynamics (Logistic 3.6 band alternation) or finite-precision absorbing states (Sine-1, Logistic 4.0 collapse to zero) are excluded from the correlation & power-law fit — leaving 12 screened sources.
  • Training budgets MNIST: 30 epochs, U-Net (64,128,128). CIFAR-10: 30k steps (~38.4 epochs), ddpm-cifar10-32 config. 1000 forward steps, 100-step DDIM (η=1).

↳ Capture-range argument

A non-trivial derivation: critical points with f′(c)=0 enlarge the rounding-induced capture width from O(Δ) to O(√Δ).

sin(πx) rounds to exactly 1
mod 1 → 0 → absorbing state

Logistic 4.0 hits the same fate at iteration 15,070,465. These are concrete, reproducible failure cases — not theoretical concerns.

⇅

Counterintuitive inversion: lower loss, worse images

L3.7 achieves the lowest training loss on both datasets (~3.3× lower than IID on MNIST, ~7.5× on CIFAR-10) yet produces only blank/texture outputs (grade 1). IID has the highest loss and the best samples (grade 6). Consistent with the orbit supplying a predictable component the model exploits instead of learning data structure.

Strengths & Limitations

● Strengths

  • Clean conceptual reframing — pseudorandom streams as deterministic orbits coupled to a learning system, with the optimization pathway written out explicitly.
  • Diffusion probe is a clever, modular tool — isolates system-level exploitability from raw predictability and supports per-role ablations (data order vs. timestep vs. noise).
  • Thorough pre-screening with rigorous numerical analysis of finite-precision absorbing states (√Δ vs. Δ capture-range argument + concrete float values).
  • Strong, consistent rank correlations across two datasets and two independent indicators.
  • Honest scope — cross-system comparison with a classifier and a VAE grounds the claim that sensitivity is system-dependent, not universal.
  • Public code & data tables at github.com/happyflatfish/prng-diffusion-learnability.

▲ Limitations

  • Small source sample — mainly Logistic + Markov-type + ShiftedSine; only one history length (32) and one MLP capacity (273 params).
  • Short training budgets — the headline loss/quality inversion is not verified at full training duration.
  • Single CIFAR-10 matched run underpins the power-law exponent.
  • Ordinal human grade as the macroscopic quality variable — within-grade ordering is unresolvable; no FID/IS/MMD supplement.
  • No theoretical justification for the power law; the (1,1) IID anchor makes the log-log fit permissive.
  • Only small datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10); nothing at ImageNet scale or modern latent diffusion.
  • Per-role contributions deferred — the five random roles (data order, timestep, train noise, init noise, sampling noise) are not separately ablated.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

Q1
Does the lower-training-loss-yet-worse-generation inversion persist when CIFAR-10 is trained to convergence, or is it a small-budget transient?
Q2
Which random role drives the degradation most — data ordering, timestep selection, training noise, initial sampling, or stepwise sampling noise?
Q3
Does the power law d = pρ survive on a third dataset and on modern latent / text-to-image architectures? Does ρ scale with model width or depth?
Q4
Can a deliberately non-IID source with controlled temporal structure outperform the operational IID baseline on a specific model?
Q5
How do production PRNGs (Philox, PCG64, MT19937, Threefry) score on the diffusion probe at scale — is there an exploitability threshold beyond which a generator becomes unsafe?
Q6
Can the human 0–6 grade be replaced by a learned classifier-of-failures or a multi-metric composite that preserves rank while adding within-grade resolution?
