At nominal α=0.10, naive per-coordinate testing at τ̂ collapses toward always-rejecting as dimension grows; ARM holds the level flat across d ∈ {5,10,20,40} and detector misplacements |τ̂−τ| ∈ {0,5,10,20}.

Why this matters

For monitoring, portfolios, sensor arrays where "which coordinates changed" is the operational question, ARM adds a selection-proof certification layer over any off-the-shelf detector.

01 / Methodology — ARM in five moves

in window X ∈ ℝn×d  +  black-box detector → estimated changepoint τ̂ (ARM never inspects τ̂)
↓
① centre within-column ranks  C_ij = R_ij − (n+1)/2
↓
② for each split t on grid G:  Zloc_j(t) (Wilcoxon)  ⊕  Zsc_j(t) (Mood)
↓
③ score  M_j = maxt∈G max{Zloc, Zsc}  → since Z_j(τ̂) ≤ M_j, selection is powerless to inflate it
↓
④ threshold c_{n,α} = (1−α) quantile of M under a single null coordinate, computed once by permuting a rank vector
↓
⑤ calibrate:  FWER Westfall–Young joint row permutation (exact) · Holm fallback  |  FDR Benjamini–Yekutieli · e-BH on boosted e-values

The defining move is max-over-splits rather than Z_j(τ̂). Because a true-null coordinate cannot be pushed above its own maximum by any detector — accurate or arbitrarily misplaced — τ̂ affects only power, never validity. The type label ("scale" if Msc > Mloc, else "location") is descriptive and sits outside the error-control statement.

Validity survives heavy tails, dependence, detector choice

At d=10 (3 changed coords, CUSUM), WY FWER stayed 0.044–0.088 and Holm 0.036–0.092 across Gaussian, t₃, Cauchy, Lognormal marginals × independent / equicorrelated / factor dependence (BY FDR 0.011–0.023). True-τ and deliberately misplaced (τ̂=τ+10) columns differed by ≤ 0.03.

High-d FDR (d=200, 15 changed)

  • BY: FDR 0.019 (indep) / 0.017 (ρ=0.4) at power 1.00.
  • e-BH (s=0.004, B=1999): FDR 0.043 / 0.048 at power 1.00.
  • Sweep s ∈ {0.002,0.004,0.006} all below boundary αk/d=0.0075 → power 1.00; s=0.008 above boundary → power collapses to 0.66, FDR still controlled.

Attribution quality & type-label accuracy

  • vs. baselines (Table 4): ARM coordinate-level F1 = 0.985–0.987 (strong δ=1.8), 0.877–0.986 (weak δ=1.1). WAVE-style AUC > 0.99, block hit-rate 0.89–1.00 — but only ARM returns individual coordinates with a finite-sample guarantee.
  • Type label: location labelled correctly 1200/1200; scale 1058/1060 (2 mislabels). FWER 0.053, power 1.00 stable across B ∈ {199,499,999}; grid step 1→4 cuts cost ~4× with FWER in [0.073,0.087].

Real data — 2008 regime shift (Table 5)

120-day window around Lehman (15 Sep 2008), five FRED series + three injected controls, α=0.10, threshold c=3.6. ARM attributes a scale change to all five asset classes — equities M=6.04, oil 5.56, Treasury 4.50, USD/EUR 4.29, VIX 4.11 — and correctly excludes all three controls (M=1.99–2.26). Conclusions identical under Holm and BY; unperturbed by the detector's two-day mislocation.

03 / Strengths & Limitations

Strengths
  • Detector-agnostic wrapper delivering finite-sample, distribution-free control on the coordinate axis, valid at an estimated changepoint.
  • The max-over-splits idea elegantly converts a nuisance (unknown/estimated τ) into a non-issue for validity.
  • Honest, useful location vs. scale type labels at near-perfect simulation accuracy.
  • Multiple practical calibrations — WY exact · Holm distribution-free · BY robust · e-BH — with clear "when to use which" guidance.
  • Genuinely reproducible: archived code, fixed seeds, public FRED series, MC standard errors reported.
Limitations & risks
  • Independent rows assumed; serial dependence only approximately handled by a future block-permutation variant — limits use on raw financial returns.
  • Type label is descriptive, not certified; closed testing for an error-controlled label is open future work.
  • Pure dependence changes with invariant marginals are out of scope for the marginal-attribution model.
  • Empirical scale tested only up to d=200; behaviour at thousands of coordinates under structured dependence is unverified.
  • e-BH power is brittle to the choice of s vs. sparsity; deviating from the §3.5 rule collapses power (0.66 at s=0.008).

04 / Questions for further exploration

  1. How does ARM behave under serial dependence in rows (e.g. GARCH-filtered returns), and how large is the size inflation of the i.i.d.-row Westfall–Young procedure before a block-permutation variant exists?
  2. Can the location/scale label be promoted to an error-controlled statement via closed testing across the two channels without prohibitive cost?
  3. How does ARM calibrate at d ≫ 200 (10³–10⁴) under factor-model or graph-structured coordinate dependence, where e-BH and BY may diverge sharply in power?
  4. Can ARM compose cleanly with an online, anytime-valid sequential detector, replacing the permutation reference with e-processes?
  5. Does pairing ARM with WAVE-style estimation weights — estimation nominates, ARM certifies — materially improve power in sparse-signal regimes over either method alone?
  6. How sensitive are the 2008 conclusions to window length, injected controls, and detector choice (variance-CUSUM vs. mean-CUSUM vs. kernel-based)?
