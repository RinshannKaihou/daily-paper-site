Key finding

ChaosProbe fingerprints frozen transformer input embeddings with a deterministic chaotic probe — recovering every same-family pair at 1.00 accuracy across three similarity metrics.

Key metric · family accuracy

1.004/4

Same-family nearest-neighbor recovery under Pearson, Spearman & cosine on the standardized 800-D signature.

vs. 0.75 for Euclidean distance (3/4, recovers only 1 of 2 mutual pairs)

Why this matters

A chaotic-dynamics response fingerprint offers a tokenizer-agnostic alternative to CKA and probing classifiers for representation geometry.

But it is validated on only four models — its real-world family-identification value is unproven.

⚠ Critical edge

No matched non-chaotic probe is tested.

So it is unclear whether the GLS chaos — or simply the (Firing Rate, Entropy, CKA, anisotropy, effective rank) summary — is doing the work. The "neurochaotic" claim is currently aspirational, not proven necessary.

01

How a frozen embedding becomes an 800-D signature

No positional, segment, or contextual-layer information — only the static learned token embeddings.

Prompt-level matrix

Tokenize prompt with model's own tokenizer; look up frozen table E → X ∈ RT×d.

Min–max normalize

Entry-wise to [0,1] (zero-guard when min=max) so every model lives on the unit interval.

GLS skew-tent probe

For each of 100 configs (Q,B,ε), run a deterministic chaotic trajectory of length L=1000; record the first index hitting within ε of each coordinate.

Two descriptor channels

Firing Rate (activity > B) and Entropy (binary Shannon of thresholded symbols), computed coordinate-wise.

Four representation measures

Mean cosine sim, linear CKA, anisotropy, and normalized effective rank — per (prompt, config, channel).

800-D signature

Average over 80 prompts → R ∈ R100×2×4, vectorize in fixed order, then standardize across the 4 models.

Compare & bootstrap

Pearson / Spearman / cosine / Euclidean nearest-neighbors; 200 paired prompt bootstraps + validity diagnostics.

100 configs × 2 descriptor channels × 4 representation measures = 800-D signature · grid: Q(10) × B(5) × ε(2), averaged over 80 prompts

03

Strengths & limitations

Strong transparency and reproducibility; the cohort is the central weakness.

↑ Strengths

  • Genuinely novel framing — response-surface fingerprinting of frozen input embeddings via a deterministic chaotic probe, distinct from CKA, probing classifiers, and raw geometric analyses.
  • Tokenizer-agnostic — maps models with different vocabularies, sequence lengths, and embedding dims into one 800-D space without coordinate alignment.
  • Excellent transparency — pre-registered family labels, descriptive-not-calibrated thresholds, six explicit limitations, honest "bounded proof-of-concept" framing.
  • Stability that supports the claim — 200 paired bootstraps plus zero-variance and collapse-rate checks, not mere assertion.
  • Fully reproducible — deterministic pipeline, fixed grid, public code & prompts.

↓ Limitations

  • Smallest possible cohort — four checkpoints in two two-model families; one pair (GPT-2/DistilGPT2) is a direct teacher–student distillation.
  • No ablation isolating chaos vs. a matched non-chaotic probe, Firing Rate vs. Entropy, or the four measures; only one grid and one L are evaluated.
  • Frozen input embeddings only — nothing on contextual hidden states, attention, layer-wise evolution, or post-fine-tuning behavior.
  • 80 neutral English prompts — no multilingual, long-context, domain-specific, or adversarial inputs; no out-of-sample evaluation.
  • Signature depends on the model set — adding a fifth model changes every other signature; BERT↔RoBERTa NN signal is negative in absolute terms.
  • Author-chosen thresholds (0.75, 0.20, 0.80) — descriptive, not calibrated; no formal hypothesis test.
04

Open questions for further work

Each tests whether the result generalizes — or whether chaos is incidental.

Q1 · necessity of chaos

Would a matched non-chaotic probe (random deterministic map, simple thresholding, or linear transform producing the same summary) recover the same family structure?

Q2 · larger cohort

How does ChaosProbe perform across independently trained models — multiple GPT-2 / GPT-Neo / Pythia scales, several BERT/RoBERTa/ALBERT checkpoints, T5 encoder-decoders?

Q3 · standardization set

Are signatures stable to the comparison set? What happens to BERT↔RoBERTa's relative-NN status when a fifth masked-encoder model is added?

Q4 · contextual states

Does extending the probe to contextual hidden states (per-layer, pre-/post-attention) reveal trajectories that distinguish fine-tuned vs. base, or distilled vs. teacher?

Q5 · prompt sensitivity

How sensitive are the 800-D signatures to prompt distribution (multilingual, adversarial, domain-specific) and to alternative grids, trajectory lengths L, and tolerances ε?

Q6 · compression

Can the per-config response surface be compressed — do a few (Q, B, ε) configs carry most of the signal, selected in a principled, model-agnostic way?
