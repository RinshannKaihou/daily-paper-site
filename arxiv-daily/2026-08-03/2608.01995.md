> **一句话总结：** 作者把一个通用大语言模型当作"唯一研究员"，在纯通道注意力（channel-attention）视觉Transformer这一非标准问题上自主跑了约10周、2400 GPU小时、~100个单变量实验，从CIFAR-10的69.67%基线一路推到96.59%；文章本身的贡献不是新结构，而是这套长周期agent行为轨迹的行为学分析——它发现了"饱和墙"、贪婪增量偏好、跨尺度反迁移等现象，并指出workflow设计（而非模型能力本身）才是长周期自主研究最关键的杠杆。

> **TL;DR:** The authors let a single general-purpose LLM act as sole researcher on a channel-primary vision transformer problem under a pure channel-attention constraint, running ~10 weeks (~2,400 GPU-hours, ~100 single-variable hypotheses across CIFAR-10 / CIFAR-100 / ImageNet-1K) with only bounded human intervention. The architecture went from a 69.67% CIFAR-10 baseline to 96.59%, but the paper's real contribution is a behavioural case study: it documents a three-regime productivity structure (rapid discovery → saturation plateau → tool-triggered recovery), shows a single early hypothesis (FFN in CA blocks) accounts for ~79.5% of the +26.92 pp CIFAR-10 gain, and decomposes the agent's greedy bias into a workflow-induced component (the commit-or-discard rule is isomorphic to greedy hill-climbing) and a smaller LLM-inherent component (post-failure risk aversion, anchoring on familiar literature).

## 核心贡献

- **提出并完整记录了一种长周期、单agent的自主研究workflow**：一个LLM同时扮演提议者、实现者、执行者、解释者和归档者五个角色；用源码管理（每条假设=一次commit）、实验跟踪、（第二阶段起的）文献访问、持久化结构化记忆四种"研究affordance"支撑；人类只做策略级介入（阶段切换、可靠性修补），所有战术决策（提哪条假设、改哪段代码、commit还是discard）都由agent完成。
- **对agent的假设轨迹做了定量的行为学分析**，揭示三段式生产力结构（发现期 / 利用期 / 长饱和期）以及"工具访问=相变"现象：Phase 1到1b加入文献访问后，code级改动占比从5%跳到76%，新颖机制从2种增到8种，引用论文从0增到24+。
- **量化了"创新归因"的长尾**：CIFAR-10总计+26.92 pp的提升里，最早被接受的H1（给channel-attention block加FFN）独占+21.41 pp（79.5%），其余全是小修小补的长尾。
- **将agent的"贪婪增量偏好"分解为workflow诱发 vs. LLM固有两部分**，并指出commit-or-discard规则在结构上等价于贪心爬山；残留固有偏差主要是"大胆失败后的风险规避"和"锚定在熟悉高引文献上"。
- **记录了跨尺度反迁移现象**（mixup / label smoothing / drop-path 在5.8M→22M时正负号翻转）和一批agent独立重新发现的经典结论（single-head优于multi-head对齐XCiT、5×5 kernel最佳、更深>更宽等）。
- **给出5条可测试的workflow改进建议**（diversified proposal、budgeted moonshots、explicit forks、regime-aware re-validation、compact synthesis docs），作为给未来自主研究系统的"待验证假设"。

## Key Contributions

- **A reproducible long-horizon single-agent research loop** in which one persistent LLM session holds all research roles (proposer, implementer, executor, interpreter, archivist) over ~10 weeks and ~2,400 GPU-hours, with a fully enumerated human-intervention record (Table 2) so other groups can audit the trace.
- **A quantitative behavioural decomposition** of the hypothesis trajectory: a three-regime productivity structure (Discovery 67% success/+5.71 pp avg → Exploitation 71%/+0.32 pp → Saturation 21%/+0.17 pp), plus a "tool access as phase transition" finding where granting literature access flips the code-vs-config mix from 5% to 76%.
- **Innovation attribution showing extreme concentration**: of the +26.92 pp total CIFAR-10 gain, the first accepted hypothesis (adding an FFN to channel-attention blocks) alone contributes +21.41 pp (79.5%); the remainder is a long tail split between Phase-1 training-recipe tweaks and Phase-1b literature-derived code changes.
- **A qualitative bias decomposition** (Table 10 / Figure 7) splitting observed behaviours into workflow-induced (always-build-on-champion, no parallel tracks, binary commit/discard) versus LLM-inherent (risk aversion after bold failures, anchoring on familiar literature) components, with the claim that workflow design was at least as consequential as agent capability.
- **A catalogue of independent rediscoveries** (single-head > multi-head matching XCiT, 5×5 kernel sweet spot, deeper > wider, FFN load-bearing) and of cross-scale anti-findings (mixup/label-smoothing/drop-path sign-flips between 5.8M and 22M regimes).
- **Five testable workflow interventions** offered as hypotheses: diversified proposal policy, budgeted moonshot hypotheses, explicit architectural forks, regime-metadata tracking with auto re-validation, and compact agent-authored synthesis documents.

## Methodology

The authors do not propose a new architecture; they study a loop. The **autonomous research loop** (Algorithm 1) is built on four design principles:

1. **Single agent, single hypothesis.** Exactly one experiment is in flight at a time. Parallelism is intentionally avoided to preserve an unambiguous per-hypothesis causal signal at the cost of wall-clock.
2. **Commit-or-discard evaluation rule.** After each experiment the agent compares the result to the current champion; if better, the change is committed and becomes the new baseline, otherwise the change is reverted. The authors later note this rule is *isomorphic to greedy hill-climbing* and is the main structural source of the agent's incremental bias.
3. **Structured persistent memory.** Each hypothesis is logged in a fixed template (motivation, literature grounding, change description, parameter/compute budget, outcome) in a single research log the agent must read at session start and append before each submission.
4. **End-of-turn after submission.** The agent ends its turn immediately after submitting a Slurm job; an external watcher returns control when the job finishes, keeping the token budget on scientific reasoning rather than scheduler polling.

The agent is given four classes of **research affordance**: (i) source & experiment management (one branch per phase, one commit per accepted hypothesis), (ii) experiment tracking, (iii) literature access via preprints/repos/model-hub from Phase 1b onward, and (iv) compute via a shared-cluster workload manager (~150 jobs, ~130 substantive runs, ~2,400 GPU-hours total).

Three **human-authored documents** bound behaviour: a *research brief* (scientific question, design constraints, parameter/compute budget, illustrative directions), a *workflow template* (the loop, log structure, explicit prohibitions), and a *literature-grounding requirement* (cite a paper or declare the hypothesis an internal extrapolation).

**Case study problem.** A *channel-primary vision transformer* under a pure channel-attention constraint (no spatial self-attention at any stage), chosen because it is substantive, constrained (no standard ViT recipes apply), and unfamiliar to a general LLM whose training data is dominated by spatial attention.

The programme is split into **three phases** separated by human-declared transitions: Phase 1 (CIFAR-10, 5.8M params, no literature), Phase 1b (CIFAR-10, +literature access + code editing), Phase 2 (CIFAR-100, 22M params, larger budget), Phase 3 (ImageNet-1K, 22M params, +multi-GPU configs). The agent does not choose transitions; it operates autonomously within each phase.

**Analytical method.** The per-hypothesis research log is treated as a dataset and analysed along three axes: success-rate evolution, distribution of accuracy gain across hypotheses (innovation attribution), and productivity changes at tool-access and scale boundaries. A separate qualitative behavioural analysis identifies six recurring hypothesis-generation patterns and decomposes the greedy/incremental bias into workflow-induced vs. inherent components. The authors are explicit that this is one contiguous trajectory, not a randomised ablation across agents or problems.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline accuracy (Table 4).** The agent's architecture improved from a **CIFAR-10 baseline of 69.67% top-1 (100 epochs, 1.72M params)** to a **Phase-1b champion of 96.59% (100ep, 5.81M params; 97.25% at 300ep)** — a total of **+26.92 pp**. On CIFAR-100 the Phase-2 champion reached **83.37% (100ep) / 85.07% (300ep)** at 22.15M params. On ImageNet-1K the Phase-3 champion (H3-12, a Linformer rank-128 stage-3 block) reached **77.91% at 100ep, a +0.26 pp delta over the 77.65% Phase-3 baseline (which itself reaches 79.00% at 300ep)**.
- **Innovation attribution (Table 6).** A single early hypothesis — H1, adding an FFN/MLP to channel-attention blocks — contributed **+21.41 pp, i.e. 79.5% of the total +26.92 pp CIFAR-10 gain**. The next largest contributions were training-recipe tweaks (+2.58 pp, 9.6%) and a depthwiseshortcut backbone (+0.81 pp). Every other accepted hypothesis contributed <1 pp.
- **Three-regime productivity structure (Table 5).** Phase 1 decomposes into Discovery (H1–H6, **67% success, +5.71 pp avg gain/success**), Exploitation (H7–H13, **71% success, +0.32 pp avg**), and Saturation (H14–H42, **21% success, +0.17 pp avg**). The saturation regime is 29 hypotheses with only 6 accepted.
- **Tool-access phase transition (Table 7).** Across the Phase 1 → Phase 1b boundary (granting literature access + authorising code edits), the fraction of hypotheses making code-level (vs config-only) changes jumped from **2/42 = 5% to 16/21 = 76%**; novel mechanisms introduced rose from 2 to 8; distinct papers cited went from 0 to 24+. Average gain per successful hypothesis *excluding H1* stayed similar (+0.29 pp vs +0.19 pp), so the transition changed *what kind* of hypothesis was proposed, not the per-hit gain magnitude.
- **Per-phase success rates (Figure 3).** Rolling 10-hypothesis success rate: **Phase 1 = 36%, Phase 1b = 38%, Phase 2 = 32%, Phase 3 = 12%**. The Phase 3 collapse reflects 18 hypotheses yielding only 2 accepted improvements, both narrow.
- **Cross-scale anti-findings (Table 8).** Three training-recipe decisions *reversed sign* between CIFAR-10 (5.8M) and CIFAR-100 (22M): Mixup (−0.68 pp off → +0.77 pp on), Label smoothing (−0.13 pp off → +0.30 pp on), Drop-path removal (+0.46 pp → −0.60 pp). The agent did not auto-flag the regime change; re-validation required an explicit human nudge.
- **Independent rediscoveries.** The agent converged experimentally on single-head > multi-head channel attention (H3 −1.13 pp, H58 −0.36 pp, confirmed twice — matching XCiT), 5×5 Q/K kernel sweet spot at 7×7 feature maps (matching ConvNeXt-style receptive-field correspondence), deeper > wider at fixed params (matching EfficientNet compound scaling), and that FFN removal costs −1.07 pp (H39) — i.e. FFN is load-bearing even in CA-only models.
- **Compute footprint (Table 1).** ~10 weeks wall-clock, ~150 jobs submitted, ~130 substantive runs (≥5 min), ~2,400 GPU-hours total, median run ≈2.5 h, longest single run ≈6 days. LLM token consumption on the order of tens of millions (budget-based estimate, not systematically logged).

## Authors & Credibility

- **Aon Safdar** and **Mohamed Saadeldin**, both at University College Dublin, Ireland. The paper has been *submitted to the IEEE for possible publication* (copyright-transfer notice on the cover page); the arXiv version is v1, 3 Aug 2026. No prior publication history on this exact topic is cited for the authors.
- The work is positioned honestly as a **single-run case study**, not a benchmark or a general law. The authors repeatedly caveat that evidence is "observational contrasts drawn from one trajectory, not randomised ablations" and frame the five workflow recommendations explicitly as "testable hypotheses for future autonomous-research systems" rather than validated prescriptions.
- Credibility is strengthened by a **complete, enumerated human-intervention record** (Table 2, every strategic intervention listed with date and type), an appendix listing six concrete autonomy failure modes and how each was fixed once (Section A.8), and a candid cost section (Section A.9, ~2.4k GPU-hours, tens of millions of tokens, 1–2 hours/day of passive human oversight).
- Anonymised supplementary materials (research log, per-hypothesis CSV, figure-generation scripts, Slurm templates, YAML champion specs) accompany the submission; full training code is withheld for double-blind anonymity and promised under a permanent DOI on acceptance.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine. Long-horizon (multi-week, ~100-hypothesis) behavioural case studies of a single LLM acting as *all* research roles are rare; the closest comparables (AI Scientist, MLE-bench, MLAgentBench, Agent Laboratory, FunSearch/AlphaEvolve, Boiko et al.'s chemistry agent) are either short-horizon, multi-agent, or focus on program synthesis / manuscript generation rather than structural architecture research where the agent writes and rewrites model code. The bias decomposition (Table 10) and the "tool access as phase transition" framing are fresh.
- **Rigour.** Mixed and the authors admit it. The single biggest weakness is the **tool-access confound** they themselves flag (Section 8): the Phase 1 → Phase 1b transition bundled literature access, code-editing authorisation, *and* a brief amendment encouraging novel mechanisms simultaneously. The headline claim that "literature access broke the saturation wall" cannot cleanly separate these. The bias decomposition into workflow-induced vs. LLM-inherent is qualitative, not a controlled ablation. There is **no counterfactual human control** and **no run-to-run replicate** — a different stochastic trajectory could produce a different champion.
- **Reproducibility.** Reasonable but partial. The loop, instruction documents, per-hypothesis CSV, and figure scripts are released; the *full training code is withheld* for anonymity, so the architectural numbers cannot be independently reproduced until acceptance. The authors explicitly disclaim exact run-level numerical reproducibility (LLM stochasticity) and predict only that the *qualitative* three-regime structure will recur — a testable but untested prediction. Six concrete autonomy failure modes and their fixes are documented (Section A.8), which is unusually useful for replication.
- **Significance.** Methodologically high; architecturally low (by design — the ImageNet champion is sub-SOTA at 77.91%). The behavioural findings (saturation plateaus, success-chasing, post-failure risk aversion, cross-scale anti-transfer) are exactly the phenomena invisible at short-horizon benchmark timescales, and the workflow-vs-capability framing is actionable for the autonomous-research community.
- **Red flags / specific concerns.**
  - The **+0.26 pp ImageNet delta** (77.65% → 77.91%) is within plausible run-to-run variance for a single seed; the authors did not run the 300-epoch validation of the Phase-3 champion and did not budget multi-seed replications of accepted hypotheses, so the Phase-3 "champion" is fragile evidence.
  - The 79.5% concentration of CIFAR-10 gain in H1 (add an FFN) is a slightly underwhelming "discovery" — adding an FFN/MLP to an attention block is one of the most well-known transformer moves, so the headline "+27 pp" mostly measures how weak the *baseline* was (a CA block *without* an FFN), not how capable the agent is.
  - Phase 3 was closed by the team after only 18 hypotheses at a self-detected saturation, which the authors use as evidence for the saturation phenomenon but which also truncates the ImageNet story.
  - Evaluation protocols differ across phases (augmentation family, epoch budget, dataset difficulty); some cross-scale anti-findings may be protocol artefacts rather than scale effects, as Section 8 concedes.

## Strengths

- Unusual intellectual honesty: limitations, confounds, and failure modes are enumerated rather than buried, and the paper is framed as "scoped observations from this case study rather than as general laws."
- Complete, auditable human-intervention record (Table 2) and a candid cost breakdown — rare in autonomous-agent papers.
- The bias decomposition (workflow-induced vs. LLM-inherent, Table 10) is a useful conceptual frame that other groups can operationalise and test.
- The cross-scale anti-findings (Table 8) and the independent rediscoveries (Table 9) are concrete, falsifiable, and directly useful to practitioners building similar loops.
- The five recommended workflow interventions are low-cost and map cleanly onto observed failure modes, making them genuinely actionable.

## Limitations

- **Single agent, single problem, single run.** N=1 along every axis. The specific quantitative findings (H1 = 79.5% of gain, the saturation length, the rediscovery list) may be problem- or trajectory-specific.
- **No counterfactual human control** running the same problem on the same compute; comparisons to typical human research behaviour are indicative, not controlled.
- **Workflow-design confounds** — the imposed greedy commit-or-discard rule directly produces the greedy behaviour observed, so workflow-vs-capability attribution is not clean.
- **Tool-access confound** — Phase 1 → Phase 1b bundled literature access, code-edit permission, and a brief amendment together; the productivity jump cannot be cleanly attributed to any single change.
- **Evaluation-protocol divergence** across phases; some cross-scale anti-findings may be protocol-driven rather than scale-driven.
- **No run-to-run replicates**; LLM stochasticity means a different seed could yield a different champion and a different behavioural trace.
- The Phase-3 ImageNet result is a fragile +0.26 pp delta without multi-seed or 300-epoch validation of the champion.

## Relevance

对研究"LLM agent长周期自主科研workflow"和"agent行为学/可靠性"方向高度相关：它定量刻画了饱和墙、贪婪锚定、跨尺度反迁移等只在长周期才暴露的失败模式，并给出了可直接落地的loop设计建议（diversified proposal、moonshot预算、显式fork、regime-aware re-validation），与当前LLM-for-science、AI Scientist、self-improving agent等课题形成互补。对我们设计可靠的agent研究流水线（尤其涉及多次实验、跨尺度、需要抗"成功追逐"偏置的场景）有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the three-regime productivity structure (discovery → saturation → tool-triggered recovery) generalise beyond architecture search to other long-horizon ML research problems (e.g. training-recipe design, data-curation pipelines), or is it an artefact of the channel-attention design space having a clean six-hypothesis discovery regime?
- How much of the Phase-1 → Phase-1b productivity jump is attributable to literature access specifically versus the simultaneous authorisation of code edits and the brief amendment? A controlled ablation staging each change independently is the obvious next experiment.
- Would the proposed "budgeted moonshot hypotheses" (every k-th hypothesis must introduce a new mechanism) actually beat pure greedy hill-climbing on the same problem, and at what k? This is the most directly testable of the five recommendations.
- How sensitive are the behavioural findings (greedy anchoring, post-failure risk aversion) to the underlying LLM? Running the same workflow with several frontier models on the same problem would separate agent-capability effects from workflow-induced effects.
- Is the cross-scale sign-flip of mixup/label-smoothing/drop-path a genuine scale effect or an artefact of the differing evaluation protocols across phases? A controlled re-run holding protocol fixed while scaling params/dataset would resolve this.
- How does a matched human researcher perform on the same problem, compute, and hypothesis budget? The authors identify this as the single most informative missing experiment.
