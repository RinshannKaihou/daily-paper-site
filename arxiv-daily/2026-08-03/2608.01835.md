> **一句话总结：** 作者提出"行为流形分析"框架，用稀疏的行为锚定坐标 (BACs) 和 ACT/NOC 两种特征空间构建低维行为图谱，并在 23 个 LLM 上证明 SFT 倾向于"改写"行为几何（projector overlap 降至 0.745、主角度 25.07°），而 DPO/RL 则主要在继承的几何中"重新加权"行为（overlap 保持 0.939、角度仅 7.64°）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces "behavioral manifold analysis," a geometric framework that lifts sparse behavior-associated FFN coordinates (Behavioral Anchor Coordinates, BACs) into low-rank ACT (activation) and NOC (normalized output-contribution) charts, then tracks these charts on the Grassmannian across 23 Llama/Gemma/Mistral/Qwen models and through controlled SFT, DPO, and SFT→DPO post-training of Llama-3.1-8B. The central finding is a clear objective-level asymmetry: SFT rewrites behavioral geometry (mean projector overlap ≈0.745, mean principal angle ≈25.07°), whereas DPO/reward optimization largely preserves the inherited chart (overlap ≈0.939, angle ≈7.64°), changing behavior through occupancy shifts and readout gain instead.

## 核心贡献

- **首次给出"行为条件化、因果验证、可跨训练追踪"的几何对象**：通过稀疏 BAC 选择 + 低秩局部 chart，把抽象的"行为相关表征区域"形式化为 Grassmannian 上的轨迹，可跨 checkpoint 跟踪，弥补了既有 SFT vs RL 工作停留在行为率或全局谱几何层面的空白。
- **提出两种互补的特征空间**：ACT（运行时激活，回答"是否处于该状态"）与 NOC（归一化输出贡献，回答"该坐标对实现层更新的功能性贡献有多强"），二者揭示出"频繁激活"与"强因果贡献"是可分离的两类属性。
- **统一刻画"几何改写 vs 几何重加权"三件套**：用 projector overlap / 主角度 / anchored Fisher retention 量化改写，用 occupancy (G_occ) 与 causal gain (G_causal) 量化对继承 chart 的使用方式，给出 post-training 改变行为的三种机制（移动 chart / 改变占据 / 改变 readout）。
- **在 23 个模型上得到核心结论的强证据**：跨 Llama、Gemma、Mistral、Qwen 家族，行为都被稀疏坐标支撑（repetition BACs 中位数仅 34 个）；经 CCA 对齐后的 chart 显示 NOC 空间暴露出更跨架构一致的功能核心。
- **通过受控 post-training 实验锁定"目标函数级别"的不对称性**：在固定 base、数据、评测、行为方向后，SFT 与 DPO/SFT→DPO 表现出可重复的"改写 vs 重加权"差异，且 SFT→DPO 不能恢复 Base chart，说明 DPO 是在 SFT 诱导的几何内继续优化。

## Key Contributions

- A behavior-conditioned, causally validated geometric object ("behavioral manifold") that can be tracked through post-training: sparse Behavioral Anchor Coordinates (BACs) are selected by an L1-logistic classifier and lifted into a low-rank local chart on the Grassmannian Gr(k,D), with formal decomposition of behavior change into chart movement, occupancy shift, and readout gain.
- Two complementary coordinate spaces — ACT (raw activation, "is the coordinate active") and NOC (normalized output contribution, "how strongly does the coordinate align with the realized MLP update") — that decouple "frequently occupied" from "functionally strong," formalized as a four-quadrant mechanism taxonomy.
- A 23-model cross-architecture atlas (Llama, Gemma, Mistral, Qwen, 0.5B–70B) showing repetition and sycophancy are supported by sparse coordinate scaffolds (median 16 BACs for repetition; 62 for sycophancy) with highly compressed charts (mean k_0.9 ≈ 9 components for repetition), alignable across families via CCA, with NOC exposing a more architecture-robust shared core than ACT.
- Controlled Llama-3.1-8B post-training (SFT, DPO-from-Base, SFT→DPO) for both behaviors and both directions that establishes the central asymmetry: SFT rewrites geometry, DPO reweights it, and SFT→DPO does not restore the Base chart.
- Open-source code release (https://github.com/ronglingze/Manifold-Analysis) and a leakage-prevention protocol (random-coordinate controls, label permutation tests, held-out linear probing).

## Methodology

The framework operates in five stages. (1) **Behavior-conditioned state extraction**: for each behavior b (repetition or sycophancy) the authors isolate behavior-relevant token windows and extract per-coordinate features. (2) **Dual feature spaces**: ACT uses raw activations ā_{ℓ,j}(x); NOC uses the signed, scale-normalized alignment ν_{ℓ,t,j}(x) = ⟨u_{ℓ,t,j}, m_{ℓ,t}⟩ / (∥m_{ℓ,t}∥² + ε) between a coordinate's output vector and the layer's realized MLP update — a normalized decomposition of the layer's update energy. (3) **Sparse scaffold selection**: an L1-regularized logistic classifier identifies behavior-discriminative Behavioral Anchor Coordinates (BACs); a Fisher direction v_Fisher ∝ Σ_w⁻¹(μ₁−μ₀) is used as a robustness check. (4) **Low-rank local chart**: PCA on the standardized BAC features yields a rank-k basis U_{θ,b} on the Grassmannian; the local behavioral model p_θ(y_b=1|x) ≈ σ(β_{θ,b}^⊤ U_{θ,b}^⊤ φ̄_θ(x) + τ_{θ,b}) is formally derived via Taylor expansion of the log-odds around the class-midpoint, with explicit curvature and chart-residual error bounds. (5) **Quantification**: chart movement is measured by projector overlap S(a,b) = tr(P_aP_b)/r*, mean principal angle, projection distance, and anchored Fisher retention R_{0→t} = G_{0→t}/(G(t)+ε); chart use is measured by occupancy G_occ (behavior-positive minus behavior-negative second-moment gap) and causal gain G_causal (finite-difference sensitivity of the behavioral logit to intervention strength α). Cross-architecture alignment uses CCA on matched instances. Causal validation runs at three levels (neuron-level scaling, subspace projection/amplification, direction-level injection), and post-training trajectories are followed checkpoint-by-checkpoint with geometric velocity, path length, and curvature (excess path length κ).

## Results & Highlights

- **Sparse scaffolds are sufficient and behavior-predictive**: across the 23 models, repetition needs only 20–61 BACs (median 34), and the initial held-out probe reaches **on-policy repetition classification accuracies of 0.9658–0.9879** (e.g., Mistral-Small-3.1-24B-Base 0.9658; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct 0.9879) — vs. random-coordinate controls whose classifier correlation is only 0.367 (ACT) / 0.317 (NOC) versus 0.938 / 0.974 on real BACs.
- **Charts are highly compressed and partially cross-architecture**: mean Universal Fisher G_uniF = 7.16 (ACT) / 7.02 (NOC), median k_0.9 = 8 components (repetition) and 9.17 (sycophancy); NOC primary CCA across all 23 models is **0.974** (ACT 0.938), and NOC top-2-PC transfer reaches **0.958** within-family and **0.950** cross-family — about 0.2 above the corresponding ACT numbers, supporting the claim that NOC exposes a more architecture-robust shared functional core.
- **Controlled Llama-3.1-8B post-training shows the headline asymmetry**: DPO-from-Base retains mean projector overlap **0.939** with mean principal angle **7.64°**, whereas SFT moves the chart to overlap **0.745** and angle **25.07°** — a roughly 3× larger angular displacement. SFT→DPO stays in the SFT-shifted regime and does not return toward the Base chart, ruling out the "DPO restores base geometry" explanation.
- **Behavior moves without chart movement under reward optimization**: in the controlled table, DPO up/down changes the N-gram repetition rate to 0.918/0.885 (from SFT's 0.968/0.920) and sycophancy preference to 0.370/0.342 while keeping projector overlap essentially at 0.998–0.999 and Fisher retention near 1.0; in contrast, SFT up moves repetition to 0.968 at overlap 0.938 and SFT down moves it to 0.920 at overlap 0.942 — i.e., the behavior-level change is comparable but the geometric cost differs by >0.05–0.25 in overlap.
- **Causal interventions confirm the scaffold is functionally relevant**: subspace and BAC-level steering on Llama-3.1-8B moves repetition rate from ≈0.78 (no intervention) up to ≈0.93 and down to ≈0.40 over steering strength α ∈ [−2, 2], while PPL stays in a controlled band — showing the low-dimensional chart is not just a predictive compression.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: High. Prior work compares SFT and RL via memorization/generalization, trajectory topology, or global spectral geometry (Chu 2025, Yue 2025, Matsutani 2025, Galichin 2026); none provides a *behavior-conditioned, causally validated, per-checkpoint* geometric object. The ACT/NOC split and the four-quadrant "mechanism vs. occupancy" taxonomy are a genuine conceptual contribution over H-Neurons (Gao 2025), Contrastive Activation Addition (Rimsky 2024), and knowledge-neuron work.
- **Rigor**: Strong. The local linear model is formally derived (Appendix A) with explicit curvature and chart-residual bounds; leakage controls include random-coordinate baselines, label permutation, rank/direction sensitivity, and held-out probing; cross-model claims use CCA reliability weighting.
- **Reproducibility**: Likely good. Code is released; the 23-model list is explicit (Gemma-2/3, Qwen2.5/3.5, Mistral-7B/Nemo/Small, Llama-3.1/3.3 with base + instruct pairs); controlled runs are single-base (Llama-3.1-8B) with stated data construction. However, full per-checkpoint weights and the exact 766,469-line sycophancy dataset are only described at archive granularity, and the paper acknowledges that the archived cleaning script does not retain a category-wise breakdown of the 10,549 removed records.
- **Significance**: Conceptually clarifying — it gives a mechanistic vocabulary ("rewrite vs reweight") that subsumes several prior SFT-vs-RL debates. Whether the framework generalizes beyond two hand-picked behaviors and to RLHF/RLVR proper (not just DPO) is the open question.
- **Red flags / specific weak points**:
  - The two chosen behaviors (repetition as a decoding pathology, sycophancy as a preference failure) are *deliberately* far apart but still only two; generalization to e.g. hallucination, toxicity, or reasoning-style behaviors is unproven.
  - "Reward optimization" is operationalized almost entirely as **DPO** (no RLHF with a learned reward model, no RLVR with verifiable rewards in the controlled experiments), so the headline "SFT vs reward optimization" framing is stronger than the controlled evidence supports; readers should mentally substitute "SFT vs DPO."
  - The controlled experiment is single-base (Llama-3.1-8B); the cross-family claims rest on the uncontrolled public base→instruct pairs that the authors themselves flag as confounded by data mixture, duration, and implementation.
  - The headline projector-overlap gap (0.939 vs 0.745) is computed at *matched rank* and the authors note that native Fisher strength is task-scale dependent; results in NOC vs ACT also differ in detail, and the paper acknowledges occupancy and causal gain are "more condition-, representation-, and estimator-dependent" — i.e., the cleaner half of the asymmetry is the geometric side, while the "how the chart is used" side is noisier.
  - The sycophancy dataset was built from a vLLM generation pipeline with a known limitation: the prompt-level instruction "CRITICAL: DO NOT repeat the starting sentence" was applied during generation, which the authors explicitly flag as a known limitation of the archived pipeline.

## Strengths

- Provides a unified, mathematically grounded geometric object that can be tracked through post-training, directly addressing a real ambiguity in interpreting behavior-rate changes.
- The ACT/NOC distinction is genuinely illuminating: it cleanly separates "frequently active" from "functionally causal," resolving confounds present in pure activation-based interpretability.
- Large-scale, multi-family evaluation (23 models) plus controlled single-base trajectories gives both ecological breadth and causal-style rigor.
- Honest about its own boundaries: explicit leakage controls, explicit acknowledgment that occupancy/gain are noisier than the geometric signal, and explicit flagging of the archived-pipeline limitation.

## Limitations

- Only two behaviors studied; generalization to reasoning, hallucination, toxicity, or multi-turn behaviors is unproven.
- "Reward optimization" in the controlled experiment is DPO only — no RLHF (learned reward + PPO) and no RLVR (verifiable rewards), despite the title/abstract using the broader term.
- Controlled-trajectory claim rests on a single base model (Llama-3.1-8B); cross-family conclusions rely on confounded public base→instruct pairs.
- Behavior charts are local linear approximations; the authors themselves report saturation and mild reversal of dose-response curves at high steering strengths, confirming the chart is a local, not global, model.
- Sycophancy dataset generation has a flagged prompt-level contamination issue, and the archived artifacts lack per-example logs.
- The asymmetry is cleanest in the geometric metrics; the occupancy/gain side, which is what actually carries "reweighting," is more estimator- and condition-dependent.

## Relevance

对"机制可解释性 + post-training 比较"方向的研究者高度相关：它把"行为率变化"提升为可追踪的几何对象，为 SFT 与 RL/DPO 的机制差异提供了统一的几何语言，可作为后续研究把 RLHF/RLVR、reasoning 行为、safety 行为纳入同一框架的起点。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the "SFT rewrites, reward optimization reweights" asymmetry survive when reward optimization is true RLHF (PPO + learned reward model) or RLVR with verifiable rewards, rather than DPO?
- Can the behavioral manifold framework scale beyond the two studied behaviors — e.g., to hallucination, chain-of-thought style, refusal, or toxicity — and do those behaviors also admit sparse, low-rank, cross-family-alignable charts?
- The paper shows SFT→DPO does not restore the Base chart; can an explicit "chart-regularized" DPO loss (penalizing projector-overlap drift from the base) recover base geometry while still changing behavior?
- How does the chart trajectory correlate with the "RL heals SFT-induced OOD forgetting" finding of Jin et al. 2025 — does reward optimization's geometry preservation explain the healing?
- Could the ACT/NOC four-quadrant taxonomy be used diagnostically at training time to predict which behaviors are "latent but strong" (NOC-high, ACT-low) and therefore at risk of surfacing after deployment?
