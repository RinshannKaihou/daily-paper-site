Lottery Tickets Are Not Deployment Tickets

LT Deployment Audit Behavioral Compatibility Audit for Sparse Replacement BP-LT

Matching a dense model's accuracy doesn't certify a sparse lottery ticket as a safe drop-in replacement behind a frozen threshold.

Key Metric
7–10%AcceptFlip

Of accept/review decisions flip when an accuracy-matched lottery ticket replaces the dense incumbent under its frozen, 10%-review confidence gate (CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, |ΔAcc| ≤ 3pp settings). The paper's largest single comparison (80% sparsity, large candidate pool) puts LT's AcceptFlip at 8.47%.

Why This Matters

Anyone swapping a pruned model into a threshold-gated pipeline — moderation, fraud, risk — needs more than matched accuracy to certify a safe replacement.

Critical Edge

The audit never retrains an independent dense challenger, so it can't separate sparsity-specific incompatibility from ordinary retraining churn.

Methodology

Audit the routing decision, not the classifier

The setup: a dense classifier f_D already runs in production behind a confidence gate τ_D, frozen at the empirical 10% quantile of its validation confidences (~10% of inputs routed to human review, 90% auto-accepted). A sparse candidate f is only eligible if it falls within a declared accuracy band of f_D (Δ_acc ≤ 1.5pp) — and τ_D is never recalibrated for the candidate, matching how a real drop-in swap would happen.

For each in-band candidate the paper computes a clean-accuracy-excluded behavior vector b⁻acc — calibration (ECE, NLL, Brier), OOD response (AUROC, IDFAR95), threshold-policy coordinates (ReviewRate, AutoErr, AcceptFlip_τD), and reliability coordinates (worst-class accuracy, MIA-AUC, CKA distance, corruption accuracy) — standardized per protocol and aggregated into a single RMS Behavioral-Compatibility Distance D_BE⁻acc.

Theorem 1 proves this distance is necessary: for any target flip rate α, one can construct a candidate with identical top-1 predictions and identical accuracy to f_D yet an AcceptFlip rate of exactly α. BP-LT then picks, training-free, the in-band pool candidate minimizing D_BE⁻acc plus a quadratic accuracy-gap penalty.

*LT accuracy exceeds the dense reference, yet D_BE⁻acc and AcceptFlip both remain high — beating the dense model on accuracy is no guarantee of behavioral compatibility.

The large-pool 80%-sparsity row is the paper's sharpest counter-example: BP-LT cuts D_BE⁻acc by 11% but AcceptFlip ticks up, not down — in the authors' words, BP-LT is "a multi-coordinate screening rule, not a policy-restoration objective."

Corruption robustness erodes too: RobAcc falls 65.71%→64.14% (–1.57pp) at 80% sparsity on CIFAR-10 R18 despite only a 0.16pp clean-accuracy gap; WideResNet-28-2 @ 80% loses 3.54pp RobAcc while staying inside its accuracy-recovery band.

Strengths

What holds up

  • Reframes LT/pruning evaluation around a frozen, already-configured decision policy rather than standalone accuracy — a genuinely more deployment-relevant question than prior "beyond-accuracy" benchmarks.
  • Two non-trivial theorems (non-identifiability + local threshold-amplification) that explain the observed flip rates rather than merely correlate with them.
  • Large, stratified empirical sweep: 56 experiment groups, 846 result rows, five-plus architectures, five datasets, multiple sparsification families (LT, IMP, SNIP, RigL, random-mask controls).
  • Unusually honest self-critique: explicitly flags the missing dense-to-dense control and BP-LT's lack of guarantees rather than overclaiming a fix.
  • Sensitivity checks with alternative distance formulations (rank distance, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage Mahalanobis) confirm the conclusion isn't an artifact of the standardization choice.
Limitations

What's missing

  • No independently retrained dense-to-dense control — can't isolate sparsity-specific incompatibility from ordinary retraining churn (acknowledged by the authors).
  • Scope limited to unstructured pruning in vision classification; no language models, no ImageNet-scale evaluation, no structured sparsity, no latency/energy measurement.
  • BP-LT only selects from an already-trained pool — it doesn't construct compatibility, and in the large-pool 80% case its AcceptFlip is slightly worse than plain LT (8.54% vs 8.47%).
  • D_BE⁻acc is an unsigned aggregate distance, not a signed harm/utility measure — production use would need application-specific signed thresholds layered on top.
  • Validated at a single operating point (the 10% review-rate quantile) per protocol; systematic threshold/budget sweeps are left as future work.
Questions for Further Exploration

Where this needs to go next

01

How much of the 7–10% AcceptFlip rate would appear between two independently-trained dense models (same architecture, different seed) as a "retraining churn" baseline?

02

Does the threshold-amplification coefficient κ_τD correlate with cheaply measurable properties of the confidence distribution near τ_D?

03

Can a training-time objective that directly regularizes for low AcceptFlip close the residual gap, and at what cost to accuracy or compression?

04

Does the behavioral-compatibility framework scale to ImageNet-1k models, token-level LLM confidence gates, or structured/quantization-based compression?

05

Would simply recalibrating (not retraining) the confidence gate on the sparse candidate meaningfully reduce AcceptFlip at lower operational cost?
