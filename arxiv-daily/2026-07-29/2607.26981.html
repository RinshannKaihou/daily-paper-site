Why This Matters

Gives auditors a ground-truth-free way to catch directional bias in LLM probability judgment, and traces its sign to alignment — not scale.

Critical Edge

The same models, on the same scenarios, flip bias sign entirely depending on task framing — undercutting the paper's own "universal bias" headline.

Methodology

Inverted pairs: ask for both sides, measure the gap

  • Core measurement: for each scenario, elicit P(positive outcome) → s⁺ and P(negative outcome) → s⁻ in separate queries. A coherent model has s⁺ + s⁻ = 100; Skew = s⁺ − (100 − s⁻) captures the signed gap (positive = optimistic, negative = pessimistic), decomposable into δ⁺ = mean(s⁺)−50 and δ⁻ = mean(s⁻)−50.
  • Four tracks: Track A (calibration control — stated base rates, expects Skew≈0 as a sanity check), Track B (primary — 60 naturalistic scenarios with no stated base rate, 6 domains), Track C (recommend action vs. inaction), Track D (salience of opportunity vs. risk).
  • Quality filters: items with cross-model variance <3 excluded as too unambiguous; pairs with >20pp average inconsistency flagged for review.
  • Robustness interventions: narrative framing, perspective shift (third-person / second-person / "friend" — rules out sycophancy), anchoring gradient, and explicit self-debiasing prompts, all holding the scenario fixed.
  • Causal design: 11 architecture-matched base-vs-chat pairs (Qwen2.5/ Qwen3, Llama-3.1/3.2, plus Gemma-2-2b & Mistral-Small-24B single-pair pilots) holding architecture and pre-training constant, varying only post-training — isolating alignment from scale.
  • Scale: 16 headline models, 5–10 independent runs at temperature 0.7 (3 exceptions at 1.0); bootstrap 95% CIs and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Bonferroni-corrected across 16 tests.

  • Calibration control (Track A): on 15 items with explicit stated base rates, all models report Skew≈0.00 — confirms the Track B bias is specific to judgment under genuine uncertainty, not a general failure to sum probabilities.
  • Robustness: direction survives prompt-detail ablation (6/6 combinations), temperature change (0.7→1.0, ≤0.8pp shift), and perspective shift (|Δ|<2, ruling out sycophancy). Explicit self-debiasing (GPT-5.4: +49.5→+46.0) absorbs only about a third of the baseline bias.
  • Cross-lingual variance (17 models × 6 languages): inter-model σ = 7.2pp vs. inter-language σ = 1.5pp — a 4.7× ratio. Which model you use matters far more than which language you query it in.
  • Task-framing reversal (7-model pilot, Track C vs. D): Track D (salience of opportunity vs. risk) is uniformly optimistic (+16 to +42pp); Track C (recommend action vs. inaction) is uniformly pessimistic in 6/7 models (−13 to −69pp) — same models, same scenarios, opposite sign.
  • Dose-response monotonicity is alignment-resistant: conjunction-axiom violations are near zero (0–10%) across 5 fully-covered models, but dose-response monotonicity violations stay high (27–47%) for all of them — post-training fixes single-step probability coherence far more readily than multi-step reasoning.
Strengths
  • Clean, ground-truth-free Skew metric isolates the direction of probability bias, where prior calibration metrics (e.g. ECE) only capture unsigned magnitude.
  • Strong robustness battery: prompt ablation, temperature, perspective-shift, and explicit self-debiasing all preserve the core finding.
  • The base-vs-chat causal probe is a well-designed natural experiment most alignment papers lack, directly implicating post-training over scale.
  • Broad coverage: 16 models × 8 providers × 6 domains × 10 languages, with full dataset and response release planned for replication.
  • Unusually honest self-critique — separates causal from merely observational evidence and surfaces the Track C/D reversal that complicates its own headline claim.
Limitations
  • Scenarios are author-constructed and self-filtered by the same team — no independent or crowdsourced validation set.
  • The family-specific causal claim rests on only 2 well-replicated families (Qwen 5/5, Llama 4/4); Gemma-2-2b and Mistral-Small-24B are single-pair pilots, not statistically powered.
  • No matched human baseline — Skew is validated only against internal complementarity, not against actual human forecasting accuracy.
  • 4 of 10 languages (DE, FR, HI, JA) confound translated scenarios with English-language system prompts, a limitation the authors flag but don't resolve.
  • The Track C/D reversal — which undercuts the "universal bias" framing — is only a 7-model, 10-scenario pilot, far smaller than the 16-model, 60-scenario Track B headline.
Questions for Further Exploration
  • 01Would reward-model probing reveal which specific RLHF component (truthfulness vs. helpfulness) drives Qwen's pessimism-compression vs. Llama's optimism-amplification?
  • 02Does the measured Skew translate into worse real-world decisions in forecasting markets or resource allocation, or does it wash out under aggregation?
  • 03Would scaling the Track C/D pilot to the full 16-model, 60-scenario design change the task-framing-reversal conclusion?
  • 04Could a human baseline on the same 60 scenarios (matching Weinstein 1980's paradigm) give an absolute reference point beyond axiom-relative Skew?
  • 05Does dose-response monotonicity violation (27–47%, alignment-resistant) correlate with Skew magnitude or sign per-model, or are they orthogonal failure modes?
