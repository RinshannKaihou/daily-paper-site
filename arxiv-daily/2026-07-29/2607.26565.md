> **一句话总结：** 论文把冻结视觉编码器逐层计算出的"表征轨迹"（而非仅最终表征）当作一种独立的可靠性证据，用类别路径分解出"类别共享搬运"与"样本特异创新"两部分，在 OpenOOD v1.5 的 152 个非饱和基准组合上为 Mahalanobis++/Relative Mahalanobis/X-Mahalanobis/kNN 分别带来 4.33/8.67/2.69/8.75 点的平均 FPR95 下降，并在 12 个数据集、6 种架构上使 71/72 个"模型-数据集"组合的分类准确率平均提升 4.41 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** This paper treats the full sequence of intermediate layer states a frozen vision encoder produces (the "representation trajectory") as a distinct reliability signal, decomposing each layer update into class-coherent transport plus sample-specific residual innovation, and validates via matched/shuffled/history/gap controls that this path carries real cross-depth continuity beyond the final representation. On the balanced OpenOOD v1.5 grid (190 backbone-benchmark pairs, 38 frozen checkpoints), fusing an ID-only trajectory-surprise score with strong final-state OOD detectors reduces mean FPR95 in 131/152 (Mahalanobis++), 142/152 (Relative Mahalanobis), 106/152 (X-Mahalanobis), and 127/152 (kNN) non-saturated cases, by 4.33/8.67/2.69/8.75 points respectively, and on Table 1's 12-dataset recognition suite, fusing trajectory-probe evidence with a final-state linear probe improves 71/72 backbone-dataset cells with a mean gain of +4.41 accuracy points (up to +9.81 on Stanford Cars for ResNet-50, and gains for every one of the 6 evaluated backbones, from +1.43 for CLIP to +6.41 for DeiT3-B).

## 核心贡献

- 将视觉编码器的前向计算路径 τ(x) = (z₁(x),...,z_L(x)) 形式化为独立于最终表征、置信度之外的"第三种可靠性对象"，并用 KL 散度链式法则（Eq. 5）证明：只要最终表征不能唯一决定轨迹，条件熵中就必然存在可被路径证据利用的残差信息。
- 提出类别路径分解：每层更新 z_{l+1}-z_l = (μ^c_{l+1}-μ^c_l)（类别共享搬运）+ (r_{l+1}-r_l)（样本特异创新），把与类别相关的系统性运动和样本个体的偏离残差分开建模。
- 通过配对轨迹 vs. 身份打乱、历史信息、层间距衰减、方向性等 5 组对照实验（Fig. 3），在 16 组 checkpoint-数据集组合上证明轨迹残差具有可预测的跨深度连续性，而非任意的层状态集合。
- 提出 ID-only 的"轨迹惊异度"分数（一个在残差状态上训练的共享 MLP，Eq. 6）与 OOD 检测的最终态分数做保守融合（固定权重 0.3，Eq. 7），以及在原生模块更新上训练线性探针并与最终态探针融合的分类流程；两者均只用 ID 标签数据训练，不接触 OOD 样本。
- 在 42 个跨架构族（Plain/Masked ViT、层级 Transformer、自监督基础模型、CNN、视觉-语言模型）的冻结 checkpoint 上系统评测，发现不同架构族把"类别证据"和"表征变化"分配到不同深度，且轨迹信息的价值与具体检测器/架构组合强相关而非泛泛提升。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the forward pass τ(x) = (z₁(x), …, z_L(x)) as a reliability object distinct from the final representation and confidence, and uses a KL chain-rule identity (Eq. 5) to show precisely where and when residual path information — beyond what the endpoint reveals — can exist.
- Decomposes every native layer update into class-coherent transport (movement of the class route μ_l^c) and sample-specific innovation (residual r_l = z_l - μ_l^c), separating what is shared across a class from what is unique to an image.
- Validates trajectory continuity with five controls — matched vs. identity-shuffled pairs, preceding-state history, layer-gap decay, and direction — showing matched trajectories win in all 16 checkpoint-dataset comparisons tested, establishing the path is not an arbitrary collection of independent layer snapshots.
- Introduces an ID-only "transition-surprise" score D(x) (a shared MLP predicting the next residual state, conditioned on a learned depth embedding) that is fused with final-state OOD scores via a fixed, deliberately conservative weight (0.3), plus a native-update linear-probe ensemble for recognition, fused with a final-state probe via a learned gate.
- Runs a large architecture-balanced study (38-checkpoint grid spanning plain/masked ViTs, hierarchical transformers, CNNs, self-supervised and vision-language encoders) across OOD detection (OpenOOD v1.5) and 12-dataset image classification (clean and under CIFAR-100-C/PACS/Office-Home shift), showing gains are architecture- and detector-dependent rather than universal, and mapping out where trajectory evidence helps, is neutral, or (rarely) hurts.

## Methodology

The paper represents a frozen encoder's forward pass as a trajectory τ(x) = (z₁(x),…,z_L(x)), with layer update u_l(x) = z_{l+1}(x) - z_l(x). Cross-stage comparisons for architectures with varying dimensionality use a fixed Gaussian random projection (Johnson–Lindenstrauss). For each ID class c, a class route μ_l^c = E[z_l(X)|Y=c] is estimated, and each sample's residual trajectory r_l(x,c) = z_l(x) - μ_l^c isolates sample-specific computation from class-shared computation (Eq. 3-4). A KL-divergence chain-rule decomposition (Eq. 5) formally separates the divergence visible from the final representation E=Z_L from a residual component conditioned on E, establishing where extra path evidence could reside without asserting it always does.

Four descriptive measurements (movement, relational deformation √(1-CKA), prototype-accuracy gain, within-class residual share) show architecture families allocate class evidence and representational change at different native-block depths (Fig. 2), motivating architecture-native (rather than hand-picked-middle-layer) analysis.

Trajectory continuity is validated before use: a one-step MLP predictor (chosen over GRU/LSTM/causal-attention/temporal-convolution alternatives for best median forward prediction, Appendix L.4) predicts r_{l+1}(x) from r_l(x) for the matched sample vs. an identity-shuffled control r_l(x')-pairing that preserves layer marginals but breaks sample identity; matched trajectories win on held-out R² in all 16 checkpoint-dataset comparisons.

For OOD detection: a shared MLP f_θ, conditioned on a learned depth embedding e_l, predicts residual transitions r̂_{l+1} = f_θ(r_l + e_l); ID-only fitting statistics estimate per-layer, per-dimension prediction-error scale σ̂²_{l,d}; the trajectory-surprise score D(x) (Eq. 6) sums standardized squared prediction errors across layers and dimensions. D(x) is standardized and fused with a final-state OOD score S₀ (e.g., Mahalanobis++) using a fixed a priori coefficient of 0.3 (Eq. 7) — deliberately conservative to keep the final-state score dominant and the trajectory acting as a correction rather than a replacement (an ablation in Appendix Fig. L3 shows larger weights give only marginal average gains while increasing worst-case degradation).

For recognition: linear probes q_l(y|u_l) are trained on every compatible native update, then fused with weights w_l, temperatures, and a final-state margin gate q_fuse = (1-α)q_L + α·q_path (Eq. 8-9), all selected via inner source-validation/fusion-validation splits using only ID labels — no OOD exposure and no target-domain tuning for shift evaluation (leave-one-domain-out for PACS/Office-Home; selection on clean CIFAR-100 only for CIFAR-100-C).

The evaluation grid comprises 42 frozen checkpoints (Table C1: 14 plain/masked-image ViTs, 4 hierarchical Transformers, 2 self-supervised foundation encoders — DINOv2/DINOv3 — 17 CNNs, 5 vision-language encoders including CLIP, PE-Core, SigLIP2), of which 38 form the balanced protocol-matched grid. OOD detection uses OpenOOD v1.5 (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-200, ImageNet-1K with prescribed near/far splits), yielding 190 backbone-benchmark pairs, fitted with labelled ID data only and three random seeds, no OOD exposure. Recognition uses 12 datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Caltech-101, DTD, EuroSAT, CUB-200, Stanford Cars, Oxford-IIIT Pets, Food-101, Flowers-102, FGVC-Aircraft, SUN397) across CLIP ViT-B/16, DeiT3-B, ViT-B (AugReg), Swin-B/SwinV2-B, ConvNeXt-B, ResNet-50, with three probe seeds. Recording intermediate representations costs 0.29–0.36 ms/image and 35.6–82.3 MiB at batch size one (Appendix G).

## Results & Highlights

- **OOD detection, balanced OpenOOD v1.5 grid**: across 152 non-saturated backbone-benchmark cases, mean FPR95 falls by 4.33 points with Mahalanobis++ (131/152 improved), 8.67 points with Relative Mahalanobis (142/152 improved), 2.69 points with X-Mahalanobis (106/152 improved), and 8.75 points with kNN (127/152 improved); the method improves every CIFAR-10 and every CIFAR-100 comparison, and 28/38 ImageNet-200 plus 27/38 ImageNet-1K architecture-benchmark pairs.
- **Near vs. Far OOD (Table 2, balanced mean FPR95)**: for Relative Mahalanobis, Near-OOD FPR95 goes 43.6→38.0 (Δ+5.66, 119/152 cases) and Far-OOD goes 24.1→13.7 (Δ+10.43, 151/152 cases); for kNN, Near 51.9→46.6 (Δ+5.31, 117/152) and Far 29.2→18.5 (Δ+10.63, 134/152); Far/Near reduction ratios range from 1.84× (Relative Mahalanobis) to 5.97× (X-Mahalanobis), showing gains concentrate on visually/semantically far shifts but remain positive (e.g., +0.64 to +5.66 points) on Near-OOD for most detectors.
- **Complementarity vs. an alternative final-state score (Fig. 5)**: adding trajectory evidence to Mahalanobis++ gives the largest mean FPR95 reduction of any single addition (3.40 points, 22/24 cases) versus adding Relative Mahalanobis itself (3.12 points, 22/24) or X-Mahalanobis (1.46 points) or a random-noise score (-0.79 points, 0/24 cases); sequentially, path evidence still reduces FPR95 by a further 2.71 points (21/24 cases) after Relative Mahalanobis is already added, and Relative Mahalanobis still contributes 2.45 points (22/24 cases) after path evidence — demonstrating the two signals are complementary regardless of order.
- **Clean image classification (Table 1, top-1 accuracy)**: fusing trajectory-probe evidence improves 71/72 backbone-dataset cells, mean gain +4.41 points, with the largest per-dataset means on Stanford Cars (+9.81), Flowers-102 (+9.04), and FGVC-Aircraft (+7.28); by architecture, mean gains range from +1.43 (CLIP-B) to +6.41 (DeiT3-B), with ViT-B (AugReg) +6.28, ResNet-50 +4.68, Swin-B +4.61, and ConvNeXt-B +3.03 — all six backbones improve despite CLIP's high 84.65% mean final-state accuracy.
- **Classification under label-preserving shift**: on CIFAR-100-C, trajectory information improves 169/180 conditions (ResNet-50 strongest at +3.11, ViT-B still +2.24); on PACS and Office-Home (leave-one-domain-out), gains of +3.01 and +0.65 accuracy points hold beyond a matched final-state-probe control across 39/48 positive folds; CLIP marks the boundary of benefit under natural shift, while synthetic-corruption failures concentrate in additive noise and severe contrast.
- **Foundation-model check**: DINOv2 and two CLIP checkpoints improve all 12 OOD benchmark combinations with both kNN (+13.01 FPR95 points) and Relative Mahalanobis (+11.03 points), indicating trajectory information from large-scale self-supervised/vision-language pretraining is not redundant with strong final representations.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The framing is a genuine and well-differentiated contribution — the "missing comparison" box in Related Work explicitly distinguishes this work from prior intermediate-representation studies (per-layer Mahalanobis scoring, learned feature mixtures, linear probes) by treating the *same input's path* across depth as a first-class object with its own identity, rather than scoring layers independently or as adjacent pairs. The KL chain-rule identity (Eq. 5) is a nice formal device for stating precisely where trajectory evidence could live without overclaiming that it always does.

**Rigor**: The five-way control battery (matched, identity-shuffled, history, gap-decay, direction) for validating trajectory continuity before using it downstream is methodologically careful and directly addresses the obvious objection that "trajectories" could just be noise dressed up as structure. The evaluation is unusually broad for this kind of paper: 190 backbone-benchmark OOD pairs and 12×6 recognition cells, with three random seeds and held-out fitting/selection splits kept strictly separate from OOD exposure.

**Reproducibility**: Appendix C's exact `timm`/HuggingFace checkpoint identifiers (e.g., `deit3_base_patch16.fb_in22k_ft_in1k`) and Appendix D/E's dataset-role table and hyperparameter table (Table D2: AdamW, lr grids, seeds, patience) are genuinely reproducible artifacts. However, the paper explicitly restricts its "reliability evidence" to a fixed weight (0.3) tuned once and never adjusted per benchmark or split — a deliberate robustness choice the authors defend (Appendix Fig. L3 shows larger weights raise benchmark-to-benchmark variance and worsen worst-case FPR95), but it also means the headline numbers likely understate the score's ceiling.

**Significance / red flags**: The paper is unusually honest about limits: Section 6 explicitly states residual gains on the *hardest* ImageNet Near-OOD splits are neutral to negative, that X-Mahalanobis already pools multiple intermediate layers and so shows the smallest marginal trajectory gain (2.69 points vs. 8.67 for Relative Mahalanobis) because of representational overlap, and that checkpoint comparisons should be read as "replicated-family evidence" rather than causal architecture interventions since checkpoints differ in pretraining corpus, scale, and optimization recipe — a fair caveat given no controlled architecture-only ablation is run. One genuine gap: the paper reports FPR95 improvements only over "152 non-saturated cases" out of 190 total backbone-benchmark pairs, silently excluding roughly 38 saturated cases (where baseline FPR95 is presumably already near-zero) from every headline average — a reasonable practice (can't meaningfully reduce an already-near-0 FPR95) but one that is easy to miss when skimming the abstract's "131/152" framing, and the paper does not report what happens on the excluded cases. There is also no head-to-head empirical comparison against the authors' own closest prior trajectory-adjacent method (Jelenić et al. 2024's between-layer transformation smoothness, or their own 2025 intermediate-CLIP-evidence work) as a baseline — those are only discussed conceptually in Related Work, not run on the same 38-checkpoint grid.

## Strengths

- Formally motivated (KL chain-rule) and empirically validated (5 controls, 16 checkpoint-dataset comparisons) claim that trajectories carry real, sample-specific cross-depth continuity, not just re-labeled layer snapshots.
- Broad, architecture-balanced evaluation (38–42 checkpoints spanning ViT, hierarchical Transformers, CNNs, self-supervised, and vision-language encoders) rather than cherry-picked backbones.
- Deliberately conservative score fusion (fixed 0.3 weight, no OOD-set tuning) that keeps the method as a genuine complement rather than a re-tuned replacement, improving robustness claims' credibility.
- Reports negative/neutral results honestly (Near-OOD residual gaps, X-Mahalanobis's smaller marginal gain, CIFAR-100-C corruption types where gains don't hold) rather than only favorable slices.
- Practically cheap: sub-millisecond per-image overhead and tens of MiB memory at batch size one.

## Limitations

- Headline OOD gains are computed only over "non-saturated" cases (152/190), and the paper does not report performance on the excluded ~38 saturated cases.
- Fusion coefficient (0.3) and gating weights are fixed/selected without any OOD validation data, and the classification branch relies on labelled ID/source data rather than being zero-shot — so both branches inherit whatever bias exists in the labelled fitting data.
- Because the backbone is frozen and deterministic, the trajectory cannot manufacture information absent from earlier states — the paper's own "what the path does not create" discussion concedes the method can only recover evidence the final layers compressed away, not create new information.
- Checkpoint-level comparisons across architecture families are confounded by differences in pretraining corpus, resolution, and training recipe, so architecture claims are described by the authors as "replicated-family" evidence rather than controlled interventions.
- No direct empirical head-to-head against the authors' own prior trajectory-adjacent OOD methods (only discussed qualitatively in Related Work).

## Relevance

该工作把"计算路径"作为独立于最终表征与置信度的第三种可靠性证据，方法轻量（毫秒级开销）且在 OOD 检测与图像分类两大任务、六种主流架构族上都验证有效，适合关注 OOD 检测、中间层表征分析或视觉基础模型可靠性评估方向的研究者深入阅读，尤其是其严谨的轨迹连续性对照实验设计值得借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the trajectory-surprise score remain complementary when combined with the ~38 "saturated" backbone-benchmark cases excluded from the headline results, or does it degrade already-near-perfect detectors?
- How does the fixed 0.3 fusion weight compare against a per-benchmark-tuned weight if a small amount of OOD validation data were permitted, and how much of the "robustness" claim would survive?
- Would the local/patch-level trajectory extension sketched in Appendix B (patch tokens or spatial feature maps rather than one global vector per depth) meaningfully improve near-OOD detection, where the current global-readout method shows its weakest and even negative results?
- How does trajectory-surprise scoring compare, on the same 38-checkpoint grid, against the authors' own prior intermediate-CLIP-evidence and between-layer-transformation-smoothness baselines (Jelenić et al. 2024; M. De la Jara et al. 2025) rather than only against final-state detectors?
- Does the class-route estimate degrade under long-tailed or few-shot class distributions, where E[z_l(X)|Y=c] would be estimated from very few samples per class?
