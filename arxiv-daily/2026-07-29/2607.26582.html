Family-balanced false-positive rate at 95% ID recall. CEG improves every wrapped detector by 9.3–35.9 points — GL-MCM: 38.1→28.8 — using only unlabeled ID calibration data, no retraining.
Why this matters

If you pick an OOD detector by its benchmark-average score, you're flying blind on deployment risk — CEG is a drop-in, training-free fix.

Critical edge

CEG needs 1,000 unlabeled ID calibration images and offers no finite-sample coverage guarantee, since its fused channels are dependent and share calibration data.

  • Audit design: implementation, preprocessing, and evaluation protocol held fixed across 17 ID datasets, 3 VLMs (CLIP B/16@224, SigLIP2 B/16@512, PE-Core B/16@224), and 7 detectors (MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, MCM, GL-MCM, Mahalanobis, NegLabel/CSP) — under both native OpenOOD-v1.5 and a reference-shift protocol that isolates ID-domain effects from benchmark-composition artifacts.
  • Mechanism: Proposition 1 formally shows MCM is a shift-invariant approximation of the centered max — it discards level, keeping only sharpness — while Energy's log-sum-exp preserves level to leading order. The two are complementary extremes, not redundant signals.
  • CEG wrapper: from the same forward pass, computes global+local level L(x) (top-k=10 patches) and global+spatial sharpness S(x), maps base/L/S scores to empirical ID-percentiles via CDFs fit on a disjoint calibration set, then takes the minimum — one alarmed channel is enough to veto acceptance.
Non-compensatory by design: a sample only needs to look anomalous on one of {base, level, sharpness} to be rejected — this is what lets CEG catch failure modes that a single-score detector misses.

Fake channels (noise, entropy, logit variance) perform no better than raw MCM — confirming CEG's gain comes from the specific informative content of level/sharpness, not merely from adding any conservative min-fusion term.
NegLabel's corpus dependence is domain-specific: its mined WordNet vocabulary overlaps 89.5–93.0% across every deployment domain tested, yet its effect on MCM flips sign — +13.1 FPR95 pts on ImageNet-1K, but −6.8 on CIFAR-100 and −38.4 on MNIST. Stable vocabulary overlap does not mean stable usefulness.

Strengths

  • Rare methodological care isolating ID-domain effects from benchmark-composition artifacts (duplicate/near-duplicate OOD-ID pairs, near vs. far OOD splits).
  • A falsifiable mechanistic explanation (Proposition 1), backed by a targeted channel-substitution ablation that rules out the "any minimum helps" confound.
  • Honest reporting of a disclosed exception (PE-Core MaxLogit near-OOD stays flat) rather than cherry-picking favorable cases.
  • No OOD supervision, auxiliary corpora, or detector retraining required — practical where OOD data is unavailable by definition.

Limitations

  • Needs a disjoint unlabeled ID calibration set (1,000 images per task) to fit empirical CDFs and the acceptance threshold.
  • No finite-sample coverage guarantee for the percentile-based guard — fused channels are dependent and share calibration data.
  • Scoped to CLIP-family VLMs (CLIP, SigLIP2, PE-Core); untested on non-contrastive or generative VLM architectures.
  • Validated as a wrapper around existing detectors, not benchmarked against learned OOD-fusion alternatives beyond the authors' own ablation baselines.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the level/sharpness decomposition generalize to non-CLIP-style VLMs (generative or non-contrastive), or is it specific to cosine-similarity logits?
  2. How sensitive is CEG to calibration-set size — what happens with 100 or 10,000 images instead of 1,000?
  3. Could a learned but still calibration-only combination rule beat the non-compensatory minimum while keeping the robustness property?
  4. Could external-corpus evidence (NegLabel/CSP) be added as a fourth calibrated channel — would it help or hurt family-balanced results?
  5. How does CEG's overhead (extra forward-pass channels, calibration fitting) compare to simply ensembling multiple detectors' raw scores?
