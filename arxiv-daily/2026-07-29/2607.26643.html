Why This Matters

A reusable pattern — structured failure attribution, Best-of-N candidate repairs, full back-test regression gating — for building agent self-improvement loops that don't quietly overfit or forget.

Critical Edge

No variance or error bars are reported anywhere, despite training splits as small as 35 questions — single-run deltas of a few points may be sampling noise, not signal.

Methodology

A skill as an addressable state, not a text blob

  • Skills are represented as a structured state vector s_t with editable coordinates — Metadata, Workflow, Constraints, References — instead of monolithic prompt text, making localized edits possible.
  • Each iteration alternates a forward rollout (the frozen target LLM executes a task batch under the current skill, producing step-level trajectories) and a backward optimization pass over the resulting failures.
  • Self-evolution is cast as an MDP M = (S, A, P, r) governed by an explicit exploration-exploitation trade-off, rather than the gradient-like / zeroth-order "fit the batch" objective used by prior methods (SkillOpt, Trace2Skill).
  • A lightweight theoretical result (Theorem 1) bounds expected directional progress from a Best-of-N candidate pool at roughly √(ln N) — explaining why returns to larger N saturate quickly, and motivating N = 4 as the default.

  • Across 20 text-only model-benchmark pairs, SkillBoost is the only method with non-negative gains on every pair (+10.6 to +28.4pp over No-Skill); human- and LLM-generated skills sometimes degrade performance, e.g. on BFCL-v4 and ALFWorld.
  • Verified-acceptance gate ablation: removing full-set back-testing drops BFCL accuracy 48.5%→37.7% (Claude-opus-4-6), 20.8%→17.0% (Kimi-k2.6), 44.6%→36.3% (DeepSeek-v4-pro); on SpreadsheetBench, 82.5%→75.7% (Claude) and 80.0%→67.5% (DeepSeek).
  • Best-of-N saturates after N=4 (SpreadsheetBench: 61.1% at N=1 → 77.9% at N=4 → 80.4% at N=8 — only +2.5pp from doubling N again while token cost nearly doubles), matching the √(ln N) prediction.
  • Transfer: a Claude-opus-4-6-optimized skill moved zero-shot to Kimi-k2.6 still beats Kimi's own human-written skill by up to 14.3pp (BFCL-v4: 16.3% vs. 12.5%); a LiveMath-optimized skill applied to OlympiadBench lifts DeepSeek-v4-pro +6.0pp and Kimi-k2.6 +14.0pp.
  • Deployment cost: 13.9% fewer inference tokens per case on average vs. SkillOpt, because constrained edits keep the evolved skill compact instead of growing unboundedly.
Strengths
  • Conceptual reframing (constrained search / MDP) is backed by a matching theory (Theorem 1) whose diminishing-returns prediction is empirically confirmed at N=4.
  • Consistent, non-negative gains across all 23 tested model-benchmark pairs — no baseline achieves this.
  • Direct, benchmark-spanning quantification of the overfitting gap it sets out to solve (Table III), not just an aggregate win.
  • Cross-model and cross-benchmark transfer experiments suggest the learned skills encode transferable procedural knowledge.
  • Appendix case study (openpyxl not evaluating formulas) gives concrete, interpretable evidence of what the method discovers.
Limitations
  • No variance/error-bar reporting despite tiny train splits (e.g. 35-item LiveMath train set) — single-run deltas are noisy.
  • Very high evaluation/back-testing cost (3–8M tokens per run) undercuts the "lightweight alternative to post-training" framing.
  • No comparison against RL-based skill-evolution baselines (Skill-r1, SkillRL) cited in Related Work — only against prompt/heuristic optimizers (SkillOpt, Trace2Skill).
  • Tested only on frontier-tier proprietary/large models; smaller open-weight generalization is unverified and self-flagged.
  • Full Analyzer/Generate prompts are not released, limiting exact reproducibility.
Questions for Further Exploration
  • 01How does SkillBoost compare against RL-based skill-evolution methods (Skill-r1, SkillRL) it cites but does not benchmark against?
  • 02Does the benefit hold on smaller/weaker open-weight backbones where self-diagnosis capability may be limited, as the authors themselves flag?
  • 03What is the actual wall-clock/dollar cost of Best-of-N back-testing vs. lightweight LoRA fine-tuning, to substantiate the "lightweight" claim?
  • 04How sensitive are results to the regression threshold ε in the verified-acceptance gate, and how is it tuned per benchmark?
  • 05Would gains persist with larger, more diverse training splits, or is part of the benefit an artifact of small-batch evaluation noise?
