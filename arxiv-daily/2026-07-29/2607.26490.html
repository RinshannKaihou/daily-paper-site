EvoPINN · arXiv 2607.26490 · 29 Jul 2026

LLM agents that separately evolve a PINN's architecture and training code discover algorithms beating expert baselines on 3 of 4 PDEs.

一句话总结：EvoPINN 用 LLM 智能体在"神经表示"和"训练程序"两个可独立演化的模块上做结构验证 + 预算匹配评测的迭代式代码搜索，在 Burgers1D 上把相对 L2 误差从种子 PINN 的 3.05e-4 降到 1.65e-4，并自主发现了新架构 SLRC-PINN。

EvoPINN: Agentic Discovery of Executable Algorithms for Physics-Informed Neural Networks ↗
Peng Yin, Kai Li, Yifan Zhang, Jian Cheng · 29 Jul 2026 · discovered artifacts: SLRC-PINN · TCROP
physics-informed neural networks LLM-agent code generation automated algorithm discovery PDE solvers evolutionary search

Key metric

55.1%

lower mean error for SLRC-PINN — the architecture EvoPINN discovered on its own — vs. a parameter-matched global MLP on Burgers1D (2.0445e-4 vs. 4.5506e-4 rel-L2, ~213k params either way).

Why this matters

Shows LLM agents can discover genuinely new, verified algorithms — not just tune hyperparameters — a template for LLM-driven scientific-computing research.

Critical edge

On Heat2D, the one anisotropic-diffusion benchmark, the hand-designed expert baseline still edges out EvoPINN (1.0963e-3 vs. 1.1142e-3) — "beats experts across regimes" isn't universally true.

Methodology

seed A₀ (5×100 tanh MLP)→ UCB picks module M or T→ LLM proposes K=8 candidates→ 3-level AST novelty filter→ smoke test + ≤3 self-repairs→ budget-matched train & score→ accept only if error ↓→ ×7 generations→ freeze best, retrain ×5 seeds on held-out set
  • A PINN is treated as a tuple A = (M, T) — neural representation × training program. Each generation mutates one module at a time, holding the other fixed, to sidestep the credit-assignment problem of jointly editing both.
  • Diagnosis-guided generation: the LLM (gpt-5.4-mini, temp 0.7) sees a compressed training-trace summary — convergence progress, late-stage oscillation, physics-error relations — rather than a bare scalar L2, plus an evolutionary memory of past motifs/failures and per-candidate diversified search foci to prevent mode collapse.
  • A UCB-style scheduler (β = 0.35) decides representation- vs. training-mutation each generation from recent reward and visitation counts, not fixed alternation.
  • Search-time reference points and final 5-seed reporting points are kept strictly disjoint, guarding against overfitting to the search metric.

  • Beats all three automated-search baselines on every benchmark tested — roughly an order of magnitude ahead of the weakest ones on Wave1D.
  • Ablation: dropping module-wise decoupling ("w/o modular", joint mutation) collapses executable candidates on Wave1D from 55/56 to 12/56 — instability, not just a slower search.
  • Ablation: dropping diagnostic feedback ("w/o diagnosis") degrades Wave1D rel-L2 from 2.36e-2 to 2.71e-1 — over 10× worse.
  • TCROP (discovered optimizer/resampling policy) cuts Poisson2D geometric-mean error 27.5% vs. a fixed Adam→L-BFGS handoff, and lowers seed variance (CV 19.0% vs. 29.6%).
  • Parameter transfer is mixed: on Heat2D at an unseen frequency (k_x=25π) EvoPINN beats the expert by >2 orders of magnitude (1.81e-3 vs. 4.33e-1); on Wave1D's shifted frequencies the expert baseline stays lower-error.

Strengths

  • Search-time candidate selection and final reporting use disjoint reference points, guarding against overfitting to the search metric.
  • AST-based structural novelty checks (raw / comment-stripped / normalized) block the LLM from disguising cosmetic renames or hyperparameter tweaks as new mechanisms.
  • A dedicated parameter-matched ablation (Table 3) isolates whether SLRC-PINN's gain is architecture or raw capacity — rare rigor for "LLM discovers architecture" papers.
  • Ablations directly test the paper's own design claims and surface a genuine failure mode: joint, non-modular mutation causes severe instability.
  • Discovered mechanisms transfer to neighboring PDE parameters not seen during search, suggesting they're not overfit to one equation instance.

Limitations

  • Loses to the strongest expert baseline on Heat2D, the one anisotropic-diffusion regime tested.
  • Search is expensive — up to ~52 GPU-hours per PDE family, since every candidate needs a full-budget training run; proxy objectives are left to future work.
  • Discovery quality is tied to the underlying LLM (gpt-5.4-mini, not a frontier reasoning model) — a limitation the authors flag themselves.
  • The 16-member "expert baseline" pool is authored and screened by the same team, not sourced from independently published state-of-the-art configs.
  • Only four canonical PDEs are tested (Poisson, Burgers, Wave, Heat) — no multi-physics, higher-dimensional, or inverse-problem settings.

Questions for further exploration

  • Would a frontier reasoning LLM close the Heat2D gap, or is it a search-budget limit rather than a model-capability ceiling?
  • Could a cheap proxy objective (short training curves, surrogate error estimators) replace full-budget training to cut the 14.7–51.78 GPU-hour search cost per PDE?
  • Does one-module-at-a-time evolution leave synergistic joint representation+training co-designs undiscovered, given that "w/o modular" failed on execution stability rather than hitting a quality ceiling?
  • How would SLRC-PINN's global-anchor-plus-gated-local-correction topology generalize to higher-dimensional or multi-physics systems beyond the 1D/2D benchmarks tested here?
  • Would a different, independently curated expert-baseline pool change the Heat2D outcome or widen/narrow the margins elsewhere?
