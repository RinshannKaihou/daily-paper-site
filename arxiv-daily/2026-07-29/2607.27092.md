> **一句话总结：** 作者用 25 种"夜空邻近关系"提示词对 7 个 32B–235B 开源模型的残差流做主成分回归探测，发现除 gpt-oss-120b 外的 6 个模型都在残差流前 8 个主成分中线性编码了 188 个天体（85 颗恒星、88 个星座、15 个星系/星云/星团）在天球上的真实位置，留一法验证 R² 达 65–85%、中位角误差低至 12°–21°，并通过表征相异矩阵（RDM）分析排除了这是"平面经纬度图"或"真实带距离的三维地图"泄漏的可能，称其为首个已发现的弯曲高维不可约特征流形。

> **TL;DR:** Probing residual streams of seven open-weight LLMs (32B–235B: Qwen3-32B, Qwen3-235B, gpt-oss-120b, Llama-3.3-70B, Mixtral-8x22B, Mistral Large 2, GLM-4.5-Air) on 188 night-sky objects (85 IAU-named stars, 88 constellations, 15 galaxies/nebulae/clusters) via 25 proximity-focused prompt templates, the authors fit leave-one-out linear regressions from the top-8 residual-stream PCA components onto true unit-sphere coordinates. Six of seven models show significant sky-sphere decodability (R² up to 65–85%, median angular error 12°–21°, best: Qwen3-235B at 12.0°, worst of the successful group: Mixtral-8x22B at 21.5°); the outlier, gpt-oss-120b, shows near-zero R² from its residual stream despite locating over 70% of objects to within 1° when simply asked its coordinates directly — a >10x gap between what the model "knows" and what the linear probe can decode. RDM analysis rules out both a flat RA/Dec map (Gurnee & Tegmark's continents/USA-states style representation) and a literal distance-aware 3D embedding as the true underlying structure.

## 核心贡献

- 首次在语言模型残差流中发现一个**弯曲的高维不可约特征流形**（球面），区别于此前发现的平面型（大陆/美国州坐标、颜色空间）或一维型（星期、月份、年份、句长）流形。
- 设计了一套"邻近性"提示词模板（如"in the night sky X is not far from"），仅提及天体专有名词、从不显式给出坐标，通过 25 个模板 × 188 个天体（4700 次前向传播）在每层残差流上做留一法（LOO）主成分回归，验证球面坐标的可解码性。
- 在 7 个 32B–235B 参数的开源模型（Dense 与 MoE 均有）中，6 个模型在残差流前 ~8 个主成分中呈现出显著的球面表征（R² 65–85%，中位角误差 12°–21°），且该表征强烈依赖提示词是否聚焦于"夜空邻近性"——换成通用提示词后信号在同样的主成分空间内几乎消失。
- 通过修改版 RDM（表征相异矩阵）回归分析，证明该球面表征不是"平面经纬度地图"（RA/Dec）或"含距离信息的真实三维地图"的简单变体或泄漏：RA 坐标系数在前 8 主成分中始终 ≤ 0，且残差流中的"径向"分量与恒星真实距离无稳定显著相关。
- 发现并分析了唯一的异常模型 gpt-oss-120b：其残差流球面可解码性接近零，但直接询问坐标时该模型定位精度极高（>70% 天体误差在 1° 以内），提示其"知道"坐标的方式可能不经过一个线性可探测的球面几何结构，这一现象作者明确留作未解决的开放问题。

## Key Contributions

- Discovers the first curved, high-dimensional, irreducible feature manifold (a sphere) in LLM residual streams, distinct from previously reported flat manifolds (continents, USA states, color space) or one-dimensional ones (days, months, years, sentence length).
- Designs a proximity-focused prompt family (e.g., "in the night sky X is not far from") that names objects only by proper name and never states coordinates, then fits leave-one-out (LOO) linear regressions from top-n PCA components of the residual stream (per layer, per model) onto true unit-sphere coordinates across 25 prompt templates × 188 objects = 4,700 forward passes.
- Shows that 6 of 7 tested open-weight models (32B–235B parameters, mixing dense and MoE architectures) exhibit a significant sky-sphere representation concentrated in the top ~8 residual-stream PCA components (R² up to 65–85%, median angular error 12°–21°), and that this signal is sharply prompt-dependent — generic (non-proximity) prompts produce near-zero R² in the same PCA subspace.
- Rules out two competing explanations via a custom RDM-regression analysis: (1) a flat RA/Dec 2D-map representation (à la Gurnee & Tegmark's continents/states work) — the RA coefficient stays ≤ 0 in top-8 PCA once the ambiguous shared dimension is excluded; and (2) a literal 3D embedding with distance information — the "radial" direction of the representation shows no consistent significant correlation with true stellar distance across models, correlating instead with object type and, more weakly, textual frequency and brightness.
- Identifies and investigates a lone outlier, gpt-oss-120b, whose residual stream shows almost no sky-sphere decodability despite the model locating >70% of objects to within 1° when asked directly for coordinates — even after reformatting prompts into the model's native "harmony" response format, decodability drops further rather than recovering, leaving the mechanism gap unexplained and flagged as an open question.

## Methodology

The authors feed each of 188 night-sky objects — 85 IAU WGSN-named stars, 88 official constellations (location defined as the Monte-Carlo-sampled area centroid of the constellation's sky region, projected back onto the unit sphere), and 15 miscellaneous objects (5 galaxies, 5 nebulae, 5 clusters, coordinates via astropy's `SkyCoord.from_name`) — into 25 hand-curated proximity-focused prompt templates (e.g., "the closest constellation to X is", "sky watchers locate X next to"), run in auto-complete mode with no chain-of-thought, on 7 open-source models spanning 32B–235B total parameters (Qwen3-32B, Qwen3-235B, gpt-oss-120b, Llama-3.3-70B, Mixtral-8x22B, Mistral Large 2, GLM-4.5-Air; both dense and MoE). For every layer, they extract the top-128 PCA components of the residual stream at the final prompt token across the full 25×188 = 4,700-prompt set, then fit a linear regression from the top-4, top-8, or top-128 PCA components onto the objects' true unit-sphere (x, y, z) coordinates, using leave-one-out (LOO) cross-validation: each object X is held out, the regression is fit on the rest, and prediction accuracy is scored via R² (variance explained) and median great-circle angular error.

To confirm the discovered structure is a genuine spherical manifold rather than a leak from a correlated but conceptually different representation, the authors introduce a modified representational-dissimilarity-matrix (RDM) analysis: they regress the residual-stream-derived RDM (whitened, squared pairwise distances in top-8 PCA space) jointly onto RDMs implied by (a) the spherical (x,y,z) model and (b) a flat RA/Dec 2D-map model, isolating each coordinate's contribution (β) per layer. Because Dec is nearly identical to the spherical x-coordinate, the two ambiguous terms are merged and reported both with and without them. A second competing-representation test examines whether the "radial" direction of the fitted embedding (length of the projection beyond the unit sphere) correlates with true stellar distance (Spearman correlation against SIMBAD parallax-derived distance), textual corpus frequency (wordfreq Zipf frequency, with and without the word "constellation" stripped), apparent brightness (negative magnitude), and object-type indicator variables. As a robustness check on prompt design, they re-run the full LOO pipeline on a "generic" (non-proximity) prompt set (e.g., "X is classified as", "the magnitude of X is") to show the spherical signal is specific to proximity-framed prompts and does not appear in the same top-8 PCA subspace under generic framing.

## Results & Highlights

- **Six of seven models show strong LOO decodability of the sky sphere from top-8 residual-stream PCA components**, with per-model median angular error (α, at each model's best layer) reported as: Qwen3-235B 12.0°, Mistral Large 2 12.7°, GLM-4.5-Air 13.4°, Qwen3-32B 15.9°, Llama-3.3-70B 17.9°, and Mixtral-8x22B 21.5° (Figure 11 table); R² across these models ranges up to 65–85% at the representation's peak layers (Figure 3/7), rising into and concentrated within the top ~8 PCA components of the residual stream.
- **The representation is prompt-dependent, not a generic artifact:** on the same top-8 PCA subspace, switching from proximity-focused prompts to a matched set of generic prompts (e.g., "X is known for") collapses R² to near zero for all 7 models (Figure 9); the signal only partially reappears when the PCA budget is expanded to the top-32 components, and even then remains far weaker and decays faster across layers than under proximity prompts.
- **gpt-oss-120b is a striking negative outlier relative to its own capability**, showing near-zero LOO R² from residual-stream PCA at every layer (Figure 7), yet when directly asked for right ascension/declination with no commentary, it (like other "thinking" models in the set: Qwen3-235B, Qwen3-32B, GLM-4.5-Air) locates over 70% of non-constellation objects to within 1° angular error — more than 10x tighter than the 12°–21° error obtained by decoding the residual stream in the other six models. Reformatting prompts into gpt-oss's native "harmony" response format (its training-time chat format) made residual-stream decodability even worse, not better (Figure 8b), leaving the discrepancy unexplained.
- **The spherical representation survives two adversarial "is it really something else" tests.** Against a flat RA/Dec 2D-map alternative (the representation type reported for continents/US-states in Gurnee & Tegmark, 2024): the RA-coordinate regression weight (β_RA) stays ≤ 0 in top-8 PCA space across all models once the ambiguous shared dimension is excluded, while the spherical model's own unambiguous component (β_Dec-complement) remains clearly positive and prominent (Figure 10) — ruling out a flat-map leak as the source. Against a literal distance-aware 3D map: the radial component of the fitted embedding shows no significant, cross-model-consistent Spearman correlation with true parallax-derived stellar distance (only Llama-3.3 barely crosses p=0.05); it instead correlates significantly and consistently with object type (constellations placed farther out, stars closer, robust across all 6 models) and, to a lesser degree, with textual (Zipf) frequency, particularly for constellations (Figure 11).
- **No quantitative comparison to a non-neural or classical baseline is reported** — the paper's "baselines" are internal (across the 7 LLMs, across prompt types, and across the two competing representational hypotheses), not an external prior method for decoding spatial knowledge from LLMs.

## Authors & Credibility

Aleksandr Berdnikov (Fields Institute; Principles of Intelligence, princint.ai) and Yevgeny Liokumovich (University of Toronto) are the sole authors; Berdnikov is the corresponding author. This is a small, independent-research-style output (not from a major industry lab), acknowledged as supported by cluster access from Cursor and by Principles of Intelligence, with informal discussion credit given to named individuals at (implicitly) other interpretability-adjacent groups. The paper explicitly discloses in a "Statement on AI Use" section that "AI agent Claude Opus 4.8 was used to code and analyze experiments" — a methodologically relevant disclosure given the paper's empirical claims rest entirely on the authors' own analysis code, and one worth weighing when assessing the reliability of the reported pipeline (e.g., correctness of the RDM regression and LOO scoring implementations, which are not independently re-derived from scratch in the text). Code is publicly released at github.com/l3erdnik/Decodable-sky, supporting independent verification.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** Genuinely interesting and, as far as the related-work section shows, a real gap: prior geometric-representation findings (Gurnee & Tegmark's flat continents/US-states maps; Engels et al.'s 1D circular manifolds for days/months/years; Gurnee et al.'s 1D manifold for sentence length; Abdou et al.'s color space) are either flat or one-dimensional. A genuinely curved, ~8-dimensional-but-effectively-2-sphere manifold — actively distinguished from its two natural confounds (flat map, 3D map) via a purpose-built RDM test — is a nontrivial addition to the "linear representation hypothesis meets non-flat geometry" literature.

**Rigor:** The adversarial RDM analysis against the flat-map and 3D-distance alternatives is the paper's strongest methodological asset and is described in enough operational detail (equations, per-model figures) to be checked. That said, a specific weak point is prompt curation: the authors state outright that the final 25-template prompt set was arrived at by "composing, weeding out and adjusting by hand" and keeping only templates that "showed better performance," using Claude to expand candidate sets (Appendix C) — i.e., the prompt set that produces the headline 65–85% R² result was itself selected, in part, for producing a strong signal on this exact task. The generic-vs-proximity contrast (Figure 9) mitigates this concern somewhat (it shows the effect isn't present under arbitrary prompt framing), but doesn't fully rule out that the surviving 25 templates were cherry-picked toward whichever specific residual-stream direction happens to correlate with sky coordinates, rather than "proximity" per se being the operative variable. The paper also relies entirely on linear regression probes with no causal verification (e.g., no activation-patching or steering experiment showing the decoded direction causally influences downstream generation) — the authors themselves list "is the mechanism more interpretable than pure memorization?" as an open, unaddressed question in Section 4.

**Reproducibility:** Strong on this axis — code and commit hashes for every HuggingFace model revision are given in Appendix A, the full object list, prompt templates, and generic-prompt control set are printed in the appendices (Tables 2–4), and the RDM/PCA methodology is specified as explicit formulas rather than described only in prose.

**Significance:** Interesting for interpretability research but the practical stakes are unclear even to the authors, who ask in their own Open Questions section why this representation exists at all given models can answer coordinate queries far more accurately (gpt-oss: >70% within 1°) than the geometric residual-stream probe achieves (12°–21° median error) — i.e., the representation is not obviously "used" by the model's more precise verbal/computational answer. This tension (decoded-representation accuracy far below direct-query accuracy) is itself a red flag worth taking seriously: it leaves open whether the "sphere" is a meaningfully causal feature or a byproduct correlate that happens to be linearly decodable.

**Overclaiming check:** The "first example of a curved high-dimensional irreducible feature manifold" claim is appropriately hedged ("to our knowledge") and the paper is candid about limitations (single unexplained outlier model, no causal test, small object/prompt set relative to typical mechanistic-interpretability corpora). It does not overclaim beyond what the LOO/RDM evidence supports.

## Strengths

- Clean adversarial design: actively tests and rules out the two most obvious alternative explanations (flat map, distance-aware 3D map) rather than merely asserting the spherical interpretation.
- Cross-model breadth (7 models, 32B–235B, both dense and MoE) with a consistent pipeline, showing the phenomenon is not a single-model idiosyncrasy.
- Transparent about a genuine anomaly (gpt-oss-120b) rather than omitting or explaining it away, and follows up with a targeted format-matching control experiment (harmony response format) even though it failed to resolve the puzzle.
- Full code release and detailed appendices (model commit hashes, exact prompt lists, RDM formulas) support independent replication.

## Limitations

- Small evidentiary base per model: 188 objects and 25 prompt templates (4,700 total data points per layer), with constellation "location" defined via a somewhat arbitrary area-centroid convention the authors acknowledge but only partially justify (via the claim that constellation accuracy isn't worse than for less-subjective objects).
- Prompt template selection was iteratively hand-tuned toward templates that "performed better," creating a risk that the specific 25-template set is fit to this exact task rather than to "proximity phrasing" as a general category.
- No causal verification (activation patching, steering, or ablation) that the decoded direction is actually used by the model, only correlational/linear-decodability evidence — explicitly flagged by the authors as unresolved.
- The central outlier (gpt-oss-120b) is unexplained; the one follow-up test performed (harmony-format prompts) made the anomaly worse rather than resolving it, so the paper's account of *why* some models show this representation and one doesn't remains incomplete.
- Only ~100B-scale models were tested; there's no data on whether/how the representation emerges across scale (e.g., in much smaller models), so claims about which model sizes possess this feature are necessarily limited to the tested range.
- AI-assisted experiment coding and analysis (Claude Opus 4.8, per the Statement on AI Use) is disclosed but not independently cross-checked in the paper, which is a minor but relevant caveat for a paper whose entire empirical contribution rests on custom regression/RDM code.

## Relevance

该论文用严谨的 RDM 对照实验证明了语言模型残差流中存在弯曲、不可约的高维几何特征流形，为"线性表征假说"与非线性/非平面几何结构之间的关系提供了新证据，对关注机制可解释性、特征流形与残差流探测方法的研究者有较高参考价值；其"解码精度远低于直接问答精度"的反差现象也为后续因果验证（如激活拼接、方向性引导）留出了明确的开放问题。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why does the decoded residual-stream representation (12°–21° median error) lag so far behind the models' own direct-query accuracy (gpt-oss: >70% within 1°) — is the spherical feature causally used at all, or is it a correlate of some other computation (e.g., proximity-in-text statistics) that happens to align geometrically with true sky coordinates?
- Would activation patching or direction-steering experiments confirm the decoded spherical direction causally influences the model's downstream proximity judgments, addressing the paper's own open question about mechanism vs. memorization?
- What specifically about gpt-oss-120b's training (harmony response format, RL post-training regime, architecture choices) suppresses this representation's linear decodability while apparently preserving (or even improving) direct-query coordinate knowledge?
- Does the spherical representation emerge gradually with scale (testing sub-30B models), or is it a threshold phenomenon tied to the ~100B parameter range the authors deliberately targeted?
- Would a less hand-tuned, larger, and more systematically sampled prompt set (rather than one iteratively filtered for "better performance") preserve the same 65–85% R² and 12°–21° angular error, or would the effect size shrink toward the generic-prompt near-zero baseline?
- Do other implicit "world map" concepts in LLMs (e.g., geographic distance judged via natural-language proximity phrasing, as opposed to explicit coordinates) show the same flat-vs-curved distinction this paper draws for the night sky?
