Causal ML Performative Prediction Hypothesis Testing Sample Complexity

Randomly assigning predictions like an A/B test reveals, offline before deployment, whether a model's own outputs reshape the outcomes it predicts.

一句话总结：论文提出 OPAB，通过随机分配预测（do-intervention）打破预测与特征的因果纠缠，再用卡方检验比较两组结果分布来离线检测"结果绩效性"，并为三类绩效性假设推导出样本复杂度公式。

Why this matters

Lets teams check, before deployment, whether costly or ethically risky intervention testing is even worth running — and exactly how much data it needs.

Critical edge

The flagship real-world result — 52.3% detection at N=10⁵ on the OBD "men" subset — is barely above a coin flip, and the paper's own effect-size measure (Φ = 0.004) admits the real effect is negligible.

01 Methodology

Naively comparing outcomes under a real classifier's predictions is confounded: a trained φ separates instances by construction, so P(X | ŷ=0) ≠ P(X | ŷ=1) — feature and prediction are entangled before you even look at Y.

DORandomize ŶReplace φ with ŷ ~ Bernoulli(0.5) — a do-intervention that decouples prediction from features.
SPLITTwo fixed groupsT instances split into ŷ=0 / ŷ=1 arms, RCT-style, and outcomes Y observed.
TESTχ² / Fisher's exactCompare P(Y|do(Ŷ=0)) vs P(Y|do(Ŷ=1)) at δ=0.05; significant gap ⇒ performativity.
BOUNDSample complexity NClosed-form, Sympy-verified formulas per assumption class define "regions of indistinguishability."

The core simplifying assumption: performative effects are uniform across the feature space, so the general test P(Y|X,do(Ŷ)) collapses to the unconditional P(Y|do(Ŷ)) — cheap to run, but a real limitation (see Critical Edge). Validated on six benchmark datasets (breast cancer, diabetes, adult census, kickstarter, titanic, loan) with simulated performativity injected per assumption class, averaged over 100 replicates.

  • Class balancing quadruples efficiency — downsampling the majority class lifts the balanced-men detection rate at N=1000 to 0.234, ~4× the 0.05 seen on the original imbalanced set at the same N.
  • Test-statistic agnostic — Chi-Squared, Fisher's Exact, and Mann-Whitney U give near-identical detection heatmaps at fixed N=50 (appendix Fig. 13).
  • δ trades off against N — a stricter significance threshold proportionally raises the sample complexity needed (appendix Fig. 14).
  • Formulas match simulation — derived closed-form N accurately predicts empirical "regions of indistinguishability" across all three assumption classes and six datasets.

Strengths

  • Clean causal fix: a do-intervention on predictions removes the X↔Ŷ confounding that dooms naive comparisons.
  • Closed-form, Sympy-verified sample-complexity bounds for three distinct assumption classes, each empirically validated.
  • "Regions of indistinguishability" gives a principled way to reason about detection feasibility under a fixed, costly, or ethically bounded budget.
  • Genuinely offline — detection can happen during labeling, before any model is trained or deployed.
  • Real positive/negative control pair in one dataset family (OBD men vs. women) — stronger than synthetic-only evaluation.

Limitations

  • The uniformity-across-feature-space assumption is never stress-tested against non-uniform performativity, despite the paper flagging it as a gap.
  • No modeling of verification latency — the delay before an outcome is realized could make the offline window impractically long.
  • Assumes noise-free outcome labels; no experiments address label noise, which the authors admit could raise Type I/II error.
  • Adam Detection baseline is a from-scratch reimplementation from a text-only description, run at only 25 replicates vs. 100–1000 for OPAB.
  • Real-world validation is a single dataset family (OBD) with a small effect size (Φ=0.004) — external validity untested on other high-stakes domains.

? Questions for further exploration

  • How much does OPAB's guarantee degrade when the uniformity assumption is violated — could a feature-stratified version recover power without prohibitive extra samples?
  • Would a faithful, source-verified reimplementation of Adam Detection narrow or widen the reported sample-efficiency gap?
  • Can the framework extend to verification latency (delayed outcome realization) without impractically long waiting windows?
  • How does OPAB's power change under noisy or partially-observed labels, and can a measurement-error-corrected test help?
  • Are there other public datasets with genuinely randomized prediction assignment to serve as further real-world controls?
  • Could "regions of indistinguishability" become an adaptive sequential test that stops early once detection is clearly feasible or infeasible?
