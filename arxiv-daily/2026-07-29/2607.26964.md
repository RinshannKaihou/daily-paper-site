> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"特征不稳定性"(FI)——留一特征版本的经典留一实例不稳定性(II)——并证明：在线性回归的精确渐近分析中，特征装袋(feature bagging)与实例装袋一样能显著降低不稳定性（在 MARSadd 合成基准上 FI+II 联合可解释泛化差距的 R² 从单独 II 的 0.143 提升到 0.345），且在随机森林式的递归特征子采样这一无模型场景下，只要采样比 q 足够小、递归深度 T 满足 T ≲ (1−q)/q，装袋后的特征不稳定性就严格优于未装袋基线（非装袋前向选择的 FI = 2T/d），并且典型表格数据规模 (n,d)~(10⁵,10²) 下只需约 10² 轮装袋即可逼近无穷装袋极限，远少于实例装袋所需的约 10⁵ 轮。
>
> **TL;DR:** This is primarily a theory paper that introduces feature instability (FI), the feature-axis analogue of leave-one-instance instability (II), to explain why feature bagging (e.g., random forests' per-split feature subsampling) stabilizes predictions. In the Gaussian linear-model setting it derives exact asymptotic formulas (via random matrix theory) showing bagging suppresses the double-descent-shaped instability peak of a single subsampled estimator; in a model-free setting it proves recursive feature-subsampling algorithms (random forward selection, a simplified "max-edge dyadic" random forest) are provably more feature-stable than their non-bagged counterparts when the recursion is shallow (T ≲ (1−q)/q) and the subsampling ratio q is small. On the MARSadd synthetic random-forest benchmark, jointly using FI and II explains far more of the train/test generalization gap than II alone (R²(FI+II)=0.345 vs. R²(II)=0.143 at noise-mix ν=1; R²(FI+II)=0.675 vs. R²(II)=0.231 at ν=0), and across 64 real-tabular-data estimator/dataset combinations (diabetes, abalone, cpu_act, house_prices) the conditional contribution of FI beyond II is positive in 100% of cases.

## 核心贡献

- 提出特征不稳定性 (Feature Instability, FI)，作为经典留一实例不稳定性 (II) 在特征轴上的对偶量：衡量移除单个特征后预测的变化程度，并给出严格的 φ-特征稳定性定义（含共享随机种子与耦合随机种子两个版本）。通过合成基准 (MARSadd) 与四个真实表格数据集 (diabetes、abalone、cpu_act、house_prices) 上的偏互信息 (PMI) 与增量 R² 分析，证明 FI 携带的泛化相关信息与 II 互补而非冗余。
- 在高斯线性回归、比例渐近区 (d/n→γ) 下，对经过草图化 (sketching) 装袋的最小范数最小二乘估计量，给出 II 与 FI 的**精确渐近极限**（定理 3.1，含闭式表达式，见文中 Table 1），揭示单次子采样估计量在插值阈值 γq=p 附近呈现双下降式的不稳定性峰值，而装袋通过把权重从"同子模型项"转移到"跨子模型项"（阶为 1/n）来抹平该峰值。
- 针对无模型、数据依赖的递归特征子采样（随机森林等）建立一套新的稳定性分析工具（一般 Hilbert 空间下的 φ-特征稳定性、单步 Lipschitz 型条件），并将其具体应用于随机前向选择算法（定理 4.5）和一个简化的"最大边最大深度二叉 (max-edge dyadic) 随机森林"模型（定理 4.7），证明特征子采样比例 q 越激进，稳定性增益越强，且浅层递归下装袋严格优于非装袋基线。
- 证明有限轮装袋 (finite B) 足以逼近无穷装袋的稳定性水平（命题 4.8），并给出实践指导：对典型表格数据规模 (n,d)~(10⁵,10²)，特征侧稳定性只需约 B ≳ d(1−q)/q ~ 10² 轮装袋，而 Soloff et al. (2024a) 给出的实例侧稳定性理论上则需要约 B ≳ n(1−p)/p ~ 10⁵ 轮，说明特征装袋在高维小样本表格场景中比实例装袋更"划算"。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **feature instability (FI)**: FI := (1/d)Σⱼ 𝔼[(f(x) − f⁽·,−j⁾(x))²] (Definition 2.2), the leave-one-feature-out analogue of classical leave-one-instance instability II. Provides both a shared-seed and a coupled-seed (Definition 2.3) version, and empirically demonstrates (via linear R², mutual information, and partial mutual information on a synthetic MARSadd random-forest benchmark and four real tabular datasets) that FI captures generalization-relevant variance that II alone misses — motivating FI as an independent object of study rather than a redundant restatement of II.
- Derives an **exact asymptotic characterization** of II and FI for bagged minimum-norm least-squares estimators under the standard Gaussian linear model (x ~ N(0,I), β* ~ N(0,I/d)) in the proportional regime d/n→γ (Theorem 3.1). The decomposition separates "same-submodel" terms (weight 1/B, inherit the double-descent peak at the interpolation threshold γq=p) from "cross-submodel" terms (weight (B−1)/B, scale as 1/n), giving closed-form limits (Table 1) for both instance- and feature-subsampling ratios (p,q) and revealing that feature removal has its own dimension-dependent scaling (Δ^{B≠} ≍ q²/d) invisible from II alone.
- Develops a **model-free stability framework for recursive feature subsampling** (Section 4): a general Hilbert-space feature-stability definition (Definition 4.2, Proposition 4.3), a stochastic-process formalization of feature-bagged recursion with a one-step Lipschitz-type condition (Proposition 4.4), and concrete instantiations for (a) random forward selection (Algorithm 1, Theorem 4.5: FI ≤ T²/(d−1) · q/(1−q) when T ≲ q⁻¹, vs. 2T/d for the non-bagged baseline) and (b) a simplified max-edge dyadic random forest (Algorithm 2, Theorem 4.7, via an affine encoding of the local tree partition — Proposition 4.6 — that reduces feature-removal sensitivity to a Frobenius-norm distance between expected split-count matrices).
- Shows that **finite bagging rounds suffice** (Proposition 4.8, via Hoeffding + union bound) to approach the infinite-bagging FI level, with concentration error O(1/B), and derives the practical implication that feature-side stabilization needs far fewer bagging rounds than instance-side stabilization at typical tabular scales — an asymmetry the paper attributes to d ≪ n in most tabular problems.

## Methodology

The paper combines exact random-matrix-theory asymptotics with model-free (assumption-light) recursive-stability arguments, plus a supporting empirical study:

1. **Definitions (Section 2).** II and FI are defined as expected squared prediction changes under instance- vs. feature-removal (Definitions 2.1–2.2), sharing the algorithmic randomness ξ by default (a "coupled" variant with independently redrawn ξ is used only in the linear-model section). Instance bagging and feature bagging are both formalized as fixed-size sub-bagging without replacement (uniform subset of size m=pn instances or s=qd features per round), averaged over B rounds, with B→∞ defining the "infinite-bagging" predictor.

2. **Linear-model analysis (Section 3).** Under y = x^T β* + ε with Gaussian x, β*, ε and proportional asymptotics d/n→γ, the bagged estimator is written as an average of B sketched minimum-norm OLS fits β^(b) = V_b(U_b^T X V_b)^† U_b^T y, where U_b, V_b are random row/column selection matrices (instance/feature sketches). Using an "affected-axis resampling coupling" (the removed-axis sketch is independently redrawn from the reduced index set after deletion), the paper derives Theorem 3.1: a finite-B decomposition of 𝔼‖β−β^ℓ‖² into same-submodel (Δ^{V=}, Δ^{B=}) and cross-submodel (Δ^{V≠}, Δ^{B≠}) terms, with closed-form γ,p,q-dependent asymptotic limits obtained via random-matrix / Wishart-type identities (leveraging Haff's (1979) Wishart identity and results from Hastie et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2023, Wu and Sun 2025).

3. **Model-free recursive framework (Section 4).** For algorithms whose state w_t (e.g., a selected-feature indicator vector, or a matrix encoding a decision path) evolves recursively via data-dependent feature resampling, the paper models the trajectory as a stochastic process in a bounded convex Hilbert-space set W_t, defines a one-step "propagated discrepancy" condition (Eq. 4.3: ‖pith_t^j‖ ≤ (1+δ_t)‖𝔼[Δ_{t-1}^{-j}]‖), and proves a general recursive stability bound (Proposition 4.4) whose price of recursion M_T accumulates the per-step inflation factors (1+δ_t) and state-space radii. This is specialized to (i) random forward selection, where the state is a binary selection vector and the orthogonal-design case (X^TX=nI_d) makes the bagged estimator exactly β_B = E_{w_B}β, and (ii) a simplified "max-edge dyadic" random forest, where a decision tree's local leaf node containing a query point x is encoded by a feature-split-count matrix w_t, and a novel affine representation (Proposition 4.6) links this encoding to the tree's local prediction under an additive data-generating model.

4. **Empirical validation.** Two kinds of experiments support the theory: (a) simulations comparing the closed-form II/FI predictions against Monte-Carlo averages over 300 realizations (Figures 2–3, varying γ, p, q, B; n=400, d∈{100,...,900}) and 100 repetitions for the forward-selection/random-forest bounds (Figures 4, 6; theoretical curves vs. empirical FI); and (b) a separate motivating study (Section 2.2, Appendix S.1) using the MARSadd benchmark (Friedman 1991) — a mix of a weak linear signal across many features and a strong nonlinear signal in four features — plus four real UCI-style tabular datasets, where random forests are trained over a hyperparameter grid (max_features, max_samples, max_depth, 500 Monte-Carlo repetitions per configuration) and FI/II are regressed against the train-test MSE gap via linear R², mutual information, and partial mutual information (residual-based, using RF/DT/AdaBoost/GBRT residualizers).

## Results & Highlights

- **Linear-model theory (Theorem 3.1, Figures 2–3):** the single-subsampled minimum-norm OLS estimator exhibits a double-descent-shaped instability curve peaking at the interpolation threshold γq=p for both II and FI; bagging (B=200 in Figure 3) suppresses this peak by transferring weight to the O(1/n) cross-submodel terms, and the theoretical closed forms (Table 1) match Monte-Carlo simulations (300 realizations, n=400, d up to 900) essentially exactly, with crosses (empirical) lying on the theoretical curves in both panels of Figures 2 and 3.
- **Generalization relevance of FI (Section 2.2/S.1, MARSadd benchmark, random forest, n=500, d=10, σ=1):** at mixing parameter ν=1, individual explanatory power is R²(II→Gap)=0.143 and R²(FI→Gap)=0.120, but the joint model reaches **R²(FI+II→Gap)=0.345** — more than double either term alone — with mutual information I((FI,II);Gap)=1.345 and both partial mutual informations positive (PMI(FI;Gap|II)=0.469, PMI(II;Gap|FI)=0.439). At ν=0, the effect is even larger: **R²(II→Gap)=0.231, R²(FI→Gap)=0.546, R²(FI+II→Gap)=0.675**, showing FI can be the dominant predictor of the generalization gap.
- **Robustness across estimators/datasets (Tables S.2–S.5):** across 96 synthetic estimator/residualizer/ν combinations (RF, DT, AdaBoost, GBRT base estimators × four PMI residualizers × six ν values) and 64 real-data estimator/residualizer/dataset combinations (diabetes, abalone, cpu_act, house_prices), the conditional contribution of FI after accounting for II — measured by PMI(FI;gap|II) and incremental ΔR² — is **positive in 100% of all 160 groups** (bootstrap positive rate = 1.0), indicating the FI-carries-extra-information finding is not an artifact of one dataset or estimator choice.
- **Random forward selection (Theorem 4.5, Figure 4):** the feature-bagged estimator satisfies FI ≤ [T²/(d−1)]·q/(1−q) for T ≲ q⁻¹, versus a non-bagged deterministic forward-selection baseline of exactly **FI = 2T/d**; bagging is therefore provably more stable whenever T ≲ (1−q)/q, and empirically smaller q yields larger stability gains without degrading parameter-estimation accuracy (Figure 4(b)).
- **Bagging-round requirement (Section 4.3):** for a typical tabular scale (n,d) ~ (10⁵, 10²), the paper computes that feature-side stability requires on the order of **10² bagging rounds** (from B ≳ d(1−q)/q), versus roughly **10⁵ rounds** implied by the dual instance-side result of Soloff et al. (2024a) (B ≳ n(1−p)/p) — a concrete, order-of-magnitude practical contrast between the two stabilization axes.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The FI concept itself is a fairly natural (if not deep) extension of II to the feature axis, but the paper's execution is substantive: an exact (not just upper-bounded) asymptotic characterization for bagged linear regression via random-matrix theory, plus a genuinely new recursive-stability toolkit for data-dependent, multi-round feature subsampling (needed because, unlike instance bagging, features are non-exchangeable and resampled repeatedly within a single tree). The affine-encoding trick for dyadic-tree local partitions (Proposition 4.6) is a clean, reusable device.

**Rigor:** The linear-model theory (Theorem 3.1) is exact under fairly strong distributional assumptions — isotropic Gaussian features (x ~ N(0,I)), Gaussian β*, and Gaussian noise — with universality claims (that the same results hold under general bounded-moment designs) asserted by citation to Hastie et al. (2022)/Chen et al. (2023)/Wu and Sun (2025) rather than proven in this paper. The recursive random-forest result (Theorem 4.7) is proved not for real CART/scikit-learn random forests but for a deliberately simplified "max-edge dyadic" forest that always splits at the coordinate midpoint and always picks the feature with the fewest prior splits among the candidate set — a real, self-acknowledged simplification of actual variance-reduction-based splitting rules used in practice (Section 4.2.2 explicitly frames this as "a simplified model of decision-tree partitions," citing Blanchard et al. 2007, Cai et al. 2023/2024). The Figure 6 experiments labeled "RF" appear to still use this simplified model rather than sklearn's actual RandomForestRegressor (which is used only in the separate, correlational PMI study of Section 2.2/Appendix S.1) — readers should not conflate the two.

**Reproducibility:** All theoretical claims come with full proofs in a 44-page appendix (S.2–S.3), and the simulation settings (n, d ranges, number of repetitions, hyperparameter grids) are stated explicitly enough to reproduce, though no code repository is linked in the excerpted pages.

**Significance / honesty about scope:** The paper is explicit that classical stability-based generalization bounds (Bousquet and Elisseeff, 2002) "do not directly apply to linear regression because the output is unbounded" (page 9) — i.e., the FI/II theory in Section 3 is a stability characterization, not a formal generalization bound, and the link to generalization is established only empirically via the PMI/R² study in Section 2.2, using a synthetic MARSadd benchmark plus four fairly small, classic UCI-style tabular datasets (diabetes, abalone, cpu_act, house_prices) — none large-scale or high-dimensional by modern ML standards. This is a real gap between the paper's theoretical contribution (stability under bagging) and its motivating claim (stability relates to generalization), which is handled honestly but not closed.

**Red flags / missing ablations:** No experiments probe correlated (non-isotropic) feature designs, even though correlated features are precisely the setting most often invoked to explain why random-forest feature subsampling helps (Mentch and Zhou, 2020; Curth et al., 2024, both cited); the linear-model theory is isotropic-Gaussian only. The random forward selection and random forest bounds are explicitly restricted to "shallow-recursion" regimes (T ≲ q⁻¹ or T ≲ (1−q)/q); the paper's own Figure 4(b) notes the theoretical bound's gap to the empirical curve "widens" as T grows, i.e., the guarantee is loosest exactly where deep trees (the practically relevant random-forest regime) live.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated new stability concept (FI) with both a shared-seed and a coupled-seed formalization, filling a genuine gap since prior algorithmic-stability literature focuses almost exclusively on the instance axis.
- Exact (not just order-of-magnitude) asymptotic formulas for bagged linear regression, with all simulations matching theory closely across a range of aspect ratios γ and subsampling ratios (p,q).
- Directly extends and pairs with an existing, well-regarded result (Soloff et al. 2024a's infinite instance-bagging guarantee), giving the paper's practical bagging-round comparison (~10² vs ~10⁵ rounds) real interpretability.
- Broad robustness checks (Tables S.2–S.5: 160 estimator/dataset/residualizer combinations, 100% positive-rate for FI's incremental contribution) strengthen the empirical motivation beyond a single cherry-picked configuration.

## Limitations

- Theorem 3.1's exact asymptotics rely on isotropic Gaussian features/coefficients; no correlated-design experiments or theory are given, despite correlated features being central to the standard "why random forests work" debate the paper engages with.
- The random-forest stability theorem (4.7) is proved for a simplified max-edge dyadic tree model, not the CART/Gini-based splitting used in practice; the gap between this model and real random forests is acknowledged but not quantified.
- The link between FI and true out-of-sample generalization is established only via correlational statistics (R², MI, PMI) on a synthetic MARSadd benchmark and four modest real datasets, not via a formal stability-to-generalization bound (the paper notes classical Bousquet–Elisseeff-style bounds don't apply here due to unbounded linear-regression outputs).
- Guarantees for recursive algorithms (forward selection, random forests) hold only in shallow-recursion regimes (T ≲ q⁻¹ or similar); the paper is transparent that the bound loosens as T grows, which is exactly the deep-tree regime most practitioners use.

## Relevance

对做集成学习/随机森林理论、算法稳定性或高维统计的研究者，本文提供了一个可直接借用的"特征不稳定性"分析框架和渐近工具（尤其是线性模型下的精确随机矩阵结果），可用于进一步研究特征装袋在相关特征、深层递归或非高斯设计下的行为，也为随机森林超参数（max_features、树深）选择提供了稳定性视角的理论依据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the linear-model asymptotic formulas (Theorem 3.1) change under correlated (non-isotropic) feature covariance, which is the regime where feature bagging is most often argued to matter in practice?
- Can the max-edge dyadic random forest result (Theorem 4.7) be extended to genuine CART splitting rules (variance/Gini-based) rather than the midpoint/max-edge simplification, and would the stability-vs-recursion-depth tradeoff still favor shallow trees?
- Does the FI-generalization relationship observed on MARSadd and four small tabular datasets hold on larger, higher-dimensional, or non-tabular (e.g., image/text feature) benchmarks?
- Can the paper's finite-bagging concentration result (Proposition 4.8) be sharpened beyond Hoeffding/union-bound to match the tighter constants typically achievable for tree ensembles, and would that change the practical "10² vs 10⁵ bagging rounds" comparison?
- Is there a way to connect FI directly to a formal excess-risk or generalization bound (rather than relying on empirical R²/PMI evidence), closing the gap the authors themselves flag regarding unbounded-output linear regression?
