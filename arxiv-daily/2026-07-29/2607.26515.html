FP4 Quantization RL Post-Training GRPO HiFloat4 Activation Outliers

The dominant error in end-to-end FP4 RL training is rollout-side activation underflow — not training-side quantization.

该论文提出 Rollout-ResQ——仅对 FP4 rollout 激活值加一个 2:4 稀疏残差修正项，配合三级层次化缩放的 HiFloat4 格式，在 Qwen2.5-3B/GSM8K 上把全 4 比特端到端 RL 训练与 BF16 的精度差距从 4.9% 压缩到 1.1%。

HiFloat4 Format for End-To-End Reinforcement Learning Post-Training of Large Language Models ↗
Hei Yi Mak, Shadan Golestan, Hoang Le, Mehran Taghian Jazi, et al. (13 authors) · 2026-07-29 · arXiv:2607.26515
All 13 authors affiliated with Huawei; preprint, not yet peer-reviewed. Follow-on to a companion HiFloat4 pretraining paper.
Key Metric
4.93%→1.06%
GSM8K-test, Qwen2.5-3B, HiF4 format. BF16-accuracy gap shrinks from naive-FP4 (82.03%) to Rollout-ResQ-S2:4 (85.90%), beating the strongest prior baseline OCC (−2.73 pts) and SmoothQuant (−8.42 pts). Under open-standard MXFP4, the same recipe narrows 13.65% → 5.31%.
Why This Matters

Sets a practical direction for FP4 hardware/format co-design in RL post-training — an increasingly costly bottleneck as chain-of-thought rollout trajectories grow longer.

Critical Edge

All results come from simulated FP4 quantization on only two small models (3B, 7B) on math tasks — the headline 2×–2.67× speedup is a theoretical FLOP estimate, never measured on real hardware.

01 Methodology
A
Setup. GRPO post-training in VeRL (vLLM rollout + FSDP training), W4A4 on every linear layer, comparing HiFloat4 (3-level hierarchical scaling, 64-elem blocks) vs. MXFP4 (single E8M0 scale, 32-elem blocks).
B
Diagnose. Ablation crossing {BF16, FP4} training × {BF16, W4A16, W4A8, W16A4, FP4} rollout isolates rollout-activation quantization as the bottleneck. Outliers inflate block scale, driving >80% of activations to zero ("zero collapse") in some layers. Counterintuitively, high-precision training + FP4 rollout is worse than fully-FP4.
C
Fix: Rollout-ResQ. Compute residual ΔX = X − Q(X), re-quantize to FP4, apply a hardware-friendly sparsity mask S (2:4 semi-structured, channel-wise, or block), add back only in the rollout matmul: Ŷ = Q(X)Q(W)ᵀ + S(Q(ΔX))Q(W)ᵀ.
D
Baselines. Compared against SmoothQuant, RHT (Hadamard rotation), and OCC (dynamic block-level fallback) — all PTQ methods adapted from static inference to the non-stationary RL rollout distribution.
Trained: Qwen2.5-3B on GSM8K (~100GB peak, ~10hrs) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B on DAPO-Math-17K (~370GB, ~20hrs). FP4 is simulated — no native FP4 hardware was available.

03 Strengths & Limitations
Strengths
  • Clean diagnostic ablation (Table 1) isolates rollout activation quantization as the true bottleneck, rather than assuming it a priori.
  • Honestly foregrounds the counterintuitive negative result — higher-precision training policy making things worse — instead of burying it.
  • Broad validation: 2 model scales, 2 FP4 formats, 4 math benchmark suites, with convergence curves shown.
  • Quantified overhead analysis with multiple sparsity variants so practitioners can pick their accuracy/speed point.
  • Transparent Limitations section names exactly what wasn't tested.
Limitations
  • No native FP4 hardware — 2×–2.67× speedup claims are theoretical FLOP counts, not measured wall-clock.
  • Only 3B/7B models on math-reasoning tasks; code, agentic, and non-math RLVR settings untested.
  • Only one sparsity ratio (50%) explored; other levels left to future work.
  • Best-performing variant (Dense vs. S2:4 vs. S50%) differs by benchmark — no single universally dominant recipe.
  • Baselines (OCC, RHT, SmoothQuant) show inconsistent, sometimes unstable behavior, weakening "beats all baselines" framing.
04 Questions for Further Exploration
  • Would gains hold on native FP4 tensor cores, or does the theoretical speedup evaporate under memory-bound sparse-kernel overhead?
  • Does the rollout-activation-underflow bottleneck generalize to code, agentic tool use, or long-horizon dialogue RLVR domains?
  • How does Rollout-ResQ behave at 70B+ scale, where outlier severity and dynamic range are known to differ?
  • Could an adaptive (per-layer, per-step) sparsity ratio close the remaining 1.1%/5.3% gaps further, and at what scheduling cost?
  • Given OCC's instability at 7B, is there a deeper link between model scale, outlier severity, and correction granularity (tensor- vs. block-level)?
