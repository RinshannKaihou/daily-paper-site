> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 CoRAS（Conformalized Rate-Adaptive Sensing），通过"水平外推 + 垂直校准"两步预测每张图像的重建误差达标停止时间，并用满合形预测（full conformal prediction）给出该停止时间的有限样本边际覆盖保证；在 Fashion-MNIST 和 M4Raw 脑部 MRI 上，CoRAS 在与两种固定采样率基线（约 90–95% 覆盖率，满足 α=0.1 目标）相当的前提下，把平均"超额采样率"从约 0.24（原始损失固定率）/0.18（模型固定率）降到约 0.12（Fashion-MNIST），以及从约 0.29/0.16 降到约 0.08（M4Raw），且更多的额外采样被分配给复杂度更高的图像。

> **TL;DR:** CoRAS predicts an image-specific "stopping time" — the first acquisition rate at which reconstruction error drops below a target level — by extrapolating the early reconstruction-residual trajectory (horizontal step) and correcting that extrapolation with calibration images sharing a similar state (vertical step), then wraps the corrected prediction in full conformal prediction to get finite-sample marginal coverage of the true stopping time (plus an approximate conditional-coverage theorem under an RKHS smoothness assumption). On Fashion-MNIST (target error c=0.003, α=0.1, decision time t0=6/32) and M4Raw brain MRI (c=0.0015, α=0.1, t0=9/38), CoRAS matches the ~90-95% coverage of two fixed-rate conformal baselines (raw-loss-calibrated and model-loss-calibrated) while cutting mean excess sampling rate from roughly 0.24 (raw fixed-rate) / 0.18 (model fixed-rate) to roughly 0.12 on Fashion-MNIST, and from roughly 0.29 / 0.16 to roughly 0.08 on M4Raw — spending the saved measurements on the images the entropy-stratified analysis shows are hardest to reconstruct.

## 核心贡献

- 把"何时停止采集/压缩"形式化为预测一个图像特有的、事先不可观测的"目标停止时间"（重建损失首次降到阈值 c 以下的采样步）问题，区别于标准 conformal prediction 中"给定协变量预测响应"的设定——这里测试时没有协变量，只有部分观测到的重建路径。
- 提出水平（horizontal）预测：基于正交变换（傅里叶/小波）逐带（band）残差的对数近似线性衰减这一理论模型（有序尾模型，Proposition 1/2），用单调多项式对每张图像早期的"下一带残差"历史做图像级交叉验证阶数选择的外推，得到停止时间的初步估计。
- 提出垂直（vertical）校准：用状态 S=(水平预测, 重建熵) 的带宽核加权近邻，从校准集图像的真实停止时间中估计并修正水平预测的系统偏差，再对修正后的残差做满合形预测（leave-one-out 排名 p 值），得到停止时间的上界。
- 给出两个理论结果：定理 1/2 证明在可交换性和损失单调性假设下停止规则具有边际有效性 P{L≤c}≥1-α；定理 3 在 RKHS 光滑性假设（Assumption 2）下给出近似条件覆盖的误差界，量化"状态相关偏差项"和 O(√(log n / n)) 的随机项。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes the stopping-time prediction problem**: defines the (unobserved) target stopping time as the first acquisition step at which per-pixel squared reconstruction error falls below a target c, and frames CoRAS as predicting/upper-bounding this quantity from the partially observed reconstruction path rather than from baseline covariates — a setting the authors argue is new to the conformal prediction literature.
- **Horizontal prediction via a band-residual extrapolation model**: derives a continuous "ordered-tail model" (Assumption 1, Proposition 1) showing that under a power-law transform-coefficient decay and logarithmically spaced acquisition bands, the log of the next-band residual is exactly linear in acquisition step in the stationary case, and generalizes this to a local polynomial approximation for time-varying decay (Proposition 2, Appendix B.1). A per-image, cross-validated, monotone-constrained polynomial fit (order chosen from {1,...,5}) extrapolates this residual history past the decision time t0 to produce a horizontal plug-in stopping-time estimate.
- **Vertical calibration with a bandwidth-selected kernel-weighted correction**: defines a 2-D state (horizontal prediction, 16-bin decision-time reconstruction entropy), computes leave-one-out kernel weights over calibration images with similar states, and uses them to debias the horizontal prediction before applying full conformal prediction (via a rank-based p-value over augmented, permutation-invariant residual scores) to produce a finite-sample-valid upper bound on the stopping time.
- **Two coverage theorems**: Theorem 1/2 establish exact finite-sample marginal validity, P{L_{n+1}(stopping rule) ≤ c} ≥ 1-α, under exchangeability and a monotone-loss assumption, for both the raw/model fixed-rate baselines and the full horizontal-vertical (HV) adaptive rule. Theorem 3 gives an approximate conditional-validity bound under an RKHS-smoothness assumption (Assumption 2) on the horizontal-prediction error, decomposing the conditional coverage gap into a state-dependent bias term (via maximum mean discrepancy between a state and its weighted neighborhood) and a stochastic term of order √(log n / n).

## Methodology

CoRAS operates on a fixed rate grid 0 < θ_1 < ... < θ_{t_max} ≤ 1 and a reconstruction model that produces Ŷ_i(t) from partial observations X_i(θ_t). At a pre-fixed early decision time t_0 (chosen so most images still have loss above target c), the system has acquired every image up to t_0 and observed the sequence of reconstructed images Ŷ_i(1),...,Ŷ_i(t_0).

**Horizontal step.** Using an orthonormal transform (normalized Fourier transform for Fourier acquisition, orthonormal wavelet for multiresolution acquisition), the reconstruction loss decomposes exactly into a sum of bandwise residual energies (Parseval's identity over nested orthogonal projections, Eq. 7). The "next-band residual" I_{i,t} at each observed step is computable without knowing the full image because it only requires the newly acquired transform coefficients. Under a power-law tail model for the transform-coefficient magnitude (Assumption 1) and logarithmically spaced band cutoffs, log I*_{i,t} is (locally) linear/polynomial in t (Propositions 1-2). CoRAS fits, per test image, a monotone-constrained polynomial of image-wise cross-validated order (≤5) to the log residual history H_{i,t}=log(I_{i,t}+ε) for t=2,...,t_0, then extrapolates it forward to build a horizontal loss proxy L̂^H_i(t) and a plug-in stopping time T̂^H_i = min{t ≥ t_0 : L̂^H_i(t) ≤ c}.

**Vertical step.** Because the horizontal extrapolation uses only a short pre-decision history, it can be biased, especially for complex images. CoRAS defines a 2-D state S_i = (T̂^H_i, Ω̂_i), where Ω̂_i is the 16-bin Shannon entropy of the reconstruction at t_0 (a decision-time-observable complexity proxy). For a bandwidth pair g=(h_T, h_Ω), it computes leave-one-out kernel weights over calibration images with nearby states and uses them to correct the horizontal prediction: T̂^{HV}_j(t) = T̂^H_j + Σ_k ω̂_{j,k}(t){T*_k(t) - T̂^H_k}. The bandwidth is reselected per candidate stopping time t via a permutation-invariant algorithm (required for exact conformal validity; an efficient online variant is given in Appendix C). The resulting residual scores R_j(t) feed a standard full-conformal rank p-value p^{HV}(t) = (1/(n+1))Σ 1{R_j(t) ≥ R_{n+1}(t)}, and the final stopping rule Û^{HV}_{n+1} takes the largest candidate t with p^{HV}(t) > α.

**Theory.** Theorem 1/2 prove exact marginal validity via the standard conformal argument (exchangeability + monotone loss ⟹ T_{n+1} ≤ conformal quantile). Theorem 3 additionally assumes the horizontal residual e*_j = T*_j - T̂^H_j decomposes as m_n(S_j) + ξ_j with m_n in a bounded RKHS (Assumption 2) and ξ_j i.i.d. sub-Gaussian, bounded-density noise; it then bounds the conditional coverage gap by 4MΛ_{κ,n}E[D_{κ,n}|S_{n+1}] + η_g√(log n/n), where D_{κ,n} is a maximum-mean-discrepancy term measuring how well the kernel-weighted neighborhood reproduces the true state.

**Datasets/models.** Fashion-MNIST (28×28 resized to 32×32, Fourier acquisition, 32-step rate grid, CNN reconstruction model trained on 2000 images for 10 epochs, 6000/6000 calibration/test images, c=0.003, α=0.1, t_0=6). M4Raw brain MRI (multi-coil, 256×256, 195 phase-encoding lines, 38-step rate grid, residual-gated U-Net trained on 72 disjoint studies for 15 epochs, 30 studies/540 slices for calibration and 56 studies/1008 slices for test, split at the study level, c=0.0015, α=0.1, t_0=9, corresponding to 50/195 lines). Because multi-coil RSS combination is nonlinear, M4Raw's next-band residuals cannot be measured causally online; they are instead computed retrospectively from the fully sampled reference, so the M4Raw evaluation is explicitly noted by the authors as retrospective rather than a fully causal deployment test.

## Results & Highlights

- **Fashion-MNIST coverage/efficiency (Figure 1, 50 independent runs)**: all three stopping rules (raw fixed-rate, model fixed-rate, CoRAS adaptive) keep coverage at or above the target 1-α=90%, but CoRAS reaches this target with a substantially lower mean sampling rate — approximately 0.45 (adaptive) vs. approximately 0.555 (raw fixed-rate) and approximately 0.495 (model fixed-rate) — and a lower mean excess sampling rate (the gap between selected and true target rate) of approximately 0.12 (adaptive) vs. approximately 0.235 (raw fixed-rate) and approximately 0.175 (model fixed-rate).
- **M4Raw coverage/efficiency (Figure 3, 50 independent runs)**: all three rules again meet the α=0.1 target (coverage ≳90%), while CoRAS uses a mean sampling rate of approximately 0.45 vs. approximately 0.65-0.66 (raw fixed-rate) and approximately 0.54 (model fixed-rate), and a mean excess sampling rate of approximately 0.08 vs. approximately 0.29 (raw fixed-rate) and approximately 0.155 (model fixed-rate) — the largest efficiency gain reported in the paper.
- **Adaptivity to image complexity (Figures 2 and 4)**: stratifying test images/slices into entropy deciles (Fashion-MNIST) or quintiles (M4Raw) by true-image entropy shows CoRAS's sampling rate rises monotonically with entropy (fixed-rate baselines are constant by construction), and its excess sampling rate stays the lowest of the three methods across nearly the whole entropy range on both datasets — direct evidence the saved measurements on easy images are being reallocated to hard ones rather than simply reducing average quality.
- **State validity check**: on Fashion-MNIST, decision-time reconstruction entropy Ω̂_i (available at test time) correlates with the unobservable true-image entropy Ω_i at Pearson r=0.935 and Spearman r=0.931 across the 6000 test images (Appendix A.1, Figure 6); a similarly strong correlation is reported for M4Raw's 1008 test slices (Appendix A.2, Figure 8), supporting the use of entropy as a state coordinate for the vertical calibration.
- **Coverage still degrades at the tail**: for both datasets, coverage of all three rules — including CoRAS — falls below the 90% target specifically in the highest true-entropy stratum (the paper attributes this to large, harder-to-predict target stopping times for the most complex images rather than a general calibration failure).
- The paper reports no head-to-head comparison against a learned/RL-based active-acquisition policy (e.g., the Pineda et al. 2020 MRI k-space RL baseline it cites in related work) or against any non-conformal adaptive baseline; all three compared methods are variants of the authors' own conformal framework.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — predicting an unobserved "stopping time" from a partially observed reconstruction path, with no covariates available at test time — is a genuinely distinct conformal-prediction setting from the standard covariate-conditional or risk-controlling-prediction-set literature the paper surveys (Bates et al. 2021, Angelopoulos et al. 2024b, Learn-then-Test), and the horizontal/vertical decomposition is explicitly and thoughtfully analogized to synthetic-control methods from causal inference (outcome regression + matching), which is a creative and clarifying framing rather than a superficial one.

**Rigor**: The marginal validity proofs (Theorems 1-2) rest on standard, well-established conformal machinery (exchangeability + monotone loss), which is a low-risk, solid foundation. The more ambitious Theorem 3 (approximate conditional validity) leans on a fairly strong idealization — Assumption 2 requires the horizontal-residual signal to live in a bounded RKHS with i.i.d. sub-Gaussian, bounded-density noise independent of the state — and the paper is commendably candid that this is "an idealized continuous approximation" whose discrete analogue (used in the actual implementation) only approximately satisfies the theorem's premises. The underlying ordered-tail residual-decay model (Assumption 1, power-law transform coefficients) is similarly acknowledged in-text as not exactly representing "local texture, reconstruction artifacts, and other deviations in coefficient magnitude" — a specific, paper-stated caveat rather than a generic disclaimer.

**Reproducibility**: Code is released on GitHub, hyperparameters (bandwidth grids, polynomial order cap q_max=5, decision times, target thresholds c) are stated explicitly in the main text and appendix, and both datasets (Fashion-MNIST, M4Raw) are public. This is a genuine strength for a theory-heavy stat.ML paper.

**Significance**: The stopping-time framing generalizes beyond imaging (the Discussion explicitly connects it to LLM test-time compute and spectroscopy scan averaging), and a distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee for "how much to acquire" is a practically useful primitive if it holds up under further scrutiny.

**Red flags / weak spots**: (1) Only two datasets are tested, and both use the authors' own reconstruction models (a small CNN, a custom residual-gated U-Net) rather than any established, widely benchmarked accelerated-MRI reconstruction network (e.g., standard fastMRI baselines), making it unclear how CoRAS interacts with reconstruction models trained differently. (2) The M4Raw next-band residual I_{i,t}^{MRI} is computed retrospectively from the fully sampled RSS reference because multi-coil combination is nonlinear (Appendix A.2) — the paper is explicit about this, but it means the flagship medical-imaging experiment is not actually a causal, online-deployable instantiation of the method as described in Section 4.1, only a retrospective proxy. (3) No comparison against the RL-based active MRI k-space sampling baseline (Pineda et al. 2020) that the related-work section itself cites as the dominant alternative approach to the same application, so it remains unknown whether CoRAS's efficiency gains are competitive with (rather than merely a valid complement to) that line of work. (4) The reported figure-based coverage/sampling-rate numbers (Figures 1-4) are given only graphically, without a numeric results table, making precise cross-paper comparison harder than it should be for a methods paper making efficiency claims. (5) The Discussion section itself flags that the fixed, single decision time t_0 is a real limitation (the rule cannot revisit its decision, and naively repeating conformal calibration at multiple decision points would break the finite-sample guarantee) — an honest, paper-acknowledged gap rather than something uncovered by outside critique.

## Strengths

- A conceptually clean and well-motivated new problem formulation (predicting/upper-bounding an unobserved image-specific stopping time from a partial acquisition path with no covariates), clearly differentiated from adjacent conformal-prediction and risk-control literatures.
- Two-tier theory (exact marginal validity plus an approximate conditional-validity bound with an interpretable bias/variance decomposition) that goes beyond the marginal-only guarantees of the fixed-rate baselines it compares against.
- Consistent empirical story across two quite different domains (natural grayscale images and multi-coil brain MRI k-space) showing the adaptive rule hits the same coverage target as fixed-rate baselines while using visibly fewer measurements, and specifically fewer measurements on images/slices that are already easy.
- Explicit, honest self-critique embedded in the paper itself (idealized-model caveats, retrospective M4Raw evaluation, single-decision-time limitation, need for sequentially valid stopping rules as future work), which strengthens rather than undermines trust in the reported results.
- Public code release enabling independent verification.

## Limitations

- Only a single, fixed early decision time t_0 is used, and the stopping rule cannot revisit its decision partway through acquisition; a multi-checkpoint version is left as future work because naive reuse of conformal calibration across multiple decision points would break the finite-sample guarantee (explicitly stated by the authors).
- Theorem 3's approximate conditional-validity guarantee depends on a bounded-RKHS/sub-Gaussian idealization (Assumption 2) that is acknowledged not to hold exactly for the discrete, grid-valued residuals actually used in the implementation.
- Evaluated on only two datasets (Fashion-MNIST, M4Raw), both with the authors' own reconstruction models; no test against a widely used, community-standard accelerated-MRI benchmark or reconstruction network, and no comparison against RL-based active-acquisition baselines from the cited related work.
- M4Raw's core observability assumption (that next-band residuals can be computed online from acquired coefficients) does not hold for multi-coil nonlinear RSS combination, so the M4Raw results are retrospective rather than a live deployment demonstration.
- Coverage for all methods, including CoRAS, still dips below the target in the highest-complexity (highest-entropy) image stratum on both datasets, indicating the hardest cases remain the least well-controlled.
- Results are reported only as box/bar plots without an accompanying numeric table, limiting precision for downstream comparison.

## Relevance

该论文的"路径式停止时间预测 + conformal 校准"框架对做医学影像加速采集、以及任何"边观测边决定何时停止"场景（如推理模型的 test-time scaling、时间序列早期分类）的有限样本保证设计具有直接参考价值，其水平-外推/垂直-校准两步结构也为构建无协变量场景下的 conformal 方法提供了可复用思路。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a sequentially valid version of CoRAS (revisiting the stopping decision at multiple checkpoints rather than one fixed t_0) be constructed without sacrificing the finite-sample marginal guarantee, as the Discussion section speculates?
- How does CoRAS's efficiency compare quantitatively against RL/policy-gradient active-acquisition baselines (e.g., Pineda et al. 2020) on the same MRI data, rather than only against the authors' own fixed-rate conformal baselines?
- Would the method's coverage gap in the highest-entropy stratum close with a richer state representation (beyond horizontal prediction + 16-bin entropy), or is it fundamentally limited by the short pre-decision history available at t_0?
- How sensitive are the reported efficiency gains to the choice of decision time t_0 — is there a principled way to choose t_0 itself, trading off "wasted" early acquisition against horizontal-prediction accuracy?
- Can the next-band residual be made causally observable in multi-coil MRI (rather than retrospective, as in the M4Raw experiment) so the method could actually be deployed online in accelerated MRI acquisition?
- Does the approximate conditional-validity bound of Theorem 3 remain informative (non-vacuous) for the actual bandwidth pairs and sample sizes used in the Fashion-MNIST/M4Raw experiments, or is it primarily of qualitative/asymptotic interest?
