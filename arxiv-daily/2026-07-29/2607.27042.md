> **一句话总结：** 论文把 GPTQ（Babai 最近平面算法）从"单侧"矩阵量化推广到"双侧"（左右各乘一个固定非奇异基矩阵 A、B），证明直接向量化会把 Gram 矩阵变成 Kronecker 积、把扫描代价从三次推高到四次 O(m²n²)，而作者提出的 GPTQ-2D 利用"同一反对角线上的舍入误差互不影响、可并行舍入"这一秩一反馈的结构性质，把扫描代价严格降回 O(mn·max(m,n))，并证明（定理 2）其产生的取整结果与直接对向量化后的一维算法逐坐标扫描完全一致（bit-exact，非近似）。

> **TL;DR:** This is a pure algorithms/complexity paper (cs.DS + cs.LG) with no empirical benchmarks: it studies two-sided adaptive rounding ||A(Z−X)B||²_F, shows that naively vectorizing and running one-sided GPTQ under the induced Kronecker Gram H⊗G costs O(m²n²) sweep work — quartic in the matrix dimension — for both "vectorized adaptive rounding" and a "direct anti-diagonal dense updates" baseline (Table 1), and proves (Theorem 2) that its new algorithm, GPTQ-2D, produces the bit-identical rounded matrix in O(mn·max(m,n)) sweep work, cubic overall for square matrices, matching one-sided GPTQ's O(m³) total cost when m≥n. It also shows GPTQ-2D can be "dropped in" as the rounding step of YAQA (Tseng et al., 2026), whose reference implementation applies Kronecker-factored feedback as dense products at O(mn·max(m,n)²) — a full factor of min(m,n) slower than GPTQ-2D's O(mn·max(m,n)).

## 核心贡献

- 把双侧量化目标 ||A(Z−X)B||²_F 通过列主序向量化恒等式改写为向量化坐标 vec(X) 在 Kronecker Gram 矩阵 H⊗G = (BBᵀ)⊗(AᵀA) 下的一维自适应舍入问题，指出直接套用一维 GPTQ 算法虽然正确但对 mn 维坐标做稠密扫描代价是 O(m²n²)，square 矩阵下为四次。
- 证明位于同一反对角线（i+j=常数）上的舍入误差反馈秩一更新互不可达（定理 1：任意满足反馈依赖的拓扑序都产生与列主序完全相同的取整轨迹），因此同一反对角线的条目可以安全并行舍入——这是 GPTQ-2D 能降复杂度的核心结构性质。
- 提出 GPTQ-2D 算法（Algorithm 3/4）：维护校正矩阵 Y 及辅助缓冲 C=LE，把每个误差只沿它自己所在的行、列传播，而不是像稠密实现那样铺满整个右下矩形，从而把扫描代价从 O(m²n²) 降到 O(mn·max(m,n))，并给出分块（lazy block update，借鉴 GPTQ 的分块技巧）与"padded skew layout"（反对角线/行/列三种访存都是固定步长的数组布局）两种工程化实现路径。
- 严格证明（定理 2）GPTQ-2D、直接反对角线稠密更新（Algorithm 2）与对 vec(X) 做列主序扫描的一维算法三者产生完全相同的取整矩阵 Z——不是近似等价，而是逐条目 bit-exact 的轨迹等价，附appendix给出嵌套列扫描（Algorithm 5）与隐式-Y（Algorithm 6）两个等价变体并列出各自复杂度。
- 指出该方法可直接替换 YAQA（Tseng et al., 2026）参考实现中把 Kronecker 反馈当稠密矩阵积处理的舍入步骤（四次代价 O(mn·max(m,n)²)），使其降到三次 O(mn·max(m,n))，同时保留 YAQA 原有的 Kronecker-factored Hessian 近似不变。

## Key Contributions

- Recasts the two-sided rounding objective ||A(Z−X)B||²_F, via the vectorization identity vec(ARB) = (Bᵀ⊗A)vec(R), as one-dimensional adaptive rounding of vec(X) under the Kronecker Gram matrix H⊗G, and identifies that the natural realization costs O(m²n²) sweep work — quartic — because the right basis B couples all columns together once vectorized.
- Proves (Theorem 1, "order invariance") that adaptive rounding is insensitive to any visitation order respecting the feedback-dependency DAG, and (as a corollary of the Kronecker feedback matrix F = Uᵀ⊗L being supported only on a lower-right rectangle) that entries sharing an anti-diagonal i+j=s are mutually unreachable and can be rounded in parallel — the structural insight the whole paper exploits.
- Introduces GPTQ-2D (Algorithm 3), which maintains the corrected matrix Y together with an auxiliary buffer C=LE and pushes each rounding error only along its own row (via U) and column (via L) rather than densely over the whole lower-right rectangle, cutting the anti-diagonal sweep from O(m²n²) (Algorithm 2, "direct anti-diagonal dense updates") to O(mn·max(m,n)), with total cost O((max(m,n))³) dominated by the one-time LDL factorization of the two Gram matrices — asymptotically matching one-sided GPTQ on the same matrix for m≥n.
- Proves exact trajectory equivalence (Theorem 2) between GPTQ-2D, the direct dense anti-diagonal realization, and the reference one-dimensional column-major sweep of vec(X) — the rounded output Z is provably identical, not approximately equal — and supplies two further equivalent realizations in the appendices (a sequential nested column-major sweep, Algorithm 5, and an implicit-Y form that carries only a triangular feedback buffer, Algorithm 6), plus a blocked variant (Algorithm 4) that fuses many small row/column updates into GPTQ-style lazy block matrix products, and a "padded skew" array layout (Section 5) that makes all three access patterns (anti-diagonal, matrix row, matrix column) fixed-stride slices for efficient array-framework implementation.
- Shows the method is a practical drop-in accelerator for YAQA (Tseng et al., 2026), the closest prior two-sided/Kronecker-factored rounding scheme: YAQA's reference implementation applies its feedback as dense matrix products at O(mn·(max(m,n))²) — quartic — while GPTQ-2D computes the identical rounding (up to sweep direction) in O(mn·max(m,n)) — cubic — leaving YAQA's Kronecker-factored Hessian sketch untouched.

## Methodology

The paper studies rounding a real matrix X ∈ ℝ^(m×n) to an integer matrix Z under the two-sided quadratic objective ||A(Z−X)B||²_F for fixed nonsingular basis matrices A ∈ ℝ^(m×m) and B ∈ ℝ^(n×n) (the familiar one-sided GPTQ/Babai case is B=I). Using vec(ARB) = (Bᵀ⊗A)vec(R) and the Kronecker mixed-product property, the objective becomes vec(R)ᵀ(H⊗G)vec(R) with G=AᵀA and H=BBᵀ — i.e., exactly one-dimensional adaptive rounding (a single forward sweep of Babai's nearest-plane algorithm) applied to the mn-dimensional vector vec(X) under the Kronecker Gram matrix H⊗G.

The key structural fact (Section 4.1) is that the LDL factorization of a Kronecker product factors per-axis: (H⊗G)⁻¹ = (U⊤⊗L) where L is the unit-lower-triangular row feedback factor from G⁻¹=LΛ_LLᵀ and U is the unit-upper-triangular column feedback factor from H⁻¹=UᵀΛ_UU. A scalar rounding error E_ij at entry (i,j) therefore produces a rank-one feedback update Y ← Y + L_{:,i} E_ij U_{j,:}, supported only on the lower-right rectangle {i,...,m}×{j,...,n}. Grouping entries by anti-diagonal I_s = {(i,j): i+j=s}, the paper shows no entry on a common anti-diagonal can reach another (Figure 1's dependency DAG), so by the order-invariance result (Theorem 1) the m+n−1 anti-diagonals can be processed in sequence with full parallelism within each anti-diagonal, reproducing the exact column-major vectorized trajectory.

Algorithm 2 realizes this literally (round each anti-diagonal, then apply the fused dense rank-one update to the entire remaining rectangle) but each of the mn updates still touches a block of size up to mn, giving O(m²n²) work. GPTQ-2D (Algorithm 3) avoids this by maintaining an auxiliary buffer C=LE alongside Y: each rounded error is folded once into C down its own column (O(max(m,n)) work) and pushed rightward through U only along its own row, so the total feedback work across the sweep is O(mn·max(m,n)) instead of quartic; Theorem 2 proves by induction over anti-diagonals (using the invariant C=LE) that this produces exactly the same Z as Algorithm 2. Section 4.3 then "blocks" the sweep (Algorithm 4) in the style of GPTQ's own lazy block updates: within a block of w anti-diagonals, updates are clipped to the block boundary; after the block, two band-like matrix products (a down-flush of accumulated error through L, a right-flush through U) deliver the remaining feedback in one dense product per block rather than many small ones, better suited to parallel hardware. Section 5 addresses the resulting three incompatible access patterns (anti-diagonal, matrix column, matrix row) by storing the working matrices in a "padded skew" array with row stride r=2m+n−2, so each access pattern becomes a plain fixed-stride slice at the cost of Θ((max(m,n))²) storage/compute overhead. Two further equivalent forms are given in appendices: a sequential nested column-major sweep (Algorithm 5, one-dimensional adaptive rounding per column with error propagated via U) and an implicit-Y form (Algorithm 6) that keeps only the triangular buffer C=LE and reconstructs corrected entries on demand.

## Results & Highlights

This is a theoretical algorithms paper — there are no experiments, datasets, or wall-clock benchmarks; all "results" are asymptotic complexity bounds proved via Theorems 1 and 2 and summarized in Table 1. The quantitative content is nonetheless concrete and comparative:

- **Sweep complexity vs. two explicit baselines (Table 1):** for the two-sided objective, both "vectorized adaptive rounding" (Algorithm 1 applied verbatim to vec(X)) and "direct anti-diagonal dense updates" (Algorithm 2) cost O(m²n²) sweep work; GPTQ-2D (Algorithms 3/4/6) cuts this to O(mn·max(m,n)) — a reduction by a factor of exactly Θ(min(m,n)), stated explicitly in the Introduction ("Reaching the same two-sided trajectory by vectorizing and sweeping costs O(m²n²)... a factor Θ(min(m,n)) more than the O(mn max(m,n)) sweep of GPTQ-2D").
- **Total cost matches one-sided GPTQ:** one-sided GPTQ on an m×m matrix costs O(m³) setup + O(m²n) sweep. GPTQ-2D's total end-to-end cost is O((max(m,n))³), dominated by the one-time O((max(m,n))³) LDL factorization of the two Gram matrices; for m≥n this is the same asymptotic order as one-sided GPTQ on the same matrix, i.e. "the two-sided extension is asymptotically free in all three [setup/sweep/depth] costs" (Table 1 caption).
- **Parallel depth:** GPTQ-2D's anti-diagonal sweep runs in O(max(m,n)) sequential parallel stages (m+n−1 anti-diagonals), versus O(mn) sequential depth for the equivalent nested column-major sweep (Algorithm 5) and O(mn) depth for the naive vectorized realization (Algorithm 1 on vec(X)) — a depth reduction of the same Θ(min(m,n)) order (Table 1).
- **Drop-in speedup for YAQA:** the paper states YAQA's (Tseng et al., 2026) reference implementation "applies the feedback as dense matrix products... O(mn(max(m,n))²) work, quartic for a square matrix," while GPTQ-2D "computes it in O(mn max(m,n)) sweep work, cubic for a square matrix, so it can be dropped in as YAQA's rounding step" — a concrete quartic-to-cubic delta on an existing published method's rounding step, though this claim is analytical (derived from YAQA's described reference implementation), not re-benchmarked empirically by the authors.

No wall-clock timings, GPU throughput numbers, or downstream quantization accuracy figures (e.g., LLM perplexity after applying GPTQ-2D to Kronecker-factored Hessians) are reported anywhere in the paper.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The central move — recognizing that the Kronecker-structured feedback matrix F=Uᵀ⊗L produces rank-one updates confined to lower-right rectangles, so entries on a shared anti-diagonal are independent — is a clean, non-obvious structural observation that yields a genuine asymptotic improvement (quartic to cubic) over the naive vectorized approach. The paper is honest that it is an extension, not a new rounding rule: "GPTQ-2D extends GPTQ rather than competing with it... one-sided adaptive rounding is the special case B=I."

**Rigor:** Unusually strong for this kind of systems/algorithms paper — both main claims are proved as formal theorems with full proofs in the body (Theorem 1 by strong induction on the dependency order; Theorem 2 by induction over anti-diagonals using the C=LE invariant), rather than asserted or only empirically demonstrated. The complexity table (Table 1) is internally consistent across five algorithm variants (Algorithms 1, 2, 3/4/6, 5), which is a good self-consistency check.

**Reproducibility:** Full pseudocode is given for all six algorithms (including the blocked and padded-skew-layout variants), which is a substitute for a code release, but no actual implementation, benchmark harness, or repository is mentioned anywhere in the paper — there is no way to independently verify the claimed constants or that the padded-skew-layout scheme (Section 5) delivers real speedups on real hardware, since the paper reports zero empirical timing.

**Significance:** The result is narrow but real: it converts an existing published two-sided rounding scheme (YAQA) from quartic to cubic asymptotic sweep cost, which matters when m,n scale with LLM weight-matrix dimensions (thousands to tens of thousands). However, the paper explicitly declines to address the more consequential open question of *how* to pick the basis matrices A and B for a given application (e.g., estimating Kronecker-factored Hessian approximations for neural network quantization) — "This paper does not address that estimation problem. The method should be read as a work-efficient way to carry out a given Kronecker-factored rounding, not as a new rounding rule" — so its practical impact is entirely contingent on downstream methods (like YAQA) that do address that modeling question.

**Red flags / weak points specific to this paper:** (1) Zero empirical validation — no runtime benchmarks against YAQA's reference implementation or against a naive O(m²n²) baseline are shown, so the practical magnitude of the claimed speedup (which depends on constants hidden in the O-notation, and on the Θ((max(m,n))²) storage overhead of the padded-skew layout) is untested. (2) The equivalence guarantee explicitly assumes A and B (and hence L, U) stay fixed throughout the sweep — the paper flags that if the basis matrices adapt to intermediate rounding decisions (e.g., data-dependent scaling, which is plausible in practical quantization pipelines), "exact equivalence to the fixed vectorized procedure need not hold," a caveat that limits applicability to purely static-basis settings. (3) Like one-sided GPTQ, the method makes no global-optimality claim and inherits the NP-hardness of exact closest-lattice-point computation (Dinur et al., 2003) — it reproduces a fixed greedy trajectory exactly and cheaply, but does not claim that trajectory is optimal.

## Strengths

- A genuinely non-trivial complexity result (quartic → cubic) proved with full, checkable theorems rather than asserted asymptotics.
- Complete pseudocode for six equivalent algorithmic realizations (dense anti-diagonal, buffered GPTQ-2D, blocked GPTQ-2D, nested column-major, implicit-Y), giving implementers real choice depending on hardware/memory constraints.
- Directly actionable for an existing published method: identifies exactly where YAQA's reference implementation is asymptotically suboptimal and shows the fix is a drop-in replacement of its rounding step.
- The "padded skew layout" (Section 5) thoughtfully addresses a real implementation obstacle (three incompatible access patterns — anti-diagonal, row, column — in array frameworks like PyTorch) rather than leaving it as a hand-wave.
- Self-aware framing of scope: explicitly separates the algorithmic question (how to compute a given Kronecker-factored rounding efficiently) from the modeling question (how to choose A, B), avoiding overclaiming applicability to quantization accuracy.

## Limitations

- No empirical results whatsoever: no wall-clock benchmarks, no GPU implementation, no comparison of measured speedup against YAQA's reference implementation or the direct dense baseline.
- Equivalence (Theorems 1–2) requires the basis matrices A, B — and thus the feedback factors L, U — to be fixed throughout the sweep; adaptive/data-dependent bases break the exact-equivalence guarantee.
- No floating-point/numerical-stability analysis of the reordered anti-diagonal computation versus the reference column-major sweep — Theorem 2 is an exact-arithmetic equivalence, and the paper does not discuss whether reordering operations changes floating-point rounding behavior in practice.
- Inherits GPTQ/Babai's lack of global-optimality guarantee; the paper is explicit that computing the true closest lattice point remains NP-hard, so GPTQ-2D is a fast way to reproduce a fixed greedy heuristic, not to improve its quality.
- The Θ((max(m,n))²) storage overhead of the padded skew layout, while described as "mild for square matrices," is not quantified for realistic rectangular (m≠n) LLM weight-matrix aspect ratios.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much wall-clock speedup does GPTQ-2D actually deliver over YAQA's reference dense-product implementation on realistic LLM weight-matrix shapes, and does the padded-skew-layout overhead erode the theoretical gain in practice?
- Does the exact-equivalence guarantee (Theorem 2) survive floating-point (non-exact) arithmetic, or can the reordered anti-diagonal accumulation introduce numerically meaningful divergence from the reference column-major trajectory at LLM scale?
- Can the method be extended to the adaptive-basis case the paper explicitly excludes (where A or B depend on intermediate rounding decisions), which seems relevant for more sophisticated quantization pipelines with data-dependent scaling?
- What is the empirical quantization-accuracy effect (e.g., downstream perplexity) of swapping YAQA's rounding step for GPTQ-2D, given that the two are only claimed equivalent "up to the direction of the sweep"?
- Could the anti-diagonal parallelism (O(max(m,n)) depth) be exploited for a GPU kernel that beats not just the O(mn·max(m,n)) sequential-work bound but also achieves near-peak hardware utilization, given the block/lazy-update structure described in Section 4.3?
