A frozen vision encoder's full layer-by-layer path — not just its final output — carries real, usable reliability evidence.

Representation Trajectories Matter: Complementary Evidence for OOD Detection and Image Classification Ignacio M. De la Jara, Cristian Rodriguez-Opazo, Hamed Damirchi, et al. · 2026-07-29
OOD detection representation trajectories image classification vision transformers Mahalanobis distance

Key metric

+4.41

avg. top-1 accuracy gain, 71/72 backbone-dataset cells improved (up to +9.81 pts on Stanford Cars, ResNet-50)

OOD detection: mean FPR95 falls up to −8.75 pts (kNN, 127/152 non-saturated cases)

Why this matters

A millisecond-cheap signal read straight from the frozen forward pass boosts OOD detection and classification across six major vision-architecture families — no retraining required.

Critical edge

Headline gains are averaged only over the 152 “non-saturated” cases out of 190 total backbone-benchmark pairs — the paper never reports what happens on the ~38 excluded cases.

Methodology

01

Treat the forward pass as trajectory τ(x)=(z₁,…,z_L). Each layer update splits into class-coherent transport (movement of the shared class route μ_l^c) and sample-specific residual innovation (r_l = z_l − μ_l^c).

02

A KL chain-rule identity (Eq. 5) formally shows where residual path information can exist beyond what the final layer alone reveals — without assuming it always does.

03

Continuity is validated before use: a shared MLP predicts next-layer residuals; matched trajectories beat identity-shuffled controls in all 16 checkpoint–dataset tests — the path is not arbitrary noise.

04

ID-only trajectory-surprise score (never sees OOD data) is fused with final-state OOD scores at a fixed, deliberately conservative weight of 0.3.

05

For recognition: linear probes trained on every layer's native update, fused with a final-state probe via a learned margin gate.

Near vs. far OOD

  • Gains concentrate on far-OOD shifts (Far/Near reduction ratios 1.84×–5.97× across detectors) but stay positive on near-OOD for most detectors too — e.g. Relative Mahalanobis Far-OOD FPR95 24.1→13.7 (151/152 cases).

Complementarity check (Fig. 5)

  • Adding trajectory evidence to Mahalanobis++ gives the largest single-addition gain (−3.40 FPR95, 22/24 cases) — bigger than adding Relative Mahalanobis itself (−3.12) or a random-noise control (+0.79, i.e. no help, 0/24 cases).

Classification & distribution shift

  • Per-dataset peaks: Stanford Cars +9.81, Flowers-102 +9.04, FGVC-Aircraft +7.28 pts.
  • Per-backbone range: CLIP-B +1.43 → DeiT3-B +6.41; ViT-B (AugReg) +6.28, ResNet-50 +4.68, Swin-B +4.61, ConvNeXt-B +3.03 — all six backbones improve.
  • Under shift: CIFAR-100-C improves 169/180 conditions; PACS +3.01 pts, Office-Home +0.65 pts (leave-one-domain-out, 39/48 positive folds).
  • Foundation-model check: DINOv2 + 2 CLIP checkpoints improve all 12/12 OOD combinations (kNN +13.01, Relative Mahalanobis +11.03 FPR95 pts).

Strengths

  • Formally motivated (KL chain-rule) and empirically validated (5 controls, 16 comparisons) claim of real cross-depth continuity.
  • Broad, architecture-balanced sweep (38–42 checkpoints) rather than cherry-picked backbones.
  • Deliberately conservative fixed-weight fusion (0.3, no OOD-set tuning) keeps it a genuine complement, not a re-tuned replacement.
  • Reports negative/neutral results honestly (near-OOD gaps, X-Mahalanobis's smaller gain, failing corruption types).
  • Practically cheap: sub-millisecond overhead, tens of MiB at batch size one.

Limitations

  • Headline OOD gains cover only 152/190 “non-saturated” cases; the ~38 excluded cases are never reported on.
  • Fusion coefficient (0.3) and gating weights are fixed without any OOD validation data.
  • Frozen, deterministic backbone: the trajectory can only recover evidence the final layers compressed away — it cannot create new information.
  • Cross-architecture comparisons are confounded by differing pretraining corpora and recipes — authors call this “replicated-family” evidence, not controlled intervention.
  • No direct empirical head-to-head against the authors' own prior trajectory-adjacent OOD methods.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does trajectory-surprise stay complementary on the ~38 excluded “saturated” cases, or does it degrade already-near-perfect detectors?
  2. How much of the “robustness” claim survives if the 0.3 fusion weight were tuned per-benchmark on a small OOD validation slice?
  3. Would the patch/spatial-level trajectory extension (Appendix B) improve near-OOD, the method's weakest spot?
  4. How does it compare, on the same 38-checkpoint grid, to the authors' own prior intermediate-CLIP-evidence and layer-smoothness baselines?
  5. Does the class-route estimate degrade under long-tailed or few-shot class distributions?
