> **一句话总结：** 本文将零温下限制二元感知机可解性的"重叠间隙性质"（OGP）与冻结 1RSB 结构推广到有限温度，证明标准误差计数损失在任意有限温度下都保持冻结（发散的动力学温度 $T_d \sim N^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log N}$），但允许正训练误差后依然存在算法可达的宽平区域，其 OGP 阈值 $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(\epsilon)$ 随容忍误差 $\epsilon$ 增大而增大，且这些有限能量区域在 teacher-student 场景下仍保留接近贝叶斯最优的泛化能力。

> **TL;DR:** Using replica theory, the authors extend the overlap-gap-property (OGP) and frozen-1RSB analysis of binary perceptrons (ABP/SBP, teacher-student and storage settings, Ising weights, Gaussian patterns) from zero to finite temperature. They show the equilibrium Gibbs measure with the standard error-counting loss is frozen at every $T>0$, but dense clone-accessible regions with positive training error persist up to a growing threshold $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(\epsilon)$; a finite-temperature reinforced AMP algorithm (N=8000) empirically tracks this threshold and, in the teacher-student ABP, generalizes well even in the regime $\alpha_{\rm IT}\simeq1.249 < \alpha < \alpha_{\rm AMP}\simeq1.492$ where zero-temperature solving is provably hard (teacher-only OGP) — a regime where the standard zero-temperature rAMP baseline fails to find any usable configuration.

## 核心贡献

- 将零温下二元感知机（ABP/SBP）的重叠间隙性质（OGP）理论和冻结的一步复本对称破缺（1RSB）几何图像系统性地推广到有限温度，其中训练误差不再被硬性禁止，而是通过 Gibbs 权重 $\mathcal{K}$ 被统计地惩罚。
- 证明对标准误差计数损失（在判决边界处存在跳跃间断的 Gibbs 权重），冻结解在任意有限温度下都主导平衡测度，动力学温度 $T_d$ 在热力学极限下发散（有限 $N$ 标度为 $T_d \sim N^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log N}$），与 Horner 早期用动力学场论得到的结果一致。
- 提出一个基于单模式 Gibbs 权重在判决边界附近光滑性的通用判据（"边界层"量 $\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{K}(b,y)$）：跳跃间断导致冻结；Horner 型软化势 $\mathcal{K}(s)=e^{-\beta(-s)^\gamma\Theta(-s)}$ 当 $\gamma>1/2$ 时消除冻结；对数势 $\mathcal{K}(s)=s^\gamma\Theta(s)$ 当 $\gamma>1$ 时消除冻结。
- 将 OGP 构造扩展到有限温度（通过在固定重叠 $q_1$ 处的多克隆配分函数），证明稠密、算法可达的有限能量解区域可以超越零温 OGP 阈值 $\alpha_{\rm OGP}$ 持续存在，直到一个随容忍误差 $\epsilon$ 增长的新阈值 $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(\epsilon)$。
- 在 teacher-student 场景下证明：由多克隆测度（偏向大局部熵区域）采样得到的有限能量宽解，其泛化误差远低于平衡测度中典型（孤立）解，且随着温度升高泛化性能只是逐渐、平缓地退化，而非突变；用一个有限能量的强化近似消息传递（rAMP）算法数值验证了这些宽区域在中等约束密度下（包括零温精确求解已知困难甚至教师不可唯一确定的区域）仍然算法可达。

## Key Contributions

- Extends the zero-temperature OGP / frozen-1RSB picture of binary perceptrons (ABP and SBP, storage and teacher-student settings) to finite temperature, where training error is no longer forbidden but statistically penalized through a single-pattern Gibbs weight $\mathcal{K}$.
- Proves that for the standard error-counting loss (whose Gibbs weight has a jump discontinuity at the decision boundary), the frozen solution dominates the equilibrium measure at every finite temperature; the dynamical temperature $T_d$ diverges in the thermodynamic limit, with finite-size scaling $T_d \simeq N^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log N}$, consistent with Horner's earlier dynamical field theory result for the ABP.
- Derives a general smoothness criterion (via a boundary-layer quantity $\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{K}(b,y)$) that predicts whether a given finite-temperature relaxation of the loss removes freezing: jump-discontinuous weights freeze at all $\beta$; Horner's soft-penalty weight removes freezing for $\gamma>1/2$; a logarithmic-potential weight removes freezing for $\gamma>1$.
- Extends the OGP construction (via an $m$-clone replicated partition function at fixed mutual overlap) to finite temperature and shows dense, algorithmically accessible finite-energy regions persist beyond the zero-temperature threshold $\alpha_{\rm OGP}$, up to a growing threshold $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(\epsilon)$.
- In the teacher-student setting, shows analytically that finite-energy wide clusters retain markedly better generalization than typical equilibrium (frozen) configurations, and numerically demonstrates with a finite-temperature message-passing algorithm that thermal noise enables effective generalization in a constraint-density regime where both exact teacher recovery and finding a zero-temperature solution are computationally hard.

## Methodology

The models are binary (Ising, $w\in\{\pm1\}^N$) perceptrons trained on $P=\alpha N$ i.i.d. Gaussian patterns, studied in two label regimes: **storage** (random Rademacher labels) and **teacher-student** (labels generated by a planted teacher $w^\star$, with generalization error $\epsilon_g(r)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arccos r$ as a function of teacher-student overlap $r$). Two perceptron variants are considered: the asymmetric binary perceptron (ABP, constraint $s^\mu(w)>0$) and the symmetric binary perceptron (SBP, constraint $|s^\mu(w)|\le\kappa$).

Finite temperature is introduced via a Gibbs measure $Z_\mathcal{D}(\beta)=\sum_w \prod_\mu \mathcal{K}(s^\mu(w))$, where the single-pattern Boltzmann factor $\mathcal{K}$ generalizes the hard zero-temperature constraint; the standard error-counting loss corresponds to $\mathcal{K}(s)=e^{-\beta\Theta(-s)}$. The quenched free entropy is computed with the replica method under both replica-symmetric (RS) and one-step replica-symmetry-breaking (1RSB) Ansätze (full derivations in Appendix A). The **dynamical temperature** $T_d$ — the largest $T$ admitting a non-trivial frozen ($q_1=1$) 1RSB solution — is computed by expanding the 1RSB free entropy around the RS solution at $m=1$ (Appendix B), yielding a general "boundary layer" criterion $\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{K}(b,y)=y\int du\,[\partial_x\mathcal{H}(b+yu,y)]^2/\mathcal{H}(b+yu,y)$ evaluated at the decision boundary $b$, whose divergence as $y\to0$ signals freezing.

To probe the geometry of near-optimal (rather than only typical) configurations, the authors construct a **cloned partition function** $Z_{m,\mathcal{D}}(q_1;\beta)$ over $m$ replicas constrained to mutual overlap $q_1$, compute its free entropy $\phi_m(q_1;\beta)$ (1RSB Ansatz, RS-level treatment of $m$ and $q_1$ as external parameters), and Legendre-transform to the entropy $s_m(q_1;\beta)$ at fixed average training error $\epsilon_m$. The OGP threshold is where the entropy first touches zero as $\alpha$ increases, i.e. $s_m(q_1;\beta)=0,\ \partial_{q_1}s_m(q_1;\beta)=0$.

Algorithmic accessibility is tested with a **fixed-temperature reinforced Approximate Message Passing (rAMP)** algorithm (Algorithm 1): standard AMP is run at the finite-temperature Gibbs measure, and a reinforcement term $\rho_{t-1}h^{t-1}_i$ (schedule $\rho_t=1-(1-\rho)^t$) is added to progressively polarize the marginals toward $\pm1$, producing a binary estimator $w^t=\mathrm{sign}(a^t)$; the run succeeds if the empirical training error drops below a target $\epsilon^{\rm targ}$.

## Results & Highlights

- **Dynamical-temperature scaling (analytic, ABP/SBP storage and teacher-student):** for the standard error-counting Gibbs weight, the equilibrium measure is frozen ($q_1=1$) at every finite inverse temperature $\beta>0$; the finite-size dynamical temperature scales as $T_d \simeq N^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log N}$, i.e. it diverges as $N\to\infty$ — in contrast to smoothed weights (Horner's $\gamma>1/2$ or the logarithmic potential's $\gamma>1$), for which freezing disappears at finite $T$.
- **OGP threshold validation against a rigorous baseline (SBP, storage, $\kappa=1$, Table II):** the paper's RS 1RSB estimate of the $m$-clone OGP threshold $\alpha_{\rm OGP}^{(\rm sbp)}(m)$ is $1.7001$ at $m=2$ and $1.6664$ at $m=3$, versus the rigorous first-moment (annealed) upper bound of Gamarnik–Kizildag–Perkins–Xu of $1.71$ ($m=2$) and $1.667$ ($m=3$) — agreement within $\sim$0.6% and $\sim$0.02% respectively, cross-validating the replica calculation against an independent, mathematically rigorous baseline.
- **Zero-temperature limits recover known thresholds:** in the $\epsilon\to0$ (i.e. $\beta\to\infty$) limit, the finite-temperature $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(\epsilon)$ curve reduces to $\alpha_{\rm OGP}\simeq1.154$ for the ABP teacher-student setting (matching the value previously reported by Baldassi et al., ref. [1]) and $\alpha_{\rm OGP}\simeq0.784$ for the ABP storage setting (consistent with refs. [1] and [22]).
- **Algorithmic accessibility (rAMP, ABP storage, $N=8000$, 60 samples, Fig. 4):** the empirical success probability of rAMP finding a configuration with training error $\le\epsilon^{\rm targ}$ shows a sharp sigmoidal drop as $\alpha$ increases; larger tolerated error shifts the algorithmic transition to larger $\alpha$ (transition point grows monotonically as $\epsilon^{\rm targ}$ is swept from 0 to 0.025), and the fitted rAMP thresholds qualitatively track the theoretical $m=2,3,4$ finite-temperature OGP curves.
- **Teacher-student phase diagram (Fig. 6):** the paper delineates four regimes bounded by $\alpha_{\rm OGP}\simeq1.154$, $\alpha_{\rm IT}\simeq1.249$, and $\alpha_{\rm AMP}\simeq1.492$ (all zero-temperature thresholds from prior work). In regime (C), $\alpha_{\rm IT}<\alpha<\alpha_{\rm AMP}$, the teacher is provably the *only* zero-error configuration at large $N$ (i.e. zero-temperature solving is information-theoretically as hard as exact inference), yet the finite-temperature rAMP algorithm (fuchsia points) still locates positive-training-error configurations whose generalization error tracks close to the 1RSB clone curve ($m=3$, $q_1=0.9$) and approaches the Bayes-optimal curve — substantially better than the generalization of typical/isolated equilibrium configurations (blue curve), which is the closest thing to a baseline comparison offered for this regime.

No formal benchmark datasets are used (all patterns are synthetic i.i.d. Gaussian, $N$ up to 8000); the "baselines" throughout are prior analytical zero-temperature thresholds (refs. [1, 7, 22]) and the zero-temperature rAMP algorithm from ref. [1], against which the finite-temperature extensions are compared.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core contribution — showing that the frozen 1RSB structure and OGP survive (and are meaningfully extendable) at finite temperature, with a clean smoothness-based criterion for when finite-temperature relaxation removes freezing — is a genuine and non-trivial extension of the well-established zero-temperature OGP literature (refs. [1, 4, 6, 7]). The unification of Horner's dynamical-field-theory freezing result with the OGP formalism via the boundary-layer quantity $\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{K}$ is a nice structural insight rather than a straightforward reproduction of prior work.

**Rigor:** The central quantitative results rest on the replica method at RS or 1RSB level, which is a standard but non-rigorous physics technique. The authors are transparent about a specific limitation of their approach: the RS-level treatment of the $m$-clone replicated free entropy produces an unphysical non-monotonic dependence of $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(m)$ on $m$ for large $m$ (violating the provable inequality $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(m)\le\alpha_{\rm OGP}(m')$ for $m'<m$), which they explicitly flag as an artifact of the RS Ansatz on the cloned problem (also observed in prior related work, refs. [7, 36, 39]) rather than a real physical effect — a commendable instance of the paper reporting a known weakness of its own method rather than hiding it. Partial validation against the rigorous (non-replica) annealed bound of Gamarnik et al. [7] for the SBP (Table II, agreement to within ~0.6%) is a meaningful, if narrow, sanity check — it is available only for $m=2,3$ where a rigorous bound exists, and no comparably rigorous cross-check is offered for the finite-temperature results or the teacher-student setting.

**Reproducibility:** All models are analytically well-specified (Gaussian i.i.d. patterns, Ising weights, standard replica formalism), and the rAMP algorithm is given in reasonable pseudocode (Algorithm 1, with details in Appendix C). However, no code repository is mentioned, and reproducing the numerics (N=8000, 60-sample averages, hyperparameter tuning of reinforcement $\rho$ and $\beta$ "for each point... to achieve the highest constraint density possible") would require re-implementing a fairly involved AMP + reinforcement pipeline without a public reference implementation.

**Significance / potential overclaiming:** The paper's most striking numerical claim — that thermal noise lets rAMP generalize well in regime (C), $\alpha_{\rm IT}<\alpha<\alpha_{\rm AMP}$, where zero-temperature solving is information-theoretically as hard as inferring the teacher — is demonstrated with a modest number of data points (Fig. 6 shows only a handful of fuchsia markers in this narrow window) and a single system size (N=8000); no error bars or scaling-with-$N$ analysis is shown for this specific claim, so the strength of the "algorithmically hard region becomes tractable at finite temperature" conclusion rests on a fairly thin empirical base relative to how central it is to the paper's narrative. The open questions the authors themselves raise (whether finite-temperature wide minima explain the $\alpha_{\rm IT}<\alpha<\alpha_{\rm AMP}$ hardness, and whether the teacher becomes "embedded" in these minima for $\alpha>\alpha_{\rm AMP}$) are left genuinely open, which is an honest framing rather than overclaiming a complete theory.

## Strengths

- Clean, general theoretical framework (the boundary-layer criterion $\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{K}$) that unifies and explains several previously disconnected observations about when finite-temperature relaxations freeze or don't (Horner's soft penalty, the logarithmic-potential weight of ref. [28]).
- Cross-validates a replica (non-rigorous) calculation against an independent rigorous mathematical bound (Table II) where such a bound exists, and honestly flags where the RS Ansatz produces unphysical artifacts (non-monotonic $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(m)$).
- Combines analytical (replica) predictions with a concrete, testable algorithm (finite-temperature rAMP) and shows qualitative but consistent agreement between the two, strengthening the practical relevance of the theory.
- Directly connects abstract landscape geometry to a practically important ML question: whether tolerating nonzero training error (as is universal in large-scale/LLM training, explicitly motivated in the introduction) helps or hurts optimization and generalization.

## Limitations

- All results are derived for i.i.d. Gaussian synthetic data and single-layer (perceptron) architectures; extrapolation to real datasets or deep/multi-layer networks is speculative and not attempted.
- The RS-level treatment of the $m$-clone free entropy is known to be inexact for the OGP threshold at larger $m$ (explicitly acknowledged non-monotonicity artifact); a fully consistent 1RSB treatment of the cloned problem is left for future work.
- The key claim of algorithmic accessibility of finite-energy wide regions in the hardest teacher-student regime is supported by a single system size (N=8000) with limited data points and no reported finite-size scaling analysis.
- The paper leaves open (explicitly, in the conclusions) whether finite-temperature wide minima are actually responsible for the information-theoretic hardness of exact inference in $\alpha_{\rm IT}<\alpha<\alpha_{\rm AMP}$, and whether the teacher becomes embedded in such minima beyond $\alpha_{\rm AMP}$ — i.e. the causal mechanism behind the numerically observed phenomenon is not established.

## Relevance

本文是统计物理视角下神经网络损失景观理论的深入延伸，与非凸优化几何、OGP、message-passing 算法等方向高度相关；其结论——"允许小的训练误差反而能保持宽平区域的算法可达性和泛化能力"——为理解大模型训练中普遍存在的非零训练误差（欠拟合式提前停止）为何不损害甚至可能有利于泛化提供了理论支撑。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a fully self-consistent 1RSB (rather than RS) treatment of the $m$-clone replicated free entropy remove the non-monotonic $\alpha_{\rm OGP}(m)$ artifact and yield a rigorous(er) finite-temperature OGP threshold?
- Is there a quantitative causal link between the persistence of finite-energy wide minima and the information-theoretic hardness of exact teacher recovery in $\alpha_{\rm IT}<\alpha<\alpha_{\rm AMP}$, as the authors conjecture but do not establish?
- Does the boundary-layer freezing criterion ($\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{K}$) generalize cleanly to multi-layer networks or to more realistic (non-Gaussian, structured) data distributions, where the notion of a single "decision boundary" per pattern is less clean?
- How does the finite-temperature rAMP algorithm's performance scale with $N$ in the hard regime (C) — does the observed good generalization survive at larger system sizes, or could it be a finite-size effect?
- Could the smoothness criterion be used prescriptively, i.e. to design loss functions/regularizers for practical (deep) networks that provably avoid landscape freezing while retaining useful inductive bias?
