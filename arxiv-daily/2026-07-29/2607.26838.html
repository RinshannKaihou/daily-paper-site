Tight Generalization Bound for AdaBoost ↗

Mikael Møller Høgsgaard · 2026-07-29 · AdaBoost Generalization Bounds VC-Dimension Margin Theory
University of Oxford (Dept. of Statistics), Carlsberg Internationalisation Fellowship — single-author paper. The author co-authored both prior results this paper reconciles: the ICML 2023 lower bound and the COLT 2025 upper bound it now closes — reads as the capstone of his own research program.
Key Metric
ln(ln(nγ²/d))²
the multiplicative log-log slack eliminated from the previous best upper bound (Høgsgaard Møller & Larsen, COLT 2025) — the new bound leaves zero asymptotic gap to the ICML 2023 lower bound.
Why This Matters
Little relevance to applied ML, but the shrunk, nonpositive-margin ghost-sample technique may transfer to other margin-based generalization bounds beyond boosting.
Critical Edge
The proof idea behind the paper's central theorem came from ChatGPT 5.5; with no Lean/Coq formalization, an independent human proof-check is the only real verification available.

Methodology how it works

γ-weak learner→ AdaBoost, T≥ln(n)/γ² rounds→ zero emp. γ/2-margin loss (Lemma 2)→ shrunk ghost sample, m=⌊εn/2⌋→ Rademacher bound (Thm 1)→ Θ(·) rate (Thm 3)
  • Purely theoretical — no experiments, datasets, or code. The contribution is a proof, following the standard PAC weak-to-strong boosting framework.
  • Piece 1 (Theorem 1, new): a margin generalization bound for voting classifiers over any VC class of dimension d. Twist on the classical ghost-sample trick: instead of a same-size ghost sample, it uses a smaller one (size ⌊εn/2⌋) built via a pooling/exchangeability argument so every ghost point has nonpositive margin — this licenses a Rademacher-complexity argument instead of a cruder covering-number bound, which the author credits as the main source of the tightened rate.
  • Piece 2 (Lemma 2, reused + extended): building on Freund & Schapire's classical exponential-loss analysis, after T≥ln(n)/γ² rounds AdaBoost's normalized vote has exactly zero empirical γ/2-margin loss — a precise "=0" not spelled out in the standard reference; the missing log-series step is filled in in Appendix A.
  • Composing piece 2 into piece 1 at margin γ/2 yields Theorem 3, the AdaBoost-specific bound.

No empirical results exist for this paper — "results" here are proved theorems, not benchmark numbers. The table above states exactly what the paper proves versus what was already known.
  • Round complexity: both the margin-loss guarantee (Lemma 2) and its use in Theorem 3 require a concrete, explicit T≥ln(n)/γ² boosting rounds — not an asymptotic "sufficiently large T."
  • Validity regime: the Θ(·) tightness holds where the ICML 2023 lower bound applies — γ≤c₁, d≥c₂·ln(1/γ), and c₃·dγ⁻²≤n≤exp(exp(c₄·d)) for universal constants. A broad but not unconditional range of n relative to d, γ.
  • Outside that regime, the new upper bound still holds unconditionally — only the matching lower bound (and hence exact tightness) is unproven there.

Strengths

  • Fully closes the gap between the author's own prior lower bound (ICML 2023) and upper bound (COLT 2025) — the cleanest possible outcome for this line of work.
  • The shrunk, nonpositive-margin-constrained ghost sample is a reusable technique that could plausibly tighten other margin-based generalization bounds.
  • Precise, quantified comparisons throughout — exact multiplicative factor removed, exact parameter regime of the matching lower bound — not vague "we improve prior work" claims.
  • Transparent acknowledgment of LLM assistance in developing the proof idea, with clear ownership of correctness claimed by the human author.

Limitations

  • Purely theoretical: no empirical validation, and AdaBoost itself is largely superseded in practice by XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost, as the paper itself notes.
  • Tightness (matching upper and lower bounds) only holds within the ICML 2023 lower bound's specific parameter regime; outside it the exact rate remains formally open.
  • Specific to AdaBoost's exact algorithmic form and empirical (not agnostic/distributional) γ-weak learners — doesn't cover AdaBoost variants, multiclass boosting, or agnostic settings.
  • No discussion of whether the T≥ln(n)/γ² round requirement is itself tight or could be relaxed.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can the smaller, nonpositive-margin-constrained ghost-sample technique tighten generalization bounds for other margin-based ensemble methods (other boosting variants, random forests via margin theory, general voting classifiers)?
  • Is the required round complexity T≥ln(n)/γ² itself tight, or can it be reduced while preserving the same generalization guarantee?
  • Does the tight Θ(·) rate extend to agnostic or distributional weak learners, as opposed to the empirical γ-weak learners assumed here?
  • Given the disclosed LLM contribution to Theorem 1's proof, would a machine-checked Lean/Coq formalization be a valuable follow-up to fully validate the ghost-sample argument?
