> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 OPAB（Outcome Performativity A/B Detection），通过随机分配预测（do-intervention）打破预测与特征的因果纠缠，再用卡方检验比较两组结果分布来离线检测"结果绩效性"（Outcome Performativity），并为三类绩效性假设推导出样本复杂度公式；在真实的 Open Bandits Dataset 案例研究中，OPAB 在 men 子集中检测到显著绩效性（p=0.014）而在 women 子集中未检测到（p=0.87），且只用 Adam Detection 基线方法 10% 的样本量就取得更高且更稳定的检测率。

> **TL;DR:** This paper introduces OPAB, an offline A/B-testing-style method that detects Outcome Performativity (where a predictor's outputs causally affect the outcomes it predicts) by randomizing predictions and comparing the resulting outcome distributions with a Chi-Squared test, and derives closed-form sample-complexity bounds N for three assumption classes (Simple, Model-based, Misclassification-based). On the real-world Open Bandits Dataset (OBD), OPAB detects significant performativity in the "men" sub-dataset (p=0.014, detection rate rising from 0.0 at N=10 to 0.523 at N=10^5) but not in "women" (p=0.87, false-detection rate ≤0.042 throughout), and against the only prior explicit detection baseline (a from-scratch reimplementation of Adam et al.'s online "Adam Detection," whose source code was unavailable), OPAB achieves higher and more consistent detection rates while using only 10% of the samples Adam Detection requires.

## 核心贡献

- 将检测"结果绩效性"重新表述为一个基于 do-intervention 的假设检验问题：不是简单比较 P(Y|X,Ŷ)（这会被 P(X|Ŷ=0)≠P(X|Ŷ=1) 的混淆所污染），而是先对预测做随机化干预（ŷ~Bernoulli(0.5)），再比较 P(Y|do(Ŷ=0)) 与 P(Y|do(Ŷ=1))，从而在离线（标注阶段）就能检测绩效性，无需先训练部署模型。
- 提出 OPAB 算法（Alg. 1）：将 T 个实例随机分为两组并各自赋予固定预测 0/1，观察结果后用卡方检验（或 Fisher 精确检验）比较两组结果分布的频数表，若差异显著则判定为绩效性。
- 为三类绩效性假设（Simple、Model-based、Misclassification-based）推导并用 Sympy 验证了闭式样本复杂度公式（Eq. 9/12/14），并引入"不可区分区域"（regions of indistinguishability）概念——在给定干预预算 N̄ 和显著性阈值 δ 下，参数空间中存在 OPAB 无法可靠检测绩效性的区域；实验用热力图直观展示了随 N 增大该区域如何收缩。
- 在 Open Bandits Dataset（真实电商多臂老虎机数据）上做案例研究，并与目前唯一的显式绩效性检测方法 Adam Detection（Adam et al.）做对比，证明 OPAB 更省样本、检测更稳定。

## Key Contributions

- **Reformulates Outcome Performativity detection as a randomized-intervention hypothesis test**: naively comparing P(Y|X, ŷ=0) vs P(Y|X, ŷ=1) under predictions from a real classifier φ is confounded because P(X|ŷ=0)≠P(X|ŷ=1) by design (the classifier's whole purpose is to separate X by predicted class); OPAB instead performs a do-intervention (random 50/50 assignment of ŷ) so P(X|ŷ=0)≈P(X|ŷ=1), isolating the causal effect of the prediction on the outcome via P(Y|do(Ŷ=0)) vs P(Y|do(Ŷ=1)).
- **OPAB algorithm** (Alg. 1): assigns T instances into two equal-sized groups of N via Bernoulli(0.5) predictions, builds frequency tables per group, and applies a Chi-Squared (or Fisher's Exact, when cell counts are 0) test at threshold δ to detect significant dissimilarity between outcome distributions — an offline procedure usable during the labeling process, before any model is trained or deployed.
- **Closed-form sample-complexity bounds for three assumption classes**, each proven via Sympy-verified derivation and validated empirically: Simple Outcome Performativity (Eq. 9, N = χ²_δ · 2(α0+α1)-(α0+α1)² / 2(α0-α1)²), Model-based Outcome Performativity (Eq. 12, using a linear classifier f_θ and feature-space displacement x+β·θ), and Misclassification-based Outcome Performativity (Eq. 14, parametrized by γ0, γ1 — the probability a desired outcome is realized despite misclassification).
- **The "regions of indistinguishability" concept**: given an allotted sample budget N̄, defines the subset of the assumption-class parameter space where N̄ is provably insufficient to detect performativity, giving practitioners a principled way to reason about whether detection is even feasible under a fixed, costly, or ethically constrained sampling budget.
- **Case study and baseline comparison on the Open Bandits Dataset**, the first (to the authors' knowledge) dataset where Outcome Performativity can be inferred from real randomized-prediction data, showing OPAB detects the phenomenon efficiently and outperforms a from-scratch reimplementation of the only other explicit detection method in the literature (Adam Detection).

## Methodology

OPAB starts from the Bayes-theorem decomposition of P(Y|X,Ŷ) and observes that testing dissimilarity between P(Y|X,ŷ=0) and P(Y|X,ŷ=1) using predictions from an actual (trained or expert) predictor φ is insufficient because φ's outputs are themselves dependent on X — the very act of prediction creates a distributional imbalance between the two groups (Fig. 6 in the appendix visualizes this: predictions from a trained θ produce near-disjoint P(X|Ŷ=0) vs P(X|Ŷ=1), while random-sampling predictions produce nearly identical P(X|Ŷ=0)≈P(X|Ŷ=1)). Taking inspiration from RCTs and prior A/B-testing work on performative feedback loops, OPAB replaces φ with a uniform random predictor (do(Ŷ)~U{0,1}), decoupling Ŷ from X. Under the simplifying assumption that performative effects are uniform across the feature space, the test reduces to comparing the unconditional distributions P(Y|do(Ŷ=0)) and P(Y|do(Ŷ=1)) with a Chi-Squared/Fisher's Exact test at δ=0.05.

The paper analyzes three assumption classes for the unknown true outcome-generating process p*(x,ŷ): (1) **Simple** — p* depends only on ŷ via constants α0, α1 (probability of y=1 given ŷ=0 or 1); (2) **Model-based** (following Mendler-Dünner et al.) — a linear classifier f_θ trained on real data is used to simulate performativity by shifting an instance x to x+β_ŷ·θ before re-evaluating f_θ, where β_ŷ controls performative strength and sign; (3) **Misclassification-based** (following Adam et al.) — performativity only manifests when a prediction ŷ mismatches a domain-specific "desirable" outcome ȳ, parametrized by γ0, γ1, the probability the desired outcome is still realized despite misclassification. For each class, sample-complexity formulas for N (instances needed per intervention group) are derived analytically (proofs in Appendix B, verified with Sympy) by populating expected vs. observed contingency tables and solving the Chi-Squared statistic for N.

Empirical validation uses six binary-classification datasets (breast cancer, diabetes, adult census, kickstarter, titanic, loan), imputed with simulated performativity per assumption class, averaged over 100 replicates with a pseudorandom generator for reproducibility. For the real-world case study, the Open Bandits Dataset (OBD, ZOZOTOWN e-commerce recommender logs) is split into "men" and "women" sub-datasets; the position (left/centre/right) of recommended items — filtered to exclude "centre" so predictions are binary — serves as the randomly-assigned prediction Ŷ, and click indicators serve as outcome Y, with OPAB run over N∈{10,10²,10³,10⁴,10⁵} sampled without replacement across 1000 replicates. OPAB is also compared against a reimplementation of "Adam Detection" (Adam et al.), which monitors deployed-model TPR drift over time via a Mann-Whitney U test; since the original source code was unavailable, the authors rebuilt it from the paper's text description, partitioning each dataset into 11 chunks (5 for initial historical TPR, 1 test, 5 for online updates), averaged over 25 replicates.

## Results & Highlights

- **Real-world detection on OBD**: OPAB (δ=0.05, Chi-Squared, 1000 replicates) detects significant Outcome Performativity in the OBD "men" sub-dataset (p=0.014) but not in "women" (p=0.87). Table 1 shows the men detection rate climbing with sample size — 0.0 (N=10), 0.0 (N=10²), 0.05 (N=10³), 0.094 (N=10⁴), 0.523 (N=10⁵) — while the women false-detection rate stays low and roughly flat: 0.0, 0.001, 0.042, 0.042, 0.016 over the same N grid. The authors attribute the modest peak detection rate (52.3% at N=10⁵) to a small effect size, quantified by a low Phi coefficient (Φ=0.004) on the men dataset, plus heavy class imbalance (only ~0.05% of impressions result in a click).
- **Class-balancing improves sample efficiency**: after downsampling the majority class to balance both OBD sub-datasets (Table 2), OPAB's detection rate on the balanced men dataset at N=1000 rises to 0.234, roughly 4× higher than the 0.05 detection rate on the original imbalanced men dataset at the same N=1000 — the paper's own headline comparison ("OP-AB was four times more effective at detecting Outcome Performativity when N=10³ on the balanced men dataset"), while the balanced women dataset stays low (0.06 at N=1000), confirming class imbalance — not just effect size — drives much of the sample-complexity cost.
- **OPAB vs. Adam Detection baseline**: on datasets imputed with Misclassification-based Outcome Performativity (Breast Cancer, Adult Census, Kickstarter), the reimplemented Adam Detection baseline shows lower and more inconsistent detection rates than OPAB, and needed the *entire* Adult Census and Kickstarter datasets to reach trends OPAB matched (less consistently for Adam) using only 10% of each dataset's instances — the paper explicitly attributes this to Adam Detection requiring a deployed, iteratively-updated model plus separate historical/online data splits, making it both less sample-efficient and structurally unable to detect performativity before deployment (unlike OPAB, which is fully offline).
- **Sample-complexity formulas match simulation**: the derived closed-form N (Eq. 9/12/14, using χ²_{δ=0.05}=3.841) accurately predicts empirical "regions of indistinguishability" across all three assumption classes and six benchmark datasets — e.g., in the Model-based setting, only N=28 instances per group are needed for Breast Cancer while N=1628 are needed for Adult Census to achieve comparable detection coverage across the (β0,β1) parameter grid, and detection reliably fails (N→∞) as |α0−α1|→0 or |β0−β1|→0 (Simple/Model-based) or as γ0,γ1→1.0 (Misclassification-based, i.e. subtler performativity).
- **Test-statistic and threshold robustness (appendix)**: results are statistically-test-agnostic — Chi-Squared, Fisher's Exact, and Mann-Whitney U tests produce near-identical detection-rate heatmaps (Fig. 13) at fixed N=50 in the Simple assumption class, and as expected, decreasing δ (stricter significance) proportionally increases the sample complexity N needed for reliable detection (Fig. 14).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — replacing a trained classifier's predictions with randomized (do-intervention) predictions to break the X→Ŷ confounding before testing for performative effects — is a clean, well-motivated adaptation of RCT/A/B-testing logic to a genuinely under-studied offline detection problem. The "regions of indistinguishability" framing and closed-form sample-complexity derivations appear to be a first in this specific literature (the paper claims, plausibly, that "there exist no other works which explicitly derive sample complexity bounds for detecting Outcome Performativity").

**Rigor**: The derivations (Appendix B) are algebraically worked through via contingency tables and cross-checked with Sympy, and the empirical validation is reasonably thorough — 100 replicates per parameter combination across 6 datasets and 3 assumption classes, plus appendix ablations varying the statistical test (Chi-Squared/Fisher/Mann-Whitney) and the significance threshold δ. A genuine strength is the self-critical framing: the paper explicitly states its central simplifying assumption (performative effects uniform across the feature space, reducing the general test P(Y|X,do(Ŷ)) to the unconditional P(Y|do(Ŷ))) and flags in the Discussion that if this assumption fails, OPAB can still tell whether a setting *is* performative but cannot certify that it is *not* — a real limitation left unaddressed empirically (no experiment tests non-uniform performativity or measures how badly OPAB degrades when the uniformity assumption is violated).

**Reproducibility**: Source code is linked, all datasets are standard public benchmarks (breast cancer, diabetes, adult census, kickstarter, titanic, loan, plus the public Open Bandits Dataset), and the appendix documents preprocessing steps (e.g., filtering out "centre" placements in OBD to binarize predictions) and the Adam Detection reimplementation procedure in detail.

**Significance**: The sample-complexity bounds have direct practical value for high-stakes domains (palliative care, credit assignment) where intervention testing is costly or ethically fraught — quantifying exactly when detection is infeasible under a budget is a genuinely useful contribution beyond just proposing a detector.

**Red flags / weak spots**: (1) The Adam Detection baseline is a *from-scratch reimplementation* built only from the original paper's text description ("we were not able to obtain the source code for Adam detection... it was slightly problematic as the paper did not describe the Online Updating procedure exactly") — this is a real fidelity risk for the paper's main comparative claim (OPAB beats Adam Detection), and it is run with only 25 replicates versus 100–1000 for OPAB's own experiments, an inconsistency the paper does not explain. (2) The flagship real-world result — 52.3% detection rate at N=10⁵ on OBD men — is barely above a coin flip even at the largest tested sample size, and the paper's own Phi coefficient (Φ=0.004) admits the real-world effect size is negligible; this tempers the "detectable in numerous cases" framing in the abstract when applied to genuinely noisy field data rather than synthetic performativity injection. (3) The uniformity-across-feature-space assumption (Eq. 7) that the whole method rests on is untested for realism or robustness to violation — no experiment simulates non-uniform performativity to show how badly OPAB's guarantees degrade. (4) Verification latency and label noise are explicitly acknowledged as unaddressed limitations (Sect. 6) rather than empirically bounded, so the method's guarantees are best understood as best-case bounds under simplifying assumptions.

## Strengths

- A conceptually clean causal fix (randomized do-intervention on predictions) to a real confounding problem in naive performativity-detection tests, clearly motivated with an intuitive appendix visualization (Fig. 6).
- Closed-form, Sympy-verified sample-complexity bounds for three distinct assumption classes, each empirically validated with detection-rate heatmaps across six real datasets.
- The "regions of indistinguishability" concept gives practitioners an actionable way to reason about detection feasibility under a fixed, costly, or ethically bounded sampling budget — directly relevant to the paper's motivating domains (palliative care, credit assignment).
- Genuinely offline: detection can occur during data labeling, before a model is ever trained or deployed, avoiding both the cost and the ethical risk of running a performative model in production first.
- Extensive appendix robustness checks: test-statistic agnosticism (Chi-Squared/Fisher/Mann-Whitney), δ-threshold sensitivity, and additional datasets (Titanic, Loan) beyond the main text.
- A real-world case study on the Open Bandits Dataset with contrasting positive (men, p=0.014) and negative (women, p=0.87) control results in the same dataset family, which is a stronger validation design than synthetic-only evaluation.

## Limitations

- Relies on a strong uniformity assumption (performative effects constant across the feature space) that is never empirically stress-tested against non-uniform performativity, despite the paper's own Discussion flagging this as a real gap.
- Does not model verification latency (the delay before an outcome is realized/observed), which the authors acknowledge could make the offline testing window impractically long in real deployments.
- Assumes noise-free, reliably-observed outcome labels; no experiments address label noise, which the paper admits could increase Type I/II error.
- The comparative baseline (Adam Detection) is a self-reimplementation from a text-only description with an unclear online-update procedure, run with fewer replicates (25 vs. 100–1000) than OPAB's own experiments, limiting confidence in the magnitude (though probably not the direction) of the reported advantage.
- Real-world validation is a single dataset family (OBD) with a small effect size (Φ=0.004), so external validity to other high-stakes domains (credit, healthcare) named in the introduction remains untested on real data.

## Relevance

该论文提供了一种可在模型部署前、离线检测"结果绩效性"的实用方法及配套样本复杂度理论，对设计需要事先评估干预测试可行性/预算的因果机器学习或 LLM-agent 决策系统的实验方案具有较强的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much does OPAB's detection guarantee degrade when the uniformity-across-feature-space assumption is violated, and could a conditional (feature-stratified) version of the test recover power in that regime without requiring prohibitively more samples?
- Would a faithful, source-verified reimplementation (or the original authors' code) of Adam Detection narrow or widen the reported sample-efficiency gap versus OPAB?
- Can the sample-complexity framework be extended to handle verification latency (delayed outcome realization) without requiring impractically long waiting windows before hypothesis testing?
- How does OPAB's power change under noisy or partially-observed outcome labels, and can the Chi-Squared framework be adapted with a noise-robust test (e.g., a measurement-error-corrected statistic)?
- Beyond the Open Bandits Dataset, are there other publicly available datasets with genuinely randomized prediction assignment that could serve as additional real-world positive/negative controls for Outcome Performativity detection?
- Could the "regions of indistinguishability" concept be turned into an adaptive sequential-testing procedure that stops early when detection is either clearly achievable or clearly infeasible, rather than requiring a fixed pre-allocated N̄?
