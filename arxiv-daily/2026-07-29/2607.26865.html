Key Finding

Jointly calibrating when to stop reasoning and when to call the cloud cuts an edge LLM agent's thinking cost 43–73%.

Think Short, Defer Smart, Act, and Repeat: Calibrated Reasoning and Uncertainty-Aware Deferral for Edge LLM Agents ↗

Amirmohammad Farzaneh & Osvaldo Simeone · 2026-07-29 · Edge-Cloud LLM Cascade Learn-Then-Test Calibration Test-Time Compute ReAct Agents
Institute for Intelligent Networked Systems, Northeastern University London. Simeone's group has a track record in statistically-grounded ML (Learn-Then-Test); the paper is explicit that it synthesizes two very recent 2026 preprints — Wu et al.'s thought calibration and Piatrashyn et al.'s ReDAct — rather than building either mechanism from scratch. Code for TSDS and the new benchmark is public on GitHub.
Key Metric
43–73%
fewer edge-side thinking tokens than the strongest deferral-only baseline (ReDAct-CD) on HotpotQA, MBPP, and a household-robot benchmark — while matching or beating its reward.
On single-step GSM8K the cut reaches ~90% (18 vs. 173 tokens) — the critical-assessment section flags this as the easy outlier, not the representative number.
Why This Matters
Gives edge-deployed LLM agents a statistically certified recipe for cutting reasoning cost and cloud calls, not just heuristic tuning.
Critical Edge
TSDS's headline MBPP numbers are averaged only over the 22 of 50 calibration splits where it actually certified — the other 28 failures are quietly folded into the non-certified baseline pool.

Methodology how it works

1 · thought calibration
reasoning token i→ probe φθ(hₛ₀ᵣ)→ score > λL?→ inject </think>, extract action
2 · uncertainty-aware deferral
edge action→ perplexity Uₜ→ Uₜ > λD?→ defer to πcloud
  • The probe is trained on a “stable-run” label: truncating at position i counts as safe only if the resulting action matches the full-thought action at that step and every later step — matching the irrevocability of a stopping rule.
  • Seven probe architectures were ablated on GSM8K; an EMA-aggregated linear probe at a shallow layer (ℓ≈5, or ℓ=10 for the household-robot task) won and is used everywhere.
  • Both thresholds λ = (λL, λD) are calibrated jointly via multi-objective Learn-Then-Test: per-candidate binomial p-values for a reward floor and a cloud-call budget, combined and Bonferroni-corrected at δ=0.10, certify ≥1−δ that both constraints hold at once (Proposition 1).
  • Among certified candidates, TSDS picks the one that minimizes thinking cost first, then episode length, then deferral cost.
  • Baselines isolate each piece: E-ReAct-TC (truncation, no deferral) and ReDAct-CD (full thoughts, deferral-only LTT calibration) — the latter is TSDS's main point of comparison throughout.

† MBPP's 0.714 is a conditional mean over the 22/50 splits that certified — see Critical Edge above. HotpotQA's exact reward/token figures aren't broken out numerically in the source beyond “clears both constraints, substantially fewer tokens.”
  • E-ReAct-TC fails LTT certification on GSM8K in all 50 splits (0.839 < Rmin=0.855) — truncating thoughts without a deferral safety net is unsafe on its own.
  • On the household-robot task, ReDAct-CD's reward is lower than TSDS's — evidence that letting the edge model reason to completion makes it overconfident, suppressing the PPL deferral signal exactly when cloud escalation is needed most.
  • Oracle bound (GSM8K): edge-only accuracy is 84.5%, cloud-only is 90.2%; an oracle deferral rule catching exactly the disagreement cases would reach 95.8% — a ceiling against which TSDS's 89.0% test reward can be read.

Strengths

  • A finite-sample statistical guarantee (Proposition 1), not just an empirical tuning recipe — directly useful for certifying reward/cost bounds before deployment.
  • Seven-architecture probe ablation with per-quartile AUROC breakdown isolates which design choices (windowed vs. single-state, shallow vs. deep layer) actually matter.
  • The overconfidence mechanism — unconstrained reasoning suppresses the PPL deferral signal — is shown concretely on a slow-converging GSM8K subset, not just asserted.
  • New household-robot benchmark is a lightweight, vision/hardware-free addition to the embodied-agent evaluation ecosystem, released standalone.
  • Consistent 50-split calibration/test protocol with error bars across four distinct task types (arithmetic, code, QA, embodied planning).

Limitations

  • All edge/cloud pairs are DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen at different scales; cross-architecture or cross-vendor generalization of the probe is untested.
  • Optimal probing layer is task-dependent (ℓ=5 for three benchmarks, ℓ=10 for household-robot) — practitioners still need a per-task layer sweep, a cost not counted against TSDS's savings.
  • MBPP's headline numbers are conditioned on the 22/50 splits that certified, not an unconditional average.
  • Uncertainty score is PPL by default; the authors' own future-work section notes richer signals (semantic entropy) as still-open, implying measurable headroom versus the oracle bound.
  • The household-robot benchmark is text-based and vision/hardware-free — doesn't test transfer to multimodal edge deployments.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the convergence probe transfer across model families (e.g., a non-reasoning edge model paired with a GPT/Claude-class cloud model), or is it tied to the DeepSeek-R1-Distill reasoning-distillation lineage?
  • Could the per-task layer-selection requirement (ℓ=5 vs. ℓ=10) be automated via a layer-ensemble probe, and at what training/inference overhead?
  • Would replacing PPL with semantic entropy meaningfully close the GSM8K oracle gap (89.0% vs. 95.8%), and at what added per-step cost?
  • How does TSDS behave under non-stationary calibration/test distributions, given the LTT guarantee assumes i.i.d. calibration data?
  • Are the 28/50 non-certifying MBPP splits systematically different (e.g., harder problem subsets), revealing a brittleness in the calibration procedure?
  • Can the multi-objective LTT framework extend to a genuine edge→mid-tier→cloud cascade without Bonferroni conservatism becoming a bottleneck as the candidate grid grows?
