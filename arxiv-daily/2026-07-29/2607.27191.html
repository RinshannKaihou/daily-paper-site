CRUX · Shadow Evaluation Report

Given six days and $3,000, an AI agent tried to redo two unpublished NeurIPS papers — the original authors rejected both.

一句话总结：CRUX 团队提出"影子评估"，把两篇尚未发表的 NeurIPS 2026 投稿的核心研究问题交给 Claude Opus 4.8 独立重做，再由论文原作者亲自评审——两篇 AI 论文均被明确拒稿，智能体在选择方向、回应负反馈、止损、管理资源和遵守指令上反复失败。

Key Metric

2/6 · 1/6

Overall NeurIPS-style score, out of 6

The Personas paper scored Reject (2/6); the TabPFN paper scored Strong Reject (1/6) — graded by the original authors themselves, acting as reviewers.

Why This Matters

Verifiable benchmarks score single numbers; blind peer review is stochastic and noisy. Shadow evaluation instead tests real open-ended research judged by the experts who own the question — sharper evidence for AI R&D automation and recursive self-improvement debates.

CRITICAL EDGE

Critical Edge

The whole study rests on n=2 shadow-evaluated papers — the authors themselves flag this as the central limitation, a far smaller evidentiary base than any benchmark with dozens of tasks.

Five Failure Modes

reproduced across both OpenClaw runs and the Codex robustness check
01

Judgment

Can't gauge the bar for what counts as publishable, quality research.

02

Creativity

Responds to negative feedback on its research design with the same idea, not a new one.

03

Backtracking

Can't effectively abandon a dead-end direction once committed to it.

04

Resource awareness

Poor self-tracking of remaining time and budget — in both directions.

05

Instruction drift

Loses the thread of its own mandate over a long, multi-day horizon.

Methodology

  • Uncontaminated question: hand an agent the exact research question behind an unpublished paper, with no access to the paper or its findings.
  • Two test cases: "Personas" (are LLM personas decomposable/controllable as weight-space directions?) and "TabPFN" (a deployment-time distribution-shift detector for tabular foundation models).
  • Agent & scaffold: Claude Opus 4.8, extra-high reasoning, on OpenClaw — a model-agnostic scaffold with subagents, a self-review subagent, and three external AI reviewers (Stanford Agentic Reviewer, CMU Paper Reviewer, refine.ink).
  • Resources: 6 days wall-clock (+24h extension granted once), $3,000 Anthropic API credit, GPU credits, full VM/web access — but zero question-specific guidance.
  • Grading: the papers' own original authors score the output on a NeurIPS-style rubric (Quality/Clarity/Significance/Originality 1–4, Overall 1–6).
  • Robustness check: the TabPFN question rerun end-to-end on Codex + GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra — a different model and scaffold entirely.

  • Real-world generalization gap: the TabPFN detector's own AUROC dropped to 0.60 on real-world shifts, down from 0.76 on synthetic ones — a gap the agent never investigated.
  • 0 of 14 internal self-review rounds ever returned an "Accept" — the agents responded by adding caveats, not by rethinking the approach.
  • Both agents left >50% of their $3,000 API budgets unspent; one declared the project "done" seven hours early despite failing its own self-review bar.
  • Both papers broke the NeurIPS 9-page limit and cited far fewer references than the human originals (36 vs. 69; 16 vs. 52).
  • The Codex + GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra rerun reproduced nearly every failure — but burned its entire $3,000 budget in ~2 days, the opposite budget-mismanagement pattern.
  • No overt reward hacking found. Two other incidents surfaced: one run committed an access token; five subagent hallucinations were caught by the orchestrator before the final draft.

Strengths

  • A genuinely new evaluation paradigm bridging verifiable benchmarks and blind peer review.
  • Table 4 shows the agent's own self-review used nearly the same words as the human critique — it "knew" the flaws and didn't act.
  • Full transparency: expert reviews, run logs, a 12-collaborator pre-experiment survey, and a cross-model robustness check, all released.
  • Explicit, itemized bias disclosure covering both known team priors and acknowledged unknowns.
  • Dollar- and hour-level resource-trajectory figures ground abstract claims like "poor resource awareness" in exact numbers.

Limitations

  • n=2 shadow-evaluated papers (plus dry runs and one robustness rerun) — the authors call this the central limitation.
  • Non-blind reviewing: the same people who spent months on the question graded the agent's answer to it.
  • Couldn't test Anthropic's strongest model (Fable/Mythos 5), deliberately restricted from frontier AI R&D use during the study.
  • The Codex/GPT-5.6 robustness check didn't get the same scaffold-tuning rounds as the main OpenClaw runs.
  • Reviewer-tool accuracy on a genuinely strong paper is untested — both agent papers were rejected.

Questions for Further Exploration

Would an explicit "abstain and restart" option change the five failure modes?
How would Fable/Mythos 5 perform, given it was restricted from this study?
Can the agent's self-review — which already "knows" the flaws — serve as a usable RL signal?
Would a larger, more diverse panel of shadow evaluations replicate all five failure modes?
Does non-blind reviewer bias favor the humans' own solution path over equally valid alternatives?
