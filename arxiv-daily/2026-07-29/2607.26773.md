> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一套"因果审计"框架，通过在发送方-接收方边界处替换潜在消息（无消息/他例消息/自生成消息/本例消息）来拆解端到端准确率变化，在 LatentMAS + Qwen3-4B/8B 上发现 GSM8K 的近零总效应实为 −6.17pp（他例消息效应）与 +5.17pp（样本特定内容效应 CAG）两个方向相反、区间均不含零的分量叠加而成，且两个分量方向在 8B 模型上双双反转。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces a causal-intervention audit (PS/PL/CIC/CAG/SSG metrics) that swaps the latent message crossing the sender-receiver boundary in latent multi-agent LLM systems, decomposing end-task accuracy gains into components. Applied to LatentMAS (Qwen3-4B/8B) on GSM8K, ARC-C, and MATH-500, it finds that Qwen3-4B's near-zero GSM8K overall effect (−1.00pp) hides a −6.17pp "other-example message" effect and a +5.17pp content-specific effect (CAG) whose confidence intervals both exclude zero, while on MATH-500 the 4B model's +15.00pp gain splits into +8.33pp (other-example) and +6.67pp (CAG, CI [0.42, 12.50]) — and separately shows content-specific value (CAG) and other-agent value (SSG) can diverge, e.g. GSM8K Qwen3-4B has CAG=+5.17pp but SSG=−2.00pp (crosses zero), meaning the sender's message beats a random other-example message but not the receiver's own self-generated message under matched compute.

## 核心贡献

- 将"潜在通信是否真的传递了有用信息"形式化为一个容量-使用（capacity-versus-usage）问题，并为概率加权嵌入、隐藏状态、KV 缓存三类载体提供统一的因果审计框架。
- 提出五项指标构成的度量套件：PS（正向信号，消息是否编码发送方信息）、PL（正向倾听，消息存在与否是否改变接收方预测分布）、CIC（消息身份是否改变预测分布）、CAG（样本特定内容的任务价值）、SSG（额外来自"另一个体"而非接收方自身计算的价值）。
- 设计四种消息设定（无消息/他例消息/自生成消息/本例消息）作为受控替换实验，使总效应可精确分解为 OPE = OME + CAG，CAG = DSC + SSG 等恒等式，并配套成分级恢复（NLD）、等效性检验、发送方遮蔽等硬性有效性检查。
- 在 LatentMAS（Zou et al. 2026）+ Qwen3-4B/8B 于 GSM8K、ARC-C、MATH-500 上的实证发现：同样的"总体效应接近零"或"总体效应为正"背后可能是方向相反、幅度可观的多个分量叠加，且这些分量在模型规模变化（4B→8B）时可以整体反号；样本特定内容有价值（CAG 显著）不等于需要一个独立发送方（SSG 可能不显著甚至为负）。

## Key Contributions

- Frames the question of whether latent inter-agent messages are causally used (not just correlated with performance) as a capacity-versus-usage problem, and provides one intervention protocol applicable across three latent carriers used in prior work: probability-weighted embeddings (CIPHER-style), latent-thought hidden states, and relayed KV caches (LatentMAS-style).
- Introduces a five-metric suite — Positive Signaling (PS), Positive Listening (PL), Causal Influence of Communication (CIC), Content-Attributable Gain (CAG), and Self-Substitution Gap (SSG) — adapted from emergent-communication literature (Lowe et al. 2019; Jaques et al. 2019) to continuous, high-dimensional LLM latent messages, each targeting a distinct question (encoded info vs. receiver sensitivity vs. task value of example-specific content vs. value of a separate sender).
- Builds a standardized four-setting intervention (no message / other-example message / compute-matched self-generated message / current-example message) that yields exact decomposition identities (OPE = OME + CAG; CAG = DSC + SSG), paired bootstrap/sign-flip inference, equivalence testing for negligible effects, and three "hard checks" (masking reduces identity effects to numerical floor, other-example→current-example substitution yields zero CIC/CAG, full restoration gives NLD=1) to validate the intervention itself is doing what it claims.
- Empirically shows latent-channel behavior is heterogeneous across models and benchmarks: a near-zero aggregate accuracy change can conceal two large, opposite-signed causal effects, and the same end-task accuracy gain can arise from qualitatively different mechanisms (content-driven vs. content-independent) depending on model scale.

## Methodology

The audit intervenes at the exact boundary where a sender-produced latent representation M (embedding sequence, hidden states, or KV cache) is injected into a receiver, holding receiver context, model weights, prompt template, decoding, and downstream computation fixed (Eq. 2: y = R(ψ(c_R, M̃))). Four message settings are compared for each test example e:
1. **No message** (M₀ = ∅) — receiver operates without any latent input.
2. **Other-example message** (M_oth = φ_S(e′)) — generated by the same sender through the same interface but from a *different*, length-matched example in the same test set; preserves message structure/carrier but not content relevant to e.
3. **Self-generated message** (M_self) — produced by the receiver itself through the same communication interface under a matched compute budget, testing whether a separate agent is even needed.
4. **Current-example message** (M_cur = φ_S(e)) — the original, sender-generated message for the actual evaluated example.

From these four settings, five metrics are computed: **PS** estimates a cross-fitted mutual-information lower bound between M_cur and a declared sender variable (here, whether the sender's own answer is correct) — this only shows information is *encoded*, not *used*. **PL** = Jensen-Shannon divergence between receiver prediction distributions under M_cur vs. M₀ (does message presence change behavior at all). **CIC** = JSD between M_cur and M_oth predictions (does message *identity* matter). **CAG** = task-accuracy(M_cur) − task-accuracy(M_oth), isolating the value of content specific to the evaluated example. **SSG** = task-accuracy(M_cur) − task-accuracy(M_self), isolating whether a genuinely separate agent adds value beyond what the receiver could generate for itself under matched compute. These satisfy exact identities: overall performance effect (OPE, current-vs-no-message) = other-example-message effect (OME) + CAG; and CAG = DSC (self-generated vs. other-example) + SSG. A component-attribution procedure (NLD, Eq. 7) additionally restores subsets of current-example components (sequence windows, layer/region, key/value cells) into an other-example message to localize which structural parts of M drive restoration of teacher-forced log-probability.

The audit is applied to **LatentMAS** (KV-cache relay) with **Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B** as sender/receiver on **GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, and MATH-500**, using m=40 latent steps (the lower-compute end of the original method's reported 40–80 high-performance range, chosen a priori, not tuned on evaluated examples), nucleus sampling (T=0.6, top-p=0.95), 2048-token generation cap, thinking mode enabled, and K=4 other-example messages averaged per test example before paired aggregation. Paired-example bootstrap confidence intervals and sign-flip/exact tests are used throughout, plus a declared-margin equivalence test (±1 pp) for negligibility claims. All runs use a single NVIDIA A40 GPU (48GB).

## Results & Highlights

- **GSM8K (Fig. 1c):** For Qwen3-4B (n=100), the overall performance effect (current-example message vs. no message) is only **−1.00 pp**, but it decomposes exactly into a **−6.17 pp** other-example-message effect and a **+5.17 pp** CAG — both component 95% CIs exclude zero, meaning the near-zero aggregate hides two large, opposing, statistically supported effects. For Qwen3-8B (n=60), the overall effect is **+1.67 pp**, but the components are **+3.96 pp** (other-example) and **−2.29 pp** (CAG) — both signs reversed relative to 4B.
- **MATH-500 (Fig. 2):** Qwen3-4B (n=60) overall gain is **+15.00 pp**, comprising **+8.33 pp** other-example-message effect plus **+6.67 pp** CAG (CI [0.42, 12.50] pp — excludes zero, a genuine content-specific gain). Qwen3-8B (n=40) overall gain is **+10.00 pp**, dominated by an **+8.13 pp** other-example effect, with CAG only **+1.88 pp** and its CI crossing zero — i.e., at 8B the same-sized aggregate gain is mostly content-independent, unlike at 4B.
- **ARC-C:** Qwen3-4B overall effect is **−0.63 pp** = **−2.97 pp** (other-example) + **+2.34 pp** (CAG); Qwen3-8B is **0.00 pp** = **−1.56 pp** + **+1.56 pp**. All ARC-C intervals include zero, offered explicitly as the paper's lower-effect / weaker-signal comparison case rather than cherry-picked positive evidence.
- **CAG vs. SSG divergence (Fig. 3):** On GSM8K, Qwen3-4B shows CAG=**+5.17 pp** but SSG=**−2.00 pp** (CI crosses zero) — the sender's message beats an other-example message but *not* the receiver's own compute-matched self-generated message, i.e., example-specific content helps but doesn't require a separate agent. Conversely, on MATH-500, Qwen3-8B shows CAG=**+1.88 pp** but SSG=**+10.00 pp** with CI **[2.50, 20.00] pp** — a separate agent adds substantial value even when current-vs-other-example content difference is small. None of the four CAG/SSG interval pairs shown falls entirely inside the declared ±1 pp equivalence margin, so the paper explicitly does not claim practical equivalence anywhere.
- **PL vs. CIC (Fig. 4):** First-token Jensen-Shannon divergences span several orders of magnitude across seeds/benchmarks; Qwen3-8B's CIC values are consistently much smaller than its PL values (message presence matters more than message identity at the prediction-distribution level), while Qwen3-4B shows large first-token sensitivity to both. Critically, this prediction-level sensitivity pattern does not predict task-level effects — MATH-500 shows positive task-level effects even with small first-token divergence, and large GSM8K/ARC-C first-token sensitivity doesn't imply a positive overall performance effect.

No single baseline method comparison table is given (this is not a "our method beats X" paper) — the "baseline" in every reported number is the paired no-message / other-example / self-generated counterfactual specific to the same model on the same examples, which is the appropriate comparator for a causal-audit paper.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core contribution — adapting positive-signaling/positive-listening distinctions from emergent-communication RL literature (Lowe et al. 2019; Jaques et al. 2019) to continuous latent messages between pretrained LLM agents, with an explicit other-example-message counterfactual — is a genuinely useful and underused idea in the latent-MAS literature. Table 1's identification-properties comparison substantiates the novelty claim concretely: no prior latent-channel paper (CIPHER, LatentMAS, OBF, DroidSpeak, C2C, Interlat, ThoughtComm) performs message-level intervention with example-specific content separation or self-substitution comparison, and no prior causal/diagnostic MAS paper (MAST, CAPE/TCTE, CAGE-CAL) intervenes on the latent object itself rather than text traces or agent-level structure.

**Rigor:** Sample sizes are modest and disclosed transparently (n=100/60 GSM8K, 80/40 ARC-C, 60/40 MATH-500 for 4B/8B respectively) — small enough that several reported effects (ARC-C entirely, several SSG estimates) have confidence intervals crossing zero, and the paper reports these honestly rather than only highlighting significant results. The "hard checks" (masking→numerical floor, substitution→zero CIC/CAG, full restoration→NLD=1) are a good-faith attempt to validate that the intervention machinery itself isn't introducing artifacts, echoing best-practice cautions from activation-patching literature (Zhang & Nanda 2024; Yeo et al. 2025) that the paper explicitly cites and adopts (in-distribution replacement over Gaussian noising, restoration sweeps as primary evidence).

**Reproducibility:** Hyperparameters are fully specified (m=40 latent steps chosen a priori from the original method's reported range rather than tuned on eval data — an important anti-cherry-picking detail; K=4 other-example messages "fixed rather than selected through tuning"; nucleus T=0.6/top-p=0.95; 2048-token cap; single A40 GPU). However, no code or data release is mentioned anywhere in the paper, and no artifact/reproducibility statement is given, which is a real gap for a methods/measurement paper whose main value is that other researchers should adopt this audit protocol going forward.

**Significance / weak spots:** The paper studies exactly one latent-communication method (LatentMAS's KV-cache relay) despite motivating the work by referencing at least six other latent-channel proposals (CIPHER, DroidSpeak, C2C, Interlat, ThoughtComm, OBF) that are never actually audited — the framework's generality across carriers (embeddings vs. hidden states vs. KV caches) is asserted architecturally (Table 1, "Operationally, each method exposes its native message representation at this boundary") but not empirically demonstrated. This is a meaningful scope gap: the title asks "Do Latent Channels Actually Communicate?" in the general plural, but the evidence covers a single carrier and a single method. Additionally, only two model sizes (Qwen3-4B/8B) from a single model family are tested, and the striking "both components reverse sign at 8B" finding on GSM8K is based on n=60 examples — a compelling qualitative pattern but not yet a robust scaling law across architectures. The PS (positive signaling) mutual-information estimator is explicitly flagged by the authors themselves as a "finite-sample lower bound rather than direct measurement," a well-calibrated caveat given known limitations of MI estimators (McAllester & Stratos 2020) they cite.

**No red flags of overclaiming:** the abstract and conclusion are appropriately hedged ("these results show that aggregate accuracy does not identify how a latent message affects the receiver" rather than "latent communication doesn't work"), and the paper explicitly declines to claim practical equivalence where CIs don't support it (Fig. 3 discussion).

## Strengths

- Exact, verifiable decomposition identities (OPE = OME + CAG; CAG = DSC + SSG) rather than loosely defined ablations, making every reported number cross-checkable.
- Transparent reporting of null/zero-crossing results (ARC-C, several SSG estimates) rather than only positive findings — a genuine honesty signal.
- Careful methodological borrowing with explicit citation and adaptation of established causal-inference and interpretability practices (activation patching best practices, emergent-communication signaling/listening distinction) rather than inventing metrics from scratch.
- The a priori (non-tuned) choice of latent-step count m=40 and other-example count K=4, explicitly stated as not selected via performance on evaluated examples, meaningfully reduces a common source of hidden researcher-degrees-of-freedom bias in this kind of ablation study.
- The finding that CAG and SSG can diverge (content is useful, but a separate agent isn't necessarily needed to produce it) is a substantive, non-obvious empirical result with direct design implications for latent-MAS systems.

## Limitations

- Only one latent-communication method (LatentMAS KV-cache relay) is empirically audited; the framework's applicability to embedding-based (CIPHER) or adapter-based (Interlat) methods is architecturally argued but not tested.
- Only two model sizes within one model family (Qwen3-4B, -8B); no cross-family generalization evidence (e.g., Llama, Mistral) for the "component directions reverse with scale" claim.
- Modest sample sizes (as low as n=40-60 per condition) mean several effects, especially on ARC-C and some SSG estimates, are not statistically distinguishable from zero, limiting how strongly the negative/positive claims in those cells can be read.
- No code/data availability statement, limiting independent reproducibility of the audit pipeline itself.
- The paper is purely diagnostic/measurement — it offers no proposed fix or redesigned latent-communication method that would improve on the observed content-independent (other-example-message) effects, leaving "what should latent-MAS designers do differently" only partially answered.

## Relevance

该工作直接触及"多智能体系统中的通信是否真正传递有效信息"这一评测方法论问题，其干预式因果审计思路对任何研究隐空间通信、KV 缓存共享或做多智能体系统可解释性分析的场景都有借鉴价值；如果关注 LLM agent 之间的信息传递效率或想审视自己方法的端到端提升是否被"虚假通信"污染，这篇论文提供了一套可直接复用的检验协议。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the same audit applied to embedding-based (CIPHER) or adapter-based (Interlat) latent channels show similarly opposing-sign component decompositions, or is this an artifact specific to KV-cache relay's working-memory mechanism?
- Does the "both components reverse sign at 8B" pattern on GSM8K hold at intermediate scales (e.g., 6B) or is it a step-function threshold effect tied to a specific capability crossing?
- What structural message components (via the NLD component-attribution ladder) are responsible for the large content-independent "other-example message effect" — is it positional/length information, generic reasoning scaffolding, or something else entirely?
- Could the negative other-example-message effect (e.g., −6.17 pp on GSM8K for Qwen3-4B) be actively harmful noise injection, and if so, could a receiver be trained to detect and discount off-distribution latent messages, directly using this audit as a training signal?
- How does the audit's verdict change under compressed KV relay (Li, An, and Du 2026's OBF), which the paper cites as evidence that transmitted-representation size doesn't map monotonically to downstream utility — would compression change the CAG/SSG balance?
