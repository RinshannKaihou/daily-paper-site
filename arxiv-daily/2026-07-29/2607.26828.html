Key Finding

Dividing search credit by realized token cost lets a controller match rivals' full-budget quality on half the budget.

Critical Edge
Both baselines are unpublished 2026 preprints from a single other lab, and there's no comparison against a non-adaptive baseline to isolate what cost-awareness itself buys.

Methodology how it works

sample frontier & parent→ search LLM proposes→ observe score ft & cost ct→ cost-calibrated utility ut

ut = (λt·gt + (1−λt)·δt) / dt — global/local score gains divided by a realized-cost divisor dt; mixture weight λt = 1−ρt swings from local exploitation (budget-rich) to global gain (budget-scarce) as the remaining-budget ratio ρt drains.

Local intensity
EMA-smoothed utility narrows sampling as recent gains rise; shrinking budget suppresses expensive exploration.
Frontier allocation
UCB-style pick ranked by global-gain-per-realized-cost; optimism bonus shrinks as budget depletes.
Tactic intervention
Buys a higher-cost "guide" call only if affordable and (stagnating or spending low-yield).

One credit principle, three coupled controls — not three separately-tuned heuristics. All constants fixed per benchmark family (Table 3), never per-benchmark; a worst-case proof (Prop. 1) shows any cost-blind controller can attain as little as (1/K + 1/κ) of optimal as frontier count K and cost ratio κ grow.

Across the full path, CostAda leads on normalized AUC in 15/16 pairs (0.8626 vs. 0.8060 GLM-5; 0.8851 vs. 0.8437 GPT-5.4).
  • Ablation (GLM-5, Signal Processing): removing cost calibration alone drops BestScore@0.5B / @B / AUC by 4.93% / 3.89% / 3.45%; removing budget conditioning or intervention gating degrades all three metrics similarly — but this ablation only covers 3 of 8 benchmarks.
  • Robustness: independent reruns at nominal B=0.5 (not read off a B=1 trajectory) still beat the strongest baseline mean in all 16 cells; budget-crossing overshoot averages just 1.1–2.5%.
  • On roughly half of the 8 GLM-5 benchmarks, AdaEvolve or EvoX individually beats the other baseline — but CostAda's mean still leads whichever one is stronger per benchmark.

Strengths

  • Provable motivation (Prop. 1) for why cost-blind credit fails under heterogeneous action costs — not just an observed empirical gap.
  • One utility formula instantiated across three distinct control decisions, rather than three separately-motivated heuristics bolted together.
  • Two backbones with genuinely different pricing (4× input / 8× output gap, GLM-5 vs. GPT-5.4), budgets denominated in dollars, not raw tokens.
  • Full algorithm listing, controller-constant table, worked iteration trace, ablation study, and public code release.
  • Independent B=0.5 reruns and budget-overshoot accounting pre-empt "is this a stopping-rule artifact" objections.

Limitations

  • No dedicated Limitations section in the paper itself — these are inferred from the ablation/appendix framing.
  • Ablation covers only 3 of 8 benchmarks, leaving the 3-component decomposition's generality partly unverified.
  • No comparison against a simple non-adaptive baseline to isolate "any adaptive controller" from "cost-aware" specifically.
  • Only 3 repeated runs per setting, visibly large std on several benchmarks, no significance testing reported.
  • ~Dozen fixed hyperparameters (Table 3) whose cross-benchmark sensitivity is not analyzed.
  • Prop. 1's guarantee is an asymptotic worst-case construction, not a direct guarantee for the tested suite.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • How does CostAda compare to a non-adaptive baseline (e.g. uniform round-robin with a fixed token cutoff), to isolate the value of "any adaptive controller" from "cost-aware" specifically?
  • Do the gains hold under much larger action-cost heterogeneity than the ~5× step-cost range observed (Appendix B.6), closer to the κ→∞ regime where Prop. 1's separation is starkest?
  • How sensitive is final quality to the roughly dozen fixed controller constants (Table 3), and were they tuned on a held-out benchmark family before being frozen?
  • Does intervention gating generalize when "guide" calls are qualitatively different tools (external solvers, symbolic simplifiers) rather than just higher-cost LLM generations?
  • What happens under adversarial or noisy cost reporting, where API cost estimates don't match realized billing — the assumption the whole method leans on?
