> **一句话总结：** SkillRise 用单一策略在"解题"和"把轨迹提炼进一份演化中的技能文档"两个角色间交替，并把当前任务的奖励只记给解题、把后续任务的折扣回报只记给技能整理，在 Qwen3-4B 上于 ALFWorld/WebShop/ScienceWorld 三个基准的 Pass@1 分别达到 85.9%/84.4%/54.6%，比最强基线 GiGPO 高出 2.3/7.1/8.5 个百分点，且仅用 GiGPO 类多阶段技能流水线（SkillRL/RetroAgent）六分之一的训练时间。

> **TL;DR:** SkillRise trains a single LLM policy end-to-end to alternate between solving a task and curating a shared, evolving skill document that is passed directly to the next related task, using decoupled credit assignment (current-task reward for solving, discounted downstream reward for curation) inside a GRPO-style clipped objective. On Qwen3-4B, SkillRise reaches 85.9% Pass@1 on ALFWorld, 84.4% on WebShop, and 54.6% on ScienceWorld, beating the strongest RL baseline GiGPO (83.6%/77.3%/46.1%) by 2.3–8.5 points, and it shows cross-task test-time scaling (83.6%→87.5% Pass@1 as sequence length K goes from 2 to 6 on ALFWorld, each task attempted only once) while running at 1.0x the time of a compact pipeline versus 6.0x for RetroAgent and 4.3x for SkillRL at matched ALFWorld accuracy (85.9% vs 85.9% vs 73.4%).

## 核心贡献

- 提出跨任务技能学习的端到端强化学习范式 SkillRise：把同一任务族中难度递增的若干相关实例组织成有序序列，用**同一个策略**在"解题"和"技能整理"两种角色间交替，技能整理产生的文本文档是连接前后任务的唯一信息通道（不携带原始轨迹），从而避免了传统"提取-检索-执行"多阶段技能流水线中职责纠缠、难以归因的问题。
- 提出解耦跨任务信用分配（decoupled cross-task credit assignment）：解题响应只用当前任务的结果奖励监督，技能整理响应用之后 K-i 个任务的折扣回报（折扣因子 γ=0.6）监督；组相对优势（role-aware GRPO 式优化）仅在相同任务位置、相同角色的 N 个并行试验（N=8）间比较，避免解题与整理互相污染基线。
- 在 ALFWorld（6 个任务族）、WebShop、ScienceWorld 三个交互式文本智能体基准上，SkillRise 在 Qwen3-4B 下取得最优 Pass@1（85.9%/84.4%/54.6%），比最强基线 GiGPO 高 2.3–8.5 个百分点；训练序列跨越不同任务实例，但学到的整理策略在同一任务重复尝试（Pass@2/3）上依然有效，甚至以 0.8/1.6/14.2 个百分点优于专为同任务重试设计的元强化学习基线 LaMer。
- 发现并验证"跨任务测试时扩展"（cross-task test-time scaling）现象：held-out 任务被划分为长度 K∈{2,4,6} 的相关序列，每个任务只攻击一次，SkillRise 的 Pass@1 随 K 增大单调提升（83.6%→87.5%，+3.9pp），而 GiGPO/GRPO/LaMer 无此趋势——说明性能提升来自跨任务技能复用而非同任务重复采样。

## Key Contributions

- **End-to-end cross-task skill-learning formulation**: organizes same-family task instances into progressively harder ordered sequences x=(x₁,...,x_K); a single policy alternates between solving task xᵢ with the current skill document Sᵢ₋₁ and curating it into Sᵢ, which is the *sole* channel carrying information to xᵢ₊₁ (raw trajectories are discarded) — collapsing the extract/retrieve/execute pipeline used by prior skill-learning work into one policy and one document.
- **Decoupled cross-task credit assignment**: solving trials are scored by the immediate task reward r_i^(n); curation trials are scored by the discounted sum of *future* task rewards Σ γ^(j-i) r_j^(n) for j>i (γ=0.6), reflecting that curation's effect only materializes on subsequent tasks. Group-relative advantages (Eq. 7) are computed only among the N trials sharing the same task position and the same phase (solve vs. curate), so the two roles never serve as each other's baseline; this is plugged into a clipped PPO/GRPO-style objective (Eq. 8) that jointly updates both phases with one shared policy.
- **State-of-the-art Pass@1 across three agentic benchmarks with Qwen3-1.7B/4B backbones**: outperforms four task-independent RL baselines (PPO, RLOO, GRPO, GiGPO), three prompting baselines (Zero-shot, ReAct, Reflexion), and a meta-RL same-task-retry baseline (LaMer), under a matched compute budget (16 sequences × K=3 tasks × N=8 trials = 384 task plays per update, same as baselines' 24×16 rollouts).
- **Demonstrated cross-task test-time scaling and within-task generalization**: performance improves monotonically as more related tasks are chained at test time (each attempted once, so gains cannot come from repeated sampling on the same instance), and the learned curation policy also improves Pass@2/3 on repeated attempts of the *same* held-out task, beating both RL baselines and the explicitly same-task-trained LaMer baseline.
- **Case-study evidence of interpretable skill evolution** (Appendix B): shows concrete before/after skill-document diffs — e.g., after a failed "put two spraybottles in cabinet" attempt that placed only one instance, curation adds a general "repeat for each instance" rule that the *next* different-object task (peppershakers) then successfully applies; and an "open the destination receptacle if closed" condition gets appended to the workflow after a task that required it, later documents remaining stable when new trajectories add nothing new.

## Methodology

SkillRise formalizes each rollout as a cross-task sequence x=(x₁,...,x_K) ~ 𝒢 drawn from a distribution over K distinct instances of the same task family (Eq. 3); sequences are constructed by partitioning tasks by environment-provided family metadata and greedily ordering instances from simple to complex (e.g., WebShop tasks grouped by product category, ordered by the number of required attributes/options). Given a sequence, the policy performs a sequential rollout while maintaining one textual skill document S_i, starting from S₀=∅. For task i, the policy first generates a solving trajectory τ_i ~ π_θ(·|x_i, S_{i-1}) and receives task reward r_i=R(τ_i) (Eq. 4); for every task except the last, the same policy then switches roles to curation and rewrites the *entire* document, S_i ~ π_θ(·|S_{i-1}, τ_i, r_i) (Eq. 5) — curation is instructed (via role-specific prompts, Appendix A, Figs. 8–10) to abstract away instance-specific object names/locations, ground every statement in what was actually observed, extract failure points as concrete pitfalls when the attempt failed, and stay concise rather than padding with generic advice. During training, N=8 independent trials are sampled per sequence, each starting from an empty document. Credit assignment then decouples the two roles' returns (Eq. 6: solving gets r_i^(n) directly; curation gets the γ-discounted sum of rewards from tasks i+1..K), and role-aware group-relative advantages (Eq. 7) are computed by comparing only trials sharing the same task position and phase before being plugged into a clipped importance-sampling objective (Eq. 8) applied token-wise to all policy-generated tokens in the corresponding phase.

**Benchmarks**: ALFWorld (TextWorld version, 6 task families: Pick, Look, Clean, Heat, Cool, Pick2), WebShop (1,000-product version, natural-language purchase requests), ScienceWorld (128 held-out grounded science-experiment tasks). **Baselines**: 3 prompting methods (Zero-shot, ReAct, Reflexion), 4 task-independent RL algorithms (PPO, RLOO, GRPO, GiGPO), and LaMer, a meta-RL baseline that adapts across repeated attempts of the *same* task via textual reflection. **Backbone/setup**: Qwen3-1.7B/4B; training batch = 16 sequences × K=3 tasks × N=8 trials (384 task plays/update, matched against baselines' 24×16 rollouts); actor LR=1e-6, mini-batch 128, max response length 1024 tokens, up to 150 updates, γ=0.6 for cross-task discounting, temperature 0.7 sampling, FSDP+vLLM on 8×NVIDIA H800.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main Pass@1 results (Table 1, Qwen3-4B)**: SkillRise reaches 85.9% on ALFWorld, 84.4% on WebShop, and 54.6% on ScienceWorld, beating the strongest baseline GiGPO (83.6% / 77.3% / 46.1%) by **+2.3 / +7.1 / +8.5 percentage points** respectively. On ALFWorld's Pick task specifically, SkillRise hits 100.0% versus GiGPO's 90.3%.
- **Within-task generalization, Pass@3 (Table 2)**: SkillRise reaches 92.2% (ALFWorld), 96.1% (WebShop), 61.0% (ScienceWorld) at Pass@3, exceeding the strongest baseline (GiGPO/LaMer) by 0.8 / 1.6 / 5.5 points, and outperforming LaMer — which is trained explicitly for repeated attempts of the same task — by 0.8 / 1.6 / **14.2 points**.
- **Cross-task test-time scaling (Fig. 2, ALFWorld held-out set)**: partitioning 128 held-out tasks into related sequences of length K∈{2,4,6} (each task attempted once), SkillRise's Pass@1 rises monotonically from 83.6% at K=2 to 87.5% at K=6 (**+3.9pp**), while the margin over the strongest baseline (GiGPO/GRPO) widens from 3.9 to 8.6 points; competing methods (GiGPO, GRPO, LaMer) show no sustained improvement as K grows.
- **Model-scale robustness (Table 3, ALFWorld)**: SkillRise improves from 78.1% (Qwen3-1.7B) to 85.9% (Qwen3-4B), a **+7.8-point** gain — larger than GRPO's +4.7 and LaMer's +3.3 — while beating the strongest baseline by 3.1 points at 1.7B and 6.2 points at 4B, indicating the advantage grows with backbone capacity.
- **Ablations (Fig. 3)**: (a) varying the cross-task discount γ∈{0.3,0.4,0.6,0.7} yields training curves converging within ~1 percentage point of each other, showing robustness to this hyperparameter; (b) a "no-curation" variant (same K=3 sequences and interaction budget, but no inter-task document transfer) trails full SkillRise by roughly 3 points and trails GRPO's own improvement rate, isolating curation itself (not just cross-task training data) as the source of gains.
- **Pipeline efficiency comparison (Fig. 4, ALFWorld)**: SkillRise matches RetroAgent's 85.9% average success rate and outperforms SkillRL by **12.5 percentage points** (85.9% vs 73.4%), while requiring only **1.0x** relative running time versus **6.0x** for RetroAgent and **4.3x** for SkillRL under the same GPU configuration — i.e., SkillRise gets RetroAgent-level accuracy at one-sixth the wall-clock cost, and beats SkillRL on both accuracy and speed simultaneously.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — collapsing skill extraction, curation, and application into a single end-to-end RL-trained policy with role-decoupled credit assignment, rather than a multi-stage retrieval/memory pipeline — is a genuinely clean simplification relative to the cited multi-stage baselines (SkillRL's teacher-distillation + hierarchical SkillBank + cold-start SFT + retrieval + iterative refinement; RetroAgent's dual intrinsic-feedback retrospection). The "role-aware group-relative optimization" (comparing advantages only within matched task-position × phase groups) is a sensible and non-obvious technical contribution specific to this dual-role setting.

**Rigor**: The ablations are reasonably targeted — the no-curation ablation (Fig. 3, right) isolates the value of curation itself from merely training on related-task sequences, and the discount-factor sweep (Fig. 3, left) checks sensitivity to γ. However, the paper's SkillRL reproduction uses "Gemini-2.5-Pro as the teacher model" for that baseline (Sec. 5.4) — a detail worth flagging since SkillRL's original design and cost profile presumably depend heavily on teacher-model choice, and it is not stated whether this substitution was validated to reproduce SkillRL's originally reported numbers or whether it might disadvantage that baseline in the accuracy/efficiency comparison (Fig. 4). The paper is a single-backbone-family study (Qwen3 only); no cross-model-family generalization (e.g., Llama, GPT-family) is tested.

**Reproducibility**: Task-sequence construction is described only at a high level ("partition by environment-provided family metadata... greedily group diverse instances"); the exact greedy ordering/similarity criterion used to rank simple-to-complex instances is not fully specified in the main text, which could matter since sequence curriculum design plausibly affects the cross-task-scaling result. Code is released on GitHub, which substantially mitigates this. All prompts (task-solving and curation, for all three environments) are fully reproduced in Appendix A, which is a genuine transparency plus.

**Significance**: The cross-task test-time scaling result (Fig. 2) — where each task is attempted exactly once yet accuracy still climbs with longer prior-task context — is the paper's most distinctive empirical finding, since it directly rules out the "more samples per task" explanation and points to genuine cross-instance skill transfer. The efficiency result (matching RetroAgent's accuracy at 1/6 its runtime) is a practically meaningful contribution given the trend toward increasingly complex multi-stage skill-management pipelines in concurrent work.

**Red flags**: The paper explicitly states its own scope limits (Sec. 8): task-family metadata is assumed to be available (automatic discovery of task relatedness in open-ended streams is left unaddressed), evaluation is capped at 4B-parameter models "due to computational constraints," and all three benchmarks are text-based with verifiable outcome rewards — multimodal, real-world, or less-readily-verifiable settings are untested. These are honestly disclosed rather than hidden. No major overclaiming detected; quantitative claims in the abstract (2.3–8.5pp gains) match Table 1 exactly.

## Strengths

- Simple, unified end-to-end design that removes the external teacher, memory bank, and retrieval module required by prior multi-stage skill pipelines (SkillRL, RetroAgent), while still beating them on accuracy or matching them at a fraction of the runtime (Fig. 4).
- Decoupled credit assignment (Eq. 6-7) is a principled response to the actual temporal mismatch between solving (evaluated immediately) and curation (only pays off later) — not just an ad hoc reward-shaping trick — and the ablation confirms curation's separate contribution beyond cross-task training alone.
- The cross-task test-time scaling result (each task attempted once, accuracy still rises with K) is a clean, hard-to-fake demonstration that the mechanism is genuine skill transfer rather than incidental multi-sampling benefit.
- Consistent gains hold across two model scales (1.7B, 4B) and three structurally different environments (embodied household tasks, e-commerce, scientific reasoning), and the appendix case studies (Figs. 11-12) give qualitative, human-checkable evidence of what the curated skill documents actually contain and how they change.
- Public code release and fully reproduced prompts support independent verification.

## Limitations

- Requires task-family metadata to construct ordered cross-task sequences; the paper explicitly flags that automatically discovering task relatedness in open-ended, unlabeled task streams remains unsolved.
- Evaluated only up to 4B-parameter backbones (Qwen3-1.7B/4B); scaling behavior at larger model sizes (10B+) is untested, and it is unclear whether the +7.8-point 1.7B→4B gain trend extrapolates.
- Restricted to three text-based agentic benchmarks with verifiable outcome rewards; no multimodal or real-world (e.g., OSWorld-style GUI, robotics) evaluation, so generality beyond clean text environments with dense verifiable signals is unestablished.
- The SkillRL baseline reproduction substitutes Gemini-2.5-Pro as the teacher model rather than using SkillRL's original configuration, which could affect the fairness of the 12.5-point accuracy gap and 4.3x runtime comparison reported in Fig. 4.
- Sequence length is fixed to K=3 during training (only test-time sequences vary K∈{2,4,6}); it's untested whether training directly on longer K sequences would further amplify or saturate the cross-task scaling trend.

## Relevance

该论文针对智能体强化学习中的跨任务技能迁移问题，用解耦信用分配把"解题"与"技能整理"两种监督信号分开，训练效率和跨任务扩展性均有实证支持，对研究 agentic RL、长程任务学习及可复用经验/技能机制的方向有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the greedy simple-to-complex sequence construction be replaced with a learned or automatically-discovered task-relatedness signal, removing the dependency on environment-provided family metadata?
- Does the cross-task test-time scaling trend (Fig. 2) continue to improve past K=6, or does it saturate/degrade once the skill document grows long enough to strain the policy's context window or introduce noise from over-generalized rules?
- How does SkillRise's skill document evolve and perform when task sequences mix multiple distinct families in a single rollout, rather than staying within one family as in the current construction?
- Would training directly at longer K (e.g., K=6 or K=10 during training, not just at test time) further widen the margin over GiGPO/GRPO, or does K=3 already capture most of the transferable signal?
- Since the SkillRL comparison used a substituted teacher model (Gemini-2.5-Pro), how would the efficiency/accuracy comparison in Fig. 4 change under SkillRL's originally specified teacher configuration?
- Does the decoupled credit-assignment idea generalize to settings with sparser or delayed rewards (e.g., multi-day real-world workflows) where the discounted downstream-return signal for curation would be much noisier?
