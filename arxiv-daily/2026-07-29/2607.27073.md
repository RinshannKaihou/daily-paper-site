> **一句话总结：** 本文提出参数无关算法 HT-PAder，把限定区间内重启的 AdaGrad 专家池与一个"路径式"（pathwise，对元损失分布零假设）的元算法 AdaGrad-Hedge 结合，在只知道时域 T 和定义域直径 D（不需要知道 Lipschitz 常数 G、噪声水平 σ、尾指数 p 或路径长度 P_T）的前提下，对重尾噪声（p 阶中心矩有界，p∈(1,2]）下的在线凸优化取得 Õ(GD√(T(1+P_T/D)) + σDT^{1/p}(1+P_T/D)^{(p-1)/p}) 的通用动态遗憾界，并证明了噪声-路径权衡指数 (p-1)/p 的匹配下界，从而证明其最优性。

> **TL;DR:** This is a purely theoretical paper that resolves parameter-free universal dynamic regret under heavy-tailed stochastic gradients (only a finite p-th central moment, p∈(1,2], rather than finite variance). The proposed algorithm, HT-PAder, combines restarted AdaGrad experts over a geometric pool of block lengths with a novel pathwise meta-algorithm (AdaGrad-Hedge) that needs no moment assumption on meta-losses, achieving Õ(GD√(T(1+P_T/D)) + σDT^{1/p}(1+P_T/D)^{(p-1)/p}) expected dynamic regret while knowing only the horizon T and domain diameter D. This exactly recovers the optimal static-regret rate O(GD√T + σDT^{1/p}) of the paper's direct predecessor, Liu (2026)'s parameter-free AdaGrad result, when the comparator path length P_T=0, and recovers the deterministic minimax dynamic-regret rate O(GD√(T(1+P_T/D))) of Zhang et al. (2018)'s Improved Ader when σ=0 — but unlike Improved Ader, HT-PAder needs no prior knowledge of G. A matching Ω(σD^{1/p}T^{1/p}P_T^{(p-1)/p}) lower bound (with explicit constant 4^{1/p}/128) is proved via a bespoke multi-block adversarial construction, establishing that the (p-1)/p path-length exponent cannot be improved by any algorithm. No experiments are reported; the contribution is entirely theoretical.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 HT-PAder：一个不需要事先知道 G、σ、p、P_T 中任何一个参数（只需要知道时域 T 和定义域直径 D）的重尾噪声下通用动态遗憾（universal dynamic regret）算法，遗憾界为 Õ(GD√(T(1+P_T/D)) + σDT^{1/p}(1+P_T/D)^{(p-1)/p})。
- 提出 AdaGrad-Hedge（Algorithm 1）：一种"路径式"元算法，其遗憾界对任意元损失序列的具体实现都成立（不要求任何矩条件），从而绕开了标准 Hedge/Optimistic Hedge 在重尾场景下因需要 E[‖m_t‖²]<∞ 而失效的问题（Remark 2 明确指出了标准 Hedge 为何会失败）。
- 提出"区间内重启的 AdaGrad"这一核心技术工具（Lemma 3）：证明将 AdaGrad-Norm 每隔 L 轮重启一次，可以把 AdaGrad 可变步长带来的全局修正项局部化到区间内，从而得到一个只依赖区间局部梯度范数平方和与区间内比较子路径长度的路径式区间动态遗憾界。
- 证明匹配下界（Theorem 3）：构造了一个多区块对抗环境，其中重尾梯度噪声被刻意校准以最大化学习者的估计误差，得到 Ω(σD^{1/p}T^{1/p}P_T^{(p-1)/p}) 的期望动态遗憾下界（显式常数 4^{1/p}/128），证明上界中噪声-路径长度权衡指数 (p-1)/p 是信息论最优的，不可被任何算法改进。
- 作为副产品，在 p=2（标准有限方差）特例下给出 Õ((G+σ)D√(T(1+P_T/D)))，这是标准有限方差 OCO 下第一个参数无关的极小极大通用动态遗憾保证，改进了 Zhang et al. (2018) 的 Improved Ader（后者需要预先知道 G 来构建其步长池）。

## Key Contributions

- **HT-PAder, the first parameter-free algorithm for universal dynamic regret under heavy-tailed gradient noise**: requiring knowledge only of the horizon T and domain diameter D (not G, σ, the tail index p, or the comparator path length P_T), it achieves expected regret Õ(GD√(T(1+P_T/D)) + σDT^{1/p}(1+P_T/D)^{(p-1)/p}) against any comparator sequence.
- **AdaGrad-Hedge, a pathwise meta-algorithm (Algorithm 1) with a distribution-free regret guarantee**: its cumulative-scale variable W_t tracks the squared ℓ∞ norm of *observed* meta-loss vectors, and its FTRL-with-shifted-negative-entropy regret bound (Lemma 6: Σ⟨w_t,m_t⟩ − Σm_{t,i} ≤ 3√(aW_T)) holds deterministically for any realization of the meta-losses — critically, without requiring E[‖m_t‖²]<∞, which the paper shows can be infinite when p<2, breaking standard Hedge/Optimistic-Hedge-based dynamic-regret algorithms (Improved Ader, Sword, Sword++).
- **Restarted AdaGrad experts over a geometric block-length pool**, which localize AdaGrad's variable-stepsize telescoping correction terms to each block (Lemma 3), yielding a clean pathwise block dynamic-regret bound; combined with a geometric pool of block lengths (K_i = 2^i∧T), this adapts to the unknown path length P_T without knowing it in advance (Section 5, Algorithm 2).
- **A matching information-theoretic lower bound (Theorem 3)**: Ω(σD^{1/p}T^{1/p}P_T^{(p-1)/p}) for any randomized online algorithm in the stochastic first-order oracle model, proved via a novel multi-block adversarial environment and a variational total-variation reduction plus Pinsker's inequality, showing the (p−1)/p exponent on P_T is optimal and cannot be improved.
- **A finite-variance byproduct (Corollary 1)**: setting p=2 in the general result gives the first parameter-free minimax universal dynamic regret guarantee for standard (non-heavy-tailed) OCO, Õ((G+σ)D√(T(1+P_T/D))), resolving a practical limitation of Zhang et al. (2018)'s Improved Ader, which needs G to construct its stepsize pool.

## Methodology

The setup (Section 3) is standard OCO with a nonempty, closed, bounded convex decision set X ⊂ ℝ^d of diameter D, G-Lipschitz convex losses ℓ_t, and a stochastic gradient oracle g_t that is unbiased (E[g_t|F_{t-1}] = ∇ℓ_t(x_t)) but whose noise ε_t = g_t − ∇ℓ_t(x_t) satisfies only a bounded p-th conditional central moment, E[‖ε_t‖^p|F_{t-1}] ≤ σ^p for some tail index p∈(1,2] — a strictly weaker assumption than finite variance (p=2), under which standard SGD/OGD analyses (which require E[‖g_t‖²]<∞) break down and can provably diverge (citing Zhang et al. 2020). The performance metric is universal dynamic regret against an arbitrary comparator sequence u_1,…,u_T, with non-stationarity measured by path length P_T = Σ‖u_t−u_{t−1}‖.

The construction has two components. (1) **AdaGrad-Hedge** is an FTRL instantiation with shifted negative-entropy potential over the probability simplex of experts, whose learning rate ε_t = √(a/W_t) adapts to the cumulative squared ℓ∞ norm of observed meta-losses W_t, giving a pathwise (moment-free) regret bound (Lemma 6) — this is the key enabler that lets the meta-algorithm interface safely with heavy-tailed gradients, since standard fixed-rate Hedge would require a finite second moment on the meta-loss, which scales with ‖g_t‖ and can be infinite for p<2 (Remark 2). (2) **Restarted AdaGrad experts**: the paper builds N+1 = ⌈log₂T⌉+1 experts E_0,…,E_N, expert E_i running AdaGrad-Norm (stepsize η_t^(i) = D/√(2V_t^(i)), local potential V_t^(i) reset at block boundaries) with block length L_i = ⌈T/K_i⌉ where K_i = 2^i∧T, restarting its iterate and local potential at the start of each block. Restarting is essential (Remark 1): without it, AdaGrad's variable stepsize makes the telescoping correction term depend on all gradients since round 1 rather than just the local interval, preventing a clean interval regret bound. HT-PAder (Algorithm 2) runs all N+1 experts in parallel, mixes their predictions x_t = Σw_{t,i}x_t^(i) using AdaGrad-Hedge weights (updated via meta-loss m_{t,i} = ⟨g_t, x_t^(i)−x_t⟩, chosen so the weighted-average meta-loss is exactly zero), and needs only T and D as inputs — no G, σ, p, or P_T.

The proof (Section 6) proceeds via five steps: (i) a meta-expert decomposition splitting regret into "meta-regret relative to expert i" plus "expert i's own linear regret"; (ii) bounding the meta-regret using the pathwise AdaGrad-Hedge guarantee plus a moment lemma (Lemma 5, via Minkowski's inequality: E[√Σ‖g_t‖²] ≤ G√|B| + σ|B|^{1/p}); (iii) bounding a fixed-block-length expert's regret (Lemma 7) using the restarted-AdaGrad pathwise bound (Lemma 3) plus Lemma 5 per block; (iv) selecting the near-optimal expert index i* whose K_{i*} ∈ [K*, 2K*] for K* = 1+P_T/D from the geometric pool (this is where parameter-freeness w.r.t. the unknown P_T comes from — the pool covers the relevant scale within a factor of 2); and (v) combining the meta-regret and expert-regret bounds. A key supporting technical tool is Lemma 2, attributed to Liu (2026): a tighter one-step OGD inequality that, by exploiting the bounded-domain assumption (‖x_t−x_{t+1}‖≤D) via Young's inequality with the correct exponent p, produces a ‖ε_t‖^p noise term rather than the standard ‖ε_t‖², which is the technical foundation that makes the whole heavy-tailed analysis possible without gradient clipping. The paper additionally presents a simpler known-G precursor, HT-IA (Appendix A, Algorithm 3), which pairs AdaGrad-Hedge with fixed-stepsize OGD experts on a geometric stepsize pool (à la Improved Ader) — a pedagogical stepping stone isolating the meta-algorithm's role from the harder parameter-free construction.

## Results & Highlights

This is a theory-only paper — its "results" are closed-form regret-rate guarantees (Theorems 1–3, Corollary 1), proved rigorously and compared explicitly against named prior algorithms' rates (summarized in Table 1). There are no empirical experiments, datasets, or benchmarks. Concrete quantitative claims:

- **Main upper bound vs. Zhang et al. (2018)'s Improved Ader (deterministic case, σ=0)**: HT-PAder's general bound Õ(GD√(T(1+P_T/D)) + σDT^{1/p}(1+P_T/D)^{(p-1)/p}) reduces exactly to O(GD√(T(1+P_T/D))), matching Improved Ader's minimax deterministic dynamic-regret rate — but Improved Ader requires knowledge of G to build its OGD stepsize pool, while HT-PAder needs no problem constants (Table 1; Remark 5).
- **Static-regret special case (P_T=0) vs. Liu (2026)'s AdaGrad result**: HT-PAder's bound reduces to Õ(GD√T + σDT^{1/p}), exactly matching the optimal parameter-free static-regret rate O(GD√T + σDT^{1/p}) that Liu (2026) proved for plain OGD/DA/AdaGrad under the same heavy-tailed noise model (Remark 5).
- **Matching lower bound (Theorem 3)**: the paper proves Ω(σD^{1/p}T^{1/p}P_T^{(p-1)/p}) with explicit constant c_p = 4^{1/p}/128, for any randomized algorithm in the stochastic first-order oracle model — this matches the noise-dependence term σDT^{1/p}(P_T/D)^{(p-1)/p} of the upper bound up to polylogarithmic factors (Remark 6), proving the (p−1)/p path-length exponent is information-theoretically optimal.
- **Vs. Zhang and Cutkosky (2022) (heavy-tailed static regret with clipping)**: their high-probability rate is Õ((G+σ)DT^{1/p}), which the paper notes does *not* recover the optimal GD/√T rate when σ=0 (i.e., is loose in the noiseless limit) and requires gradient clipping; HT-PAder's rate (like Liu 2026's) recovers the deterministic O(GD√T) rate exactly when σ=0, with no clipping (Table 1, Section 2).
- **Finite-variance byproduct (p=2) vs. Improved Ader**: HT-PAder gives Õ((G+σ)D√(T(1+P_T/D))) without knowing G, σ, or P_T — the paper states this is the first parameter-free minimax universal dynamic regret guarantee for standard finite-variance OCO, whereas Improved Ader achieves the same O(·) rate but needs G known in advance (Corollary 1, Section 1).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The paper's own framing (Remark 3) is appropriately modest — it states explicitly that "the novelty of this paper is not the meta-algorithm in isolation" (AdaGrad-Hedge is described as "an AdaGrad-FTRL/Hedge instantiation, related to known parameter-free potentials") but rather the pathwise refined form, its coupling with restarted AdaGrad experts, and the resulting parameter-free dynamic-regret guarantee. This is an honest, well-scoped claim: the contribution is a non-trivial but incremental extension of Liu (2026)'s static-regret result to the dynamic-regret, parameter-free setting, rather than a new algorithmic paradigm.

**Rigor**: Full proofs are given for every lemma and theorem, including explicit universal constants (e.g., C=3√2/2 in Lemma 3, c_p=4^{1/p}/128 in Theorem 3), and the matching upper/lower bound (up to polylog factors) is strong internal evidence of correctness and tightness. The lower-bound construction (Theorem 3) is a self-contained, carefully executed multi-block Bayesian/adversarial argument using total-variation data-processing and Pinsker's inequality.

**Reproducibility**: Not applicable in the empirical sense — there is no code or experiment to reproduce. On the positive side, all three algorithms (AdaGrad-Hedge, HT-IA, HT-PAder) are given as fully specified pseudocode (Algorithms 1–3) with explicit hyperparameter formulas, which is good practice for a pure-theory paper.

**Significance**: Closing the stated open problem (parameter-free universal dynamic regret under heavy tails) is a genuine contribution to the OCO theory literature, and the p=2 byproduct (first parameter-free minimax dynamic regret for standard finite-variance OCO, improving on the well-established Improved Ader of Zhang et al. 2018) may have broader relevance than the headline heavy-tailed result, since finite-variance OCO is a much more established topic.

**Red flags / paper-specific weak spots**: (1) Zero empirical validation anywhere in the paper — whether the promised parameter-freeness materializes as competitive real-world performance (given the Õ(·) hides a √(log log T) meta-regret factor from running O(log T) restarted-AdaGrad experts in parallel, per Table 1's footnote) is entirely untested. (2) Table 1's own footnote (‡) admits HT-PAder "assumes known horizon T and diameter D," and the horizon dependence is only claimed removable "by standard doubling" as an unproven remark (Remark 4) rather than a proven result in this paper — so the "parameter-free" headline claim is narrower than it first appears (it removes G, σ, p, P_T, not T or D). (3) The entire analysis depends critically on the bounded-domain assumption D<∞ (used explicitly to set AdaGrad's stepsize η=D/√2 and in Lemma 2's Young's-inequality step); the paper itself notes (Section 2) that Liu (2026) showed OGD/AdaGrad can diverge under heavy tails on *unbounded* domains, so this line of work does not extend to the common unconstrained optimization setting (e.g., unconstrained deep-network training) without further work. (4) All guarantees are in expectation, not high-probability — in contrast, two of the cited heavy-tailed baselines (Zhang and Cutkosky 2022; Yang et al. 2025) target high-probability bounds (at the cost of gradient clipping and/or restrictive settings), so this paper's contribution is not strictly stronger along every axis simultaneously.

## Strengths

- Closes a clearly stated open problem with matching upper and lower bounds, proving (not just conjecturing) that the (p−1)/p path-length exponent is optimal.
- Achieves full parameter-freeness with respect to G, σ, p, and P_T simultaneously, which is technically nontrivial — the paper correctly identifies (Remark 1) why naively combining AdaGrad with dynamic-regret telescoping fails without restarting, and resolves it cleanly.
- Modular, reusable proof architecture: the pathwise AdaGrad-Hedge meta-regret bound (Lemma 6) is completely distribution-free and could plausibly be reused in other online-learning settings with adversarial or heavy-tailed meta-losses, independent of this specific application.
- Disciplined sanity-checking: the general bound is shown to correctly recover every relevant special case — Liu (2026)'s static rate at P_T=0, Zhang et al. (2018)'s deterministic minimax rate at σ=0, and a new best-known parameter-free rate at p=2 — which is good evidence the bound is not vacuous or loose in known regimes.
- Provides a simpler known-G precursor (HT-IA) that pedagogically isolates the meta-algorithm's contribution from the added difficulty of removing knowledge of G, aiding readability of an otherwise dense technical construction.

## Limitations

- Entirely theoretical: no experiments, simulations, or empirical comparison against baselines (Improved Ader, Sword++, clipped-SGD) are reported anywhere in the paper, so practical performance and the real-world magnitude of the log log T / block-pool overhead constants are unknown.
- Requires the domain diameter D to be known and bounded in advance; the approach does not extend to unbounded domains, where the underlying static-regret technique (Liu 2026) is already known to fail.
- The "parameter-free" claim is narrower than the framing suggests — Table 1's own footnote states the algorithm still assumes a known horizon T and diameter D; only G, σ, p, and P_T are removed.
- All regret guarantees are in expectation only; no high-probability version is provided, unlike some cited heavy-tailed baselines (Zhang and Cutkosky 2022; Yang et al. 2025).
- The tail index p is assumed fixed and uniformly bounding the noise moment across the entire horizon (E[‖ε_t‖^p|F_{t-1}] ≤ σ^p for all t); there is no treatment of time-varying or unknown/adaptive tail behavior.

## Relevance

该论文是纯理论工作，对在线学习理论、非平稳优化和重尾随机优化方向的研究者有直接的方法论价值（尤其是"路径式元算法+区间重启"这一可能复用的技术组合）；但由于完全没有实验验证，其对应用型深度学习优化器设计（如重尾梯度噪声下的自适应优化器调参）的直接指导意义有限，更多是提供了理论上界/下界的参照系。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the parameter-free construction be extended to unbounded domains, given that Liu (2026) already showed the underlying static-regret approach (OGD/AdaGrad without clipping) can diverge under heavy tails without the bounded-domain assumption?
- Does the Õ(√(log log T)) meta-regret overhead from running O(log T) restarted-AdaGrad experts in parallel translate into a measurable computational or convergence cost in practice, e.g. on deep-learning training runs with empirically documented heavy-tailed gradient noise (Simsekli et al. 2019)?
- Can the expected-regret guarantee be upgraded to a high-probability bound (as in Zhang and Cutkosky 2022 or Yang et al. 2025) without reintroducing gradient clipping or sacrificing parameter-freeness?
- How would HT-PAder perform empirically against Improved Ader, Sword++, and clipped-SGD baselines under realistic heavy-tailed noise (e.g., simulated Student-t gradients or real deep-learning training curves), and would the hidden log log T and block-pool constants matter at practical values of T?
- Can the framework be adapted to a time-varying or a priori unknown tail index p_t that changes across the horizon, rather than a single fixed p assumed for the entire run?
- Is there a way to remove the "known horizon T" assumption with a rigorously analyzed doubling trick (rather than the current one-line remark), and how much would that cost in the resulting rate?
