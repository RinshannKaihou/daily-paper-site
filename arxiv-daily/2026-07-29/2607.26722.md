> **一句话总结：** DREvo 通过"函数级证据锚定 + 状态相关证据重校准 + 角色条件化搜索意图蒸馏"三部曲，把历史试验日志转化为可判断当前是否仍然有效的细粒度证据，并显式指定下一步该改哪个组件、怎么改，在 USPTO/S2D/Law 三个领域推理任务和 Terminal-Bench 2.0/SWE-Bench Verified 两个 Agent 任务上相对 Meta-harness、ACE 等基线平均提升 16.2%（领域推理）和 13.8%（Agent 任务），且演化轨迹明显更平滑。

> **TL;DR:** DREvo tackles the non-stationary, oscillating accuracy trajectories seen in history-driven harness self-evolution (e.g., Meta-harness with a 1M-token context) by anchoring trial feedback to specific harness functions, recalibrating each evidence unit's validity via a reliability×compatibility score, and distilling the calibrated evidence into one of four explicit search roles (Exploit/Avoid/Retest/Explore) before the proposer (Claude Opus 4.6) generates the next harness. Against baselines including Meta-harness, ACE, MCE, AHE, and hand-built harnesses (OpenHands, Terminus-2, Claude Code), DREvo achieves the best accuracy on all five benchmarks under a limited budget (20 iterations for reasoning, 10 for agentic tasks) — e.g., 25.0% vs. second-best ACE's 16.0% on USPTO, 42.7% vs. Terminus-2's 34.8% full-suite accuracy on Terminal-Bench 2.0, and 67.6% vs. OpenHands' 61.8% / AHE's 61.8% on SWE-Bench Verified — while keeping proposer context bounded at ~2.9K tokens versus Meta-harness's context growing past 5.3K tokens with worse oscillation.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断历史驱动式 harness 自演化的不稳定性**：用 Meta-harness（Claude Opus 4.6，1M-token 上下文窗口）在 S2D、USPTO、Law 三个数据集上做全历史/压缩历史复用实验，发现准确率随迭代不是单调上升，而是反复震荡回落，指出问题根源在于"历史证据的有效性从未被显式重新判断"以及"证据没有被转化为明确的搜索方向"。
- **函数级证据锚定（Function-Level Evidence Anchoring）**：把粗粒度的迭代级试验日志，通过代码 diff 到函数边界的映射，转化为绑定具体组件（如 tool use、memory management）的证据单元 et,c = (c, fc, δt,c, ot, ∆t, ρe, we, pt)，并区分单组件修改（权重 1.0）与多组件修改（权重 0.5，归因置信度更低）。
- **状态相关证据重校准（State-Dependent Evidence Recalibration）**：从"历史可靠性"（结果一致性 const(e) 乘以按 0.9 衰减因子计算的新鲜度 fresh_t(e)）与"结构兼容性"（当前组件代码与证据创建时代码的 AST 树编辑距离 TED）两个维度，用调和平均式（式 6）重新计算证据权重 we，使得任一维度低都无法被另一维度补偿。
- **角色条件化搜索意图蒸馏（Role-Conditioned Search Intent Distillation）**：按证据极性和权重把检索到的证据划分为高置信正例/负例/低置信三类，通过 Algorithm 1 的优先级规则激活 Exploit（沿用已验证方向）、Avoid（规避已知有害修改）、Retest（重新验证低置信证据）、Explore（无证据约束的自由探索）四种角色之一，将其转化为显式指定目标组件与修改方向的搜索意图，再交给 proposer 生成下一版 harness。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnoses instability in history-driven harness self-evolution**: Runs Meta-harness (Claude Opus 4.6, 1M-token context) under both full-history and compressed-history reuse on S2D, USPTO, and Law, showing accuracy oscillates rather than trending upward as experience accumulates, and traces this to (1) no dynamic reassessment of whether historical evidence is still valid, and (2) no explicit mechanism translating valid evidence into actionable search directions.
- **Function-Level Evidence Anchoring**: Maps line-level code diffs between consecutive harness versions onto the source-span boundaries of functional components (e.g., tool use, memory management), producing anchored evidence units et,c = (c, fc, δt,c, ot, ∆t, ρe, we, pt) with polarity ρe set from the trial-level performance delta and an initial weight of 1.0 (single-component edit) or 0.5 (multi-component edit, lower attribution confidence).
- **State-Dependent Evidence Recalibration**: Computes historical reliability rt(e) = const(e)·fresh_t(e) — outcome consistency weighted by observed performance changes, times a 0.9^(t−ℓ) temporal decay — and structural compatibility qt(e) = 1 − TED(Te,Tt)/(|Te|+|Tt|) via AST tree-edit distance between the component's code at evidence creation and its current state, then combines them into a harmonic-mean-style calibrated weight (Eq. 6) so neither factor alone can rescue a low score in the other.
- **Role-Conditioned Search Intent Distillation**: Retrieves evidence matching the current iteration's failure patterns, partitions it by polarity/weight into reliable-positive, reliable-negative, and low-confidence sets, and activates one of four roles (Exploit, Avoid, Retest, Explore) via a priority-ordered policy (Algorithm 1, including a Retest→Explore fallback when retesting fails) to produce an explicit search intent st = G(at, Ft, Z^at_t) that conditions the proposer's next harness generation.
- **Empirical validation across five benchmarks**: Under a limited evolution budget (20 iterations for domain reasoning, 10 for agentic tasks), DREvo attains the top accuracy on USPTO, S2D, Law, Terminal-Bench 2.0, and SWE-Bench Verified, with average gains of 16.2% (domain reasoning) and 13.8% (agentic tasks, abstract) / 14.2% (agentic tasks, contributions list — the paper's two mentions differ slightly) over evaluated baselines, plus smoother, less-oscillating evolution trajectories than Meta-harness.

## Methodology

DREvo formalizes harness self-evolution as history-conditioned sequential optimization: at iteration t, proposer P generates candidate harness ht conditioned on ht−1 and experience repository Rt−1, evaluates it under environment E to get trial log τt, and the goal is to find h* = argmax J_E(h) over all iterations (Eq. 1). DREvo modifies this loop with three sequential components (Figure 2):

1. **Function-Level Evidence Anchoring**: Parses each trial log τt into feedback ot = (gt, Qt, Ft) — global statistics (pass rate, avg score, resource use), per-task outcomes, and failure-pattern frequencies (timeouts, runtime exceptions, tool-call errors, context overflow) — then computes a line-level code diff between ht−1 and ht and maps each diff hunk to its enclosing functional component (Eq. 2, FuncDiff). The proposer is encouraged to modify a single component per iteration to keep attribution clean. Each modified component yields an evidence unit with polarity (sign of the performance delta ∆t) and initial weight (1.0 single-component / 0.5 multi-component).

2. **State-Dependent Evidence Recalibration**: Maintains a per-evidence interaction log recording every reuse's polarity and performance change. Outcome consistency const(e) (Eq. 3) is the fraction of total observed |∆| that agrees with the evidence's current polarity; freshness fresh_t(e) = 0.9^(t−ℓt(e)) decays with disuse; historical reliability rt(e) = const(e)·fresh_t(e). Structural compatibility qt(e) (Eq. 5) parses the anchored component's code at evidence-creation time and at present into ASTs (normalizing identifiers but preserving literals, prompts, control flow, call patterns, and state-update operations) and computes 1 minus normalized tree-edit distance. The final calibrated weight we (Eq. 6) is a harmonic-mean-style combination of rt(e) and qt(e), so a high score in one cannot compensate for a low score in the other.

3. **Role-Conditioned Search Intent Distillation**: After running ht, DREvo extracts current failure patterns Ft, retrieves evidence Zt tied to the same failure categories, and partitions it by threshold θ=0.5 into reliable-positive Xt, reliable-negative At, and low-confidence Tt sets (Eq. 7). Algorithm 1 activates a role — Exploit (bind to Xt's direction), Avoid (steer away from At), Retest (non-binding reference to Tt, with a fallback to Explore if the prior Retest attempt failed), or Explore (no evidence constraint) — and function G distills (at, Ft, Z^at_t) into an explicit search intent st specifying the target component and modification direction, from which ht+1 is sampled.

**Experimental setup**: Proposer = Claude Opus 4.6 (1M-token context) for all evolution-based methods. Task-execution models: GPT-OSS-120B for domain-reasoning tasks (USPTO chemical reaction prediction, Symptom2Disease medical diagnosis, Law/LawBench legal reasoning), DeepSeek-V4-Flash (no thinking) for agentic tasks (Terminal-Bench 2.0, SWE-Bench Verified). Evolution runs 20 iterations for domain reasoning and 10 for agentic tasks. Domain-reasoning harnesses initialize from a combination of "no_memory" + Few-Shot (all); agentic harnesses initialize from Terminus-KIRA (a terminal-oriented harness), notably not a coding-specific one, so SWE-Bench gains reflect cross-domain harness adaptation. Baselines span non-evolutionary hand-built harnesses (Naive, Few-Shot variants, OpenHands, Terminus-KIRA, Terminus-2, Mini-SWE-Agent, Claude Code v2.1.150) and evolution-based methods (MCE, ACE, Meta-harness, AHE).

## Results & Highlights

- **Domain reasoning (Table 1)**: DREvo reaches 25.0% accuracy on USPTO vs. second-best ACE's 16.0% (+9.0pp), 89.2% on S2D vs. second-best Meta-harness's 86.8% (+2.4pp), and 49.0% on Law vs. second-best Meta-harness's 45.0% (+4.0pp).
- **Agentic tasks (Table 1)**: On Terminal-Bench 2.0 full suite, DREvo scores 42.7% vs. second-best Terminus-2's 34.8% (+7.9pp). On SWE-Bench Verified, DREvo scores 67.6% vs. joint second-best OpenHands (61.8%) and AHE (61.8%) (+5.8pp) — notably starting evolution from the terminal-oriented Terminus-KIRA harness (25.8%/41.4%) rather than a coding-specific one.
- **Fine-grained difficulty breakdown (Table 2)**: On Terminal-Bench 2.0, DREvo ties for best on Easy (75.0%) and leads on Medium (49.1% vs. second-best OpenHands' 41.8%) and Hard (26.7% vs. second-best Terminus-2's 16.7%), with the gain growing from 7.3pp (Medium) to 10.0pp (Hard) as task difficulty increases.
- **Context-efficiency comparison (Figure 3)**: DREvo keeps proposer context bounded at ~2.9K tokens across 20 iterations while Meta-harness's context grows past 5.3K tokens and still shows larger accuracy oscillations, indicating DREvo's gains come from better evidence targeting rather than more context.
- **Robustness to component-state drift (Figure 4)**: Across 16 settings (4 drift types × 4 benchmarks) where a component of the final evolved harness is randomly perturbed by Claude Opus 4.6, "blind reuse" of un-recalibrated evidence loses 5.3 points on average, while DREvo gains 0.6 points on average and maintains-or-improves in 11/16 settings vs. only 2/16 for blind reuse; the largest single gap is prompt drift on S2D, where blind reuse loses 10.0pp but DREvo gains 6.0pp.
- **Ablation study (Table 3)**: Starting from a no-component baseline (12.0% USPTO, 78.8% S2D, 34.0% Law, 29.2% TB2-full), adding only Function-Level Anchoring (A) improves USPTO to 20.0% but leaves TB2 unchanged at 29.2%; adding State-Dependent Recalibration (R) on top of A pushes TB2 to 35.9% (+6.7pp over A alone); the full A+R+D system reaches 25.0%/89.2%/49.0%/42.7%, beating A+D (without R: 18.0%/84.0%/42.0%/37.1%) by 5.6pp on TB2 full suite, showing R and D are complementary rather than redundant.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — that historical experience in iterative self-improvement loops is state-dependent and needs explicit staleness detection rather than static accumulation — is a genuine and well-motivated observation, empirically grounded in Figure 1's demonstration that both full-history and compressed-history Meta-harness oscillate rather than converge. The three-part mechanism (function-level anchoring, reliability×compatibility recalibration, role-conditioned distillation) is a reasonably novel systems contribution, though each individual piece (AST diffing for code compatibility, decay-weighted reliability scoring, rule-based role routing) draws on fairly standard techniques recombined for this setting.

**Rigor**: The ablation study (Table 3) is a genuine strength, cleanly isolating each component's marginal contribution and showing they interact non-trivially (A+D without R actually underperforms on aggregate despite hitting 100% on TB2-Easy). The drift-robustness experiment (Figure 4) is a thoughtful stress test that directly targets the paper's central claim. However, a specific weak point: the paper's headline baselines for agentic tasks include several hand-engineered, non-evolutionary harnesses (OpenHands, Terminus-2, Mini-SWE-Agent, Claude Code) that were never given an evolution budget at all — comparing DREvo's 10-iteration evolved harness against these static systems is not an apples-to-apples ablation of "evolution mechanism quality," only of "evolved vs. hand-built," so the true evolution-specific gain is better read from the Meta-harness/AHE/ACE/MCE comparisons alone (still favorable to DREvo, but a smaller and more specific claim than the headline table suggests).

**Reproducibility**: No code repository is linked in the paper text (a gap relative to some cited baselines), though the method, hyperparameters (θ=0.5 confidence threshold, 0.9 decay base, we∈[0,1] weight formula), model choices, and iteration budgets are specified precisely enough to reimplement. One data quirk: the paper reports two slightly different aggregate improvement numbers for agentic tasks — "13.8%" in the abstract and "14.2%" in the contributions list — a minor internal inconsistency that suggests the summary statistics weren't fully reconciled across paper drafts.

**Significance**: A limited-budget setting (10-20 iterations) is a practically important and underexplored regime for harness self-evolution, since each iteration is costly (multiple LLM calls, task-environment interactions). The demonstrated context-efficiency (bounded ~2.9K tokens vs. Meta-harness's >5.3K with worse stability, Figure 3) is a meaningful practical advantage independent of the raw accuracy numbers.

**Red flags / caveats**: AT_t compatibility (Eq. 5) relies on AST tree-edit distance as, per the paper's own admission, "a lightweight structural proxy for compatibility rather than a proof of semantic equivalence" — so behaviorally significant but structurally minor edits (or vice versa) could be miscalibrated, and the paper doesn't quantify how often this proxy fails. The agentic evolution budget (10 iterations) is notably smaller than domain reasoning's (20), and no ablation isolates whether DREvo's advantage would shrink or grow with a larger budget — the "limited budget" framing is central to the paper's motivation but the budget itself isn't varied as an experimental axis.

## Strengths

- Clear empirical motivation (Figure 1) showing that both full-history and compressed-history reuse in an existing SOTA method (Meta-harness) genuinely oscillate rather than steadily improve, grounding the paper's problem statement in concrete evidence rather than assumption.
- Well-designed ablation (Table 3) that isolates each of the three proposed components' contributions and reveals a non-trivial finding: Role-Conditioned Distillation without State-Dependent Recalibration can overfit to easy cases (100% on TB2-Easy) while lagging on aggregate metrics, demonstrating the components are complementary rather than independently sufficient.
- The drift-robustness experiment (Figure 4) directly stress-tests the paper's central "stale evidence" hypothesis under controlled component perturbation, rather than relying solely on end-to-end benchmark numbers.
- Demonstrates cross-domain harness adaptation (agentic evolution starting from a terminal-oriented Terminus-KIRA harness reaching competitive SWE-Bench Verified performance), which is a stronger generalization claim than evolving within a single task family.
- Maintains bounded proposer context size (~2.9K tokens) throughout evolution, addressing a practical scalability concern for long-running self-evolution loops.

## Limitations

- No code release is mentioned in the paper, limiting independent reproducibility of the exact anchoring/recalibration/distillation implementation.
- The headline agentic-task comparison table mixes evolution-based and purely hand-engineered non-evolutionary baselines (OpenHands, Terminus-2, Mini-SWE-Agent, Claude Code), which received no evolution budget at all, making the evolution-specific contribution harder to isolate from the raw accuracy table alone.
- The evolution budget (10 iterations for agentic tasks vs. 20 for domain reasoning) is fixed per task type rather than varied as an experimental axis, so how DREvo's advantage scales with budget size is untested despite "limited evolution budget" being the paper's central framing.
- Structural compatibility relies on AST tree-edit distance as an explicitly acknowledged proxy, not a semantic-equivalence guarantee, and the paper does not quantify cases where this proxy misjudges compatibility.
- Minor inconsistency between the abstract's reported agentic-task gain (13.8%) and the contributions section's figure (14.2%).
- The paper's own limitations section (Conclusion) flags cross-component evidence calibration and adaptive role-selection policies for longer horizons as unaddressed future work, implying the current recalibration and role-routing logic may not scale cleanly to substantially longer evolution runs.

## Relevance

该工作对关注 Agent harness / context engineering 自动化演化、以及如何在有限预算下让 LLM 系统自我改进的读者有较强参考价值，其"证据必须显式重校准有效性，而非静态累积"的思路也可能迁移到其他基于历史反馈迭代优化 prompt/workflow 的场景。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does DREvo's advantage change as the evolution budget is varied (e.g., 5 vs. 20 vs. 50 iterations) rather than fixed at the paper's chosen 10/20-iteration settings?
- Would a semantic-equivalence check (e.g., behavioral testing or embedding-based similarity) instead of AST tree-edit distance improve structural-compatibility estimation, and how often does the current AST proxy diverge from actual behavioral compatibility?
- How sensitive are the results to the choice of proposer model (Claude Opus 4.6) — would a smaller or different-family LLM proposer still benefit proportionally from role-conditioned search intent?
- Can the reliability×compatibility recalibration formula (Eq. 6) be extended to handle cross-component evidence (e.g., an evidence unit whose validity depends on the joint state of two interacting components), as the authors flag as future work?
- Given the mixed evolution-based/non-evolution-based baseline comparison for agentic tasks, what would DREvo's margin look like if all baselines (including OpenHands, Terminus-2, Claude Code) were given the same 10-iteration evolution budget using their own proposer mechanisms?
