> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 CDPI（跨尺度方向参数注入）这一无需训练的线性探针，通过形状截断、注意力头映射和 LLM 层映射把大模型参数投影进小模型空间再按比例注入，在四对 Qwen3-VL 模型（4B→2B、8B→2B、32B→4B、32B→8B）和十二个基准上系统性地发现：跨尺度融合带来的能力提升几乎只集中在高层推理（如 32B→4B 对在 α=0.02 时 MATH-Vision 从目标模型的 19.41 提升到 23.81，+4.40），感知能力基本不变，且这种选择性迁移仅出现在小注入比例区间，随机扰动对照组完全无法复现该效应。

> **TL;DR:** This paper diagnoses what actually transfers when heterogeneous MLLMs are fused without training, using a new linear probe called CDPI (Cross-Scale Directional Parameter Injection) that projects a larger Qwen3-VL model's parameters into a smaller target's shape (via truncation, attention-head mapping, and LLM-layer mapping) and injects the direction at ratio α. Across four Qwen3-VL source→target pairs (4B→2B, 8B→2B, 32B→4B, 32B→8B) and twelve benchmarks, gains concentrate almost entirely in high-level reasoning — e.g., on the 32B→4B pair, MATH-Vision rises from the unfused target's 19.41 to 23.81 at α=0.02 (+4.40), while the perception average barely moves (75.12→74.96, -0.16) — and a random multiplicative-perturbation control of matched magnitude fails to reproduce these gains (high-level-reasoning average 24.02±1.31 across 5 seeds vs. CDPI's 26.66 at α=0.02 on the 8B→2B pair, against the unfused target's 24.96).

## 核心贡献

- 提出 CDPI（Cross-Scale Directional Parameter Injection）：一种无需训练的线性探针，通过异构对齐算子 A_π（形状截断 + 注意力头等间隔映射 + LLM 层映射）把源模型参数投影到目标模型参数空间，再按注入比例 α 沿该方向线性插值，用于诊断异构 MLLM 融合过程中"什么发生了迁移"。
- 给出局部二阶理论分析：能力增益 Gain_c ≈ αA_c − (α²/2)I_c，其中一阶信号 A_c 衡量投影方向与该能力局部改进方向的对齐程度，二阶曲率项 I_c 代表形状截断、参数错位等结构性干扰；据此预测小 α 区间内正向选择性迁移，大 α 时二阶干扰主导导致性能退化。
- 在 Qwen3-VL 四个尺度（2B/4B/8B/32B）构成的四对源-目标模型和涵盖高层推理、通用推理、感知三大类共十二个基准上系统评测，发现跨尺度知识迁移高度集中于高层推理（尤其数学、逻辑、组合视觉推理），感知能力几乎不受影响。
- 通过组件消融（ViT/投影层/LLM）、参数范围消融（仅线性层 vs 全部可对齐参数）、四种层映射策略（Prefix/Depth-proportional/Suffix/Dual-end）以及随机乘性扰动对照实验，定位出高层推理增益主要来自 LLM 组件、线性层权重迁移效率更高、最优层映射因模型对而异，且迁移仅在小注入比例（约 α=0.01–0.04）区间内稳定为正。

## Key Contributions

- **Introduces CDPI**, a training-free linear probe that injects a projected source-model direction into a smaller target model via a heterogeneous alignment operator A_π (shape truncation for mismatched tensor dimensions, uniform-interval attention-head mapping, and one of four LLM-layer mapping strategies), controlled by an injection ratio α, to mechanistically analyze cross-scale heterogeneous MLLM fusion rather than to maximize fused performance.
- **Provides a local second-order theoretical account** of selective transfer: Gain_c ≈ αA_c − (α²/2)I_c decomposes the capability-level gain into a first-order transfer signal (alignment between the injected direction and the capability's local improvement direction) and a second-order curvature/interference term (structural mismatch from truncation, misalignment, or component conflict), predicting a narrow low-α regime of positive selective transfer.
- **Empirically characterizes what, where, and how knowledge transfers** across four Qwen3-VL source-target pairs (4B→2B, 8B→2B, 32B→4B, 32B→8B) and twelve benchmarks grouped into high-level reasoning, general reasoning, and perception, showing transfer is capability-selective (concentrated in reasoning), component-selective (LLM linear weights carry most of the gain), and ratio-selective (confined to a narrow low-injection regime).
- **Validates directional transfer against a random-perturbation control**: shows via a matched-magnitude random multiplicative-noise baseline (same target parameter subset, 5 seeds) that CDPI's gains depend on the aligned source-target direction itself, not merely on injecting generic parameter noise of comparable norm.

## Methodology

CDPI partitions each MLLM into three components — vision encoder (ViT), cross-modal projector (Proj), and language model (LLM) — and defines the fused model as Θ_F = Θ_T + α·ΔΘ_{E,π}, where ΔΘ_{E,π} = M_E ⊙ [A_π(Θ_S) − Θ_T] is the masked, aligned source-minus-target direction, M_E is a binary mask selecting the chosen injection space E = (C, P) (component subset C ⊆ {ViT, Proj, LLM} and parameter scope P ∈ {linear, full}), and α ∈ [0,1] is the injection ratio. The heterogeneous alignment operator A_π handles three sources of architectural mismatch between differently-scaled Qwen3-VL checkpoints: (1) shape truncation, which keeps only the leading slice of the source tensor along each mismatched dimension so it matches the target's shape; (2) attention-head mapping, which selects complete source query-head blocks at approximately uniform intervals (ρ_q(j) = ⌊j·H_S^q/H_T^q⌋) to avoid splitting head blocks (K/V heads are equal across the studied pairs, so no additional mapping is needed there); and (3) LLM-layer mapping π, one of four strategies — Prefix (early source layers), Depth-proportional (uniformly sampled by depth), Suffix (late source layers), or Dual-end (mixing shallow and deep source layers) — that maps each target layer index to a source layer index while leaving the internal parameter path unchanged.

A local theoretical analysis performs a second-order Taylor expansion of the target's generalization loss around Θ_T under the CDPI update, yielding Gain_c ≈ αA_c − (α²/2)I_c per capability c, where A_c = −∇_Θ L_c(Θ_T)^T Δ is the first-order transfer signal and I_c = Δ^T H_c Δ is the second-order curvature/interference term (H_c the Hessian). This predicts a locally optimal ratio α* = A_c/I_c and a positive-gain window 0 < α < 2A_c/I_c when both A_c and I_c are positive.

Experiments use Qwen3-VL-Instruct at four scales (2B/4B/8B/32B), forming four source→target pairs with exact checkpoint identifiers documented (e.g., 32B→4B uses Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct as source and Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct as target). Twelve benchmarks are grouped by capability: high-level reasoning (MMMU-Pro, MathVista, MATH-Vision, VisuLogic, VisualPuzzles), general reasoning (MMMU, MMVU), and perception (MME, MMStar, BLINK, OCRBench, ChartQA). The default configuration is C = All (all three components), P = linear (linear-layer weights only), and Prefix layer mapping; the main ratio sweep is α ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.08}, with α = 0.16 and 0.32 used only as stress tests on the 8B→2B pair. All evaluation uses the lmms-eval harness with benchmark-standard rule-based/exact-match scoring and no GPT-based judging, run on 8×A100 GPUs. A random-perturbation control multiplies each selected target weight tensor element-wise by i.i.d. noise U(1−δ, 1+δ) with δ=0.08 (matching CDPI's α=0.04 perturbation scale), averaged over 5 seeds, to test whether CDPI's gains require the aligned source direction specifically.

## Results & Highlights

- **High-level reasoning gains are large and consistent; perception gains are near zero.** On the 32B→4B pair, MATH-Vision improves from the unfused target's 19.41 to 23.81 at α=0.02 (+4.40 vs. target; source/32B scores 33.22), while the perception average across MME/MMStar/BLINK/OCRBench/ChartQA moves only from the target's 75.12 to a best-of-sweep 75.37 (+0.24), and at the matched fixed ratio α=0.02 the same pair's perception average actually drops slightly (74.96, −0.16) even as high-level reasoning gains +1.88 and general reasoning +0.86.
- **Component ablation isolates the LLM as the primary carrier of reasoning transfer.** Injecting only the LLM component yields the largest high-level-reasoning gain for every source-target pair — +2.78 (4B→2B), +2.96 (8B→2B), +2.62 (32B→4B), +1.69 (32B→8B) — versus substantially smaller gains from ViT-only or Projector-only injection (e.g., for 8B→2B, ViT-only gives +1.10 and Proj-only +0.63, both well below LLM-only's +2.96).
- **Parameter scope matters: restricting injection to linear-layer weights transfers reasoning more efficiently than injecting all alignable parameters.** Averaged across the four pairs, the All-linear scope improves high-level reasoning by +2.26 versus +1.30 for All-full, and general reasoning by +1.07 versus roughly 0 for All-full, while perception stays near the target under both settings.
- **Directional transfer is not reproduced by generic parameter noise.** On the 8B→2B pair, CDPI at α=0.02 lifts the high-level-reasoning average from the target's 24.96 to 26.66, whereas a magnitude-matched random multiplicative perturbation (5 seeds, δ=0.08) averages only 24.02±1.31 — below the unfused target. At the benchmark level the gap is starker: VisuLogic improves +9.50 under CDPI at α=0.04 (16.70→26.20) versus the random control's 13.72±6.45 (below target), and MATH-Vision/VisualPuzzles gain +3.29/+2.40 under CDPI versus essentially no consistent gain under random perturbation.
- **Transfer is confined to a narrow, low-injection-ratio regime and reverses at higher ratios.** On the 8B→2B pair (Fig. 6), high-level reasoning peaks at α=0.02 and stays positive at α=0.04 but both high-level reasoning and perception fall below the target model by α=0.08; the α=0.16/0.32 stress tests show catastrophic degradation — e.g., MMMU-Pro collapses from the target's 23.76 to 0.00 at both α=0.16 and 0.32, and MATH-Vision falls from 7.89 to 0.33.
- **The best LLM-layer mapping strategy is pair-dependent, with no single mapping dominating.** Suffix and Dual-end achieve the largest high-level-reasoning gains for 4B→2B (+3.08 each), Suffix is best for 8B→2B (+3.02), Prefix is best for 32B→4B (+2.62), and Dual-end is best for 32B→8B (+1.73), indicating the depth region carrying reasoning signal shifts with model scale.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: Reframing heterogeneous MLLM fusion as a mechanism-analysis problem — using a deliberately simple, controlled linear probe (CDPI) rather than proposing yet another performance-maximizing fusion algorithm — is a genuinely useful contribution distinct from the cited prior work (LS-Merge, Transport and Merge, AdaMMS, OptMerge, UQ-Merge, RobustMerge), all of which optimize for fused accuracy rather than explain what transfers.

**Rigor**: The local second-order theoretical analysis is a reasonable Taylor-expansion formalization of an otherwise intuitive story (small perturbations preserve signal, large ones invoke curvature/interference), and the paper backs it with a genuinely convincing random-perturbation control (Table 4, 5 seeds) — a check many merging papers skip — showing CDPI's gains are direction-dependent, not noise-dependent. However, CDPI itself is evaluated with single runs (no seeds/repetitions) while its random control is averaged over 5 seeds, an asymmetry in reported statistical rigor between the method and its own baseline.

**Reproducibility**: Exact checkpoint identifiers (Table S1), full injection-space/mask formalization, and complete fixed-ratio result tables for all twelve benchmarks and four pairs are given in the supplementary material — strong documentation for a probe-style paper. No code repository link is visible in the read text, however, which limits independent verification of the exact CDPI implementation.

**Significance**: The central finding — that training-free cross-scale fusion transfers reasoning selectively and only within a narrow injection-ratio window, while claims of broad "capability inheritance" don't hold up under a 12-benchmark capability-level breakdown — is a useful corrective for a fusion literature that the paper itself notes is "largely designed and evaluated on limited task sets or aggregate metrics."

**Red flags / weak spots**: (1) All experiments stay within a single model family (Qwen3-VL) at different scales; the paper's title and framing invoke "heterogeneous MLLM fusion" broadly, but the actual heterogeneity tested is architecture width/depth within one family, not cross-family heterogeneity (different vision encoders, tokenizers, or training corpora) that methods like LS-Merge and AdaMMS in the related work were built to address — so the generality of "reasoning transfers, perception doesn't" beyond same-family scale differences is untested. (2) The paper never compares CDPI's fused outputs against any of the actual heterogeneous-fusion baselines it cites (LS-Merge, Transport and Merge, AdaMMS, OptMerge); its only comparisons are against the unfused target model and a random-perturbation control, so it remains unclear whether the observed selectivity pattern is specific to CDPI's simple linear injection or would also emerge under more sophisticated learned-mapping fusion. (3) The headline "best-of-sweep" tables (Tables S2–S13) pick the best α per benchmark post-hoc from a 5-point grid, which the authors themselves label "an upper-bound estimate of CDPI's beneficial effect" — an honest caveat, but it means the reported gains are not achievable in a realistic deployment without a labeled validation set to select α. (4) A few individual benchmarks show negative deltas even at best-of-sweep (e.g., MathVista −0.50 for 4B→2B, ChartQA −0.12 for 32B→8B), and because CDPI itself isn't repeated across seeds, it's not established whether these negative points are statistically distinguishable from the ~1.3-point noise floor seen in the random-perturbation control.

## Strengths

- A clean, falsifiable theoretical prediction (narrow positive-transfer window from the first-order/second-order decomposition) that the empirical ratio sweep (Fig. 6, Table 3) directly confirms and the stress-test ratios (α=0.16/0.32) directly stress.
- The random multiplicative-perturbation control, matched in magnitude and averaged over 5 seeds, is a rigorous ablation that isolates directional alignment as the source of CDPI's gains rather than generic parameter noise.
- Systematic multi-axis ablation (component × parameter scope × layer mapping × injection ratio) across four source-target pairs and twelve benchmarks gives a comprehensive picture rather than a single cherry-picked configuration.
- Full experimental transparency: exact model identifiers, complete fixed-ratio tables for every benchmark/pair/ratio combination, and the random-control construction with explicit variance formulas are all provided in the supplementary material.

## Limitations

- Only same-family, different-scale Qwen3-VL pairs are tested; whether the reasoning-selective transfer pattern and the LLM-as-primary-carrier finding generalize to genuinely heterogeneous architectures (different vision backbones, tokenizers, or training data) is untested.
- No empirical comparison against existing heterogeneous-fusion methods (LS-Merge, Transport and Merge, AdaMMS, OptMerge) cited in the related work, so CDPI's mechanistic findings cannot be positioned relative to what those more sophisticated methods actually transfer.
- The reported "best" results require sweeping and post-hoc selecting the injection ratio α per benchmark; no validation-free rule for choosing α (or the layer-mapping strategy) at deployment time is proposed.
- The optimal LLM-layer mapping strategy varies unpredictably across source-target pairs (Prefix, Suffix, and Dual-end are each best for a different pair) with no predictive account of why.
- Evaluation relies solely on rule-based/exact-match scoring via lmms-eval with no GPT-based judging, which is reproducible but may undercount partial credit on open-ended reasoning outputs.

## Relevance

该工作对做模型融合、知识蒸馏或跨尺度模型协同的研究者有直接参考价值：它提供了一个轻量的诊断工具和一套值得复用的评估范式（随机扰动对照、按能力分组的基准拆解），提醒在报告"融合后性能提升"时应避免仅依赖聚合指标，而应像本文一样拆解到能力层面核实迁移的真实来源。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the reasoning-selective, LLM-dominant transfer pattern still hold for genuinely heterogeneous MLLM pairs (different vision encoders, tokenizers, or base LLM families), or is it an artifact of testing only same-family Qwen3-VL scale variants?
- How does CDPI's selective-transfer signature compare directly against learned-mapping fusion methods like LS-Merge or AdaMMS on the same twelve-benchmark suite — do they show the same reasoning/perception split, or does learned alignment transfer perception too?
- Can a cheap, validation-free estimator of A_c and I_c (the paper's own first-order signal and second-order interference terms) be used to predict the optimal α and layer mapping in advance, removing the need for a full benchmark sweep?
- Why does reasoning transfer concentrate in the LLM component even for visually-grounded benchmarks like MathVista and MATH-Vision — does this reflect genuine language-side reasoning transfer, or that these benchmarks are substantially solvable from text/OCR content already visible to the target model?
- Does the catastrophic collapse observed at α=0.16/0.32 (e.g., MMMU-Pro falling to 0.00) reflect pure second-order interference as the theory predicts, or a more brittle failure mode (e.g., broken attention-head alignment) that the local quadratic approximation doesn't capture?
