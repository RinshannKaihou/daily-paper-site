> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 OptimismBench，用"反转配对"（同一情境分别问成功概率和失败概率，正常应互补为100）来无需真值地测量LLM的方向性乐观/悲观偏差；在16个来自8家厂商的模型中，14个表现出乐观偏差（Skew最高GLM-4.7-flash达+16.6），只有Anthropic的前沿模型（Opus -5.1、Sonnet -7.7）表现出悲观偏差，且经过基座-对话模型的对照实验证明这一方向性由post-training（对齐）过程决定，而非模型规模。

> **TL;DR:** OptimismBench introduces an "inverted-pair" method (asking both P(success) and P(failure) for the same scenario) to measure directional probability bias in LLMs without ground truth, computing Skew = P(success) − (100 − P(failure)). Across 16 models from 8 providers on 60 scenarios (Track B), 14 models are optimistic (Skew from +4.2 to +16.6) while only Anthropic's frontier tier is pessimistic (Opus 4.6: −5.1, Sonnet 4.6: −7.7); a controlled base-vs-chat probe across 11 model pairs from 4 families (Qwen, Llama, Gemma, Mistral) shows post-training/alignment — not scale — flips the sign, with Qwen alignment shifting all 5 pairs toward pessimism (−5.6 to −16.2 pp) while Llama alignment shifts all 4 pairs toward optimism (+15.2 to +40.9 pp).

## 核心贡献

- 提出"反转配对"（inverted pairs）测量法：对同一情境分别提问"成功概率"和"失败概率"，通过 Skew = s⁺ − (100 − s⁻) 计算方向性偏差分数，无需外部真值即可检测LLM概率判断中系统性的乐观/悲观倾向。
- 构建 OptimismBench 基准，含60个跨6大领域（日常、学术、项目、商业、政策、健康）的反转配对情境，扩展到10种语言、四类概率公理测试（合取律、条件、剂量-反应单调性、校准），共发布3,870个测试项。
- 在16个模型（8家厂商）上系统评测，发现所有模型都存在显著方向性偏差：14个乐观、仅Anthropic前沿层（Opus、Sonnet）悲观，且该模式在prompt变化、温度变化、视角转换（第三人称/第二人称/朋友）及显式自我纠偏提示下均保持稳定。
- 通过11对匹配的base-vs-chat模型（4个模型家族：Qwen、Llama、Gemma、Mistral）做受控因果探针，证明是post-training（对齐）过程而非模型规模决定偏差方向：Qwen对齐使5/5配对朝悲观方向压缩，Llama对齐使4/4配对朝乐观方向放大。
- 通过17模型六语言的跨语言比较发现，模型身份（inter-model方差7.2pp）对Skew的影响是语言（inter-language方差1.5pp）的4.7倍，说明"用哪个模型"比"用哪种语言"更重要；同时发现推荐（Track C）与显著性评估（Track D）任务框架下偏差方向会反转，说明偏差是任务框架依赖的，而非单一潜在倾向。

## Key Contributions

- **Inverted-pair methodology**: A ground-truth-free method for detecting directional probability bias by eliciting both P(positive outcome) and P(negative outcome) for the same scenario and measuring their deviation from complementarity (Skew = s⁺ − (100 − s⁻)), decomposable into δ⁺ and δ⁻ valence components.
- **OptimismBench dataset**: 60 controlled inverted-pair scenarios across 6 domains (everyday, academic, project, business, policy, health), extended to 10 languages, 4 probability-axiom batteries (conjunction, conditional, dose-response, calibration), totaling 3,870 released items.
- **16-model, 8-provider evaluation**: Found universal directional bias — 14/16 models optimistic (Skew +4.2 to +16.6), only Anthropic's frontier tier (Opus, Sonnet) pessimistic (−5.1, −7.7), while lightweight Haiku 4.5 within the same lab is optimistic (+6.1), ruling out a simple "bigger models are less biased" account.
- **Causal alignment probe**: 11 matched base-versus-chat pairs across 4 families (Qwen, Llama, Gemma, Mistral) holding architecture and pre-training fixed, showing post-training sets bias direction in a family-specific way (Qwen compresses toward pessimism on 5/5 pairs; Llama amplifies optimism on 4/4 pairs; Gemma-2-2b flips +53.5 pp single-pair pilot).
- **Cross-lingual variance decomposition**: 17-model, 6-language comparison shows inter-model σ (7.2 pp) is 4.7x inter-language σ (1.5 pp) — model identity dominates language in determining bias.

## Methodology

The core measurement is the **inverted-pair** design (Fig. 1): for each of 60 scenarios, the model is asked (in separate queries) to estimate P(positive outcome) → s⁺ and P(negative outcome) → s⁻ on a 0–100 scale. A perfectly coherent model produces s⁺ + s⁻ = 100; **Skew = s⁺ − (100 − s⁻)** captures the signed deviation (positive = optimistic, negative = pessimistic). Skew is further decomposed into δ⁺ = mean(s⁺) − 50 and δ⁻ = mean(s⁻) − 50 to separate "good-side" and "bad-side" distortion (e.g., compound overclaim when both δ⁺>0 and δ⁻>0, vs. classical optimism when δ⁺>0 and δ⁻<0).

Four tracks apply the same inverted-pair structure to different elicitation types: **Track A** (calibration control, 15 items with explicitly stated base rates, expected Skew≈0 as a sanity check), **Track B** (primary track: 60 naturalistic scenarios with no stated base rate, forcing the model to judge under genuine uncertainty), **Track C** (recommendation strength for action vs. inaction), and **Track D** (salience rating of opportunities vs. risks). Scenarios were quality-filtered by cross-model variance (<3 excluded as too unambiguous) and pair-consistency (>20 pp average deviation flagged for review).

To isolate the *source* of bias, the authors run four factorial interventions holding the scenario fixed: **narrative manipulation** (append one positive/negative context sentence), **perspective shift** (third-person vs. second-person vs. "friend," to rule out sycophancy), **anchoring gradient** (vary explicitness of stated base rates, tracing the Track B→Track A transition), and **self-debiasing** (prepend an explicit system-prompt warning about LLM optimism bias). The key causal design is the **base-vs-chat probe**: 11 architecture-matched pairs across Qwen2.5/Qwen3 (5 pairs, 1.7B–14B), Llama-3.1/3.2 (4 pairs, 1B–70B), plus single-pair pilots for Gemma-2-2b and Mistral-Small-24B, holding architecture and pre-training constant while varying only the post-training/alignment procedure — this lets the paper attribute bias direction to alignment rather than scale. All 16 headline models are evaluated with 5–10 independent runs at temperature 0.7 (three exceptions at 1.0), with significance assessed via bootstrap 95% CIs and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Bonferroni-corrected across 16 tests.

## Results & Highlights

- **Universal directional bias (Track B, 16 models, 60 scenarios, Table 1/9)**: All 16 models show statistically significant Skew (all p<.0001 to p<.001 after Bonferroni correction at α=0.05/16≈0.003). GLM-4.7-flash (Zhipu) is most optimistic at Skew=+16.6 (95% CI [+13.6, +19.7]); Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic) is most pessimistic at Skew=−7.7 (95% CI [−9.5, −6.0]). Fourteen of 16 models are optimistic; only Anthropic's Opus 4.6 (−5.1) and Sonnet 4.6 (−7.7) are pessimistic, while Anthropic's own lightweight Haiku 4.5 is optimistic (+6.1) — a same-lab sign split ruling out a simple scale account.
- **Post-training causally sets bias direction (Table 2)**: Across 11 base-vs-chat pairs, every Qwen pair shifts negative after alignment (5/5, Δ from −5.6 to −16.2 pp; binomial p=1/32), while every Llama pair shifts positive (4/4, Δ from +15.2 to +40.9 pp; binomial p=1/16). Concretely, Llama-3.2-1B goes from base Skew −33.7 to chat Skew +7.3 (Δ=+40.9), and Qwen3-14B goes from base +16.1 to chat −0.1 (Δ=−16.2). Gemma-2-2b (single-pair pilot) shows the largest flip: base −17.5 → chat +36.0 (Δ=+53.5 pp).
- **Calibration control validates the method (Track A)**: On 15 items with explicitly stated base rates (e.g., "acceptance rate is 25%"), all models report Skew≈0.00, confirming the Track B bias arises specifically from judgment under uncertainty, not from a general computational failure to sum probabilities correctly.
- **Robustness ablations (Table 3, §4.3)**: Direction is preserved under prompt-detail ablation (6/6 model-track combinations), temperature change (0.7 vs 1.0, ≤0.8 pp shift for 4 models, e.g. GPT-5.4 +9.7→+10.0), perspective shift (third-person vs. second-person vs. friend, |Δ|<2 for both tested models, ruling out sycophancy as a confound), and self-debiasing (explicit warning to GPT-5.4 moves Skew from +49.5 to +46.0, Δ=−3.6, absorbing only about a third of the +10.0 baseline bias — meaning the bias survives explicit correction attempts).
- **Cross-lingual variance decomposition (§4.6, Table 4)**: Across 17 models × 6 native-prompt languages, inter-model σ within a language is 7.2 pp versus inter-language σ within a model of 1.5 pp — a 4.7x ratio, meaning which model is used matters far more than which language it is queried in. Aggregate per-language Skew ranges narrowly from +6.5 (KO) to +8.1 (RU), a 1.6 pp band (Table 4 aggregate row), showing language-specific effects largely cancel across models.
- **Task-framing reversal (Track C/D pilot, 7 models, Appendix C.1, Table 17)**: The same models on the same scenarios produce opposite-sign Skew under different elicitation frames: Track D (salience of opportunities vs. risks) is uniformly optimistic (+16 to +42 pp across all 7 models) while Track C (recommendation strength for action vs. inaction) is uniformly cautious/pessimistic in 6/7 models (−13 to −69 pp; Mistral Large +5.5 is the lone exception). This shows directional bias is not a single latent tilt but is task-framing-dependent.
- **Dose-response monotonicity is alignment-resistant (§4.5)**: Conjunction-axiom violations are near-zero (0–10%) across 5 fully-covered models, but dose-response monotonicity violations remain consistently high (27–47%) across all of them — meaning post-training corrects single-step probability coherence far more readily than multi-step monotonic reasoning across an evidence ladder.

## Authors & Credibility

Seonglae Cho and Adriano Koshiyama, affiliated with Holistic AI and University College London. Holistic AI is an AI governance/auditing firm, which aligns with the paper's framing around auditable, deployment-relevant bias metrics. The paper is a preprint (arXiv, cs.CL, July 2026) with no indication of peer review venue; it is methodologically careful (pre-registered-style construction principles, quality-control filters, Bonferroni correction, bootstrap CIs, multiple independent robustness ablations) but is a two-author work without large-scale community vetting.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The inverted-pair Skew metric is a genuinely useful methodological contribution — it recovers directional information (optimistic vs. pessimistic) that standard calibration metrics like ECE structurally cannot, since ECE aggregates unsigned errors. The paper explicitly and correctly differentiates itself from Zhu and Griffiths (2025) and Freedman and Toni (2025), which use similar paired-complement elicitation but report only unsigned coherence violations, not signed direction.

**Rigor**: The base-vs-chat causal probe (Table 2) is the strongest part of the paper — holding architecture and pre-training fixed while varying only post-training is a legitimate quasi-causal design. However, the authors themselves are careful to flag that the "within-provider observational gradient" (§4.2, e.g., OpenAI +13.1→+10.0, Anthropic +6.1→−5.1→−7.7) is *confounded* with training data, provider, and safety-tuning intensity and "does not stand on its own as causal evidence" — this honesty is a positive sign, though it does mean the headline "smaller/cheaper models carry greater optimistic bias" claim (Discussion, citing Rathi et al. 2025) rests on a mix of causal (4 families) and correlational (provider-tier) evidence that isn't always clearly separated in how results get summarized.

**Reproducibility**: The paper commits to releasing the 3,870-item dataset and all model responses, and documents exact system/user prompt templates in the appendix (A.1) — this is good practice for a benchmark paper. Per-model run counts (5–10) and temperatures are documented, though the base rate of only 5–10 runs per item for models with reported σ up to 11.9 (GLM-4.7-flash, Table 9) leaves meaningful per-item uncertainty even where model-level CIs are tight.

**Paper-specific weak point**: The scenarios are "author-constructed" (§3.4) rather than sourced from an external, independently validated dataset — quality control relies on cross-model variance and pair-consistency filters applied by the same team that wrote the items, which is a self-referential validation loop (items that don't provoke sufficient model disagreement or that produce >20pp pair inconsistency get replaced by the authors themselves, potentially shaping the item set toward whatever produces the cleanest Skew signal). Additionally, only 2 of the 11 base-vs-chat family pairs (Qwen and Llama) have ≥4 replications to support the "family-specific direction" claim with binomial tests (p=1/32, p=1/16) — Gemma-2-2b and Mistral-Small-24B are explicitly flagged by the authors as "single-pair pilots" that do not support power claims, which the paper is transparent about but which nonetheless means half the family evidence is anecdotal-strength.

**Significance**: The Track C/D task-framing reversal (§C.1) is an important and somewhat underemphasized finding — it substantially complicates the paper's own headline narrative ("directional bias is universal") by showing that the *same* models on the *same* scenarios flip sign entirely depending on whether they're asked to rate probability, salience, or recommend an action. The paper acknowledges this in the Discussion ("bias is task-framing-dependent... practitioners should profile models per task type rather than assume a single bias direction propagates uniformly") but this is a 7-model pilot (Appendix, not the main 16-model track) and arguably deserves more prominence given it undercuts a naive reading of the abstract.

**Red flags**: None indicating fabrication or malpractice; the paper's explicit "Limitations" section (§7) appropriately flags that cross-lingual evidence for 4 of 10 languages (DE, FR, HI, JA) mixes translated scenario text with English system prompts (a confound the authors name themselves), and that no matched human baseline exists (Weinstein 1980's human optimism-bias literature is cited but not replicated) — this is intellectually honest scoping.

## Strengths

- Clean, ground-truth-free metric (Skew) that isolates *direction* of probability bias where prior calibration work only captured magnitude.
- Strong robustness testing: prompt ablation, temperature, perspective-shift (ruling out sycophancy), and explicit self-debiasing all preserve the core finding.
- The base-vs-chat causal probe is a well-designed natural experiment that most alignment papers lack, directly implicating post-training rather than scale or architecture.
- Broad coverage: 16 models × 8 providers × 6 domains × 10 languages, with full dataset and response release for replication.
- Honest, granular self-critique — explicitly separates causal-suggestive evidence from merely observational/confounded evidence, and surfaces a finding (Track C/D reversal) that complicates its own headline claim.

## Limitations

- Scenarios are author-constructed and self-filtered by the same team, without an independent or crowdsourced validation set.
- The family-specific causal claim rests on only 2 well-replicated families (Qwen 5/5, Llama 4/4); Gemma and Mistral are single-pair pilots explicitly not treated as statistically powered evidence.
- No matched human baseline — Skew is validated only against the internal complementarity axiom, not against actual human forecasting accuracy or human optimism bias magnitudes, so cross-species comparisons in the Discussion are speculative.
- 4 of 10 languages (DE, FR, HI, JA) confound translated scenarios with English-language system prompts, a limitation the authors flag but do not resolve.
- Track C/D (recommendation, salience) evidence is only a 7-model, 10-scenario pilot, much smaller than the 16-model, 60-scenario Track B headline result, yet is used to support a fairly strong claim (bias sign reverses by task framing).

## Relevance

该论文与LLM可信度/对齐评测方向高度相关，为审计模型在不确定性下的方向性偏差提供了可复现、无需真值的量化工具，其"post-training决定偏差方向"的因果发现对理解RLHF副作用（如过度自信、谄媚）具有参考价值，适合关注模型校准、对齐副作用和LLM决策辅助可靠性的研究者深入阅读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the Qwen-compresses/Llama-amplifies split replicate with reward-model probing (as the authors suggest but don't test) to identify which specific RLHF reward component (truthfulness-weighted vs. helpfulness-weighted) drives the sign difference?
- How would Skew interact with real downstream decision pipelines (e.g., actual forecasting markets or resource-allocation tools) — does the measured probability distortion translate into measurably worse decisions, or does it wash out under aggregation?
- Would extending the Track C/D pilot to the full 16-model, 60-scenario scale change the "task-framing reversal" conclusion, and could a single per-model "bias-direction fingerprint" be constructed across all four tracks?
- Could a human baseline (matching Weinstein 1980's optimism-bias paradigm) be run on the same 60 scenarios to give an absolute reference point rather than only the axiom-relative Skew?
- Does dose-response monotonicity violation (27–47%, alignment-resistant per §4.5) correlate with Skew magnitude or sign at the per-model level, suggesting a shared underlying mechanism, or are they orthogonal failure modes as the paper's framing implies?
