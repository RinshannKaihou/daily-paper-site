A training-free audit finds that 12.73–26.25% of MLLMs' “correct” spatial-reasoning answers were never actually supported by the image.

Visual Credit Assignment for Multimodal Spatial Reasoning ↗
Feixiang Liu, Qiang Qiu, Lanbo Sun et al. ·29 Jul 2026 ·CAS Institute of Computing Technology ·preprint, not peer‑reviewed
Key metric — C‑U rate
12.73–26.25%
of nominally “correct” yes/no decisions — across 4 open MLLMs (7B–14B) × VSR & GSR‑COCO, 8 model×dataset runs — show the original image gave no more support than a text‑only or blank‑gray‑image control. Correct, but the image gets no credit.
Why this matters

Gives benchmark designers and hallucination/shortcut researchers a reusable, training‑free way to check whether accuracy numbers reflect real image use or language priors.

Critical edge

VCA is diagnostic only — it flags uncredited correctness but fixes nothing, and its edge over the closest prior baseline (VisNec, +3.12 pts) is modest while it is essentially tied with the Shortcut Score baseline (33.64 vs 33.92).

01 Methodology

VCA never retrains or fits anything. For each frozen yes/no query it caches the log‑likelihood margin between “no” and “yes” under three contexts, then asks whether the original image beats both no‑image controls.

A second, independent axis — Semantic Relation Contrast — uses GSR‑COCO's paired true/false relation statements on the same pixels to test whether the model can tell the queried relation from a different one, something no‑image controls structurally cannot probe. Relo requires accepting the true statement and rejecting every false alternative on the original image alone; Joint = Relo ∧ paired image dependence.

Robustness checks: matched same‑split image permutation (is it “any image” or the queried one?), a 1,800‑input‑per‑model controlled evidence‑source factorial, box‑erasure and verbalizer‑ensemble transport checks, and a human‑audited natural‑image edit set. Backbones: Qwen3‑VL‑8B‑Instruct, InternVL3.5‑8B, LLaVA‑OneVision‑Qwen2‑7B‑OV, Ministral‑3‑14B‑Instruct‑2512. All CIs use 5,000 paired‑bootstrap resamples.

Strengths

  • Clean, principled decomposition of accuracy into image‑dependent vs. image‑independent correctness, plus a second orthogonal relation‑consistency axis null controls provably cannot capture.
  • Extensive, multi‑angle robustness analysis — matched permutation, threshold sensitivity, verbalizer/answer‑surface transport, controlled factorial, human‑audited edits — not a single headline metric.
  • Fully training‑free and label‑free for the primary audit event, so it's cheap to apply to new benchmarks/models without fitting a scorer.
  • Honest framing of scope ("post‑hoc companion to accuracy, not a fitted replacement score") and code released for reproducibility.

Limitations

  • Measures relative support under prespecified controls, not internal causal pathways — cannot say which image regions drive a decision.
  • Fixed forced‑choice (yes/no) interface, two spatial benchmarks, four 7B–14B open models only — no open‑ended generation, no proprietary/frontier models.
  • The controlled evidence‑source factorial uses simple, procedurally rendered scenes, not natural images, limiting generalization of its responsiveness findings.
  • Natural‑image edit corroboration covers only 108/440 (≈24.5%) geometry‑feasible seeded groups, judged by three non‑author volunteers with no formal review.
  • Improvement over the closest prior baseline (VisNec‑style, +3.12 pts) is modest, and VCA is roughly tied with Shortcut Score.

03 Questions for further exploration

  • Does correct‑but‑uncredited (C‑U) shrink, persist, or worsen at frontier/proprietary scale (GPT‑4V, Gemini, Claude‑class) vs. the 7B–14B open models tested here?
  • How sensitive is the D‑CC/C‑U split to the choice of no‑image control — mid‑gray blank vs. random noise vs. average‑color vs. none at all?
  • Could the label‑free dependence signal (Gimg) become a training‑time regularizer or data filter to actively reduce C‑U mass, not just audit it post hoc?
  • How would the two‑axis decomposition extend to multi‑choice or open‑ended spatial QA beyond the binary yes/no interface studied here?
  • Would the 21.25–47.80 pt permutation excess and 12.73–26.25% C‑U range replicate on other spatial benchmarks (WhatsUp, CLEVR, SpatialBench) not evaluated here?
