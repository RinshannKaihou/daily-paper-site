Parameter-Free Dynamic Regret for Online Convex Optimization under Heavy-Tailed Noise ↗
Vaneet Aggarwal (Purdue University) · 29 Jul 2026 · single-author, arXiv preprint
Online Convex Optimization Dynamic Regret Heavy-Tailed Noise Parameter-Free AdaGrad
Key Metric
(p−1)/p
tradeoff exponent — proven optimal

The noise–path-length tradeoff exponent is matched exactly by a lower bound Ω(σD1/pT1/pPT(p−1)/p) with explicit constant cp=41/p/128 (Theorem 3) — no algorithm can do better.

Why This Matters

Gives online-learning theorists a reusable pathwise-meta-algorithm-plus-restart technique, but offers zero empirical guidance for tuning real heavy-tailed optimizers today.

Critical Edge

The "parameter-free" claim is narrower than it sounds: HT-PAder still must know the horizon T and diameter D in advance — only G, σ, p, and PT are removed.

Methodology

1 · SetupOCO on bounded domain D, G-Lipschitz losses, gradient noise with bounded p-th moment (not variance)
→
2 · AdaGrad-Hedgepathwise FTRL meta-algorithm — regret bound holds for any meta-loss realization, no moment assumption needed
→
3 · Restarted experts⌈log₂T⌉+1 AdaGrad-Norm experts, each restarted every block to localize the stepsize correction
→
4 · Combine & provegeometric block pool tracks unknown PT; 5-step meta-expert decomposition + sharper one-step inequality

The gradient oracle is only assumed to have a bounded p-th central moment (p∈(1,2]) — strictly weaker than finite variance, a regime where ordinary OGD/AdaGrad can provably diverge. AdaGrad-Hedge is an FTRL instantiation over a pool of experts whose regret bound holds pathwise, for any realization of meta-losses — this sidesteps the E[‖mt‖²]<∞ requirement that breaks standard Hedge once p<2 (Remark 2).

Each expert is an AdaGrad-Norm instance restarted every Li rounds; restarting is essential, since without it AdaGrad's variable stepsize drags in a correction term from round 1 instead of just the local block (Remark 1). A geometric pool of block lengths lets the near-optimal expert's scale track the unknown path length PT within a factor of 2, and a tighter one-step inequality (Lemma 2, credited to Liu 2026) replaces the usual ‖εt‖² noise term with ‖εt‖p — the technical move that makes the whole heavy-tailed analysis go through without gradient clipping.

  • PT=0 (no drift) → reduces exactly to Liu (2026)'s optimal static rate.
  • σ=0 (no noise) → recovers Improved Ader's deterministic minimax rate, without needing G.
  • p=2 (finite variance) → first parameter-free minimax dynamic regret for standard OCO, Õ((G+σ)D√(T(1+PT/D))).

Strengths

  • Closes a clearly stated open problem with matching upper and lower bounds — the (p−1)/p path-length exponent is proven, not conjectured, optimal.
  • Full parameter-freeness w.r.t. G, σ, p, and PT simultaneously; correctly diagnoses why naive AdaGrad + dynamic-regret telescoping fails without restarting (Remark 1).
  • The pathwise AdaGrad-Hedge meta-regret bound (Lemma 6) is fully distribution-free and plausibly reusable in other adversarial or heavy-tailed online-learning settings.
  • Disciplined sanity-checking: the general bound correctly recovers every known special case (Liu 2026, Zhang et al. 2018, and a new p=2 best rate).
  • A simpler known-G precursor (HT-IA) isolates the meta-algorithm's contribution from the harder parameter-free construction, aiding readability.

Limitations

  • Entirely theoretical — no experiments, simulations, or empirical comparison against Improved Ader, Sword++, or clipped-SGD anywhere in the paper.
  • Requires the domain diameter D to be known and bounded; does not extend to unbounded domains, where the underlying static-regret technique is already known to fail.
  • "Parameter-free" only removes G, σ, p, and PT — T and D must still be known in advance (Table 1's own footnote).
  • All guarantees are in expectation only; no high-probability version, unlike some cited heavy-tailed baselines.
  • The tail index p is assumed fixed and uniform across the whole horizon — no treatment of time-varying or adaptive tail behavior.

Open Questions

  1. Can the construction extend to unbounded domains, given that OGD/AdaGrad can already diverge there under heavy tails without clipping?
  2. Does the Õ(√(log log T)) meta-regret overhead from running O(log T) parallel experts cost anything measurable in practice?
  3. Can the expected-regret guarantee be upgraded to high-probability without reintroducing clipping or sacrificing parameter-freeness?
  4. How would HT-PAder actually perform against Improved Ader, Sword++, and clipped-SGD under realistic heavy-tailed noise?
  5. Can the framework handle a time-varying or a priori unknown tail index pt?
  6. Can the "known horizon T" assumption be removed via a rigorously analyzed doubling trick, rather than a one-line remark?
