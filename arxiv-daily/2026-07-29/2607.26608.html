Training-free parameter injection between differently-sized MLLMs lifts high-level reasoning while perception barely moves at all.

CDPI 是一种无需训练的线性探针：跨尺度 MLLM 融合带来的能力提升几乎只集中于高层推理，感知能力基本不变，且随机扰动对照组完全无法复现该效应。

Understanding Knowledge Transfer Mechanism in Heterogeneous MLLM Fusion: A Simple Linear Approach ↗

Yinghao Hou, Jiahe Fan, Yuanhao Pu · et al. (5 authors) · arXiv:2607.26608 · 2026‑07‑29

Key metric

19.41 → 23.81MATH-Vision · 32B→4B pair · α=0.02

+4.40 points over the unfused target (source 32B model itself scores 33.22). The same pair's five-benchmark perception average barely shifts: 75.12 → 74.96 (−0.16).

Why this matters

Offers model-fusion and distillation researchers a lightweight diagnostic probe and reusable eval protocol — don't trust aggregate fusion-improves-performance claims without a capability-level breakdown.

Critical edge

All heterogeneity tested is scale within one Qwen3-VL family — CDPI is never benchmarked against the actual heterogeneous-fusion methods (LS-Merge, AdaMMS) it cites, and reported gains use post-hoc best-of-sweep α selection.

Methodology

a Heterogeneous alignment operator A_π. Reshapes a larger source model's parameters into a smaller target's shape via shape truncation (keep the leading tensor slice), uniform-interval attention-head mapping (whole query-head blocks, never split), and one of four LLM-layer mapping strategies — Prefix, Depth-proportional, Suffix, Dual-end.
b Directional injection. Θ_F = Θ_T + α·ΔΘ, where ΔΘ = M_E ⊙ [A_π(Θ_S) − Θ_T] is the masked source-minus-target direction over a chosen injection space E (component subset ViT/Proj/LLM × parameter scope linear/full), scaled by ratio α ∈ [0,1].
c Local second-order theory. A Taylor expansion of the target's loss gives Gain_c ≈ αA_c − (α²/2)I_c per capability c — first-order alignment signal A_c vs. second-order curvature/interference I_c from truncation and misalignment. Predicts a narrow positive-transfer window 0 < α < 2A_c/I_c, then collapse.
d Evaluation grid. Four Qwen3-VL pairs (4B→2B, 8B→2B, 32B→4B, 32B→8B) × twelve benchmarks in three capability groups (high-level reasoning, general reasoning, perception), scored with lmms-eval rule-based/exact-match on 8×A100. Ratio sweep α ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.08}, plus 0.16/0.32 as stress tests on 8B→2B.
e Random-perturbation control. Each selected target weight multiplied element-wise by i.i.d. noise U(1−δ, 1+δ), δ=0.08 matched to α=0.04's perturbation scale, averaged over 5 seeds — tests whether gains need the aligned source direction, not just noise of similar magnitude.

Random-perturbation control (5 seeds, δ=0.08) never reproduces the gain: high-level reasoning averages 24.02 ± 1.31 — below the 24.96 target — vs. CDPI's 26.66 at α=0.02. VisuLogic's random control lands at 13.72 ± 6.45, also below target.

Parameter scope: restricting injection to linear-layer weights beats injecting all alignable parameters — high-level reasoning +2.26 vs. +1.30, general reasoning +1.07 vs. ≈0 (averaged across all four pairs).

Best LLM-layer mapping is pair-dependent with no single winner: Suffix/Dual-end for 4B→2B, Suffix for 8B→2B, Prefix for 32B→4B, Dual-end for 32B→8B.

Strengths

  • A falsifiable theoretical prediction (narrow positive-transfer window) that the ratio sweep directly confirms and the α=0.16/0.32 stress tests directly stress.
  • The random multiplicative-perturbation control, matched in magnitude and averaged over 5 seeds, isolates directional alignment as the true source of the gains rather than generic parameter noise.
  • Systematic multi-axis ablation — component × parameter scope × layer mapping × injection ratio — across four pairs and twelve benchmarks, not a single cherry-picked configuration.
  • Full transparency: exact checkpoint identifiers, complete fixed-ratio tables for every benchmark/pair/ratio, and explicit random-control variance formulas in the supplement.

Limitations

  • Only same-family, different-scale Qwen3-VL pairs are tested — generalization to genuinely heterogeneous architectures (different vision backbones, tokenizers, training data) is untested.
  • No empirical comparison against cited heterogeneous-fusion methods (LS-Merge, Transport and Merge, AdaMMS, OptMerge), so CDPI's findings can't be positioned relative to what those methods actually transfer.
  • "Best" results require sweeping and post-hoc selecting α per benchmark — no validation-free rule for choosing α or layer mapping at deployment time.
  • Optimal LLM-layer mapping varies unpredictably across pairs (Prefix, Suffix, Dual-end each win somewhere) with no predictive account of why.
  • Evaluation relies solely on rule-based/exact-match scoring (lmms-eval), which may undercount partial credit on open-ended reasoning outputs.

Questions for further exploration

  • Does the reasoning-selective, LLM-dominant pattern hold for genuinely heterogeneous MLLM pairs (different vision encoders, tokenizers, base LLM families) — or is it an artifact of same-family scale variants?
  • How does CDPI's selectivity signature compare directly against learned-mapping fusion (LS-Merge, AdaMMS) on the same twelve benchmarks — does learned alignment transfer perception too?
  • Can a cheap, validation-free estimator of A_c and I_c predict the optimal α and layer mapping in advance, removing the need for a full sweep?
  • Why does reasoning transfer concentrate in the LLM component even for visually-grounded benchmarks like MathVista — genuine language-side reasoning transfer, or text/OCR shortcut?
  • Does the α=0.16/0.32 collapse (MMMU-Pro → 0.00) reflect pure second-order interference as theory predicts, or a more brittle failure like broken attention-head alignment?
