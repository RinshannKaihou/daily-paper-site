Key metric

1.249 < α < 1.492

The constraint-density window where a zero-error solution is provably as hard to find as the teacher itself — yet finite-temperature rAMP still locates configurations that generalize close to Bayes-optimal.

αIT ≈ 1.249  →  αAMP ≈ 1.492  (N=8000, teacher–student ABP)

Why this matters

Explains why tolerating nonzero training error in large-scale training doesn't hurt — and may help — generalization, by keeping wide solution regions algorithmically reachable.

Critical edge

The paper's central claim — that thermal noise tames the provably-hard regime — rests on a single system size (N=8000), a handful of data points, and no error bars or finite-size-scaling analysis.

01Method

Binary (Ising) perceptrons, w∈{±1}^N, trained on P=αN i.i.d. Gaussian patterns, in two label regimes — storage (random labels) and teacher–student (planted teacher w★, generalization error εg(r)=arccos(r)/π) — and two geometries: ABP (sμ>0) and SBP (|sμ|≤κ).

  • Finite temperature enters through a Gibbs measure Z(β)=ΣwΠμ𝒦(sμ); the standard error-counting loss is a hard step, 𝒦(s)=e-βΘ(-s). Free entropy is computed via the replica method (RS and 1RSB, full derivation in Appendix A).
  • A new boundary-layer criterion ℬ𝒦(b,y), evaluated at the decision boundary, diverges exactly when the equilibrium measure freezes — a single smoothness test for any loss shape (sketch below).
  • To probe near-optimal (not just typical) configurations, an m-clone replicated partition function at fixed mutual overlap q₁ locates the OGP threshold where the clone entropy first touches zero.
  • Accessibility is stress-tested with a fixed-temperature reinforced AMP (rAMP): standard AMP plus a reinforcement term that progressively polarizes marginals toward ±1; a run succeeds once training error drops below a target ε.

The jump-discontinuous loss always freezes; smoothing it (Horner's soft penalty, or a log-potential weight) removes freezing above a critical exponent γ.

02Results

  • Dynamical temperature diverges: for the standard loss, Td ≈ N1/4/√(log N) — so the equilibrium measure stays frozen at every finite T as N→∞, matching Horner's earlier dynamical field theory.
  • Zero-T limits recovered: αOGP≈1.154 (ABP teacher–student) and αOGP≈0.784 (ABP storage), consistent with prior literature — a sanity check that the finite-T machinery reduces correctly.
  • rAMP tracks theory: empirical success probability drops sharply (sigmoidal) with α; larger tolerated error ε shifts the transition to higher α, qualitatively tracking the m=2,3,4 finite-T OGP curves (N=8000, 60-sample average).

03Strengths

  • The boundary-layer criterion ℬ𝒦 unifies previously disconnected freezing results (Horner's soft penalty, a log-potential weight) under one smoothness test.
  • Cross-validates the non-rigorous replica calculation against an independent rigorous bound (Table II) where one exists, and openly flags where the RS ansatz produces artifacts.
  • Pairs analytical (replica) predictions with a concrete, testable algorithm (finite-T rAMP), showing consistent qualitative agreement.
  • Directly connects landscape geometry to a live ML question: does tolerating nonzero training error help or hurt optimization and generalization?

04Limitations

  • All results are for i.i.d. Gaussian synthetic data and single-layer perceptrons; extrapolation to real data or deep networks is speculative and untested.
  • The RS-level treatment of the m-clone free entropy is known-inexact at larger m (explicit non-monotonic αOGP(m) artifact); a fully self-consistent 1RSB clone treatment is left for future work.
  • The headline "thermal noise tames the hard regime" result rests on one system size (N=8000), few data points, and no finite-size-scaling analysis.
  • Whether wide finite-T minima actually cause the information-theoretic hardness of exact inference — or the teacher becomes embedded in them beyond αAMP — is left explicitly open.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Can a fully self-consistent 1RSB (rather than RS) treatment of the m-clone free entropy remove the non-monotonic αOGP(m) artifact?
  • Is there a quantitative causal link between finite-energy wide minima and the IT-hardness of exact teacher recovery in αIT<α<αAMP?
  • Does the boundary-layer criterion ℬ𝒦 generalize to multi-layer networks or structured, non-Gaussian data, where "decision boundary per pattern" is less clean?
  • How does finite-T rAMP's performance in the hard regime scale with N — does the good generalization survive at larger sizes, or is it a finite-size effect?
  • Could the smoothness criterion be used prescriptively, to design practical loss functions that provably avoid landscape freezing while keeping useful inductive bias?
