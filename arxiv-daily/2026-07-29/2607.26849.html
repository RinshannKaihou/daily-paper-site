ToxScreen · Backdoor Trigger Recovery Benchmark

GCG-style gradient search never recovers a planted LLM backdoor trigger — simple token look-up ranks it #1 in half the cases.

一句话总结：论文发布 ToxScreen（约800个后门模型基准），发现基于梯度的提示优化（GCG/AG-GCG）完全无法恢复植入的触发器，而简单的候选词按攻击成功率排序（token look-up）却能稳定把真实触发器排到第一名。

Why This Matters

Real auditors have no training data or clean reference model — this benchmark shows which trigger-hunting methods actually work under those realistic constraints, and which just look plausible.

Critical Edge

No tested method reliably finds every backdoor: the two gradient baselines are repurposed jailbreak-search tools, not the established trigger-inversion techniques (Neural Cleanse, embedding-drift) the paper cites but never actually benchmarks against.

Methodology

  • Threat model. Defender has white-box weights and knows the attack objective, but has no poisoned/clean training data, no trusted reference model, no prior on trigger form — and isn't even certain the model is poisoned.
  • Benchmark construction. Matched clean + backdoored fine-tunes across 4 attack objectives × 5 trigger mechanisms × 3 poison rates × 6 model scales, quality-checked for high triggered ASR, out-of-distribution generalization, and preserved clean-task utility.
  • GCG — 5-token adversarial suffix via gradient-guided discrete search toward a compliance string ("Sure, here is").
  • AG-GCG — GCG plus minimizing the projection onto a precomputed refusal/negativity direction.
  • Token look-up — ~24,000 vocabulary candidates injected at the trained position, ranked by ASR over ~150 held-out prompts.
  • σ1 curvature — operator norm of the mixed second derivative ∂²B/∂θ∂x of a "behavior functional"; suppression ratio ρ(c) flags candidates that suppress refusal-direction sensitivity relative to baseline.

Results & Highlights

Beyond the headline 14/28 recovery rate, per-cell numbers show how thin the margin between "found it" and "buried under noise" really is:

  • A high ASR hit ≠ a found backdoor. On Qwen3-4B refusal, 97% of random candidates already flip the safety label — the real trigger's rank (#345) is buried entirely under this jailbreak floor.
  • Utility mostly holds, but TruthfulQA leaks the tell. Mean accuracy across ARC/HellaSwag/TruthfulQA/Winogrande stays within ~2.5% of a clean fine-tune, yet TruthfulQA alone deviates up to 10%.
  • Pruning is selective. At 50% sparsity, structured pruning nearly halves anti-refusal ASR while holding MMLU — but barely touches sentiment-steering backdoors.
  • Stealth vs. potency trade-off. "Ghost" regularization to hide clean-input drift collapses triggered ASR to ~9% (vs. ~39% without it) — hiding the backdoor also tends to break it.

Strengths

  • Broadest benchmark coverage yet across trigger mechanism, attack behavior, position, and poison rate simultaneously.
  • Backdoor quality verified (high ASR, held-out generalization, low utility cost) before being used as a detection testbed.
  • Reports GCG/AG-GCG total failure transparently instead of only showcasing a favorable method.
  • Novel mechanistic angle (σ1 suppression ratio) connecting trigger recovery to second-order weight structure.
  • Full release of poisoned models and eval code — unusually generous for a security paper.

Limitations

  • No method reliably surfaces every backdoor; "the model is broadly jailbreakable" remains the most consistent anomaly signal.
  • Token look-up only localizes single-token triggers at trained positions; semantic-pool triggers recover only at the concept-class level, with leakage to unplanted phrases.
  • No comparison against established trigger-inversion baselines (Neural Cleanse, embedding-drift) the related work cites but doesn't implement.
  • Entity-steering data generation and judging both come from the Claude model family — a possible self-referential bias.
  • σ1 curvature is validated only as a post-hoc filter on token-lookup candidates, not as a standalone detector.
  • All models ≤70B, open-weight only; no test against situational-awareness triggers, which the authors flag as most realistically dangerous.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can the σ1 suppression-ratio detector become a standalone, lighter-weight monitor that skips the token-lookup sweep entirely?
  • How would these methods fare against triggers described in natural language or tied to situational/deployment cues rather than lexical tokens?
  • Would fusing AG-GCG's representation objective with the σ1 signal let continuous optimization recover multi-token or paraphrase triggers?
  • How does ToxScreen's approach compare quantitatively to Neural Cleanse or BEEAR under the same realistic-affordance constraints?
  • Does the "invisible" entity-steering attack generalize to multiple simultaneously targeted entities?
  • Can the ghost-regularization stealth/potency trade-off be resolved with a smarter schedule, or is the tension fundamental?
