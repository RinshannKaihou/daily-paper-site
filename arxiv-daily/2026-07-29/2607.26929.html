Key Finding

A linear probe on frozen hidden states decodes which causal question evidence addresses, far outperforming answer-logit and text-embedding baselines.

Same Evidence, Different Target: Decoding How Diagnostic Evidence Bears on Causal Questions from Language-Model States ↗
Weiyi Kong, Zhuoran Li • 29 Jul 2026 •
Causal Reasoning LLM Interpretability Linear Probing Paired Benchmark

一句话总结：研究者构造了一个"同一诊断证据、不同因果目标"的49对（98条）配对基准，发现在Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct倒数第二个transformer块的末token隐藏状态上训练线性探针，能以0.659的平衡准确率、21/49的"完整配对恢复率"识别证据究竟支持哪个因果问题——显著超过仅用答案选项logits的线性基线（8/49）和纯文本嵌入基线（TF-IDF 4/49，GTE-large 0/49），但也仅恢复不到一半的配对，说明该信号存在但远未"解决"因果推理。

Key Metric

21/49

complete pairs recovered by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's hidden-state probe (balanced accuracy 0.659) — the best of three checkpoints, and 2.6× the strongest baseline (8/49).

Why This Matters

Gives interpretability researchers a clean, reusable way to test whether causal-diagnostic reasoning is encoded in LLM hidden states, not just final answers.

Critical Edge

Complete-pair recovery tops out at 21/49 (43%) even for the best checkpoint — the majority of paired causal questions are still not correctly resolved.

  • Task construction: each endpoint = target claim + causal condition + study context + one evidence sentence. Paired endpoints share the identical evidence and setting but change the target across 28 distinct types of change — one endpoint the evidence actually addresses, the other always labeled Wrong Target.
  • Models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B (non-thinking), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct — all in 8-bit inference weights, official chat templates, no generation performed.
  • Extraction: the final non-padding prompt token's hidden vector from the penultimate transformer block — this location was fixed before touching the primary benchmark.
  • Readout: a 4-class ridge classifier fit on a disjoint 144-endpoint dev set (18 scenario-grouped folds, no leakage), evaluated once, unchanged, on the 49-pair primary benchmark.
  • Ablations: evidence-omitted / evidence-only inputs, leave-one-diagnostic-family-out, plus baselines on option logits and three text embeddings (TF-IDF, RoBERTa, GTE-large).

Per-label recall, Qwen2.5 (endpoint level): 17/25 Favors, 13/24 Challenges, 37/49 Wrong Target — the distractor class is easiest to recover, which is why the paper treats complete-pair recovery as the stricter test. All three checkpoints beat their scenario-grouped permutation nulls (p = .001, Holm-adjusted p = .003).

Strengths

  • Paired complete-pair-recovery metric directly closes a specific shortcut: models could otherwise win by pattern-matching favorable/adverse wording instead of the target.
  • Strong statistical hygiene — scenario-grouped cross-validation, permutation nulls with Holm correction, bootstrap CIs, and a dedicated regularization-sensitivity check, applied consistently across three checkpoints.
  • Diverse ablation suite (evidence-omitted, evidence-only, leave-one-family-out, answer-score and three text-embedding baselines) jointly rules out several plausible shortcut explanations.
  • High-quality double-blind human annotation with reported inter-annotator agreement (α = 0.953) lends credibility to the gold labels.
  • Honest, well-scoped limitations: explicitly distinguishes "linearly decodable" from "causally used during generation."

Limitations

  • Small benchmark — 49 pairs / 98 endpoints — limited statistical power, reflected in wide bootstrap CIs (e.g. [0.306, 0.571] for Qwen2.5's recovery rate).
  • Only one hidden-state location tested (penultimate block, final token) — no layer or position sweep, so its optimality is unverified.
  • Only three mid-size (7–8B) open-weight models evaluated — no frontier-scale or closed-source checkpoints, so scaling trends are unknown.
  • 8-bit quantized inference used throughout, an untested reproducibility variable relative to full precision.
  • Tests linear decodability of a label from a static representation only — not whether the model's actual generated answer reflects that information.
  • Regularization choice materially shifts results for at least one model (Qwen3's recovered pairs range 21→29 depending on α), suggesting some fragility in the headline numbers.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would sweeping layers and token positions reveal a location with substantially higher pair-recovery than the penultimate-block, final-token choice used here?
  • Does complete-pair recovery improve with model scale (70B+, frontier closed-source), or does the signal plateau around 40–45%?
  • Does linear-readout pair recovery correlate with the model's own generated-answer accuracy on the same paired prompts?
  • How sensitive are results to inference precision, given all reported checkpoints were loaded in 8-bit?
  • Would non-linear probes (small MLPs) recover substantially more pairs than the ridge linear readout?
