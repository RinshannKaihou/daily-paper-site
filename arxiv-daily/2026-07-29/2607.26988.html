Why This Matters

Gives formal-language-theory and interpretability researchers an exact, floating-point-aware account of what attention heads can compute — not idealized bounds.

Critical Edge

△No empirical validation of any kind — these bounds are proved about the architecture class and never checked against real trained models.

Method

attention as a finite-state fold

  • Four working assumptions pin down the model class: F finite-precision floating point, M causal masking, P no positional embeddings (NoPE), E a fixed left-to-right softmax evaluation order.
  • Causal soft attention is rewritten as a left-to-right recurrence over numerator/denominator accumulator pairs — the same computation underlying online-softmax / FlashAttention-style streaming.
  • Because precision is finite, the accumulator state space is finite, so each head generates a finite transformation semigroup; its class (Def, R, LR, or Ap) fixes exactly what "memory" the head can realize.
  • Heads combine via parallel composition (independent heads within a layer) and wreath / cascade composition (cross-layer dependence); a divisibility chain (Prop. 13) reduces whole-architecture expressivity to finite semigroup algebra.
  • Each of the four attention types is proven to land in Def / R / LR / Ap respectively, paired with a construction-witness lemma showing the bound is achievable, not vacuous.

Under the "free-wiring assumption" (arbitrary encoders, QKV maps, inter-layer wiring, accepting regions), all four bounds are exactly achieved — matching construction witnesses realize the prime semigroups U1, U2, U3 for every class. The star-free exclusion of parity and modular counting holds irrespective of depth, width, or number of heads.

Assessment

what holds up, and what's explicitly out of scope

Strengths

  • Four-way hierarchy mapped one-to-one onto concrete attention mechanisms, with intuitive memory metaphors (checklist / bounded window / register, Fig. 2).
  • Every bound is matched top and bottom — proven tight, not just a one-directional inclusion result.
  • Reconciles two separate prior literatures — Krohn–Rhodes results for hard attention and LTL-based results for soft attention — inside a single algebraic account.
  • Treats evaluation order as a first-class assumption, with an explicit note on the R-trivial → L-trivial duality if order is reversed.

Limitations

  • NoPE only — no positional embeddings. The authors call this restriction "substantial," not a mere simplifying convenience.
  • Zero empirical component: no experiments on trained models or synthetic tasks anywhere in the paper.
  • Practical utility for analyzing one specific trained model is explicitly limited — the accumulator state space can be combinatorially large; the tool is structural, not enumerable.
  • RoPE and other now-standard architectural components are entirely out of scope of the theory as presented.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can the divisibility-chain framework extend to positional embeddings (RoPE, learned positions), given that the string-to-position map isn't a semigroup morphism?
  2. Does the R-trivial vs. aperiodic gap between sharp and floating-point soft attention have any measurable signature in real trained models?
  3. Could "expressivity controlled by construction" become a concrete architecture — and at what cost to representational efficiency or training dynamics?
  4. How sensitive are the four classes to floating-point format (bfloat16 vs. fp32) or quantization?
  5. Does relaxing the fixed left-to-right evaluation order to parallel/tree-reduction (as in some efficient attention kernels) change the classification?
