> **一句话总结：** 本文用半群理论把因果掩码、无位置编码（NoPE）、有限精度 Transformer 的每个注意力头形式化为有限状态转移系统，证明滑窗宽度1头对应 definite 语言、sharp soft attention 对应 R-trivial 语言、两者级联对应 locally R-trivial 语言、而标准浮点 soft attention 对应 star-free（无星）语言，且在自由布线假设下四条边界都是紧的（不可再收紧）。
>
> **TL;DR:** This is a pure theory paper (no experiments/benchmarks/datasets) that derives transformer expressivity directly from finite-precision floating-point attention dynamics rather than idealized real-arithmetic semantics. By modeling each causally-masked attention head as a transformation-semigroup-generating transition system and composing heads/layers via parallel and wreath (cascade) products, the authors prove exact language-class characterizations for four attention variants on NoPE transformers: width-one sliding-window attention recognizes only definite languages, sharp (thresholded) soft attention only R-trivial languages, sliding+sharp cascades only locally R-trivial languages, and full floating-point soft attention only star-free languages (excluding parity and modular counting regardless of depth/width/heads) — recovering and sharpening prior LTL-based results of Li and Cotterell (2025) and the hard-attention star-free result of Yang, Chiang, and Angluin (2024) as special cases, while showing the R-trivial vs. aperiodic distinction hinges specifically on whether floating-point rounding can silently change accumulated attention mass.

## 核心贡献

- 提出一个从 Transformer **实际实现的浮点运算**（而非理想化的实数运算）出发推导表达能力的代数框架，将其纳入 Nowak, Cotterell & Boumasmoud (2026) 提出的"代数 RNN"理论体系。
- 将每个注意力头的运行状态形式化为**按查询索引的累加器族**（query-indexed accumulator state），并证明该状态是头对任意未来查询行为的充分统计量（Proposition 9）。
- 用半群的**并行组合**（层内多头独立更新）与**级联/wreath 组合**（层间状态依赖）刻画整个 Transformer 的转移系统，从而把头层面的表达能力结果提升为整架构的上界，且该上界与深度、宽度、头数无关。
- 借助 Stiffler (1973) 对 Krohn–Rhodes 分解的细化（三个素半群 U1、U2、U3），给出比朴素 Krohn–Rhodes（只能得到 aperiodic/star-free）更精细的四级表达能力谱系：definite ⊂ R-trivial ⊂ locally R-trivial ⊂ star-free（对应滑窗、sharp soft、二者级联、全浮点 soft 四种注意力）。
- 证明 sharp（理想化阈值化）soft attention 与真实浮点 soft attention 在表达能力上存在本质差异（R-trivial vs. aperiodic），根源在于浮点加法不满足结合律，使得某次贡献虽被舍入吸收却仍可能改变归一化后的注意力值——这解释了为何精确的运算语义会改变模型能计算什么。
- 在"自由布线假设"（编码器、QKV 映射、层间布线、接受区域可任意选取）下证明全部四条边界都是**紧的**（Corollary 34），即每一类都能被相应注意力类型的 NoPE Transformer 实际实现。

## Key Contributions

- An algebraic framework that derives transformer expressivity from the **implemented finite-precision floating-point arithmetic** rather than idealized real-number semantics, situated within the "algebraic RNN" theory of Nowak, Cotterell & Boumasmoud (2026).
- Formalizes each attention head's running state as a **query-indexed family of accumulators** and proves this state is a sufficient statistic for the head's entire future behavior (Proposition 9, with an extended-transition identity proof).
- Uses **parallel composition** (independent per-head updates within a layer) and **wreath/cascade composition** (cross-layer state dependence) of transformation semigroups to lift head-level expressivity results to whole-architecture bounds that hold irrespective of depth, width, or number of heads.
- Applies Stiffler's (1973) refinement of Krohn–Rhodes decomposition (three prime semigroups U1, U2, U3) to obtain a four-level expressivity hierarchy — definite ⊂ R-trivial ⊂ locally R-trivial ⊂ star-free — corresponding respectively to sliding-window, sharp soft-attention, their cascade, and full floating-point soft attention.
- Shows that sharp (idealized, thresholded) soft attention is strictly less expressive (R-trivial) than genuine floating-point soft attention (aperiodic/star-free), tracing the gap to the non-associativity of floating-point addition: a contribution can be numerically absorbed by the normalizer yet still change the accumulated value, altering what information the model can retain.
- Proves all four expressivity bounds are **tight** under a "free-wiring assumption" (Corollary 34), i.e., matching construction witnesses exist for each attention type/language class pair.

## Methodology

The paper is entirely theoretical/proof-based (no code, no experiments). Its approach:

- **Four working assumptions** define the model class: **F** (finite-precision, constant-precision floating-point arithmetic), **M** (causally masked attention), **P** (no positional embeddings — "NoPE"), **E** (fixed left-to-right evaluation order for the softmax fold).
- **Attention as a fold over finite accumulators.** Standard causal soft attention is rewritten as a left-to-right recurrence over numerator/denominator accumulator pairs `a_{q,i} = (n_{q,i}, d_{q,i})`, updated by a finite-precision transition function `δ_q` (Eq. 5–6) — the same computation underlying online-softmax/FlashAttention-style streaming attention.
- **Head state and transition semigroups.** Because the accumulator domain is finite under finite precision, the query-indexed accumulator family `Z_H = A^Q` is a finite state set; each input induces a coordinatewise transformation of `Z_H`, and the semigroup generated by these transformations (`T_H`) is the head's transition semigroup — a formal object whose membership in classes **Def** (definite), **R** (R-trivial), **LR** (locally R-trivial), or **Ap** (aperiodic) determines what "memory" the head can realize.
- **From heads to acceptors.** Transformer cores (parallel composition of heads within a layer) and full transformer acceptors (cascade/wreath composition across layers, plus a memoryless decoder) are defined algebraically (Def. 11, 43); a **divisibility chain** (Proposition 13) shows the syntactic semigroup of any language recognized by such an acceptor must divide the realized wreath product of head transition semigroups — reducing language-recognition impossibility/possibility results to pure finite semigroup algebra.
- **Four attention types are analyzed head-by-head:** sliding-window-width-one, sharp soft attention (thresholded, "idealized" rounding), full soft attention (finite-precision, standard softmax), each proven (Lemmas 23, 25, 27) to have transition semigroups in Def, R, and Ap respectively, and matching "construction witness" lemmas (24, 26, 28) show each bound is achievable (not vacuous) by exhibiting explicit heads realizing the prime semigroups U1, U2, U3.
- **Upper bounds are lifted to full architectures** via closure properties of the semigroup classes under products/division (Theorems 29–32), and **tightness** is established via a "free-wiring assumption" allowing arbitrary encoders/wiring/accepting regions (Corollary 34).

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — this is a pure theory paper with no experiments, datasets, benchmarks, or trained models. All "results" are proved theorems establishing exact language-class characterizations. The core findings (Table 1 in the paper):

- **Theorem 29:** NoPE transformers using only width-one sliding-window attention heads recognize exactly the **definite** languages (bounded-suffix memory).
- **Theorem 30:** NoPE transformers using only sharp soft-attention heads recognize exactly the **R-trivial** languages.
- **Theorem 31:** Transformers whose lower cores use sliding-window heads and whose higher cores use sharp soft-attention heads recognize exactly the **locally R-trivial** languages.
- **Theorem 32:** NoPE transformers with (real, floating-point) soft attention recognize exactly the **star-free** languages — this excludes parity and all modular counting, irrespective of depth, width, or number of heads, matching/extending the hard-attention star-free result of Yang, Chiang & Angluin (2024) and sharpening the R-trivial bound of Li & Cotterell (2025), whose result the authors show applies specifically to *sharp* (idealized) rather than full floating-point soft attention.
- **Corollary 34 (tightness):** under the free-wiring assumption, all four of the above bounds are exactly achieved (not merely upper bounds) — explicit construction witnesses realize the required prime semigroups U1 (sliding), U2 (sharp), and U1,U2,U3 jointly (full soft attention).

## Authors & Credibility

Franz Nowak, Ryan Cotterell, and Reda Boumasmoud, all at ETH Zürich. Ryan Cotterell is a well-established researcher in computational linguistics and formal-language-theoretic analysis of neural sequence models (co-author of the closely related NoPE/LTL characterization papers by Li & Cotterell, 2025/2026, cited extensively here as the direct predecessor work this paper extends and sharpens). This paper is an explicit follow-up/extension of the authors' own prior ICML 2026 paper, "An Algebraic View of the Expressivity of Recurrent Language Models" (Nowak, Cotterell & Boumasmoud, 2026), reusing its algebraic-RNN machinery (transformation semigroups, wreath products, divisibility) and applying it to transformers for the first time.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The central technical move — deriving expressivity from the head's *implemented* finite-precision numerics (floating-point non-associativity) rather than treating attention accumulation as an idealized associative/order-invariant operation — is genuinely novel and produces a real distinction (R-trivial sharp attention vs. aperiodic full floating-point attention) that prior circuit-complexity and LTL-based analyses (Merrill, Sabharwal & Smith 2022; Li & Cotterell 2025) collapse or don't draw. The use of Stiffler's (1973) finer decomposition (U1, U2, U3) instead of plain Krohn–Rhodes to get a four-level hierarchy rather than a single aperiodic/star-free bucket is a nice technical contribution specific to this paper.

**Rigor:** The paper is proof-heavy and self-consistent; each upper bound (head transition semigroup membership) is paired with a matching lower-bound "construction witness" lemma, so the bounds are shown tight rather than merely sufficient — a standard of rigor above many expressivity papers that only prove one direction.

**Scope limitation (paper-specific, acknowledged by the authors):** the entire analysis excludes positional embeddings (NoPE only); the authors explicitly flag this as "substantial" because a position-aware encoder is not a semigroup morphism from strings to position-indexed strings, so the divisibility-chain machinery (Proposition 13) does not extend to positional architectures, and this is left as future work rather than resolved.

**Practical relevance is explicitly hedged by the authors themselves**: they note the query-indexed accumulator state space `A^Q` "can be extremely large" for a trained model, so the framework is "primarily a structural tool for proving expressivity bounds, rather than a prescription for explicitly enumerating the transition semigroup of a trained model" — i.e., the paper does not claim (and cannot easily be used) to characterize what a specific trained transformer actually computes, only what the architecture class can in principle compute.

**No empirical validation of any kind** — there is no attempt to check whether these theoretical bounds are observed in practice (e.g., via probing trained models for star-free vs. non-star-free behaviors, or verifying the sharp-vs-floating-point gap empirically). This is consistent with the paper's stated purpose as a formal-language-theory contribution, but readers looking for practical guidance on model design will find only a qualitative closing suggestion ("this perspective may enable attention variants whose expressivity is controlled by construction") with no concrete architecture proposal or evaluation.

**Red flag / caveat:** "sharp soft attention" is itself an idealized/thresholded simplification of real softmax attention (defined via a $\tilde{d} > d_q$ threshold rule, Def. 16) rather than a deployed attention mechanism — it functions here mainly as a proof device to isolate the source of the R-trivial vs. aperiodic gap, and readers should not mistake it for a description of any attention variant actually used in practice.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated four-way hierarchy (definite ⊂ R-trivial ⊂ locally R-trivial ⊂ star-free) mapped one-to-one onto concrete attention mechanisms, with an intuitive illustration (Fig. 2) connecting each semigroup class to a memory metaphor (checklist, bounded-suffix window, register).
- Matching upper- and lower-bound (tightness) proofs for every claimed characterization, not just one-directional inclusion results.
- Explicitly reconciles and extends two distinct prior literatures — Krohn–Rhodes/star-free results for hard attention (Yang, Chiang & Angluin 2024) and LTL-based R-trivial results for soft attention (Li & Cotterell 2025/2026) — within a single unified algebraic account, explaining why they differ (sharp vs. full floating-point semantics).
- Careful treatment of evaluation order as a first-class assumption (Assumption E), with an explicit remark on what changes (R-trivial → L-trivial dual) if evaluation order is reversed — a subtlety often left implicit in prior expressivity work.

## Limitations

- Restricted to NoPE (no positional embeddings) transformers; the authors state plainly that this restriction is "substantial" and not merely a simplifying convenience, since unbounded positional information can induce non-regular behavior outside this framework.
- No empirical component whatsoever — purely a proof-theoretic contribution with no experiments on real trained models or synthetic tasks.
- The framework's practical utility for analyzing a specific trained transformer is explicitly limited by the authors, since the relevant accumulator state space can be combinatorially large and the tool is structural rather than constructive/enumerable.
- Positional embeddings, RoPE, and other now-standard architectural components are entirely out of scope; the theory as presented applies to a narrower model class than most transformers deployed in practice.

## Relevance

对做 Transformer 表达能力/形式语言理论、或需要理解注意力机制"能算什么"的可解释性研究有直接价值；提供的浮点语义 vs. 理想化语义的区分，也为后续研究量化误差如何影响模型计算能力（呼应论文中提到的低精度/量化架构相关性）提供了理论工具。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the divisibility-chain/algebraic-RNN framework be extended to transformers with positional embeddings (RoPE, learned absolute/relative positions), given the authors' own note that the string-to-position-indexed-string map is not a semigroup morphism?
- Does the R-trivial vs. aperiodic gap between sharp and full floating-point soft attention have any measurable behavioral signature in real trained models (e.g., can probing or targeted synthetic tasks empirically detect the extra expressivity floating-point non-associativity grants)?
- Could the paper's closing suggestion — designing attention variants with "expressivity controlled by construction" — be turned into a concrete architecture, and would it trade off against representational efficiency or training dynamics?
- How sensitive are the four expressivity classes to the specific choice of floating-point format (e.g., bfloat16 vs. fp32) or to quantization, given the authors' remark that heavily quantized architectures substantially reduce the accumulator/query state space?
- Does relaxing Assumption E (fixed left-to-right evaluation order) to allow parallel or tree-reduction evaluation (as used in some efficient attention implementations) change the R-trivial/aperiodic classification, beyond the noted left-to-right vs. right-to-left duality?
