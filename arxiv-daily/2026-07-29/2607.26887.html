CoRAS predicts each image's own acquisition stopping time from its partial reconstruction path, with finite-sample conformal coverage guarantees.

论文提出 CoRAS：通过"水平外推 + 垂直校准"两步预测每张图像的重建误差达标停止时间，并用满合形预测给出该停止时间的有限样本边际覆盖保证。

Conformalized Rate-Adaptive Sensing ↗ Jiawei Yang, Yao Zhang 2026-07-29
conformal-prediction adaptive-sensing stopping-time MRI-reconstruction image-compression
Key metric −72% cut in mean excess sampling rate on M4Raw brain MRI, at matched ~90% coverage (α=0.1) — the paper's largest reported efficiency gain. 0.29 → 0.08 (M4Raw)  ·  0.235 → 0.12 (Fashion-MNIST, −49%)
Why this matters

Gives a distribution-free, finite-sample rule for deciding when to stop acquiring data online — in accelerated MRI, LLM test-time compute, or early time-series classification.

Critical edge

The flagship MRI result is retrospective, not causal: multi-coil residuals can't be measured online during acquisition, so it's a proxy for deployment, not a live test.

01 Methodology

Horizontal

Extrapolate

At a fixed early decision time t₀, fit a per-image, cross-validated monotone polynomial (order ≤5) to the log band-residual history, then extrapolate past t₀ for a plug-in stopping-time estimate T̂ᴴ.

→
Vertical

Calibrate

Build a 2-D state S = (T̂ᴴ, decision-time entropy). Use leave-one-out, kernel-weighted calibration neighbors to debias T̂ᴴ against complexity-related error.

→
Conformal

Wrap

Rank-based full conformal p-value over the debiased residual scores yields a stopping rule with exact finite-sample marginal coverage P{loss ≤ c} ≥ 1−α.

  • Fashion-MNIST — 32×32, Fourier acquisition, 32-step grid, CNN reconstructor; c=0.003, α=0.1, t₀=6/32; 6000/6000 cal/test images.
  • M4Raw brain MRI — multi-coil, 256×256, 38-step grid (t₀=9, i.e. 50/195 phase-encoding lines), residual-gated U-Net; c=0.0015, α=0.1; 30 studies/540 slices cal, 56 studies/1008 slices test.

Theory. Theorems 1–2 prove exact marginal validity from exchangeability + monotone loss. Theorem 3 adds an approximate conditional-coverage bound under an RKHS-smoothness assumption, splitting the gap into a state-dependent bias term and an O(√(log n/n)) stochastic term.

  • Adaptivity to complexity — stratified by true-image entropy (deciles/quintiles), CoRAS's sampling rate rises monotonically with entropy while fixed-rate baselines stay flat by construction; saved budget on easy images is spent on hard ones.
  • State validity — decision-time entropy (observable) correlates with the true unobservable image entropy at Pearson r=0.935 / Spearman r=0.931 (Fashion-MNIST, n=6000); a similarly strong correlation holds on M4Raw's 1008 test slices.
  • Tail caveat — coverage for all three methods, including CoRAS, dips below the 90% target specifically in the highest-entropy stratum on both datasets.
  • Missing comparison — no head-to-head against an RL-based active-acquisition baseline (e.g. Pineda et al. 2020); all three compared methods are the authors' own conformal variants.

✓ Strengths

  • Genuinely new conformal setting — no test-time covariates, only a partially observed path — cleanly distinct from covariate-conditional / risk-control literature.
  • Two-tier theory: exact marginal validity plus an approximate conditional bound with an interpretable bias/variance split.
  • Consistent gains across two very different domains (grayscale images, multi-coil MRI k-space) at matched coverage.
  • Unusually candid self-critique embedded in the paper itself (idealized-model caveats, retrospective MRI evaluation, single-decision-time limit).
  • Public code release enabling independent verification.

⚠ Limitations

  • Single fixed decision time t₀ — the rule can't revisit its decision; naive multi-checkpoint reuse would break the guarantee.
  • Theorem 3's conditional bound leans on an idealized bounded-RKHS/sub-Gaussian assumption not exactly met by the discrete implementation.
  • Only 2 datasets, both with the authors' own reconstruction models; no standard fastMRI benchmark, no RL-baseline comparison.
  • M4Raw's observability assumption fails for nonlinear multi-coil combination — results are retrospective, not a live deployment test.
  • Coverage still dips below target in the highest-entropy stratum for all methods, including CoRAS.
  • Results reported only as box/bar plots, with no accompanying numeric table.

? Questions for further exploration

  1. Can a sequentially valid CoRAS (revisiting the stopping decision at multiple checkpoints) be built without sacrificing the finite-sample marginal guarantee?
  2. How does CoRAS compare quantitatively against RL/policy-gradient active-acquisition baselines (e.g. Pineda et al. 2020) on the same MRI data?
  3. Would a richer state representation close the coverage gap in the highest-entropy stratum, or is it limited by the short pre-decision history at t₀?
  4. How sensitive are the efficiency gains to the choice of t₀ — is there a principled way to choose it?
  5. Can the next-band residual be made causally observable in multi-coil MRI so the method could deploy online?
  6. Does Theorem 3's conditional-validity bound stay non-vacuous for the actual bandwidths/sample sizes used, or is it mainly of asymptotic interest?
