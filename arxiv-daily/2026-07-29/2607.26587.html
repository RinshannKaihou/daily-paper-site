Why This Matters
AI‑Scientist‑style loops prune research ideas from a single run's score — this shows that call is often just implementation luck, not idea quality.
Critical Edge
Card generation, both implementation setups, and the fidelity judge all run on one model family (DeepSeek) — the headline reversal rates may be that family's quirk, not a universal constant.
Methodology

A nested variance model separates four sources of score noise — task, idea, implementation, and rerun — then isolates them empirically.

Illustrative case (task 146822, Bounded) — three blind‑faithful implementations of the same frozen card span +2.31% to −1.85% relative utility, while a competing card holds steady at +0.46%. Every one of the three one‑draw "wins" is reversed once the other two implementations are averaged in.
  • Holds under two independent outcome‑blind card‑filtering rules: reversal stays 33.3–43.6%.
  • Exploratory materials‑science arm (Matbench, deterministic evaluator): implementation still dominates at 71–82% of variance; ICC drops to .290 / .177.
  • Execution/fidelity audit clean: 147/156 assignments per setup execution‑valid, zero code‑invalid patches.

Strengths

  • Names a reusable, citable distinction: idea‑level quality Q vs. best‑of‑N artifact utility.
  • Outcome‑blind card freezing, session‑independence checks, and constructed judge‑calibration controls.
  • Byte‑identical reruns isolate real implementation variance from rerun noise — not conflated.
  • External, cross‑pipeline reviewer (OpenAI Codex) validates card quality independently.
  • Discloses its own irregularities plainly: one unverifiable draw, a failed Materials preregistration gate.

Limitations

  • Single model family (DeepSeek) for cards, implementations, and judging — no cross‑family replication.
  • Narrow domain: small/medium OpenML tabular tasks, one boosting baseline only.
  • Materials arm is exploratory only — preregistered gate not validly completed, wide confidence intervals.
  • No human implementers or human fidelity raters anywhere in the pipeline.
  • Doesn't test best‑of‑N stability or a live closed‑loop deployment — measures the problem, not a fix.
  • LOO reversal is scored against a finite "other‑two‑mean" proxy, not a validated ground‑truth ranking.
Questions for Further Exploration
  • 01Would the 25.6–43.6% reversal rate hold with mixed model families across cards, implementations, and judging — instead of one DeepSeek pipeline throughout?
  • 02Does idea ICC drop further as workflow complexity grows (deep‑learning architecture/optimization/checkpointing choices), as the authors predict but don't test?
  • 03Can the Idea Reliability Audit run as a live gate inside a closed‑loop system rather than a post‑hoc audit, and does gating on it actually improve research memory?
  • 04Bounded and Agentic show no consistent reliability ordering — what process choices (token budget, repair passes, turn limits) actually drive idea reliability?
  • 05How would LOO reversal behave with 5–10 implementation draws per card, and what's the practical minimum before a decision can be trusted?
