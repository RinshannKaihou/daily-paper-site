> **一句话总结：** 针对开放式生成任务无法用"答案投票"构造伪奖励的问题，SERPO 用同一冻结模型自生成的 Good-Normal-Bad 分档响应档案与查询专属评分标准（rubric）互相迭代，把 Boolean 判定的 token 级 log-prob 转成连续奖励喂给 GRPO，在 Qwen3.5-9B 上把 HealthBench 从 44.68 提到 65.31（+20.63），把 ResearchQA 从 68.62 提到 83.18（+14.56），六基准宏平均最高提升 8.06～8.24 分，全面超过投票类 TTRL 基线（claim-consensus 平均仅 +4.06～+4.30）。

> **TL;DR:** SERPO reframes fixed-set test-time RL (TTRL) for open-ended generation, where answer-voting pseudo-rewards break down because valid responses can't be reduced to one canonical string. It maintains, per prompt, a rolling Good-Normal-Bad (G-N-B) response archive, a co-evolving pool of atomic natural-language rubric criteria, and a shared GRPO-trained actor, converting frozen judge Pass/Fail token log-probabilities into continuous criterion-satisfaction rewards. On Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen3.5-9B across HealthBench/ResearchQA (in-domain) and MedQA/LLMEval-Med/GPQA-Diamond/RaR-Science (OOD), SERPO beats the strongest label-free baseline (claim-consensus TTRL, which improves the six-benchmark average by only +4.06 to +4.30 points) by raising the average by +8.06 (Qwen3-4B) to +8.24 (Qwen3.5-9B) points, improving HealthBench by up to +20.63 and ResearchQA by up to +20.31/+14.56 points over the un-adapted base model, and even outperforms a privileged external-judge-plus-official-rubric reference on all eight OOD settings.

## 核心贡献

- 把固定测试集下的 TTRL（test-time RL）明确形式化为"标签自由、开放式生成"设定，并指出现有答案投票（含 self-consistency、pseudo-label 选择）依赖可归约为规范答案的任务，天然不适用于医疗建议、科研问答等开放式回复。
- 提出 SERPO 三元共演化闭环：(1) G-N-B 响应演化——每个 prompt 维护滚动的 Good/Normal/Bad 有序档案，通过最大化分档间对比分数选出最具区分度的三元组；(2) rubric 演化——用响应档案的对比结果筛选/合并/淘汰原子评分标准，仅保留能区分强弱响应的准则；(3) 策略演化——用校准后的准则满足概率作为奖励，通过 GRPO 更新同一套 actor 参数，新 rollout 再反哺档案与 rubric，形成闭环。
- 提出概率化的评分标准满足接口：从判官模型对 Pass/Fail verdict token 的 log-prob 做 softmax，得到 [0,1] 的连续满足概率而非硬性二值判断，避免离散判定压缩 GRPO 所需的组内优势差异。
- 实验验证：两套模型（Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507、Qwen3.5-9B）、两个域内基准（HealthBench、ResearchQA）、四个 OOD 基准（MedQA、LLMEval-Med、GPQA-Diamond、RaR-Science）上，SERPO 相对基座模型 HealthBench 最高提升 20.63 分、ResearchQA 最高提升 20.31 分，六基准宏平均最高提升 8.06 分，并支持 OOD 迁移与跨基准持续演化（HealthBench→ResearchQA 序贯训练不遗忘反而继续提升）。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes fixed-set TTRL for open-ended generation as a label-free adaptation problem and shows why answer-voting/self-consistency pseudo-rewards (majority vote, claim consensus) are structurally mismatched: open-ended responses (e.g., medical advice) can share a coarse recommendation while differing in factuality, completeness, or safety, so no single canonical answer exists to vote toward.
- Introduces SERPO, a closed-loop framework co-evolving three pieces of per-prompt test-time state: rolling Good-Normal-Bad (G-N-B) response archives, a query-specific rubric pool of atomic natural-language criteria, and a single shared actor updated by GRPO — with only the actor trained; the rubric generator and judge remain frozen initial-weight copies of the same deployed model.
- Proposes a probabilistic reward interface that converts post-reasoning Boolean Pass/Fail verdict-token log-probabilities into a continuous criterion-satisfaction probability q_J(o,c) ∈ [0,1] (Eq. 3), preserving reward resolution that hard binary judgments would collapse into ties.
- Designs a criterion-utility rule (response-score variance × G-N-B order agreement) with a "recoverable elimination" mechanism (bottom-quartile criteria enter an at-risk region, are removed after 3 consecutive at-risk refresh rounds) that lets the rubric pool discard low-discriminating or redundant criteria while keeping stable ones.
- Empirically establishes, via ablations, that both rubric evolution and G-N-B archive/policy evolution are complementary and individually necessary (freezing the actor causes the largest single-component loss, ~36% relative), and shows continued gains over a 45-epoch extended run and a 60-epoch HealthBench→ResearchQA cross-benchmark sequence without catastrophic forgetting of the first benchmark.

## Methodology

**Setting.** Fixed-set transductive TTRL: the policy only sees unlabeled adaptation prompts D_adapt and its own sampled rollouts — no references, no external reward models, no stronger judges are queried during adaptation (a frozen copy of the deployed model plays both the rubric-generator and judge roles). At each global step t, the policy samples a group of G=8 responses per prompt and must construct a pseudo-reward r̂_t(x,o) from this permitted information alone (Eq. 1).

**Why voting fails.** Standard TTRL maps each response to an extracted answer via Ext(·) and rewards agreement with a majority pseudo-label Sel(·) (Eq. 2) — well-defined for categorical/normalized answers but ill-defined when open-ended responses can share a coarse recommendation while differing in factuality, completeness, or safety.

**G-N-B response evolution.** For a new prompt, a frozen rubric generator first proposes a nonempty initial criterion set. At each later encounter, the judge scores the G sampled rollouts against the currently active rubric to produce a provisional archive-ordering score (Eq. 5, a weight-normalized average over active criteria). SERPO then searches all ordered index triples (i,j,k) for the one maximizing a separation score D_t(i,j,k) = (r_i−r_j)(r_j−r_k)(r_i−r_k) (Eq. 6), and appends the resulting Good/Normal/Bad responses to bounded FIFO archives (archive width W=3 per Appendix Table 5) — keeping query-local evidence both ordered and separated rather than storing only the single best/worst sample.

**Rubric evolution.** On a fixed refresh interval (F=3 visits), the frozen rubric generator receives the prompt, current rubric, and updated G-N-B archives, and proposes new atomic criteria explaining stronger-vs-weaker contrasts; candidates are merged with the existing pool by canonicalizing duplicate requirements. Each criterion gets a discrimination utility (Eq. 7) combining response-score variance across archive entries and G-N-B "order agreement" (fraction of concordant vs. discordant cross-bucket score pairs under a margin τ). When the pool exceeds 4 criteria, the bottom ζ=0.25 fraction enters a recoverable elimination region and is deleted only after 3 consecutive at-risk refresh rounds (deletion patience), giving criteria a chance to regain utility as responses evolve. Active-pool cap is 15 criteria (Appendix Table 5).

**Policy evolution (reward + GRPO).** After the rubric update, the final GRPO reward (Eq. 8) uses the surviving criteria's utility-derived weights and G-N-B-calibrated oriented scores: the Bad-archive mean is calibrated toward 0 and the Good-archive mean toward 1 (falling back to the static oriented score if this range is too narrow, Δμ < δ=0.05). GRPO (Shao et al. 2024 / Schulman et al. 2017-style, PPO clipped objective + KL penalty) then normalizes rewards within each rollout group into advantages and updates only the actor; the rubric generator and judge stay frozen throughout.

**Backbones and compute.** Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen3.5-9B, both in non-thinking mode, trained with GRPO under FSDP2 with offload on 8×H100 (80GB) GPUs; a 30-epoch run takes ~10 GPU-hours (4B) / ~18 GPU-hours (9B). Rollout group G=8, train batch/PPO mini-batch 48/24 prompts, actor LR 1e-6, KL coefficient 0.001.

## Results & Highlights

- **HealthBench (in-domain), Qwen3.5-9B:** SERPO reaches **65.31** vs. base **44.68** (**+20.63** points), clearly ahead of the strongest label-free voting baseline, claim-consensus TTRL, at **60.88** (+16.20) and standard response-vote TTRL at 53.75 (+9.07); the privileged external-judge + official-rubric reference (not label-free) reaches 68.41 (+23.73), so SERPO closes most of that gap without any external supervision.
- **ResearchQA (in-domain), Qwen3.5-9B:** SERPO reaches **83.18** vs. base **68.62** (**+14.56**), beating claim-consensus TTRL's 79.45 (+10.83) by 3.7 points and response-vote TTRL's 67.43 (−1.19, i.e. voting actually *hurts* ResearchQA). On Qwen3-4B, SERPO's ResearchQA gain is even larger: 77.60 vs. base 57.29 (**+20.31**), vs. claim-consensus's 69.39 (+12.10).
- **Six-benchmark macro-average:** SERPO raises the average from 53.92→**61.98** (**+8.06**) on Qwen3-4B and from 66.75→**74.36** (**+7.61**) on Qwen3.5-9B — both exceeding claim-consensus TTRL's average gains of +4.06 and +4.30 respectively, and both close to (Qwen3-4B: 61.98 vs. 62.89) or matching in spirit the privileged external-judge+official-rubric reference's average gains (+8.96 / +8.24), despite SERPO using no external judge or official rubric.
- **OOD transfer:** all eight SERPO OOD cells (HealthBench-trained → MedQA/LLMEval-Med; ResearchQA-trained → GPQA-Diamond/RaR-Science) improve over base, by up to 11.4% relative; the OOD-evolved SERPO policy outperforms the privileged external-judge+official-rubric reference in all eight OOD settings even though that reference remains strongest in-domain — e.g. on GPQA-Diamond, SERPO (Qwen3-4B) reaches 59.09 vs. the privileged reference's 57.32.
- **Ablations (Table 2, Qwen3-4B/HealthBench):** removing rubric evolution drops HealthBench from 49.83 to 43.54 (≈13% relative loss) while inflating response length to 2.29× base; freezing the actor (w/o actor evolution) is the single largest loss, dropping HealthBench to 32.06 (≈36% relative loss, essentially erasing all gains); replacing the probabilistic judge with hard Pass/Fail verdicts drops HealthBench by ~6% (46.73 vs. 49.83) because ties collapse GRPO's group-relative advantages; the full method achieves both the best average (55.69 vs. next-best 54.14) and shorter responses (1.38× base) than any actor-updating ablation.
- **Sequential cross-benchmark evolution (Table 3, Qwen3-4B):** after 30 epochs on HealthBench then 30 more on ResearchQA, SERPO reaches epoch-60 checkpoint scores of HealthBench 51.35 / ResearchQA 75.80 — HealthBench keeps rising (not forgotten) while ResearchQA gains an additional +11.8 points during the second stage, whereas the claim-consensus baseline's HealthBench score at matched epoch 60 (41.64) is *below* its own epoch-30 score (42.18), indicating instability/regression under continued voting-based adaptation.

## Authors & Credibility

Eight authors from the School of Artificial Intelligence and Automation at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, the University of Science and Technology of China, and Alibaba Group; the paper notes work was done during a research internship at Alibaba, and two authors are marked as equal first contributors. The paper positions itself carefully against a dense recent related-work landscape (RLCER, EvoRubric, EvoLM, DynamicRubric, RGSD/TTS, ARBOR, TTCS, SCOPE — mostly 2026 arXiv preprints), and explicitly instantiates one of them (RGSD) as a controlled ablation baseline rather than only citing it, which is a credible sign of genuine engagement with contemporaneous work rather than a superficial literature survey. No large public benchmark leaderboard entries or widely-cited prior work from this author group is evident from the reference list, so credibility rests primarily on the empirical rigor of this paper itself (multi-seed averaging, ablations, long-horizon extensions) rather than track record.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — replacing answer-voting pseudo-labels with a co-evolving loop of ordered response archives (G-N-B) and query-local atomic rubrics, scored via continuous verdict-token probabilities rather than hard judgments — is a genuinely useful adaptation of rubric-reward ideas (EvoRubric, DynamicRubric, RLCER) to the *label-free, fixed-set TTRL* regime specifically, where prior rubric-evolution methods typically assume broader post-training budgets, external corpora, or task generation. The G-N-B triple-selection rule (maximizing a three-way contrastive separation score rather than just best-vs-worst) is a small but sensible design choice that the ablations (Table 2) show actually matters (removing it costs ~13% on HealthBench).

**Rigor:** The paper reports 3-seed averages (seeds 40/41/42) for the main table and provides a genuinely useful set of controlled ablations isolating each of the three evolving components plus the two evaluator roles (judge, rubric generator) — notably showing that *training* either evaluator role (rather than keeping it frozen) actually *hurts* performance (43.22/43.82 vs. 49.83 full), which supports the paper's design choice to freeze both and is a specific, falsifiable claim rather than a vague appeal to "stability."

**Reproducibility:** Hyperparameters are fully disclosed in Appendix Tables 4-5 (rollout temperature, archive width W=3, refresh interval F=3, elimination fraction ζ=0.25, deletion patience=3, active-pool cap=15, replay bank size=12, etc.), and the paper commits to releasing training/evaluation/analysis code under a research-use license upon publication — though as of this read no repository link is given, so reproducibility is currently a stated intention rather than a verified artifact.

**Significance / weak spots:** The comparison baselines are reasonable but not maximally strong: TTRL response-vote and claim-consensus both use a *frozen self-judge* copy of the same base model rather than the strongest available open judges, and RGSD (TTS) is explicitly instantiated by the authors themselves as a static-rubric ablation rather than run with its originally reported configuration — meaning the paper's strongest non-privileged competitor (claim-consensus, its own contribution) is somewhat of an in-house baseline the authors constructed for this comparison rather than a widely validated external method, which slightly weakens claims of the size of SERPO's margin over "the state of the art." The evaluation itself also depends on GPT-5.1 as an external grader with official rubrics for *scoring* HealthBench/ResearchQA/LLMEval-Med/RaR-Science (not used during adaptation, but used for the reported numbers throughout Table 1), so the absolute numbers are only as trustworthy as that external judge's rubric fidelity — a limitation the paper doesn't discuss. The two model configurations (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-9B) are both from the same family, and the long-horizon experiments (Fig. 3/4) are run only on Qwen3-4B, so scale/family generalization of the reported dynamics (continued rise past 30 epochs, no forgetting across benchmarks) is not yet demonstrated beyond this single model line.

**No major red flags:** the paper explicitly reports cases where its own baselines beat SERPO's mechanism in isolation (e.g., static-rubric RGSD *lowers* the average below base, showing rubric guidance alone without policy updates can hurt — a result the authors report against their own related idea rather than hiding it) and is upfront in Limitations that rubric generation/judging inherit the deployed model's biases and add test-time compute overhead.

## Strengths

- Clean three-way decomposition (response archive evolution, rubric evolution, policy evolution) with ablations that isolate and quantify each component's individual contribution, rather than reporting only the full-system number.
- The probabilistic verdict-token reward interface (Eq. 3) is a simple, well-motivated fix for a concrete failure mode (hard verdicts collapsing GRPO advantages into ties) and is directly validated by an ablation showing a 6% HealthBench drop when reverted to hard verdicts.
- Demonstrates genuine label-free OOD transfer (HealthBench-trained policy improves on MedQA/LLMEval-Med; ResearchQA-trained policy improves on GPQA-Diamond/RaR-Science) and sequential cross-benchmark evolution without catastrophic forgetting, which is a stronger and rarer result than typical single-benchmark TTRL papers report.
- Full hyperparameter and compute disclosure (Appendix Tables 4-5) with a stated commitment to code/data release.

## Limitations

- All non-privileged baselines (response-vote TTRL, claim-consensus TTRL, RGSD/TTS) use frozen copies of the same base model as judge, so the comparison is essentially "self-evolving reward construction" vs. other self-evolving reward constructions built by the same authors for this paper — an external, independently-published open-ended TTRL baseline is not tested.
- Evaluation scores throughout Table 1 rely on GPT-5.1 as an external grader with official per-benchmark rubrics, introducing a dependency on that judge's rubric fidelity that is not itself studied or ablated.
- Experiments cover only two model configurations from one family (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3.5-9B); the long-horizon (45-epoch) and cross-benchmark (60-epoch) extended experiments are run on Qwen3-4B only.
- The authors' own stated limitations note that rubric generation and judging may inherit biases from the deployed model itself (no independent verifier) and that the closed loop increases test-time compute versus a non-adapting baseline.
- No repository link was found in the read portion of the paper; code/data release is stated as a future commitment tied to publication, not yet verifiable.

## Relevance

该工作针对"开放式生成任务下测试时强化学习缺乏可靠奖励信号"这一具体痛点，用同模型自蒸馏的 rubric+响应档案共演化替代答案投票，思路和 GRPO 训练管线、评分标准演化（rubric evolution）方向高度相关，适合关注 test-time RL、LLM-as-judge 奖励构造、以及无标注自适应场景的研究参考；其消融实验（尤其是"训练判官反而变差""硬判决降低奖励分辨率"两条）对设计类似奖励管线时的具体取舍也有直接借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would SERPO's gains hold with a genuinely external, more capable judge/rubric-generator swapped in for the frozen self-judge, or does the method specifically depend on same-model rubric generation matching what the actor can plausibly satisfy?
- How sensitive are the reported margins to the choice of GPT-5.1 as the external evaluation grader — would a different official-rubric grader (e.g., a different frontier model) preserve the same ranking between SERPO and claim-consensus TTRL?
- Does the "freezing the actor causes ~36% relative loss" ablation finding generalize to larger or smaller model scales, or is response-length inflation (2.29× base under frozen actor) a Qwen3-4B-specific failure mode?
- Can the recoverable-elimination rubric mechanism (3-strike deletion patience) be tuned to trade off rubric-pool stability against adaptation speed, and how does SERPO behave if the criterion pool cap (15) is substantially raised or lowered?
- Beyond the tested HealthBench→ResearchQA sequence, does SERPO sustain gains across longer cyclic benchmark streams (the paper's own stated future-work direction), and what role would the proposed replay/rollback mechanisms play in preventing eventual saturation or judge drift?
