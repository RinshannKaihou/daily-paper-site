> **一句话总结：** EvoPINN 用 LLM 智能体在"神经表示"和"训练程序"两个可独立演化的模块上做结构验证 + 预算匹配评测的迭代式代码搜索，在 Burgers1D 上把相对 L2 误差从种子 PINN 的 3.05e-4 降到 1.65e-4（优于最强专家基线 2.54e-4），并自主发现了新架构 SLRC-PINN，在参数匹配对比下相对同参数全局 MLP 降低 55.1% 的平均误差。

> **TL;DR:** EvoPINN is an LLM-driven evolutionary framework that decouples a PINN's neural representation from its training program, mutates one module at a time under UCB scheduling, and only accepts candidates that pass AST-based structural-novelty checks and reduce relative L2 error under a shared training budget. Across four PDE benchmarks (Poisson2D, Burgers1D, Wave1D, Heat2D) it beats both the seed PINN and a pool of 16 expert-designed variants on 3/4 tasks, and outperforms three automated-search baselines (LLM-Best-of-56, DPSTE, PINNsAgent) everywhere, e.g. on Burgers1D achieving 1.6536e-4 ± 5.18e-5 vs. PINNsAgent's 6.201e-4 ± 8.19e-5 and the expert baseline's 2.5420e-4. It also autonomously discovered SLRC-PINN, a global-anchor + gated local-correction architecture that beats a parameter-matched global MLP (2.0445e-4 vs. 4.5506e-4 mean rel-L2, a 55.1% reduction) and domain-decomposition baselines (APINN, FBPINN, HyResPINNs) under identical capacity and budget.

## 核心贡献

- 将 PINN 设计问题形式化为在算法空间 A = (M, T)（神经表示 M × 训练程序 T）中的可执行算法发现问题，用模块化的"一次只改一个模块"演化策略解决联合修改带来的信度分配难题。
- 提出诊断引导的程序生成：把训练轨迹压缩为结构化诊断摘要（收敛进度、后期稳定性、物理误差-损失关系），结合演化记忆和批内多样化搜索焦点，为 LLM 提供比标量 L2 误差更丰富的反馈信号。
- 设计 UCB 式模块调度器，根据近期奖励和探索次数动态决定每一代该修改表示模块还是训练程序模块，而非固定交替。
- 建立严格的候选验证流水线：基于标准/归一化 AST 的结构新颖性检测（排除纯改名、加注释、常数微调）、可执行性冒烟测试与有限次自动修复、共享预算下的公平评测，确保发现的机制真实有效而非取巧。
- 在 Burgers1D 上自主发现了新颖的 SLRC-PINN（Self-Localizing Residual-Correction PINN）架构——一个持续全局背景解码器叠加门控、零初始化的局部修正分支，该优势在参数匹配的对照实验中依然成立；同时在 Poisson2D 上发现了协调优化器分期与自适应重采样的训练策略 TCROP。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes PINN development as executable-algorithm discovery over a joint search space A = (M, T) of neural representation and training program, using a module-wise ("one module per generation") evolution strategy to address the credit-assignment problem of jointly mutating both.
- Introduces diagnosis-guided program generation: training traces are compressed into a structured diagnostic summary (convergence progress, late-stage stability, physics-loss-to-error relation) that, together with an evolutionary memory bank and diversified per-candidate search foci, gives the LLM richer signal than a single scalar L2 error.
- Uses a UCB-style scheduler that dynamically decides each generation whether to mutate the representation or the training-program module, based on recent reward and visitation counts, rather than fixed alternation.
- Builds a rigorous verification pipeline: AST-based structural novelty checks (raw, comment-stripped, and identifier/constant-normalized ASTs) reject cosmetic or hyperparameter-only edits, executable smoke tests with bounded self-repair catch invalid/unstable code, and budget-matched evaluation (same steps, collocation counts, seed) ensures fair comparison.
- Autonomously discovers SLRC-PINN, a novel architecture combining a persistent global decoder with a gated, zero-initialized local corrective branch, whose gains survive parameter-matched ablation against a global MLP and domain-decomposition baselines (APINN, FBPINN, HyResPINNs); also discovers TCROP, a telemetry-conditioned optimizer/resampling policy for Poisson2D.

## Methodology

EvoPINN represents a PINN algorithm as a tuple A = (M, T), where M is the neural representation and T is the training program that optimizes the composite physics-informed loss (interior/boundary/initial residuals). Starting from a seed algorithm A0 (5-hidden-layer, width-100 tanh MLP trained with Adam then L-BFGS), the framework runs G=7 generations, each generating K=8 candidate proposals via an LLM (gpt-5.4-mini, temperature 0.7).

At each generation, a UCB-style scheduler (Eq. 6: c_g = argmax[mean recent reward + β·sqrt(log(N_g+1)/N_g,c)], β=0.35) picks whether to mutate the representation module M or the training module T, holding the other fixed. The selected module's proposal generation is conditioned on three context sources: (1) training diagnostics D_g — a compact summary of loss contraction, late-stage oscillation, and physics-error relations derived from checkpointed training traces, rather than raw scalar L2; (2) evolutionary memory — an archive of prior successful motifs, failing proposal families, and recent modifications to avoid redundant exploration; (3) diversified search focuses injected per-candidate to prevent mode collapse in parallel LLM generation (e.g., feature construction vs. architectural organization for M; optimizer staging vs. collocation sampling for T).

Every candidate undergoes structural verification via AST comparison against its parent at three levels (raw source, comment-stripped AST, and identifier/literal-normalized AST) — a candidate is rejected if it matches the parent at any level, filtering pure hyperparameter tuning or cosmetic edits. Surviving candidates are smoke-tested on a small PINN instance (forward pass, differentiation, optimizer/sampling interface checks) with up to 3 bounded self-repair attempts using execution feedback before being disqualified. Verified candidates are trained under a shared, generation-specific budget B_g (fixed optimization steps, initial collocation counts, optimizer family) and scored by relative L2 error against a search-time reference set X_search (Eq. 4); only candidates that reduce this score replace the parent (strict-improvement acceptance, Algorithm 1). The frozen best-scoring algorithm from the source search is then retrained from scratch across five independent evaluation seeds on a disjoint reporting set, ensuring search-time selection and final reporting never share reference points.

Four PDE benchmarks span distinct regimes: Poisson2D (elliptic, multi-hole domain, COMSOL FEM reference), Burgers1D (nonlinear transport-diffusion, ν=0.01/π, numerical reference), Wave1D (oscillatory/hyperbolic, u_tt=4u_xx with a=4 frequency parameter, analytic reference), and Heat2D (anisotropic dissipative diffusion, analytic reference). Baselines include the seed PINN, a pool of 16 expert-designed PINN variants (Fourier/SIREN features, residual-adaptive sampling, curriculum learning, weighted losses, NTK balancing) screened and retrained under the same protocol, and three automated-search paradigms: LLM-Best-of-56 (no execution feedback/memory), DPSTE (evolutionary architecture search over fixed hyperparameter spaces), and PINNsAgent (multi-agent LLM workflow with memory-based reasoning).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main benchmark table (Table 1, relative L2 error, mean ± std over 5 seeds):** Frozen EvoPINN beats the seed PINN on all four PDEs and beats the best expert baseline on 3/4. Poisson2D: EvoPINN 4.1413e-3 ± 9.77e-4 vs. expert (boundary-aware NTK + L-BFGS) 4.7025e-3 ± 8.23e-4 vs. seed 6.9938e-1 ± 1.69e-2. Burgers1D: EvoPINN 1.6536e-4 ± 5.18e-5 vs. expert (multiscale Fourier) 2.5420e-4 ± 1.24e-4 vs. seed 3.0500e-4 ± 1.16e-4. Wave1D: EvoPINN 1.3894e-2 ± 3.04e-3 vs. expert (Fourier + capped NTK) 1.5989e-2 ± 2.44e-3. Heat2D is the one exception: expert (anisotropic multiscale Fourier) 1.0963e-3 ± 1.86e-4 beats EvoPINN's 1.1142e-3 ± 3.94e-4, though EvoPINN remains close and far ahead of the seed (4.4574e-3).
- **Search-strategy comparison (Table 2) under matched 56-candidate budgets:** On Burgers1D, EvoPINN achieves 1.6536e-4 ± 5.18e-5 vs. PINNsAgent 6.201e-4 ± 8.19e-5, DPSTE 1.6684e-2 ± 3.31e-3, and LLM-Best-of-56 2.0571e-4 ± 1.10e-5. On Wave1D, EvoPINN reaches 1.3894e-2 ± 3.04e-3 vs. PINNsAgent 2.8160e-2 ± 3.96e-3, DPSTE 1.3201e-1 ± 4.86e-2, and LLM-Best-of-56 2.3181e-1 ± 1.85e-2 — roughly an order-of-magnitude gap versus the weakest search baselines.
- **SLRC-PINN controlled comparison (Table 3, Burgers1D, matched capacity/budget, 5 seeds):** SLRC-PINN mean rel-L2 = 2.0445e-4 (std 6.8721e-5, median 1.9064e-4, 213,286 params) vs. parameter-matched global MLP (width 230) 4.5506e-4 (std 1.4387e-4, 213,441 params) — a 55.1% mean-error reduction attributed to the discovered global-local topology rather than raw capacity. Domain-decomposition baselines under the same protocol: APINN 2.7056e-4, FBPINN 3.3811e-4, HyResPINNs 2.0787e-3, all worse than SLRC-PINN.
- **Ablations (Table 4):** Removing training diagnostics ("w/o diagnosis") degrades Wave1D rel-L2 from 2.3640e-2 (full) to 2.7097e-1 — over an order of magnitude worse — confirming diagnostic feedback is essential, not decorative. Removing module-wise decoupling ("w/o modular", joint mutation) causes execution instability, dropping successful evaluations from 55/56 to 12/56 on Wave1D. Restricting search to a single module (repr-only or train-only) underperforms the full joint search on both benchmarks.
- **Neighboring-parameter transfer (Table 5):** Frozen EvoPINN algorithms (fixed architecture, retrained from scratch) generalize to shifted PDE parameters not seen during search — e.g., on Wave1D at a=4.5, EvoPINN achieves 6.1859e-2 vs. expert baseline 1.8483e-2 (worse there) but at a=3.5, EvoPINN 1.3542e-2 vs. expert 1.0530e-2; on Heat2D at k_x=25π, EvoPINN 1.8144e-3 vs. expert 4.3262e-1 — over two orders of magnitude better, showing the expert's rigid Fourier basis overfits to the source frequency while EvoPINN's mechanism is frequency-resilient.
- **TCROP optimizer-policy validation (Table 7, Poisson2D, 5 seeds):** TCROP achieves geometric-mean rel-L2 5.0588e-3 (CV 19.0%) vs. a fixed 10k/10k Adam-LBFGS handoff baseline 6.9808e-3 (CV 29.6%) and pure 20k-step Adam 2.1104e-1 — a 27.5% reduction over the conventional handoff while also lowering seed variance.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — decoupling representation and training-program search with module-wise, execution-grounded LLM evolution — is a meaningful extension beyond prior LLM-for-PINN work (PINNsAgent, Lang-PINN), which the authors correctly characterize as bounded to fixed templates/configuration selection. The discovered SLRC-PINN architecture (persistent global anchor + zero-initialized gated local correction) is a genuinely interesting design that differs from prior hard-partitioning domain-decomposition methods (FBPINN, APINN), and the authors go further than most "LLM discovers X" papers by running a dedicated parameter-matched controlled ablation (Table 3) to isolate the architectural contribution from raw capacity — this is a methodologically strong move that many similar papers skip.

**Rigor:** The paper's evaluation protocol is unusually careful for this genre: strict separation of search-time reference points from final reporting points (Section 5.1, Appendix D.1), AST-based structural novelty filtering to prevent counting hyperparameter tweaks as "discoveries," budget-matched candidate evaluation, and 5-seed retraining for final numbers. The ablation study (Table 4) is genuine and shows real vulnerabilities being tested (e.g., "w/o modular" causing catastrophic instability, only 12/56 executable candidates on Wave1D vs. 55/56 for the full method).

**Red flags / weaknesses to note:** (1) On Heat2D — the one benchmark with anisotropic multiscale structure — the expert-designed Fourier baseline actually beats EvoPINN (1.0963e-3 vs. 1.1142e-3), which the paper reports honestly rather than hiding, but it does mean the headline claim of beating experts "across diverse PDE regimes" is not universally true. (2) The reported "expert baseline" pool, while described as 16 variants screened under a unified pipeline, is selected and retrained by the same authors — there's no external/independent verification that these represent truly state-of-the-art expert PINN designs rather than a locally-tuned strawman set. (3) The LLM used (gpt-5.4-mini) is a moderately capable but not frontier model; results likely depend substantially on the underlying LLM's code-synthesis quality, a limitation the authors themselves flag in the conclusion. (4) Compute cost for search itself (14.7–51.78 GPU-hours per PDE, per Table 8) is non-trivial and the paper explicitly notes evaluating each candidate via full-budget PINN training is expensive, with proxy/surrogate objectives left as future work — meaning the method as presented does not scale cheaply to new PDE families. (5) The neighboring-parameter transfer experiment (Table 5) reuses the same discovered architecture/expert identity fixed at the source instance and only reinitializes+retrains at shifted parameters — this is a reasonable generalization test but is not true zero-shot inference; "zero-shot" here means no additional search, not no additional training.

## Strengths

- Rigorous separation of search-time candidate selection from final reporting evaluation (disjoint reference/reporting point sets), which guards against the common pitfall of overfitting to the metric used during search.
- Structural AST-based novelty verification is a clever, concrete mechanism to prevent the LLM from "cheating" via cosmetic renaming or pure hyperparameter changes while calling them new mechanisms.
- Dedicated parameter-matched controlled comparison (Table 3) isolating whether the discovered SLRC-PINN architecture's gains come from genuine topology vs. raw capacity — a level of scientific care above typical "LLM discovers architecture" papers.
- Comprehensive ablations (diagnosis, modularity, single-module search) directly test the paper's own design claims and reveal a genuine failure mode (severe instability under joint multi-module mutation).
- Demonstrates transferability beyond the exact search instance (neighboring PDE parameters), which strengthens the claim that discovered mechanisms are not overfit to one specific equation instance.

## Limitations

- Underperforms the strongest expert baseline on Heat2D, the anisotropic diffusion benchmark, indicating the method's mechanism-discovery advantage is not uniform across all PDE regimes.
- Search cost is substantial (up to ~52 GPU-hours per PDE family) since each candidate requires a full-budget PINN training run; the authors acknowledge this as a scalability bottleneck and defer proxy-objective solutions to future work.
- Discovery quality is explicitly acknowledged by the authors to depend on the underlying LLM's code-synthesis and scientific-reasoning capacity (gpt-5.4-mini here), raising questions about how results would shift with a different or weaker/stronger LLM.
- Expert baseline pool, though screened under a shared pipeline, is authored/selected internally rather than sourced from independently published state-of-the-art configurations for each specific benchmark instance.
- Only four PDE benchmarks are evaluated, all fairly standard/canonical (Poisson, Burgers, Wave, Heat); no coupled multi-physics, higher-dimensional, or inverse-problem settings are tested, leaving open how well the search scales to more complex PDE systems.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does EvoPINN's performance and discovery quality change with a stronger frontier LLM (e.g., a full-scale reasoning model) versus the gpt-5.4-mini used here — is the current expert-competitive gap on Heat2D an LLM capability ceiling or a search-budget limitation?
- Could a cheaper proxy objective (e.g., short-horizon training curves or surrogate error estimators) replace full-budget PINN training during candidate evaluation to reduce the 14.7–51.78 GPU-hour search cost per PDE, as the authors suggest for future work?
- Does the module-wise one-at-a-time evolution strategy leave synergistic joint representation-training co-designs undiscovered that a (more stable) joint-mutation strategy might find, given that "w/o modular" only failed due to execution instability rather than a fundamental ceiling on joint search quality?
- How would SLRC-PINN's global-anchor-plus-gated-local-correction topology generalize to genuinely higher-dimensional or multi-physics PDE systems beyond the 1D/2D canonical benchmarks tested here?
- What is the sensitivity of results to the choice of the 16-member expert baseline pool — would a different, externally curated set of state-of-the-art PINN variants change the Heat2D outcome or narrow/widen the gap on other benchmarks?
