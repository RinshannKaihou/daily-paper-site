> **一句话总结：** 论文用一次跨17个ID数据集、3个VLM(CLIP/SigLIP2/PE-Core)、7种零样本OOD检测器的受控可迁移性审计证明"benchmark冠军"在换部署域后会失灵(每个检测器都至少在一个域上FPR95超过80%)，并将根因归结为不同检测器只保留了logit中互补的"level"(绝对匹配度)或"sharpness"(相对/空间尖锐度)证据；据此提出无需重训练的Complementary Evidence Guard(CEG)，通过对多个证据通道做非补偿式(取最小值)分位数融合，把GL-MCM的family-balanced FPR95从38.1降到28.8、MCM从42.6降到30.5。
>
> **TL;DR:** A controlled portability audit across 17 in-distribution (ID) datasets, 3 VLMs, and 7 zero-shot OOD detectors shows every detector exceeds 80% FPR95 on at least one ID domain, and the best-on-benchmark detector changes in 7/13 deployment units — the underlying cause is that detectors like MCM (softmax) preserve only "sharpness" while MaxLogit/Energy preserve only "level," and neither is universally sufficient. The paper's fix, Complementary Evidence Guard (CEG), fuses base-score, level, and sharpness percentiles via a non-compensatory minimum using only unlabeled ID calibration data, improving family-balanced FPR95 from 38.1→28.8 for GL-MCM and 42.6→30.5 for MCM (both CLIP-B/16), with gains of 9.3–35.9 FPR95 points across every wrapped detector on the strict 5-task OpenOOD-v1.5 protocol.

## 核心贡献

- **可迁移性审计（portability audit）：** 在保持实现、预处理、评估协议一致的前提下，横跨17个ID域、3个VLM主干（CLIP、SigLIP2、PE-Core）与7个代表性零样本OOD检测器，系统证明"在一个benchmark上排名最优的检测器"在换到另一个部署域后并不可靠——每个检测器都至少在一个ID域上FPR95超过80%，最优的"最差情况"FPR95也高达89。
- **证据层面的机制解释：** 提出CLIP风格的余弦logits中包含两个互补但可分离的信号——**level**（图像与最佳锚点类别的绝对匹配程度）和**sharpness**（最强匹配类别相对于其余词表/空间区域的突出程度）。用一个命题（Proposition 1）证明MCM在大温度极限下近似于对中心化最大值的偏移不变近似，因此只保留sharpness；而Energy近似保留level。NegLabel/CSP等引入WordNet外部语料的方法则额外依赖语料对ID-OOD偏移的覆盖度，这种覆盖度本身会随部署域变化而剧烈波动（如NegLabel在ImageNet上改善MCM 13.1个FPR95点，但在CIFAR-100和MNIST上却分别恶化6.8和38.4点）。
- **Complementary Evidence Guard (CEG)：** 一个检测器无关（detector-agnostic）的包装框架，不需要OOD样本、辅助语料或检测器专属改造，只用未标注的ID校准数据，把基础检测分数、level通道、sharpness通道分别标定为经验ID分位数，再取三者的最小值作为非补偿式决策规则，从而保证"任一通道给出的强OOD信号都足以否决接受"。
- **部署导向的评估协议建议：** 主张报告最差域（worst-domain）风险而非仅报告benchmark均值，并在ID域或底层VLM发生变化时重新验证检测器排名。

## Key Contributions

- **Portability audit:** A controlled study holding implementation, preprocessing, and evaluation protocol fixed across 17 ID datasets, 3 VLMs (CLIP, SigLIP2, PE-Core), and 7 representative zero-shot OOD detectors, showing that "benchmark-best" detector rankings reverse as either the ID domain or the underlying VLM changes; every detector exceeds 80% FPR95 on at least one domain, and the best achievable worst-case FPR95 across detectors is still 89.
- **Evidence-level explanation:** Identifies two complementary evidence channels latent in vision-language logits — **level** (max_c ℓ_c, absolute match strength) and **sharpness** (max_c ℓ_c − mean_c ℓ_c, relative/spatial peakedness). Proves (Proposition 1) that MCM is a shift-invariant approximation to the centered maximum (i.e., discards level, keeps sharpness only), while Energy's log-sum-exp preserves level to leading order. WordNet-corpus-augmented methods (NegLabel, CSP) add a third channel whose usefulness depends on external corpus coverage of the specific ID–OOD shift, which is domain-dependent and not fixed by vocabulary-mining alone (89.5–93.0% WordNet-negative overlap across domains, yet detector impact reverses sign).
- **Complementary Evidence Guard (CEG):** A detector-agnostic wrapper that standardizes the base score, level, and sharpness channels to empirical ID percentiles (using a disjoint unlabeled ID calibration set) and combines them via min-fusion — a non-compensatory rule so that any single channel signaling strong OOD-ness is sufficient to reject the sample. No OOD supervision, auxiliary corpora, or learned fusion weights are used.
- **Deployment-oriented evaluation protocol proposal:** Recommends reporting worst-domain risk (not just benchmark-average performance) and re-validating detector choice whenever the ID domain or VLM changes.

## Methodology

The audit fixes implementation, preprocessing, and evaluation across 17 ID datasets (ImageNet-1K/200, CUB-200, Food-101, Oxford Pets, Caltech-101, EuroSAT, DTD, MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, Pascal VOC, and 6 DomainNet styles) and 3 VLMs (CLIP B/16@224, SigLIP2 B/16@512, PE-Core B/16@224), evaluated under both native OpenOOD-v1.5 suites and a reference-shift protocol that scores different ID datasets against a shared ImageNet-based OOD reference, to separate ID-domain effects from benchmark-composition artifacts. Seven detectors are compared: MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, MCM, GL-MCM (corpus-free class-name scores), Mahalanobis (fits ID feature statistics), and NegLabel/CSP (augment the ID vocabulary with a mined WordNet noun/adjective corpus).

The mechanism analysis decomposes CLIP cosine logits ℓ_c into **level** = max_c ℓ_c and **sharpness** = max_c ℓ_c − mean_c ℓ_c, and formally shows (Proposition 1, with a log-sum-exp expansion for Energy) that MCM discards level (shift-invariant to additive logit shifts) while Energy preserves level to leading order with only a second-order dependence on logit variance — i.e., MCM and Energy are two complementary extremes of the same decomposition.

CEG then defines two complementary channels from the same VLM forward pass: L(x) combines global level (max_c ℓ_c) with local level averaged over the top-k=10 highest-confidence patches, and S(x) combines global sharpness with spatial sharpness over image patches (using a κ*s kernel). Each channel — base detector B, L, and S — is mapped to its empirical ID percentile U_R(x) = F̂_R(R(x)) via CDFs estimated on a disjoint unlabeled ID calibration set C (1,000 images), and the guard score is G_B(x) = min over R∈{B,L,S} of U_R(x), with acceptance threshold τ fixed on a disjoint ID operating split. Because taking the minimum is non-compensatory, a single atypical channel is enough to veto acceptance — directly implementing the paper's "preserve complementary evidence" deployment principle. The primary evaluation protocol uses five strict OpenOOD tasks (IN-1K, IN-200, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, MNIST) with capped calibration/test sets for reproducibility, plus uncapped full-benchmark reruns, SigLIP2/PE-Core transfer with hyperparameters frozen from CLIP, and five-fold ID-only cross-fitting (no OOD data or labels are used in fitting).

## Results & Highlights

- **Portability failure (headline audit):** Across the full 17-domain CLIP study, every one of the 7 detectors exceeds 80% FPR95 on at least one ID domain, and the lowest achievable worst-case FPR95 across all detectors is still 89 — selecting the detector with the lowest benchmark-mean FPR95 does not control the failures practitioners actually encounter after the ID domain changes.
- **Detector-ranking reversal under domain/VLM change:** The all-five-detector winner changes in 7 of 13 deployment units (2 native, 5 reference-shift) when only the VLM is swapped (CLIP → SigLIP2 → PE-Core), and the frozen "global" corpus-free subset still changes winner in 5/13 units after controls; winner probability exceeds 0.95 in 33/39 all-method controls, indicating these reversals are not merely statistical noise.
- **NegLabel's WordNet-corpus dependence is domain-specific despite stable vocabulary overlap:** NegLabel's mined WordNet negative set overlaps 89.5–93.0% with the ImageNet-derived vocabulary across the tested deployment domains, yet its effect on MCM's FPR95 flips sign — improving MCM by 13.1 FPR95 points on ImageNet-1K, but degrading it by 6.8 points on CIFAR-100 and 38.4 points on MNIST — showing the limitation is corpus *coverage* of the specific shift, not vocabulary selection.
- **CEG's main strict-benchmark result (CLIP-B/16, family-balanced FPR95, Raw→CEG):** GL-MCM improves 38.1 → 28.8, and MCM improves 42.6 → 30.5 (matching the abstract's headline claim and the paper's own Figure 8 ablation baseline of raw MCM = 42.6). Per-dataset numbers for MaxLogit (strict OpenOOD-v1.5, Raw→CEG) show FPR95 72.7→54.3 / AUROC 77.3→82.3 on ImageNet-1K and FPR95 45.7→30.2 / AUROC 89.3→92.2 on ImageNet-200 (Table 1a).
- **Channel-substitution control (Figure 8, CLIP-B/16 MCM, family-balanced FPR95):** Raw MCM = 42.6. Replacing level/sharpness with uninformative channels performs no better than raw MCM — seeded random noise: 54.1 (worse), entropy: 47.2, logit variance: 47.2 — whereas the real channels reduce error: sharpness alone: 37.5, level alone: 32.0, and level+sharpness (CEG): 30.5. This directly demonstrates the gain comes from the specific informative content of level/sharpness, not merely from adding a conservative min-fusion term.
- **Broad multi-VLM deployment audit (CEG-P, shrunk variant):** Improves in all 15 near/far OOD deployment summaries across 5 base detectors × 3 VLMs, with both near and far improving simultaneously in 14/15; every reported confidence interval excludes zero. The single exception is PE-Core MaxLogit on near-OOD (30.77 → 30.92, essentially flat), though the same base still improves on the corresponding far-OOD benchmark (24.83 → 23.85). Across the full 65 CLIP detector-domain pairs, 61 improve under CEG.
- Overall, CEG "improves the family-balanced FPR95 of every wrapped detector, by between 9.3 and 35.9 points," and AUROC also rises for every base detector (Table 1 caption/body text) — the effect is not simply a threshold shift at the 95% ID-recall operating point.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core diagnostic contribution — showing formally (Proposition 1) that MCM and Energy are complementary extremes of the same logit decomposition (sharpness vs. level), and empirically that this explains cross-domain ranking reversals — is a genuinely useful mechanistic insight rather than just another benchmark leaderboard entry. CEG itself is a relatively simple non-compensatory min-fusion over percentile-calibrated channels; its novelty is more in the diagnosis-driven design (which channels to combine, and why) than in the fusion mechanism per se.

**Rigor:** The controlled portability audit is unusually careful for this literature — holding VLM, preprocessing, and protocol fixed while varying ID domain, and separately isolating benchmark-composition artifacts (Figure 3's verified Places/ImageNet duplicate and near-neighbor analysis) from genuine domain effects. The channel-substitution ablation (Figure 8: entropy, logit variance, seeded noise vs. the real level/sharpness channels) is a strong internal control that rules out "any conservative min-fusion helps" as an alternative explanation — noise (54.1) and entropy/variance (47.2/47.2) are worse than or comparable to raw MCM (42.6), while the real channels clearly help (30.5). The paper is also honest about a disclosed exception: PE-Core MaxLogit's near-OOD FPR95 is essentially flat (30.77→30.92) under CEG-P, which the authors report rather than omit.

**Reproducibility:** Extensive appendices (roughly half the 22-page PDF) document frozen components, exact score/token definitions, dataset/OOD pools, calibration splits, and uncertainty accounting (5,000 paired resamples). The five strict OpenOOD tasks use capped manifests, consistent with reproducible small-scale evaluation, with uncapped full-benchmark reruns reported as robustness checks.

**Significance:** The central message — that benchmark-average rankings for zero-shot OOD detectors do not transfer across deployment domains, and that this stems from complementary (not redundant) evidence channels — has practical implications for how the OOD-detection community should evaluate and report results (worst-domain risk vs. mean performance). This is a meaningful corrective to a benchmark-driven subfield.

**Red flags / caveats:** (1) CEG explicitly requires unlabeled ID calibration data (1,000 images) to estimate channel CDFs and set the operating threshold — a resource dependency the corpus-free base detectors (MSP/MaxLogit/MCM) do not need; the paper is transparent about this "resource tier" distinction but it is a real deployment cost. (2) The paper is scoped as a diagnostic/audit + wrapper paper, not a bid to beat published SOTA leaderboard numbers on standard benchmarks — readers expecting CEG to be the new best score on, e.g., raw OpenOOD leaderboards, should recalibrate expectations; the value proposition is robustness/worst-case reduction, not best-case improvement. (3) The authors themselves note CEG's percentile transform gives "no finite-sample coverage guarantee" since channels are dependent and share calibration data — an honest limitation but one that weakens any claim of formal reliability guarantees. (4) The multi-VLM and cross-dataset generalization tests (SigLIP2, PE-Core) reuse hyperparameters frozen from CLIP without VLM-specific retuning, which is a deliberate portability test but also means CEG's absolute numbers on non-CLIP VLMs are not necessarily optimized.

## Strengths

- Rare degree of methodological care in isolating ID-domain effects from benchmark-composition artifacts (duplicate/near-duplicate OOD-ID pairs, near vs. far OOD splits).
- A clean, falsifiable mechanistic explanation (Proposition 1) rather than a purely empirical correlation, backed by a targeted channel-substitution ablation that rules out the "any minimum helps" confound.
- Honest reporting of a disclosed exception (PE-Core MaxLogit near-OOD near-flat result) rather than cherry-picking only favorable cases.
- CEG requires no OOD supervision, auxiliary corpora, or detector retraining — practical for deployment settings where OOD data is unavailable by definition.

## Limitations

- CEG needs a disjoint unlabeled ID calibration set (1,000 images per task) to fit empirical CDFs and the acceptance threshold — an added resource requirement relative to corpus-free zero-shot baselines.
- No finite-sample coverage guarantee for the percentile-based guard, acknowledged explicitly by the authors, since the fused channels are dependent and share calibration data.
- The paper's focus is CLIP-family VLMs (CLIP, SigLIP2, PE-Core); it does not test non-contrastive or generative VLM architectures, so it's unclear whether the level/sharpness decomposition generalizes beyond CLIP-style cosine logits.
- CEG is validated as a wrapper around existing detectors, not benchmarked against learned/trained OOD-fusion alternatives beyond the entropy/variance/noise ablation baselines chosen by the authors themselves.

## Relevance

这篇论文对做zero-shot OOD检测或VLM可靠性评测的研究者很有参考价值：它提醒我们不能只看单一benchmark均值来选检测器/选VLM，而应关注worst-domain风险，且提供了一个即插即用、不需要OOD监督的min-fusion包装方案(CEG)，可直接用于现有MCM/GL-MCM/Energy等检测器的部署鲁棒性改造。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the level/sharpness decomposition (Proposition 1) generalize to non-CLIP-style VLMs (e.g., generative or non-contrastive vision-language models), or is it specific to cosine-similarity logits?
- How sensitive is CEG's performance to the size and representativeness of the unlabeled ID calibration set — what happens with only 100 or 10,000 calibration images instead of 1,000?
- Could the min-fusion rule be replaced with a learned but still calibration-only (no-OOD-supervision) combination rule that outperforms the non-compensatory minimum while retaining the robustness property?
- The paper shows NegLabel/CSP's external-corpus channel is domain-dependent in usefulness — could CEG be extended to treat external-corpus evidence as a fourth calibrated channel, and would it help or hurt the family-balanced results?
- How does CEG's overhead (extra forward-pass channels, calibration fitting) compare in wall-clock/deployment cost to simply ensembling multiple existing detectors' raw scores?
