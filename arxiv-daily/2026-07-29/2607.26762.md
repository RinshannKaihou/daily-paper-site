> **一句话总结：** 该研究用一个双线性探针（bilinear probe）在 ModernBERT、LLaDA-8B、LLaMA-3.1-8B 三类语言模型（掩码/扩散/自回归）的语义空间中系统检验六种语义关系（上位、下位、整体、部分、同义、反义）是否各自占据可分离的"关系区域"，发现所有模型在真实语义关系上的受控 F1 都低于 0.30（LLaMA 在上位词关系最高仅 0.25），非对称关系（上位/下位/整体/部分）明显优于对称关系（同义/反义，最佳仅 0.12–0.16），而对称性、传递性等关系性质在语义空间中只被中等程度捕捉，反义关系甚至是唯一一个静态 fastText 基线（0.16）反超所有大模型的关系。

> **TL;DR:** This paper trains a bilinear relation probe (softmax over $e_w^T W_r e_v$) to test whether relata of six WordNet relations (hypernymy, hyponymy, holonymy, meronymy, synonymy, antonymy) occupy linearly separable "relata regions" in the token-representation space of ModernBERT (MLM), LLaDA-8B (diffusion LM), and LLaMA-3.1-8B (causal LM), using 6.8M+ SemCor-derived triplets with strict lemma-based train/test splits to prevent lexical leakage. Controlled relation-predictability scores stay below 0.30 for every real relation (best: LLaMA on hypernymy, F=.25; unrelated-pair control reaches .46), asymmetric relations beat symmetric ones (best HYP/HPO/HOL/MER in .20–.27 vs. best SYN/ANT in .12–.16), directionality tops out at D=.18 for LLaMA on hypernymy, transitivity decays with hypernymy distance (dropping below chance by distance 9 for the static baseline and LLaDA), and — notably — the static fastText baseline (0.16) beats all three neural LMs on antonymy predictability. An ablation shows LLaMA relies more on lexical (surface-form) information while ModernBERT and LLaDA rely more on contextual information for most relations.

## 核心贡献

- 提出"关系几何"（relation geometry）的可解释探针方法：用双线性函数 $f_r(w,v)=\mathrm{softmax}(e_w^T W_r e_v)$ 同时对多种关系建模，几何上等价于为每个目标词学习一组超平面分类器，将不同关系的相关词（relata）划分到语义空间中的不同线性区域（relata regions）。
- 定义三个互补指标——关系可预测性（Relation Predictability，F1）、方向性（Directionality，交换词序后能否预测出逆关系）、传递性（Transitivity，按跳数评估间接三元组）——并均通过随机探针做受控（controlled）处理，剔除探针本身拟合标签分布的能力。
- 构建严格的评测协议：基于 SemCor + WordNet 抽取超过 680 万个三元组，覆盖上位、下位、整体、部分、同义、反义六种关系及一个"无关"对照类；按词元（lemma）划分训练/测试集杜绝词汇泄漏，剔除同句共现三元组防止探针走捷径，且训练集只含直接关系对以避免传递性信息泄漏。
- 首次系统比较三类语言模型家族（掩码式 ModernBERT-large、扩散式 LLaDA-8B-Base、自回归式 LLaMA-3.1-8B）及静态 fastText 基线在六种关系上的关系几何表现，并对同义、反义等此前研究较少涉及的关系进行了补充。
- 设计一套通过操纵注意力分布（退化去语境化/均衡去语境化）来量化词汇信息与语境信息各自对关系几何贡献大小的新方法（ΔLEX、ΔCTX），发现 LLaMA 更依赖词汇信息，而 ModernBERT、LLaDA 更依赖语境信息。

## Key Contributions

- **Bilinear relation probe with a clean geometric interpretation**: for a target word w and relatum v, $f_r(w,v)=\mathrm{softmax}(e_w^T W_r e_v)$ across relations R; each $W_r$ defines a hyperplane (normal vector $W_r^T e_w$) separating v's that stand in relation r from those that don't, so high probe performance directly evidences the existence of a linearly separable "relata region" per relation.
- **Three controlled metrics**: Relation Predictability ($\mathcal{F}_r$, F1-based), Directionality ($\mathcal{D}_r$, tests whether swapping (w,v)→(v,w) flips the prediction to the reverse relation for asymmetric relations / keeps it unchanged for symmetric ones), and Transitivity ($\mathcal{T}_{r,k}$, F1 on indirect triplets at hypernymy-hierarchy distance k, 2–9). All scores are "controlled" by subtracting a random-probe baseline trained on random vectors of the same dimensionality, isolating genuine semantic geometry from probe capacity.
- **Leak-proof large-scale dataset**: 2.7M direct triplets (Table 1) plus 6.88M indirect hypernymy/hyponymy triplets (Table 2) built from SemCor (68,228 sense-tagged noun tokens, 10,409 WordNet synsets), with lemma-disjoint train/test splits, intra-sentential co-occurrence removal, and direct-only training triplets to prevent transitivity leakage — addressing methodological gaps the authors identify in prior work (Ravichander et al. 2020; Limisiewicz & Mareček 2021; Aspillaga et al. 2021).
- **Three-model-family comparison**: modernbert-large (MLM, 395M params), LLaDA-8B-Base (DLM, 8B params), LLaMA-3.1-8B (CLM, 8B params), plus a fastText static-embedding baseline, evaluated with 4-fold cross-validation × 10 trials and Bonferroni-corrected permutation tests (α=0.01 over 120 tests).
- **Lexical vs. contextual information ablation**: removes lexical information (masking the target token, or using the preceding token's representation for unidirectional models) and/or contextual information (via attention-distribution manipulation — "degenerate" one-hot or "egalitarian" uniform decontextualisation with temperature τ=100), then measures performance drops (ΔLEX, ΔCTX) to quantify which information source each model relies on more, per relation and per metric.

## Methodology

The core object of study is whether relata (words standing in a semantic relation r to a target word w) occupy a distinct, linearly-separable region of an LM's token-representation space ("relata region"). The probe is a bilinear scorer $\phi_r(w,v)=e_w^T W_r e_v$ (one learned matrix $W_r$ per relation), softmaxed across all relations in R to produce a probability distribution $f(w,v)$; the argmax is the predicted relation. Geometrically this is equivalent to a set of per-target-word linear separators, directly operationalizing the "relata region" hypothesis (Figure 1 in the paper: target word as a star, relata for each relation clustering in a distinct colored region).

Data comes from SemCor (sense-disambiguated Brown Corpus subset), restricted to nouns (only nouns support all six relations), excluding multiword expressions, named entities, sentences under 5 tokens, and sentence-initial words. Six relations are studied — hypernymy (HYP), hyponymy (HPO), holonymy (HOL), meronymy (MER), synonymy (SYN), antonymy (ANT) — plus an "unrelated" (UNR) control class of 500,000 randomly sampled non-related pairs. Triplet construction removes trivial same-lemma synonymy pairs, intra-sentential co-occurring pairs (to prevent the probe from exploiting local syntactic shortcuts, a leakage mode the authors attribute to Limisiewicz & Mareček 2021's setup), and enforces a strict lemma-disjoint train/test split (no lemma appears as w or v in both sets) to eliminate lexical leakage identified by Ravichander et al. (2020). For transitivity, only direct hypernymy/hyponymy pairs go into training; indirect pairs (distance 2–9, built by chaining direct WordNet edges, capped at distance 10 for reliability and filtered for path ambiguity) form a held-out evaluation-only set of 6.88M triplets.

Three metrics are defined, all reported as "controlled" scores (raw performance minus a random-probe baseline trained on random vectors of matching dimensionality, to cancel out the probe's own capacity to exploit label-distribution skew): (1) Relation Predictability $\mathcal{F}_r$ — macro-averaged F1 across 4-fold CV × 10 trials; (2) Directionality $\mathcal{D}_r$ — binary indicator of whether swapping (w,v) input order flips the predicted relation to its reverse (for asymmetric relations) or leaves it unchanged (symmetric relations); (3) Transitivity $\mathcal{T}_{r,k}$ — F1 on indirect triplets at hypernymy-hierarchy distance k, averaged over k=2–9.

Three LM families are compared under a common protocol: modernbert-large (MLM, 28 layers, 1024-dim, 395M params), LLaDA-8B-Base (DLM, 32 layers, 4096-dim, 8B params), LLaMA-3.1-8B (CLM, 32 layers, 4096-dim, 8B params) — plus a fastText static-embedding baseline (300-dim). Token representations use the final layer's average-of-subwords output. Probes are trained with cross-entropy loss, Adam, 5 epochs, batch size 1024, under 4-fold CV × 10 trials. Statistical significance uses one-sided paired/non-paired Monte Carlo permutation tests (1,000,000 permutations) with Bonferroni correction (α=0.01/120 tests).

For the lexical/contextual ablation, lexical information is removed by masking the target token (bidirectional LMs) or substituting the preceding token's representation (unidirectional LMs); contextual information is removed by manipulating the softmax attention distribution — either "degenerate" decontextualisation (identity/one-hot attention, i.e., the token attends only to itself) or "egalitarian" decontextualisation (uniform attention via temperature τ→∞, empirically set to τ=100). ΔLEX and ΔCTX are then computed as average controlled-performance drops when lexical or contextual information (respectively) is removed, across the six lexical/contextual status combinations (Eq. 12–13).

## Results & Highlights

- **Relation Predictability (Table 4)**: All models perform poorly on real semantic relations — controlled $\mathcal{F}_r$ stays below 0.30 across the board. LLaMA is best on all four asymmetric relations: HYP=.25, HPO=.22, HOL=.27, MER=.21 (vs. ModernBERT's best of .23 on HYP, LLaDA's best of .19 on HYP, and the static baseline's best of .14 on HOL). On symmetric relations the ranking flips: the static baseline wins ANT (.16 vs. LLaMA's .15, ModernBERT's .07, LLaDA's .04), and ModernBERT wins SYN (.12 vs. LLaMA's .10, static's .10, LLaDA's .08). The UNR control class is predicted far better than any real relation by every model (LLaMA .46, ModernBERT .42, static .36, LLaDA .28), showing unrelated pairs are more linearly separable than related ones.
- **Directionality (Table 5)**: No model-relation combination exceeds $\mathcal{D}_r$=0.20. LLaMA again leads on hypernymy (.18) and hyponymy (.17); holonymy and meronymy directionality stays in the .02–.07 range for all models. Antonymy is an outlier where the static baseline (.12) clearly beats all three LMs (ModernBERT .01, LLaDA .00, LLaMA .05), and synonymy directionality is at or below chance for the static baseline (−.04) and ModernBERT (−.02).
- **Transitivity (Table 6, Figure 2)**: Controlled $\mathcal{T}_r$ (averaged over hypernymy distances 2–9) is highest for LLaMA on both HYP (.15) and HPO (.12); the static baseline and LLaDA go negative on both relations (static: −.06/−.16; LLaDA: −.02/−.06), meaning their relata geometry does not reflect transitivity better than chance. All models' transitivity scores decline monotonically with hypernymy distance, with the static baseline and LLaDA dropping below zero by around distance 4–5 and LLaMA itself falling below zero at distance 9.
- **Lexical vs. contextual ablation (Tables 7–9)**: For relation predictability, LLaMA consistently shows larger |ΔLEX| than |ΔCTX| (e.g., HYP: ΔLEX=−.17 vs. ΔCTX=−.06; UNR: ΔLEX=−.31 vs. ΔCTX=−.16), i.e., it depends more on lexical/surface information, while ModernBERT and LLaDA show the opposite pattern for most relations (e.g., ModernBERT HYP: ΔLEX=−.11 vs. ΔCTX=−.15). For transitivity, the pattern partially reverses: even ModernBERT and LLaDA come to rely more on lexical information for indirect (long-distance) hypernymy/hyponymy pairs than they do for direct pairs.
- The paper explicitly frames the antonymy finding as a "possible counterexample" to the standard distributional-semantics claim that word sense/relations can be learned purely from context, since the simplest, context-blind static baseline outperforms all three billion-parameter-class LMs on this relation.

No quantitative deep-learning-style accuracy numbers beyond F1/D/T controlled scores are reported (this is a probing/interpretability study, not a downstream-task benchmark), but the above F1, directionality, and transitivity deltas against the fastText baseline and cross-model comparisons constitute the paper's core quantitative claims.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core methodological contributions — a bilinear (rather than concatenation-based) probe applied uniformly across six relations, the directionality/transitivity metrics with random-probe control, and the lexical/contextual ablation via attention-distribution manipulation — are genuinely new combinations, even though each individual ingredient (bilinear scoring from KG embeddings, probing classifiers, attention-based decontextualization) has precedent elsewhere. The explicit test of whether relata occupy linearly-separable regions, rather than just whether a probe can classify pairs, is a meaningful reframing.

**Rigor**: The experimental design is unusually careful about leakage — lemma-disjoint splits, intra-sentential exclusion, direct-only training for transitivity, and random-probe controls applied consistently. The use of Bonferroni correction across 120 statistical tests and 10 repeated CV trials to combat relation-distribution skew is a strength rarely seen in probing papers. One genuine limitation the authors themselves flag: the three LMs differ not just in architecture (MLM/DLM/CLM) but also in parameter count (395M vs. 8B vs. 8B) and training data, so LLaMA's apparent superiority over ModernBERT cannot be cleanly attributed to its causal-LM objective — this is an important, paper-specific confound the authors are honest about (Limitations, p.25) rather than papering over.

**Reproducibility**: Full formulae for all metrics are given, appendices report raw (non-controlled) scores (Table B.1) and a layer-wise breakdown (Appendix C), and the dataset construction from SemCor+WordNet is described in enough procedural detail to reproduce. No code/data release is mentioned in the excerpted text, however, which is a gap for a paper this methodologically involved.

**Significance and potential overclaiming check**: The headline claim — "relation geometry is not equally well-represented for all relations... possible counter-evidence to the distributional semantic view" — is reasonably calibrated given the numbers (all controlled F1 < 0.30) and is not oversold; the authors explicitly caveat in the Limitations section that (a) they only tested three specific LMs of differing scale (LLaMA's win could be a parameter-count/context-window artifact, not a CLM-architecture advantage), (b) linear probes cannot rule out non-linear relation encoding (the synonymy near-failure is flagged as possibly a probe-linearity limitation, not proof of absent geometry), and (c) probing performance doesn't establish that downstream tasks actually use this knowledge. This is a good-faith, non-cherry-picked presentation — the antonymy result (static beats all LMs) undercuts rather than supports the "LMs are good at this" narrative, which is evidence of genuine reporting rather than result-shopping. A minor weak point: the "weakest baseline" in this study is the fastText static embedding, which is not a strong ceiling to compare 8B-parameter LMs against — the paper is honest that fastText sometimes wins (antonymy), but for the main asymmetric-relation results, comparisons are LM-vs-LM plus one static point of reference rather than against, say, contextualized alternatives like sentence-embedding models or larger LMs.

## Strengths

- Methodologically rigorous leakage prevention (lemma-disjoint splits, intra-sentential removal, direct-only transitivity training) that directly fixes documented flaws in prior probing studies the authors cite by name.
- Broad relation coverage (6 relations vs. prior work's near-exclusive focus on hypernymy) with a unified bilinear-probe framework applied consistently across all of them.
- Random-probe control applied to every reported metric, isolating genuine semantic geometry from probe overfitting/capacity effects.
- Novel, well-motivated ablation quantifying lexical vs. contextual information contribution via principled attention-distribution manipulation (degenerate vs. egalitarian decontextualisation), rather than heuristic feature removal.
- Honest, non-cherry-picked reporting: results that complicate the "LMs learn semantics" narrative (poor synonymy geometry, static baseline beating LMs on antonymy) are reported and discussed rather than downplayed.

## Limitations

- Confounded model comparison: LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaDA-8B, and ModernBERT-large differ simultaneously in parameter count, training corpus, and context window in addition to architecture family, so claims about "causal LMs capturing relations better" are not isolable from scale/data effects (explicitly acknowledged by the authors).
- Only linear probes are used; the paper cannot address whether relations like synonymy are encoded non-linearly, which the authors flag as a major open question given how poorly synonymy performs under the linear probe.
- Data is restricted to nouns and drawn from SemCor/Brown Corpus, reflecting pre-1970s English usage, limiting ecological validity for contemporary language.
- No downstream-task validation: strong or weak probing performance is not shown to correlate with actual task performance (e.g., WSD, entailment), so the practical implications of "poor relation geometry" remain unestablished.
- Excludes multiword expressions, named entities, and subword-level relations entirely, narrowing the scope of what "semantic relation geometry" claims actually cover.

## Relevance

该论文用严谨的探针方法系统量化了不同架构语言模型对语义关系的几何编码能力，对关注语言模型可解释性、知识探测与分布语义理论边界的研究者具有较高参考价值，其反义词/同义词的反直觉发现也为"分布假设是否能解释语义关系"提供了新的实证材料。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would non-linear probes (e.g., small MLPs or kernel methods) recover a clearer synonymy/antonymy geometry, or does the failure persist regardless of probe capacity — genuinely testing whether these relations are absent from the space rather than just non-linearly encoded?
- How would results change with same-scale model comparisons (e.g., a causal, masked, and diffusion LM all at 1B or all at 8B parameters trained on matched corpora) to isolate architecture effects from scale/data confounds?
- Does poor relata-region separability for synonymy/antonymy actually predict downstream failure on tasks requiring those relations (e.g., paraphrase detection, contradiction detection in NLI), or do models compensate via non-geometric mechanisms?
- Would the antonymy finding (static embeddings beating LLMs) replicate with contrastive/instruction-tuned embedding models specifically optimized for semantic similarity (e.g., sentence-transformers), which the paper does not test?
- How sensitive are the reported scores to the choice of final-layer representations versus the best-performing intermediate layer per relation (Appendix C suggests layer effects exist but aren't deeply analyzed)?
