> **一句话总结：** BayesAME 用分桶隐能力的贝叶斯高斯模型对参考模型的历史表现建模，通过信息增益主动选题、双阈值停止准则自动确定评测子集（coreset）大小，在 GPQA、MMLU-Pro 等 7 个基准上，RMSE 相比随机采样均值基线（RS-Mean）降低约 20%-50%，并大幅优于 IRT、ProEval 等现有方法。

> **TL;DR:** BayesAME models a benchmark's per-item scores as noisy draws from bucket-level latent "abilities" with a Gaussian prior whose covariance encodes similarity to reference models, then uses an information-gain criterion plus a two-condition stopping rule to actively grow a coreset until estimation is precise enough — no user-specified coreset size required. Across GPQA, MMLU-Pro, BBH, ARC-Challenge, MuSR, IFEval, and HELM Lite's Natural QA Openbook (up to 448 reference models, 200 random seeds), BayesAME's Evaluation-Weighted Integrated RMSE beats the RS-Mean baseline substantially (e.g., GPQA binary/interpolation: 61.54 vs. 76.48; MMLU-Pro continuous/interpolation: 13.11 vs. 27.80) and outperforms IRT, ProEval, and a Bayesian-AIPW baseline in nearly every setting, while a weak baseline (Seq-APW) blows up to RMSE > 1000 on several benchmarks.

## 核心贡献

- 将"高效模型评测+自动确定 coreset 大小"形式化为一个贝叶斯序贯过程：不断扩充 coreset，直到性能估计的波动幅度和不确定性（95% 可信区间宽度）都低于用户设定的阈值。
- 提出一种计算高效的方法，具备良好性质的鲁棒性能估计器（在"参考分数唯一"和"参考分数非唯一"两种情形下分别推导估计器，并统一为一个加权公式）。
- 设计基于信息增益（微分熵减少量）的主动选题策略，用于挑选下一个最值得评测的基准样本。
- 将方法扩展到多目标模型场景，用协区域化线性模型（LMC）刻画多个目标模型之间的表现相关性，进一步压缩所需 coreset 大小。
- 在不同参考模型数量、插值/外推（interpolation/extrapolation）场景、不同目标模型相关度下做了大规模实证分析，证明 BayesAME 优于对现有方法做的序贯化改造版本；即使 coreset 大小固定，BayesAME 依然占优。
- 推翻了近期文献中的一个悲观结论：非随机 coreset 选择确实能可靠地超过随机采样均值基线，尤其是在利用连续对数似然分数（而非二值分数）时效果显著提升。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates efficient model evaluation with automatic coreset-size determination as a Bayesian sequential process: the coreset is grown until both the fluctuation of the performance estimate and the width of its 95% credible interval drop below user-defined thresholds (ε₁, ε₂).
- Derives closed-form, computationally efficient performance estimators for two regimes (unique vs. non-unique reference score vectors across items), unified into a single weighted estimator (Eq. 5), with posterior updates costing O(min(n,B)²) per iteration via Cholesky updates.
- Designs an information-gain (differential-entropy-reduction) active selection criterion (Eq. 6) that has a closed form under the Gaussian posterior, and defaults to random sampling when reference scores are not sufficiently unique (to avoid a documented failure mode where one oversized bucket biases active selection).
- Extends the framework to multi-target evaluation via a linear model of coregionalization (mixing squared-exponential and Matérn-3/2 kernels) that shares latent components across target models, reducing coreset size further when target models are correlated.
- Runs an extensive empirical study (7 benchmarks, up to 448 reference models, 200 seeds, varying reference-pool sizes of 10/50/90%, interpolation vs. extrapolation regimes) that overturns prior claims (Zhang et al., 2025) that random coreset selection and the random-sample-mean baseline are hard to beat.
- Shows continuous response log-likelihoods (softmax-derived scores) meaningfully outperform binary correctness scores for estimation accuracy across nearly all benchmarks tested.

## Methodology

BayesAME treats the benchmark items as belonging to disjoint "buckets" — sets of items on which all reference models produced identical score vectors (with a tolerance margin N₀). Each bucket b is assigned a latent scalar "ability" θ_b for the target model, with a joint Gaussian prior N(μ^θ, Σ^θ) whose mean is the average reference-model score on that bucket and whose covariance decays with squared Euclidean distance between buckets' reference-score vectors (Eq. 1) — encoding "buckets where reference models behave similarly imply correlated target abilities." Item scores within a bucket are modeled as Gaussian around the bucket ability, with per-item noise variance scaled by bucket size so that the variance of the bucket mean stays constant at σ² regardless of bucket population.

Given the scores observed so far on the coreset C, the posterior mean/variance of the target model's full benchmark performance R = (1/N)Σsᵢ are computed in closed form (Eqs. 3–4), unified into an estimator R̂ (Eq. 5) that behaves like the natural Bayesian posterior expectation when reference score vectors are nearly unique across items (B ≥ N−N₀), and switches to a control-variate-style estimator when many items collapse into a few large buckets (B < N−N₀) — this avoids a heavily-populated bucket's posterior mean dominating and amplifying error. Posterior computation is made efficient by working with per-bucket sufficient statistics (an n×n matrix inversion collapses to at most B×B), and covariance hyperparameters (α, β) are periodically re-optimized (every F=50 iterations) by minimizing the negative log-marginal likelihood.

Item selection uses expected information gain — the reduction in differential entropy of R from evaluating a candidate item — which has a closed form under the Gaussian posterior (Eq. 6); all items within the same bucket share the same information-gain value. When B < N−N₀ (many singleton/near-singleton buckets with imbalanced sizes), the method defaults to random selection to avoid a documented pathology where a single oversized bucket biases active selection.

The stopping criterion is a two-condition rule: (i) the performance estimate's range over the last W=30 iterations falls below threshold ε₁, and (ii) the width of the 95% credible interval (2z₀.₉₇₅√Var(R|s_C)) falls below ε₂. ε₂ is empirically identified as the dominant factor governing when the process halts.

For the multi-target extension, target-model abilities θ^t are modeled as θ^t = μ^θ + Σ_l w_l^t u_l, where u_l are L=K_t shared latent random vectors (half using squared-exponential, half Matérn-3/2 kernels) and w_l^t are learned model-specific weights — a linear model of coregionalization. To avoid biasing early correlation estimates via greedy information gain in the expanded item×model search space, the multi-target variant first "warm starts" by randomly evaluating all target models on 10% of items, then deterministically cycles through target models using per-model information gain.

Notably, the paper reports (Sec. 6, Appendix B.3) that a more "principled" logit-transformed model of binary/bounded scores was tried but underperformed the simpler joint Gaussian approach (which uses an improper prior for bounded data) — the Gaussian model won on both computational efficiency (closed-form info-gain) and empirical accuracy. Richer item representations (e.g., prompt embeddings) also failed to improve results.

## Results & Highlights

Evaluated on 7 benchmarks: GPQA (102 models), MMLU-Pro (165, restricted to business/law subjects, 1890 items), BBH (279, 5 scenarios), ARC-Challenge (125), MuSR (288) — all from the Open LLM Leaderboard v2 with both binary and softmax-derived continuous scores; IFEval (448 models, binary only, from Zhang et al. 2025's data); and HELM Lite's Natural QA Openbook (44 models, F1/continuous, up to v1.13.0). Metrics (computed over 200 random initialization seeds) include RMSE Log, RMSE Gain vs. RS-Mean, Evaluation-Weighted Integrated RMSE, and a Cost-Accuracy Trade-off at the ε₁/ε₂-determined stopping point.

Concrete quantitative results from Table 1 (Evaluation-Weighted Integrated RMSE, 50% of reference models):
- **GPQA, binary scores, interpolation regime:** BayesAME = 61.54 vs. RS-Mean (random-sample-mean baseline) = 76.48, IRT = 89.61, ProEval = 96.29, and Seq-APW (Vivek et al. 2024 anchor-points extension) = 866.61 — a roughly 20% RMSE reduction over RS-Mean and >14x better than Seq-APW.
- **MMLU-Pro, continuous scores, interpolation regime:** BayesAME = 13.11 vs. RS-Mean = 27.80 (>50% reduction) and the closest competitor Bayes-AIPW = 17.19.
- **IFEval, binary scores, extrapolation regime:** BayesAME = 58.08 (coincides with BayesAME-RS here since B < N−N₀) vs. RS-Mean = 64.54, Bayes-AIPW = 60.52, and ProEval = 1017.33.
- **Natural QA Openbook, continuous scores, extrapolation regime:** BayesAME = 20.83 vs. RS-Mean = 41.76 (~50% reduction), Bayes-AIPW = 27.70, and Bayes-RS-Learn = 226.18.

Across nearly all benchmark/regime combinations, BayesAME achieves the lowest RMSE Log across the full range of coreset sizes (Fig. 1) and establishes a strictly superior Pareto frontier in the Cost-Accuracy Trade-off (Fig. 2) — reaching a given accuracy threshold at a smaller coreset than any baseline. Gains over RS-Mean scale with the number of reference models available (richer priors → bigger advantage). The paper explicitly demonstrates (contra Zhang et al. 2025) that active/non-random coreset selection reliably beats RS-Mean once reference models provide sufficiently unique item signals (more reference models or continuous scores) — when reference scores are largely non-unique (B < N−N₀), BayesAME provably defaults to random selection and ties BayesAME-RS/Bayes-AIPW.

In the multi-target setting (Fig. 3), jointly modeling correlated target models yields a clear efficiency win under high correlation (steeper RMSE decline, superior Pareto frontier at all thresholds) but exhibits mild negative transfer under low correlation, where BayesAME Multi-target slightly underperforms BayesAME Single-target — though it degrades gracefully to roughly match the Bayes-AIPW baseline rather than collapsing.

No quantitative comparison against full-benchmark ("gold") evaluation cost/latency is reported — all comparisons are RMSE-based against the true full-benchmark score R*.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — bucket-level latent abilities with a Gaussian prior correlated by reference-model similarity, closed-form information-gain active selection, and a principled two-condition stopping rule for automatic coreset sizing — is a genuinely useful combination. The paper is explicit that its main departure from the closest prior work, ProEval (Huang et al., 2026), is precisely the automatic coreset-size determination and more robust handling of limited prior information, which is a fair and specific novelty claim rather than a vague one.

**Rigor:** The empirical protocol is reasonably thorough — 200 seeds, 7 benchmarks, both binary and continuous scores, interpolation and extrapolation regimes, and reference-pool ablations at 10/50/90%. However, some baselines are notably weak: Seq-APW's RMSE explodes past 1000 on several binary benchmarks (e.g., ARC-Challenge interpolation = 1391.22, MMLU-Pro interpolation = 1141.06), and ProEval likewise blows up in several continuous settings (e.g., MMLU-Pro extrapolation = 570.76). The paper itself flags why ProEval degrades (its posterior mean estimator can deviate arbitrarily from true performance even at large coreset sizes) — a fair, paper-specific critique — but the sheer magnitude of these failures on some rows raises the question of whether these baselines were tuned as carefully as BayesAME. The IRT baseline is also handicapped by updating its MCMC-fit parameters only every 50 evaluations (a stated computational-cost compromise), producing "piecewise-constant curves" that could disadvantage it in the reported metrics.

**Reproducibility:** Implementation details (hyperparameters σ², N₀=10, W=30, optimizer choices, learning rates) are documented in Appendix C.1, and baseline implementations are traced to public repos (Zhang et al.'s benchmark-prediction repo, DeepMind's public ProEval repo). However, no code release is mentioned for BayesAME itself, which limits independent verification.

**Significance:** Automatic coreset-size determination addresses a real practical gap — nearly all prior coreset-selection methods require a pre-specified budget, which is awkward when reliability, not cost, is the priority. The extrapolation-regime results (mirroring Zhang et al.'s stress test) are a meaningful contribution since many "efficient evaluation" papers only validate under interpolation.

**Red flags / honest self-reporting:** To its credit, the paper reports negative results transparently — a more principled logit-transformed model of the abilities underperformed the simpler (technically improper-prior) Gaussian approach, and richer item features (prompt embeddings) didn't help. It also honestly reports negative transfer for multi-target BayesAME under low correlation rather than only showcasing favorable cases. This intellectual honesty strengthens the paper's credibility, even as it means some proposed extensions (richer item modeling, embeddings) didn't pan out.

## Strengths

- Automatic coreset-size determination via a principled two-condition Bayesian stopping rule, addressing a genuine gap versus prior fixed-budget coreset methods.
- Closed-form posterior and information-gain computations keep the active-selection loop computationally cheap (O(min(n,B)²) per iteration).
- Comprehensive empirical validation across 7 diverse benchmarks, both binary and continuous scoring, interpolation and extrapolation regimes, and multiple reference-pool sizes, with 200-seed RMSE estimates.
- Directly engages with and empirically refutes a specific skeptical claim from recent literature (Zhang et al., 2025) about the difficulty of beating random-sample-mean baselines.
- Transparent reporting of negative results (logit-transform ability model, richer item embeddings, low-correlation multi-target negative transfer) rather than only favorable outcomes.
- Multi-target extension via coregionalization is a principled, not merely heuristic, way to exploit cross-model correlation.

## Limitations

- Framework assumes scalar per-item scores; does not natively handle multi-dimensional or unstructured evaluation formats (explicitly acknowledged as future work).
- Reliance on a Gaussian model for the abilities, despite the acknowledged mismatch with binary/bounded score support (an "improper prior" is used for tractability, per the authors' own admission).
- Multi-target extension shows mild negative transfer when target models are not actually correlated, so practitioners must have some prior belief in correlated performance to benefit.
- The margin parameter N₀ = 10 is fixed empirically as a "safe" heuristic rather than derived principledly; the authors themselves note a more sophisticated, benchmark-specific setting is left to future work.
- Comparisons focus on RMSE against the true full-benchmark score under the paper's own experimental protocol; no wall-clock or dollar-cost comparison against actually running the full benchmark is provided.
- Several baselines (Seq-APW, ProEval, IRT) underperform dramatically in some settings, which strengthens BayesAME's relative numbers but leaves open how much of the gap reflects baseline implementation/tuning choices versus fundamental limitations of those methods.

## Relevance

该论文针对"如何用少量样本可靠评测大模型"这一实际痛点，提出的贝叶斯主动选题+自动停止方法在多基准、大规模参考模型池下表现稳健，且诚实报告了负面实验结果（如更复杂的似然建模反而不如简单高斯模型），方法论和实证部分都值得关注，可作为设计高效评测/主动测试流程时的参考基线。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does BayesAME perform when reference models themselves are noisy, adversarial, or systematically biased relative to the target model (beyond the interpolation/extrapolation distribution-shift framing already tested)?
- Would a more principled (rather than fixed N₀=10) determination of when to trust bucket structure recover further gains, particularly for benchmarks with highly imbalanced bucket sizes?
- Can the multi-target coregionalization model be extended with an explicit correlation-detection step so it can automatically fall back to single-target behavior early, mitigating the observed negative transfer under low correlation without expending "wasted" active-selection iterations?
- How sensitive are the reported gains to the specific choice of 300 sampled models via quantile-based stratification (Sec. 5) — would results hold on a substantially different or more adversarially chosen model pool?
- Is there a principled way to combine architecture/training metadata (mentioned as future work) with the current reference-score-based covariance without reintroducing the overhead the authors found made richer item representations (e.g., embeddings) fail to help?
