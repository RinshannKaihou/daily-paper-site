SERPO replaces broken answer-voting with self-evolving Good-Normal-Bad response archives and query-specific rubrics, turning frozen-judge log-probs into continuous GRPO rewards.

SERPO: Self-Evolving Rubric Policy Optimization for Open-Ended Test-Time Reinforcement Learning →
Jianze Wang, Kunwang Zheng, Ying Liu, Yu Cao, et al. · 8 authors | 2026-07-29 | arXiv:2607.26873
Key Metric — HealthBench, Qwen3.5-9B +20.63 44.68 → 65.31 pts, label-free adaptation only — beats the strongest voting baseline (claim-consensus TTRL, 60.88) and closes most of the gap to a privileged external-judge reference (68.41). 6-benchmark macro avg: +8.06 (Qwen3-4B) to +8.24 (Qwen3.5-9B) pts, vs. +4.06–4.30 for the best voting baseline
Why This Matters

Open-ended tasks like medical advice have no single canonical answer to vote toward — SERPO shows a concrete way to build test-time RL rewards without one.

Critical Edge

Every non-privileged baseline uses the same frozen self-judge as SERPO — its margin is proven against the authors' own in-house baselines, not an independently published external TTRL method.

Methodology

1. G-N-B response evolution
→
2. Rubric evolution
→
3. Policy evolution (GRPO)
new rollouts refresh the archive & rubric every cycle

Setting

  • Fixed-set, label-free TTRL: policy only sees unlabeled prompts + its own G=8 rollouts. A frozen copy of the deployed model plays both rubric-generator and judge — no external supervision.

The loop

  • G-N-B archive: per prompt, a rolling Good/Normal/Bad archive (width 3) keeps the ordered triple that maximizes a 3-way contrastive separation score — not just best-vs-worst.
  • Rubric pool: every 3 visits, the frozen generator proposes atomic criteria from G-N-B contrasts. Utility = score variance × order agreement; bottom-quartile criteria get 3 "at-risk" strikes before deletion (cap: 15 active criteria).
  • Reward + GRPO: criterion satisfaction = softmax over the judge's Pass/Fail token log-probs (continuous, not binary), calibrated so Bad-archive mean → 0 and Good-archive mean → 1. GRPO updates only the actor; judge and rubric-generator stay frozen.
Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 / Qwen3.5-9B · 8×H100 · G=8 rollouts · ~10–18 GPU-hrs / 30 epochs

Strengths

  • Clean three-way decomposition (archive / rubric / policy evolution) with ablations that isolate each component's contribution individually.
  • Probabilistic verdict-token reward (Eq. 3) is a simple, validated fix — reverting to hard verdicts costs 6% on HealthBench.
  • Genuine label-free OOD transfer plus sequential cross-benchmark evolution without catastrophic forgetting — rarer than typical single-benchmark TTRL results.
  • Full hyperparameter and compute disclosure (Appendix Tables 4–5), with a stated code/data release commitment.

Limitations

  • All non-privileged baselines share the same frozen self-judge — no independently-published external open-ended TTRL baseline is tested.
  • Reported scores rely on GPT-5.1 as external grader with official rubrics; that judge's rubric fidelity is never itself ablated.
  • Only two model configs from one family (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-9B); long-horizon and cross-benchmark runs use 4B only.
  • Rubric generation/judging can inherit the deployed model's own biases (no independent verifier), and the closed loop adds test-time compute overhead. No repo link yet — release is a stated future commitment.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would gains hold with a genuinely external, more capable judge/rubric-generator, or does SERPO depend on same-model rubric generation matching what the actor can plausibly satisfy?
  2. How sensitive are the reported margins to GPT-5.1 as grader — would a different frontier-model grader preserve the same ranking vs. claim-consensus TTRL?
  3. Does the "frozen actor → 36% relative loss" finding generalize across model scales, or is the 2.29× response-length inflation a Qwen3-4B-specific artifact?
  4. Can the 3-strike rubric-elimination mechanism be tuned to trade pool stability against adaptation speed, and how does behavior change if the 15-criterion cap is raised or lowered?
  5. Does SERPO sustain gains across longer cyclic benchmark streams beyond HealthBench→ResearchQA, and what role would replay/rollback play against eventual judge drift?
