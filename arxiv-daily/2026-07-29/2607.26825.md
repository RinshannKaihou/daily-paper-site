> **一句话总结：** 这篇立场论文提出一个二维分类框架——"概念结构在流水线的哪个阶段被引入"（目标函数/架构/推理/事后解释）× "概念结构来自模型内部还是外部知识库"——把现有的概念感知 LLM 研究归入 4×2 的表格中，指出推理阶段严重欠探索、各阶段研究彼此孤立、外部知识来源的方法其实贯穿全流水线只是命名不统一，但全文不含任何新实验或量化结果。

> **TL;DR:** This is a short position paper (no new experiments) that organizes prior work on "concept-aware" LLMs — from concept-level pretraining objectives (Iyer et al. 2026's concept-level training objectives, Barrault et al. 2024's Large Concept Models) to sparse-autoencoder post-hoc interpretability (Shu et al. 2025's SAE survey, Templeton et al. 2026's monosemanticity work) — into a 4-stage × 2-origin taxonomy (Table 1), arguing that concepts should be designed into LLM architectures rather than discovered post hoc via probing/dictionary learning. It reports no benchmarks or metrics of its own; its central empirical citation is Gerasimov et al. (2026) and Paulo & Belrose (2025), who show only a fraction of sparse-autoencoder features reproduce across different random training seeds, used as evidence that post-hoc "found" concepts lack architectural guarantees compared to a hypothetical "designed" alternative.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"概念作为设计轴"的立场**：反对当前主流做法（用探针或字典学习在训练后从模型中"发现"概念），主张应把概念结构作为架构设计的显式组成部分，与 tokenization、记忆机制、注意力机制并列为一等设计考量。
- **二维分类法（Table 1）**：第一轴是概念结构进入流水线的**阶段**（训练目标/核心架构/生成时推理/事后解释）；第二轴是概念结构的**来源**（内部从模型自身表征/训练动态推导 vs. 外部接入知识图谱、本体、概念词表等人工资源）。将现有方法逐一放入 4×2 网格。
- **三条观察结论**：(1) 推理阶段的概念感知方法在内部和外部两侧都明显偏少，作者认为这恰恰是概念最该发挥作用的阶段；(2) 各流水线阶段的相关研究基本各自独立发展，很少互相引用或统一术语；(3) 外部知识驱动的方法其实贯穿全流水线（从 2019 年的 ERNIE 实体注入预训练到 2024+ 的知识图谱融合 RAG 验证），只是不同社区用了不同名字（"实体注入"、"知识整合"、"推理引导"、"幻觉验证"）而非"concept"。
- **提出概念表征形式的第三个开放问题**：现有方法主要把概念实例化为潜空间中的点或知识图谱中的节点，作者指出组合分布语义学（compositional distributional semantics, Coecke et al. 2010）意义上的、带显式组合规则的结构化概念表征在现代 LLM 中几乎未被探索。
- **归纳四个跨阶段的开放挑战**：组合性（explicit 不等于 compositional）、"found vs. designed" 的判据缺失（如何区分模型真正编码的概念和仅与探针/目标函数相关的特征）、"谁的概念"（内部推导 vs. 外部强加的取舍）、以及缺乏跨阶段统一评测基准。

## Key Contributions

- **A position, not a method**: argues that concept-level structure in LLMs should be an explicit *design* choice (analogous to tokenization or attention) rather than something recovered post hoc via probing or sparse dictionary learning, since post-hoc recovery gives no architectural guarantee of stability, compositionality, controllability, or human alignment.
- **A 4×2 taxonomy (Table 1)**: crosses *pipeline stage* — (i) language-modeling objective, (ii) core/latent architecture, (iii) generation-time inference, (iv) post-hoc interpretation — with *structural origin* — internally-derived (from the model's own representations/training dynamics) vs. externally-grounded (knowledge graphs, ontologies, concept inventories) — and places representative prior work (e.g., Barrault et al. 2024's Large Concept Models, Sun et al. 2025's concept bottleneck LLMs, Yasunaga et al. 2021's QA-GNN, Sansford et al. 2024's KG-based hallucination evaluation) into each cell.
- **Three cross-cutting observations from the map**: inference-time concept-awareness is comparatively underexplored on both the internal and external side despite being, in the authors' view, the most natural place to operate over semantic units instead of tokens; related ideas have developed largely in isolation across pipeline stages without shared terminology; and externally-grounded approaches (entity-infused pretraining, KG-fused architectures, KG-guided reasoning, KG-based hallucination verification) actually span the entire pipeline, with the apparent "architectural dominance" of concept language being partly an artifact of naming rather than substance.
- **A third open axis beyond "where" and "how"**: existing concept representations are either points in embedding space or nodes in relational graphs; a compositional representation with explicit composition rules (per compositional distributional semantics, Coecke et al. 2010) remains largely unrealized in modern LLM architectures.
- **Four open challenges**: (1) compositionality — explicitness alone (a concept bottleneck, graph node, or self-supervised objective) does not guarantee reusable compositional rules, and Transformer LLMs are cited (via Dziri et al. 2023's "Faith and Fate") as relying on memorized patterns that degrade under compositional complexity; (2) found-vs-designed — no accepted criterion distinguishes a genuinely encoded concept from a feature merely correlated with the probe/objective/graph node used to elicit it, with seed-stability testing (Gerasimov et al. 2026) offered as one possible but so-far post-hoc-only methodological direction; (3) whose concepts — externally-imposed vs. internally-derived conceptual boundaries trade off interpretability/alignment against discovery flexibility, and the paper argues this choice is currently left implicit rather than deliberate; (4) no shared benchmark exists across pipeline stages to compare conceptual fidelity, stability, compositionality, and downstream utility on common ground.

## Methodology

This is a 5-page ACL/EACL-style position paper with no experiments, models, or datasets of its own. Its "methodology" is a literature-organization exercise: the author (a PhD-adjacent researcher who is co-author on several of the cited works, including Shani et al. 2023 "Towards concept-aware large language models," Iyer et al. 2026, Zhang et al. 2026, and Mizrahi et al. 2026) surveys prior work on concept-like structure in LLMs and sorts it into the Table 1 grid by (a) which pipeline stage the intervention targets and (b) whether the concept signal is internally derived or externally grounded in resources like knowledge graphs. The paper explicitly states the grid is "illustrative, not exhaustive" and does not attempt systematic literature-review coverage criteria (no search protocol, no inclusion/exclusion criteria, no PRISMA-style methodology) — it is closer to an opinionated survey/roadmap than a systematic review. Section 4 ("Why the Map Matters") offers qualitative tradeoff reasoning (cost, invasiveness, latency, auditability) for choosing among the four pipeline-stage entry points, again without quantitative support.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported. The paper contains no experiments, benchmarks, ablations, or numerical evaluations of its own — it is purely a position/taxonomy paper. As evidence for its central claim that post-hoc "found" concepts lack architectural guarantees, it leans on quantitative findings *from other papers*, which it cites but does not reproduce or extend:
- It cites Paulo & Belrose (2025), who found that sparse autoencoders trained on the same data but with different random seeds learn substantially different feature sets ("only a fraction of SAE features consistently recur after retraining"), and Gerasimov et al. (2026), who found reproducible features are concentrated in "a relatively small subset of functionally important representations" — both used as secondhand evidence that current interpretability-recovered concepts are model-instance-specific artifacts rather than stable architectural properties, not as results the author generated.
- It cites Dziri et al. (2023, "Faith and Fate") for the empirical finding that transformer performance degrades as compositional task complexity increases, to argue that explicit concept units alone don't guarantee compositional generalization.
- No comparative numbers (accuracy, F1, perplexity, etc.) for any concept-aware method vs. baseline appear anywhere in the paper; Table 1 is a qualitative taxonomy table, not a results table.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by Chen Shani, Tel Aviv University. The author has a directly relevant publication track record in this exact niche — she is a co-author on several of the paper's own key citations, including Shani, Vreeken, and Shahaf (2023) "Towards concept-aware large language models" (EMNLP Findings), Jacob, Shani, and Shahaf (2023) "FAME: Flexible, scalable analogy mappings engine" (EMNLP), Iyer, Somani, Guo, Jurafsky, and Shani (2026) "Beyond tokens: Concept-level training objectives for LLMs" (EACL), Zhang, Jurafsky, and Shani (2026) "Learning concepts, not tokens" (arXiv), Mizrahi, Shani, Stanovsky, Jurafsky, and Shahaf (2026, TACL), Ye, Shani, and Vitercik (2025) "Bridged clustering," and Zheng and Shani (2026). This self-citation density (7+ of ~45 references are the author's own prior work) is expected for a position paper staking out a research agenda the author has been building toward, but a reader should note the taxonomy is partly a retrospective framing of the author's own research program alongside others' work, which is disclosed transparently in the text (e.g., "Recent work demonstrates the feasibility of this perspective, including...") rather than hidden.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core contribution — a clean two-axis taxonomy (pipeline stage × internal/external origin) — is a genuinely useful organizing device that the paper itself notes has not existed before ("these approaches have emerged independently, without a unified framework"). It's a modest but real contribution: naming an implicit design space explicitly, not a new algorithm or finding.

**Rigor**: As a position paper, "rigor" here means honesty and completeness of the survey, not experimental controls. The paper is reasonably careful to hedge — it explicitly states Table 1 entries "should not be interpreted as claiming that each method learns human concepts in the cognitive sense," and the Limitations section admits the grid "is illustrative and almost certainly incomplete, particularly for the sparser cells." One paper-specific weak point: the "Externally-grounded / Objective" cell in Table 1 is explicitly marked "Sparse" by the authors themselves — a self-acknowledged gap in the taxonomy's own evidence base rather than a claim papered over.

**Reproducibility**: Not applicable in the traditional sense (no code, data, or experiments to reproduce). The taxonomy itself is not falsifiable or independently checkable beyond re-reading the ~45 cited papers and agreeing/disagreeing with their cell placement — a structural limitation of position papers in general, which the author does not claim otherwise.

**Significance**: The argument that "inference-time concept-awareness is underexplored" is plausible but is asserted mainly from the sparsity of Table 1's Inference row (3 internal citations: Shani et al. 2023, Mizrahi et al. 2026, Jacob et al. 2023 — two of which are the author's own work) rather than from a systematic literature search establishing that sparsity is real and not an artifact of the paper's own citation choices.

**Red flags**: None indicating overclaiming of empirical results, since none are claimed. The main thing a reader should weigh is the moderate self-citation rate (the author is a co-author on roughly 1 in 6 references, including several of the "recent work demonstrates the feasibility of this perspective" examples used to motivate the position) — not dishonest, since it's disclosed, but it means the "landscape" being mapped is partly the author's own prior research trajectory rather than a fully independent literature scan.

## Strengths

- Clear, simple, memorable two-axis framework (pipeline stage × internal/external origin) that plausibly helps researchers locate their own work and spot underexplored combinations (e.g., inference-stage, externally-grounded methods).
- Honest and specific self-limitation: explicitly flags the "Externally-grounded / Objective" cell as "Sparse" rather than papering over a thin part of its own taxonomy, and the Limitations section explicitly declines to adjudicate which pipeline stage/origin is "best," citing setting-dependent tradeoffs (compute, auditability, tolerance for architectural change).
- Draws a precise and useful distinction between "operating above the token level" (efficiency-motivated, e.g., hourglass transformers) and "operating over human concepts" (semantically motivated) — a conflation the paper explicitly warns against with a concrete counter-example (Nawrot et al. 2022's hierarchical transformers).
- Connects concept-bottleneck-style architectures (Koh et al. 2020, originally vision/tabular) to LLM-specific analogues (Sun et al. 2025) and to knowledge-graph-fusion literature that rarely gets cited alongside "concept" papers, surfacing genuine cross-community connections.

## Limitations

- No new experiments, benchmarks, or empirical validation of the taxonomy's own claims (e.g., that inference-stage methods are "comparatively underexplored") beyond citation counting in a self-constructed, admittedly non-exhaustive table.
- The paper does not propose or evaluate a concrete "designed" concept-aware architecture — it stops at mapping the design space and naming open challenges, explicitly declining to adjudicate which cell in Table 1 is preferable for any given application.
- Moderate self-citation (7+ of ~45 references are the author's own work), which the paper discloses but which means part of the "existing landscape" being surveyed is the author's own research agenda.
- The "found vs. designed" distinction leans heavily on one empirical result (SAE feature seed-instability from Gerasimov et al. 2026 and Paulo & Belrose 2025) that the paper itself notes has "focused on post-hoc interpretability methods," leaving open — as the paper itself admits — whether concept bottlenecks, concept-aware objectives, or graph-integrated architectures would show the same instability if tested the same way.

## Relevance

这篇立场论文本身不含实验，但其 4×2 分类框架和四条开放挑战（组合性、found-vs-designed 判据、概念来源取舍、跨阶段基准缺失）对做可解释性、概念瓶颈模型或知识增强 LLM 架构设计的研究者是一份有用的文献地图和研究选题参考，尤其是它指出的"推理阶段概念感知方法稀缺"这一空白点可能值得关注。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the seed-instability methodology used for sparse autoencoders (Gerasimov et al. 2026; Paulo and Belrose 2025) be extended to test whether concept bottleneck models, concept-level training objectives, or knowledge-graph-fused architectures are similarly seed-unstable, or do "designed" concepts actually show the stability the paper hypothesizes they would?
- Is the apparent sparsity of the Inference row in Table 1 a genuine research gap, or partly an artifact of the paper's own (admittedly non-exhaustive) citation selection — would a systematic literature search surface substantially more inference-time concept-aware methods under different terminology, mirroring the paper's own point about externally-grounded methods being mislabeled across communities?
- What would a concrete compositional (rather than point-in-embedding-space or graph-node) concept representation look like in a trained LLM, and could the "bridging" approach of Ye et al. (2025) between embedding and graph representations be extended toward Coecke et al.'s (2010) compositional distributional semantics?
- What would a shared benchmark spanning pre-, mid-, and post-training concept interventions actually measure to make conceptual fidelity, stability, compositionality, and downstream utility comparable across such structurally different methods (a bottleneck layer vs. a KG-fusion module vs. a post-hoc SAE)?
