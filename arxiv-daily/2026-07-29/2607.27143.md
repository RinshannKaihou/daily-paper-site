> **一句话总结：** 该论文用 15 个 OpenML 不平衡表格数据集、7 种分类器、3 种概率校准方法共 3,150 次实验证明：标准边际保形预测（marginal CP）在极端不平衡下少数类覆盖率会崩溃到 30.5%（aps_failure 数据集上甚至不足 1%），而类条件（Mondrian）CP 能将其恢复到 92.2%（比边际 CP 提升 61.7 个百分点，p=1.52×10⁻⁸²），并进一步与成本敏感弃权机制结合，在 Crev=0.5 时把期望决策成本比默认点预测器降低 54.1%、比贝叶斯成本调优阈值降低 38.6%。

> **TL;DR:** This benchmark paper shows that standard marginal conformal prediction (CP) satisfies its global 90% coverage target while catastrophically under-covering the minority class (average 30.5% across 315 dataset-model runs on 15 OpenML imbalanced tabular datasets, dropping below 1% on `aps_failure`, 58:1 imbalance). Class-conditional (Mondrian) CP restores minority coverage to 92.2% (+61.7 points, p=1.52×10⁻⁸²), and coupling it with a cost-controlled abstention rule that defers multi-label/empty prediction sets to human review cuts expected decision cost by 54.1% versus a default τ=0.5 point classifier and 38.6% versus a Bayes cost-tuned threshold at a review cost of Crev=0.5.

## 核心贡献

- 系统性量化边际 CP 在不平衡数据上的"少数类覆盖赤字"：跨 15 个数据集、7 个模型、3 种校准方法共 315 组合，边际 CP 平均少数类覆盖率仅 30.5%（目标 90%），在 aps_failure（58:1 不平衡）等极端场景下甚至跌破 1%。
- 证明类条件（Mondrian）CP 能在所有测试模型和数据集上系统性恢复有效的少数类覆盖，平均提升 61.7 个百分点（p<10⁻⁸⁰，配对 Wilcoxon 符号秩检验），并通过 Friedman-Nemenyi 临界差异图确认这一差距具有统计显著性而非个别数据集的偶然现象。
- 提出成本可控弃权框架：把 Mondrian 预测集中的多标签歧义集（|Ĉ(X)|≠1，包括空集和双标签集）路由给人工专家复核，在非对称成本矩阵（C_FN、C_FP、C_rev）和人工错误率 ε_hum 下推导出解析形式的"盈亏平衡"复核成本阈值 C*_rev。
- 在 Crev=0.5 的适中复核成本下，成本可控 Mondrian CP 相比默认点分类器降低 54.1% 期望成本，相比贝叶斯成本调优阈值降低 38.6%（均通过配对 Wilcoxon 检验，p 值分别低至 10⁻⁴⁰ 和 10⁻⁷²量级）；并给出信用违约、工业设备故障、医疗再入院三个真实案例研究验证实用性。

## Key Contributions

- Empirical quantification of the minority-coverage deficit in marginal conformal prediction: across 315 dataset×model×calibration combinations spanning 15 imbalanced OpenML tabular datasets, marginal CP achieves only 30.5% average minority-class coverage against a 90% target, collapsing below 1% on the most extreme-imbalance dataset (`aps_failure`, 58:1).
- Demonstration that class-conditional (Mondrian) CP systematically restores valid minority coverage across every tested base model and dataset, yielding an average +61.7 percentage-point gain over marginal CP (p=1.52×10⁻⁸², paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test), confirmed model-agnostic via a per-base-model heatmap (Figure 6) and statistically dominant via a Friedman–Nemenyi critical-difference ranking (Figure 4).
- A cost-controlled abstention framework that routes non-singleton Mondrian prediction sets (multi-label ambiguity or empty sets) to human review, integrating an asymmetric cost matrix (C_FN, C_FP, C_rev) and a noisy human-error rate ε_hum, with a closed-form derivation of the break-even human-review-cost threshold C*_rev below which deferral beats calibrated-threshold point classification.
- Empirical cost reduction: at a moderate review cost (Crev = 0.5), cost-controlled Mondrian CP cuts mean per-instance decision cost by 54.1% relative to a default τ=0.5 point predictor and 38.6% relative to a Bayes cost-tuned threshold, validated with three domain case studies (credit default, industrial component failure, hospital readmission).

## Methodology

The paper formalizes a binary cost-sensitive decision problem with asymmetric misclassification costs C_FP, C_FN and a per-instance human review cost C_rev, then compares three conformal set-construction strategies. **Marginal CP** computes a single non-conformity quantile q_marg over the calibration set using score S(X,y)=1−P̂(Y=y|X); because the score distribution is dominated by majority-class samples under severe imbalance, q_marg approaches the majority quantile and drives minority coverage toward zero even though global marginal coverage is satisfied by construction. **Class-conditional (Mondrian) CP** instead computes independent quantiles q_0, q_1 within each class-specific calibration subset, guaranteeing P(Y∈Ĉ(X)|Y=y) ≥ 1−α for both classes. A **cost-controlled abstention rule** then maps prediction-set outputs to actions: singleton sets {0} or {1} become automated decisions, while multi-label ({0,1}) or empty (∅) sets — signaling high non-conformity across both classes — trigger deferral to a human reviewer at cost C_rev (or C_rev + ε_hum·(C_FN·1[Y=1] + C_FP·1[Y=0]) under a noisy human oracle). The authors derive a closed-form break-even threshold C*_rev (Eq. 13) by equating expected per-instance cost under the cost-controlled conformal predictor against a Bayes-optimal point classifier at threshold τ*=C_FP/(C_FN+C_FP), expressed in terms of the false-positive/false-negative rates of each approach and the observed abstention rate r_abs.

Experiments span 15 real-world imbalanced tabular datasets from OpenML (Table 1: `uci_default`, `bank_marketing`, `adult`, `aps_failure`, `diabetes130us`, `miniboone`, `fraud`, `mammography`, `sick_numeric`, `wilt`, `ozone_level_8hr`, `seismic_bumps`, `pc1`, `oil_spill`, `credit_g`), with imbalance ratios ranging from 2.3:1 to 580:1 (`fraud`, 0.17% minority prevalence) and sample sizes from under 1,000 to over 284,000. Seven base classifiers are evaluated (HistGradientBoosting, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Extra Trees, Gradient Boosting, AdaBoost, Gaussian Naive Bayes) under three probability-calibration regimes (uncalibrated, Platt/sigmoid, isotonic regression), each run across 10 fixed random seeds, giving 3,150 total experimental runs (15×7×3×10). Baselines compared against cost-controlled Mondrian CP include a default τ=0.5 point predictor, a Bayes cost-tuned threshold τ*, standard marginal CP, a confidence rejector (defers when max posterior probability < 1−γ), and a conformal-risk-control rejector (Angelopoulos et al.). Statistical significance is assessed with two-tailed paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p<0.05) and 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over 1,000 resamples; the experimental pipeline is stated to be fully deterministic given the fixed seed set {7,19,31,42,101,202,303,404,505,606}, and code/data are claimed to be publicly released (OpenML datasets, GitHub repo).

## Results & Highlights

- **Minority-class coverage (Table 2, averaged over 315 dataset-model-calibration combinations, target 1−α=90%):** marginal CP achieves only 30.5% minority coverage vs. Mondrian CP's 92.2% — a 61.71-percentage-point gain (95% CI [58.20%, 65.22%], paired Wilcoxon p=1.52×10⁻⁸²). On `aps_failure` (58:1 imbalance) under gradient-boosted models, marginal CP minority coverage falls below 1% (0.8%), while Mondrian CP restores it to 89.8%, essentially hitting the 90% target.
- **Mean per-instance decision cost (Table 2):** Bayes cost-tuned threshold (τ*) = 0.340; marginal CP = 0.413; APS CP = 0.380; RAPS CP = 0.444; Mondrian CP with raw (uncontrolled) sets = 0.143; cost-controlled Mondrian CP = 0.021 (lowest of all methods), though this comes with a 71.9% abstention rate and average set size 1.71, meaning nearly three-quarters of instances are deferred to a human reviewer.
- **Cost reduction at moderate review cost (Section 5.2, Crev=0.5, C_FP=1.0, C_FN=10.0):** cost-controlled Mondrian CP achieves mean cost 0.313 vs. 0.682 for the default point classifier (54.1% reduction) and vs. 0.510 for Bayes cost-tuned thresholding (38.6% reduction); paired Wilcoxon tests confirm the improvement over Bayes thresholding (p=2.93×10⁻⁴⁰), confidence rejectors (p=1.18×10⁻⁷²), and risk-controlled rejectors (p=5.12×10⁻⁵⁴).
- **Case study, `aps_failure` (industrial, 58:1 imbalance):** marginal CP minority (failure) coverage < 1%; Mondrian CP restores it to 89.8%; cost-controlled abstention routes 34.1% of ambiguous cases to technicians and reduces expected breakdown costs by 65.3% relative to Bayes threshold tuning.
- **Case study, `uci_default` (credit default, 3.5:1 imbalance):** marginal CP yields only 57.7% minority coverage; Mondrian CP restores it to 90.3%; cost-controlled deferral at Crev=0.5 reduces expected default losses by 13.6% compared to Bayes threshold tuning.
- **Break-even sensitivity (Section 5.4):** as false-negative penalty C_FN increases from 5.0 to 50.0, the break-even review threshold C*_rev rises from 0.82 to 4.35 — i.e., under harsher asymmetric penalties, paying substantially more for human review remains cost-justified. Net savings persist provided the noisy human error rate ε_hum < 0.18 under standard operating parameters.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The paper does not introduce a new conformal-prediction algorithm — Mondrian (class-conditional) CP dates to Vovk et al.'s original framework, and cost-sensitive decision theory and selective classification/rejection are both well-established. The contribution is empirical and integrative: a systematic multi-domain benchmark connecting class-conditional coverage guarantees to a cost-controlled human-deferral policy, plus a closed-form break-even threshold derivation (Eq. 13). This is useful applied/systems work but incremental relative to the cited theory it builds on (Vovk 2005, Angelopoulos & Bates 2021, conformal risk control).

**Rigor:** The experimental design is commendably broad (15 datasets × 7 models × 3 calibrations × 10 seeds = 3,150 runs) with proper paired statistical testing (Wilcoxon signed-rank, bootstrap CIs, Friedman–Nemenyi critical-difference diagrams). However, there is a concrete internal inconsistency worth flagging: Section 5.3's narrative text states isotonic calibration "reduc[es] average set size |Ĉ(X)| from 1.42 to 1.35 on random forest models and lower[s] abstention rates by 6.8 percentage points," but the accompanying Table 3 shows the opposite for the Random Forest/Extra Trees family — average set size *increases* from 1.22 (uncalibrated) to 1.40 (isotonic), and abstention rate *increases* from 26.2% to 41.3% (+15.1 points, not a 6.8-point reduction). The same table shows isotonic calibration on the Linear/LogReg family spiking mean cost from 0.178 (sigmoid) to 0.351 — roughly double — which is not discussed or explained in the text. Neither discrepancy is acknowledged in the paper, which undercuts confidence in the calibration-effect narrative in Section 5.3. Separately, the headline "97.7% minority coverage at mean cost 0.021" for cost-controlled Mondrian (Table 2) is achieved at a 71.9% abstention rate — i.e., the cost win is largely an artifact of pushing most decisions onto a (in this benchmark, cost-free-per-error) human oracle, not of the conformal method itself being cheaper to run automatically; the paper's own Section 6.2 acknowledges finite human review capacity (M/M/k queuing) as a deployment constraint but does not connect this back to the 71.9% figure.

**Reproducibility:** Fixed seeds, stated deterministic pipeline, public OpenML datasets, and a claimed public GitHub repository are all reproducibility positives. All baselines (Bayes threshold, confidence rejector, conformal risk control rejector) are standard and clearly specified with formulas, which aids replication.

**Significance:** The practical framing (break-even C*_rev thresholds, domain case studies in credit, industrial safety, and healthcare) is a genuine strength for practitioners deciding whether to route ambiguous cases to human review. The scope is limited to binary classification on tabular data — the paper's own Limitations section (5 threats to validity) candidly flags non-stationary distribution shift, multi-class combinatorial complexity, sparse-calibration quantile variance under n₁<30, static (non-adaptive) modeling of human fatigue/error rate, and the tabular-only feature scope as open problems, which is honest but also means the "actionable principles" claimed in the abstract are narrower than the title's "decision support systems" framing might suggest.

## Strengths

- Very large, systematic experimental matrix (3,150 runs) with proper paired statistical testing and multiple robustness checks (critical-difference ranking, per-base-model breakdown, calibration-method breakdown).
- Directly addresses a practically important and under-examined failure mode: marginal CP's silent minority-class coverage collapse under imbalance, demonstrated concretely (aps_failure < 1% coverage) rather than only theoretically.
- Closed-form break-even threshold derivation (Eq. 13) gives a genuinely actionable, dataset-specific decision rule for practitioners deciding on human-review budgets, distinct from generic "abstain when uncertain" heuristics.
- Real domain case studies (credit default, industrial failure, hospital readmission) ground the abstract statistical results in interpretable, dollar/operational-cost terms.

## Limitations

- Internal inconsistency between Section 5.3's prose and Table 3's actual numbers regarding the effect of isotonic calibration on set size and abstention rate (see Critical Assessment) — the paper's calibration-effect narrative should not be taken at face value without checking the table.
- Scope restricted to binary classification; the paper explicitly notes multi-class extension introduces K(K−1) combinatorial cost-matrix complexity and hierarchical Mondrian partitioning challenges not solved here.
- Sparse minority calibration samples (n₁<30) under extreme imbalance are flagged by the authors as inflating quantile estimation variance, which is a live vulnerability for exactly the highest-imbalance datasets (e.g., `fraud`, 580:1) the paper highlights as motivating examples.
- The human error rate ε_hum and review cost C_rev are modeled as static constants; the paper's own discussion (Section 6.3) notes real human reviewers exhibit cognitive fatigue under high abstention volume, which is not incorporated into the cost model, and the M/M/k queuing capacity constraint (Section 6.2) is discussed only qualitatively, not integrated into the main empirical results.
- Cost-controlled Mondrian's headline low mean cost in Table 2 relies on a 71.9% abstention rate, which may be operationally unrealistic in high-throughput settings; the paper does not report cost figures at fixed, realistic abstention-rate caps for its main table.

## Relevance

该论文对不平衡数据下的高风险决策系统（信用风控、欺诈检测、医疗、工业安全）具有较强的实践参考价值，尤其是把保形预测的覆盖保证与人机协同弃权成本结合的思路，可用于评估或改进现有异常检测/决策支持系统中的置信度校准与复核策略设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the Section 5.3 isotonic-calibration claim (set size 1.42→1.35, abstention −6.8pp) be reconciled with Table 3's opposite trend — was this a typo, a different sub-slice of the data, or an error in one of the two artifacts?
- How does cost-controlled Mondrian CP perform under a realistic, capacity-constrained abstention budget (e.g., capped at 10-20% of traffic) rather than the unconstrained 71.9% abstention regime reported in Table 2?
- Does the break-even threshold C*_rev derivation and its sensitivity conclusions hold under non-stationary distribution shift, given that the entire framework assumes calibration-test exchangeability that the authors themselves flag as a key threat to validity?
- How would the framework extend to multi-class or multi-label settings (e.g., multi-class medical diagnosis) where the authors acknowledge combinatorial cost-matrix growth, and would the break-even formula remain tractable?
- Would the reported cost reductions and coverage gains replicate on non-tabular high-stakes data (clinical notes, medical imaging) where the authors note representation learning would need to precede non-conformity scoring?
