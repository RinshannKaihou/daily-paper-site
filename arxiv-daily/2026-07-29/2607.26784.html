Agentic RL · Cross-Task Skill Evolution

One policy alternates between solving tasks and curating a shared skill document that alone carries lessons to the next task.

一句话总结：SkillRise 用单一策略在"解题"和"把轨迹提炼进一份演化中的技能文档"两个角色间交替，并把当前任务的奖励只记给解题、把后续任务的折扣回报只记给技能整理，在 Qwen3-4B 上于 ALFWorld/WebShop/ScienceWorld 三个基准的 Pass@1 分别达到 85.9%/84.4%/54.6%，比最强基线 GiGPO 高出 2.3/7.1/8.5 个百分点，且仅用同类多阶段技能流水线六分之一的训练时间。

Key metric
6.0×
SkillRise matches RetroAgent's 85.9% ALFWorld accuracy (Fig. 4) while running at just 1.0x relative time vs. RetroAgent's 6.0x and SkillRL's 4.3x — and it beats SkillRL's accuracy outright by +12.5pp (85.9% vs. 73.4%).
Why this matters

Gives agentic-RL researchers a cheap, end-to-end recipe for agents to accumulate reusable skills across tasks, with both efficiency and cross-task scaling empirically shown.

Critical edge

The SkillRL baseline reproduction substitutes Gemini-2.5-Pro as the teacher model instead of SkillRL's original configuration — it is not stated whether this was validated, which could tilt the reported 12.5pp accuracy gap and 4.3x runtime edge in Fig. 4.

Methodology
1 · SequenceSame-family task instances x=(x₁…x_K) ordered simple→complex from environment metadata; K=3 during training
→
2 · SolvePolicy generates trajectory τᵢ given (xᵢ, S_{i-1}); scored by immediate task reward rᵢ
→
3 · CurateSame policy rewrites the entire document Sᵢ from (S_{i-1}, τᵢ, rᵢ); raw trajectory discarded — the doc is the only channel forward
→
4 · CreditCuration scored by discounted future reward Σγ^(j-i)rⱼ, γ=0.6; role-aware GRPO compares only same-position, same-phase trials (N=8) before a clipped PPO-style update

Curation is prompted (Appendix A) to abstract away instance-specific object names/locations, ground every statement in what was actually observed, extract failure points as concrete pitfalls, and stay concise. During training, N=8 independent trials are sampled per sequence, each starting from an empty document S₀=∅.

Appendix B case study A failed "put two spraybottles in cabinet" attempt places only one instance; curation adds a general "repeat for each instance" rule, which the next, different-object task (peppershakers) then applies successfully — concrete evidence the document carries a transferable rule, not a memorized trajectory.

Setup: Qwen3-1.7B/4B backbones; ALFWorld (6 task families), WebShop, ScienceWorld; batch = 16 sequences × K=3 × N=8 trials (384 task plays/update, matched to baselines' 24×16 rollouts); actor LR=1e-6, temperature 0.7, FSDP+vLLM on 8×H800.

  • Model-scale robustness (Table 3, ALFWorld): SkillRise improves 78.1% (Qwen3-1.7B) → 85.9% (Qwen3-4B), a +7.8pp gain — larger than GRPO's +4.7pp and LaMer's +3.3pp — and its lead over the strongest baseline grows from 3.1pp at 1.7B to 6.2pp at 4B.
  • Discount-factor ablation (Fig. 3a): sweeping γ∈{0.3,0.4,0.6,0.7} for the cross-task discount converges training curves within ~1pp of each other — the method is not fragile to this hyperparameter.
  • No-curation ablation (Fig. 3b): removing inter-task document transfer (same K=3 sequences, same interaction budget) trails full SkillRise by roughly 3pp and trails GRPO's own improvement rate — isolating curation itself, not just cross-task training data, as the source of gains.
Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • Removes the external teacher, memory bank, and retrieval module required by prior multi-stage skill pipelines (SkillRL, RetroAgent), while matching or beating them on accuracy at a fraction of the runtime.
  • Decoupled credit assignment (Eq. 6–7) is a principled response to the real temporal mismatch between solving (evaluated immediately) and curation (pays off only later) — not an ad hoc reward-shaping trick — and the no-curation ablation confirms curation's own separate contribution.
  • Cross-task test-time scaling (each task attempted once, accuracy still rises with K) is a clean, hard-to-fake demonstration of genuine skill transfer rather than incidental multi-sampling benefit.
  • Consistent gains hold across two model scales (1.7B, 4B) and three structurally different environments (embodied household, e-commerce, scientific reasoning), backed by public code and fully reproduced prompts.

Limitations

  • Requires task-family metadata to build ordered cross-task sequences; automatic discovery of task relatedness in open-ended, unlabeled task streams remains unsolved and is explicitly flagged as out of scope.
  • Evaluated only up to 4B-parameter backbones (Qwen3-1.7B/4B) — scaling behavior at 10B+ is untested, and it's unclear whether the +7.8pp 1.7B→4B gain extrapolates.
  • Restricted to three text-based benchmarks with verifiable outcome rewards; no multimodal or real-world (GUI, robotics) evaluation.
  • SkillRL baseline reproduction substitutes Gemini-2.5-Pro as the teacher model rather than SkillRL's original configuration, which could affect the fairness of the Fig. 4 comparison.
  • Training sequence length is fixed at K=3; whether training directly on longer K would further amplify or saturate the test-time scaling trend is untested.
Critical assessment at a glance

Novelty

Collapsing skill extraction, curation, and application into one end-to-end RL policy with role-decoupled credit assignment is a clean simplification vs. multi-stage retrieval/memory pipelines. Role-aware group-relative optimization is a non-obvious fit for the dual-role setting.

Rigor

Targeted ablations isolate curation's value from related-task training alone, and a γ sweep checks hyperparameter sensitivity. But the SkillRL baseline's teacher-model substitution (Gemini-2.5-Pro) is not validated against SkillRL's original numbers.

Reproducibility

Sequence-construction criterion is described only at a high level ("greedily group diverse instances"). Code is publicly released and all prompts (task-solving + curation, all three environments) are reproduced in Appendix A.

Significance

The single-attempt cross-task scaling result rules out the "more samples per task" explanation. Matching RetroAgent's accuracy at 1/6 its runtime is practically meaningful given increasingly complex concurrent skill-pipeline work. Scope limits (Sec. 8) are honestly disclosed.

Questions for further exploration
  • Can the greedy simple-to-complex sequence construction be replaced with a learned or automatically-discovered task-relatedness signal, removing the dependency on environment-provided family metadata?
  • Does the cross-task test-time scaling trend (Fig. 2) continue past K=6, or does it saturate/degrade once the skill document grows long enough to strain the policy's context window?
  • How does SkillRise's skill document evolve when task sequences mix multiple distinct families in one rollout, rather than staying within one family?
  • Would training directly at longer K (e.g. 6 or 10) further widen the margin over GiGPO/GRPO, or does K=3 already capture most of the transferable signal?
  • How would the Fig. 4 efficiency/accuracy comparison change under SkillRL's originally specified teacher configuration instead of the substituted Gemini-2.5-Pro?
  • Does decoupled credit assignment generalize to sparser or delayed-reward settings (e.g. multi-day real-world workflows) where the discounted downstream signal for curation would be much noisier?
