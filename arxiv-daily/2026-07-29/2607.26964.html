"Feature Bagging Provides Stability"

Yuheng Ma, Qiang Sun · 29 July 2026 · arXiv:2607.26964
algorithmic stability feature bagging random forests ensemble learning high-dim statistics
Key Metric
102vs105
Bagging rounds needed for feature-side vs. instance-side stability at typical tabular scale (n,d) ~ (10⁵, 10²) — feature bagging converges roughly 1000× faster.
Why This Matters

Gives ensemble-learning and high-dimensional-statistics researchers a ready-made stability framework for tuning random forest hyperparameters like max_features and tree depth on principled grounds instead of trial and error.

Critical Edge

The theory's sharpest guarantees hold only for shallow recursion and a simplified "max-edge dyadic" tree model, not real CART/Gini splitting — and the bound provably loosens exactly in the deep-tree regime practitioners actually use.

Methodology

Define the dual instability

Feature instability (FI) := expected squared change in prediction when one feature is removed — the leave-one-feature-out mirror of classical leave-one-instance instability (II). Shared-seed and coupled-seed versions given.

Exact linear-model asymptotics

For bagged minimum-norm least-squares under Gaussian design (d/n→γ), random-matrix theory yields closed-form II/FI limits (Thm 3.1). Bagging shifts weight from "same-submodel" terms (which inherit a double-descent instability peak) to O(1/n) "cross-submodel" terms that flatten it.

Model-free recursive framework

For data-dependent, multi-round algorithms (random forward selection; a simplified "max-edge dyadic" random forest), a one-step Lipschitz-type condition propagates a stability bound through the recursion (Prop 4.4, Thms 4.5 & 4.7).

Empirical validation

Monte-Carlo simulations confirm the closed forms; a separate MARSadd + four real tabular datasets (diabetes, abalone, cpu_act, house_prices) study regresses FI/II against the train–test gap via R², mutual information, and partial mutual information.

Results & Highlights

On the MARSadd random-forest benchmark, adding FI to II roughly doubles the explained variance in the generalization gap — evidence that feature-axis instability carries information instance instability alone misses.

  • Robustness: across 160 estimator/dataset/residualizer combinations (96 synthetic + 64 real-tabular), FI's incremental contribution beyond II is positive in 100% of cases.
  • Random forward selection (Thm 4.5): bagged FI ≤ T²/(d−1)·q/(1−q) for T ≲ q⁻¹, vs. exactly FI = 2T/d for the non-bagged baseline — bagging wins whenever T ≲ (1−q)/q.
  • Linear model (Thm 3.1): the single-subsampled estimator shows a double-descent-shaped instability peak at the interpolation threshold γq=p; bagging (B=200) suppresses it, matching Monte-Carlo simulations essentially exactly.

Strengths

  • Clean, well-motivated new stability concept (FI), filling a genuine gap in algorithmic-stability literature that focuses almost exclusively on the instance axis.
  • Exact — not order-of-magnitude — asymptotic formulas for bagged linear regression, matching simulations closely across aspect ratios and subsampling rates.
  • Directly extends Soloff et al. (2024)'s instance-bagging guarantee, giving the ~10² vs ~10⁵ rounds comparison real, citable interpretability.
  • Broad robustness checks: 160 estimator/dataset/residualizer combinations all show FI's positive incremental contribution — not a cherry-picked configuration.

Limitations

  • Theorem 3.1's exact asymptotics assume isotropic Gaussian features/coefficients — no correlated-design theory or experiments, despite correlation being central to "why random forests work" debates.
  • The random-forest theorem (4.7) covers a simplified max-edge dyadic tree, not CART/Gini splitting; the gap to real forests is acknowledged but unquantified.
  • FI-to-generalization link rests on correlational R²/MI/PMI evidence on modest datasets, not a formal excess-risk bound — a gap the authors flag but don't close.
  • Recursive guarantees hold only in shallow-recursion regimes (T ≲ q⁻¹); the paper's own Figure 4(b) shows the bound loosening as depth grows.

Questions for Further Exploration

01How do the linear-model formulas change under correlated (non-isotropic) feature covariance — the regime feature bagging is usually argued to matter most?
02Can the max-edge dyadic forest result extend to genuine CART/Gini splitting, and would shallow trees still win the stability tradeoff?
03Does the FI–generalization link hold on larger, higher-dimensional, or non-tabular (image/text) benchmarks beyond MARSadd and four small UCI-style datasets?
04Can Proposition 4.8's finite-bagging concentration bound be sharpened beyond Hoeffding/union-bound, and would that shift the "10² vs 10⁵" practical comparison?
05Is there a path from FI to a formal excess-risk/generalization bound, closing the gap the authors themselves flag around unbounded linear-regression outputs?
