> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 CostAda，一种把"每步实际花费的 token 成本"和"剩余预算比例"直接嵌入信用分配公式的自适应搜索控制器，在 GLM-5 和 GPT-5.4 两个后端、16 组 benchmark-backbone 组合中的 12 组上，只用不到一半预算就达到了最强基线（AdaEvolve/EvoX）用满预算才能达到的最终质量。

> **TL;DR:** LLM-driven discovery systems (AlphaEvolve-style search over program/config candidates) typically allocate exploration credit based only on score progress, ignoring that different actions (short refinements vs. long-context guide calls) cost very different amounts of tokens. The authors prove this "cost-blind" credit assignment can provably forfeit almost all attainable quality as frontier count and cost heterogeneity grow (Proposition 1), then introduce CostAda, which divides progress by realized action cost and reweights local/global credit by the remaining budget ratio. Across 8 benchmarks (Circle Packing, Heilbronn Triangle/Convex, MinMaxDist, Third Autocorrelation, Signal Processing) and 2 backbones (GLM-5, GPT-5.4), CostAda beats AdaEvolve and EvoX baselines, e.g. on Signal Processing under GPT-5.4 CostAda scores 0.7965 vs. AdaEvolve's 0.7011 (+13.6%), and reaches the strongest baseline's full-budget quality using at most half the budget in 12 of 16 benchmark–backbone pairs.

## 核心贡献

- 将 LLM 驱动的迭代式发现（inference-time search，如 AlphaEvolve/AdaEvolve/EvoX 一类系统）在**显式搜索侧 token 预算**下形式化为一个带成本约束的最优化问题，并证明：只根据"分数进展"分配信用的控制器（cost-blind credit）在成本异质性增大时，期望只能达到最优预算目标值的 (1/K + 1/κ)(1+o(1)) 倍，几乎可以任意接近于零（Proposition 1，附录 A 给出构造性证明）。
- 提出**成本校准前沿效用**（cost-calibrated frontier utility）：把局部/全局进展的加权和除以"归一化实际步成本"，并让局部-全局权重 λ_t 随剩余预算比例 ρ_t 从 0 线性升到 1——预算充裕时偏向局部前沿开发，预算将尽时偏向全局最优改进。
- 将该效用原则统一实例化到三层控制：局部探索强度（决定采样广度/上下文集大小）、前沿分配（用类 UCB 规则按"全局增益/实际成本"排序前沿）、预算门控的战术介入（仅当局部搜索不再把预算转化为进展、且剩余预算能负担引导调用时才触发）。
- 在 GLM-5、GPT-5.4 两个价格体系差异约 4-8 倍的后端上，用统一的搜索脚手架、评估器和预算协议做 8 个 benchmark 的对比实验，并做了组件消融（去掉成本校准/剩余预算条件/介入门控分别会降低质量）。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes LLM discovery under an explicit search-side token budget and proves (Proposition 1, Appendix A) that any *cost-blind* controller — one whose action distribution depends only on the history of actions/scores, never on realized cost — can attain at most a (1/K + 1/κ)(1+o(1)) fraction of the optimal budgeted objective as the number of frontiers K and the cost ratio κ grow, while ranking frontiers by gain-per-realized-cost attains (1−o(1)) OPT.
- Introduces **cost-calibrated frontier utility**: u_t = (λ_t·g_t + (1−λ_t)·δ_t) / d_t, where g_t/δ_t are global/local normalized score gains, d_t = 1 + λ_t^c·φ_t is a realized-cost divisor (φ_t = log(1+normalized step cost)), and λ_t = 1 − ρ_t rises from 0 to 1 as the remaining-budget ratio ρ_t drains — so cost penalization is soft early and strict near the horizon.
- Instantiates one credit principle across three coupled controls: budget-gated local exploration intensity, UCB-style frontier allocation ranked by global gain per realized cost, and budgeted tactic intervention gated by affordability, stagnation, and low-yield-spending predicates (with guide backoff and post-success consolidation).
- Empirically validates on an 8-benchmark suite (geometric packing, extremal geometry, additive combinatorics, executable program optimization) against two contemporary adaptive baselines (AdaEvolve, EvoX) under two backbones (GLM-5, GPT-5.4) with matched dollar-denominated budgets, plus a full component-ablation study (Appendix G) and a step-by-step controller trace (Appendix B.6) for interpretability.

## Methodology

The paper frames discovery as population-level search over a candidate space P with a deterministic evaluator F. At each iteration the controller selects a frontier k_t (a local archive with a local-best score), samples a parent/context from it, invokes a search-side LLM to generate a candidate, and observes both the score f_t and the realized LLM cost c_t (excluding deterministic evaluator execution). Cumulative cost C_t defines the remaining-budget ratio ρ_t = max(0, 1 − C_t/B); a normalized step cost c̃_t = c_t/(c̄+ε_c) makes control scale-invariant to a benchmark-family reference cost c̄. The objective is to maximize the expected best-so-far score at the first iteration where cumulative cost crosses the nominal budget B.

CostAda computes, per candidate, a local gain δ_t (vs. the selected frontier's local best) and a global gain g_t (vs. the global best-so-far y_{t−1}), both normalized by max(|f_t|, |prior|, 1) to stay stable near zero/sign changes. These are combined into the cost-calibrated utility u_t (Eq. 1) using a budget-dependent mixture weight λ_t = 1−ρ_t and a cost-penalty weight λ_t^c = max(λ_t, λ_min) that floors early-run cost pressure; the divisor d_t = 1 + λ_t^c·log(1+c̃_t) means costly low-gain steps get progressively less credit as the ledger drains. An EMA-smoothed utility H_t^(k) sets local exploration intensity I_t^(k) = I_min + (I_max−I_min)·ρ_{t−1}/(1+√(H_{t−1}^(k)+ε_H)) (after a short bootstrap for new frontiers): high recent utility narrows sampling toward exploitation, low utility broadens it, and shrinking budget suppresses expensive exploration overall. Frontier allocation uses only global gain per realized cost, r_t^(k) = g_t/d_t, smoothed into R_t^(k) and combined with a UCB-style optimism bonus scaled by ρ_t (so exploration bonuses shrink as budget depletes) to pick the next frontier (Auer et al. 2002-style rule). Budgeted tactic intervention (buying a higher-level "guide"/tactic call) fires only when an affordability predicate (enough remaining budget for the guide plus ordinary follow-up generations) conjoins with either a sustained-stagnation predicate or a low-yield-spending predicate — with additional bookkeeping (Appendix B.4) for consolidation windows after a successful guide cycle and backoff after an unproductive one, switching between "breakthrough" and "refinement" intervention modes. All controller constants are fixed per benchmark family (not tuned per benchmark), and the full loop is given as Algorithm 1.

## Results & Highlights

- **Final quality at matched budgets (Table 1):** Under GLM-5, CostAda attains the strongest mean benchmark-objective value on all 8 benchmarks vs. AdaEvolve and EvoX (e.g., Circle Packing: CostAda 2.6306 ± 0.0054 vs. AdaEvolve 2.5617 ± 0.0773 and EvoX 2.5717 ± 0.0752; Signal Processing: CostAda 0.7054 ± 0.0124 vs. EvoX 0.6592 ± 0.0573, a +7.0% improvement over the stronger baseline). Under GPT-5.4, CostAda's Signal Processing score (0.7965 ± 0.0346) beats AdaEvolve's 0.7011 ± 0.0128 by 13.6%.
- **Ties/near-misses reveal honest limits:** On MinMaxDist (n=16, d=2) under GPT-5.4, both baselines plateau at exactly 13.00 ± 0.00 while CostAda reaches 12.93 ± 0.06 (matching the human reference value of 12.89 on its best run); on Heilbronn Convex under GPT-5.4, CostAda ties EvoX at 0.0252 mean.
- **Budget efficiency (Section 5.3, Appendix E):** CostAda reaches the strongest baseline's full-budget quality using at most half the budget in 12 of 16 benchmark–backbone pairs. At a quarter of the budget, CostAda's mean normalized score already averages 0.8753 (GLM-5) / 0.8859 (GPT-5.4), exceeding the strongest baseline's *full-budget* averages of 0.8725 / 0.8800. Over the full budget path, CostAda attains the highest normalized AUC on 15 of 16 benchmark–backbone pairs, averaging 0.8626 (GLM-5) vs. the strongest baseline's 0.8060, and 0.8851 (GPT-5.4) vs. 0.8437.
- **Robustness checks:** Independently re-running CostAda at nominal B=0.5 (rather than reading the midpoint of a B=1 trajectory) still beats the strongest baseline mean in all 16 benchmark–budget cells (Appendix F, Table 8). Runs complete their budget crossing with only 1.1–2.5% average overshoot of the nominal budget across both backbones (Appendix J), so the efficiency gains aren't an artifact of stopping-rule slack.
- **Component ablations (Appendix G, Tables 9–10):** On GLM-5 Signal Processing, removing cost calibration lowers BestScore@0.5B, BestScore@B, and normalized AUC by 4.93%, 3.89%, and 3.45% respectively (Full CostAda: 0.6682/0.7121/0.6583 vs. No-cost-calibration: 0.6352/0.6844/0.6355); removing remaining-budget conditioning or intervention gating similarly degrades all three metrics on all three ablated benchmarks (Circle Packing, Heilbronn Triangle, Signal Processing) under both backbones.
- **Variance/stability:** On Circle Packing Rect under GLM-5, CostAda pairs the strongest mean with by far the smallest cross-run standard deviation (0.0081) against the two baselines' 0.1635 and 0.2563 — evidence that cost-blind credit lets some runs sink budget into unproductive branches.
- No quantitative failure cases are reported for CostAda itself, but the paper is transparent that AdaEvolve/EvoX each lead CostAda's paired baseline on roughly half of the 8 GLM-5 benchmarks in the "stronger single-baseline" framing (Section 5.2), even though CostAda's mean still leads whichever baseline is stronger per benchmark.

However, all three systems — CostAda, AdaEvolve, and EvoX — are very recent (2026) arXiv preprints, none of them peer-reviewed at time of writing, so this is a comparison among bleeding-edge, contemporaneous, unvalidated methods rather than against an established/stable baseline. The paper's AI Use Statement discloses that OpenAI Codex was used to polish language and presentation. Code is released at https://github.com/Forrest-Stone/CostAda.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — dividing progress credit by realized cost and conditioning the local/global mixture on remaining budget — is a natural and previously under-explored extension of prior "adaptive-to-progress" controllers (AdaEvolve, EvoX, DeltaEvolve) that credit iterations/samples as interchangeable units regardless of token cost. The formal impossibility result (Proposition 1) is a genuine contribution: it's a clean, if simple, two-scale adversarial construction (one cheap frontier vs. K−1 frontiers at κ× the cost, equal per-step gains) showing cost-blind credit is asymptotically bounded by (1/K+1/κ), and it correctly identifies that the failure mode belongs to a *class* of controllers (any cost-blind rule) rather than being a strawman critique of one specific baseline.

**Rigor:** The proof in Appendix A is a clean adversarial-instance argument but is a worst-case construction with equal gains across frontiers — it demonstrates a possibility, not that AdaEvolve/EvoX actually suffer this failure mode in practice on the tested benchmarks; the empirical section is what has to carry that claim, and it does so only indirectly (via aggregate score/AUC gaps, not by directly showing AdaEvolve/EvoX spending disproportionately on a high-cost, low-yield frontier). Each benchmark–method cell is averaged over only 3 independent runs, and several standard deviations are large relative to the mean differences (e.g., GLM-5 Signal Processing AdaEvolve: 0.6022 ± 0.0479, EvoX: 0.6592 ± 0.0573) — no significance testing is reported, so some of the narrower margins in Table 1 (e.g., Heilbronn Convex under GLM-5: CostAda 0.0263 vs. AdaEvolve 0.0229, both with overlapping-ish std) should be read cautiously despite being nominally "boldable" by mean.

**Reproducibility:** Strong — the paper gives explicit formulas for every control signal (Eqs. and Appendix B), a full controller-constant table (Table 3), an iteration-by-iteration worked trace on one real run (Appendix B.6, Table 4), fixed OpenRouter per-token prices for cost accounting, and a public GitHub repo. This is unusually thorough documentation for a controller with this many moving parts (evidence-state variables ν_t, L_t, Z_t, o_t, w_t, κ_t, 𝒢_t).

**Significance:** The efficiency framing (reach baseline's full-budget quality at ≤50% budget) is a meaningful practical claim for token-cost-sensitive discovery pipelines, and the two-backbone, price-heterogeneous (4× input / 8× output price gap between GLM-5 and GPT-5.4) design is a genuine strength — it shows the method isn't tuned to one pricing regime.

**Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) The component ablation (Appendix G) covers only 3 of the paper's 8 benchmarks (Circle Packing, Heilbronn Triangle, Signal Processing) — the full benchmark suite's ablation behavior is not shown, so it's unclear whether cost calibration/remaining-budget conditioning/intervention gating each matter uniformly or whether some benchmarks are ablation-insensitive. (2) Both compared baselines are themselves unpublished, few-months-old preprints from a single other lab, so this is not a comparison against a mature, widely-validated SOTA — there is no comparison to, e.g., vanilla AlphaEvolve-style search without any adaptive control, which would help isolate how much of the gain comes from "cost-aware credit" specifically vs. "having any adaptive controller at all." (3) The controller has many hand-set constants (Table 3: I_min/I_max, α, γ, λ_min, c_ucb, ε_g, ε_inc, ν_0, ε_c, ε_H, ĉ_guide) that are "fixed within each benchmark family and not retuned per benchmark" — the paper doesn't report sensitivity to these constants, so it's unclear how much tuning went into picking values that work across 8 disparate benchmarks.

## Strengths

- Clear, provable motivation (Proposition 1) for why cost-blind credit assignment is fundamentally flawed under heterogeneous action costs, not just an empirically-observed heuristic gap.
- Single unifying control principle (cost-calibrated frontier utility) cleanly instantiated across three distinct control decisions (local intensity, frontier allocation, intervention timing), rather than three separately-motivated heuristics bolted together.
- Evaluated across two backbones with substantially different, realistic per-token pricing, denominating budgets in dollars rather than raw token/iteration counts — a more faithful proxy for real deployment cost constraints.
- Thorough reproducibility artifacts: full algorithm listing, controller-constant table, worked iteration trace, ablation study, and public code release.
- Robustness checks beyond the headline result (independent B=0.5 reruns, budget-overshoot accounting) that pre-empt some obvious "is this an artifact of the stopping rule" objections.

## Limitations

- No dedicated "Limitations" section in the paper; limitations must be inferred from the ablation/appendix framing rather than stated as first-class caveats by the authors.
- Ablation study covers only 3 of 8 benchmarks, leaving the generality of the three-component decomposition (cost calibration vs. remaining-budget conditioning vs. intervention gating) partially unverified.
- All comparisons are against two other very recent (2026), non-peer-reviewed adaptive controllers from a single other research group; no comparison against a simple non-adaptive or purely iteration-count-budgeted baseline.
- Only 3 repeated runs per setting, with visibly large standard deviations on several benchmarks, and no statistical significance testing reported.
- Controller has a substantial number of fixed hyperparameters/thresholds (Table 3) whose sensitivity across benchmarks is not analyzed.
- The theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) is an asymptotic, worst-case adversarial-instance result; it does not directly translate into a guarantee for the empirical benchmark suite used.

## Relevance

该工作与"LLM 驱动的科学/算法发现"及"推理时搜索的自适应资源分配"密切相关，其"按剩余预算和实际成本校准信用"的思路可迁移到任何多分支/多智能体搜索或 test-time compute 分配场景（例如 agent 工具调用预算、tree-search token 分配），值得关注其开源代码中信用公式的具体实现细节。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does CostAda perform against a non-adaptive baseline (e.g., uniform round-robin frontier selection with a fixed token cutoff) to isolate the marginal value of "any adaptive controller" from "cost-aware adaptive controller" specifically?
- Would the gains hold on benchmarks with much larger action-cost heterogeneity than the observed ~5× step-cost range in Appendix B.6 (Table 4), closer to the κ→∞ regime where Proposition 1's separation is starkest?
- How sensitive is final quality to the roughly dozen fixed controller constants (Table 3), and were they tuned on a held-out benchmark family before being frozen for the reported evaluation?
- Does the intervention-gating mechanism (guide/tactic calls) generalize to settings where "guide" calls are not simply higher-cost LLM generations but qualitatively different tools (e.g., external solvers, symbolic simplifiers)?
- What happens under adversarial or noisy cost reporting (e.g., API cost estimates that don't match realized billing), which would directly stress-test the cost-calibration assumption the whole method depends on?
