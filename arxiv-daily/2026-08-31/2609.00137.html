Key Finding · Theory of Recursive Self-Improvement

AI self-improvement turns self-amplifying when RAI = χa/σ > 1 — a property of the R&D loop, not of any capability threshold.

Critical Edge

Almost nothing load-bearing is empirically identified — β, a, k are unconstrained, so the framework is “unfalsifiable in the strict sense”: vocabulary, not yet a testable model.

— Critical Assessment

Methodology

  • Capability coordinate: a log resource-cost index over a fixed task panel, normalized to x₀=0 with frontier X=1 — explicitly a local coordinate, not a universal intelligence scalar.
  • Core model: a delayed differential equation where last generation’s capability multiplicatively boosts current research productivity after an end-to-end development-cycle delay τ.
  • Four ingredients: baseline productivity r; recursive gain a; operational closure χ ∈ [0,1] (fraction of potential gain surviving evaluation→integration→deployment); frontier hardening σ = −d ln f/dx.
  • Analysis: linearization yields the characteristic equation λ + vσ = vg·e−λτ; Prop 1 gives stability iff RAI < 1, with λ=0 exactly at threshold.
  • Calibration is thin by design: only rref = 1/24 yr−1 (anchored to the 2023 expert survey, n=2,778, 50% date 2047; LEAP median AGI 2050) and τ = 0.5 yr carry data; scenarios a ∈ {0.5, 3, 6, 15}; AGI placed at x=0.50, ASI at 0.80.
  • All figures reproducible from the public code and notebook (github.com/burtsev/recursive-criticality-ai).

  • Subcritical feedback still compounds: a system that never amplifies (a=0.5, RAI<1 throughout) still pulls ASI ~22 years earlier — cumulative shift without local instability.
  • The ecosystem result: every actor individually subcritical (max Kii=0.65), yet ρ(K)=1.55 via cross-actor transfer — and with the lowest effort, the shortest transition (2.82 vs 6.13 yr for closed labs).
  • Delay caps amplification: at high throughput λ* → ln(RAI)/τ — the successor-development cycle itself becomes the limiting timescale.
  • Physical headroom binds deployment, not criticality: with compute growth 0.25 yr−1, scarce headroom (F=0.75) adds up to ~20 yr of AGI→ASI time on the explored grid without changing RAI.

Strengths

  • Disciplines both camps: rapid progress is shown to be neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of self-amplification — a newly supercritical system can look like ordinary acceleration.
  • Fresh angle on open-weight debates: formalizes “openness accelerates everyone” — the network can be supercritical while every lab is subcritical.
  • A measurement agenda, not just a model: names in-principle-estimable quantities (a, χ, τ, σ, gij) that discriminate recursion from scaling.
  • Honest and reproducible: explicit non-forecast disclaimers, acknowledged overlapping prior work (Cunningham et al.), public code for every figure.

Limitations

  • Hand-picked parameters: β=2, k=10, the a-grid, and AGI/ASI thresholds are all chosen; no sensitivity analysis over xAGI/xASI or χ(0) — yet χ(0)=0.5 alone implies RAI(0)=a/4, fixing which regimes ever go supercritical.
  • Local analysis only: linearized stability; takeoff excursions are numerical, one functional form per ingredient, no robustness sweep.
  • Fixed task panel, exogenous frontier: benchmark churn and paradigm jumps X→X+ΔX are discussed but not modeled — the finite-frontier result is within-paradigm only.
  • Precision theater risk: one calibrated parameter mixed with five uncalibrated ones yields precise-looking years (4.50 / 4.95 / 0.45) that invite misquotation as forecasts; no Monte Carlo, no confidence bands.

The Criterion

RAI = χ a / σ
a — recursive gain
capability multiplier on research productivity, one generation back
χ — operational closure
fraction of that gain surviving the development pipeline
σ — frontier hardening
rate at which marginal research productivity decays near the frontier
Three corollaries
onset at RAI>1 · speed capped at ln(RAI)/τ · finite frontier forces RAI→0 (takeoff is transient); ecosystems judged by spectral radius ρ(K)

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can recursive gain a and closure χ be estimated from existing telemetry — MLE-bench/RE-Bench score trajectories, agent-patch acceptance rates, agent-generated code entering successor training runs?
  2. Is the “0.45-year AGI→ASI interval” robust to χ(0) ≠ 0.5, k ≠ 10, or different xAGI/xASI placements — or an artifact of the chosen geometry?
  3. Can repeated paradigm jumps sustain quasi-supercritical dynamics indefinitely (“punctuated supercriticality”), and what observable signature would distinguish that from independent scale-ups?
  4. Does the spectral-radius criterion survive strategic behavior — is there a game on the reproduction matrix, and does competition push ρ(K) above or below 1?
  5. Could MLE-bench-style automation evaluations be repurposed as direct instruments for the feedback term g, rather than capability alone?
