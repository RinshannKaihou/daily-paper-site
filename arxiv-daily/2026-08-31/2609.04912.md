> **一句话总结：** Inria/IRISA 团队提出 TreeFI——首个"取值感知"的统计故障注入方法：用一维回归树把 DNN 每层激活/权重的取值轴切成"位翻转后果相近"的区间并为每区间学习 sigmoid 风险分数，再按 Neyman 式分层采样（n ∝ 频率 × √(r(1−r))）分配注入预算；在保持 1% 绝对误差/95% 置信度目标下，激活故障注入次数相对 SOTA 的 data-aware one-shot SFI 平均减少 44.9×（DeiT-Tiny 上最高 72.1×），权重故障（TreeFI-20×）平均减少 11.2×，且在唯一能做穷举对照的 ResNet8/CIFAR-10 上以 34,934 次注入（穷举需 9,437,134 次，约 270×更少）复现了穷举失效率，MAE 全部落在 1% 目标内。

> **TL;DR:** TreeFI is a value-aware statistical fault injection (SFI) methodology for FP32 single-bit flips in DNN activations and weights, built on the observation that the damage of flipping a given bit depends strongly on the value being corrupted (on ResNet8 activations, flipping bit 27 is harmless below value ≈2.5 but causes accuracy drops above it, while bit 30 flips are harmless at any value). It partitions each layer's value axis into intervals of similar bit-flip severity using 1-D regression trees trained on value-change magnitude (not model output), assigns each interval a sigmoid risk score, and allocates injections by frequency-weighted stratified sampling. On ResNet8/CIFAR-10 activations, TreeFI-10× needs only 34,934 injections to reproduce the exhaustive reference (9,437,134 injections, ~270× more) within the 1%-error/95%-confidence target — 21.2× fewer than the data-aware one-shot SFI baseline (740,534) and 28.0× fewer than iterative IFI (979,021). Scaling to RepVGG-A0 and DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base on ImageNet, injection budgets shrink 5.9×–72.1× vs SFI (avg 44.9×) for activations and ~6.4×–17.9× (avg 11.2×) for weight campaigns, with a full DeiT-Tiny activation analysis in 6 h 42 min where SFI needed 111 h 30 min for just the first three layers.

## 核心贡献

- **问题刻画：** 首次系统指出并量化了现有统计故障注入（SFI：data-aware one-shot [7]；IFI：迭代式 [8]）普遍忽略的"取值依赖性"——同一比特位翻转的后果强烈依赖被篡改的原始取值（ResNet8 激活的 bit 27 在取值 <2.5 时几乎不影响准确率，>2.5 时造成大幅下降；bit 30（最高位）翻转则几乎总是无害），因此不分取值区间的均匀采样会在低影响区域浪费大量注入。
- **TreeFI 方法：** 两阶段设计——离线表征阶段对每层每比特位用一维回归树（约 ≤15 个叶子，最小叶重 10、最多 100 叶节点、加权 MSE 分裂阈值 10⁻⁷）把取值分布切成"位翻转严重度相近"的区间，并按 r = σ(s·(avg_log − β_l))（β_l = log10(y·a_max)）为每区间计算风险分数；注入阶段按 n ∝ W·√(r(1−r)) 的 Neyman 式分层采样公式把注入集中到"出现频繁且估计方差大"的区间，最后按经验频率加权聚合各区间实测失效率。
- **低成本离线表征：** 区间学习不需要真实注入（只用"翻转后取值变化量"而非模型输出），直方图法把取值分布预处理从原始值排序的"数天级"降到"分钟级"；权重故障的分布直接取自模型参数，无需数据集前向。
- **全面实验验证：** 在 ResNet8/CIFAR-10 上与 9,437,134 次注入的穷举 FI 对照，TreeFI-10× 仅 34,934 次即复现穷举失效率且 MAE 与 IFI 相当；扩展到 RepVGG-A0 与 DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base（ImageNet），激活故障预算相对 SFI 减少 5.9×–72.1×（平均 44.9×），权重故障平均 11.2×；附 88 组 (s, y) 超参消融，并开源实现与复现文档。

## Key Contributions

- Identification and empirical quantification of the **value-dependence gap** in existing statistical FI: two injections targeting the same bit position can have opposite outcomes depending on the corrupted value (Figure 2: bit 30 vs bit 27 on ResNet8 activations), yet SFI/IFI organize sampling only by layer and bit position.
- **TreeFI**, a two-phase methodology: (1) offline characterization — per-layer/per-bit 1-D regression trees partition the value axis into intervals of similar bit-flip severity, each scored by a sigmoid risk function learned from how much the value changes when the bit flips (no fault injections needed for training); (2) a stratified campaign where the injection count per interval follows n_{l,b,k} ∝ W_{l,b,k}·√(r_{l,b,k}(1−r_{l,b,k})) (frequency × variance), with the final failure rate computed as the frequency-weighted combination of per-interval measured rates.
- **Empirical validation against exhaustive ground truth** (rare in this literature): on ResNet8 activations, TreeFI-10× reproduces the 9.44M-injection exhaustive failure rates within the 1% statistical error target using 34,934 injections, with per-layer MAE on par with IFI at ~28× fewer injections.
- **Scalability evidence on models where exhaustive FI is impossible** (RepVGG-A0, DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base on ImageNet), plus an 88-configuration hyperparameter ablation and an open-source release with reproduction documentation.

## Methodology

**Fault model.** FP32 single-bit flips under the single-fault assumption (independent bit corruptions), matching the fault models of the SFI [7] and IFI [8] baselines. A fault is a "failure" if the corrupted execution's top-1 prediction differs from the golden (fault-free) output. The explicit goal is not to assess fault-model realism but to minimize the number of injections needed to reach a target statistical accuracy for a given fault model.

**Phase 1 — offline characterization (no fault injection).**
- *Value distribution analysis:* for each target layer, accumulate all tensor values over the validation set (activations) or read model parameters directly (weights) into weighted histograms; bins whose inter-histogram difference is <10⁻⁴ are grouped. This reduces distribution preprocessing from days (raw-value sorting) to minutes.
- *Learning value intervals:* for every layer-bit pair, a 1-D regression tree trained on the value histogram places split points where the local effect of flipping that bit changes sharply. Crucially, training targets the **magnitude of the value change** caused by the flip (average log-distance between original and bit-flipped values, avg_log), not the model output — so no DNN executions are needed. Each interval's risk score is r_{l,b,k} = σ(s·(avg_log_{l,b,k} − β_l)) with layer bias β_l = log10(y·a_{l,max}); s (sharpness, fixed across layers, default 2) and y (bias multiplier) are the only hyperparameters. Trees use ~≤15 leaves per layer-bit pair (min leaf weight 10, max 100 leaves, weighted-MSE split threshold 10⁻⁷).

**Phase 2 — stratified fault injection campaign.**
- *Allocation:* injections per interval n_{l,b,k} ∝ W_{l,b,k}·√(r_{l,b,k}(1−r_{l,b,k})), the standard stratified-sampling idea (Neyman [31]): more samples for intervals that occur often (W = empirical frequency) and for intervals whose expected effect is hardest to estimate (variance term maximal at r = 0.5). The total over intervals is set by the desired confidence level and error margin.
- *Execution:* for activation faults, the model input is chosen so a golden activation falls inside the selected interval, then the bit is flipped; for weight faults, a parameter whose value falls inside the interval is flipped (input-independent, so one corrupted weight is evaluated across all considered inputs). Each faulty run is compared to the golden output.
- *Aggregation:* per-layer/bit failure rate p̄_{l,b} = Σ_k W_{l,b,k}·p_{l,b,k} — the frequency weighting is what lets TreeFI match baseline estimates with fewer injections, because rare-but-impactful value ranges are not over-weighted.

**Experimental setup.** Absolute error margin E = 1% with 95% confidence; IFI follows its recommended protocol (looser E' = 5%, refined to E = 1%); TreeFI reported at y ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10} (TreeFI-1×…10×) for activations and TreeFI-20× for weights. Models: ResNet8 (CIFAR-10), RepVGG-A0 (ImageNet), DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base (ImageNet); all campaigns on an NVIDIA H100 (96 GB).

## Results & Highlights

- **Validation against exhaustive FI (ResNet8 activations, CIFAR-10):** the exhaustive campaign needs 9,437,134 injections; TreeFI-10× needs only 34,934 (~270× fewer) and 28 min. Under the same 1%-error/95%-confidence objective, one-shot SFI requires 740,534 injections (21.2× more than TreeFI-10×) and IFI-13min requires 979,021 injections / 9 h 35 min (28.0× more); measured wall-clock reductions are 15.3× vs SFI and 20.3× vs IFI. All MAEs are within the 1% error target, and in the most failure-prone regions (layers L0–L2, bit 30) TreeFI's per-layer MAE is on par with IFI while using far fewer injections.
- **Estimate stability:** across all layers and bits, the MAE gap between TreeFI-1× (100,096 injections) and TreeFI-10× (34,934) stays below 0.05 percentage points — an order of magnitude smaller than the 1% target margin — indicating the allocation, not raw volume, drives accuracy.
- **Scalability to ImageNet-scale models:** reduction vs SFI ranges from 5.9× (RepVGG-A0, γ=1×) up to 72.1× (DeiT-Tiny, γ=10×); averaged over models, TreeFI-10× achieves the same statistical results as SFI with 44.9× fewer injections. On RepVGG-A0 activations the full exhaustive campaign would need 33 h 47 min under IFI and 21 h 46 min under SFI vs only 1 h 41 min for TreeFI-10× (20.1×/15.9× wall-clock). On DeiT-Tiny's first layer, TreeFI-10× uses 1,042 injections vs 124,089 (IFI) and 139,778 (SFI) — 119× and 134× fewer; a full DeiT-Tiny activation analysis takes 6 h 42 min with TreeFI-10×, whereas SFI needed 111 h 30 min for only the first three layers (IFI is not tractable for DeiT at all).
- **Weight faults (TreeFI-20×):** ResNet8: 79,426 injections vs 753,050 (SFI) and 1,043,629 (IFI) — 9.5×/13.1× fewer; RepVGG-A0: 62,063 vs 1,108,340 and 1,560,584 — 17.9×/25.1× fewer; DeiT-Tiny: 48,464 vs 310,444 and 694,557 — 6.4×/14.3× fewer (averages 11.4× vs SFI, 17.8× vs IFI). Per Table 2, TreeFI-20× failure-rate MAE vs both baselines is at most 0.0437% (RepVGG-A0, all-bits vs IFI) — more than 20× below the 1% target margin, i.e., the injection savings do not change the estimates.
- **Hyperparameter ablation (ResNet8 activations, 88 (s, y) configurations vs exhaustive reference):** 60 of 88 configurations stay within the 1% layer-bit error margin; every configuration with moderate sharpness (s ≤ 3) passes for all tested bias multipliers, while for s ≥ 16 only three bias values satisfy the criterion — overly sharp sigmoids degrade the allocation. The shipped default (s = 2, y = 10) sits in the conservative region of the accuracy-vs-injections Pareto front (Figure 7).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but incremental.** The motivating observation (Figure 2's bit 30 vs bit 27) is convincing and the framing is crisp, but the ingredients are all standard: weighted histograms, 1-D regression trees, and Neyman stratified sampling (which they cite, [31]). Prior work — including the value/format-aware resilience-analysis line they cite ([27] Guitierrez-Zaballa et al.) — already knew resilience depends on the corrupted value; TreeFI's contribution is turning that observation into a *sampling strategy* rather than a protection technique. That is a fair and useful contribution, not a new algorithmic idea.
- **Ground truth exists for only the smallest setting.** The "more accurate than SOTA" claim is directly validated exclusively on ResNet8/CIFAR-10 activations, a tiny CNN where exhaustive FI is feasible. For RepVGG and all DeiT models there is no reference: correctness rests on TreeFI agreeing with SFI/IFI trends. Since all three methods share the same single-bit fault model and the same top-1 failure definition, cross-method agreement cannot exclude a shared systematic bias — the abstract's accuracy claim is strongest exactly where it is least checkable.
- **Wall-clock gains trail the headline numbers.** The abstract leads with injection-count reductions (up to 72.1×, avg 44.9×), but measured speedups are lower (15.3×/20.3× on ResNet8 vs 21.2×/28.0× in counts) because offline characterization costs real time. The authors report both honestly, but a practitioner budgeting compute should plan on the wall-clock numbers.
- **The method introduces a tuning surface its baselines lack.** One-shot SFI has essentially no knobs; TreeFI adds sigmoid sharpness s and bias multiplier y, and its own ablation shows 28 of 88 configurations miss the 1% margin, with sharp sigmoids (s ≥ 16) failing in most bias settings. A careless user can silently get a poor allocation — the default (s = 2) is safe precisely because it is conservative.
- **On weights, TreeFI is a cost win, not an accuracy win.** Table 2 shows TreeFI-20× agrees with SFI to within 0.0008%–0.0259% MAE — i.e., it essentially reproduces SFI's estimates more cheaply (weight value distributions vary less, leaving less structure to exploit, as the authors themselves note). The accuracy story is confined to activations.
- **Fault-model scope.** Everything is FP32, single-bit, single-fault, top-1-classification only; the abstract concedes real hardware faults can be multi-bit and temporally/spatially correlated. Extensions to FP16/bfloat16/quantized formats are stated as future work, unvalidated. Also, both baselines were run by the authors under their own protocol choices (e.g., IFI's 5%→1% refinement), so runtime comparisons partially reflect implementation quality.

## Strengths

- Rare direct validation against an exhaustive 9.44M-injection ground truth, with MAE reported per layer and per bit rather than only globally.
- Statistically principled: the Neyman-style variance-aware allocation and frequency-weighted aggregation preserve the confidence/error-margin guarantees by construction, rather than heuristically.
- Genuine scalability: it delivers full-model results where one baseline is extremely slow (SFI: 111 h 30 min for 3 DeiT-Tiny layers) and the other is infeasible (IFI on DeiT), which is precisely the regime that matters for modern models.
- Honest, self-critical evaluation: wall-clock vs theoretical reductions are both reported; the ablation identifies a concrete failure mode (over-sharp sigmoids) and gives actionable guidance; the weight-fault section candidly notes smaller headroom.
- The core idea is fault-model-agnostic — only "how a bit modifies a value" is needed — making extension to other numerical formats plausible.
- Open-source artifacts with per-figure reproduction documentation.

## Limitations

- Ground-truth validation is confined to one small CNN (ResNet8) on CIFAR-10 activations; all larger-model results lack an exhaustive reference.
- Restricted to FP32 single-bit faults under the single-fault assumption; no multi-bit, no correlated faults, and no experiments on reduced-precision formats (FP16/bfloat16/quantized) where bit significance differs sharply.
- Only image classification with top-1 prediction change as the failure criterion; no confidence-degradation, calibration, or other task metrics.
- Offline characterization overhead (per-layer histograms + per-layer-bit regression-tree training) erodes wall-clock gains, and activation histograms require validation-set forward passes whose support may not cover out-of-distribution inputs.
- Hyperparameters (s, y) add a failure surface: ~32% of ablation configurations missed the 1% margin, mostly at high sharpness.
- Injection-count reductions for weights (avg ~11×) are notably smaller than for activations, and the reported weight-campaign runtimes for DeiT-Tiny show per-injection costs that vary substantially across models, complicating runtime extrapolation.

## Relevance

与用户核心关注方向"Inference Reliability & SDC Detection"直接对口：TreeFI 研究的正是 DNN 推理中硬件位翻转故障导致的静默输出损坏（top-1 改变）如何被更高效地统计评估，可直接用于以更低成本量化模型/加速器在 SDC 下的失效率；其对 FP16/bfloat16/量化格式扩展的展望也与"极端性能优化下的可靠性"方向相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the interval-learning approach transfer to FP16/bfloat16/INT8-quantized models, where exponent/mantissa structure and value distributions differ sharply from FP32?
- Can the learned interval risk scores be reused as a *protection* signal (e.g., selective redundancy or exponent-aware hardening concentrated on high-risk value intervals), connecting TreeFI's evaluation output to mitigation?
- How does stratified allocation behave under multi-bit or temporally/spatially correlated faults, where per-interval independence and the single-fault assumption break down?
- Could the regression trees be replaced by closed-form, exponent-aware binning for floating-point formats, removing the training step and the s/y hyperparameters (and the 28/88 failing configurations)?
- How robust are the offline-characterized value histograms to out-of-distribution inputs whose activation ranges fall outside the characterized support — does the allocation remain statistically valid at inference-time distribution shift?
