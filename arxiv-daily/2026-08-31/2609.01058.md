> **一句话总结：** 阿里 Qwen 团队提出 ARISE-RL：一个以 rubric 为媒介的"生成器-求解器"闭环共进化 RL 框架——生成器产出工具落地（tool-grounded）的开放式查询与评分 rubric，其难度整形奖励把任务钉在求解器约 50% 成功率的能力边界上，求解器则用 LLM 裁判逐条核验 rubric 的满足率获得细粒度奖励；再配合仅在"记忆增强版自身策略经验性提升奖励（Δr>τ=0.05）"时才激活的奖励门控自蒸馏 RG-SED。在 Qwen3.5-9B 上，四个开放式智能体基准平均分从 0.477 提升到 0.569（+9.2 pts），超过同底座的 Dr. Zero（0.531）、Absolute Zero（0.508）、闭源最强的 Claude-4.6-Sonnet（0.495）以及 397B 总参/17B 激活的 Qwen3.5-397B（0.508）；消融显示去掉 RG-SED 在 ECR-Travel 上掉 8.1 分。

> **TL;DR:** ARISE-RL (Alibaba/Qwen) is a full-cycle self-evolution RL framework for open-ended agents: a Generator authors tool-grounded queries + judging rubrics with a difficulty-shaped reward peaked at ~50% Solver success (K=8 rollouts, threshold γ=0.9), while the Solver is trained on rubric-satisfaction rewards scored by an LLM judge (gpt-5.2); a Reward-Gated Self-Evolution Distillation (RG-SED) builds a transient memory-augmented teacher from the same policy and applies token-level reverse-KL distillation only on groups where coach-memory-augmented rollouts empirically beat standard ones (Δr > 0.05). On Qwen3.5-9B it raises the average over four open-ended agentic benchmarks (ResearchRubrics, ECR-DeepResearch, VitaBench, ECR-Travel) from 0.477 (base) to 0.569 (+9.2 pts), beating the same-backbone self-evolution baselines Dr. Zero (0.531) and Absolute Zero (0.508) by +3.8/+6.1 pts, and surpassing closed-source Claude-4.6-Sonnet (0.495) and the 397B-MoE Qwen3.5-397B (0.508). The paper also contributes ECR-Bench (ECR-DeepResearch: 100 expert-calibrated deep-research queries; ECR-Travel: 500 queries over 5 multi-tool travel-planning sub-tasks), with gpt-5.2-vs-human rubric agreement of 82.3%/77.9%.

## 核心贡献

- **ARISE-RL 闭环共进化框架**：把任务生成（Generator）、rubric 构建、任务求解（Solver）与 RL 策略优化统一进一个闭环。生成器奖励 = 工具落地门 R_tool × 格式门 R_fmt × (1+难度奖励 R_diff)，其中 R_diff = 2·max(0, 1−|c−K/2|/(K/2))（K=8 个 Solver rollout，c 为成功数，成功率阈值 γ=0.9），在约一半 rollout 成功时最大——把生成任务持续推向 Solver 的能力边界，直接缓解开放域长程任务中 group-based RL 的 advantage collapse / rollout 对比度弱的问题。
- **工具落地 rubric 门控**：生成器必须先真实调用工具、观察到真实输出，才允许撰写依赖工具结果的 rubric 条目，从源头抑制幻觉式 rubric；消融显示去掉该门控在 ECR-Travel 上掉 5.0 分（0.599→0.549）。
- **RG-SED（Reward-Gated Self-Evolution Distillation）**：用 coach LLM 把 group 级反馈总结成角色条件记忆 m_a，同参数策略加上记忆提示即构成"瞬态教师"；仅当记忆增强 rollout 的经验奖励显著优于标准 rollout（Δr>R̂_m−R̂_s>τ_a=0.05）时，才在标准 on-policy 轨迹上加 token 级 reverse-KL 蒸馏（对教师分布 stop-gradient）。学生-教师仅差提示词，天然约束分布偏移（KL≈0.2–0.35 vs GKD 固定教师的 0.4–0.55）。
- **ECR-Bench 基准套件**：ECR-DeepResearch（100 条单工具 web_search 深研查询，rubric score rate 为指标）+ ECR-Travel（500 条查询、5 个子任务各 100 条：路线规划 Direction / 交通方式对比 Compare / 周边 POI 搜索 Search / 单日行程 1-Day / 多日行程 M-Day，任务通过率为指标，7 个工具）。rubric 由 6 名领域专家（3 旅行 + 3 深研）按双语标注手册撰写，经规则增强 LLM 质检 + 人工复核。
- **系统性实证**：8B/9B 两个底座、4 个基准、3 类基线（闭源 LLM、开源 LLM、同底座自进化框架）+ 4 项消融 + 门控动力学/分布散度/人机裁判一致性分析，代码开源（github.com/Alibaba-NLP/qqr）。

## Key Contributions

- **ARISE-RL**, a rubric-mediated Generator–Solver co-evolution RL loop: the Generator is rewarded for tool-grounded, format-valid tasks with difficulty shaped to the Solver's capability boundary (R_diff peaks when ~half of K=8 rollouts succeed, γ=0.9), attacking the advantage-collapse problem of vanilla group-based RL on long-horizon open-ended tasks.
- **Tool-grounded rubric construction**: the Generator must observe real tool outputs before authoring tool-dependent rubric criteria, preventing hallucinated supervision; ablating it costs 5.0 pts on ECR-Travel (0.599→0.549).
- **RG-SED**, a role-conditioned, reward-gated self-distillation: a coach LLM distills group-level feedback into role-specific memory; the same policy under a memory-augmented prompt acts as a transient teacher, and distillation (token-level reverse KL on standard on-policy trajectories, stop-grad through the teacher) activates only when the memory-augmented rollouts empirically improve reward (Δr > τ_a = 0.05), avoiding blind imitation of noisy guidance and the distribution mismatch of fixed-teacher OPCD/GKD.
- **ECR-Bench**, an Expert-Calibrated Rubric benchmark suite: ECR-DeepResearch (100 single-tool deep-research queries, rubric score rate) and ECR-Travel (500 queries, 5 sub-tasks × 100: Direction / Compare / Search / 1-Day / M-Day, pass rate, 7 tools), with rubrics written by 6 domain experts under a bilingual annotation handbook and filtered by a rule-augmented LLM quality checker plus manual review.
- **Extensive evaluation**: 8B/9B backbones, 4 benchmarks, 3 baseline groups (closed-source, open-source, same-backbone self-evolution frameworks), 4 ablations, plus gate-dynamics, distribution-divergence, and LLM-vs-human judge consistency studies; code released at github.com/Alibaba-NLP/qqr.

## Methodology

**Rubric-mediated Generator–Solver co-evolution (global level).** At each iteration the Generator produces an open-ended query q and a rubric set ρ = {ρ_j}; the Solver attempts q via multi-step reasoning and tool use (up to 40 tool rounds). *Generator reward*: R_G = R_tool(ξ)·R_fmt(q,ρ)·(1+R_diff(q,ρ)). R_tool is a binary gate requiring the trajectory to contain the real tool output referenced by each tool-dependent rubric item (prevents hallucinated criteria); R_fmt requires a parseable query and a non-empty rubric list; R_diff = 2·max(0, 1 − |c−K/2|/(K/2)) with c = #successful rollouts out of K=8 (success threshold γ=0.9), maximized when ~half succeed so tasks sit at the Solver's frontier where group-relative advantage signal is most informative. *Solver reward*: an LLM judge (gpt-5.2) checks each rubric item against the trajectory, giving satisfaction rate s_i ∈ [0,1]; r = α·s_i + (1−α)·1[s_i=1] with partial-credit weight α=0.8 and full-completion bonus 0.2. The same empirical difficulty signal is reused for Generator training, coupling the two roles into an adaptive curriculum.

**RG-SED (local level).** For each role a ∈ {Generator, Solver}, a coach LLM summarizes group-level feedback into role-specific memory m_a (Generator-side: why queries were too easy/hard/under-grounded; Solver-side: missing constraints, useful hints, rubric items to address). The same deployment policy conditioned on (p_a ⊕ m_a) forms a transient teacher π̂_a. Distillation is activated only when the group-level empirical reward gap Δr = R̂_m − R̂_s exceeds a role-specific threshold τ_a = 0.05, scaled by λ_a = λ0·1[Δr>τ_a]·w_a(t)·d_a(t) with warm-up and decay schedules (λ0=0.5). When active, a token-level reverse-KL term L_SESD = E[Σ_t D_KL(π̂_a^G(·|h_t,p_a⊕m_a) ‖ sg-stopped) ] is applied on the *standard* on-policy trajectories, so the policy internalizes reward-validated high-confidence behaviors while training/deployment consistency is preserved. Total objective: L^a = L^a_RL + λ^a L^a_SESD.

**ECR-Bench.** ECR-DeepResearch: 100 queries, single web_search tool (Google Custom Search; pages >7,500 chars summarized by Qwen3-Max before returning), each with expert rubrics covering factual grounding, evidence coverage, reasoning, completeness, report structure; metric = rubric score rate. ECR-Travel: 500 queries across 5 balanced sub-tasks, 7 tools (poi_search, around_search, direction, weather = Amap wrappers; web_search = Google; search_flights/search_train_tickets = GPT-5-mini simulations over a closed database); rubrics cover itinerary quality and tool-use correctness; metric = pass rate (4 independent runs). Rubrics were authored by 6 in-house domain experts with a bilingual handbook (each rubric a single verifiable assertion with explicit pass criteria and expected tool calls), filtered by a rule-augmented LLM checker and iteratively revised.

**Training setup.** PyTorch on 32 H20 GPUs; G=16 rollouts/query at temperature 0.8; 3 Generator–Solver co-evolution cycles with the query pool refreshed every 120 training steps (500 strictly deduplicated queries per refresh; the 10 most recent queries injected as in-context negatives to avoid duplication). The interactive UserSimulator for VitaBench is Qwen3.5-397B; all judges (rubric scoring, coach summarization, format checking) are gpt-5.2.

## Results & Highlights

- **Overall average (Table 1)**: ARISE-RL on Qwen3.5-9B reaches an average of **0.569** across the four benchmarks (RR 0.479, ECR-DR 0.781, VitaBench 0.417, ECR-Travel 0.599) vs **0.477** for the untrained Qwen3.5-9B base (**+9.2 pts**), vs **0.531** for same-backbone Dr. Zero (**+3.8**) and **0.508** for Absolute Zero (**+6.1**) — also surpassing the best closed-source model Claude-4.6-Sonnet (**0.495**) and the 397B-total/17B-activated MoE Qwen3.5-397B (**0.508**). On Qwen3-8B: ARISE-RL **0.358** vs Dr. Zero 0.312 and Absolute Zero 0.298 (base 0.267).
- **Largest gains on interactive multi-tool tasks (Table 3)**: over three co-evolution cycles VitaBench climbs 0.256→0.417 (**+16.1 pts**) and ECR-Travel 0.470→0.599 (**+12.9 pts**), with the OTA sub-task jumping 0.118→0.373 (**+25.5 pts**); single-tool rubric-score-rate gains are smaller (RR +3.2, ECR-DR +4.6).
- **Ablations (Table 2, Qwen3.5-9B)**: removing RG-SED is the most damaging — ECR-Travel 0.599→0.518 (**−8.1**), VitaBench 0.417→0.350 (**−6.7**), ECR-DR 0.781→0.716 (**−6.5**), RR 0.479→0.422 (**−5.7**). Removing reward gating (always-on distillation) degrades to 0.438/0.725/0.371/0.523 — ungated distillation amplifies rather than suppresses noisy supervision. W/o tool-grounded rubric: ECR-Travel 0.549 (−5.0); w/o difficulty-shaped reward: 0.537 (−6.2).
- **Distillation mechanism comparison (Figure 3)**: with an identical Qwen3.5-9B backbone, training data, and rubric-judging pipeline, RG-SED beats OPCD and GKD (both using an external Qwen3.5-397B teacher) on **all 11 benchmark axes**; token-level student–teacher KL under RG-SED stays ≈**0.2–0.35** vs GKD's ≈**0.4–0.55** on ECR-Travel, supporting the prompt-bounded-distribution-gap design.
- **Gate selectivity (Figure 5, ECR-Travel)**: across training groups, **72.1%** show a positive reward gap (Δr > τ_a), **8.3%** a negative one (Δr < −τ_a) and **19.6%** are ambiguous (|Δr| ≤ τ_a) — an ungated objective would apply neutral or harmful supervision to ~28% of groups; the gate-active sample rate ramps from ≈5% to ≈22% during warm-up and retracts in real time when coach memory degrades (dip around rollouts 80–90).
- **Curriculum verification (Figure 4)**: the pass rate of the *frozen* Qwen3.5-9B base policy on freshly generated questions falls across cycles (VitaBench 50.2→38.1; ECR-Travel 30.7→19.4), direct evidence that the Generator tracks the improving Solver with increasingly difficult tasks.
- **Judge reliability (Figure 13)**: gpt-5.2 judge agrees with the majority vote of 3 annotators on **82.3%** of ECR-DeepResearch and **77.9%** of ECR-Travel responses (50 per benchmark).

Fifteen authors from **Alibaba ATH Token Foundry** and **Hema, Alibaba Group**, including Fanrui Zhang and Zheng-Jun Zha as project leaders. This is the same team behind Armarri (arXiv:2601.06487, tournament-based relative ranking RL for open-ended agents), so the work builds on an established in-house line of research on open-ended agent RL. The technical-report format follows Qwen-team conventions, with all prompts (Generator/Solver/Judge/coach memory) reproduced in the appendix, full hyperparameters disclosed (32 H20 GPUs, K=8, γ=0.9, α=0.8/0.2, λ0=0.5, τ_a=0.05), an ethics/PII statement, and code released on GitHub (Alibaba-NLP/qqr). Institutional resources are substantial (gpt-5.2 as judge, Qwen3.5-397B as user simulator, 32-GPU RL runs), and the evaluation protocol applies the identical judge and tool stack to all baselines, which strengthens fairness. Credibility: high for an industrial report; the caveat is that key infrastructure (judge, user simulator, simulated flight/train tools) is proprietary or closed-API.

## Critical Assessment

- **Judge circularity is the central risk.** gpt-5.2 is simultaneously the training reward judge, the RG-SED coach, the format checker, and the evaluation judge for all four benchmarks — including the authors' own ECR-Bench, whose rubric style (single verifiable assertions) matches the rubrics the Generator was trained to author. Outputs stylistically aligned with this loop may be scored favorably. The human-agreement study (82.3%/77.9%) partially mitigates this, but it covers only 50 responses per benchmark, scored by 3 in-house annotators, and a 77.9% agreement floor on ECR-Travel means more than a fifth of rubric verdicts diverge from humans — a non-trivial noise floor for the reward signal itself.
- **Self-built benchmark, in-house annotation.** ECR-Bench rubrics were written by 6 in-house domain experts "as part of their research collaboration, with no external compensation" (Appendix B.3). Composition analysis shows ECR-Travel rubrics are dominated by coverage (36%) and location/time constraints (16%), i.e., checklist-style criteria that a system trained to satisfy exactly this rubric format is optimized for. The most defensible numbers are on the external benchmarks (ResearchRubrics +3.2, VitaBench +16.1); the modest RR gain hints that transfer across rubric styles is weaker than the headline suggests.
- **Benchmark realism quirks.** ECR-Travel's search_flights and search_train_tickets are GPT-5-mini simulations over a closed database — and the Generator and Solver *train against the same simulated tool stack that the benchmark evaluates on*. Also, the ECR-DeepResearch judge is explicitly instructed *not* to verify factual truth against tool returns ("do NOT externally verify factual truth... mark true only if a plausible relevant source exists"), so "factual grounding" is checked only superficially; some deep-research gains may be surface-level (structure/citation shape) rather than substantive accuracy.
- **Scale and statistical rigor.** All training is at 8/9B (acknowledged by the authors); comparisons with Qwen3.5-397B and closed-source models are evaluation-only, at very different parameter counts and (presumably) different tool-use training. Despite averaging 4 independent runs for multi-tool pass rates, no standard deviations are reported anywhere in Table 1; there appears to be a single training run per backbone, and no seed variance for RL training is given.
- **Missing ablations**: no sensitivity analysis for τ_a, λ0, K, or the number of co-evolution cycles beyond 3; no ablation swapping the judge (e.g., an open-source judge), which is exactly the confound that would address the circularity concern; the compute cost of per-group gpt-5.2 coach/judge calls is never quantified against the 32-GPU training budget.
- **Genuine strengths worth crediting**: the reward-gating analysis is unusually thorough for this genre (gate-active-rate dynamics, empirical gate response vs the theoretical sigmoid, student–teacher KL tracking, and a per-group Δr histogram showing 28% of groups should not be distilled); the tool-grounded rubric gate is a clean, mechanistic fix for hallucinated rubrics; and every baseline is re-implemented under the identical judge and tool stack rather than quoted from other papers.

## Strengths

- Closes the loop: task generation, rubric construction, solving, and policy optimization are co-optimized, removing reliance on static human-authored training queries and rubrics while provably increasing task difficulty in step with the Solver (frozen-policy pass rate falls monotonically).
- The difficulty-shaped Generator reward (peak at ~50% success) is a principled, directly measurable answer to advantage collapse in open-ended settings — and Figure 4 validates the curriculum it induces.
- RG-SED is a well-motivated middle ground between fixed-teacher off-policy distillation (OPCD/GKD: distribution mismatch, noise imitation) and pure self-distillation (no criterion for *when* to distill): same-policy teacher bounds the gap by construction, and the empirical reward gate decides *when*.
- Process-level, expert-calibrated evaluation (ECR-Bench) with per-dimension rubric composition analysis, released prompts, and a same-judge/same-tool-stack protocol for all baselines.
- Consistent gains across two backbones (8B, 9B), with honest component-wise ablations showing the pieces are complementary rather than redundant.

## Limitations

- Training validated only on 8/9B open-source backbones; scalability to substantially larger models is explicitly left unverified (stated in the paper's Limitations).
- Heavy dependence on closed proprietary infrastructure: gpt-5.2 for all judging/coaching, Qwen3.5-397B as the user simulator, GPT-5-mini-simulated flight/train tools — costly to reproduce and a potential source of judge-family bias.
- No variance reporting (single training run per backbone; std omitted even for the 4-run pass-rate averages); only 3 co-evolution cycles, so long-run stability of the co-evolution loop is unknown.
- ECR-Travel is Chinese-language-centric (travel queries) and partially simulated; the ECR-DR judge cannot fact-check claims against retrieved evidence.
- RG-SED adds a coach-LLM call plus memory-augmented rollouts per group — the inference overhead is unquantified, and coach memory can degrade (the 80–90 rollout dip), which the gate retracts but never fixes.

## Relevance

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the ~50%-success difficulty target remain optimal beyond 3 cycles, or does the co-evolution loop plateau/diverge when the Generator can no longer outpace the Solver's improvement?
- How large is the judge-family effect: would re-running training and evaluation with an open-source judge (or human-judged subset) preserve the multi-tool gains, particularly on the self-built ECR-Bench?
- Can rubric-mediated rewards extend to domains without tool-verifiable facts (open-ended writing, hypothesis generation), given the ECR-DR judge's explicit no-fact-verification instruction?
- What is the full cost accounting of RG-SED (coach calls, memory-augmented rollouts, gpt-5.2 judging) relative to the 32-GPU RL budget, and does the reward gate remain calibrated when coach memory degrades for extended stretches?
- Would a hybrid of RG-SED's gated same-policy teacher with a fixed external teacher (e.g., Qwen3.5-397B) outperform either alone at larger scales, and does the 28% non-beneficial group fraction grow or shrink with model scale?
