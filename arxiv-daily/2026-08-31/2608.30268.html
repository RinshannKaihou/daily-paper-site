Why this matters

A full agentic-discovery loop in a mechanically verifiable domain — a template for trustworthy autonomous research, plus a reality check on LLM agents’ real engineering gains.

Critical edge

The headline multi-agent framework is narrated, not audited — no ablation isolates its contribution over a single serial agent or careful human design.

01Methodology

  • Problem. Shallow-light Steiner trees: minimize wirelength under per-sink budgets dT(r,t) ≤ (1+ε)·dM(r,t). Metrics: shallowness α, FLUTE-normalized wirelength β, Elmore-delay γ.
  • Discovery loop. A file-based single source of truth anchors every round: a Web Search Agent pulls math; 8 isolated Hypothesis Agents propose mechanisms; a Critic solidates 3 specs; 3 parallel Programmer–Verifier chains implement and independently check them; a fixed Evaluator validates tree connectivity and every sink budget before any wirelength is read.
  • Negative results steer. Two candidate families with zero gain were labeled verified negatives — redirecting search from local attachment fixes to shared root-side structure, which produced FABO.
  • FABO. Lemma 1: each flow’s feasible breakpoints form one contiguous interval ending at its sink. Theorem 1: a greedy interval cover seeds a provably minimum breakpoint set. Refinement ranks deeper breakpoints by all-or-nothing flow-aware gain — an edge counts as saved only when every flow using it leaves (Lemma 2). Theorem 2: only trees re-checked against every budget are accepted.
  • FABO-FAST. SALT everywhere; an XGBoost gain predictor (22 features, leave-one-design-out per ε) picks which nets get FABO within a runtime budget.

  • Dominance everywhere. Lower mean β than postprocessed SALT at all 20 ε settings on 1,294,541 ICCAD15 nets, without increasing mean α or γ; at ε=0 both hit α=1.000 but FABO is 0.39% shorter.
  • Mechanism confirmed. Savings land exactly where SALT shares least: 1.38% in the low-sharing tercile vs 0.12% in the high-sharing tercile.
  • Worked case. Superblue18 net 323 (8 pins, ε=0.1125): 1,042,970 → 929,794 (−10.85%) under identical budgets — one sink spends its full permitted detour to release shared wire.
  • Runtime trade. Full FABO costs 10.66× SALT at the peak setting; FABO-FAST at 1.3× recovers 99.0–100% of ≥25%-gain nets and 96.0–99.3% of the 20–25% group.
  • Honest accounting. A footnote discloses the released benchmark (1.29M nets, no 2-pin) differs from SALT paper’s 2.38M — both methods use the same files and evaluator.

Strengths

  • Crisp, provable core: intervals (Lemma 1), minimum cover (Theorem 1), all-or-nothing gain (Lemma 2), feasibility of every accepted tree (Theorem 2) — the joint decision SALT never makes becomes explicit.
  • Strong empirical method: 1.29M nets, all 20 tolerances, same input and checker, plus stratified analyses that test the proposed mechanism rather than just averaging.
  • FABO-FAST analysis is well designed: leave-one-design-out per ε, recovery measured against ground-truth full-FABO gains, monotone recovery-vs-gain curve.
  • Intellectual honesty: guarantees vs heuristics separated, benchmark discrepancy footnoted, a caught SALT-reproduction bug disclosed, code released.
  • Transferable agent template: isolation against path dependence, independent verification separating bugs from algorithmic evidence, SSOT-based resumability.

Limitations

  • Sub-1% mean on the full benchmark; value concentrates in high-fanout trunk nets — adopters must care about those to justify integration.
  • 10.66× SALT runtime for full FABO; the practical variant depends on per-ε predictors whose training-label generation is expensive and whose transfer to new designs is untested.
  • Geometry-only evaluation: no detailed routing, congestion, DRC, extracted parasitics, or sign-off timing — downstream benefit is asserted, not demonstrated.
  • Single benchmark family (ICCAD15 superblue); no comparison with PD-Rev, PatLabor, or Wu et al. even as a reference point.
  • Framework not audited: rounds, token cost, and failure statistics unreported; no ablation against a serial agent or simpler search.
  • Hand-fixed constants (pool size, refinement rounds, ≥31-pin second-support rule) released with code but never sensitivity-studied.

03Questions for Further Exploration

01How much of the gain comes from the multi-agent scaffold itself? A serial-agent or random-search ablation on the same spec space would substantiate the framework claim.
02Could FABO’s interval-overlap selection be made optimal — is the greedy-plus-refinement gap bounded?
03Does the wirelength reduction survive detailed routing and real parasitics — does a larger shared trunk relieve or concentrate congestion hotspots?
04Can one FABO-FAST predictor generalize across all ε and transfer to a new design family without re-labeling?
05Would the generate–check framework transfer to adjacent EDA problems with cheap verifiers — clock-tree synthesis, buffer insertion?
