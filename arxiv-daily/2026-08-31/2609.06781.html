BLINK: Batch Normalization-based Integrity Checkpoints for In-Situ Detection and Mitigation of Diverse Weight Corruptions in DNN Accelerators

ResNet-50/CIFAR-10 accuracy under 0.5% random bit flips, recovered on-chip with zero host or labeled data (clean baseline: 94.33%).
Why This Matters

Silent data corruption quietly wrecks accelerator outputs; BLINK catches and fixes it on-chip during inference itself — no downtime, no labeled data, no host round-trip.

⚠ Critical Edge

The PBFA evaluation assumes a non-adaptive attacker: an adversary aware of BN-score monitoring could spread flips across layers to stay under every threshold — evasion is argued away, not tested.

01 Methodology — How It Works

  • The reference is free. BN normalizes activations with running statistics (μ, σ) frozen at training time; any weight fault pulls inference-time batch statistics away from this clean reference.
  • Per-layer score. Each channel contributes |inference batch mean ÷ frozen std|; averaged into a layer score Sl. Offline fault-injection sweeps map Sl to end-to-end accuracy drop, and a threshold Tl is picked at 1% FNR for a user-chosen accuracy-drop budget τ.
  • Repair at the point of origin. A flagged layer re-centers/rescales its running statistics toward stored per-channel clean targets within the same forward pass, so error never compounds downstream.
  • Safety net. Trust gates bar layers with weak profiling AUC from flagging (FPR → 1.1%); the final BN acts as a "watcher" that aborts inferences whose residual shift implies catastrophic (>~50%) accuracy loss.
  • Offline price. Per model/dataset: layer-by-layer fault sweeps + 200 clean batches (size 128) to build targets; thresholds, targets and gate bits stored on-chip.

  • Detection (MobileNetV2/CIFAR-10): TPR 100% on random & PBFA faults, 98.5%/96.6% on localized MSB sub-block faults; precision 99.6–99.9% at 1.1% FPR via trust gates.
  • Gray zone: milder-than-calibrated localized faults are caught only 39.7%/34.0% (τ=10%/5%) — late-layer faults have few downstream BN layers to notice.
  • PBFA (ResNet-20): undefended accuracy collapses 88.9% → ~10% within the first gradient-ranked flips; BLINK holds 80–83% in the same pass.
  • Watcher abort: AUC 0.98, recall 94.4%, false-abort 7.0% over 150 trials at 0.5–4% global fault rates.
  • Overhead (128×128 systolic array): 0.53% compute / 0.03% latency (ResNet-50 worst case); 4.5% / 1.03% (MobileNetV2 worst case).
  • vs. baselines: BLINK+BN-FT recovers a few points more, but only by halting inference with host access + 5,000 labels; standalone FPT/BN-FT cannot monitor continually at all. Hardest setting — MobileNetV2/CIFAR-100 at 0.5% — still recovers 7.84% → 52.01%.

Strengths

  • Genuinely autonomous — detection and mitigation fused into one forward pass; no host, halts, labels, or retraining, unlike FPT/BN-FT and most PBFA defenses.
  • One mechanism spans four fault models (bit-flip, stuck-at, localized MSB sub-block, PBFA) where each prior defense committed to a single model.
  • Headline detection is excellent: 100% TPR (random/PBFA), 98.5% (localized), 1.1% FPR, 99.6–99.9% precision.
  • The FP-cheap / FNR-constrained thresholding argument is sound engineering.
  • Watcher converts "beyond repair" into a graceful, measurable abort instead of confident garbage output.
  • Cheap and BN-native — batch stats already exist; logic is comparisons + a few parameter updates. Blind spots are disclosed, not hidden.

Limitations

  • CNN/BN-only as evaluated — no mechanism shown for LayerNorm Transformers; no BN-fusion variant tested.
  • Small scale: CIFAR-10/100, ≤ResNet-50, INT8 weights; no ImageNet, no ViTs, no sub-INT8 stress.
  • Structural blind spot: late-layer and final-classifier faults see no downstream BN — localized gray-zone detection falls to 34–39.7%.
  • Per-model offline profiling + on-chip per-channel storage required; no storage/area/energy numbers, no RTL synthesis — true accelerator cost unknown.
  • Non-adaptive PBFA attacker assumed; BN-score evasion unexplored.
  • Recovery degrades sharply on harder tasks: MobileNetV2/CIFAR-100 reaches only 52.01% vs 69.89% clean.

03 Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can the frozen-reference mechanism adapt to LayerNorm Transformers (e.g. maintained LN running stats), or does it fundamentally require BN?
  2. How does the clean-reference margin behave under aggressive quantization (INT4 / mixed precision), where per-channel σ targets tighten?
  3. What is the real on-chip storage/area cost of per-channel z* targets plus thresholds at ImageNet scale, and can thresholds transfer across models or datasets to amortize profiling?
  4. Can an adaptive PBFA adversary jointly optimize damage and BN-score evasion (spreading flips under thresholds), and does the trust-gate FP asymmetry argument survive it?
  5. Could BLINK compose with classical ABFT/ECC redundancy — each catching the failure modes the other misses?
