> **一句话总结：** 作者把"潜语言识别（LLID）"重构为一个测量学问题：在同一批隐状态上对比表征探针（逐层 GMM 后验）与解码探针（logit lens / tuned lens + argmax/top-p rollout）后，发现两类探针系统性不一致——GMM 探针显示更早的跨语言混合、更低的语言主导性，而解码探针保留更尖锐、更偏英语的信号；例如在 INCLUDE 开放式问答的 50–75% 层窗口中 GMM 探针把概率质量更多分给任务相关语言而 raw logits 仍偏向英语，且在 OLMo-2 与 Apertus-8B 约 210B→4T token 的 10 个 token 对齐检查点上，两族探针的主导语言一致率全程保持低位——说明"多语言模型以英语为内部枢轴/lingua franca"的结论在相当程度上是探针选择（测量方法）的产物，而非对模型内部计算的直接揭示。

> **TL;DR:** This EPFL paper treats latent language identification (LLID) as a measurement problem instead of asking "do multilingual LLMs think in English?" It compares a GMM-based representation probe against decoding-based probes (raw logit lens, tuned lens, argmax/top-p rollouts classified by GlotLID) on the same hidden states, across 12 autoregressive models (7–9B, INCLUDE zero-shot macro-accuracy spanning 25.9 for GPT-2-XL to 65.0 for Apertus-8B-Instruct), up to 27 languages, controlled copy/cloze/translation tasks (16,146 translation prompts), open-ended PUD/INCLUDE QA, and 10 token-aligned OLMo-2/Apertus-8B checkpoints spanning ~210B→4T training tokens. The probes systematically disagree: on open-ended INCLUDE prompts in the 50–75% layer window the GMM probe assigns substantially more probability to the task-relevant language while raw-logits top-p decoding remains English-biased; representation–decoding agreement stays low across the entire 210B→4T pretraining span; and Apertus-8B's late-layer dominance only jumps to ~0.9 after a sharp transition between 20% and 40% of training, whereas OLMo-2's representation-probe dominance is high and stable from the first checkpoint. The takeaway for the Wendler et al. (2024) line of work: "English pivot" claims are partly an artifact of which probe you choose — each probe exposes a partial, evidence-specific view of multilingual computation rather than a single internal lingua franca.

## 核心贡献

- **测量学重构**：将潜语言识别（LLID）形式化为从隐状态估计语言分布 q(ℓ) ≈ Pr(Z=ℓ|h) 的诊断量，并提出熵（分布弥散度）、主导性（最大语言概率）、枢轴率（主导语言 ≠ 任务相关语言的比例）、探针间一致率四个互补指标，把"哪个探针"从技术细节提升为结论的一部分。
- **两族探针的系统性对比**：表征探针（逐层 GMM，以语言条件化的激活几何为证据）与解码探针（raw logits / tuned lens 投影 + START(w) 概率或 GlotLID 分类）作用于同一隐状态，却发现两族探针在层位轨迹、英语偏置程度上系统分歧：GMM 探针揭示更早的跨语言混合，解码探针保留更尖锐、更英语偏置的信号。
- **跨维度验证**：覆盖 12 个模型（按 INCLUDE 零样本宏准确率 25.9–65.0 排序的多语言轴）、至多 27 种语言、copy/cloze/translation 受控任务与开放式 PUD/INCLUDE 问答、3 个 QA 领域、语言清单变化（PUD9/PUD21/UD6），以及 OLMo-2-7B 与 Apertus-8B 各 10 个 token 对齐预训练检查点（~210B→4T token）。
- **训练动态与指令微调的实证结论**：两族探针的主导语言一致率在整个预训练过程中保持低位（Apertus-8B 尤其接近于零）；Apertus-8B 的晚层主导性在训练进度 20%–40% 之间经历剧变后达到 ~0.9，而 OLMo-2 的表征探针主导性从最早检查点（210B token）起就高且稳定；与预期相反，指令微调对 LLID 几乎没有朝英语方向的系统性影响，其效应远小于预训练检查点之间的差异。
- **对"英语枢轴"叙事的修正**：域与语言清单变化对 LLID 估计影响不大，层位行为主要由模型决定且无法仅凭多语言程度预测；现有探针揭示的是多语言计算的不同侧面，而不是单一的内部 lingua franca。

## Key Contributions

- **Measurement reframing of LLID:** formalizes latent language identification as estimating a diagnostic distribution q(ℓ) ≈ Pr(Z=ℓ|h) from a hidden state, with four complementary metrics — entropy, dominance (max-language probability), pivot rate (dominant language ≠ task-relevant language), and inter-probe agreement — making probe choice itself part of any LLID claim.
- **Head-to-head probe-family comparison on identical hidden states:** representation-based evidence (per-layer GMM posteriors over language-conditioned activation regions) systematically disagrees with decoding-based evidence (raw logits / tuned lens projections scored via START(w) probability or GlotLID-classified rollouts): the GMM probe reveals earlier cross-lingual mixing and weaker English dominance; decoding probes retain sharper, more English-biased language signals.
- **Breadth of evidence:** 12 models ordered by multilinguality (INCLUDE zero-shot macro-accuracy 25.9–65.0), up to 27 candidate languages, controlled copy/cloze/translation formats (16,146 translation prompts), open-ended PUD and INCLUDE QA across 3 domains, language-inventory variants, and 10 token-aligned pretraining checkpoints (~210B→4T tokens) for OLMo-2-7B and Apertus-8B.
- **Training-dynamics and instruction-tuning findings:** representation–decoding agreement stays low throughout pretraining for both model families (near zero for Apertus-8B); Apertus-8B shows a sharp transition between ~20% and ~40% of training after which late-layer dominance reaches ~0.9, while OLMo-2's representation dominance is high and stable from the earliest checkpoint and its decoding dominance consolidates within the first ~20–30%; instruction tuning produces little systematic shift toward English in LLID — far smaller than checkpoint-to-checkpoint differences.
- **Corrective to the "English pivot" narrative:** domain and language-inventory changes shift LLID only modestly; layerwise trajectories are model-specific and not predictable from multilinguality alone — current probes expose complementary aspects of multilingual processing rather than revealing a single internal lingua franca.

## Methodology

**Task definition.** Latent language Z is a latent variable over a finite candidate language set L; from a hidden state h ∈ R^d extracted at a given layer and position, each estimator produces a layerwise distribution q(ℓ) ≈ Pr(Z=ℓ|h). The paper is explicit that q is a *diagnostic estimate*, not ground truth.

**Two probe families.** (1) *Representation-based*: a per-layer GMM with one language-conditioned component per candidate language, fit on hidden states from ~100 training prompts per language (layerwise PCA retaining 98% variance, diagonal covariance, uniform priors); the posterior gives the layerwise language distribution. Calibration sets: pud9, pud21, pud9_ud6, pud21_ud6, and include_101lang for INCLUDE prompts (English added to the PUD calibration). (2) *Decoding-based*: the hidden state (after final-layer norm) is projected through the unembedding — either raw logits (logit lens) or a per-layer tuned lens (Belrose et al. 2025), the latter fit on FineWeb2 over a 27-language inventory, windows up to 2048 tokens — and language evidence is obtained either by summing next-token probability over START(w), the set of vocabulary tokens that can begin a language-specific answer word (controlled regime), or by decoding short continuations (deterministic argmax, or top-p with p = 0.9 sampled rollouts) from intermediate layers and classifying them with GlotLID (open-ended regime). Open-ended estimates aggregate over a 5-position forward window from the prompt anchor.

**Metrics.** Entropy H = −Σ q(ℓ) log q(ℓ); dominance D = max_ℓ q(ℓ); pivot rate P_x = 1[argmax q(ℓ) ≠ task-relevant ℓ_x]; agreement A = fraction of prompt-layer pairs where two estimators share the same dominant language. Training-dynamics plots additionally use a *confident pivot rate* requiring dominance ≥ 0.6.

**Evaluation regimes.** Controlled: copy, cloze, and translation formats built from the target-word setups of Wendler et al. (2024), Dumas et al. (2025), Zhong et al. (2025) — 414 prompts per copy/cloze task over 6 languages and 16,146 translation prompts over 9 languages. Open-ended: PUD sentence-level data (PUD9: 9 languages, 900 train + 900 held-out; PUD21: 21 languages, 2,100 + 2,100; plus a 6-language Universal Dependencies extension) and INCLUDE QA (882 prompts, 10 languages × Social Science / Arts & Humanities / STEM domains).

**Models and multilinguality axis.** 12 autoregressive models (GPT-2-XL, Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and its Instruct, Mistral-Nemo-9B and its Instruct, EuroLLM-9B, Aya-23-8B, OLMo-2-1124-7B and its Instruct, Apertus-8B and its Instruct), ordered by zero-shot macro-accuracy on INCLUDE over 44 language groups (lm-evaluation-harness on include-base-44), complemented by cross-lingual perplexity and byte-normalized likelihood (BPB) on PUD21+UD6. Checkpoint study: 10 token-aligned checkpoints per family (OLMo-2: 210B→3.95T tokens; Apertus: 210B→3.90T tokens), tracking dominance, confident pivot rate, and agreement in early/middle/late layer bins. Experiments ran on NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs (1–6 h per run each); code is public (github.com/bayazitdeniz/latent-lid).

## Results & Highlights

- **The multilinguality axis itself is wide:** on INCLUDE zero-shot QA (44 language groups) the evaluated models span 25.9 macro-accuracy (GPT-2-XL) to 65.0 (Apertus-8B-Instruct), with Apertus-8B base at 63.3 and Llama-2-7B near the bottom at 27.7 — the ordering used throughout the paper as the multilinguality proxy.
- **Decoding probes are systematically more English-biased in the open-ended regime:** on INCLUDE prompts, raw-logits top-p decoding assigns substantially more probability mass to English than the controlled START(w) setting suggests, while the tuned lens shifts mass toward the task-relevant language; the GMM probe goes further, assigning substantially more mass to the task-relevant language and less to English — the gap is most pronounced in the 50–75% layer window and largest for moderately multilingual models (e.g., Llama-3.1) while nearly closing for the most multilingual ones (Apertus / Apertus-Instruct).
- **Controlled regime:** across Llama-2-7B, Aya-23-8B, and Apertus-8B on 16,146 translation prompts, the decoded latent language signal is weak in early layers and becomes most visible in late layers; Llama-2-7B keeps relatively high probability mass on English lexicalizations (a stronger English-centered internal bias), Aya-23 is intermediate, and Apertus-8B's target-language probability rises earlier and becomes dominant in later layers — the English-pivot effect weakens as multilinguality increases.
- **Pretraining dynamics (PUD21, 10 token-aligned checkpoints, ~210B→4T tokens):** Apertus-8B undergoes a sharp transition in both estimators between roughly 20% and 40% of normalized training progress, after which late-layer dominance reaches ~0.9; OLMo-2-7B instead shows representation-probe dominance high and stable from the first checkpoint (210B tokens) with decoding dominance consolidating over the first ~20–30% of training; most checkpoint-dependent changes occur in the late layer bin (j/J ≥ 0.75), and representation–decoding agreement remains low across the entire trajectory for both families.
- **Robustness checks:** repeating the analysis over PUD9 / PUD9+UD6 / PUD21 / PUD21+UD6 and the three INCLUDE domains shows curves largely cluster — domain and language-inventory changes shift LLID estimates only modestly (PUD21 yields lower dominance and higher pivot rates than PUD9, as expected when mass spreads over 21 vs 9 candidate languages); confident-pivot analyses use a dominance ≥ 0.6 threshold to avoid counting fluctuations in diffuse distributions.
- **Instruction tuning is a non-event for LLID:** comparing base vs Instruct variants (e.g., Llama-3.1, OLMo-2, Apertus) shows instruction tuning produces little systematic shift toward English in LLID estimates — its overall effects are considerably smaller than the changes observed across pretraining checkpoints.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Genuinely novel framing: rather than adding another LLID probe, the paper interrogates whether LLID probes measure the same thing at all, and lands a pointed critique of the Wendler et al. (2024) "Llama-2 thinks in English" line — showing that on the same hidden states, a representation probe and a decoding probe can disagree sharply. This is a useful measurement-hygiene contribution to multilingual interpretability.

**Rigor.** The matched-condition design is careful: same prompts, same hidden states, same metrics across estimators; token-aligned checkpoints instead of step counts; multiple metrics with standard errors; and honest framing that no ground truth exists. However, several specifics weaken the headline asymmetry claim: (1) the entire "representation-based" family is instantiated with a *single* estimator — the GMM of Shani & Basirat (2025) — calibrated on PUD/INCLUDE rows in the very languages being evaluated, so its "task-relevant language advantage" could partly reflect in-domain calibration rather than purer evidence about internal geometry; no subspace-based (Zhao et al. 2025) or neuron-level (Zeng et al. 2025) representation estimators are tried, even though both are cited. (2) The paper is purely observational — no causal interventions (à la Dumas et al. 2025 activation patching) are run to test which probe tracks *computation* rather than geometry or decodability; the authors admit the work "cannot adjudicate which (if any) best reflects internal computation," which is honest but leaves the central interpretive question open. (3) Many headline comparisons (e.g., "substantially more task-relevant mass in the 50–75% window") are conveyed as figure trends without tabulated effect sizes — the reader cannot quote a single number for the central disagreement claim. (4) The checkpoint study covers only OLMo-2 and Apertus, the two families with public checkpoints — both deliberately balanced, non-English-centric models — so training-dynamics conclusions are untested on the English-centric models (Llama-2 family) for which the original English-pivot claims were made.

**Reproducibility.** Good: public code, documented fitting details (~100 prompts/language, 98% PCA variance, p = 0.9 rollouts, 5-position window), public checkpoints and standard datasets (PUD, INCLUDE, FineWeb2). The main reproducibility risk is the tuned-lens fitting corpus and GMM calibration choices, which the authors themselves flag as capable of shifting absolute values.

**Significance and red flags.** The claim that probe disagreement is "shaped by multilinguality" is supported, but the paper also concedes layerwise trajectories cannot be predicted from multilinguality alone — which somewhat undercuts the practical utility of the multilinguality axis. A quirk worth noting: GPT-2-XL (INCLUDE 25.9, a 2019-era model) serves as the extreme low anchor of the model ordering, a rather outdated baseline that mostly stretches the axis. No p-hacking concerns are apparent, error bars are reported, and the honesty of the Limitations section is above average for this subfield.

## Strengths

- Reframes a heated debate ("do LLMs think in English?") as a tractable measurement question, with estimator-agnostic metric definitions (entropy, dominance, pivot rate, agreement) that future probe papers can adopt directly.
- Unusually broad empirical scope for an interpretability paper: 12 models, up to 27 languages, 2 regimes (controlled + open-ended), 3 QA domains, and real pretraining checkpoints rather than only final models.
- Token-aligned checkpoint comparison (OLMo-2 210B→3.95T vs Apertus 210B→3.90T) is the right way to compare training dynamics across families, and the confident-pivot threshold (dominance ≥ 0.6) guards against over-reading diffuse distributions.
- Intellectual honesty: the Limitations section explicitly concedes there is no ground truth, that only one representation estimator is tested, and that probe-specific design choices may shift absolute values.
- Actionable takeaway for practitioners: never report LLID from a single probe family; domain and inventory changes matter little, so the probe-family disagreement is the stable phenomenon.

## Limitations

- Only decoder-only 7–9B models; no evidence that probe disagreement persists at other scales or in non-decoder architectures.
- Checkpoint analysis restricted to OLMo-2 and Apertus-8B — both balanced multilingual models — leaving the training dynamics of English-centric models (where English-pivot claims originated) unexamined.
- The representation-based family is instantiated only with the GMM estimator; language subspaces and neuron-level indicators are not compared, so "representation probes say X" rests on one implementation.
- No causal interventions or neuron-level attribution — the paper documents disagreement but cannot determine which probe (if either) reflects the computation actually used.
- No ground truth for latent language; probe-specific choices (GMM components, tuned-lens fitting corpus, open-ended anchor position, concept-word construction) may shift absolute LLID values, though the authors expect qualitative robustness.
- The 27-language inventory excludes very low-resource languages, code, and mathematics; and whether probe disagreement predicts downstream cross-lingual transfer or generation failures is left as an open question.

## Relevance

该文与用户"LLM 隐状态与可解释性"方向高度相关：它在同一组隐状态上系统比较了不同探针（GMM 表征几何 vs logit lens/tuned lens 解码）对"内部语言"读数的分歧，是探针方法学和表征几何分析的直接参考。文中基于 token 对齐检查点（210B→4T）追踪探针指标随训练进程演化的部分，对关注训练动态与检查点可靠性的第二优先方向也有一定借鉴价值；但与智能体自进化、训练稳定性告警、SDC 检测等核心方向无直接交集。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does representation–decoding disagreement causally predict downstream cross-lingual transfer or generation failures (e.g., language mixing in long-form generation)?
- Would other representation-based estimators — language subspaces (Zhao et al. 2025) or neuron-level indicators (Zeng et al. 2025) — agree with the GMM probe, or is the reported asymmetry GMM-specific?
- Can activation-patching interventions dissociate the two families: does editing the residual stream shift one probe's reading while leaving the other (and the output behavior) unchanged?
- Is Apertus-8B's sharp dominance transition at 20–40% of training attributable to an identifiable data-mixture or optimization event, and does an analogous transition exist in English-centric models whose checkpoints are private?
- If calibration-set overlap drives part of the GMM probe's task-relevant-language advantage, does fitting the GMM on a disjoint corpus eliminate the asymmetry between probe families?
