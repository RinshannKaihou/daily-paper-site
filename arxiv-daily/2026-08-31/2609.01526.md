> **一句话总结：** 提出 EvoSCM，把科学 agent 的"信念"从自由文本升级为可演化的结构因果模型（SCM）假设种群，通过"溯因-干预-预测-归纳-修正-演绎"的闭环发现循环持续修正因果结构与机制；在 DiscoverPhysics 基准上，GPT-5.5 骨干将机制解释分从 0.516 提至 0.751、归一化 MSE 从 2.83×10⁻² 降到 2.77×10⁻⁴（约两个数量级），pass@5 从 27.27% 升至 63.64%，且实验轮数从 2,206 降到 1,685；演化出的 SCM 还可零样本迁移到 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B，把后者 pass@1 从 0% 拉到最高 39.85%。

> **TL;DR:** EvoSCM replaces free-form scientific hypotheses with an explicit population of structural causal models (SCMs) that evolve through a closed discovery loop — discriminatory experiment design (Pearl's abduction-action-prediction), discrepancy-driven induction of correction rules, four structural revision operators, and deductive validation against accumulated evidence. On the DiscoverPhysics interactive benchmark, with GPT-5.5 as backbone it beats the standard-prompting baseline by raising mechanism explanation score from 0.516 to 0.751, cutting normalized forward-prediction MSE from 2.83×10⁻² to 2.77×10⁻⁴ (~100×), lifting pass@5 from 27.27% to 63.64%, all with fewer episodes (1,685 vs 2,206). Evolved SCMs also transfer zero-shot across model families: injected into Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (whose baseline pass@k is 0.00% on all k), a GPT-5.5-Sol SCM reaches 39.85% pass@1 and 63.64% pass@5 with near-zero prediction error.

## 核心贡献

- **显式认识论状态（epistemic state）**：将科学 agent 的信念表示为一个相互竞争的 SCM 假设种群 P_t = {H_1...H_K}，每个假设包含潜变量 U、因果图 G、结构方程 F 与参数 Θ，使假设可检验、可归因（预测-观测偏差可追溯到具体结构或参数）、可修正。
- **闭环发现循环**：两个耦合阶段——(1) 因果实验设计：按 Pearl 的溯因→行动→预测框架，为每个假设推断潜变量状态，选择使种群内假设预测分歧最大化的干预（Lindley 1956 / Box & Hill 1967 式的判别性实验设计），并在执行前提交可证伪预测；(2) 假设修正：把预测-观测偏差归纳为显式修正规则，通过加/删边、加/删潜变量、更新机制、更新参数四种算子做"偏好浅层编辑"的结构修改，再经证据验证（不得只修复最近偏差而引入新矛盾）与一致性检查（DAG 合法、方程良定义）做种群级淘汰。
- **可迁移的知识产物**：演化后的 SCM 是可执行、可审计的因果模型，与产生它的 agent 解耦；可跨基座模型零样本注入（GPT-5.5 → Qwen3.6-35B-A3B），并支持对未见干预做 do(·) 算子式反事实预测与种群共识聚合。
- **实证结果**：在 DiscoverPhysics 上于 GPT-5.4 与 GPT-5.5 两个骨干上全面超越基线，在解释质量、预测误差、pass@k 与实验效率四个维度同时领先。

## Key Contributions

- **Explicit epistemic state for scientific agents**: beliefs are a population of K competing SCM hypotheses (latent variables U, causal graph G, structural equations F, parameters Θ) rather than free-form text, making predictions falsifiable, discrepancies attributable to specific structural/parametric commitments, and revisions testable.
- **A closed discovery loop** alternating (1) Causal Experiment Design — per-hypothesis abduction of latent states, intervention selection that maximizes prediction disagreement among competing hypotheses (population-level Box & Hill 1967-style discrimination), pre-registered falsifiable predictions, and (2) SCM Hypothesis Revision — induction of natural-language correction rules from prediction-observation discrepancies, targeted structural edits via four operators (add/remove edge, add/remove latent, update mechanism, update parameter; preferring shallow edits), and deductive validation (evidence validation + DAG/mechanism consistency check) that discards contradicted hypotheses to form the next population.
- **Portable discovered knowledge**: the evolved SCM is decoupled from the agent trajectory that produced it — it is inspectable, auditable, supports do-operator counterfactual prediction on unseen interventions with population-consensus aggregation, and transfers zero-shot across model families.
- **Empirical wins on all four metrics** (mechanism explanation quality, normalized forward-prediction MSE, pass@k, episode efficiency) on DiscoverPhysics across two frontier backbones (GPT-5.4, GPT-5.5).

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** Agentic scientific discovery is formalized as sequential recovery of an unknown causal structure in environment E governed by a true SCM M* = (V, U*, G*, F*, Θ*) (Pearl 2009): V observable, U* latent exogenous, G* the DAG, F* structural equations with parameters Θ*. Over T rounds the agent selects interventions do(X_t = x_t) on a subset of V, observes outcomes for remaining variables Y, and accumulates evidence D_t = {(do(x_i), y_i)}. The agent's hypothesis H = (V, U, G, F, Θ) shares observables V but posits its own latents, graph, mechanisms, and parameters; the goal is to evolve H until it recovers M*'s structure, mechanisms, and predictive behavior.

**Population-based epistemic state.** Instead of committing to a single hypothesis, EvoSCM maintains P_t = {H_1^t, ..., H_K^t}. Diversity guards against premature convergence to a wrong-but-consistent explanation, and disagreement between hypotheses directly drives experiment design via interventions under which candidate predictions diverge maximally.

**Phase 1 — Causal Experiment Design** (Pearl's abduction-action-prediction, extended with an experimentation step):
- *Abduction*: for each H_k, infer latent variable states most consistent with accumulated evidence D_t, conditioning on all past interventions and observations — each hypothesis gets its own causal interpretation of the same data.
- *Action*: choose the next intervention do(X_{t+1} = x_{t+1}) that maximally discriminates among the competing hypotheses, i.e. exploits population disagreement so the experiment yields maximal evidence for distinguishing candidates.
- *Prediction*: before executing, each hypothesis commits a hypothesis-specific prediction y_k^pre ~ P(Y | do(X_{t+1} = x_{t+1}), D_t, H_k^t) — a pre-registered, falsifiable claim preventing post-hoc rationalization and making discrepancies unambiguously attributable.
- *Experimentation & feedback*: execute the intervention in E, observe y_{t+1}^obs from the true M*, compute per-hypothesis prediction-observation discrepancies, and append to evidence D_{t+1}.

**Phase 2 — SCM Hypothesis Revision** (three steps):
- *Induction*: rather than treating each discrepancy as an isolated error, examine the accumulated evidence for recurring patterns the hypothesis fails to capture, distilled into explicit natural-language correction rules — a qualitative interface specifying *what* must change without prescribing *how*.
- *Revision*: apply the minimal set of four operators sufficient to accommodate the rules — add/remove edge (graph edit), add/remove latent (unexplained confounding or redundant latent), update mechanism (change functional form, e.g. linear → inverse-square law), update parameter (quantitative fit while preserving causal form) — preferring shallow (parameter/mechanism) edits and reserving deeper structural edits for when evidence warrants.
- *Deduction (validation)*: each candidate revised hypothesis must pass (1) Evidence Validation — predictions must account for all accumulated interventions, not merely the latest discrepancy, without introducing new contradictions; and (2) Consistency Check — G remains a valid DAG, equations well-defined, mechanisms mutually compatible (no variable both exogenous and a descendant of endogenous variables). Failures are discarded from the population; survivors seed P_{t+1} and the next experiment-design round.

**Inference with the evolved SCM.** After T rounds, a novel intervention is answered by each surviving H's structural equations, with predictions aggregated across the population into a consensus outcome; because prediction derives from the causal graph and mechanisms via the do-operator, it generalizes to unseen interventions rather than interpolating stored input-output associations.

**Evaluation setup.** DiscoverPhysics (Wiemann et al. 2026): an interactive benchmark where agents probe noncanonical physical worlds through active interventions and iteratively refine hypotheses of underlying physical laws, isolating genuine discovery from memorized canonical physics. Metrics: mechanism explanation quality, forward prediction error (normalized MSE), pass rate across trials (pass@k), interaction efficiency (episodes). Backbones: GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5 vs a standard-prompting baseline; transfer targets: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B injected with SCMs evolved by GPT-5.4 / GPT-5.5 / GPT-5.5-Sol / Qwen3.6 itself.

## Results & Highlights

- **GPT-5.5 backbone (Table 1)**: EvoSCM raises mechanism explanation score from 0.516 (baseline) to 0.751 and reduces normalized MSE by two orders of magnitude, from 2.833×10⁻² to 2.770×10⁻⁴ (~102× lower), while pass@5 jumps from 27.27% to 63.64% (+36.4 pts) and pass@1 from 7.25% to 53.04% — all with fewer simulator episodes (1,685 vs 2,206).
- **GPT-5.4 backbone (Table 1)**: the gap is even larger — explanation 0.653 vs 0.298, normalized MSE 2.336×10⁻³ vs 5.240×10⁻¹ (~224× lower), pass@1 38.08% vs 1.86%, pass@5 54.55% vs 9.09%, with 736 vs 1,045 episodes.
- **Cross-model transfer (Table 2)**: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B alone fails outright on DiscoverPhysics (explanation 0.307, MSE 0.190, pass@1–5 all 0.00%). Injecting SCMs evolved by stronger models progressively boots it up: +Qwen3.6's own SCM → 14.95% pass@1 / 36.36% pass@5 (MSE 0.063); +GPT-5.4 SCM → 16.40% pass@1 / 27.27% pass@5 (MSE 0.015); +GPT-5.5-Sol SCM → explanation 0.740, MSE 0.000 (rounded), 39.85% pass@1 and 63.64% pass@5 — rivaling the zero-shot discovery capability of frontier models.
- **Sample efficiency (Figure 1a)**: the baseline GPT-5.5 agent plateaus around ~10⁻² forward-prediction error over 2,000 episodes, while EvoSCM converges to its best performance (~10⁻⁴) by roughly episode 1,400–1,600, with the steepest gains between episodes ~1,000–1,400 after the exploration phase.
- **Transfer ranking is consistent with source strength**: explanation score on Qwen3.6 rises monotonically with the transferring model's capability (0.489 own-SCM < 0.576 GPT-5.4-SCM < 0.625 GPT-5.5-SCM < 0.740 GPT-5.5-Sol-SCM), supporting the claim that the SCM encodes portable epistemic content rather than backbone-specific habits.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from Sun Yat-sen University (Qing Zhao, Haowei Li, Pengxu Wei, Liang Lin) and Tsinghua Shenzhen International Graduate School (Weijian Deng). Liang Lin is a senior, well-established researcher in computer vision and embodied/agentive AI with a long publication record, lending the team credibility in agent systems. The work is a September 2026 preprint (arXiv:2609.01526v1, cs.AI) with no stated peer-review venue; the paper is short (8 pages, no appendix or supplementary visible), and it engages appropriately with the relevant literature (self-evolving agents — GEPA, ReasoningBank, SkillOpt, AFlow; closed-loop discovery — Klabra et al., automated hypothesis falsification — Huang et al.; the DiscoverPhysics benchmark authors). Citations are current (2025–2026) and the evaluation builds directly on an external benchmark rather than a home-grown one, which is a good sign.

## Critical Assessment

- **Single-benchmark evaluation.** Every headline number comes from DiscoverPhysics physical-world discovery. There is no evidence the loop transfers to other scientific domains (chemistry, biology, data analysis), and SCM-with-differential-equation-style mechanisms is a natural fit for physics worlds specifically; generality is asserted, not shown.
- **The baseline is weak.** The comparison is "standard prompting / direct baseline exploration strategy" on the same backbone — essentially an unstructured agent against the structured one. The paper cites closed-loop discovery systems it does not compare against (e.g., Klabra et al. 2026's closed-loop active experimentation, Huang et al. 2025's agentic sequential falsifications), so the delta attributable to EvoSCM's specific design versus simply "any structured hypothesis-plus-experiment loop" is unmeasured.
- **No ablations.** The paper never isolates its own components: population vs single hypothesis, the four revision operators (are latent add/remove or mechanism updates actually exercised?), correction-rule induction vs direct discrepancy-to-edit prompting, and discriminatory experiment design vs random/heuristic interventions. Population size K and round count T are never stated in the main text, and there is no sensitivity analysis over either.
- **No variance reporting.** pass@k is reported without the number of trials/worlds, seeds, or error bars. Given that single-run discovery on stochastic environments can swing widely, the 1.86% → 38.08% pass@1 claim at GPT-5.4 needs more than a point estimate.
- **Compute cost is hidden.** Episodes go down (2,206 → 1,685 at GPT-5.5), but each EvoSCM round runs abduction for K hypotheses, population-wide prediction, rule induction, revision, and deductive validation — token cost per episode is plausibly several times the baseline's, yet no token or wall-clock accounting is given. "Sample efficiency" may not mean cost efficiency.
- **Transfer result conflates knowledge with capability.** The strongest transfer row (Qwen3.6 + GPT-5.5-Sol SCM) means the small model executes a frontier model's distilled physics; that demonstrates portability of the artifact (the paper's actual claim), but it is not evidence the small model learned to discover. Also, "GPT-5.5-Sol" — apparently a single best evolved SCM rather than the full population — is never precisely defined in the main text.
- **Explanation quality is a benchmark-internal metric.** The 0.516 → 0.751 mechanism-explanation gain is scored by DiscoverPhysics's own rubric; no human or independent verification of the evolved causal graphs is presented, so "mechanism alignment" rests on the benchmark's automatic judge.

## Strengths

- Genuinely addresses a recognized gap: procedural self-evolving agents improve *how* to reason, not *what* to believe; making beliefs explicit, structured, and falsifiable is the right kind of orthogonal contribution.
- The design is principled and well-grounded — Pearl's abduction-action-prediction, Box & Hill model discrimination, and pre-registered falsifiable predictions give the loop real epistemological discipline rather than prompt engineering.
- Cross-model transferability is a distinctive, well-motivated result: the epistemic artifact decouples from the producing agent, and the monotonic transfer ranking across source model strength (0.489 → 0.576 → 0.625 → 0.740) is a clean piece of supporting evidence.
- Wins on all four reported axes simultaneously (explanation, prediction error, pass@k, episodes) rather than trading one for another, and the two-orders-of-magnitude MSE reduction is a large, unambiguous effect.
- The evidence-validation step (rejecting hypotheses that fix the latest discrepancy but contradict older evidence) is a thoughtful guard against the well-known LLM failure of myopically over-fitting to the most recent observation.

## Limitations

- Validated only on DiscoverPhysics; no second domain, no real-world scientific data, no ablation of the discovery loop's components, and unspecified K and T.
- No token/cost accounting despite a per-round pipeline (K-way abduction + population prediction + induction + revision + validation) that is inherently more expensive per episode than the baseline.
- No variance, seeds, or number-of-worlds disclosure for the pass@k statistics.
- Transferability is demonstrated in one direction (strong → weak) on one small receiver (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, a MoE model); whether SCMs transfer between comparable-strength models or to API-constrained settings is untested.
- The evolutionary dynamics are unexamined: no analysis of population diversity over time, hypothesis collapse, or failure modes when all K hypotheses are wrong in compatible ways.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What do the token/cost curves look like per episode — does the K-hypothesis population make EvoSCM strictly more expensive than the baseline even with fewer episodes?
- How does performance degrade as K shrinks to 1 (single hypothesis) — is the population mechanism or the structured representation doing the heavy lifting?
- Can the four revision operators be audited empirically: how often are latents added/removed versus parameters updated, and do correction rules converge to the true mechanisms of the hidden worlds?
- Does the loop survive contact with noisier, partially observable, or non-physical domains (chemistry, biology, online experimentation) where mechanisms are less cleanly expressible as SCM equations?
- When a transferred SCM encodes physics *wrong* for the new world (e.g., transferring a model evolved on Newtonian worlds to a noncanonical world with different laws), does the receiving agent's revision loop detect and repair the imported beliefs, or does the portable artifact actively mislead?
- Does population consensus aggregation dominate selecting the single best-validated hypothesis at inference time, and by how much?
