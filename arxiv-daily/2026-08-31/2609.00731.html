Key metric

OOS Sharpe — rolling re-execution vs its own static arm

Same starting library, never re-discovering, scores 0.71 — and the raw-400 no-discovery floor is 0.62. Panel A (JKP), ~71 OOS months, 3 replicates.

Why this matters

No surveyed autonomous-discovery system backtests its own process; this rolling protocol transfers directly to evaluating agentic scientific discovery in any field.

Critical edge

With 5 replicates, 59–71 OOS months, and the strongest baseline reconstructed from never-released code, the flagship “no consistent winner” finding may partly be noise — the paper never separates the two explanations.

Methodology

  1. Formal definition. AEAP = an LLM agent autonomously executing a full hypothesis → formalization → evaluation cycle with no human in those steps; five building blocks B1–B5 (generation, formalization, sandboxed execution, evaluation gate, memory & iteration).
  2. Three principles. P1 point-in-time data, P2 point-in-time process, P3 OOS-only results — and no surveyed system satisfies P2.
  3. SEADS loop. Planner emits explore/exploit seeds → parallel Proposers expand them into spec+formula → AST sandbox scoped to statically-read columns, a deterministic look-ahead firewall (7 rule classes) and an alignment checker → a nine-check gate with ex-ante thresholds and a Bonferroni-dynamic Rank-IC bar → self-evolution mutates near-passes through the identical gate → ridge (α=1) rank-normalized combinator builds a monthly long-short book.
  4. Rolling protocol. Re-run the loop at historical decision dates (annual re-discovery, 4-month combinator refit); a static-arm control isolates re-propose itself.
  5. Comparison. Six systems × two panels (JKP ~400 characteristics; CRSP/Compustat 87 raw variables), matched 300-candidate budget, identical GPT-5.2 backbone and scoring code.

  • No single static metric ranks all six systems: Beyond Prompting tops Panel A performance (0.41) but is the least novel; SEADS leads Panel B novelty outright (0.46) and ties atop Panel B performance (0.11).
  • Productivity without a gate is not discovery: Beyond Prompting admits 96.4 vs SEADS’s 14.0 — partly a looser gate admitting predominantly redundant formulas (Appendix H).
  • Deflationary negatives: independently-admitted factors do not guarantee a monotone joint OOS contribution; a soft GPT-5.2 backbone-leakage residual persists. Spanning-test alpha: SEADS positive on both panels (1.27 / 1.28); AlphaForge −0.77 on Panel A.

Strengths

  • A shared vocabulary the field lacked: the AEAP definition and B1–B5 taxonomy cleanly separate autonomous discovery from copilots, debate pipelines and non-agentic search — operationalized precisely enough to classify every surveyed system.
  • The output-vs-system distinction is a real methodological advance; the static-arm control is an elegant identification device, and its 0.71-vs-0.96 result is itself a substantive finding.
  • Pre-registered gate design: nine checks, ex-ante thresholds, Bonferroni-dynamic Rank-IC bar, a schema-agnostic sandbox + look-ahead firewall running unmodified across two very different panels.
  • Multi-axis scoring resists gaming — the Beyond Prompting case shows the standard doing exactly its job.
  • An unusually deflationary, negative-finding reporting posture; the limitations section discloses that replicate counts reflect API budget.

Limitations

  • Underpowered comparisons: tens of admits per replicate, 3–5 replicates, no formal cross-system significance testing — most differences are not reliably distinguishable, by the authors’ own admission.
  • Two of five baselines are paper-text reconstructions, including the dominant one (Beyond Prompting releases no code); reimplementation fidelity is unverifiable.
  • Soft, unquantified backbone leakage: one GPT-5.2 backbone plays every role at every historical date.
  • Compute matching is circular — self-evolution, the mechanism behind the 68% swing, adds uncontrolled LLM calls beyond one proposal per slot.
  • Portfolio results are combinator- and cost-agnostic: ridge-only aggregator, no transaction costs or turnover, small-cap capacity unmodeled.
  • Scope confined to factor discovery on two US equity panels with different OOS endpoints; single backbone tested; no code release stated.

Questions for further exploration

  1. With more replicates and longer OOS windows, do the multi-axis rankings stabilize — or is “no consistent winner” itself partly a small-sample artifact? Can the two be separated statistically?
  2. Does rolling re-execution transfer to trading policy, structural estimation, or agentic discovery in biology, chemistry and materials?
  3. How large is the GPT-5.2 leakage residual — do proposed factor families systematically cluster around post-in-sample published findings?
  4. Do the rolling-advantage conclusions survive a portfolio-aware admission gate, transaction costs, turnover constraints and capacity limits?
  5. Is self-evolution’s 68% contribution specific to formulaic factors, or does it persist for tree/NN-based signals where the firewall needs redesign?
