Critical edge

No compute accounting: induction, per-criterion verification, and up to 3 revision rounds add substantial un-costed LLM calls the baselines never receive — part of every headline gain may simply be extra compute per task.

Critical Assessment

01 Methodology — rubrics as intermediate specifications

  • Stage I — Rubric Induction (before execution). Four steps turn the under-specified instruction into an executable rubric: skeleton induction (atomic, traceable scientific goals) → literature grounding (broad retrieval over arXiv/OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar; 5–7 core papers summarized by theme) → task-data exploration (files, fields, units, feasibility — planning only) → criterion synthesis (M criteria, each linking goals to required experiments, metrics, and expected evidence artifacts).
  • Stage II — Guided Revision (during/after execution). A verifier scores each criterion 0/1 and diagnoses evidence gaps; the agent then revises targetedly — add missing experiments or comparisons, remove unsupported claims — capped at 3 rounds with adaptive early stopping when all criteria pass.
  • Controlled protocol. Paired vanilla vs. +AutoSciRub share harness, backbone, data, workspace, and tools; official benchmark judges score each submission three times (mean taken): GPT-5.1 for ResearchClawBench, MiniMax-M3 for AstaBench. All gains are absolute paired differences.

  • Ablation decomposes the value: base 17.25 → +skeleton +0.36 → +grounded rubric 18.31 → +revision +2.05 (full 20.36). The rubric matters most when used to verify criteria and drive targeted fixes — not just to guide execution.
  • Not "more iteration": from the same checkpoint, three rounds of rubric-free self-refinement reach only +0.77 (with a −0.21 regression mid-way) vs the rubric-guided +2.05 — the ~2.7× control.
  • Rubric quality shifts: Specificity 1.65 → 4.40, Evidence Verifiability 1.78 → 4.08 — but Scientific Core Coverage drops 3.55 → 3.07; 49/60 domain pairs improve, with degradations in astronomy and materials (MiniMax-M3).
  • Broad, not outlier-driven: 34/40 tasks improve; 35/40 pass the verifier within 3 revisions; best absolute score is 22.73/100 vs ~50 for target-paper re-discovery — far from real scientific competence.

03 核心贡献

  • 「先学会评估，再谈改进」：把开放式科研任务中隐含的分析、方法与成功标准，在执行之前转化为显式的任务专属可执行量表，而非事后打分。
  • 两阶段设计：骨架归纳 → 文献接地（保留 5–7 篇核心文献）→ 任务数据探查 → 准则综合；执行后逐准则验证（满足/不满足 + 证据缺口）并针对性修订，最多 3 轮。
  • 广泛对照：3 骨干 × Codex + 3 框架 × DeepSeek-V4-Flash 共 6 组配对实验，平均 +2.08 / +2.95；AstaBench 20 任务子集平均 +16.78。
  • 机制分析：Specificity 1.65→4.40、可验证性 1.78→4.08，但科学核心覆盖率 3.55→3.07 略降；量表引导修订收益约为自由重写的 2.7 倍。

04 Strengths

  • Correct, well-motivated thesis: rubrics as intermediate scientific specifications, not post-hoc grade sheets.
  • Controlled paired design across 3 backbones, 3 harnesses, and 2 external community benchmarks — gains are not idiosyncratic.
  • Stage-wise ablation with a rubric-free self-refinement control cleanly rules out the generic "more iteration helps" explanation.
  • Honest negative signals: coverage regression, domain degradations, and the low absolute ceiling are all reported prominently.

05 Limitations

  • No token/dollar normalization — the compute effect on +2.08/+2.95/+16.78 cannot be quantified.
  • Single run per configuration, no error bars or significance tests; the unexplained 6× gap between AstaBench (+16.78) and RCB (+2.08) deltas weakens the headline.
  • LLM-judge evaluation throughout — including a same-vendor-family judge — with no human agreement study and plausible bias toward rubric-conformant artifacts.
  • Novelty is framing-and-system, not algorithm: standard prompt-orchestrated tool use; frontier-model dependence limits replication despite the code release.

06 Questions for further exploration

  • How much of the gain survives a token- or dollar-matched budget (budget-matched self-refinement or best-of-N for the vanilla baseline)?
  • Can criterion-level verification signals (per-criterion 0/1 + evidence gaps) become a training reward for RL on research agents?
  • Why is the AstaBench gain ~6× the RCB gain for the same agents — evaluator, task difficulty, or rubric-protocol sensitivity? A judge-swap ablation would settle it.
  • Does the Scientific Core Coverage regression (3.55 → 3.07) persist or worsen with weaker grounding models or open-weights-only setups?
  • Can rubrics be induced once per task family and failed criteria mined into a reusable domain-level scientific-requirements library?
