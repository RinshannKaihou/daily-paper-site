> **一句话总结：** 在 mGPT-1.3B、Aya Expanse-8B、Llama 3-8B 三个多语言模型上，用三组受控词序最小句数据集（NP 197 句/8 语、SVO 134 句/5 语、MV 31 句/4 语）配合 LogitLens 与激活修补（pyvene），证明翻译按"句法结构 S → 表层语言 L → 词汇内容 C"三阶段依次确立（9 个模型×数据集组合中 8 个成立；如 Aya 在 NP 上仅修补句法的峰值 Δmax=0.10 出现在第 11 层，加语言为 0.52@19 层，加内容为 0.65@31 层），且目标语词序由 S-switch 层中单个对词序敏感、对语言身份不变的注意力头（Llama 3 的 H14.25、Aya 的 H15.0，修补影响比最高达 R≈3–5）承载——句法是独立于语言与语义的第三个翻译组件，英语充当概念锚点而非句法中介。

> **TL;DR:** This mechanistic study extends the known two-way decomposition of translation in multilingual LLMs (conceptual content vs. output language) to a three-way one by establishing target-language word order (syntax, S) as a third, independently mechanized stage. Using LogitLens and activation patching on three controlled minimal-sentence datasets (NP: 197 samples across 8 languages; SVO: 134/5; MV: 31/4; ~24K one-shot translation prompts total) fed to mGPT-1.3B, Aya Expanse-8B, and Llama 3-8B, the staged S→L→C ordering holds in 8 of 9 model–dataset combinations — e.g., on NP with Aya Expanse, syntax-only patches peak at layer 11 (Δmax 0.10), adding surface language at layer 19 (0.52), and full lexical content at layer 31 (0.65). Syntactic commitment localizes to single word-order-sensitive, language-invariant attention heads (Llama 3 H14.25, Aya H15.0; patch-effect ratios up to R≈3–5 vs ≈2 for mGPT's distributed heads), and mean-activation patching shifts word order across languages without raising patch-language probability (KL divergence separates word-order contrasts from language contrasts) — so English acts as a conceptual anchor, not a syntactic pivot.

## 核心贡献

- **三分解框架**：将 Dumas et al. (2025) 的"概念内容/输出语言"二分解扩展为三分解——句法结构（词序）是第三个可独立追踪、且在机制上独立的翻译组件，先于表层语言确立（S→L→C，9 个模型×数据集组合中 8 个成立）。
- **句法先于表层语言**：模型在中间层已按目标语词序组织预测，而预测的 token 仍是英语/源语（如德→法翻译中，中间层偏好英语名词 "apple" 而非形容词，与法语 "pomme rouge" 的名词在前语序一致）——修正了 Wendler et al. (2024) 的"英语枢纽"叙事：英语是概念锚点，不是句法中介。
- **头级定位**：Llama 3（H14.25）与 Aya Expanse（H15.0）的 S-switch 层中各有一个注意力头单独承载词序选择（对所有 S_plant token 的修补影响比 R(h,t)>1）；而训练语料均衡的 mGPT 将该功能分散到 H11.2（R≈1.44）、H11.7（R≈1.27）等多个头，且其最强头同时影响表层语言。
- **功能性而非几何性**：S-head 的跨语言平均激活余弦相似度不按词序聚类——这些头在功能上对词序敏感、对语言身份不变，但并未把词序表示在一个全局共享的激活空间中。
- **受控数据与开源**：构建了三组隔离跨语言词序差异的最小句平行数据集（NP/SVO/MV，共 ~24K 翻译 prompt），基于 pyvene 扩展了 Aya Expanse 的干预代码并开源。

## Key Contributions

- **A three-way decomposition of translation.** Word order is introduced as a third independently trackable component alongside lexical content and surface language, extending Dumas et al. (2025); syntactic structure is established *before* surface-language realization while representations remain aligned with an English-adjacent conceptual space.
- **Causal staging evidence.** Layer-wise activation patching shows the layer of maximal patch effect (Δmax) increases monotonically from syntax-only → syntax+language → full patches in 8 of 9 model–dataset combinations; the single exception (Llama 3 on SVO) collapses S and L to the same layer 14.
- **Head-level localization with a language-invariance test.** Single heads in the S-switch layers (Llama 3 H14.25, Aya H15.0) causally drive the word-order switch; mean activation patching with same-order vs. different-order languages shows these heads encode word order independently of surface language.
- **Functional-vs-geometric distinction.** The S-heads' activations do not cluster by word order across languages (cosine similarity analysis), so syntax is computed through shared mechanisms without a globally shared word-order subspace — a genuinely new observation about how multilingual circuits organize.
- **Controlled datasets and released code.** Three minimal-pair parallel datasets isolating adjective–noun order (NP), SVO/SOV (SVO), and modal–infinitive order (MV), with construction pipeline (200 Wendler et al. picture-nouns, gpt-4o adjectives, DeepL translation + manual word-order checks) documented in the appendix.

## Methodology

**Models.** Three mLLMs chosen to span the English-bias spectrum: mGPT 1.3B (balanced multilingual pretraining, no dominant language), Aya Expanse 8B (pretraining mix not publicly disclosed), and Llama 3 8B (predominantly English-trained).

**Data.** Three parallel minimal-sentence datasets, each isolating one syntactic contrast that differs across the included languages (Table 1): **NP** (197 samples; adjective–noun order; 5 adjective-first languages — Chinese, Dutch, English, German, Russian — plus 3 noun-first: French, Italian, Vietnamese), **SVO** (134 samples; SVO languages Chinese/English/Russian vs. SOV Japanese/Turkish), **MV** (31 samples; modal-before-infinitive Chinese/English/German vs. Turkish). All are generated from English source expressions using 200 simple concrete nouns (from Wendler et al.'s picture-word list); adjectives via ChatGPT (gpt-4o), SVO sentences written manually, translation via DeepL with manual checks/corrections of non-canonical word order. Language triplets (90 for NP, 36 for SVO, 16 for MV) cross base and plant target languages that differ in word order, yielding 17,730 / 5,698 / 496 translation prompts respectively (~24K total). Prompts are one-shot ("[Source language]: [sentence] - [Target language]: [incomplete target]") with the exemplar drawn from the same dataset.

**Interventions.** Each experiment pairs a base prompt p_base with a plant prompt p_plant varying along three factors: surface language L, syntactic structure S (fully determined by L in natural language, but separable through the pairing), and lexical content C. Using pyvene (Wu et al., 2024, extended for Aya Expanse), activations are replaced in the clean run at selected layers/positions/modules, and next-token probability shifts are tracked across the 8 base/plant combinations of S, L, C. The headline metric is Δmax — the maximum probability difference between patched and clean runs — together with the layer where it occurs.

**Head analysis.** Within each model's S-switch layer (the single layer causing the S_base→S_plant switch: layer 11 for mGPT, 15 for Aya, 14 for Llama 3), individual attention heads are patched and scored by the influence ratio R(h,t) = P(t | Patch(h)) / mean over heads of P(t | Patch(h')), so a head disproportionally influencing S_plant tokens stands out.

**Language-independence tests.** (i) *Mean activation patching*: the most S-sensitive head is patched with its average activation from a different target language, varying whether the patch language shares the base target's word order; effects are quantified with KL divergence (Eq. 2) on token distributions that contrast parts of speech (word-order signal) vs. languages (language signal). (ii) *Geometry check*: cosine similarity between the S-head's mean activations across target languages tests whether word order forms a shared representational subspace. A small natural-data control (27 annotated FLORES-200 sentences, English/German/French) and a LogitLens+patching fusion analysis (Appendix C.2) round out the design.

## Results & Highlights

- **Staged S→L→C ordering (Table 3, averaged across language pairs).** On NP with Aya Expanse, the syntax-only patch (S_plant, L_base, C_base) peaks at Δmax = 0.10 in layer 11, adding the plant language peaks at 0.52 in layer 19, and the full patch (S, L, C_plant) at 0.65 in layer 31 — strictly increasing layers for S, L, C. Llama 3 on NP shows the same staircase: 0.09@14 → 0.21@16 → 0.55@25. The layer ordering holds in 8 of 9 model–dataset combinations; the one exception is Llama 3 on SVO, where syntax and language switch at the same layer 14 (0.00 and 0.11 respectively; full-content Δmax 0.34@27), a collapsed "S,L→C" strategy.
- **Syntax-specific effects are real but modest.** Syntax-only Δmax values range from 0.00 to 0.10 across models and datasets (mGPT: 0.00–0.09; e.g., mGPT on MV: 0.00@7 vs. full patch 0.35@23), yet their layers consistently precede the language switch — the authors take this as evidence for dedicated syntax-specific translation components, weakest in the balanced-multilingual mGPT.
- **English intermediates already carry target word order (LogitLens).** For German→French NP items, Llama 3 and Aya's intermediate layers favor the English noun ("apple") rather than the adjective ("red"), consistent with French noun-first order ("pomme rouge"); the inverse holds for French→German (adjective "roter Apfel" preferred). So syntax is resolved before surface language while the predicted token itself remains English; mGPT shows no English intermediates at all.
- **Single-head localization (Section 4.3).** In Llama 3 and Aya Expanse, one head each in the S-switch layer — H14.25 and H15.0 — strongly influences syntax with R(h,t) > 1 for *all* S_plant tokens; head-patch effects reach R ≈ 3–5 for Aya/Llama 3 versus below R ≈ 2 for mGPT. mGPT instead distributes the effect across heads (H11.2 R ≈ 1.44, H11.7 R ≈ 1.27), and its top-scoring head also shifts surface language, so syntax and language cannot be cleanly disentangled for that model.
- **Word order is encoded independently of language (Section 5).** Under mean activation patching of the S-head (NP, English source), KL divergence between base and patched distributions is consistently higher when base and patch languages differ in word order (adj-noun vs. noun-adj) and low when they share word order, with no comparable structure for pure language contrasts — patching shifts the predicted part of speech without raising probability mass for the patch language. The effect is most pronounced in mGPT and Llama 3 and weaker/less symmetric in Aya; a flagged exception is that when German is the base target language, the syntax effect disappears in Aya and Llama 3.
- **No geometric word-order clustering (Figure 8).** Cosine similarities between the S-head's mean activations across target languages show no clustering by word order — the heads are functionally word-order-sensitive without representing word order in a globally similar activation space.
- **Fragile natural-data check (Table 6).** On the 27-sentence FLORES-200 control, the S→L→C pattern is retained for mGPT (0.02@11 → 0.41@21) and Aya (full patch 0.66@30), but Llama 3 collapses to S,L→C (syntax and language both at layer 14; full patch 0.56@31).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** This is a genuine, well-scoped incremental advance rather than a reinvention: relative to Dumas et al. (2025) it adds a third tracked factor (S) and shows it resolves first, and relative to Wendler et al. (2024) it *refines* the English-pivot story — English tokens persist in intermediates but already carry target-language word order, making English a conceptual rather than syntactic anchor. The functional-vs-geometric finding (heads steer word order across languages, yet activations do not cluster by word order) is the most original observation and deserves follow-up.

**Rigor concerns.** The evidential base is thin in places. The MV dataset has only 31 samples and 16 language triplets (496 prompts), so layer-ordering claims for the modal-verb construction rest on very few sentence templates; no variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests are reported anywhere — every Δmax and the "8 of 9" count are point estimates averaged across language pairs, and no formal threshold defines when a Δmax counts as a "switch." The single-head localization claim really holds for only 2 of 3 models: mGPT's syntax-only patch effects are admittedly "generally modest and negligible" (0.00–0.09), its top head conflates syntax with surface language, and the balanced-multilingual model also lacks English intermediates — i.e., the model least biased toward English deviates from the clean story in three ways, yet receives the least analysis. The natural-data control is 27 FLORES-200 sentences and actually *breaks* the staged pattern for Llama 3 (collapsed S,L→C in Table 6), which undercuts the external validity of the headline claim more than the authors' "difficult to draw substantial conclusions" acknowledges. An unexplained anomaly — the word-order steering effect vanishing when German is the base target language in Aya and Llama 3 — is waved toward "language relatedness and resource effects" without investigation.

**Methodological assumptions.** LogitLens is known to over-project frequent and English-like tokens, which interacts directly with the English-intermediate finding (mGPT's null result could partly be a projection artifact rather than a representational difference); activation patching presumes the translation and grammar-mapping computations are interchangeable and linear — acknowledged in Limitations but never stress-tested against alternatives such as attention knockout or attribution methods. The one-shot exemplar is drawn from the same synthetic dataset, and the authors themselves concede that more natural parallel prompts might induce different translation strategies.

**Reproducibility and significance.** Reproducibility is good: code is linked, all 138 language triplets are enumerated (Table 4), dataset construction is documented down to the gpt-4o adjective prompt, and per-model S-switch layers (11/15/14) are stated. Significance is moderate: for the multilingual-interpretability community this cleanly extends the staging narrative and gives concrete head indices to audit, but the scope is three word-order phenomena in template sentences — nothing about case, agreement, or long-distance dependencies, and no demonstration that ablating the S-heads changes free-form translation quality, so the causal story is only established within the minimal-pair regime.

## Strengths

- Elegant disentangling design: base/plant prompts crossing S, L, C in all 8 combinations, with source language held fixed for tractability; the same-order vs. different-order mean-activation-patching control is a clean causal test of language-invariance that most probing studies lack.
- Four converging methods (LogitLens trajectories, layer patching, head patching, activation-geometry comparison) tell one consistent story rather than a grab-bag of analyses.
- Model selection spanning the English-bias spectrum (balanced mGPT vs. English-dominant Llama 3) makes the English-anchor claim meaningful instead of an artifact of one English-centric model.
- Honest, specific limitations section; code and complete data recipes released; deterministic per-model layer indices enable direct replication.
- Careful dataset hygiene for a synthetic resource: non-canonical DeepL outputs were manually corrected, and a failed earlier SketchEngine-based design is documented as a negative result.

## Limitations

- Only three models, and only mGPT is verifiably non-English-dominant; Aya Expanse's pretraining mix is undisclosed, so the "English-bias spectrum" framing is partly assumed for the middle model.
- Grammatical scope is three word-order contrasts in minimal sentences (NP order, SVO/SOV, modal placement); case marking, agreement, and long-distance dependencies may use entirely different mechanisms.
- Small, template-generated data (197/134/31 sentences; 27 FLORES sentences for the natural check, where Llama 3's pattern collapses); no statistical uncertainty on any reported number.
- The mGPT deviation (distributed heads, S/L conflation, no English intermediates) is reported but not explained, leaving the generality of the "single head carries syntax" claim confined to Aya and Llama 3.
- LogitLens frequency bias and the linearity/interchangeability assumption of patching are acknowledged but unquantified; the German-base-target anomaly is unexplained.

## Relevance

属于你关注的"LLM 隐藏状态与可解释性"方向的核心工作：同时使用 LogitLens 探针、层/头级激活修补与激活几何分析，在多语言 LLM 中定位句法-语言-语义的分层解析电路，是机制可解释性与跨语言表示几何的直接参考。虽然与你的自进化智能体主线无直接交集，但其"信息在不同层按阶段先后承诺"的分析框架，对理解长流程智能体系统中内部状态的分阶段形成有方法论借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the S→L→C staging survive on natural parallel text at scale, given that the 27-sentence FLORES-200 check already flipped Llama 3 to a collapsed S,L→C strategy?
- Why does the word-order steering effect disappear when German is the base target language in Aya Expanse and Llama 3 — language relatedness, resource level, or prompt-format sensitivity?
- Do morphology-bearing phenomena (case marking, agreement) recruit the same S-sensitive heads or entirely separate circuits, and do the minimal-pair findings transfer to free-form translation output quality when the S-heads are ablated?
- Is single-head localization an artifact of 8B-scale dense models — how does syntactic commitment organize in larger, sparse, or MoE multilingual models, and does balanced multilingual pretraining (mGPT-like) systematically predict distributed syntax circuits?
- Since the S-heads are functional but not geometric word-order encoders, what representation format do they actually pass downstream — and could that intermediate format be a better target for steering multilingual behavior than token-level interventions?
