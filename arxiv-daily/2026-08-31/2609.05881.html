Broken on Arrival: Silently Defective LLM Artifacts in Public Model Registries and How to Catch Them

Aditi Patodiya· independent researcher· quantizationllm-supply-chainmodel-registriesfunctional-testinggguf

Key metric
1.6%

of the official Ollama library is defective on arrival: 5 of 305 artifacts score 0/164 on HumanEval+ under both CUDA and Metal, while independent conversions of the same models solve 8–14/15.

Why this matters

Quantization can ship functionally dead models with no error; the worst defects emit fluent, wrong code that only running the artifact exposes.

Critical edge

The 15-task smoke suite was calibrated on the very Qwen defect that is also one of the paper's five headline findings — and the screen's prospective miss-rate bound spans a loose 0%–27.8%.

— Critical Assessment: calibration circularity

01 Methodology

  • Census, not sample. Every official Ollama tag that is instruct, code-capable, explicitly quantized and ≤8 GB — 305 artifacts across 15 model lines — plus 22 most-downloaded HuggingFace community conversions and 7 cross-distributor referees; over 1 TB downloaded, inventory frozen before testing.
  • Calibrated 15-task smoke suite. Built from the 39 HumanEval+ tasks every healthy configuration (down to 0.5B at 3-bit) solves: healthy passes 15/15, the known-broken q3_K_M fails. Certifies competence, not capability.
  • Two-stage screen. Flag <9/15 (catches 50/305); compare against the family's high-precision baseline (q6_K/q8_0/fp16) — ≤2/15 with a baseline ≥8 becomes a defect suspect. Seven suspects emerge.
  • Adjudication. Full 164-task HumanEval+, replication on a second backend (CUDA RTX 4090 + Apple Metal — a defect must fail on both), a cross-distributor referee (0/15 vs ≥5/15, gap ≥4), and manual transcript review. Greedy decoding, pinned Ollama v0.3.26, byte-identical reruns.

a. conversion defect b. capability collapse c. backend-dependent failure
  • The naive threshold flags 50/305 (16.4%); the adjudication chain narrows this to 7 suspects and 5 confirmed defects — the referee exonerates llama3.2-1B at 2-/3-bit (independent conversions fail equally: capability collapse) and reclassifies two TheBloke deepseek-coder-6.7B files as backend-dependent (5–6/15 on CUDA vs 13–15/15 on Metal).
  • Cheap output statistics cannot substitute: the best rule (truncation ≥0.3 or repeated-6-gram ≥0.3) catches only 3 of 5 defects at a 2.3% false-positive rate over 300 healthy artifacts; the two missed sit at the 94th percentile of healthy repetition share — statistically invisible.
  • Screen sensitivity: every confirmed defect was flagged; ten unflagged artifacts solved ≥22% of 49 held-out tasks (miss-rate Wilson CI 0%–27.8%, reported as loose). Screening costs a median 30 s per artifact on a commodity GPU.
  • Community channel is cleaner (20/22 healthy) — but in a day-zero vignette, 4 of 5 conversions of a 3-day-old 30B model failed to parse in the pinned runtime: conversions reach users before any validation pipeline exists.

03 Strengths

  • A real denominator. A full census of the eligible official population (every compliant tag ≤8 GB), not a convenience sample — the 1.6% rate is meaningful where most supply-chain numbers are not.
  • Falsifiable verdicts. Family baselines, dual-backend replication, and a pre-registered cross-distributor referee produce the reusable three-way taxonomy instead of one naive threshold.
  • Reproducible by construction. Greedy decoding with byte-for-byte reruns, one pinned runtime, archived per-artifact transcripts, released tool + dataset, disclosure reports per defect.
  • Practical weight. Median 30 s per artifact, a one-command tool (quantcheck), and practitioner guidance each backed by a specific census finding.
  • Statistical honesty. Wilson CIs even when unflattering (0.7%–3.8% artifact rate; 0%–27.8% miss bound labeled loose); per-artifact vs per-incident rates separated.

04 Limitations

  • Construct: the smoke suite covers Python code generation only — defects invisible to code tasks escape, so prevalence figures are explicit lower bounds.
  • Internal: both backends share one pinned llama.cpp-based release; a defect manifesting only under another runtime is indistinguishable from a healthy file.
  • External: one registry's ≤8 GB code artifacts plus a 22-file popularity-weighted community sample; chat models, larger artifacts and other registries are unmeasured.
  • Statistical power: the sensitivity check uses ten artifacts (miss-rate CI 0%–27.8%); the "community is cleaner" claim rests on 22 files (group CI 1.9%–22.0%).
  • Mechanism gap: no root cause is established for the Qwen-3B batch, the phi3.5-mini q2_K, or the backend-dependent pair; distributor responses are not reported.
  • Detector ceiling: cheap signals top out at 3-of-5 detection — scalable deployment still requires executing every artifact.

05 Questions for Further Exploration

  • What is the actual mechanism behind the four Qwen2.5-Coder-3B batch failures — corrupted conversion run, tokenizer/pretokenization regression, or tensor-mapping errors — and do distributor responses to the disclosures confirm it?
  • Why does the same TheBloke GGUF file behave healthily on Apple Metal but lose most capability on CUDA under the identical pinned runtime — kernel dispatch, memory layout, or something else — and does it persist across llama.cpp versions?
  • Should artifact verification be pinned to (file, runtime version, backend) triples rather than files alone, given that a "defective file" verdict is explicitly runtime-relative?
  • Do the fluent-but-wrong signatures generalize to chat or math workloads where no test-based scorer exists, and can capability-collapse thresholds be calibrated without code tasks?
  • Can learned detectors trained on the released transcripts push cheap-signal detection past the 3-of-5 ceiling at acceptable false-positive rates — or is execution fundamentally irreplaceable?
