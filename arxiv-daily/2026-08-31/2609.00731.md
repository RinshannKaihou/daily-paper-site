> **一句话总结：** 斯坦福（Pan/Giesecke）、南京大学（Ding）与 HPI 团队提出"智能体实证资产定价"（AEAP）的首个正式定义与五模块分类（假设生成/形式化/执行/评估门/记忆迭代），并给出以生产力-表现-新颖度三联合必要属性 + "滚动重执行"为核心的系统级评测标准；其实例系统 SEADS 在 JKP（约 400 特征）与 CRSP/Compustat（87 个原始变量）两个美股面板上与 5 个复现基线在 300 候选同预算下对比：滚动重执行 OOS Sharpe **0.96±0.04** 名义第一（无发现地板仅 0.62，Beyond Prompting 0.91±0.02 不可区分），静态对比却无单一指标能一致排序；消融显示自进化是最大承重机制——去掉后 Sharpe 从 0.254 跌至 0.080（**−68%**），而关掉 Planner 反而使入选因子数从 14.0 膨胀到 37.2、Sharpe 崩至 0.101。

> **TL;DR:** This paper defines Agentic Empirical Asset Pricing (AEAP) — LLM agents that autonomously execute the full hypothesis→formalization→evaluation discovery loop over asset-pricing data — proposes a five-building-block taxonomy and a system-level evaluation standard (productivity / performance / novelty, all three jointly necessary, plus a spanning test), and contributes SEADS, an agentic factor-discovery system evaluated against five re-implemented baselines (AlphaAgent, AlphaForge, RD-Agent(Q), QuantaAlpha, Beyond Prompting) on two US equity panels (Panel A: JKP ~400 curated characteristics; Panel B: CRSP/Compustat 87 raw accounting/market variables) at a matched 300-candidate budget with an identical GPT-5.2 backbone. Under the paper's rolling re-execution protocol on Panel A (~71 OOS months), SEADS posts the top mean OOS Sharpe 0.96±0.04 vs 0.62 for the raw-400-only no-discovery floor, ahead of Beyond Prompting (0.91±0.02) and QuantaAlpha (0.90±0.02), though not reliably separable from them; SEADS's own static arm (identical library, never re-discovering) reaches only 0.71±0.05, showing the re-propose loop — not the initial library — drives the gain. In static per-factor scoring, no single metric ranks all six systems consistently: Beyond Prompting leads Panel A performance (0.41 vs AlphaForge 0.34, SEADS 0.25) but is the least novel (2.84 vs SEADS 0.65), while SEADS leads Panel B novelty outright (0.46 vs QuantaAlpha 0.61) and ties atop performance (0.11). Ablations show self-evolution is the most load-bearing mechanism (removal cuts Sharpe 68%, 0.254→0.080), and removing the Planner inflates admissions 14.0→37.2 while Sharpe collapses to 0.101 — productivity without a gate is not discovery.

## 核心贡献

- **AEAP 的正式定义（C1）**：首次将"LLM 智能体自主完成至少一轮完整科学发现循环（假设→形式化→评估），且人类不执行这三步中的任何一步"定义为 AEAP 系统。据此清晰切分了边界：ML 预测器（Gu 2020/Kozak 2020，人设计假设空间）、LLM 副驾驶（人提出想法）、多智能体辩论（人决定验证）都不是 AEAP；并归纳出五模块分类：B1 假设生成（因子/交易原型）、B2 形式化（可检验 artifact）、B3 沙箱执行/估计、B4 评估门、B5 记忆与迭代——对应通用 agentic AI 中的规划/行动、工具使用、批评者模型与记忆，但以因子发现原语表述。
- **面向"系统"而非"输出"的评测方法论（C2）**：指出全部现有评测都只回测*输出*（因子或交易），而从不回测产生它的*发现系统*——一个自主发现系统可以通过运气、预训练信息泄漏或精心挑选的单次运行得到漂亮数字。提出三条原则（P1 point-in-time 数据、P2 point-in-time 过程、P3 仅样本外报告），并证明**被调研的所有 AEAP 系统无一满足 P2**；给出因子发现参考架构（生成器 B1–B2 / 沙箱执行器 B3 / 验证门 B4 / 持久化 B5）与三联合必要属性评分标准（productivity、performance、novelty，缺一不可）+ spanning 检验。
- **滚动重执行协议（adaptivity）**：在一系列历史决策日期上重放整个发现循环（每步只允许看到该时点为止的滑动样本内窗口；年度重发现 + 4 个月组合器重拟合），回答静态打分无法回答的问题——**发现过程本身**在时变条件下是否可靠、能否随市场状态自我改进，而不是某一次输出是否走运。配套静态对照（同一初始库、永不重发现）隔离"重提议"本身的贡献。
- **SEADS 系统与六方实证对比（C3）**：Stanford Engine for Agentic Discovery of Signals（"seeds"，呼应 Planner 的种子记录）：Planner 产出 explore/exploit 混合种子 → 并行 Proposer 实例展开为完整规格与公式 → 结构预检 + AST 沙箱（命名空间静态限定为所读列）+ 前瞻防火墙（确定性 AST 遍历、7 类规则 + 分类白名单）+ 对齐检查器 → 九重验证门（重复指纹、plain-rank 塌缩、覆盖率、Rank-IC t、跨年代 regime 一致性、新颖度、Fama-MacBeth 偏 IC 与 |t|、符号一致；阈值全部事先固定，Rank-IC 门槛随运行数做 Bonferroni 动态收紧）→ 自进化变易近通过者 → 持久库 + ridge（α=1）组合器月度多空组合。在两个面板上与 5 个复现基线、300 候选同预算、同一 LLM 骨干、同一评分代码对比。
- **审慎的负面发现**：明确报告 (1) 无单一静态指标能一致排序六个系统（不同面板上冠军漂移）；(2) 独立通过门控的因子并不能保证组合层面单调为正的贡献；(3) GPT-5.2 骨干存在"软性"信息泄漏残留。将这三点定位为"该领域应有的预期"而非失败。

## Key Contributions

- **A formal definition of AEAP (C1)**: a system where an LLM-based agent autonomously executes at least one full cycle of hypothesis → formalization → evaluation over asset-pricing data with no human performing any of those steps. This cleanly excludes ML predictors and copilots (human designs the hypothesis space), multi-agent debate pipelines (human owns validation), and the non-agentic AlphaForge; five recurring building blocks (B1 hypothesis generation, B2 formalization, B3 sandboxed execution/estimation, B4 evaluation gate, B5 memory & iteration) ground the new vocabulary in standard agentic-AI primitives (planning/action, tool use, critic model, memory).
- **An evaluation methodology for the *system*, not the output (C2)**: the key observation is that every existing evaluation backtests only the *output* (factor or trade), never the autonomous process that produced it — a gate admitting a tiny fraction of candidates can be well-calibrated, can leak information through its own ticking bug, or can have drawn a favorable in-sample window. Three principles follow (P1 point-in-time data, P2 point-in-time process, P3 out-of-sample-only results), with the finding that **no surveyed AEAP system satisfies P2**. A factor-discovery reference architecture (generator B1–B2, sandboxed executor B3, validation gate B4, persistence B5) plus a scoring standard built on three *jointly necessary* pool properties — productivity, performance, novelty — no two of which substitute for the third.
- **Rolling re-execution ("adaptivity")**: re-run the entire discovery loop at a sequence of historical decision dates, each seeing only the in-sample window ending at that date (annual re-discovery cadence, 4-month independent combinator refit, state carried forward). A static-arm control (identical starting library, never re-discovering) isolates the contribution of re-propose itself — a distinction no static backtest can draw.
- **SEADS + a six-system, two-panel comparison (C3)**: Planner emits explore/exploit seeds → parallel per-seed Proposer instances expand seeds into full spec+formula → structural pre-check, AST-sandboxed executor restricted to statically-read columns, a deterministic look-ahead firewall (AST walk, 7 rule classes + taxonomy whitelist, up to 2 LLM-assisted repairs), and an LLM Alignment Checker verifying formula-vs-rationale → a nine-check validation gate with all thresholds fixed ex ante and a Bonferroni-dynamic Rank-IC bar → self-evolution mutating near-passes through the identical gate → persistent library combined by a ridge (α=1) rank-normalized combinator into a monthly long-short portfolio (deliberately simpler than XGBoost/LightGBM as a reproducibility-motivated default). Evaluated against five re-implemented baselines at a matched 300-candidate budget, same in-sample window (2010–2019), identical scoring code, one GPT-5.2 backbone for every role.
- **Deliberately deflationary negative findings**: (1) no single static metric ranks the six systems consistently — the winner shifts by panel; (2) independently-admitted factors do not guarantee a coherent combined portfolio (non-monotone OOS contribution); (3) a soft backbone-leakage residual remains even when proposing from feature descriptions rather than realized returns.

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** Factor discovery on an asset-period panel (i,t) with features x_{i,t} observable at the close and next-period excess return r_{i,t+1}. A candidate is a scoring function f_θ: x_{i,t} → s_{i,t} (symbolic hypothesis, not a learned parameter vector); per-factor signal quality is ρ_θ = Spearman(s, r) across stocks (the Rank-IC the gate is built on). The discovery target is a factor set G = {f_1,…,f_G} that is simultaneously out-of-sample valid and non-redundant w.r.t. an explicit, disclosed reference set Z; a factor is admissible only if |corr(s, z)| stays below a threshold for every z ∈ Z.

**Evaluation principles.** P1: point-in-time data (a feature observable only after decision date t never enters the inner loop). P2: point-in-time *process* — the outer loop actually re-executes and is scoreable at each historical date. P3: out-of-sample-only results (headline numbers come from a split the inner loop never touched). Surveyed systems satisfy at most P1+P3; none satisfies P2.

**SEADS loop.** (1) *Planner*: one call per round emits *seeds* (explore: hypotheses from a different signal family; exploit: bounded variations of prior successful recipes), with two deterministic backstops correcting observed Planner failure modes. (2) *Proposer*: parallel per-seed LLM instances, scoped to a prior-attempt summary rather than the Planner's full history, expand a seed into a complete specification and formula. (3) *Sanity checks*: structural pre-check of malformed specs; the Implementation Agent runs the surviving spec's formula in a sandboxed namespace restricted to exactly the columns it statically reads, checked by a look-ahead firewall — a deterministic AST walk enforcing seven rule classes plus a taxonomy whitelist, so the identical pipeline runs unmodified on JKP's ~400 characteristics and CRSP/Compustat's 87 variables; up to two LLM-assisted repairs; an Alignment Checker (LLM) verifies the formula line-by-line implements its rationale. (4) *Validation gate* (nine checks, order and thresholds fixed ex ante in Appendix D): duplicate fingerprint; plain-rank collapse (formula algebraically collapsing to a rank of one declared input); coverage; Rank-IC t-stat; out-of-decade regime consistency; novelty against reference set Z; partial-IC against the top-k most-correlated controls; Fama-MacBeth |t|; sign agreement among Rank-IC / partial-IC / Fama-MacBeth. The Rank-IC bar is dynamically tightened via a Bonferroni-style adjustment on cumulative trial count — a rule set ex ante, never adjusted after seeing results. (5) *Self-evolution*: near-passes (cleared a minimum progress floor or failed at a specific gate stage) are mutated through the identical gate for a bounded number of rounds, stopping early once a mutation clears the Rank-IC bar; feedback names the exact failed stage and a progress-weighted score rewards reaching a later stage. (6) *Persistent state*: baseline-characteristic statistics, an append-only attempt log, a capped lesson memory, and the admitted-factor library; a combinator fits ridge regression (α=1) on the library's rank-normalized values to rank stocks monthly into a long-short portfolio.

**Scoring a discovered pool (§3.4).** Four metrics over a shared 300-candidate budget: *productivity* (total admissions), *performance* (mean per-factor OOS Sharpe across the admitted set), *novelty* (each admit's maximum |correlation| against the union of the system's own other admits and the panel's reference set, averaged; lower = more novel), and a *spanning test* (ridge regression of the admitted factors against the reference set; the OOS long-short return series are regressed against each other and the t-statistic of alpha reported — suggestive, not confirmatory).

**Backtesting the discovery system (§3.5).** Rolling protocol: re-execute the full loop at a sequence of decision dates, each seeing only the in-sample window ending there; SEADS re-proposes (new seeds → gate → growing library) annually and refits the combinator every 4 months, so a newly admitted factor is re-embedded in the portfolio by the next refit. The *static* arm — same starting library, refit schedule, never re-discovering — controls for persistence alone. Both panels use in-sample 2010–2019; Panel A's OOS runs to December 2025 (n≈71 months), Panel B's to November 2024 (n≈59 months).

**Data and baselines.** Panel A: US subset (ex-microcap) of the JKP globally-constructed characteristics database (~400 characteristics; novelty checked against that same curated zoo). Panel B: 87 primitive CRSP/Compustat accounting/market variables (novelty checked against the external 45-characteristic reference panel of Bryzgalova et al. 2025). Five re-implemented baselines — AlphaAgent, AlphaForge, RD-Agent(Q), QuantaAlpha, Beyond Prompting — at the matched 300-candidate budget, same in-sample window, identical OOS scoring code, same GPT-5.2 backbone. AlphaForge and QuantaAlpha are verified against released source, RD-Agent(Q) against the official package; AlphaAgent and Beyond Prompting are close reconstructions from paper text (the latter releases no code).

## Results & Highlights

- **Rolling re-execution, Panel A (JKP, ~71 OOS months, 3 replicates each):** all five discovery systems clear the no-discovery floor by a wide margin — mean OOS Sharpe 0.82–0.96 vs **0.62 for Raw-400-only** (deterministic ridge on the ~400-characteristic panel with no admitted factors). SEADS nominally leads at **0.96±0.04**, ahead of Beyond Prompting **0.91±0.02** and QuantaAlpha **0.90±0.02** (not reliably distinguishable), and more clearly separated from RD-Agent(Q) and AlphaAgent at **0.82±0.03** each. Crucially, **SEADS's static arm scores only 0.71±0.05** — closer to the no-discovery floor than any rolling system — so most of SEADS's rolling advantage comes from the re-propose loop itself, not the initial library.
- **Ablations (Panel A, mean±SE over 5 replicates):** removing **self-evolution** is the largest drop in the battery — admitted factors shrink from **14.0±1.4 to 7.8±0.6** and Sharpe from **0.254±0.033 to 0.080±0.02 (−68%)**. Removing the **Planner** instead inflates admissions nearly threefold (**14.0 → 37.2±2.5**) while Sharpe collapses to **0.101±0.044** — "too many weak rather than too few." Removing the Alignment Checker costs ~15% of Sharpe (0.254→0.215±0.033) and removing the attempt-log memory ~62% (0.254→0.096±0.02), while novelty moves little across all ablations (0.627–0.676 vs 0.653 full).
- **Static multi-axis comparison (Table 1, mean over replicates; A=JKP / B=CRSP-Compustat):** no single metric ranks all six systems consistently. *Performance*: Beyond Prompting leads Panel A at **0.41** (AlphaForge 0.34, SEADS 0.25), while SEADS leads Panel B at **0.11** (tied with AlphaAgent's 0.11; AlphaForge last at **−0.08**). *Novelty* (lower = more novel): SEADS and QuantaAlpha tie atop Panel A at **0.65**, AlphaForge is worst of all six at **0.96**, and SEADS leads Panel B outright at **0.46** vs QuantaAlpha's next-best **0.61**. *Productivity*: Beyond Prompting dominates both panels (**96.4 / 85.0** admissions) vs SEADS's **14.0 / 13.8** — but the authors show its productivity and Panel-A performance partly reflect a looser gate and predominantly redundant formulas (Appendix H), illustrating §3.4's thesis that performance without novelty is cheap.
- **Spanning test (t-stat of OOS alpha vs the reference set):** Beyond Prompting posts the largest Panel A value (**2.04**) but a Panel A-negative AlphaAgent (**−0.77**) and AlphaForge (**−0.13**) show that per-factor Sharpe does not imply value added beyond the reference set; SEADS is positive on both panels (1.27 / 1.28). Reported as suggestive, not confirmatory.
- **Honest negative findings:** independently-admitted factors do not guarantee a monotone positive joint OOS contribution (Fig 3); and the soft backbone-leakage residual persists — reported as evidence an unsettled field should expect, not a failure of one system.

## Authors & Credibility

Yingjian Pan is at Stanford's Advanced Financial Technologies Laboratory (Management Science & Engineering); Kay Giesecke — a leading authority in credit risk, financial engineering, and ML-for-finance, here affiliated with Hasso Plattner Institute's Artificial Intelligence & Quantitative Finance group — anchors the finance side; Xiaowei Ding (Nanjing University, School of Information Management) bridges information systems and LLM agents. The team combines deep domain knowledge of empirical asset pricing (the paper engages seriously with Gu/Kozak/Xiu ML asset pricing, the Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen replication debate, and Fama-MacBeth methodology) with agentic-AI literacy. AI usage is transparently disclosed (generative AI assisted part of the supporting code and manuscript editing, with human review of all artifacts). This is a preprint under review (draft July 2026; posted 1 Sep 2026) whose five baselines are 2025–2026 systems, several still working papers — contemporaneous, fast-moving scholarship. No code release is stated in the main text, which matters for a "methodological foundations" claim.

## Critical Assessment

- **Statistical power undermines the headline "no consistent winner" claim.** The authors admit it themselves (§6): admitted counts run in the tens per replicate, OOS windows are 59–71 months, and replicate counts (5 static, 3 rolling) are "a deliberate power limit" set by API budget. With such samples, Table 1's rankings (e.g., SEADS 0.25 vs AlphaForge 0.34 vs Beyond Prompting 0.41 on Panel A performance) carry wide uncertainty, and no cross-system significance tests are reported beyond ±SE. The flagship conclusion "no single metric ranks systems consistently" may partly be an artifact of noise rather than a genuine multi-axis trade-off structure — the paper never separates those two explanations.
- **The strongest baseline is a reconstruction of code that was never released.** Beyond Prompting — which dominates productivity (96.4/85.0) and leads Panel A performance (0.41) — is a "close reconstruction from paper text" because its authors release no code; AlphaAgent is likewise reconstructed. The paper's defusing argument (its looser gate admits predominantly redundant formulas, Appendix H) is plausible but itself unverified against the original implementation. Reimplementation-fidelity risk cuts both ways: weak reconstructions can make SEADS look relatively better, and the paper has no way to quantify this.
- **An internal prose/table inconsistency in the ablation narrative.** §5.3 claims "removing the Alignment Checker or either memory tier alone costs 15–62% of Sharpe," but Table 2 shows that removing the *lessons* memory yields Sharpe 0.263±0.033 — nominally *above* the full pipeline's 0.254±0.033. Only Align-Check-off (0.215, −15%) and Attempts-off (0.096, −62%) fit the stated range. All values are within overlapping standard errors, which is exactly the point: the prose overstates the cost of the lessons tier, and the paper does not flag that this ablation is a wash.
- **Compute matching is circular.** The "matched 300-candidate budget" counts initial proposals only; self-evolution's mutation retries and repairs add uncontrolled extra LLM calls per slot, and since removing self-evolution collapses Sharpe by 68%, the mechanism doing the heavy lifting is precisely the one that breaks compute parity. Productivity comparisons across systems are therefore not compute-normalized.
- **Soft backbone leakage is acknowledged but unquantified.** GPT-5.2 plays every role at every historical date; its training corpus plausibly encodes which factor families worked post-2019. Proposing from feature descriptions rather than realized returns mitigates but does not eliminate this, and the paper offers no measurement (e.g., testing whether proposed families cluster around post-in-sample literature) — only the qualitative caveat. The release-dated alternative (GPT-4o-mini, Oct 2023) is dismissed for leaving only ~25 clean OOS months, which is fair but leaves the leakage magnitude unknown.
- **Portfolio-level claims rest on a deliberately simplistic combinator.** Ridge (α=1) on rank-normalized values is an ex-ante reproducibility default, but the rolling Sharpe comparison (Fig 2) is entirely a property of this combinator; a different aggregator could reorder systems. Reported Sharpe is gross of transaction costs and turnover, and SEADS's admits are "mostly multiplicative interactions," plausibly the highest-turnover family — the paper concedes eroded realized performance is "a gap no number reported here reflects." The panels include small-cap names with unmodeled capacity limits.
- **Genuine strengths to weigh against this:** the P1–P3 framing and the observation that no surveyed system satisfies P2 are, to my knowledge, novel and correct; the static-arm control in the rolling protocol is an elegant identification device; the gate's thresholds are pre-registered (ex-ante, Bonferroni-dynamic) in a field where gate-tuning is endemic; and the deflationary, negative-finding reporting posture is exemplary for agentic-claims papers.

## Strengths

- **A shared vocabulary the field lacked**: the AEAP definition and five-building-block taxonomy cleanly separate autonomous discovery from copilots, debate pipelines, and non-agentic search (AlphaForge), and are operationalized precisely enough to classify every surveyed system (Table 3 / Appendix B).
- **The output-vs-system distinction is a real methodological advance**: backtesting the *process* via rolling re-execution, with a static-arm control isolating repropose contribution, answers a question no static backtest can — and the static-arm result (0.71 vs 0.96) is itself a substantive finding: the loop, not the library, earns the edge.
- **Rigorous, pre-registered gate design**: nine checks with ex-ante thresholds, a Bonferroni-dynamic Rank-IC bar, a schema-agnostic sandbox + look-ahead firewall that runs unmodified across two very different panels, and an explicit refusal to combine independent checks into one tuned admission rule.
- **Multi-axis scoring resists gaming**: requiring productivity + performance + novelty jointly, plus a spanning test, directly attacks the "backtest one lucky factor" failure mode; the Beyond Prompting case (highest productivity and Panel-A Sharpe, least novel) demonstrates the standard doing exactly its job.
- **An honest, unusually deflationary posture**: the paper crowns no winner, reports its own negative findings (non-monotone combination, backbone leakage), and dedicates a limitations section that is more candid than most — including disclosing that replicate counts reflect API budget.
- **A serious ablation battery** (Table 2) that identifies self-evolution as the load-bearing mechanism and distinguishes two failure modes (too few strong admits vs too many weak admits).

## Limitations

- **Underpowered comparisons**: tens of admits per replicate, 59–71 OOS months, 5 static / 3 rolling replicates; most cross-system differences are, by the authors' own admission, not reliably distinguishable, and no formal cross-system significance testing is performed.
- **Two of five baselines are paper-text reconstructions**, including the dominant one (Beyond Prompting releases no code); reimplementation fidelity is unverifiable.
- **Soft, unquantified backbone leakage**: one current GPT-5.2 backbone for all roles and dates; the release-dated alternative is infeasible (~25 clean OOS months, confounded with weaker reasoning).
- **Compute not matched across systems** (self-evolution adds LLM calls beyond one proposal per slot), so productivity and possibly performance comparisons are not resource-normalized.
- **Portfolio results are combinator- and cost-agnostic**: ridge-only aggregator, no transaction costs or turnover modeling, small-cap capacity unmodeled — realized performance of multiplicative-interaction factors could erode disproportionately.
- **Independent admission ≠ coherent pool**: the gate certifies marginal in-sample signal, not joint OOS contribution; combination is non-monotone (Fig 3).
- **Scope confined to factor discovery** on two US equity panels with different OOS endpoints (Dec 2025 vs Nov 2024); AEAP objectives like trading policy and structural estimation are defined but not evaluated, and only one LLM backbone is tested. The look-ahead firewall may also be overly conservative (static pattern matching can reject safe formulas alongside unsafe ones).

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你的第一优先方向"自进化智能体与自动化科研"：它把金融领域的 agentic scientific discovery 形式化，且核心关切恰恰是**长程自主发现系统本身的可靠性与可评测性**（point-in-time 过程、滚动重执行、静态对照、消融定位自进化机制为最大承重件），其"回测系统而非输出"的评测思想可直接迁移到任何领域的自动化科研智能体评测。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- With more replicates and longer OOS windows, would the multi-axis rankings stabilize — or is "no single metric ranks systems consistently" itself partly a small-sample artifact? Can the two be separated statistically?
- Can the rolling re-execution protocol transfer beyond factor discovery to other AEAP objectives (trading policy, structural estimation) and to agentic scientific discovery in other domains (biology, chemistry, materials)?
- How large is the GPT-5.2 backbone-leakage residual? Could one quantify it by testing whether proposed factor families systematically cluster around post-in-sample published findings?
- Do the rolling-advantage conclusions survive a portfolio-aware admission gate (joint contribution rather than marginal), transaction costs, turnover constraints, and capacity limits?
- Is self-evolution's 68% Sharpe contribution specific to formulaic factors, or does it persist for learned representations (tree/NN-based signals) where the look-ahead firewall and plain-rank checks would need redesign?
- How does the schema-agnostic claim hold on non-equity panels (futures, fixed income, crypto), where the taxonomy whitelist and Fama-MacBeth controls are less natural?
