> **一句话总结：** 作者把变异测试（metamorphic testing）的靶标从模型反转为评分器：对 GSM8K/MATH/Big-Math 的 4,990 个标准答案施加 43 种"构造性保义"变换（正确判定由构造已知，无需人工裁决），对 4 个常用数学答案验证器跑了 307,420 次判定，发现自验证通过率在 53.8%–95.2% 之间——41.3 个点的差距完全由实现配置而非任务本身决定（同一库的两种配置在 49.9% 的等价对上互相矛盾）；93.0% 的在契约错误来自空白/标点规范化而非 LaTeX 语法解析；并首次刻画了一个确定性假阳性机制——sympy 级联验证器因 10^-4 相对容差与量级无关，对 ≥10^4 的"差一位"对抗答案 100% 误判为对（<10^4 时为 0%）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper audits the reward/accuracy verifiers behind RLVR and math benchmarks by inverting metamorphic testing onto the grader itself: 43 certified transformations in 14 strata are applied to 4,990 gold answers from GSM8K, MATH, and Big-Math (plus 2,000 synthetic forms), yielding 307,420 verdicts across four open-source verifiers — Hugging Face math-verify in plain-expression (mv-expr) and LaTeX-extraction (mv-latex) modes, a DeepSeek-Math-lineage string normalizer (strip-string), and a three-level exact/numeric(1e-4)/SymPy cascade (sympy-cascade). Self-validation on certified-equivalent variants spans 53.8% (mv-expr) to 95.2% (strip-string) on identical inputs — a 41.3-point spread, with the two math-verify configurations disagreeing on 49.9% of pairs — so the previously published ~94% figure describes one harness, not the task. The residual is string hygiene, not grammar: whitespace/punctuation cause 93.0% of mv-latex's in-contract false rejections (a trailing period flips the verdict), and sympy-cascade's scale-invariant 1e-4 relative tolerance makes it accept 100% of off-by-one adversarial answers at magnitude ≥10^4 (0% below), an aggregate 16.0% false-positive rate that is a deterministic step function rather than noise.

## 核心贡献

- **认证式蜕变测试协议（靶标反转）：** 给定标准答案 g 与保义变换 T，(g, T(g)) 的正确判定由构造已知；任何验证器拒绝它即"认证假阴性"——不需要人工裁决、更强模型或可信仲裁。对偶地，改变语义的变换被接受即"认证假阳性"。真值因此免费，样本量随 gold × 变换 × 验证器组合式增长，全程纯 CPU 可跑。
- **逐类别分解而非聚合率：** 43 种变换 × 14 个层（strata）× 4 个实现 = 307,420 次判定，每个单元格附 Wilson 置信区间。已发表的 ~94% 自验证数字（Hugging Face 2025b）被证明只刻画某一个 harness 在某个输入分布上的表现，而非"验证数学答案"这一任务。
- **契约矩阵 + 覆盖率作为一等指标：** 只对验证器声明处理的层打分（把实现缺陷与规范歧义分开，越界行为单独报告、不计为缺陷）；再把"拒绝"（判断错误）与"执行失败"（crash/timeout）分开——否则一个在每条输入上都崩溃的验证器会被记为 0.0% 假阳性、"全部正确拒绝"。总体在契约覆盖率 96.5%，缺口集中于单一实现。
- **分离聚合率相同的相反失败模式：** sympy-cascade（总残差 12.7%）与 strip-string（4.8%）失败原因截然相反：前者只在 87.3% 的输入上给出判定但对判过的每一条都正确（残差全是解析失败，如对集合/未约分分数层覆盖率为 0），后者全判但靠"错误地拒绝"出错。
- **尺度依赖的假阳性机制：** sympy-cascade 的相对容差 10^-4 是尺度不变的，导致对"差一位"答案的接受率在 10^4 处从 0% 阶跃到 100%——聚合假阳性率 16.0%（95% CI [14.1, 18.1], n=1,287）完全掩盖了这一确定性阈值。这是"结果由错误的 pattern 而非聚合率决定"（Egashira et al. 2026）的最极端实例。
- **发布全套工件：** 变换套件、契约矩阵、逐样本判定记录（github.com/ethinxin0011/verifier-error-budget），契约赋值公开以便质疑。

## Key Contributions

- A **certified metamorphic protocol for verifier auditing**: ground truth is constructed rather than adjudicated — rejecting a certified-equivalent variant is a provable false negative requiring no human arbiter, no stronger model; its dual certifies false positives. Scales combinatorially in golds × transforms × verifiers; runs entirely on CPUs.
- A **per-category decomposition** across four widely used implementations, 43 transformations in 14 strata, 307,420 verdicts, with Wilson intervals on every cell — showing the published ~94% self-validation figure is implementation-specific, not a property of the task.
- A **contract matrix** separating implementation defects from specification ambiguity (out-of-contract behaviour is reported but never counted as a bug), plus a **coverage measure** separating rejection from execution failure (crashes/timeouts are distinct verdict classes).
- Identification of a **scale-dependent false-positive mechanism with an exact threshold**: a numeric relative tolerance of 1e-4 accepts off-by-one answers as a deterministic step function of gold magnitude.
- **Released artifacts**: the transform suite, the contract matrix, and per-sample verdict records for independent re-analysis.

## Methodology

- **Certified equivalence.** Let g be a gold answer and T a semantics-preserving transformation; then T(g) is correct by construction, and V(g, T(g)) = FALSE is a certified false negative. For a meaning-changing T′, acceptance is a certified false positive. This removes the adjudication bottleneck of prior verifier audits and makes ground truth free.
- **Transform suite.** 43 transformations across 14 strata, in three classes: CERTIFIED-EQUIV (mathematically identical; rejection scored as false negative), CONTRACT-DEP (boxing, units, text wrappers — scored as specification ambiguity, never as defects), and ADVERSARIAL (meaning-changing; acceptance scored as false positive). Transforms producing empty output are auto-discarded; a verifier is scored only on strata it claims to handle per the published contract matrix.
- **Coverage vs. rejection.** n_eval = n_TRUE + n_FALSE, coverage = n_eval/n; both the all-inputs rate (denominator n) and the evaluated rate (denominator n_eval) are reported. A verifier that crashes on every input in a stratum cannot be credited with a 0.0% false-positive "correct rejection" — the same accuracy/availability distinction argued for security-scanner evaluation (Lan et al., 2026).
- **Verifiers under test.** V1 = math-verify with plain-expression extraction (mv-expr); V2 = math-verify with LaTeX extraction, the binary default (mv-latex); V3 = DeepSeek-Math-lineage string normaliser (strip-string); V4 = a refined three-level reference cascade — exact string, then numeric with relative tolerance 1e-4, then symbolic via SymPy (sympy-cascade). ANTLR4 runtime pinned at 4.13.2 because behaviour differs across runtimes.
- **Data.** 4,990 unique gold answers drawn from GSM8K, all seven MATH subjects, and Big-Math, plus 2,000 synthetic answers in standard MATH forms (synthesis was needed because set/interval notation appears in only 0.1% of corpus answers, leaving those strata underpowered). Synthetic golds are tagged and reported separately; a corpus-only robustness check confirms the findings.
- **Execution.** Azure ML parallel pipeline on five CPU nodes; each verification runs in an isolated subprocess with a 5-second per-item budget matching the documented symbolic-comparison timeout; crashes and timeouts are recorded as distinct verdict classes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Self-validation is implementation-specific (41.3-point spread).** On identical 4,990-gold inputs (GSM8K/MATH/Big-Math), acceptance of certified-equivalent variants is 95.2% for strip-string vs 53.8% for math-verify plain-expression (mv-expr) — a 41.3-point spread; sympy-cascade scores 87.3% and math-verify LaTeX 79.9%, so the published ~94% figure (Hugging Face, 2025b) sits near the top of the range and characterizes one harness, not the task. The two configurations of the *same library* (mv-latex vs mv-expr) differ by 26.1 points and disagree on 49.9% of the 31,266 certified-equivalent pairs both judged.
- **Whitespace and punctuation dominate the error budget.** The whitespace/punctuation strata account for 93.0% of mv-latex's in-contract certified false rejections (74.1% for mv-expr). On the whitespace stratum itself (n = 15,704), mv-expr rejects 50.0% of correct variants (95% CI [49.2, 50.8]) and mv-latex 37.2% [36.5, 38.0], versus only 4.4% for strip-string. A trailing period or newline — which no reasonable specification treats as meaningful — flips the verdict (e.g., "\frac{1}{2}" accepted, "\frac{1}{2}\n" rejected by both math-verify configurations, Table 8).
- **A deterministic, scale-dependent false positive.** sympy-cascade accepts 0% of off-by-one adversarial answers below gold magnitude 10^4 and 100% at or above it (n = 131 and 75 respectively), while the other three verifiers reject all off-by-one variants at every magnitude (0.0%). Its aggregate off-by-one acceptance of 16.0% (95% CI [14.1, 18.1], n = 1,287) conceals this exact threshold, caused by the scale-invariant 1e-4 relative tolerance (10,000 vs 10,001 differ by 0.01%).
- **Similar aggregates hide opposite failure modes.** sympy-cascade (aggregate residual 12.7%) returns a verdict on only 87.3% of inputs but is correct on 100% of what it judges — its residual is entirely parse failure (0% coverage on the sets and unreduced-fraction strata) — whereas strip-string (residual 4.8%) judges everything and errs by rejecting. Overall in-contract coverage is 96.5%, with the deficit concentrated in a single implementation.
- **Reported accuracy is not comparable across papers.** On contract-dependent input classes, acceptance of identical inputs spans 0.0%–75.1% on boxed answers (strip-string/mv-expr vs mv-latex) and 0.0%–100.0% on scientific notation — undocumented contract differences, not defects.
- **Corpus-only robustness.** Excluding all 2,000 synthetic golds, self-validation becomes 57.5% (mv-expr), 84.1% (mv-latex), 87.8% (strip-string), 88.3% (sympy-cascade) — ordering and magnitude preserved, so the synthesis is not driving the results.

## Authors & Credibility

- **Esther Xin, Independent Researcher** (single author, no institutional affiliation). Sole-author audit papers warrant extra scrutiny, but this one is deliberately mechanical rather than opinion-driven: ground truth by construction, Wilson intervals on every table cell, a pinned ANTLR4 runtime (4.13.2), isolated 5-second subprocess execution, and full release of the transform suite, contract matrix, and per-sample verdict records.
- The claims are carefully scoped: the limitations section explicitly disclaims model-level effects, RLVR training runs, closed frontier systems, and treats the contract assignments as contestable judgement calls published in code.
- The paper engages the relevant 2025–2026 literature precisely: Cai et al. (stochastic reward channel, ρ0/ρ1), Egashira et al. (systematic vs random verifier error), Huang et al. (rule-based vs model-based verifiers failing in opposite directions), Ammanamanchi et al. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The inversion is real and cleanly stated: prior metamorphic work (Asgari et al. 2025; Hyun et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2026) perturbs the *input* to test the *model*, whereas here the transform is applied to the answer string and the system under test is the grader. The core idea is simple — arguably obvious in hindsight — but the paper's value lies in the disciplined execution and the decomposition; it also honestly reframes its own headline ("the residual is string hygiene, not grammar") as more actionable, not more glamorous, than the prior LaTeX-parsing narrative.
- **Rigor.** Above average for this genre. The contract matrix prevents blaming a verifier for strata it never claimed (mv-expr's 8.0% acceptance on boxed answers is reported as an undocumented contract difference, not a defect), and the coverage split prevents the seductive error of scoring a crashing verifier as "correctly rejecting everything". The corpus-only check (§3.5) and the pinned runtime address the two most obvious confounds.
- **Weak baselines by omission — the audit slice is narrow.** All four audited verifiers are rule-based/string-symbolic. The increasingly common LLM-as-judge grading path — which Huang et al. (2026) show fails through reward hacking rather than parsing — is entirely out of scope, yet the title's claim about "reward signals in RLVR" rests on this rule-based slice. The certified-equivalence protocol could in principle be applied to a model judge, and its absence is the paper's most conspicuous gap.
- **Data quirk.** 2,000 of 4,990 golds (40.1%) are synthetic. The §3.5 corpus-only check mitigates drift, but the synthetic strata are exactly the underpowered hard cases (set/interval notation, 0.1% of corpus answers), so per-stratum rates there carry the most construction bias.
- **Missing the number practitioners care about.** The paper is verifier-level only: it never estimates how much a fixed set of real model outputs would shift in reported MATH/GSM8K accuracy under each configuration — the end-to-end consequence that would make the 41.3-point spread directly actionable for benchmark reporting (acknowledged in §5, but still the biggest gap between the finding and its significance).
- **Small adversarial sample.** The step-function false-positive result rests on n = 1,287 constructed adversarial answers, only 206 at magnitude ≥10^4; the mechanism is well explained, but the sample is designed, not drawn from real model output distributions, so its training-time frequency is unknown.

## Strengths

- Certified ground truth eliminates the adjudication bottleneck that has limited prior verifier audits; everything scales combinatorially and runs on CPUs only.
- Actionable headline: the dominant failure mode (trailing period/newline, whitespace) is a near-trivial normalization fix — 93.0% of one verifier's in-contract failures — directly contradicting the "LaTeX parsing is hard" attribution, and the recommendation to report verifier identity, version, extraction configuration, runtime, and coverage alongside accuracy is concrete and adoptable.
- The magnitude-threshold false positive is a genuinely new, deterministic mechanism that instantiates Egashira et al.'s systematic-error theory (a threshold is maximally systematic and invisible in any aggregate rate).
- Every cell carries a Wilson interval; the contract matrix and coverage split are methodological contributions beyond the empirical findings; artifacts are fully released for re-analysis.

## Limitations

- Verifier-level only: no estimate of the effect on any model's benchmark score, and no RLVR training runs (the author cites Zhang 2026's bounded-effect result for leaky vs hardened code-RLVR rewards as adjacent, not substituting).
- Rule-based, open-source implementations only; no closed frontier graders and no LLM-based judges.
- 40.1% of gold answers are synthetic; the contract assignments are the author's judgement calls (published so they can be contested — e.g., treating boxed-answer extraction as out-of-contract for mv-expr is debatable given math-verify is used in practice to grade \boxed{} outputs).
- Real model outputs do not produce transform variants at equal rates, so per-stratum false-negative rates do not translate directly into reward-noise parameters (ρ0/ρ1) for RLVR training.
- Single-author independent work; 8 pages — focused, but some aggregate figures (e.g., the exact coverage column for sympy-cascade) require the released CSVs to reconstruct in full.

## Relevance

与你的高优先级方向"automated benchmark and reward design 以及长程自动化系统的可靠性"直接相关：RLVR 的奖励信号和所有数学基准分数的底层都建立在这类自动验证器上，而本文证明其误差主要来自可一行修复的字符串规范化、且存在可被答案量级确定性利用的假阳性机制。任何依赖自动评分做自我评测或奖励反馈的自动化研究循环，都应把"验证器身份/配置 + 覆盖率"当作必须报告的一等指标。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. If a fixed corpus of real model outputs on MATH/GSM8K were re-scored under all four verifier configurations, how large is the end-to-end spread in reported accuracy attributable purely to verifier configuration?
2. Can the certified-equivalence suite be ported to LLM-based judges — and does the failure profile shift from string hygiene to semantic, reward-hackable modes as Huang et al. (2026) suggest?
3. During RLVR training with a sympy-cascade-style verifier, does the 10^4 tolerance threshold create an exploitable reward niche — do policies learn to emit large-magnitude or near-tolerance answers?
4. What fraction of the whitespace/punctuation error budget does a one-line normalizer patch remove, and do the verifier rankings survive such a patch?
5. Do the same failure strata (units, boxed answers, scientific notation, whitespace) recur in execution-based settings — e.g., SWE-bench Verified, where 28.5% of tasks were reported to accept Docker-verified incorrect patches (Rajan 2026)?
