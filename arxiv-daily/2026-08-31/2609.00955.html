Why this matters

As AI moves onto analog chips, persistent hardware noise compounds across multi-step inference — this scheduler-level fix needs no retraining or extra compute.

Critical edge

Despite the 100-chip grounding, the entire diffusion trajectory runs in simulation against the fitted noise model — no end-to-end on-silicon demonstration exists.

01 Methodology

Setup. On analog CIM, each matrix-vector multiply is y = xT(W + εW) + εy: each layer gets a weight-perturbation map sampled once and fixed for the whole deployment, while output noise is redrawn per MVM — so every denoising step shares the same dominant spatial error.

  1. Chip-calibrated noise model: σ²W = αW|W| + bW, fitted from hundreds of measured MVMs on a 28-nm eFlash platform (4×2.2-Mb arrays) and validated on 100 physical chips (R²=.993, DKL=.0198).
  2. Why early steps hurt: first-order linearization gives εS = Σ PS,t+1}·αt·Ht·Δθ — the reused Δθ keeps propagated errors directionally aligned across steps, and early windows accumulate more of it (sufficient condition sketched, gains not measured).
  3. ASSERT schedule: hold stochastic DDIM at ηmax=0.8 for the first 20% of steps, then cosine-decay ηt to 0 for deterministic refinement — training-free, parameter-free, zero extra NFE. Injected noise changes activation trajectories, decorrelating them from the persistent error.
  4. Evaluation: five datasets (32×32 → 256×256), FID over 50k images, three seeds with shared latents and noise realizations; baselines are all DDIM-family schedules (deterministic, linear, step, reverse).

Public Hugging Face checkpoints (BF16, native parameterization) · ηmax and the 20% plateau tuned once on CIFAR-10, then fixed across all datasets.

  • Placement beats presence: concentrating stochasticity in the final refinement (reverse schedule) performs far worse — where the noise goes matters more than having it at all.
  • High resolution transfers: at noise 0.08, three-seed mean FID is 2.39× (CelebA-HQ), 2.58× (LSUN-Church), 2.54× (LSUN-Bedroom) lower than DDIM — reported honestly as ratios of means.
  • Ablation shape: too little stochasticity hurts badly (FID 17.08 at ηmax=.6), too much too (7.58 at 1.0) — a short, strong stochastic phase then gradual determinism wins.
  • Noise-source decomposition: deterministic DDIM degrades most under persistent weight noise specifically; ASSERT tracks the clean baseline.
  • Honest costs: 20-step clean FID 13.96 vs 11.08 for DDIM (~26% worse); noisy FID is non-monotonic in step count — 500-step 21.27 but 1000-step 80.59, flagged by the authors as unresolved.

03 Strengths

  • Rare grounding in real physical chip measurements — fitted from hundreds of MVMs, validated on 100 chips with R²=.993 — instead of ad-hoc synthetic noise.
  • The stage-sensitivity finding is clean, well-isolated, and converts directly into a concrete scheduling rule.
  • The fix is training-free, parameter-free, and NFE-neutral — deployable to any existing DDIM-compatible checkpoint.
  • Hyperparameters tuned once on CIFAR-10 and fixed across five datasets (32×32 → 256×256) — a fair transfer protocol.
  • Unusually candid error reporting: flags its own anomalous 20-step result and the ratio-vs-absolute-FID distinction.

04 Limitations

  • No end-to-end on-chip demonstration; everything runs in simulation against the fitted model on a single eFlash platform.
  • The mechanistic theory (Jacobian gains, cross-step alignment) is asserted but never measured.
  • Baselines limited to DDIM-family schedules; no noise-aware fine-tuning or hardware-level mitigation comparison, no cost/Pareto analysis.
  • Headline gains concentrated on CIFAR-10; no ImageNet- or text-to-image-scale (SD/DiT) evidence.
  • Clean FID regression at short step counts; noisy FID non-monotonic in step count with no predictive account.
  • Chip data and code are not released; may not transfer to RRAM / PCM without refitting the noise model.

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. Can the assumed propagation-gain and cross-step-cosine conditions be measured directly on real trajectories — or is the gain mostly stochastic averaging?
  2. How does ASSERT compare quantitatively (FID and energy/cost) against noise-aware fine-tuning and hardware mitigations such as noise-compensating ADCs?
  3. Does the early-stochastic schedule transfer to transformer-based diffusion backbones (DiT) and text-to-image scale, where the “early = global structure” argument needs revalidation?
  1. Can ηmax and the plateau length be auto-tuned online from chip telemetry instead of a fixed CIFAR-10-tuned constant?
  2. What explains the non-monotonic noisy FID vs step count — can the optimal step count be predicted per noise level?
  3. Does the schedule generalize to other analog technologies (RRAM, PCM) whose noise statistics differ from eFlash?
