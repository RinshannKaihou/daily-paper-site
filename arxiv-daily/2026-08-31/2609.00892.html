47% final win-rate vs GPT-4.1 anchors — the only method still improving at step 300 (+21.0 pp in all 3 seeds).

Why this matters

A concrete recipe for detecting and patching reward loopholes mid-training — reliability engineering for self-improving LLM pipelines, where scoring rules quietly erode as policies optimize against them.

Critical edge

Headline benchmarks are single-run, and the 47% win-rate is measured against the very GPT-4.1 anchors the policy was trained to chase — partial circularity, only partly mitigated.

01 Methodology

Setup. Rubric-based GRPO: 16 rollouts per prompt, each scored by an LLM verifier against a prompt-specific rubric set. Hacking happens when proxy scores match but true quality does not — so the rubrics’ discriminative accuracy in the high-reward region must be maintained online.

  1. Anchors, offline and once. GPT-4.1 writes one reference response per prompt + rubric set; dual frontier judges (93.8% unanimous) plus a human audit (199/200) filter them. Anchors are cached reference points — explicitly not a quality ceiling.
  2. Per step: take the top-scoring rollout and split its rubrics into satisfied vs unsatisfied. Hack detection examines satisfied rubrics only — a rubric the response fails cannot be hacked.
  3. Adaptive branch (hack found): rewrite the exploited rubric or append a [Resist] hard constraint, enforced by a veto reward — any [Resist] violation zeroes the entire reward, making hacking categorically unprofitable.
  4. Chase branch (no hack): crystallize the single largest substantive gap between rollout and anchor into a sharper atomic [llm] rubric. Comparison is bidirectional, so the rollout may also win — this is not imitation.

Training: Qwen2.5-7B-Base · GRPO on 16×A100 · ~300 steps · Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct as both verifier and judge · verbatim prompts released.

  • Anchor ablation is the sharpest result: judge–human agreement on hack calls is 99.1% with anchors vs 67.1% without; anchor-free detection is worse than static rubrics on two benchmarks.
  • Veto reward matters: weighted averaging of [Resist] rubrics leaks — the policy buys back violations with high scores elsewhere, hurting every benchmark.
  • Rigor: 3-seed win-rate 24.0% → 45.0% (cross-seed SD 0.0); paired bootstrap +23.0 pp [15.0, 29.0], CI excludes zero.
  • Generalizes: Llama-3.1-8B Arena-Hard 8.9 vs 2.6; Qwen3-8B 13.1 vs 10.9; checkpoints scored against Qwen3-8B-Thinking (outside the training loop) climb 16% → 60% win-rate.
  • Cost: +41.1% per-step training time (318.2 s → 448.9 s); anchor generation is one-time and cached.

03 Strengths

  • Three failure modes of prior dynamic-rubric methods crisply articulated, each mapped to a concrete design decision.
  • Satisfied-only detection scope is simple, clever, and validated by the 95.2% vs 65.3% agreement audit.
  • Veto reward turns soft preferences into hard constraints; the ablation shows weighted averaging measurably leaks.
  • Unusually strong hygiene: 3 seeds with near-zero variance, bootstrap CIs, independent-checkpoint eval, human + frontier-judge audits, explicit cost model.
  • Verbatim prompt templates and full hyperparameters released — reproducible for an LLM-judge paper.

04 Limitations

  • Evolution fires at every step (+41.1% overhead); selective activation proposed but never implemented.
  • No principled metric for generated-rubric quality — success is inferred only from downstream behavior.
  • Depends on a frontier teacher (GPT-4.1 anchors) and a 72B judge; degradation with weaker teachers unknown.
  • Main benchmark table is single-run per configuration; only the win-rate trajectory has multi-seed support.
  • 7–8B scale, instruction-following benchmarks only — no multi-turn, coding, creative writing, or non-English.
  • Appendix A theory is idealized; no end-to-end guarantee that the updates reduce frontier distortion.

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. How much anchor quality is load-bearing — would self-generated or open-weight anchors preserve the 95.2% hack-detection agreement?
  2. Can evolution be gated to high-risk steps, and what trigger works — reward variance, judge confidence, or satisfied-rubric drift?
  3. Does the MECE/no-overlap prompt rule actually bound rubric-set growth over 300 steps, and how does growth interact with the overhead?
  1. Would a cross-family training-time judge eliminate the Qwen-family coupling, and how much of the gain survives?
  2. Does the [Resist]/veto mechanism transfer to verifiable-reward settings where hard constraints already exist programmatically?
  3. Beyond 3 epochs, does the win-rate eventually plateau like Chase-only, and can Adaptive repair sustain Chase’s sharpening indefinitely?
