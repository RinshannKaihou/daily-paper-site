On the first benchmark scoring ideas against real future paper streams, summarize‑then‑predict wins on every backbone — yet rankings flip when the gate tightens.

Can Large Language Models Forecast What Researchers Study Next? (IDEAFORECASTBENCH)

Fenghai Li, Zihan Tang, Haofei Yu, et al.  ·  Jiaxuan You’s group, UIUC  ·  2026-09-01

research-idea forecasting benchmark LLM-as-judge ai-for-science evaluation protocol

一句话总结：IDEAFORECASTBENCH 用 52 个主题 × 12 个月度截止点 = 624 个滚动评测窗（arXiv cs.ML，2024-04 至 2025-09），以「后续 3 个月真实发表论文流」检验 LLM 能否预测研究社区的下一步；Summary 压缩策略在所有骨干上 Hit@5 最高（Qwen2.5-14B 达 0.954），但盲评显示 Qwen2.5 的高分部分源于预测更宽泛，换判官或收紧特异性阈值（S≥3 时 Qwen3.5 从 0.532 崩至 0.050）后排名会翻转。

Key metric
Hit@5 0.954
Qwen2.5-14B + Summary compression · 95% CI 0.925–0.978 · Precision@5 0.553, MRR 0.716

624 rolling topic–cutoff episodes over arXiv cs.ML (52 topics × 12 monthly cutoffs, Apr 2024–Sep 2025); ideas credited only when retrieved papers published in the next 3 months match.

Why this matters

First benchmark scoring LLM research ideas against real future papers — a yardstick for auto-research loops, and a cautionary tale for LLM-as-judge reliability.

Directly measures the “propose ideas” step of automated research cycles, not just novelty/feasibility self-ratings.

Critical edge

The headline Qwen2.5 score is confounded by forecast breadth — broader forecasts match more (rate 0.205→0.406) — and there is no human ground truth: annotators agreed at Fleiss’ κ = 0.135.

Pretraining exposure to target papers cannot be excluded either; cutoffs sit inside the training windows.

Methodology

  • Episode task. A forecaster sees only papers first submitted by monthly cutoff t and emits up to 5 ranked natural-language ideas; credit requires matching papers published within the following 3 months — a community-level “realization” target, orthogonal to idea-execution (AI Scientist) or single-abstract reconstruction (PreScience) benchmarks.
  • Retrieve-then-judge protocol. Each idea embedded (VOYAGE-3-LARGE); top R=10 candidate papers retrieved; GPT-4.1-mini judge scores Problem / Method / Specificity (0–3); credited iff P+M≥5 ∧ S≥2, one credit per paper. Metrics: Hit@5, Precision@5, MRR, plus judge-independent Novelty; 10,000 topic-clustered bootstrap CIs.
  • Five compression strategies. Direct (20 recent abstracts), Retrieval (hybrid semantic+keyword), Summary (60 abstracts distilled into one ~8-sentence paragraph), Topic Trend (cluster trajectories), Memory (8 bullets + 20 recent abstracts) — across GPT-4.1, Qwen2.5-7B/14B, Qwen3.5-9B.
  • Learned reference (MDF). Innovations as (base, operator, gap) triples from a typed memory prior; realization policy trained with GRPO on hindsight-extracted triples — evaluated as a reference point, never ensembled with the judge.

Flow: freeze literature at cutoff → forecast ≤5 ideas → retrieve post-cutoff candidates → judge gates credit → diagnostics dissect breadth, budget fill, and gate sensitivity.

  • Summary leads on every backbone (≥5-point margins); Memory and Retrieval also beat Direct but trail Summary. Token/call budgets were not equalized — flagged by the authors.
  • Realization and yield are different quantities. At Precision@5 0.553, nearly every episode contains a credited idea but only ~half the five slots earn distinct-paper credit; Topic Trend tops MRR on GPT-4.1 (0.598) while filling only 60% of its budget.
  • Gates reshape the leaderboard. Under S≥3 the 14B advantage persists (+0.181 [0.133, 0.229]) while 7B’s interval includes zero; Qwen3.5 Summary collapses 0.532→0.050. Judge–judge Cohen’s κ is only 0.538–0.588.
  • The learned MDF reference underperforms (0.545) — an audit of all 3,120 forecasts found 4-word median approach fields from a 500-char truncation artifact, so the authors refuse to declare a capability ranking.

Strengths

  • Genuinely new evaluation target: community-level realization against the full future paper stream, with a deterministic, calendar-verified 624-episode manifest — exactly reproducible and cross-method comparable.
  • Exceptional measurement hygiene: judges reported separately (never ensembled), one-credit-per-paper dedup, paired gate-sensitivity reusing stored judgments, topic-clustered bootstrap, and budget-validity diagnostics before letting readers over-read MRR.
  • Unusually honest failure analysis: Section 5.5 audits its own learned component’s 3,120 forecasts, traces the artifact to adapter truncation, and refuses attribution without ablations.
  • Actionable regardless of interpretation: Summary compression is a transferable recipe; the breadth–match-rate correlation and S≥3 reversal quantify how threshold conventions dominate rankings.

Limitations

  • Judge-dependent, human-uncalibrated credit: single primary judge, inter-judge κ 0.538–0.588, Qwen-judge execution failures in 72/624 MDF windows, and the κ=0.135 human study does not calibrate current scores.
  • Breadth vs anticipation unresolved: the gate does not control intrinsic specificity; no controlled intervention exists — Qwen2.5’s dominance cannot be causally attributed.
  • Pretraining contamination cannot be excluded: temporal filtering does not remove prior exposure; the contamination probe is observational only.
  • Narrow, proxy target: arXiv cs.ML only, 52 correlated topics, 3-month horizon, R=10 retrieval; publication is a delayed, incomplete record and the Novelty diagnostic does not measure originality; compute budgets not equalized across strategies.

Questions for further exploration

  • Can a judge or gate be calibrated against human labels on the current manifest — or is idea–paper matching fundamentally low-agreement, needing ensemble or retrieval-augmented judging?
  • What controlled specificity intervention (constrained decoding, specificity-conditioned prompts) would causally decompose Qwen2.5’s advantage into breadth vs genuine anticipation?
  • Does the Summary gain survive equalized token/call budgets, and does it transfer beyond cs.ML — to theory-heavy or clinical subfields with slower publication cycles?
  • How much residual score is pretraining contamination? Prospective freezing of forecasts, or trained-vs-frozen comparisons on post-2025 cutoffs, would bound memorization.
  • With MDF’s information loss fixed (no rationale truncation, populated key terms) and ablations added, can a learned forecaster actually beat Summary prompting?
  • Do Hit@5 and Precision@5 diverge into different rankings at 6–12-month horizons, where realization opportunities grow but the record gets noisier?
