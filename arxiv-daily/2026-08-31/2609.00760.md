> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了 213 组匹配对照四元组 (KR, SR, KC, SC)，首次在受控条件下对知识型拒答（KR）与安全型拒答（SR）做机制层面的头对头比较，发现两者共享一个"先承诺拒答、后说明理由"（commit-then-specify）的分层机制：峰值层 KR−KC 与 SR−SC 位移方向的余弦对齐达 0.699–0.794、且各位移的 0.92–0.95 投影到公共方向上，但转移是不对称的——SR 上训练的线性探针迁移到 KR 达 0.80–0.86，而 KR→SR 仅 0.58–0.63（六个模型中差距恒为 0.22–0.27），且类型特异信号（知识框架 vs 规范框架）直到上层才分化。

> **TL;DR:** Using a new controlled benchmark of 213 matched contrastive quadruples (KR/SR refusal prompts + KC/SC benign controls) and three 7–9B open-weight model families (Llama-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B, each also sequentially refusal-tuned with CRaFT then FalseReject), the paper shows KR and SR are governed by overlapping yet distinguishable mechanisms. Against the implicit prior view that refusal is one monolithic direction (e.g., Arditi et al. 2024's single refusal direction for SR), the mean KR−KC and SR−SC shift directions align at 0.699–0.794 cosine at the peak layer (0.92–0.95 of each shift projects onto the shared bisector), yet probe transfer is asymmetric: SR-trained probes transfer to KR at 0.80–0.86 accuracy vs only 0.58–0.63 for KR→SR (gap 0.22–0.27 in every model), and type-specific signals (epistemic vs normative framing, decoded via logit lens) separate only in upper layers — supporting a "commit-then-specify" account with practical consequences: safety-first-then-knowledge tuning (Seq-KS) preserves utility (Llama* MMLU 61.84 vs original 62.90) while knowledge-first tuning inflates benign-prompt over-refusal (XSTest Safe Ref. up to 84.20 on Gemma).

## 核心贡献

- **统一的受控基准：** 构建了 213 组匹配对照四元组 q_i = (x^KR, x^SR, x^KC, x^SC)——每组的四个问题共享同一话题、语义框架、句式模板和锚点词，仅"拒答理由"不同（KR：虚构/不可验证指称；SR：真实敏感目标；KC/SC：去掉触发因素的良性最小编辑版）。流水线为 600 候选对 → 规则过滤 475 → GPT-5 质量判定保留 384 → 控制项验证 269 → 跨六模型行为验证后定稿 213；人工校验 75 组，LLM 与人工判定一致性 Cohen's κ = 0.82。
- **回答 RQ1（共享机制）：** 逐层的平均拒答方向 d_l^r = E[h(x^R) − h(x^C)] 显示 KR 与 SR 位移强对齐（峰值层 cos 0.699–0.794），且关键的控制实验证明这不是共享模板的伪影——对照组相似度 sim(KC,SC) 反而在所有层都高于 sim(KR,SR)。
- **回答 RQ2（不对称转移）：** 线性探针的跨类型迁移存在稳定的不对称：SR→KR 0.80–0.86 vs KR→SR 0.58–0.63，六个模型全部呈现 0.22–0.27 的差距，且该不对称在指令微调基座、不同微调顺序（Seq-KS/Seq-SK）、更新的模型（Qwen3.5-9B 等）和独立的 XSTest 派生集（Δ = +0.232）上均成立。
- **回答 RQ3（上层特化）：** 类型特异信号在中下层几乎为零，仅在最后几层急剧分化（logit lens 显示 KR 侧激活 Based/presently 等认知框架词，SR 侧激活 regulation/privacy 等规范框架词），注意力头与 MLP 消融进一步定位了这一晚出现的接地信号。
- **"先承诺、后具体化"框架与实践配方：** 提出拒答 = 共享的"承诺拒答"成分 + 上层的"理由具体化"成分；由此推导出训练顺序配 Seq-KS（先安全后知识）优于 Seq-SK 和数据混合训练（Merged），在保住通用能力的同时同时实现两类拒答。

## Key Contributions

- **A matched contrastive benchmark for refusal-type attribution:** 213 (KR, SR, KC, SC) quadruples across 20 topic categories where surface form, topic, and template are held fixed and only the refusal-inducing factor varies — enabling confound-free comparison that existing KR and SR benchmarks cannot support.
- **RQ1 — a shared refusal substrate, with the template confound ruled out:** mean KR−KC and SR−SC shift directions align at 0.699–0.794 cosine at the peak layer; the control check Δ_sim = sim(KR,SR) − sim(KC,SC) is negative at every layer in all six models (matched controls are *more* similar than refusal pairs), so KR–SR similarity cannot be explained by shared templates.
- **RQ2 — persistent asymmetric cross-type transfer:** probes trained on SR/SC boundaries generalize to KR/KC at 0.80–0.86 while KR-trained probes reach only 0.58–0.63 on SR/SC — SR representations behave like a superset of KR's shared signal, not vice versa.
- **RQ3 — late, layer-localized type-specific specialization:** the KR- and SR-specific projections stay near zero through most of the network and diverge sharply only in final layers, decoded via logit lens as epistemic framing (KR) vs normative framing (SR); attention-head and late-layer MLP ablations (relative depth > 0.9) selectively reduce the KR-specific projection.
- **A "commit-then-specify" model of refusal and an actionable tuning recipe:** refusal is first committed via a shared component, then grounded differently; hence Seq-KS (safety tuning before knowledge tuning) best preserves both capability and both refusal behaviors, while the reverse order or naive data mixing degrades utility or causes over-refusal.

## Methodology

**Controlled benchmark construction (Appendix A).** A hierarchical schema (20 semantic categories, e.g., personal data access, document authenticity, regulated guidance) defines semantic frames, shared templates, and anchor keywords. GPT-4o-mini generates matched KR/SR pairs as minimal edits (a second pass removes giveaway markers like "fictional" or "illegally"); a rule filter (invalid target assignment, invalid refusal trigger, prefix/suffix-only delta, length asymmetry) cuts 600 → 475; a GPT-5 LLM judge rates five dimensions (keep 257 / revise 127 / drop 91 → 384). GPT-4o-mini then builds controls x^KC (fictional referent → real answerable one) and x^SC (sensitive target → benign one) under a strict instruction (Jaccard ≥ 0.5, no refusal markers), validated on four automated criteria → 269 clean quadruples. Behavioral verification finally keeps only quadruples where all six target models refuse x^KR/x^SR and answer x^KC/x^SC → **213 quadruples**. A stratified sample of 75 quadruples was human-validated by 5 annotators (LLM–human agreement κ = 0.82).

**Models and controlled tuning (Section 3.2, Appendix B).** Three families: Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-it. Knowledge refusal is injected via CRaFT (Zhu et al., 2025b) and safety refusal via FalseReject (Zhang et al., 2025), combined by sequential tuning (Seq-KS = safety→knowledge; Seq-SK = knowledge→safety) or data-merged training. All variants are full-parameter SFT (AdamW, lr 2e-6, cosine schedule, 3 epochs, bf16, 2×H200). Seq-KS is selected for all families by behavioral validation on MMLU, GSM8K, TriviaQA, Natural Questions, AdvBench, and XSTest; tuned variants are denoted Llama*, Qwen*, Gemma*.

**Representational analyses (Section 4, Appendix C).** Hidden states are taken at the final prompt-token position (float32, seed 42). Per layer, the mean refusal direction is d_l^r = E[h(x^R_r) − h(x^C_r)] with matched controls subtracted to cancel length/suffix effects. Alignment is cos(d^K, d^S) with a 1000-permutation label test; the common direction is the normalized bisector of the two shifts, decomposing each shift into a shared projection and an orthogonal type-specific residual. ℓ2-regularized logistic probes (C = 1.0) test within-type, cross-type, common-refusal, and type-specific-contrast decoding with 5-fold CV and bootstrap CIs (1000 paired resamples). Type-specific signals are localized by projecting layer-wise shifts onto a fixed final-layer KR-specific reference (checked against all reference layers), decoded semantically with a vocabulary logit lens, and attributed via attention-head scoring/ablation and per-layer MLP zero-ablation. Behaviorally, activation steering adds +αd^r at the peak-alignment layer with random unit-vector controls; refusal is scored by a conservative rule-based lexical classifier; generation is greedy (256 tokens).

## Results & Highlights

- **KR and SR share a dominant direction, and it is not a template artifact.** Peak-layer alignment between mean KR−KC and SR−SC shifts is 0.794 (Llama-3-8B), 0.789 (Qwen2.5-7B), 0.711 (Gemma-2-9B); tuned variants 0.745/0.745/0.699 — versus a 1000-permutation null (Appendix C). Crucially, sim(KC,SC) exceeds sim(KR,SR) at every layer (e.g., Llama: 0.938 vs 0.898 overall), i.e., the *controls* are more template-similar than the refusal pairs, so shared KR–SR alignment cannot come from surface form. Alignment peaks at relative depth 0.25–0.50 and rises steadily through the first half of the network.
- **The common component is large; a non-trivial type-specific residual persists.** At the peak layer, 0.92–0.95 of each normalized shift projects onto the common bisector, with the orthogonal residual norm at 0.32–0.39. Probes agree: the common-residual probe beats its shuffled-label null by ~0.45 AUC while the type-specific probe beats its null by only ~0.11, a pattern holding across all six models and all layers.
- **Asymmetric transfer is large, universal, and robust.** On the 213 quadruples, within-type probe accuracy is 0.947–0.962 (KR) and 0.816–0.861 (SR); cross-type transfer is SR→KR 0.805–0.863 vs KR→SR 0.580–0.631, a gap Δ = 0.223–0.273 in all six models (e.g., Llama: 0.847 vs 0.589, Δ +0.258). The gap appears from early layers and is stable across depth; it persists across tuning orders (Seq-SK Llama: +0.237), in instruction-tuned base models (Δ ≈ 0.23–0.27, Figure 12), in newer models (Qwen3.5-9B: +0.255), and on an independently constructed XSTest-derived set of 125 matched examples (SR→KR 0.824 vs KR→SR 0.592, Δ +0.232).
- **Type-specific specialization is late and semantically interpretable.** Projections onto the final-layer KR-specific direction stay near zero through most layers and split only near the end (p^K positive, p^S negative), robust to the choice of reference layer (Figure 7 heatmaps). Logit lens: the common direction promotes "sorry/regret/currently" and suppresses "yes/sure/certainly"; the K−S contrast separates uncertainty/availability-framed tokens (e.g., "Based", "presently") from policy/privacy-framed tokens (e.g., "regulation", "legal", "sensitive", "privacy"). Ablating the top-5 common heads reduces the d_common projection more than random-head ablation in all three tuned models; late-layer MLP blocks (relative depth > 0.9) disproportionately reduce the KR-specific projection.
- **Tuning order matters (Table 4).** Seq-KS preserves utility while hitting both refusal targets: Llama* MMLU 61.84 vs original 62.90 and GSM8K 66.57 vs 65.50, AdvBench unsafe-refusal 69.42, XSTest Safe Ref. 15.20; Qwen* even exceeds the original (MMLU 70.47 vs 69.00, GSM8K 70.05 vs 62.62). By contrast, Seq-SK balloons over-refusal on benign prompts (XSTest Safe Ref. 74.80 for Llama, 84.20 for Gemma) with sharper MMLU/GSM8K drops, and knowledge-only tuning leaves benign Ref. at 59.60/54.80/68.60 across the three families.
- **Steering confirms behavioral relevance.** Adding +αd^r at the peak layer raises refusal rates on control prompts for both directions — strongest in Llama* (near-monotonic up to α = 20), moderate in Qwen*, weak in Gemma* (Figure 15) — and type-specific steering shifts the *rationale* framing: +d^K_spec induces epistemic framing while +d^S_spec induces safety-oriented framing on the same matched KR/SR prompt.

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans Hanyang University (Yuri Son, Seunghee Kim, and corresponding author Taeuk Kim, who runs an established NLP/interpretability group) and Samsung Electronics (Hyuhng Joon Kim), funded by Korean IITP/MSIT and NRF grants. The group has prior work directly relevant here — Kim et al. (2025), "When to speak, when to abstain" — so the KR side of the analysis is a natural extension of the authors' own abstention research line. Methodological hygiene is above average for interpretability papers: matched-control design, human validation with reported Cohen's kappa, permutation tests, bootstrap CIs, random-head/random-vector baselines, and robustness sweeps over tuning order, dataset construction, and two additional recent models. All experiments are on open-weight 7–9B models that the authors instrument fully, and training/evaluation hyperparameters are documented in detail (Appendices B–C), supporting reproducibility of the *analyses* even though no dataset release is stated.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is genuine.** Prior mechanistic work studied SR alone (single refusal direction, Arditi et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2025) or KR alone (abstention/hallucination directions), leaving the shared-vs-distinct question open. The matched-quadruple design plus the negative Δ_sim control is a clean way to kill the most obvious confound, and the asymmetric-transfer result (SR→KR > KR→SR) is a specific, falsifiable finding rather than a restatement that "refusal directions exist."
- **Selection effect in the analysis set (paper-specific).** The 213 quadruples survived a filter requiring *all six* models to refuse KR/SR and answer KC/SC. This conditions the benchmark on prompts sitting comfortably inside every model's existing decision band — precisely where probes are easiest — which likely inflates the high within-type probe accuracies (0.95+) and possibly the peak alignment itself. No out-of-band or adversarial evaluation probes whether the geometric story holds near decision boundaries.
- **Synthetic, LLM-generated data with partial human validation.** All 600 candidate pairs were generated by GPT-4o-mini and quality-judged by GPT-5; humans reviewed only a 75-quadruple stratified sample. The Δ_sim check addresses template overlap, but not subtler artifacts of LLM-generated phrasing style (e.g., systematic lexical tells distinguishing "fictional referent" from "sensitive target" that models could exploit rather than a genuine epistemic/normative computation).
- **The causal story is thinner than the geometric one.** The authors honestly note the decomposition is "a geometric diagnostic" and the orthogonal residual "does not necessarily correspond to an independent causal mechanism." Steering effects are strongly family-dependent — Gemma* shows only a weak positive response (Figure 15) — and steering is scored by a conservative lexical refusal classifier, so the intervention-based support for commit-then-specify is uneven across models.
- **Within-type probe accuracies are unequal (KR 0.947–0.962 vs SR 0.816–0.861),** which makes raw cross-transfer numbers not strictly commensurable; to the authors' credit, the asymmetry runs *opposite* to what a "better-fit probe transfers better" null would predict, which actually strengthens their claim — but a chance- or calibration-controlled transfer metric would have been more rigorous.
- **Scale and scope.** Only three 7–9B open-weight families plus two recent Qwen models; no closed-weight models (acknowledged limitation), no scaling analysis, and single-turn prompts only. Notably, the paper (all 26 pages) nowhere states that the 213-quadruple benchmark or tuned checkpoints will be released — for a paper whose main artifact is a new dataset, that is a real reproducibility gap.

## Strengths

- The matched contrastive quadruple design is the right experimental instrument: it isolates refusal *grounds* from topic, format, and template confounds that have blocked direct KR-vs-SR comparison so far.
- Convergent multi-angle evidence — direction geometry, probe transfer, layer-wise projection, logit-lens decoding, attention-head/MLP ablations, and activation steering — all pointing to the same commit-then-specify structure.
- Unusually careful falsification effort: template-similarity control, 1000-permutation nulls, bootstrap CIs, random-head and random-vector baselines, robustness across 8 models, 2 tuning orders, and an independently built XSTest-derived set.
- Practical payoff: the Seq-KS vs Seq-SK comparison (Table 4) turns the mechanistic insight into a concrete training-order recipe with quantified trade-offs, and the shared-vs-specific decomposition suggests *where* to intervene for selective refusal control.

## Limitations

- **Open-weight only:** the method requires hidden-state access; extending to closed models would need output-level proxies (authors' stated limitation).
- **Single-turn only:** refusal arising from multi-turn context, follow-ups, or accumulated user information is out of scope (authors' stated limitation).
- **Diagnostic, not ecological:** the 213 synthetic quadruples deliberately maximize topic/surface alignment and, by the authors' own admission, underrepresent ambiguous, borderline, and off-domain refusals encountered in real deployments.
- **Causal grounding is uneven:** steering strength varies sharply by family (weak on Gemma*), head/MLP ablations establish sensitivity, not a full circuit, and the orthogonal residual is explicitly not claimed to be an independent mechanism.
- **Scale:** 7–9B models only; whether the asymmetry and late specialization persist at 70B+ or in RLHF-distilled closed models is untested.
- **No release statement** for the dataset or checkpoints appears in the paper.

## Relevance

与你关注的"LLM 隐状态与可解释性"方向高度契合：论文综合运用 mean-difference 方向、线性探针、logit lens、注意力头/MLP 消融与激活导向，对拒答行为做了完整的表征几何剖析，其中"共享成分 + 上层特化残差"的分解思路和 matched-control 数据构造方法可直接迁移到其他模型行为的隐状态分析。主题虽属安全/对齐侧（不在你的 SDC 检测范围内），但作为机制可解释性方法论文值得一读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the SR→KR > KR→SR asymmetry survive in much larger or closed-weight RLHF models, or is it an artifact of 7–9B scale and SFT-style tuning (CRaFT/FalseReject) used here?
2. Is the orthogonal type-specific residual causally load-bearing? Directly ablating d_spec during generation should, under the commit-then-specify account, preserve refusal rate while erasing the epistemic/normative rationale — this intervention is not reported.
3. How does the bisector-derived common direction relate to Arditi et al.'s (2024) single SR refusal direction — is one recoverable as a projection of the other, and does the shared component extend beyond KR/SR to other "decline-to-answer" behaviors?
4. What happens near the decision boundary? Re-running the analysis on prompts that failed the all-model behavioral-verification filter (i.e., where models disagree) would test whether the clean geometry is a selection artifact.
5. The asymmetry already exists in instruction-tuned base models (Figure 12, top row) — which stage of pretraining or alignment installs SR as the broader signal? A checkpoint-level training-dynamics study could localize its origin.
6. Can targeted calibration of the KR-specific late-layer component reduce knowledge-related over-refusal (e.g., the 59.60/54.80/68.60 benign Ref. rates after knowledge-only tuning) without perturbing the shared refusal commitment, as the Discussion predicts?
