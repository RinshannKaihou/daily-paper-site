> **一句话总结：** 针对深度研究智能体在"发散状态"（多个候选方向同时可信）下过早单路提交、被错误方向锁死的失效模式，提出 HypoSearch：先用假设智能体生成 K 个轻量级自然语言假设，各自在隔离的有界分支上并行搜集证据，再由聚合智能体做跨分支证据对比（冲突消解、拒绝理由）后作答或精化假设进入下一轮；在 BC-small 上把 Qwen3.5-122B 从 46.7 提升到 60.0，超过 5 路 Best-of-N（58.3），且比 Majority Voting 少用 22.1%–35.4% 的工具调用；配套的轨迹级行为指标（CIR/SSR）还可用于 SFT 数据筛选——用 1/8 数据训练的 Qwen3-32B 在 AIME25 上保留 43.3 分，而全量 80K 数据训练掉到 26.7。

> **TL;DR:** Deep-research agents commit to one search direction too early when several directions remain plausible, and later tool calls just reinforce that channel. The paper first shows via a failure taxonomy (gpt-4.1 + human annotation, 5 trajectories x 300 problems per benchmark) that Exploration Failure dominates BrowseComp (~78% of failed trajectories) but not GAIA (~6%), and that on BrowseComp success requires BOTH candidate-grounded queries (CIR) and controlled direction switching (SSR): the High-CIR/High-SSR cell reaches 88% success vs 25% for High-CIR/Low-SSR. It then proposes HypoSearch, a pre-hoc test-time scaling framework: generate K lightweight hypotheses at divergent states, run isolated bounded search branches per hypothesis, and aggregate branch-level evidence comparatively instead of voting. Across Kimi-K25, Qwen3.5-122B, and DeepSeek-V3.2 on BC-small / BrowseComp-zh / FutureX / ResearchRubrics, it beats Pass@1 and 5-way Majority Voting/Best-of-N — e.g., Qwen3.5-122B goes 46.7 → 60.0 on BC-small (+13.3 over Pass@1, +1.7 over Best-of-N) — while cutting tool calls by 22.1–35.4% relative to Majority Voting. A pilot SFT study shows CIR/SSR-filtered trajectories (10K of 80K from LongCat-Flash-Thinking-260T) match full-data search gains on BC-small while halving reasoning degradation on AIME25 (43.3 vs 26.7).

## 核心贡献

- **失效模式定位与分类学**：将智能体轨迹按最早失效阶段标注为探索失败 / 工具使用失败 / 综合失败 / 长度限制（每 benchmark 抽 300 题 × 5 条轨迹，gpt-4.1 + 人工专家标注，早期错误吞没后续统计）。发现 BrowseComp 上约 78% 的失败轨迹死于探索失败（方向选错），而 FRAMES 约 15%、GAIA 仅约 6%——失败分布高度依赖 benchmark 的检索结构。
- **两个可操作的行为指标**：CIR（Candidate-targeted Investment Rate，搜索查询落在具体候选方向上的比例）与 SSR（Search-direction Switch Rate，方向切换频率）。在 BrowseComp 上，高 CIR + 高 SSR 的轨迹成功率 88%，高 CIR + 低 SSR 仅 25%，低 CIR + 低 SSR 仅 3%——"锚定具体候选 + 受控换向"缺一不可。
- **HypoSearch 框架**：发散状态检测（prompt-based 路由器）→ 假设生成（K 个自然语言假设：候选实体、解释路径、证据视角）→ 每假设一条隔离有界分支并行搜集支持/矛盾/约束证据 → 比较式聚合（跨分支对比证据、消解冲突、给出拒绝理由，证据不足时精化假设再来一轮）。区别于投票式并行采样：比较发生在证据层而非最终答案层。
- **效率优势**：假设分支是"按方向切分"而非"整轨迹复制"，在 Kimi-K25 上每题工具调用约 132.5 次 vs Majority Voting 约 205 次（-35.4%），Qwen3.5-122B 上 -29.1%，DeepSeek-V3.2 上 -22.1%。
- **行为信号可用于训练数据筛选（pilot study）**：用 Q = 0.8·CIR + 0.2·SSR 从 80K 条 LongCat-Flash-Thinking-260T 轨迹中筛出 top-10K 做 SFT，BC-small 上以 1/8 数据匹配甚至超过全量数据训练，AIME25 上推理能力保留显著更好（43.3 vs 26.7）。

## Key Contributions

- **A failure taxonomy localized to the commit moment**: trajectories are labeled by their earliest unrecoverable stage (Exploration / Tool-use / Synthesis / Length). On BrowseComp, ~78% of failed trajectories fail at exploration (direction choice), vs ~15% on FRAMES and ~6% on GAIA — motivating pre-hoc (before-commit) rather than post-hoc scaling.
- **Two measurable behaviors behind successful divergent exploration**: CIR (fraction of queries targeting a concrete candidate) and SSR (rate of direction switches). On BrowseComp, high-CIR + high-SSR trajectories succeed 88% of the time, while high-CIR + low-SSR reaches only 25% and low-CIR + low-SSR only 3% — grounding and movement must co-occur.
- **HypoSearch, a hybrid pre-hoc test-time scaling framework**: a prompt-based detector routes direct states to single-path search and divergent states to hypothesis-guided branching; K hypotheses spawn isolated bounded branches that collect support, contradictions, verified constraints, and open issues; a comparative aggregation agent weighs cross-branch evidence, returns an answer with rejection reasons, or refines hypotheses for another round.
- **Better accuracy at lower tool cost than full-trajectory sampling**: consistent gains across 3 backbones and 4 benchmarks with 22.1–35.4% fewer tool calls than Majority Voting, because branches share the query and split the space instead of duplicating whole trajectories.
- **Behavioral signals as an SFT data filter (pilot study)**: ranking 80K LongCat-Flash-Thinking-260T trajectories by Q = 0.8·CIR + 0.2·SSR and training Qwen3-32B on the top 10K matches full-data search performance on BC-small while preserving far more non-search reasoning (AIME25 43.3 vs 26.7 for Full-80K).

## Methodology

**Motivating analysis (Section 2).** The authors sample 300 problems from each of BrowseComp, FRAMES, and GAIA, roll out 5 independent ReAct trajectories per problem, and label each failed trajectory with the earliest failure stage using gpt-4.1 plus human expert verification. They then characterize search structure via the average pairwise Jaccard similarity of search queries across the 5 independently sampled trajectories per round: BrowseComp decays from 0.53 (Round 1) to 0.16 (Round 3), meaning independent runs naturally diverge — divergence is intrinsic to these tasks, so committing early to one linear path is fragile. FRAMES and GAIA stay above ~0.54 in Round 3, matching their later-stage failure profiles. Two behavioral metrics are defined over each trajectory's query sequence: CIR = (1/N)·Σ c_i where gpt-4.1 assigns c_i = 1 if query i investigates a concrete candidate (entity, event, hypothesis) and 0 for broad keyword/generic-clue queries; SSR = (T−1)/N where T is the number of consecutive same-direction segments. A 3×3 grid of success rate over CIR × SSR buckets on BrowseComp shows the compounding effect described above.

**HypoSearch (Section 3).** The framework operates on local search states s_t = (x, m_t) (original query + accumulated local context) and has three stages:

1. *Hypothesis generation.* A prompt-based detector first decides whether the current state is direct (clear next constraint/step → continue single-path search) or divergent (several perspectives remain plausible → invoke HypoSearch). For a divergent state, a hypothesis agent emits K natural-language hypotheses H_t = {h_1, …, h_K}, each a plausible direction (candidate entity, explanatory path, or evidence perspective) — soft search hints, not claims to be proven.
2. *Parallel search.* Each hypothesis spawns one bounded, isolated branch that receives (x, m_t, h_i) and is instructed to gather evidence around its direction — support, contradiction, verified constraints, remaining uncertainties — rather than continue broad keyword exploration. Isolation prevents one early direction from suppressing alternatives; each branch ends with a structured summary (hypothesis, key evidence, verified constraints, unresolved issues, branch-level conclusion).
3. *Comparative aggregation.* An aggregation agent receives the original question plus all branch summaries and checks: do the collected hypotheses cover the question's constraints; is each branch's evidence direct and reliable; which evidence conflicts. It either returns a final answer with supporting evidence and rejection reasons for weaker alternatives, or — if hypotheses are indistinguishable or evidence is insufficient — emits refined hypotheses for another round of parallel search.

**Experiments (Section 4).** Four benchmarks: BC-small (a lightweight self-constructed subset of BrowseComp), BrowseComp-zh, FutureX (Feb 2026 week 3 slice), and ResearchRubrics; gpt-4.1 is the judge wherever LLM-based evaluation is needed; all methods share the same tool environment and evaluation protocol per benchmark. Backbones: Kimi-K25, Qwen3.5-122B, DeepSeek-V3.2. Baselines: Pass@1 (single ReAct trajectory), Majority Voting (N=5), Best-of-N with self-reported confidence (N=5) — N=5 matches HypoSearch's default of up to 5 hypothesis branches. Ablation over K ∈ {1..5} on BrowseComp-zh. The pilot study (Section 5) SFTs Qwen3-32B on Full-80K vs Filtered-10K trajectories (Q = 0.2·SSR + 0.8·CIR, top-10K by Q) and evaluates on BC-small and AIME25.

## Results & Highlights

- **BC-small, Qwen3.5-122B**: HypoSearch reaches **60.0** vs **46.7** for Pass@1 (+13.3 pts) and **58.3** for Best-of-N (+1.7 pts); Kimi-K25 goes **58.3 → 66.7** (+8.4 over Pass@1, +3.4 over BoN 63.3) and DeepSeek-V3.2 **53.3 → 63.3** (+10.0 over Pass@1, +3.3 over BoN 60.0).
- **BrowseComp-zh (Kimi-K25)**: HypoSearch **71.6** vs 60.9 Pass@1 (+10.7), 70.6 Majority Voting (+1.0), and 69.6 BoN (+2.0). DeepSeek-V3.2: 70.9 vs 59.5 Pass@1; Qwen3.5-122B: 66.1 vs 51.2 Pass@1.
- **FutureX (hard-to-verify, future-event prediction)**: DeepSeek-V3.2 improves from **44.7** (Pass@1) to **53.9** (+9.2), also above BoN's 52.2; Qwen3.5-122B goes 30.5 → 39.8 vs BoN 35.4; Kimi-K25 38.9 → 47.1 vs BoN 45.8. ResearchRubrics gains are smaller (+0.9 to +4.9), and Majority Voting/BoN cells are absent ("-") for these two benchmarks.
- **Tool-call cost (Figure 5)**: per question, Majority Voting uses ~205 calls on Kimi-K25 vs HypoSearch's ~132.5 (**−35.4%**); −29.1% on Qwen3.5-122B; −22.1% on DeepSeek-V3.2 — HypoSearch still uses more calls than Pass@1, as expected.
- **Number of hypotheses K (Figure 6, BrowseComp-zh)**: at small K, HypoSearch is unstable and can fall *below* MV and BoN (a narrow hypothesis set over-constrains the search); at K=5 it reaches 71.6 / 66.1 / 70.9 (Kimi-K25 / Qwen3.5-122B / DeepSeek-V3.2), above the corresponding MV and BoN.
- **Failure taxonomy (Figure 2)**: Exploration Failure accounts for ~78% of failed BrowseComp trajectories, vs ~15% on FRAMES and ~6% on GAIA (tool-use/synthesis dominate there) — the pre-commitment problem is benchmark-dependent, not universal.
- **Behavior grid (Figure 3b, BrowseComp)**: success rate 88% in the High-CIR/High-SSR cell vs 25% (High-CIR/Low-SSR), 26% (Low-CIR/High-SSR), and 3% (Low/Low).
- **Pilot SFT study (Figure 7)**: Qwen3-32B trained on Filtered-10K (top 10K of 80K by Q = 0.8·CIR + 0.2·SSR) slightly exceeds Full-80K on BC-small while using one-eighth of the data, and on AIME25 Full-80K collapses to **26.7** while Filtered-10K retains **43.3** (base Qwen3-32B ≈ 70) — behavioral filtering preserves non-search reasoning far better.

## Critical Assessment

- **The baseline set is the weakest point.** MV and BoN with self-reported confidence are the two easiest test-time scaling baselines to beat; Table 1's comparison against parallel aggregation, reactive recovery, and verifier-based selection is *conceptual only* — there is no empirical head-to-head with tree search (e.g., LATS-style), process-reward verification, or even BoN with an external verifier. Given the paper's own framing as a better way to allocate test-time compute, the absence of a compute-matched stronger baseline (e.g., BoN with N=9 or 12 to match HypoSearch's tool-call budget) is a real gap: on ResearchRubrics, HypoSearch's edge over BoN shrinks to +0.7/+2.3/+0.5, suggesting gains compress when evaluation is rubric-based rather than answer-exact.
- **Two of four benchmarks lack the strongest baselines.** In Table 2, MV and BoN are simply "-" on FutureX and ResearchRubrics, so on half the benchmark suite the headline comparison is against Pass@1 alone; the claim of "consistently outperforming single-trajectory and standard parallel baselines" is only fully verifiable on BC-small and BrowseComp-zh.
- **BC-small is self-constructed.** Results on the full BrowseComp are not reported; evaluating on a self-made "lightweight subset" whose selection procedure is deferred to the appendix leaves open the possibility of easier-than-original coverage, and cross-paper comparability is lost.
- **gpt-4.1 is both the judge and the annotator.** The same model that scores answers on three of four benchmarks also assigns the c_i labels that define CIR and grades failure stages. HypoSearch's evidence-rich, structured answers may be systematically favored by an LLM judge; no inter-annotator agreement numbers or judge-bias controls are reported in the main text.
- **The K-sensitivity result cuts against robustness.** Figure 6 shows HypoSearch at K=1–3 can fall below MV/BoN on BrowseComp-zh; the method only clearly wins at K=5 (which the paper acknowledges is needed "to cover a useful hypothesis"). This means real deployments need enough budget to hit the sweet spot, and the prompt-based divergent-state detector (admitted by the authors to be fallible) adds a further unquantified error source.
- **The pilot study is a proof of concept, not a training recipe.** The Q = 0.8·CIR + 0.2·SSR weights are a heuristic ("rather than tuning the weights"), only one base model (Qwen3-32B) is tested, SFT-only with no RL comparison, and both SFT variants catastrophically degrade AIME25 relative to the base model (~70 → 43.3 at best) — behavioral filtering mitigates but does not solve search-tuning's reasoning tax.
- **Genuine strengths offset these concerns**: the CIR×SSR 88%-vs-25%-vs-3% grid is a genuinely novel and reusable empirical finding; gains replicate across three very different backbones; the tool-call savings relative to MV are real and mechanistically explained (direction-split rather than trajectory-duplicated compute); and the limitations section is unusually candid.

## Strengths

- **Diagnosis before prescription**: the failure taxonomy and Jaccard-divergence analysis (BC query similarity dropping 0.53 → 0.16 by Round 3) give the method a data-driven motivation, and cleanly explain *why* pre-commit allocation (HypoSearch) rather than post-hoc verification is the right lever for BrowseComp-style tasks.
- **The behavioral metrics (CIR/SSR) are a contribution in their own right**: simple, model-agnostic, computable from query logs, and shown to be strongly predictive (88% vs 3% success spread) — useful for evaluation, data curation, and possibly reward shaping beyond this paper.
- **Evidence-level aggregation, not answer-level voting**: branches preserve per-direction evidence and the aggregation agent outputs rejection reasons, which is more interpretable and auditable than majority votes on final answers.
- **Efficiency argument is concrete**: 22.1–35.4% fewer tool calls than MV while scoring higher, because branches split the space instead of duplicating whole trajectories.
- **Consistency**: improvements hold across Kimi-K25, Qwen3.5-122B, and DeepSeek-V3.2 and across four benchmarks with a shared per-benchmark tool environment, and the pilot study connects the test-time behavior back to training-data selection — an unusually complete loop for a workshop-style framework paper.

## Limitations

- **Fixed branching configuration** (stated by the authors): the number of hypotheses K and per-branch budgets are fixed rather than adapted to state uncertainty; ambiguous states may get redundant hypotheses while hard states are under-explored.
- **Prompt-based divergent-state detector** may misclassify ambiguous states, routing divergent problems to single-path search or vice versa; no detector accuracy is reported.
- **Small-K instability**: with K < 4, HypoSearch can underperform simple MV/BoN (Figure 6), so the effective minimum budget is ~5 branches.
- **No empirical comparison with verifier-based or tree-search scaling**, despite Table 1 arguing they are complementary.
- **Evaluation depends on gpt-4.1** as judge (and annotator for CIR/failure labels), with BC-small being a self-constructed subset rather than the full BrowseComp.
- **Pilot study scope**: heuristic Q weights, one base model, SFT only, and severe AIME25 degradation even under filtering (43.3 vs ~70 base) — search-oriented SFT still taxes general reasoning.

## Relevance

与你最优先的"自进化智能体与自动化研究"方向直接相关：HypoSearch 本质上是测试时的自动假设生成 + 实验（搜索）+ 证据比较闭环，正是你关注的 automated hypothesis generation 在检索智能体场景的落地；其 pilot study 展示用行为信号（CIR/SSR）筛选 SFT 轨迹能让模型"越训越会搜"，也触碰了长程自主智能体系统的可靠性与训练数据自我改进问题。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a compute-matched Best-of-N (e.g., N=9–12, matching HypoSearch's total tool-call budget) close the accuracy gap on BC-small and BrowseComp-zh?
- Can the prompt-based divergent-state detector be replaced with a learned or confidence-aware router, and what fraction of states does it currently misroute?
- Does HypoSearch compose with post-hoc verification (running a verifier on the aggregated answer), and does the CIR/SSR signal transfer as an RL reward rather than an SFT data filter?
- The failure taxonomy shows GAIA failures are mostly downstream (tool-use/synthesis) — does HypoSearch actually help or waste compute on such benchmarks, given its ResearchRubrics gains are under +5?
- Can the K-sensitivity be removed by adaptive hypothesis merging/splitting (e.g., expand K only when branch evidence conflicts remain after aggregation)?
