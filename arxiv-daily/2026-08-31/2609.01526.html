Replacing free-text scientific beliefs with evolving structural causal models cuts prediction error ~100× and nearly doubles pass@5.

一句话：把科学 agent 的信念从自由文本升级为可演化的结构因果模型假设种群，闭环“判别实验—假设修正”—— DiscoverPhysics 上归一化 MSE 降约两个数量级，pass@5 27.27%→63.64%，演化出的 SCM 还可零样本迁移到更小模型。

EvoSCM: Scientific Belief Revision Through Causal Model Evolution and Experimentation

Qing Zhao, Haowei Li, Weijian Deng, et al. · Sun Yat-sen University & Tsinghua SIGS

  • agentic scientific discovery
  • causal models
  • belief revision
  • experiment design
Key Metric ~100×

lower normalized forward-prediction MSE: 2.83×10⁻² → 2.77×10⁻⁴ · GPT-5.5 backbone, DiscoverPhysics (~224× at GPT-5.4).

Why This Matters

Shifts self-evolution from improving how agents reason to evolving what they believe — and the evolved causal knowledge transfers zero-shot across model families.

Critical Edge

Every headline number comes from one physics benchmark against an unstructured prompting baseline — no ablations, no error bars, no cost accounting; generality is asserted, not shown.

Methodology

  1. Beliefs as a population of SCMs. The epistemic state Pt holds K competing hypotheses, each a structural causal model (latents U, DAG G, structural equations F, parameters Θ) — predictions falsifiable, discrepancies attributable to specific commitments.
  2. Phase 1 — discriminate, don't explore. Per-hypothesis abduction of latent states; choose the intervention do(X=x) that maximizes disagreement among hypotheses (Box & Hill discrimination); every hypothesis pre-registers a falsifiable prediction before the experiment runs.
  3. Phase 2 — minimal structural edits. Recurring prediction–observation gaps are induced into natural-language correction rules, applied via four operators (add/remove edge, add/remove latent, update mechanism, update parameter), preferring shallow edits.
  4. Deductive gate. Revised hypotheses must explain all accumulated evidence — not just the latest discrepancy — and pass DAG/mechanism consistency; failures are discarded, survivors seed the next population.
  5. Inference. Unseen interventions are answered by the do-operator on surviving models, aggregated into a population consensus — generalization, not interpolation of stored pairs.
  6. Evaluation. DiscoverPhysics interactive benchmark; GPT-5.4 / GPT-5.5 backbones vs standard prompting; transfer into Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.

Wins on all four reported axes simultaneously — explanation quality, prediction error, pass@k, and episode efficiency.

  • GPT-5.4 gap is even larger: explanation 0.653 vs 0.298, MSE ~224× lower, pass@1 38.08% vs 1.86%, in 736 vs 1,045 episodes.
  • Convergence shape: the baseline plateaus around 10⁻² error over 2,000 episodes; EvoSCM reaches ~10⁻⁴ by episode ~1,400–1,600, with the steepest gains after the exploration phase.
  • Transfer tracks source strength monotonically (0.489 → 0.576 → 0.625 → 0.740) — evidence that the SCM encodes portable epistemic content, not backbone-specific habits.

Strengths

  • Targets a real gap. Procedural self-evolving agents improve how to reason, not what to believe; making beliefs explicit, structured, and falsifiable is the right orthogonal contribution.
  • Principled, not prompt-engineered. Pearl's abduction–action–prediction, Box & Hill model discrimination, and pre-registered predictions give the loop genuine epistemological discipline.
  • Wins on all four axes at once — no trade-off between explanation, error, pass rate, and efficiency; the ~100× MSE reduction is large and unambiguous.
  • Cross-model transfer is a distinctive result, with a clean monotonic ranking across source-model strength.
  • Anti-myopia guard: evidence validation rejects hypotheses that fix the latest discrepancy but contradict older evidence.

Limitations

  • Single-benchmark evaluation. Everything is DiscoverPhysics; SCM-with-differential-equation mechanisms fit physics worlds specifically — no second domain, no ablations, and K and T are never stated.
  • Weak baseline. The comparison is standard prompting; cited closed-loop discovery systems are not compared, so the delta attributable to EvoSCM's specific design is unmeasured.
  • Cost is hidden. Episodes drop, but each round runs K-way abduction, population prediction, induction, revision, and validation — sample efficiency may not be cost efficiency.
  • No variance reporting: pass@k without trials, worlds, seeds, or error bars; point estimates like 1.86% → 38.08% need more support.
  • Transfer conflates knowledge with capability — the small model executes a frontier model's distilled physics; only strong→weak direction, one small MoE receiver. Population dynamics (collapse, compatible-but-wrong hypotheses) unexamined.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. What do per-episode token/cost curves look like — is the K-hypothesis population strictly more expensive than the baseline even with fewer episodes?
  2. Does performance degrade gracefully as K → 1? Is the population mechanism or the structured representation doing the heavy lifting?
  3. Can the four revision operators be audited empirically — how often are latents added/removed vs parameters updated, and do correction rules converge to the true mechanisms?
  4. Does the loop survive noisier, partially observable, or non-physical domains where mechanisms are less cleanly expressible as SCM equations?
  5. When a transferred SCM encodes wrong physics for a new world, does the receiving agent's loop detect and repair the imported beliefs — or does the portable artifact actively mislead?
  6. Does population consensus aggregation beat selecting the single best-validated hypothesis at inference time, and by how much?
