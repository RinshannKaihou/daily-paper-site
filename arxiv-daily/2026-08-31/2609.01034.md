> **一句话总结：** Inria/Sorbonne 的 Raphaël Berthier 提出"锐谷假设"（把损失写为 f = g + εh，其中 g 定义极小值谷、ε≪1 相当于谷锐度的倒数），将边缘稳定性（edge of stability）下的梯度下降视为 ε 的奇异摄动动力系统，用经典多重尺度（method of multiple scales）方法形式化导出三个时间尺度——t₁=k（沿最陡方向的振荡）、t₂=ε^{1/2}k（自稳定机制）、t₃=εk（谷内慢速演化）——并证明 Cohen 等人 (ICLR 2025) 的 central flow（约束在锐度 ≤ 2/η 稳定谷内的 h 梯度流）正是 ε→0 时的 leading-order 极限，自稳定机制出现在次阶 ε^{1/2} 项；同时给出多特征值同时处于边缘稳定时自稳定系统一般不存在不动点、因而波动持续存在的机制解释，并澄清了 Cohen 等人含糊的"局部时间平均"算子 𝔼 即固定 t₃ 下对 t₂ 求平均。

> **TL;DR:** The central flow of Cohen et al. (ICLR 2025) — the gradient flow of the loss constrained to the region where sharpness ≤ 2/η — empirically tracks averaged gradient descent across architectures, but its original derivation was heuristic. This paper gives it a formal footing via a "sharp valley" regime: writing the loss as f = g + εh (ε = inverse sharpness of the valley of minimizers of g), gradient descent becomes a singularly perturbed system with three timescales, t₁ = k, t₂ = ε^{1/2}k, t₃ = εk, analyzed by the method of multiple scales. On the 2D minimal example (η = 0.3, ε swept from 10⁻² to 10⁻⁵), iterates collapse onto the central flow on t₃ and the rescaled deviations ε^{-1/2}(x−x₀), ε^{-1/2}(y−y₀) collapse on t₂ = ε^{1/2}k, confirming the next-order term; the derived self-stabilization energy law ∂_{t₃}E = η²(2−9ζ²)⟨y₁²⟩_{t₂} matches the empirical energy decay for ζ = 0.5 at ε = 10⁻⁵ (E − E_min = ½(1−log 2) falls from ≈10⁻¹ to ≈2×10⁻² over t₃ ∈ [0,80]) and predicts the observed saturation for ζ = 0. On an MLP (two hidden layers of width 256, GELU, CIFAR-10 subset, MSE, η = 0.03) whose top Hessian eigenvalue fluctuates around 2/η ≈ 66.7, the theory explains why self-stabilization fluctuations shrink while a single eigenvalue sits at the edge and stay large once a second eigenvalue reaches 2/η. Versus competing approximations (rod flow, edge gradient descent, free energy flow), the central flow is the coarsest — it averages over both t₁ and t₂ — but also the simplest.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"锐谷假设"并确立 central flow 的摄动化地位**：假设损失 f = g + εh，g 定义极小值谷 V = {z : g = min g}（线性/流形局部坐标），ε≪1 为小参数（等价于以学习率 εη 跑 ε⁻¹g + h，即 ε 是谷锐度的倒数）；在此体制下 ε→0 时梯度下降的形式极限就是 central flow——h 在稳定谷 V_S = {z ∈ V : ηS(z) ⩽ 2 Id} 内的约束梯度流，这回答了"Cohen 等人的启发式推导到底在什么体制下成立"这一悬而未决的问题。
- **三时间尺度分解**：t₁ = k（迭代数，最陡方向逐迭代振荡）、t₂ = ε^{1/2}k（自稳定机制的中间尺度）、t₃ = εk（沿谷演化的慢尺度，即 central flow 本身）；用多重尺度法把三者解耦，central flow 是 leading-order 项，自稳定振荡与波动是 ε^{1/2} 次阶项。
- **一般性结果（Result 3.1/3.2/5.1/6.1）**：余维 1 与任意余维谷情形下，z₀(t₃) 服从带 KKT 乘子的约束梯度流 ∂_{t₃}y₀ = −η[∇_y h + (σ²/2)∇_y S]（σ²=0 或 ηS(0,y₀)=2，互补松弛成立）；在边缘稳定处次阶项 a₁、y₁ 满足显式自稳定 ODE 组，并把 σ² 精确化为 σ² = ⟨a₁²⟩_{t₂}——从而澄清了 Cohen 等人"局部时间平均"算子 𝔼 的含义：固定 t₃ 对 t₂ 求平均。
- **多特征值情形的新机制（Result 6.1）**：定义临界子空间 C = ker(2Id − ηS(0,y₀))，给出矩阵版自稳定系统（∂_{t₂}a₁ = ηU₁a₁，∂_{t₂}U₁ = −η[Ψ(∇_y h) + ½ΨΨ*[a₁a₁ᵀ]]）；由于秩一解条件 ΨΨ*[ã₁ã₁ᵀ] = −2Ψ[∇_y h] 非通有（non-generic），自稳定系统一般**没有不动点**，a₁a₁ᵀ 必须持续演化——这解释了多个 Hessian 特征值同时处于边缘稳定时训练中波动持续存在的现象（Damian 等人 [10] 遗留的开放问题）。
- **损失的 O(ε) 精细结构（Corollary 6.2）**：f(x,y) = min g + εf₂ + o(ε)，对 t₂ 平均后 ⟨f₂⟩_{t₂} = h(0,y₀) + TrΣ/η，与 Cohen 等人结果一致；自稳定波动在 t₂ 上诱导 loss 尖刺，为训练中观察到的 loss 振荡提供解析刻画。
- **边界条件与失效模式（Remark 4.3、Result 4.4）**：能量定律 ∂_{t₃}E = η²(2−9ζ²)⟨y₁²⟩_{t₂} 给出耗散判据；构造反例（h = −y 时轨迹离开稳定谷并发散）说明横向扰动（∇h 的垂直分量、噪声、数值误差）对 central flow 成立是必要的——这是一个明确的猜想式必要条件。

## Key Contributions

- A **sharp valley assumption** (loss f = g + εh, ε ≪ 1 the inverse sharpness of the valley) defining a perturbative regime in which the **central flow is the formal limit of gradient descent** as ε → 0 — previously the central flow had only a heuristic derivation.
- A **three-timescale decomposition** of edge-of-stability gradient descent via the method of multiple scales: t₁ = k (oscillations along the sharpest direction), t₂ = ε^{1/2}k (self-stabilization mechanism), t₃ = εk (dynamics along the valley = central flow); the central flow is the leading term, self-stabilization appears at next order ε^{1/2}.
- **General results** for valleys of codimension one (Results 3.1, 5.1) and arbitrary codimension (Results 3.2, 6.1): the leading dynamics obey the gradient flow of h constrained to the stable valley with an explicit KKT multiplier (σ² or a matrix Σ ∈ Sym₊X enforcing ηS ⩽ 2 Id), and the next-order terms satisfy explicit self-stabilization ODE systems; the multiplier is pinned down as σ² = ⟨a₁²⟩_{t₂}, clarifying Cohen et al.'s informal "local time-averaging" operator 𝔼 as averaging over t₂ at fixed t₃.
- **A mechanism for persistent fluctuations** (Result 6.1): with several Hessian eigenvalues at the edge of stability, the self-stabilization system generically has no fixed point (the rank-one condition ΨΨ*[ã₁ã₁ᵀ] = −2Ψ[∇_y h] is non-generic), so fluctuations must persist — resolving a question left open by Damian et al.'s single-eigenvalue analysis [10].
- **Loss structure at order ε** (Corollary 6.2): ⟨f₂⟩_{t₂} = h(0,y₀) + TrΣ/η, consistent with Cohen et al. Sec 3.2.3; self-stabilization spikes on t₂ explain observed loss oscillations.
- **Failure-mode analysis**: an energy law ∂_{t₃}E = η²(2−9ζ²)⟨y₁²⟩_{t₂} with a dissipation threshold, and a constructed counterexample (Remark 4.3, h = −y) where the central flow prediction fails, showing a transversal perturbation in x (component of ∇h, noise, numerical error) is necessary for the central flow to hold.

## Methodology

**Setting.** Gradient descent z(k+1) = z(k) − η∇f(z(k)) on a loss f = g + εh, where g defines a valley of minimizers V = {z : g = min g} (assumed linear for clarity, z = (x,y) ∈ X ⊕⊥ Y), S(z) = ∇²_y g(z) is the Hessian in the sharp directions, and the stable valley is V_S = {z ∈ V : ηS(z) ⩽ 2 Id}. The parameter ε is interpreted as the inverse sharpness of the valley (running GD with learning rate η on f = g + εh equals running GD with learning rate εη on ε⁻¹g + h). The analysis assumes the rescaled limit iterates z₀(t₃) = (x₀, y₀) remain in the stable valley (x₀ = 0, ηS(0,y₀) ⩽ 2 Id).

**Method of multiple scales.** The iterates are expanded as x = x₀ + ε^{1/2}x₁ + εx₂ + …, y = y₀ + ε^{1/2}y₁ + εy₂ + …, and all quantities are treated as functions of the three independent timescales z = z(t₁, t₂, t₃). The discrete update Δ_k z = −η∇f(z) becomes a partial difference-differential equation; expanding both sides in ε (orders 1, ε^{1/2}, ε, and ε^{3/2} in Appendix A for the energy law) and matching orders, **secular terms are forbidden** — any unbounded-in-t₁ component would eventually outweigh the leading term — which closes the system and yields evolution equations for the lower-order terms. A key tool is Lemma 4.2: the difference equation Δ_{t₁}φ + 2φ = α + β(−1)^{t₁} has a bounded solution iff β = 0, which converts the (−1)^{t₁} structure of the edge-of-stability oscillations into ODE constraints. At the edge of stability (ηy₀ = 2), x₁ = (−1)^{t₁}a₁(t₂,t₃) with a₁ governed by the self-stabilization system ∂_{t₂}a₁ = ηa₁y₁, ∂_{t₃}y₁ = η(1 − a₁²/2), which preserves the energy E = y₁²/2 + a₁²/4 − ½log a₁² (the invariant of Damian et al. [10]). The order-ε^{3/2} computation (Appendix A) then gives the slow energy drift of Result 4.4. The general-codimension case (Result 6.1) projects the oscillation onto the critical subspace C = ker(2Id − ηS(0,y₀)) and lifts the scalar self-stabilization loop to a matrix-valued system driven by the operator Ψ: Y → Sym C, Ψ[δy] = P_C D_y S(0,y₀)[δy]|_C.

**Status of the results.** The method of multiple scales is *formal* (asymptotic matching without convergence proofs or remainder bounds): the author repeatedly and explicitly labels every result "a conjecture with strong supporting evidence," not a theorem.

**Validation.** (i) A minimal 2D example g(x,y) = ½(y+ζx)x², h(x,y) = −x−y — deliberately engineered (the x³ term reproduces the damping of the self-stabilization loop; the −x term breaks metastability), with experiments at η = 0.3 and ε ∈ {10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵}; (ii) energy diagnostics comparing E^emp against the predicted linear law at ε = 10⁻⁵; (iii) one neural-network illustration — an MLP with two hidden layers of width 256, GELU, trained with MSE on a subset of CIFAR-10 at η = 0.03, using the code of Cohen et al. [8]; (iv) higher-dimensional toy valleys (Fig 1: 2D valley, Fig 2: codimension-2 valley).

## Results & Highlights

- **Convergence to the central flow (Figs 1, 4):** on the minimal example with η = 0.3, ζ = 1 and ε swept over three orders of magnitude (10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵), the iterates collapse onto the central-flow trajectory on the t₃ = εk axis, and the rescaled deviations ε^{-1/2}(x−x₀) and ε^{-1/2}(y−y₀) collapse onto single curves on t₂ = ε^{1/2}k — numerically confirming that the central flow is the leading term and that oscillations/self-stabilization enter at order ε^{1/2}, exactly as the expansion predicts.
- **Energy law confirmed to its own breaking point (Fig 7, ε = 10⁻⁵, η = 0.3):** Result 4.4 predicts ∂_{t₃}E = η²(2−9ζ²)⟨y₁²⟩_{t₂}; for ζ = 0.5 the empirical energy E^emp − E_min (E_min = ½(1−log 2)) matches the predicted decay almost exactly, falling from ≈10⁻¹ to ≈2×10⁻² over t₃ ∈ [0,80]; for ζ = 0 the predicted growth tracks the data only until the energy saturates at a plateau around 10¹ — roughly three orders of magnitude above its initial ≈10⁻² level — where the paper shows the O(1)-energy asymptotic expansion breaks down (the theory does not cover the saturation regime).
- **Neural-network phenomenology explained (Fig 8):** an MLP (2 hidden layers × width 256, GELU, CIFAR-10 subset, MSE, η = 0.03) has its top two Hessian eigenvalues climb from ≈20 to fluctuate around 2/η ≈ 66.7 within ≈1000 iterations; the amplitude of self-stabilization fluctuations decreases while only one eigenvalue is at the edge, then stabilizes at a visibly larger value once the second eigenvalue reaches 2/η — exactly the persistent-fluctuation behavior predicted by the no-fixed-point argument of Result 6.1 (and previously observed in Cohen et al.'s experiments).
- **Comparison with competing approximations (Fig 10, η = 0.3, ε = 0.03):** in the same toy setting, the rod flow [30] fails to detect the onset of the edge-of-stability phase entirely; edge gradient descent [27] and the free energy flow [7] generate the self-stabilization loops but with wrong magnitude or phase; the central flow averages over both t₁ and t₂ (the others average only over t₁) and is the coarsest — hence simplest — of the four, at comparable or only marginally worse accuracy.
- **Loss structure (Corollary 6.2, Fig 9):** with ε ∈ {0.01, 0.001, 0.0001} on the 2D-valley example, the rescaled loss ε⁻¹(f − min) converges to the central-flow prediction h(0,y₀) − min + σ²/η, showing the loss progress made along the valley and the loss increase due to oscillations are both exactly of order ε; the t₂ self-stabilization fluctuations appear as order-ε spikes in the raw loss.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is genuine but positioning matters.** The paper does not prove anything about real neural networks; its contribution is to exhibit a regime (sharp valley, ε → 0) where the central flow is the correct leading-order limit and to supply the machinery (multiple scales) that generates both the central flow and the self-stabilization corrections in one systematic expansion. That is a real advance over Cohen et al. [8], whose "local time-averaging" operator 𝔼 was left informal — this paper pins 𝔼 down as t₂-averaging at fixed t₃ (σ² = ⟨a₁²⟩_{t₂}) — and over Damian et al. [10], whose self-stabilization analysis covered a single eigenvalue and explicitly left the multi-eigenvalue case open (Result 6.1 here handles it, with the no-fixed-point/persistent-fluctuation conclusion).
- **Everything is formal.** No convergence theorem is proven; remainders are not bounded; the leading-dynamics-in-the-stable-region assumption (ηS(0,y₀) ⩽ 2 Id) is assumed, not derived, and the author admits artificial counterexamples exist where it fails (Remark 4.3). The fix — a transversal perturbation in x is necessary — is offered as a conjecture. Readers should treat all "Results" as structured conjectures with numerical support, exactly as the author does.
- **The bridge to deep learning is empirical and thin.** The sharp valley structure is motivated only indirectly (anti-alignment of successive iterates [42], approximate low-rank Hessians [31,32,28,29,4], tiny-subspace gradients [13,19]); no experiment fits f = g + εh to a real network or estimates ε from a training run. The single deep-learning validation is one small MLP (2×256, CIFAR-10 subset, MSE) — and even there the agreement is qualitative (amplitude goes down, then up), with no quantitative trajectory-fit metric computed. Nothing at transformer/LLM scale or with stochastic mini-batch noise, even though the whole analysis is full-batch GD and Remark 4.3 itself argues noise-like transversal perturbations are load-bearing.
- **The minimal example is reverse-engineered.** The author candidly states that simpler choices (g = ½yx², h = −y) do not reproduce the phenomenology: the x³ term in g was chosen to reproduce the damping of the self-stabilization loop and the −x term in h to break metastability in x. Pedagogically legitimate, but it means the toy model's fidelity is by construction, and the ζ-dissipation threshold (9ζ² vs 2) is a property of this engineered family, not of a identified class of real losses.
- **Known internal failure mode.** When 9ζ² < 2 the predicted energy growth saturates instead of diverging, breaking the ε^{1/2}-expansion (Fig 7b); the author notes the analysis holds only at O(1) energy levels while neural networks appear to systematically dissipate energy with one eigenvalue at the edge — and why they do so is left open (attributed to loss-structure question, cf. [38]). So even within its own regime, the framework's coverage is partial.
- **Reproducibility.** Toy experiments are fully specified (equations, η, ζ, ε values) and simple to re-run; the MLP experiment depends on Cohen et al.'s public code; no separate code release for this paper's new figures is mentioned. AI-use disclosure is present and appropriately scoped.
- **Overall:** an honest, well-executed piece of formal applied-math theory that genuinely clarifies an influential empirical model. Its main weakness is not what it claims but what it cannot yet claim: the regime is an idealization (linear valley, full-batch, O(1) energy), and the empirical validation on real networks is one illustrative figure.

## Strengths

- First perturbative regime in which the central flow is the formal limit of gradient descent, turning a heuristic model into the leading term of a systematic ε-expansion.
- One machinery yields everything at once: central flow (order 1), oscillations and self-stabilization ODEs (order ε^{1/2}), loss spikes (order ε), and the energy drift (order ε^{3/2}) — a clean hierarchy from codimension one to arbitrary codimension.
- Settles two open questions from prior work: the meaning of Cohen et al.'s 𝔼 operator, and the multi-eigenvalue self-stabilization case of Damian et al. [10], with a falsifiable prediction (persistent fluctuations) confirmed qualitatively on the MLP experiment.
- Exemplary scientific honesty: formal status stated at every result; the paper's own breakdown (energy saturation, Fig 7b) and a constructed failure case for the central flow (Fig 6, Remark 4.3) are presented prominently rather than hidden.
- The framework is extensible by design — the author outlines rigorous singular-perturbation formalization, curved valleys, and extension to inertial/adaptive optimizers as future work.

## Limitations

- Entirely formal: no rigorous convergence results, no explicit remainder bounds; all results are conjectures with supporting numerics.
- Assumes a linear valley of minimizers; curved valleys (the realistic manifold case) are deferred to future work, and the f = g + εh decomposition is never fitted to a real network.
- The constraint ηS(0,y₀) ⩽ 2 Id is an assumption, not a derived property; the paper constructs examples where the central flow prediction fails without it (Fig 6), and the remedy (transversal perturbation) is conjectural.
- Empirical validation is limited to 2D/3D toy valleys and one small MLP on a CIFAR-10 subset with MSE loss — no quantitative fit metric, no transformers, no LLM-scale or stochastic (mini-batch) setting.
- The self-stabilization/energy analysis is valid only at O(1) energy levels; the saturation regime (ζ = 0 in Fig 7b) is outside the theory, and why real networks dissipate self-stabilization energy is left open.
- Full-batch gradient descent only; SGD noise — plausibly the dominant transversal perturbation in practice per the paper's own Remark 4.3 — is not modeled.

## Relevance

与"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"方向高度相关：本文给出边缘稳定区自稳定波动与 loss 尖刺（O(ε) 阶，⟨f₂⟩ = h + TrΣ/η）的解析机制，以及"多特征值同时到达锐度 2/η 时波动持续、单特征值时波动衰减"的可检验判据，可直接用作训练监控中 sharpness/loss 振荡异常的理论基线与归因依据。不过它是纯理论工作（全批量 GD、线性锐谷假设、玩具模型 + 一个小 MLP），本身不提供任何可落地的监控工具或大规模训练证据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the sharp valley decomposition f = g + εh be quantitatively fitted to real transformer training runs — e.g., estimating ε as the inverse valley sharpness and testing the predicted t₂ = ε^{1/2}k and t₃ = εk scalings of loss-spike statistics?
- Does an analogous constrained-flow + KKT-multiplier structure survive for momentum, Adam, and other adaptive optimizers (the author announces this extension), and what replaces the sharpness constraint ηS ⩽ 2 Id there?
- Why do neural networks systematically dissipate self-stabilization energy when a single eigenvalue is at the edge of stability — what property of the loss structure (cf. the river-valley picture of [38]) forces ζ into the 9ζ² > 2 regime?
- In the multi-eigenvalue regime, the self-stabilization system generically has no fixed point — can the resulting quasi-periodic fluctuation amplitude be predicted quantitatively, and does it correlate with observed loss-spike magnitudes at scale?
- How does mini-batch noise interact with the t₂ self-stabilization loop when noise is itself the transversal perturbation that Remark 4.3 shows is necessary for the central flow to hold?
- Can the saturation regime beyond O(1) energy (Fig 7b) be captured by pushing the expansion to higher orders, or is it a genuine route to training divergence that the current framework cannot see?
