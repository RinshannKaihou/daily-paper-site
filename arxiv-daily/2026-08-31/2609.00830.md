> **一句话总结：** 通过对语言模型首层解码器入口的视觉 token 做零消融因果扰动，本文首次系统评估 VLM 视觉注意力忠实性，发现其呈三种异构模式——在 Qwen2.5-VL-7B 的 VQAv2 子集（85 样本）上，忠实-充分模式占 32.9%（Comp 0.978 / Sgap −0.026）、忠实-分布式占 47.1%（Comp 0.952 / Sgap 0.818）、非聚焦占 20.0%（Comp 0.213）；且注意力排序的 comprehensiveness（0.813）显著高于人类标注 GT 区域的 0.505（+0.308），人类标注仅在约 60% 的样本上满足 comprehensiveness，说明它并非模型视觉依赖的可靠代理。

> **TL;DR:** The authors causally test whether attention-ranked visual tokens in VLMs are faithful, by zero-ablating their hidden states at the first decoder layer and measuring normalized comprehensiveness (Comp) and sufficiency gap (Sgap). On VQAv2 (85 valid samples, Qwen2.5-VL-7B), attention-ranked top-k tokens reach mean Comp 0.813 vs only 0.505 for human-annotated GT regions (+0.308 delta) and the lowest Sgap 0.469 (GT: 0.846, Random: 0.599). Faithfulness splits into three modes: Faithful-Sufficient (32.9% of samples, Comp 0.978 / Sgap −0.026), Faithful-Distributed (47.1%, Comp 0.952 / Sgap 0.818), and Non-Focal (20.0%, Comp 0.213). The framework transfers to document IE (VRDU) and ChartQA and across model scales, but the same task lands in different modes under different architectures: Qwen2.5-VL treats document IE as extreme Mode B (Sgap ≈ 0.947) while InternVL2.5-8B treats it as Mode A (Sgap drops to 0.189 at k=10%).

## 核心贡献

- **首次对 VLM 视觉注意力做系统性忠实性评估**：将 NLP 中的 comprehensiveness / sufficiency 忠实性度量（DeYoung et al. 2020）迁移到视觉 token，通过对语言模型解码器第一层入口的视觉 token 隐状态做零消融（zero-ablation），建立注意力排序与预测概率之间的因果关系，而非仅停留在注意力热力图的相关性观察。
- **发现三种异构视觉处理模式**：用两阶段 Otsu 阈值 + 人工修正把 (Comp, Sgap) 空间划分为忠实-充分（Faithful-Sufficient，top-k token 既必要又充分）、忠实-分布式（Faithful-Distributed，top-k 必要但不充分，需更广上下文）、非聚焦（Non-Focal，无局部区域单独必要但图像整体仍是预测触发器）三区；模式与问题类型强相关（如开放型问题 34.5% 落入 Mode C）。
- **揭示模型注意力与人类直觉的系统性分歧**：GT 区域仅在约 60% 的样本上满足 comprehensiveness；对齐子集的 GT-Comp 达 0.963，而未对齐子集的 GT-Comp 为 −0.150（消融人类标注区域反而让预测概率上升），此时注意力 top-k 的 Comp 仍高达 0.945——人类标注不是模型视觉依赖的完整代理。
- **跨任务、跨架构、跨规模的验证**：三模式框架从 VQA 推广到文档信息抽取（VRDU）和 ChartQA；在 Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B 与 InternVL2.5-4B/8B 上均复现，但同一任务的模式归属随架构变化（Qwen 的 IE 为 Mode B，InternVL 的 IE 趋近 Mode A），提示注意力感知的 token 选择/压缩方法可迁移性受架构影响。

## Key Contributions

- First systematic causal evaluation of visual attention faithfulness in VLMs: comprehensiveness and sufficiency-gap metrics adapted from NLP are applied to visual tokens via zero-ablation of their hidden states at the first decoder layer, establishing a causal (not merely correlational) link between attention rankings and predictions.
- Identification of three heterogeneous processing modes — Faithful-Sufficient, Faithful-Distributed, and Non-Focal — obtained by two-stage Otsu thresholding in the (Comp, Sgap) plane, with mode shares tied to question types (Yes/No → 55.6% Mode B; open-ended "other" → 34.5% Mode C).
- Systematic divergence between model attention and human intuition: GT regions satisfy comprehensiveness in only ~60% of cases; the misaligned subset shows mean GT-Comp of −0.150 while attention top-k Comp stays at 0.945.
- Cross-task, cross-architecture, cross-scale evidence: the three-mode structure holds on VRDU, ChartQA, and on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B and InternVL2.5-4B/8B, while the mode assigned to a given task is architecture-dependent (Qwen IE = Mode B with Sgap ≈ 0.947; InternVL IE ≈ Mode A with Sgap 0.189 at k=10%).

## Methodology

**Attention-based token ranking.** For a VLM answering query y from visual tokens V = {v_1..v_N} and text tokens T, attention weights α_{l,h}(i,j) are aggregated over all L layers (sum) and H heads (mean) and over the generated answer tokens, yielding one importance score α_i per visual token. Head-mean plus layer-sum avoids assuming a single dominant routing head (citing Michel et al. 2019). The top-k set S_k is the k tokens with largest α_i.

**Causal perturbation.** Instead of masking pixels or intervening in the vision encoder, the authors zero-ablate the selected tokens' hidden states at the entry of the first decoder layer of the language backbone (h_i^(0) ← 0). Zero-ablation preserves sequence length and 2D positional structure (unlike token deletion, which would shift positions and distort layout encoding) and is a standard activation-level intervention. Inference is greedy (temperature 0) so every P(y | V, T) change is attributable to the ablation rather than sampling variance.

**Faithfulness metrics** (normalized per-sample, adapted from DeYoung et al. 2020):
- Comprehensiveness: Comp(S_k) = (P(y|V,T) − P(y|V\S_k,T)) / P(y|V,T) — does removing the top-k tokens destroy the prediction?
- Sufficiency gap: Sgap(S_k) = (P(y|V,T) − P(y|S_k,T)) / P(y|V,T) — does keeping only the top-k tokens preserve it?

**Four matched-size strategies.** For VQA, all four strategies ablate the same number of tokens k (the token count covered by the human-annotated GT region per sample): Overall Top-k (model attention), GT Object (human-annotated region), Non-GT Top-k (highest-attention tokens *outside* the GT region), and Random. For document IE, model attention on documents is already highly localized, so proportion-based perturbation (top 1%/3%/5%/10% of visual tokens) is used instead; ChartQA adopts the same proportion protocol.

**Three-mode partition.** Two-stage Otsu thresholding — first on Comp over all samples, then on Sgap within the high-Comp subset — followed by manual inspection/refinement of a small number of boundary assignments yields the A/B/C partition used throughout.

**Setup.** Main experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B; generality on InternVL2.5-8B; scaling on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL2.5-4B. Data: 90 image-question pairs sampled from VQAv2 balanced across yes/no, number, and other (30 each), each with a semi-automatically produced, human-verified GT region annotation (COCO instance segmentation masks as reference, one expert annotates, a second expert reviews; 5 samples needing whole-image understanding are excluded → 85 valid); 60 correctly-answered samples from VRDU (advertising and registration forms); 100 samples from the ChartQA test set.

## Results & Highlights

- **Attention ranking beats human GT at explaining predictions (VQAv2, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 85 valid samples).** Overall Top-k achieves mean Comp 0.813 and the lowest mean Sgap 0.469, vs GT Object 0.505 / 0.846, Non-GT Top-k 0.659 / 0.653, and Random 0.502 / 0.599 — a +0.308 Comp delta over the human-annotation baseline, meaning attention-ranked tokens are both more necessary and more sufficient than human-marked regions.
- **Three modes with distinct signatures.** Mode A (Faithful-Sufficient): 32.9% of samples, Comp 0.978, Sgap −0.026, accuracy 92.9; Mode B (Faithful-Distributed): 47.1%, Comp 0.952, Sgap 0.818, accuracy 100.0; Mode C (Non-Focal): 20.0%, Comp 0.213, Sgap 0.400, accuracy 94.1. Bootstrap re-sampling at n = 50/60/70 yields 95% CIs that exclude zero for all three shares (A [23.9, 43.5], B [36.8, 57.6], C [12.9, 29.7]), supporting the partition's stability.
- **Human-model divergence is bimodal, not noise.** In the aligned subset, mean GT-Comp reaches 0.963 vs mean Top-k Comp 0.992 (model accuracy 97.4%); in the misaligned subset, mean GT-Comp is −0.150 while Top-k Comp remains 0.945 (accuracy 94.4%). Concrete example: for "What color is the sink on the right?", ablating the human-annotated sink region leaves the prediction essentially unchanged, while ablating the attention top-k collapses it.
- **Document IE is an extreme Mode B for Qwen2.5-VL.** On VRDU, ablating only the top 1% of visual tokens reduces answer probability by 43.8%; Comp rises to 0.826 at 10%, yet retaining only the top-k recovers at most ~5% of prediction probability (Sgap ≈ 0.947) — focal evidence is necessary but layout context is indispensable. ChartQA shows the same pattern (Top-k Comp 0.37 → 0.77 from 5% to 30% mask ratio, Sgap ≥ 0.91 throughout, vs Random Comp ≤ 0.27).
- **Architecture changes the mode, framework does not.** On InternVL2.5-8B VQA, Overall Top-k again wins (Comp 0.733, Sgap 0.468 — Table 8 confirms it is highest-Comp/lowest-Sgap on both models), and Otsu re-thresholding recovers the same three-mode partition (τ = −0.49 vs Qwen's 0.59), though per-sample mode overlap with Qwen is only 50.6%. On IE, InternVL saturates Comp near 0.650 by k=3% and Sgap drops to 0.189 at k=10% (Mode A), the opposite of Qwen. Scaling (Table 6): Top-k Comp is stable — Qwen2.5-VL-3B 0.813 vs 7B 0.813; InternVL2.5-4B 0.792 vs 8B 0.753 — with all mode shares present at every scale.

## Authors & Credibility

The team is an industry-academic collaboration: Xunni Song, Weishi Wang, Kuluhana Binici, Tao Bai, Hongxin Shao, and Daniel Dahlmeier are at SAP (work performed during Song's internship at SAP), Zhongqi Yue is at Microsoft, and Jun Luo is at NTU Singapore's College of Computing and Data Science. Dahlmeier is a well-established NLP researcher, and the corresponding author is at SAP. The paper is methodologically literate: it correctly situates itself in the NLP attention-faithfulness debate (Jain & Wallace 2019; Wiegreffe & Pinter 2019; DeYoung et al. 2020), adapts established metrics rather than inventing ad-hoc ones, and openly discloses AI-assistant use for grammar polishing (Appendix E). No prior work is claimed to have systematically measured whether attention-ranked visual tokens are causally necessary/sufficient for VLM predictions, and the claim is plausible given the cited related work (FastV, LLaVA-prumerge, attention-sink and hallucination-lens papers) all assume or exploit attention rather than validate it. Credibility: good, with the caveat that this reads as a strong internship-scale project rather than a large-lab effort, and the evaluation scale reflects that.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core idea is genuinely new in the visual modality — no cited prior work causally verifies attention-ranked visual tokens against VLM predictions — and the "heterogeneous modes" framing is a real conceptual advance over binary faithful/unfaithful debates. However, both metrics (comprehensiveness/sufficiency) and the intervention technique (zero-ablation) are imported wholesale from NLP (DeYoung et al. 2020; standard activation interventions), so the contribution is a careful empirical mapping, not methodological invention.

**Rigor concerns.**
1. *Small evaluation sets.* 85 valid VQA samples, 60 VRDU, 100 ChartQA. The mode shares carry wide bootstrap CIs (Mode B: 36.8–57.6%), yet the abstract reports percentages to one decimal.
2. *Post-hoc taxonomy.* The three-way partition is Otsu-thresholded and then **manually refined** on the very samples being characterized (Fig. 2c) — the mode taxonomy is partly constructed from the data it describes, with no held-out validation that mode labels predicted on new samples behave as claimed.
3. *Missing ablation of the ablation.* The paper justifies zero-ablation over deletion (positional structure) and cites mean-ablation and Gaussian-noise ablation as standard alternatives, but never runs them — ablation-type sensitivity of Comp/Sgap is unmeasured, and zero is known to sit far off-manifold.
4. *50.6% cross-model mode overlap is interpreted favorably.* The paper frames Qwen-vs-InternVL disagreement as "architectures process the same task differently," but 50.6% overlap is close to chance-level agreement for a three-way split with unbalanced shares; no inter-rater-style baseline or re-thresholding control distinguishes genuine architectural differences from assignment instability.
5. *Annotation quality unaudited.* GT regions come from a semi-automatic COCO-mask pipeline with two experts, but no inter-annotator agreement statistics are reported, and the ~60% human-comprehensiveness headline rests entirely on this annotation quality.
6. *Fixed attention aggregation.* Sum-over-layers/mean-over-heads is used without testing alternative aggregations (e.g., attention rollout, per-layer rankings), despite citing Agmar & Zuidema (2020) on aggregation sensitivity.

**Reproducibility.** No code or data release is mentioned anywhere in the paper. Reproducing the GT-region annotations would require re-running the two-expert pipeline; per-sample Comp/Sgap values are only partially recoverable from figures and appendices.

**Significance.** The practical takeaway — attention-aware token pruning/compression (FastV-style methods) and human-GT-based faithfulness proxies are both unsafe in Mode B regimes, where distributed layout context matters — is concrete and actionable. But all conclusions are scoped to dynamic-resolution VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5); LLaVA-style fixed-resolution and reasoning-oriented VLMs are explicitly excluded, and the "model-agnostic framework, architecture-dependent modes" claim rests on exactly one architecture pair at two scales.

## Strengths

- Causal, intervention-based design with matched-ablation-size controls (all four strategies ablate the same token count), cleanly separating token-selection quality from ablation-size artifacts.
- The Non-GT Top-k and Random controls are well chosen: Non-GT Top-k (Comp 0.659, Sgap 0.653) falling between Overall Top-k and Random shows the result is not merely "any ablation hurts."
- The mode taxonomy is validated from multiple angles: question-type association (Table 3), bootstrap stability (Table 4), and re-analysis of the 5 excluded whole-image samples, where a ratio-based protocol recovers the expected B/C labels.
- Honest scoping: the Limitations section explicitly names fixed-resolution and reasoning VLMs as out of scope, and the appendix transparently discusses perturbation-design alternatives, the gradient-saliency relationship, and comparison with Uppaal et al. (2026).
- The GT-vs-attention divergence analysis (bimodal GT-Comp, aligned vs misaligned subsets with per-sample examples in Fig. 5) is the most memorable result and is carefully evidenced at the sample level.

## Limitations

- Evaluation scale is small (85 + 60 + 100 hand-managed samples) and GT annotations exist only for VQA; no large-scale automated replication.
- The three-mode taxonomy is descriptive: derived by Otsu + manual refinement on the same data, with wide CIs and no predictive validation on held-out samples.
- Limited to dynamic-resolution VLM architectures (Qwen2.5-VL native-resolution encoding, InternVL2.5 dynamic tiling); LLaVA-style fixed-resolution models and multi-step reasoning VLMs are excluded by the authors' own admission.
- Only zero-ablation is tested; no mean/noise ablation, activation patching, or gradient-based saliency comparison (the latter is deferred to future work), leaving ablation-choice sensitivity unquantified.
- No code or annotation release is stated, and the 50.6% cross-model mode overlap leaves the architecture-dependence claim only weakly supported.
- Absence-detection and holistic-scene questions (where GT covers the whole image) are excluded from the GT-aligned protocol and left as open work, so the framework's coverage of VQA question space is incomplete.

## Relevance

这篇论文与你的「LLM 隐状态与可解释性」方向直接相关：它用因果消融（activation-level intervention）严格检验注意力权重是否忠实反映模型对输入信息的依赖，把 NLP 的忠实性 debate 首次推进到多模态 VLM。其"注意力 top-k 是否可安全剪枝/压缩取决于处理模式"的结论，对推理阶段 token 剪枝与可靠性评估也有直接的参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the three-mode structure survive on reasoning-oriented VLMs with multi-step deliberation, or on fixed-resolution architectures like LLaVA, where visual information is not a separable token block?
- Are the Comp/Sgap values and mode assignments robust to mean-ablation or Gaussian-noise ablation instead of zero-ablation, given zero vectors sit far off the activation manifold?
- Can mode membership be predicted from question features or attention statistics alone on held-out data — turning the taxonomy from descriptive clustering into a usable pre-hoc diagnostic for token-pruning safety?
- Does the Non-Focal mode (attention uninformative but image essential) correlate with object hallucination, since the model relies on diffuse image-conditioned knowledge rather than localized evidence?
- How does attention-based faithfulness compare with gradient-based saliency on the same samples — do the two rankings agree within each mode?
