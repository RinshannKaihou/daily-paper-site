The Multiple Timescales of Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability: A Perturbative Derivation of the Central Flow

Raphaël Berthier · Sorbonne Université · Inria · single-author theory paper

edge-of-stability gradient-descent optimization-theory multiple-scales training-dynamics
Key Metric
10−2 → 10−5

Sweeping ε across four orders of magnitude, iterates collapse onto the central flow on t₃ and rescaled deviations onto single curves on t₂ — each expansion term appears exactly at its predicted order.

Why This Matters

Gives training monitors an analytic account of loss spikes and sharpness oscillations — plus a testable rule for when fluctuations persist — though it ships no deployable tool itself.

Critical Edge

No convergence theorem is proven — every result is a formal conjecture — and the only neural-network validation is one small MLP with no quantitative fit metric.

Methodology formal perturbation theory

Sharp valley regime. Write the loss as f = g + εh with ε ≪ 1 the inverse sharpness of the valley of minimizers of g; GD at rate η on f equals GD at rate εη on ε⁻¹g + h. The stable valley is Vₛ = {z : ηS(z) ≤ 2 Id}.

  • Method of multiple scales: expand x, y in half-powers of ε and treat all terms as functions of three independent timescales t₁ = k, t₂ = ε1/2k, t₃ = εk.
  • Secular terms forbidden: any component unbounded in t₁ would outweigh the leading term; Lemma 4.2 (boundedness of Δφ + 2φ = α + β(−1)k iff β = 0) converts the (−1)k edge oscillation into ODE constraints.
  • Self-stabilization loop at the edge: ∂t₂a₁ = ηa₁y₁, ∂t₃y₁ = η(1 − a₁²/2), preserving Damian et al.’s energy invariant; the order-ε3/2 computation yields the slow energy drift.
  • Arbitrary codimension: project oscillations onto the critical subspace C = ker(2Id − ηS); a matrix-valued self-stabilization system driven by the curvature operator Ψ.
Status: formal asymptotics — no convergence proofs, no remainder bounds. The author labels every result “a conjecture with strong supporting evidence.”

Results beyond the headline

  • Energy law confirmed to its own breaking point: ∂t₃E = η²(2 − 9ζ²)⟨y₁²⟩t₂. For ζ = 0.5, E − Emin falls ≈10−1 → ≈2×10−2 over t₃ ∈ [0, 80] at ε = 10−5, matching prediction; for ζ = 0, growth tracks until saturation at a plateau ≈10¹ — where the O(1)-energy expansion itself breaks down.
  • MLP phenomenology explained: 2×256 GELU MLP, CIFAR-10 subset, MSE, η = 0.03 — top Hessian eigenvalues climb from ≈20 to fluctuate around 2/η ≈ 66.7 within ~1000 steps; fluctuation amplitude shrinks with one eigenvalue at the edge, then jumps up once a second eigenvalue reaches 2/η — the no-fixed-point prediction of Result 6.1.
  • Loss structure: ε−1(f − min) converges to h(0,y₀) + TrΣ/η; progress along the valley and the cost of oscillation are both exactly order ε, and t₂ fluctuations appear as order-ε loss spikes.

Strengths

  • First perturbative regime where the central flow is the formal limit of GD — a heuristic model becomes the leading term of a systematic ε-expansion.
  • One machinery yields everything: central flow (order 1), self-stabilization ODEs (ε1/2), loss spikes (ε), energy drift (ε3/2) — a clean hierarchy from codimension one to arbitrary codimension.
  • Settles two open questions: the meaning of Cohen et al.’s 𝔘 operator (t₂-averaging at fixed t₃), and the multi-eigenvalue case left open by Damian et al. — with a falsifiable prediction, confirmed qualitatively on the MLP.
  • Exemplary honesty: formal status stated at every result; the paper’s own breakdown (energy saturation) and a constructed failure case for the central flow are shown prominently.

Limitations

  • Entirely formal: no rigorous convergence results, no explicit remainder bounds — all results are conjectures with supporting numerics.
  • Linear valley assumed; curved valleys deferred, and f = g + εh is never fitted to a real network or ε estimated from a training run.
  • The stability constraint ηS ≤ 2 Id is assumed, not derived; counterexamples exist without it (Fig 6) and the remedy — transversal perturbation — is conjectural.
  • Validation is thin: toy valleys plus one small MLP (CIFAR-10 subset, MSE); no quantitative fit, no transformers, no LLM scale, no mini-batch noise — even though the paper’s own Remark 4.3 argues noise-like perturbations are load-bearing.
  • Energy analysis holds only at O(1) energy; the saturation regime lies outside the theory, and why real networks dissipate self-stabilization energy is left open.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can f = g + εh be quantitatively fitted to real transformer runs — estimating ε as inverse valley sharpness and testing the t₂ = ε1/2k and t₃ = εk scalings of loss-spike statistics?
  • Does the constrained-flow + KKT-multiplier structure survive momentum, Adam, and adaptive optimizers — and what replaces the sharpness constraint ηS ≤ 2 Id there?
  • Why do networks systematically dissipate self-stabilization energy with a single eigenvalue at the edge — what loss structure forces ζ into the 9ζ² > 2 regime?
  • In the multi-eigenvalue regime the self-stabilization system has no fixed point — can the resulting quasi-periodic fluctuation amplitude be predicted and correlated with loss-spike magnitudes at scale?
  • How does mini-batch noise — itself the transversal perturbation Remark 4.3 shows is necessary — interact with the t₂ self-stabilization loop?
