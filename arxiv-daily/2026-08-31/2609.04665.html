HackProbe watches self-evolving LLM loops for reward hacking through two black-box hooks — no weights, no activations, no harness changes.

Harness-Agnostic Detection and Immunization of Reward Hacking in Self-Evolving Language Models

Rongxin Yang, Yang Liu, Shang Luo, Haoxuan Jia, et al. · 13 authors · Fullive AI, Peking University, JD.com, NTU, Wuhan University · Aug 31, 2026

  • Reward hacking
  • Self-evolving LLMs

Key metric · detection AUROC

0.763AUROC

Over four hacking channels and 1,152 generations; strongest baseline scores 0.663. False positives cut 0.706 → 0.434, at the cost of recall 1.000 → 0.845.

Why this matters

Reframes reward hacking from offline audit to online monitoring — with a concrete probe-budget formula any self-improvement loop can size.

Critical edge

Not deployable as-is: a 0.434 false-positive rate interrupts four honest generations in ten, and the gameable-grader channel transfers at only 0.600 AUROC.

Methodology

  • Two hooks only. (I1) observe the host’s visible score M each generation; (I2) run the current model on a fixed budget of q = 4 read-only queries per probe. No weights, no activations.
  • Secret comparison core. 240 probes over 6 math subdomains, distribution frozen and never exposed — its capability proxy Ć stays comparable across generations; paired scoring halves variance.
  • Rotated fresh layer. 60 new probes per generation, regenerated under air-gap, non-reuse, OOD-drift and diversity rules to resist co-adaptation; contributes no statistic.
  • Four blind-spot-complementary statistics — level residual, scale-aligned divergence Dₜ = ΔMₜ−λΔĆₜ, capability stagnation, confidently-wrong rate — fused via Šidák correction into a family-wise-calibrated risk score (τ = 0.90).
  • Immunization F0–F3. Reselect from the candidate pool using a structural gaming footprint; F2 caps feedback at 2 bits/generation by quantizing the M−Ć gap into 4 levels.
  • Controlled testbed. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct prompt-evolution host, 4 switchable hacking channels (contamination, gameable grader, self-consistency bias, judge collusion) × 6 seeds = 1,152 labeled generations.

  • Leave-one-channel-out AUROC; no held-out value beats the pooled 0.763 — grader exploits act on output format, which the fusion lacks statistics for.
  • Theory validated: detection rises 0.13 → 0.81 as the core grows 30 → 480 probes, tracking the 1/δ² prediction within 0.035 — but Eq. (4) wants 600 probes for 0.90 detection, so the monitor is under-provisioned by ~360.
  • Against a co-adapting host: a fixed fresh layer inflates to 0.31 by generation 80 (detection 0.588); rotated + diversity holds inflation ≤0.03 with detection 0.749.

Strengths

  • Truly black-box: two minimal hooks, one sample suffices; mounts on any self-evolving loop.
  • Non-circularity by construction: the frozen secret core never touches the host’s selection or reward path; the held-out set is spent exactly once.
  • Calibrated statistics, not ad-hoc thresholds: Šidák fusion, empirical recalibration on honest runs, Bonferroni fallback.
  • The blind-spot design is tested: leave-one-statistic-out matches theory (dropping J3 costs 0.201 AUROC on self-consistency bias, exactly as Lemma 1 predicts).
  • Falsifiable theory actually checked: the detection curve tracks the probe-budget prediction within 0.035; the attack-strength sweep moves 3.6× vs 4× predicted.
  • Exceptional candor: every headline caveat (nominal vs empirical FPR, narrow 1.11 ratio, uncertified cells) is self-reported.

Limitations

  • Not deployable as-is: empirical FPR 0.434 at a nominal 0.10; 15.5% of hacking generations are missed; no sequential across-generation correction.
  • Single host, single domain family: one Qwen2.5-7B prompt-evolution loop over grade-school math; “harness-agnostic” is argued, not demonstrated.
  • Degenerate baseline field: three baselines flag everything; the only real competitor is the authors’ own absolute-Ć ablation.
  • Immunization mostly uncertified: 12 of 16 cells not individually significant; F2’s 1.11 ratio compares unrounded averages and is narrow; F3’s gain is hyperparameter-sensitive.
  • Grader-channel transfer fails (0.600 AUROC, FPR 0.661) — no format-keyed statistic.
  • Theory and cost gaps: no lower bound for Prop. 1; partial-memorization protection undefined; inference overhead of F1–F3 unmeasured; 240 probes vs the 600 Eq. (4) wants.

Questions for further exploration

  • Does HackProbe transfer to weight-level self-evolution (checkpoints, not prompts), where the per-probe query budget balloons?
  • Can a format-keyed statistic cover gameable-grader exploits without re-introducing circularity through the detection signal?
  • Does provisioning the full 600-probe budget — or a sequential correction — bring the 0.434 FPR to deployable levels?
  • What is the covering number N(ε) for a concrete probe generator, and does surface diversity scale it as hoped?
  • Under many-run observation, can a host learn to exploit the 2-bit reselection channel itself — is there a provable “resolution, not volume” claim?
  • What is the wall-clock overhead of F1–F3 relative to the host’s own generation loop?
