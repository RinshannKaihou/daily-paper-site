> **一句话总结：** 将"AI 参与 AI 研发"建模为带时滞反馈的动力系统，导出递归再生数 **R_AI = χa/σ**（递归增益 × 流程闭环率 ÷ 研究前沿硬化速率）：R_AI > 1 时增量改进跨研发周期自我放大，且该临界点与任何能力水平（如 AGI）解耦；在专家调查校准的无 RSI 基线上（AGI 第 24 年、ASI 第 96 年、间隔 72 年），a=15 的"快速转变"情景把 AGI→ASI 间隔压缩到 0.45 年，而全程亚临界的 a=0.5 情景也能把 ASI 时点提前约 22 年，多主体扩展进一步表明个体亚临界的实验室生态整体仍可能超临界。

> **TL;DR:** This theory paper models recursive AI self-improvement (RSI) as a delayed-feedback dynamical system over an AI R&D process and derives a recursive reproduction number **R_AI = χa/σ** (realized recursive gain × operational closure ÷ frontier hardening rate). Against the expert-survey-calibrated no-RSI baseline (AGI at year 24, ASI at year 96, a 72-year interval), the a=15 "rapid transition" scenario moves AGI to year 4.50 and ASI to year 4.95, compressing the AGI→ASI interval from 72.00 to 0.45 years, while a purely subcritical gain (a=0.5) still shifts ASI ~22 years earlier; the multi-actor extension shows an ecosystem can go supercritical (spectral radius ρ(K)=1.55 in the open-ecosystem scenario) even when every lab is individually subcritical, shortening the transition to 2.82 vs 6.13 years for closed labs. The key practical claim: rapid capability growth is neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of self-amplification — diagnosing it requires measuring recursive gain, operational closure, development-cycle duration, and frontier hardening directly.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"递归再生数"判据 R_AI = χa/σ**：把 Good (1966) 以来的智能爆炸直觉改写为时滞微分方程的局部稳定性问题。R_AI > 1 时能力扰动跨研发周期放大，R_AI < 1 时衰减。核心机制：递归增益必须先补偿前沿硬化造成的边际研究生产力损失，才能自我复制。
- **临界性与能力水平解耦**：进入自我放大区取决于 AI 研发反馈环的结构，而非某个能力阈值（如 AGI）。基线研究吞吐量 v 不改变是否临界，只改变显现速度：λ* ≈ vσ(R_AI − 1)/(1 + vσR_AI·τ)，且高吞吐极限下 λ* → ln(R_AI)/τ —— 研发周期时长成为放大速度的硬上限。刚越过临界的系统可能与普通加速无法区分。
- **有限前沿终止命题（Prop 2）**：取 f(x) = (1 − x/X)^β 时硬化率 σ = β/(X−x) 随逼近有效前沿发散，故 R_AI → 0：超临界区必然是暂态的。强放大可以发生但会自我熄灭，除非范式跃迁重新扩大前沿 X。
- **多主体生态临界（Prop 3）**：耦合系统以再生矩阵 K_ij = g_ij/σ_i 的谱半径 ρ(K) 判定。每个主体各自亚临界（所有 K_ii < 1）不足以保证网络稳定——改进在组织间流通重组可使整个生态系统超临界。
- **三组分分离 + 可测量议程**：将研发投入（吞吐量/compute）、递归临界性本身、物理部署约束（算力/电力头顶空间）三者解耦；并列出原则上可实证估计的量：递归增益 a、运营闭环率 χ、研发周期时长 τ、前沿硬化 σ、跨组织迁移 g_ij。代码与计算笔记本开源（github.com/burtsev/recursive-criticality-ai）。

## Key Contributions

- **Recursive reproduction number R_AI = χa/σ** reformulates the intelligence-explosion debate as a local stability transition of a delayed capability equation (characteristic equation λ + vσ = vg·e^(−λτ)); the self-amplifying regime begins when realized recursive gain outpaces the marginal loss of research productivity caused by frontier hardening — a property of the R&D loop's structure, not of any capability level such as AGI.
- **Onset vs speed vs persistence are separated**: baseline throughput v does not change whether the system is supercritical (only how fast amplification becomes visible, λ* ≈ vσ(R_AI − 1)/(1 + vσR_AI·τ), capped at ln(R_AI)/τ by the development-cycle delay); Proposition 2 shows supercriticality is necessarily transient under a finite frontier (σ = β/(X−x) diverges); paradigm shifts can re-open the runway.
- **Collective criticality (Prop 3)**: network-level supercriticality is governed by the spectral radius ρ(K) of the cross-actor reproduction matrix — an ecosystem can self-amplify while every individual actor is subcritical, connecting epidemiology-style R_t reasoning with R&D economics and open-diffusion debates.
- **A measurement agenda**: the framework names the observables that discriminate recursive amplification from ordinary scaling — recursive gain, operational closure, development-cycle duration, frontier hardening, cross-actor transfer — arguing that benchmark-trajectory slope alone cannot diagnose criticality.
- All figures and scenarios are reproducible from the released code and notebook.

## Methodology

- **Capability coordinate**: x(t) = ∫_Q φ(q) ln(c0(q;ε;p)/c(q;ε;p)) dq — a resource-cost-log measure of improvement over a stable task panel Q at fixed prices p, explicitly "a local coordinate for research capability, not a universal scalar measure of intelligence". Normalized to x0 = 0 today, effective frontier X = 1.
- **Core model**: the delayed DDE ẋ(t) = r(t)·f[x(t)]·e^(Φ(x(t−τ), t)), where r(t) is exogenous baseline research productivity, f(x) encodes frontier difficulty, and the potential Φ (with ∂Φ/∂x = g = χ·a, the realized recursive gain) captures the multiplicative effect of last-generation capability on current research productivity after an end-to-end development-cycle delay τ.
- **Four ingredients**: baseline productivity r(t); recursive gain a(x,t); operational closure χ(x,t) ∈ [0,1] — the fraction of potential recursive gain surviving the development pipeline (evaluation, integration, training, deployment; modeled as logistic χ(x) = 1/(1+e^(−kx)) with χ(0) = 0.5); frontier hardening σ(x) = −d ln f/dx.
- **Analysis**: linearization around a slowly varying reference trajectory gives the characteristic equation λ + vσ = vg·e^(−λτ); Proposition 1 (via standard stability theory for positive delayed systems) shows all roots are stable for R_AI < 1, λ = 0 at R_AI = 1, and a positive real root exists for R_AI > 1. Finite-frontier termination and the coupled-actor spectral-radius criterion (K_ij = g_ij/σ_i) follow from the same linearization.
- **Reference parameterization**: β = 2, k = 10, τ = 0.5 yr, four scenarios a ∈ {0.5, 3, 6, 15} (subcritical smooth scaling / weak supercriticality / transient takeoff / rapid transition), thresholds x_AGI = 0.50, x_ASI = 0.80. The baseline rate r_ref = 1/24 ≈ 0.0417 yr⁻¹ is the only data-anchored parameter: it places the no-RSI AGI crossing at 2050, referencing the 2023 Expert Survey on Progress in AI (2,778 authors, aggregate 50% date 2047 for high-level machine intelligence) and the Longitudinal Expert AI Panel (conditional median AGI 2050 among 205 experts; 25%/75% dates 2039/2065).
- **Strategic scenarios (Sec. 4.1)**: a linearized three-actor network ξ̇_i = −v_iσ_iξ_i + v_i Σ_j g_ij ξ_j(t−τ_ij) with an effort multiplier m_r, comparing closed frontier labs (strong K_ii, weak transfer), an open competitive ecosystem (subcritical actors, strong symmetric transfer), and global competition (blocs, asymmetric transfer, m_r = 1.2).
- **Physical limits (Sec. 4.2)**: compute requirement C_req(x,t) = C_req(x_AGI)·e^(κ(x−x_AGI)) with illustrative Q = e^(κ(x_ASI−x_AGI)) = 100 between AGI and ASI and compute growth φ_phys = 0.25 yr⁻¹; deployability requires C_req ≤ C_phys, and physical headroom F_AGI^soft ∈ {8, 2, 0.75} is varied. The paper stresses throughout that the numerical scenarios are conditional demonstrations, not probabilistic forecasts.

## Results & Highlights

- **Table 1 (threshold-crossing times in years from 2026; baseline = the matched no-RSI trajectory with T_AGI = 24.00, T_ASI = 96.00, ΔT_AGI→ASI = 72.00):** the rapid-transition scenario (a = 15) reaches AGI at 4.50 yr (−19.5 yr vs baseline) and ASI at 4.95 yr (−91.05 yr), compressing the AGI→ASI interval from 72.00 yr to 0.45 yr (~160× compression) — both thresholds crossed inside a single amplification episode before frontier hardening dominates.
- **Subcritical feedback still matters:** smooth scaling (a = 0.5, R_AI(t) < 1 throughout) advances AGI only to 21.35 yr (−2.65 vs baseline) but advances ASI to 74.13 yr (−21.87 yr, i.e., ~22 years earlier) — a system that never amplifies local perturbations can still accumulate a large cumulative shift, because feedback compounds over the capability interval.
- **Intermediate regimes:** weak supercriticality (a = 3) gives T_AGI = 12.90 yr with ΔT_AGI→ASI = 11.83 yr; transient takeoff (a = 6) gives T_AGI = 8.40 yr with Δ = 2.69 yr — R_AI rises above 1 and falls back below before AGI, yet leaves a durably advanced capability level.
- **Table 2 (strategic networks):** the open competitive ecosystem has every actor individually subcritical (max K_ii = 0.65) yet ρ(K) = 1.55 via strong cross-actor transfer; with the *lowest* research effort (m_r = 0.8) it produces the shortest AGI→ASI interval, 2.82 yr (T_AGI = 9.75, T_ASI = 12.58) vs 6.13 yr for closed frontier labs (leading lab at K_ii = 1.00, weak 0.02 transfer, m_r = 1.0) and 4.45 yr for global competition (m_r = 1.2, ρ(K) ≈ 1.15) — the latter reaches AGI first (8.97 yr) despite the most effort, but its 1.0-yr feedback delay slows the transition.
- **Delay-limited amplification ceiling:** as vστ → ∞ the dominant root λ* → ln(R_AI)/τ; at high research throughput the successor-development cycle itself becomes the limiting timescale, so no amount of parallel throughput makes amplification arbitrarily fast.
- **Physical headroom scenarios:** with Q = 100 and φ_phys = 0.25 yr⁻¹, headroom F_AGI^soft = 8 leaves infrastructure ahead of the software trajectory; F = 2 binds between AGI and ASI and lengthens the transition; F = 0.75 binds before the unconstrained AGI crossing and delays both thresholds (Fig. 5 shows up to ~20 yr of additional AGI→ASI time on the explored grid). Compute constrains deployable capability without changing R_AI itself.

## Critical Assessment

- **The core threshold is assembly, not discovery.** Proposition 1 is a scalar instance of textbook stability theory for positive delayed systems (the paper itself cites Briat 2018), and the author concedes Cunningham et al. [17] "independently obtain a closely related unit-threshold condition" from economic elasticities. The genuinely new pieces are the finite-frontier termination argument (Prop 2), the spectral-radius ecosystem criterion (Prop 3), and the explicit decoupling of physical headroom from recursive criticality — valuable, but each is a modest analytical step.
- **Almost nothing load-bearing is empirically identified.** The paper admits the frontier-hardening exponent β, recursive gain a, and closure steepness k "are not presently constrained by direct empirical estimates"; k is pinned at 10 across all primary scenarios, and the four regimes are hand-picked to span behaviors. Only r_ref (anchored to expert surveys) and τ (0.5 yr, called "plausible") carry any empirical content. Consequently Table 1's calendar years are structurally underdetermined, and calibrating r_ref to expert surveys lends an unearned air of empiricism to outputs the author himself calls "conditional outputs of the stated parameterization". Anyone quoting "AGI-to-ASI in 0.45 years" as a prediction would be over-reading — though to its credit the paper says exactly this.
- **The capability coordinate embeds arbitrary choices that drive the headline numbers.** x is a fixed-task-panel log cost index normalized to x0 = 0, X = 1, with AGI placed exactly at x = 0.50 and ASI at 0.80 with no empirical content and no reported sensitivity analysis; the dramatic AGI→ASI compression arises precisely because feedback compounds across that assumed interval. Likewise, χ(0) = 0.5 (assumed) together with β = 2 implies R_AI(0) = a/4, so the entire regime map — which scenarios ever go supercritical at all — hinges on the assumed starting closure of exactly one half.
- **Strong structural assumptions go unexamined.** Eq. 3's multiplicative exponential feedback assumes recursive gain does not itself saturate within a paradigm (diminishing returns enter only via σ); the "stable panel of tasks Q" ignores endogenous benchmark churn, which is precisely what real AI R&D does; and χ is modeled as capability-only even though the text concedes it depends on review capacity, experimental throughput, and security constraints. Until a, χ, σ, and cross-actor transfer g_ij are actually measured, R_AI cannot be confronted with data — the framework is currently unfalsifiable in the strict sense, useful as vocabulary rather than as a testable model.
- **No red flags of overclaiming** — the hedging is exemplary — but also no uncertainty quantification: no Monte Carlo over (a, β, k, τ), no confidence bands on the year outputs, and the strategic-network parameters (transfer weights of 0.02/0.50/0.40, m_r ∈ {0.8, 1.0, 1.2}) are illustrative numbers with no stated provenance.

## Strengths

- **Decision-relevant conceptual separation**: rapid progress is shown to be neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of self-amplification; a newly supercritical system can look like ordinary acceleration (λ* passes smoothly through zero). This directly disciplines both "we're in the explosion" and "no explosion, calm down" arguments currently made from benchmark slopes alone.
- **The ecosystem result is a fresh angle** on open-weight/open-source debates: diffusion of improvements across organizations can make the *network* supercritical while every lab is individually subcritical — formalizing an intuition ("openness accelerates everyone") without hand-waving.
- **Converts intelligence-explosion discourse into a measurement agenda**: recursive gain, operational closure, development-cycle duration, frontier hardening, and cross-actor transfer are named, in-principle-estimable quantities — a genuinely useful checklist for empirical work on AI R&D automation.
- **Honest and reproducible**: explicit disclaimers that scenarios are not forecasts, transparent acknowledgment of overlapping prior work, and public code for every figure.

## Limitations

- **No calibration of the mechanism parameters**: β = 2, k = 10, τ = 0.5 yr, the a-grid, and the AGI/ASI thresholds are all hand-chosen; k is never varied in the primary trajectories, and no sensitivity analysis over x_AGI/x_ASI or χ(0) is reported.
- **Local analysis only**: Propositions concern linearized stability; the interesting global excursions (the takeoff episodes of Fig. 3) are studied numerically for one fixed functional form per ingredient, with no robustness sweep over alternative closures/hardening curves.
- **Fixed task panel and exogenous frontier**: new benchmarks, new task distributions, and paradigm-driven jumps X → X + ΔX are discussed verbally but not modeled; the finite-frontier result should be read as within-paradigm only (the author says so, but this sharply limits calendrical interpretation).
- **Organizational realism is thin**: three-actor illustrative networks; strategic behavior (actors endogenously choosing openness, latency, or transfer) is outside the linear model; χ's dependence on operating scale is acknowledged but not formalized.
- **Output presentation risk**: mixing one calibrated parameter (r_ref) with five uncalibrated ones produces precise-looking year numbers (e.g., 4.50 / 4.95 / 0.45) that invite misquotation as forecasts despite the disclaimers.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can recursive gain a and operational closure χ be estimated from existing telemetry — e.g., MLE-bench/RE-Bench score trajectories over model generations, acceptance rates of agent-authored patches, or the fraction of agent-generated code entering successor training runs?
- How do the calendar outputs of Table 1 shift under χ(0) ≠ 0.5, k ≠ 10, or alternative x_AGI/x_ASI placements — is the "0.45-year AGI→ASI interval" robust to any of these, or an artifact of the chosen geometry?
- Can repeated paradigm jumps (X → X + ΔX) sustain quasi-supercritical dynamics indefinitely ("punctuated supercriticality"), and what observable signature would distinguish that from a sequence of independent scale-ups?
- Does the spectral-radius criterion survive strategic behavior, where actors endogenously choose openness and transfer weights g_ij — i.e., is there a game on the reproduction matrix, and does competition push ρ(K) above or below 1?
- How does benchmark/task-panel churn bias measured frontier hardening σ(x), and could MLE-bench-style automation evaluations be repurposed as direct instruments for the feedback term g rather than capability alone?
