ATLAS: Dual-Horizon Diagnostic Evaluation for Industrial Tool-Use Agents

Wei Chen · Peilun Zhou · Zhaoyu Hu · Jiajun Chai, et al.  — USTC · Meituan (Xiaotuan, 100M+ users)

Key metric

+7.32% paid GTV

3-day online A/B on 10% of Meituan Xiaotuan live traffic — with session follow-up +6.9% and P0 hallucination −6.9pp; the judge itself calibrates to 0.952 F1 (vs 0.877 Curated Rubric).

Why this matters

A complete, deployment-proven blueprint for agent-evaluation infrastructure: calibration gating, judge distillation, and diagnosis-as-reward in one semantic loop.

校准式 LLM-judge 门控、judge 蒸馏与"诊断信号即奖励"的闭环 —— 工业级 agent 评测基础设施的完整落地样本。

Critical edge

Self-referential validation: DJudge's decision boundaries were refined to match ATLAS's own signal specs, trajectory ground truth is three-teacher consensus rather than human-verified — and nothing outside Meituan's stack is reproducible.

Methodology

  1. Dual-horizon structure. Within-turn: a 3×5 matrix of execution locations × capability concerns localizes where a tool-call trajectory fails and why; across-turn: six user-wise signals check whether context, corrections, and constraints carry over later lifecycles.
  2. Executable signal specs. Locally assessable objects → local verdicts → metric-specific aggregation; five-part judge prompts with explicit evidence scopes and exemption boundaries.
  3. Calibration gate. Admission at F1 ≥ 0.90 on production-log references — manual annotation (5 annotators, ≥3 labels/sample) for response signals; three-teacher unanimity (Claude Opus-4.6, GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.1-Pro) plus controlled perturbations for trajectory signals; below-threshold judges enter a boundary-refinement loop.
  4. Group-wise distillation. 17 signals grouped by dimension × concern × I/O form distilled onto Qwen3.5-9B (multi-teacher voting, confidence filtering, <think> loss weight 0.1, 8×H20).
  5. Diagnosis-as-reward. The weighted sum of calibrated signals forms the GRPO reward for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (32×H20); the optimized policy is reassessed under the same framework, offline via production tool interfaces, online via A/B.

Strengths

  • The dual-horizon split matches how production failures actually manifest; the across-turn layer (correction carry-over, cross-round contradiction) is rarely formalized in agent evaluation.
  • End-to-end semantic continuity: the same calibrated signal drives diagnosis, distillation supervision, RL reward, and reassessment — cleaner than disjoint eval/reward setups.
  • Rigorous F1 ≥ 0.90 calibration gate with explicit boundary refinement — a discipline most LLM-as-judge papers skip, with F1 honestly chosen over accuracy for class imbalance.
  • Distillation shows calibrated boundary transfer beats raw capacity: a 9B student near its 95.2% teacher, beating 27B dense and 35B-A3B general models.
  • Full offline-to-online evidence chain, with per-signal regressions visible rather than hidden; genuinely deployment-aligned evaluation environment.

Limitations

  • All evidence is internal to Meituan Xiaotuan — datasets, judges, interfaces, and the policy cannot be reproduced or externally audited; no public release stated.
  • No ablations for key design choices: group-wise distillation, λ_s reward weights, 0.1 think-loss weight, 0.90 threshold — all asserted, not measured.
  • RQ3 is unquantified (chart only) and lacks an outcome-only or generic-rubric GRPO baseline to isolate the value of diagnostic feedback.
  • Online window is 3 days on a 10% slice with no significance testing; the 1,000-response audit is small for hallucination-rate deltas.
  • Trajectory ground truth rests on teacher unanimity, coupling judge evaluation to the model family being evaluated; human–teacher disagreement is unquantified.
  • Signal families are Xiaotuan-specific (POI traceability, supply attribution); porting to another domain means re-deriving most of the inventory.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the per-signal F1 ≥ 0.90 gate survive transfer to a different industrial domain, or is the inventory too Xiaotuan-specific to reuse?
  • How would teacher-constructed trajectory references compare against genuine human adjudication on long tool-use trajectories — is three-teacher unanimity biased near subtle boundaries like Reflection Hallucination?
  • Can diagnostic-reward GRPO be separated from the outcome signal, via ablation against outcome-only and generic-rubric rewards with λ_s swept?
  • Do the −6.9pp P0-hallucination and +7.32% GTV gains persist beyond 72 hours, across traffic slices and supply-seasonality effects?
  • Is group-wise distillation actually better than per-signal independent models at equal parameter budget, given POI Traceability regressed below the untuned backbone?
