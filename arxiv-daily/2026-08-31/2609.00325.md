> **一句话总结：** 在 Gemma-2-2B 与 Qwen3-4B 的跨层转码器（CLT）上系统比较三种语言控制潜变量选择方法（激活幅值 ValSel、激活频率 FreqSel、新提出的基于 LLM 标注的 AnnSel）与四种干预策略，发现"干扰语言潜变量置零 + 目标语言放大"（Zero+Amp）最有效，FreqSel 总体最强（Gemma-2-2B 的 Antonyms 任务上 top-1 logit margin 总变化 99.07，比 ValSel 的 79.83 高 19.2），而 AnnSel 用少至 7 个潜变量提供可解释选择但受 Neuronpedia 标注质量制约（韩语仅挖到 7 个潜变量），敲除实验进一步表明三种方法找到的潜变量集是部分冗余的功能子集而非同一规范"语言方向"。

> **TL;DR:** This paper compares three methods for selecting language-controlling latents in cross-layer transcoders (CLTs) of Gemma-2-2B and Qwen3-4B — value-based ValSel (after Deng et al. 2025), frequency-based FreqSel (after Andrylie et al. 2025), and a new annotation-based AnnSel that traces attribution graphs with Circuit Tracer and keeps latents whose Neuronpedia descriptions explicitly mention the target language — and evaluates four inference-time interventions on two new controlled code-switching benchmarks (Antonyms, Enumerations) across 7 languages. Zero-ablating distractor-language latents plus amplifying target-language latents (Zero+Amp) is the best strategy: on Gemma-2-2B Antonyms, FreqSel reaches a total top-1 logit-margin change of 99.07 vs 79.83 for ValSel (+19.2) and 96.72 for AnnSel, while target amplification alone only manages 90.83/69.57/87.00. AnnSel is the interpretable option (keyword-verifiable latents) but costs ~10x more compute (54 vs 5 GPU-h on Gemma-2-2B) and collapses on Korean (7 latents found). Cross-method knock-out experiments show partial redundancy: amplifying FreqSel latents while ablating ValSel directions still yields 92.16 on Gemma, but the reverse pair gives only 49.52 — so the methods do not converge on a single canonical language direction.

## 核心贡献

- **三种选择方法的受控对比：** 首次在跨层转码器（CLT，Ameisen et al. 2025 的 Gemma Scope 转码器 + Qwen3-4B 转码器）上横向比较 ValSel（Deng et al. 2025 的激活幅值/单语性得分）、FreqSel（Andrylie et al. 2025 的激活频率阈值）与新提出的 AnnSel，覆盖 7 种语言（en/fr/de/es/zh/ja/ko）与 7×6=42 个上下文-目标语言对；此前工作只在单层 SAE 上做过，没有系统性对比。
- **提出 AnnSel：** 三阶段流水线 —— 用 Circuit Tracer 构建归因图（node_threshold 0.8、edge_threshold 0.98）→ 贪心路径剪枝（每 token 至多 75 条路径，首边权重 ≥0.5、末边 ≥0.25）→ 按 Neuronpedia 标注是否显式提及语言/国名做关键词筛选（频率 ≥ 0.1×最大值），得到天然可解释的语言潜变量。
- **两个新评测集 + 评测协议：** Antonyms（每语言 100 对反义词，模板句"The opposite of 'X' is"以上下文语言 l0 写出、目标语言 l 作答）与 Enumerations（7 类有序列举的开放式生成）；以对最优竞争语言（同一词的其他语言译法）的 top-1 logit margin 变化 Δm_l = −(m_after − m_before) 为 Antonyms 指标，长度归一化 logprob 变化为 Enumerations 指标。
- **干预策略结论：** 四种策略（目标放大、干扰语言置零、干扰方向消融单层/多层、组合）中 Zero+Amp 全面最优；零化这种"粗"操作反而胜过理论上更强的多层方向消融，作者归因于相关语言的潜变量重叠——多层方向消融在 Qwen3-4B+FreqSel 上总变化达 −165.01，是主动有害的。
- **冗余性发现：** 敲除实验（放大 A 方法的潜变量、同时沿 B 方法的方向消融残差流）显示各方法潜变量集互相交叉：Gemma-2-2B 上 FreqSel 放大×ValSel 消融仍得 92.16，但 ValSel 放大×FreqSel 消融只有 49.52，不对称且未被完全解释——语言表示不是单一方向而是多个部分冗余的功能子集。
- **附录诚意十足：** 超参敏感性全套扫描（ValSel 的 k、FreqSel 的 T/N/M、AnnSel 的 6 个阈值）、固定预算对比、逐语言明细表、计算成本（GPU-h 与 kWh）、FLORES+ 保留集上的自由生成示例，代码与数据在 GitHub 开源。

## Key Contributions

- A controlled comparative study of three latent-selection methods for language control — ValSel (activation-magnitude "monolinguality" score), FreqSel (activation-frequency thresholds T=0.8, N=98%, M=10%), and the new AnnSel — applied to cross-layer transcoders (Gemma-2-2B-pt Gemma Scope CLTs: 26 layers × 16,384 latents; Qwen3-4B: 36 layers × 163,840 latents), where prior work used only single-layer SAEs.
- AnnSel, a new interpretable selection pipeline: Circuit Tracer attribution graphs (max_n_logits=5, desired_logit_prob=0.95) on mid-cut FLORES+ sentences, greedy path pruning (≤75 paths/token, first-edge ≥0.5, last-edge ≥0.25), then keyword filtering of Neuronpedia annotations that explicitly name the language or its country (frequency ≥ 0.1·max).
- Two novel controlled code-switching benchmarks, Antonyms (100 antonym pairs per language, context frame in language l0, answer in language l, 42 ordered (l0, l) pairs) and Enumerations (7 ordered categories), with a top-1 logit-margin-change protocol against translation competitors in the other six languages.
- A systematic comparison of four intervention strategies (target amplification, distractor zero ablation, one-layer/multi-layer distractor direction ablation, and combinations Zero+Amp / 1L+Amp) across two models, with the finding that zero ablation beats the nominally stronger direction ablation due to latent overlap across related languages.
- Knock-out experiments demonstrating that the latent sets of different methods are partially redundant, each-functional subsets rather than a single canonical language direction — with notably asymmetric transfer (FreqSel→AnnSel 90.63 vs AnnSel→FreqSel 5.20 on Gemma-2-2B).
- Thorough appendices: hyperparameter sensitivity sweeps for every method, fixed-budget comparison, computational cost accounting in GPU-hours and kWh, and qualitative held-out FLORES+ free-generation checks; code and data released.

## Methodology

**Setup.** All experiments run on pre-trained cross-layer transcoders (CLTs) — SAE-style dictionaries trained jointly across layers, replacing MLP blocks everywhere — for Gemma-2-2B-pt (Gemma Scope, Lieberum et al. 2024) and Qwen3-4B. Language-specific latent corpora are built from FLORES+ in seven languages: en, fr, de, es, zh, ja, ko.

**Selection methods.**
- *ValSel:* for each latent s, the l-monolinguality score v_s^l = μ_s^l − (1/(|L|−1))·Σ_{i≠l} μ_s^i, where μ_s^l is the mean activation on language-l's corpus; take the top-K=50 latents per language (K chosen to parallel Deng et al.'s 2-layer choice).
- *FreqSel:* activation probability P_s^l = fraction of tokens with positive activation in language l; a latent is l-specific if P_s^l is the max across languages and exceeds T=0.8, active in ≥98% of examples (N) and ≥10% of tokens (M).
- *AnnSel (new):* (1) cut FLORES+ sentences mid-way so the next-token prediction is a valid target-language word, then build attribution graphs with Circuit Tracer; (2) greedily prune to high-influence paths (up to 75 paths per token; keep paths whose first edge ≥0.5 and last edge ≥0.25); (3) keep contributing latents whose Neuronpedia description contains the language name or a highly relevant country name (e.g., "Japanese"/"Japan"), with frequency ≥ 0.1× the max. To justify porting ValSel/FreqSel from single-layer SAEs to CLTs, the authors replicate the validation experiments of both original papers (Appendix C).

**Interventions.** Given a language-specific set {S^l}, at inference time toward target language l from context language l0: (a) target amplification — set each latent to its mean nonzero activation on l's corpus; (b) distractor zero ablation — zero all latents of the other languages ∪_{i≠l}{S^i}; (c) distractor direction ablation — project the residual stream along the mean latent-set direction, at the single best layer (one-layer) or all layers (multi-layer); (d) combined Zero+Amp and 1L+Amp.

**Evaluation.** Antonyms: a sentence frame in context language l0 ("The opposite of 'adj1' is ...") requires the antonym in target language l; success metric is the change in top-1 logit margin m_l = max_{i∈L\{l}}(a_l − a_i) against translation competitors, Δm_l = −(m_l^after − m_l^before), summed over all 42 ordered pairs. Enumerations: open-ended list completion scored by length-normalized logprob change of the target sequence. Additional analyses: cross-method knock-out (amplify method A's latents while ablating B's directions), per-language breakdowns, hyperparameter sweeps, fixed-budget comparison (all methods capped at the smallest set size), computational cost, and qualitative top-p=0.5 continuations of held-out FLORES+ sentences.

## Results & Highlights

- **Zero+Amp is the best intervention on Gemma-2-2B (Antonyms, total Δ in top-1 logit margin):** FreqSel 99.07 vs AnnSel 96.72 vs ValSel 79.83 — FreqSel beats the weakest method by +19.2. Target amplification alone is much weaker for every method (FreqSel 90.83, AnnSel 87.00, ValSel 69.57), i.e., adding distractor zero-ablation buys +8.2 to +10.3 points.
- **Zero ablation beats "stronger" direction ablation, sometimes catastrophically so in reverse:** on Gemma-2-2B, ValSel distractor zero-ablation alone yields −8.02 while multi-layer direction ablation yields only +13.98; on Qwen3-4B with FreqSel latents, multi-layer direction ablation is actively harmful (−165.01) whereas zero ablation gives +16.55 — consistent with the authors' latent-overlap-of-related-languages explanation.
- **Selection-method ranking under Zero+Amp:** on Gemma-2-2B Antonyms, FreqSel averages 14.15 per language vs ValSel 10.16 and AnnSel 9.08, with FreqSel especially strong on Chinese (34.03) and Korean; on Qwen3-4B the same ranking holds (FreqSel 9.34 vs ValSel 6.23 vs AnnSel 6.17 avg Δ; FreqSel peaks at zh 29.87 and ko 20.86) and on Enumerations (avg normalized-logprob change: FreqSel 1.05 vs ValSel 0.45, AnnSel 0.36).
- **Knock-out asymmetry reveals partial redundancy (Antonyms, aggregate over 7 languages):** amplifying FreqSel latents while ablating ValSel directions still gives 92.16 on Gemma-2-2B (FreqSel→AnnSel 90.63), but ValSel→FreqSel only 49.52 and AnnSel→FreqSel just 5.20; the pattern repeats on Qwen3-4B (FreqSel→AnnSel 155.29 vs AnnSel→FreqSel 1.28) — FreqSel's set largely subsumes the others' effect, the converse does not hold.
- **Fixed-budget twist for AnnSel (Appendix F1, Gemma-2-2B + Antonyms):** when all methods are capped at the same latent budget, AnnSel averages 13.73 vs ValSel 11.50 and FreqSel 10.57 — AnnSel wins despite using as few as 7 latents for Korean (FreqSel uses 938 for ko; ValSel uses 50 by design), though this reversal appears in only one model/task setting.
- **AnnSel's cost is ~10x:** processing 100 sentences × 7 languages takes 54 GPU-h (4.4 kWh) for AnnSel vs 5 GPU-h (0.8 kWh) each for ValSel and FreqSel on Gemma-2-2B; on Qwen3-4B (163,840 latents/layer, 36 layers) it grows to 107 GPU-h (11.8 kWh) vs 19-23 GPU-h.
- **Failure modes the headline hides:** ValSel on Qwen3-4B backfires on English (Zero+Amp Δ = −6.17, 1L+Amp −14.71), AnnSel finds zero effective English amplification signal on Qwen3-4B (target amplification Δ = 0), and AnnSel's Korean collapse (4.81 avg Δ vs FreqSel's ~35.9 on Gemma) tracks its annotation yield (7 latents) rather than language structure.

## Critical Assessment

- **No external steering baselines — the comparison is circular in scope.** The paper compares three *CLT-latent selection methods against each other*, but never runs the cheaper prior approaches it cites: PCA language directions (Goncharov et al. 2025, which explicitly targeted unintended code-switching) or language neurons (Tang et al. 2024). A difference-in-means or PCA direction costs ~nothing compared to AnnSel's 54 GPU-h, so without that baseline the headline "FreqSel wins" says nothing about whether CLT latents are even the right tool for language steering.
- **AnnSel's fragility is a keyword-matching artifact, not just annotation sparsity.** The authors themselves report that the criterion (does the Neuronpedia description contain "Japanese"/"Japan"-style strings?) selects a latent described as "Non-English characters" as an *English* latent, and that Korean yields only 7 latents vs 45+ for other languages — which fully explains AnnSel's Korean collapse (avg Δ 4.81 vs FreqSel's ~35.9). Method performance here tracks annotation coverage, not language structure, yet the abstract still markets AnnSel as offering "interpretable language annotations" without this caveat.
- **Synthetic, highly templated evaluation.** Antonyms is a single sentence frame repeated over 100 pairs per language; Enumerations is list completion. The only free-text evidence is Table 29 — a handful of qualitative FLORES+ continuations with no automatic metric (e.g., language-ID accuracy or perplexity under the target language), so real-world code-switching behavior is asserted, not measured.
- **Aggregate headline numbers are hard to interpret and hide backfiring cases.** "Total change in top-1 logit margin" summed over 42 language pairs (99.07 etc.) is a unitless quantity; the per-language tables reveal that ValSel on Qwen3-4B actively hurts English (−6.17 under Zero+Amp) and multi-layer direction ablation reaches −165.01 — failures the main text mentions only in passing.
- **The knock-out asymmetry is the most interesting finding but is left unexplained.** FreqSel→AnnSel 90.63 vs AnnSel→FreqSel 5.20 (Gemma) is reported with "requires further investigation"; no overlap statistics, no ablation of set size as a confound (FreqSel has up to 938 latents vs AnnSel's 7-59, and knock-out ablates *directions* of unequal-size sets). Appendix M's cosine-similarity observations are suggestive, not conclusive.
- **Generalization is thin by the authors' own admission:** two small models (2B/4B base, not instruction-tuned), 7 mostly high-resource languages, and results tied to CLT availability — the paper explicitly warns findings may not transfer to standard single-layer SAEs or larger models.
- **On rigor otherwise: good.** Hyperparameter sensitivity sweeps for every method (effects are small, e.g., FreqSel Δ stays 14.25-15.38 across T∈[0.6,1.0]), fixed-budget ablation, cost accounting, justified adaptation of prior methods, honest limitations, and public code/data — this is above-average empirical hygiene for an interpretability preprint.

## Strengths

- First systematic head-to-head of language-latent selection methods on CLTs, with the methods' original validation experiments replicated to justify the port from single-layer SAEs (Appendix C).
- Clean controlled evaluation design: context language vs target language is fully crossed (42 ordered pairs), and the logit-margin metric compares against the *same word's translations* in the other six languages — a sensible, adversarial competitor set.
- The Zero+Amp > 1L+Amp and zero-ablation > direction-ablation findings are practically useful and mechanistically motivated (latent overlap of related languages).
- Knock-out experiment is a clever design for probing set redundancy that goes beyond "does steering work".
- Exceptional appendix transparency: full hyperparameter sweeps, per-language tables, computational cost in GPU-h and kWh, held-out generation examples, and released code + benchmarks.

## Limitations

- Only two small base models (Gemma-2-2B-pt, Qwen3-4B); no instruction-tuned or larger models, and conclusions are explicitly tied to CLT availability rather than standard SAEs.
- Seven predominantly high-resource languages; no low-resource language stress test beyond Korean's annotation-starvation accident.
- Both benchmarks are synthetic templates (antonym frames, list completion); held-out free generation is qualitative only (no language-ID or fluency metric).
- AnnSel depends on Neuronpedia annotation quality and naive keyword matching (mislabels "Non-English characters" as English; Korean yields 7 latents); it costs ~10x more compute than the statistical methods.
- ValSel's Qwen3-4B failure on English (−6.17) and the negative multi-layer direction ablation (−165.01 on Qwen3-4B) are observed but not analyzed in depth.
- No comparison against cheap non-CLT steering baselines (PCA directions, language neurons), and the interesting knock-out asymmetry is left as future work.

## Relevance

本文正中“LLM 隐状态与可解释性”兴趣点：它在 SAE/跨层转码器上做语言潜变量的选择、因果干预与冗余性分析，其 Zero+Amp 干预设计、敲除实验方法和“多方法潜变量集部分冗余、不存在单一语言方向”的结论，对电路/稀疏字典方向的研究与多语言可控生成都有直接参考价值；与用户其余兴趣方向（agents、训练稳定性等）无重叠。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the FreqSel > AnnSel > ValSel ranking hold on standard single-layer SAEs and on larger instruction-tuned models, or is it an artifact of these two specific transcoder releases?
- Does a near-free baseline — PCA/difference-in-means language directions as in Goncharov et al. (2025) — match CLT-latent steering on the same Antonyms/Enumerations protocol? Without this, is the 54 GPU-h AnnSel pipeline ever worth it?
- What exactly drives the knock-out asymmetry (FreqSel→AnnSel 90.63 vs AnnSel→FreqSel 5.20)? Is it set size, direction-norm differences, or genuine subsumption of AnnSel's latents by FreqSel's?
- Can AnnSel be made robust by replacing keyword matching with embedding similarity or LLM-judged annotation relevance — and would that fix the Korean (7 latents) and "Non-English characters" failures?
- Why does ValSel backfire on Qwen3-4B English (−6.17 under Zero+Amp) — transcoder quality, or the monolinguality score picking cross-lingual latents?
- Do these interventions preserve task ability (perplexity, QA accuracy) on non-templated text, and do they behave differently in genuine code-switching contexts rather than the paper's controlled frames?
