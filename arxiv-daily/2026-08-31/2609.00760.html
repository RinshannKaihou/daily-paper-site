A Unified Mechanistic Analysis of Knowledge- and Safety-Based Refusals

Key metric
0.86vs0.58

Asymmetric probe transfer: safety-trained probes classify knowledge refusals at 0.80–0.86, but knowledge-trained probes reach only 0.58–0.63 on safety — the 0.22–0.27 gap holds in all six models.

Shared substrate: 0.794 peak cosine alignment between KR and SR shift directions; 0.92–0.95 of each shift projects onto the common direction.

Why this matters

Mechanism becomes recipe: safety-first-then-knowledge tuning preserves utility and both refusal types — and the matched-control toolkit transfers to any LLM behavior.

Critical edge

The benchmark survived a filter requiring all six models to refuse/answer identically — conditioning on the easiest decision band, likely inflating the headline probe accuracies.

Methodology

  1. Matched quadruple benchmark

    213 (KR, SR, KC, SC) quadruples across 20 topic categories: identical topic, template, and anchor words — only the refusal trigger varies (fictional referent vs sensitive target vs benign controls). Pipeline 600→475→384→269→213; 75 quadruples human-checked (κ = 0.82).

  2. Models & controlled tuning

    Llama-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B, each refusal-tuned with CRaFT (knowledge) and FalseReject (safety) via sequential orders Seq-KS / Seq-SK or data merging (full-parameter SFT).

  3. Representational analysis

    Per-layer mean refusal direction d = E[h(xᴺ) − h(xᴸ)] with matched controls; cosine alignment vs 1000-permutation null; bisector decomposition into a shared projection plus a type-specific residual; ℓ2-regularized logistic probes with 5-fold CV and bootstrap CIs.

  4. Localization & causality

    Logit-lens decoding of directions, attention-head scoring/ablation, late-layer MLP zero-ablation, and activation steering +αd at the peak-alignment layer with random-vector controls.

Hidden states at the final prompt-token position (float32, seed 42) · refusal scored by a conservative lexical classifier · greedy decoding (256 tokens) · behavioral validation on MMLU, GSM8K, TriviaQA, NQ, AdvBench, XSTest.

Results

  • The shared direction is not a template artifact: matched controls are more similar to each other than the refusal pairs at every layer (Llama: sim(KC,SC) 0.938 vs sim(KR,SR) 0.898); alignment peaks at relative depth 0.25–0.50.
  • The common component dominates: 0.92–0.95 of each normalized shift lies on the bisector (residual norm 0.32–0.39); the common probe beats its null by ~0.45 AUC, the type-specific probe by only ~0.11.
  • Late, interpretable specialization: type-specific projections stay near zero until final layers; logit lens shows epistemic framing (“Based”, “presently”) vs normative framing (“regulation”, “privacy”); late MLPs (depth > 0.9) disproportionately reduce the KR-specific projection.
  • Tuning order matters: Seq-KS preserves utility (Llama* MMLU 61.84 vs original 62.90; Qwen* 70.47 vs 69.00) while Seq-SK balloons benign over-refusal (XSTest Safe Ref. 74.80 Llama, 84.20 Gemma).
  • Steering confirms behavior: +αdᴺ raises refusal (strongest on Llama*, weak on Gemma*); type-specific steering flips the rationale framing on the same prompt.

Strengths

  • The right instrument: matched quadruples isolate refusal grounds from the topic, format, and template confounds that blocked direct KR-vs-SR comparison.
  • Convergent evidence: direction geometry, probe transfer, layer-wise projections, logit lens, head/MLP ablations, and steering all point to the same commit-then-specify structure.
  • Unusually careful falsification: negative Δsim control, 1000-permutation nulls, bootstrap CIs, random-head/vector baselines, robustness across 8 models, 2 tuning orders, and an independently built set.
  • Practical payoff: the Seq-KS vs Seq-SK comparison turns the mechanism into a quantified training-order recipe.

Limitations

  • Selection effect: behavioral verification kept only prompts all six models agree on — no out-of-band or decision-boundary evaluation of the geometric story.
  • Synthetic, LLM-generated data: GPT-4o-mini generated, GPT-5 judged; humans reviewed only a 75-quadruple sample; subtle LLM phrasing tells remain possible.
  • Diagnostic, not ecological: deliberately maximally-aligned quadruples underrepresent ambiguous, borderline, and off-domain refusals.
  • Uneven causal grounding: steering is weak on Gemma*; ablations show sensitivity, not a full circuit; the residual is explicitly not claimed as an independent mechanism.
  • Scope: 7–9B open-weight models only, single-turn prompts; and no release statement for the dataset or checkpoints.

Questions for further exploration

  • Does the SR→KR > KR→SR asymmetry survive at 70B+ or in closed-weight RLHF models, or is it an artifact of 7–9B SFT-style tuning?
  • Is the type-specific residual causally load-bearing — does ablating it preserve refusal rate while erasing the epistemic/normative rationale?
  • How does the bisector-derived common direction relate to Arditi et al.’s single SR refusal direction — and does the shared component extend to other decline-to-answer behaviors?
  • Does the clean geometry hold near the decision boundary, on prompts that failed the all-model behavioral-verification filter?
  • Which stage of pretraining or alignment installs SR as the broader signal? A checkpoint-level training-dynamics study could localize its origin.
  • Can targeted calibration of the KR-specific late-layer component reduce knowledge-related over-refusal without perturbing the shared refusal commitment?
