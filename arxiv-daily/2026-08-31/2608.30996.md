> **一句话总结：** 对 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 的离线 RAG KV cache 做受控精度审计：INT8 近乎无损，但 INT4 在答案 EM 完全不变的"精度保持"子集上，LLM-judge 忠实性翻转仍以恶化为主（RGB 上 291 恶化 vs 24 改善，McNemar p<10⁻³⁸），且 INT4−BF16 幻觉率差距随检索深度 K 从 0.09（K=1）单调放大到 0.26（K=5）——EM/F1 无法发现这种"隐性失真"，压缩缓存前必须单独审计忠实性。

> **TL;DR:** This is the first audit of offline KV-cache quantization in RAG that measures faithfulness (grounding in retrieved evidence) rather than just accuracy. On RGB and HotpotQA (300 examples each, K∈{1,3,5}, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), INT4 quantization versus the BF16 cache baseline flips faithfulness on accuracy-preserved examples 291 worse vs 24 better (RGB, McNemar p<10⁻³⁸; 173 vs 31 on HotpotQA, p<10⁻¹⁰), widens the HHEM hallucination-rate gap from 0.09 to 0.26 as K goes 1→5 on RGB, and is the only condition producing degenerate outputs (6% of generations on RGB at K=5), while INT8 is near-lossless at ~1.9× compression (vs nominal 2×). Practical takeaway: EM/F1 cannot certify a compressed RAG cache; faithfulness must be audited before deploying low-bit offline caches.

## 核心贡献

- **首个离线 KV-cache 量化忠实性审计**：此前离线缓存系统（TurboRAG、CacheBlend）只优化延迟/存储，量化审计（KIVI、KVQuant、KVTuner 等）只看 accuracy/perplexity，没有工作检查"压缩缓存后回答是否仍被检索证据支撑"。本文填补该空白。
- **避免位置拼接混淆的受控协议**：整段检索上下文在单次 prefill 中构建统一 cache、量化存储、解码前反量化回 bfloat16；用一个 Stage-0 门控（BF16 缓存往返在 50 个留存样本上与 Oracle 完全逐位一致）证明任何 C2/C3 差异都纯粹来自量化。
- **"隐性伤害"（H1）发现**：INT4 在 containment-EM 完全不变的样本上，忠实性三类信号仍大幅恶化——正确答案不再依赖被检索的证据，构成压缩 RAG 的隐藏失效模式。
- **噪声放大效应（H2/H3）**：INT4 的忠实性退化随检索深度 K 和干扰段落（distractor）比例单调加剧；INT8 则全面安全。
- **方法论警示**：HotpotQA 低 K 下 INT4 把拒绝率从 51% 压到 32%，反而"虚增" EM 和 NLI 指标——单一 accuracy 指标不仅看不见伤害，甚至可能反向美化。

## Key Contributions

- **First faithfulness audit of offline KV-cache quantization in RAG.** Prior offline-caching systems (TurboRAG, CacheBlend) and quantization studies (KIVI, KVQuant, KVTuner) evaluate only speed/storage or accuracy/perplexity; none checks whether compressed caches keep answers grounded in the retrieved evidence.
- **A controlled, confound-free audit protocol.** The full retrieved context is prefilled once into a single unified KV cache (avoiding the positional-stitching artifacts of per-chunk caching), quantized for storage, dequantized to bfloat16 before decoding, and compared across C0 (Oracle, no cache round trip), C1 (BF16 cache baseline), C2 (INT8), C3 (INT4). A Stage-0 gate verifies the BF16 cache round-trip exactly reproduces the Oracle on all 50 hold-out examples, so C1–C3 differences are attributable purely to quantization.
- **The "hidden harm" finding (H1).** On the accuracy-preserved subset (containment-EM unchanged between C1 and C3), INT4 faithfulness flips remain overwhelmingly negative — the model still emits the correct string but silently stops using the retrieved evidence, a failure mode invisible to EM/F1.
- **Noise amplification (H2/H3).** The INT4 faithfulness gap grows monotonically with retrieval depth K and with distractor fraction, while INT8 remains safe across the board.
- **A metric-calibration warning.** On HotpotQA at low K, INT4 noise suppresses the model's refusal instinct (refusal rate drops from 0.518 to 0.317 at K=1), which artificially *inflates* containment-EM and NLI entailment — accuracy not only misses the harm but can mask it.

## Methodology

**Isolation design.** The goal is to vary only the stored cache precision while keeping retrieved evidence, prompt, and decoding fixed. For each query, the system prompt plus the top-K retrieved chunks are concatenated and run in one causal prefill, producing a single unified cache for the full context. The authors explicitly avoid caching chunks independently and stitching them later, because independently cached chunks are created at local token positions but consumed at global positions — a positional mismatch that would itself degrade outputs and confound precision comparisons. A single prefill pass removes this confound and makes cache bit-width the only variable.

**Conditions.** C0 (Oracle) generates from full context with no cache round trip; C1 (BF16) stores and reloads the uncompressed cache; C2 (INT8, per-token asymmetric) and C3 (INT4, group-wise with groups of 64 channels) store quantized caches and dequantize before decoding. C1−C2 isolates INT8; C1−C3 isolates INT4. Because per-group scale/zero-point metadata is stored, realized compression ratios fall below the nominal 2×/4×: measured as ~1.9× (INT8) and ~3.6× (INT4) at K=5, with metadata consuming ~5% and ~11% of the respective cache footprints (Equation 1 models footprint as M(b,g,o) = 2LHd_h·N·b/g + (2LHd_h·N/g)·o).

**Data and scale.** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (bf16 native, 32K context). Two complementary benchmarks: RGB (300 examples, own positive/distractor passages, controlled noise) and HotpotQA (300 examples, multi-hop distractor split). K ∈ {1,3,5} retrieved chunks, giving 300×3×4 = 3,600 generations per dataset. Retrieval uses FAISS over BAAI bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings (top-10 retrieved, sliced to first K).

**Metrics.** Accuracy: containment exact-match (EM) plus token-level F1. Faithfulness: three independent signals — HHEM-2.1-Open hallucination rate (threshold 0.5, lower better), DeBERTa-v3-large NLI entailment (higher better), and an LLM-as-judge (Claude Haiku 4.5) that achieves 92% agreement with 50 hand-checked labels on RGB (76% on HotpotQA, where refusal artifacts add noise). HHEM and NLI correlate only moderately (Pearson r=0.44 on RGB), justifying the multi-signal design. Hypotheses are tested with paired McNemar tests on label flips; H3 regresses the per-example INT4 faithfulness gap on distractor fraction within each K.

## Results & Highlights

- **Hidden faithfulness harm under preserved accuracy (H1).** Restricting to examples whose containment-EM outcome is unchanged between the BF16 baseline (C1) and INT4 (C3), the LLM judge records 291 worsenings vs 24 improvements on RGB (McNemar p<10⁻³⁸) and 173 vs 31 on HotpotQA (p<10⁻¹⁰) — over 90% of faithfulness changes are negative even though the answer string is still correct. HHEM corroborates the trend on RGB at all K and on HotpotQA at K=5.
- **Accuracy-level damage from INT4.** Correct→wrong flips vastly outnumber wrong→correct: 133 vs 10 on RGB and 117 vs 24 on HotpotQA. INT8 shows no such degradation (marked safe across every row of Table 1).
- **Retrieval-noise amplification (H2).** The INT4−BF16 hallucination-rate gap grows monotonically with retrieval depth: from 0.09 at K=1 to 0.26 at K=5 on RGB, and from 0.05 to 0.25 on HotpotQA. The slope test over only K∈{1,3,5} is underpowered (p=0.12) and reported as directional.
- **Distractor amplification (H3, RGB only).** Regressing the per-example INT4 faithfulness gap on distractor fraction within each K yields β=0.22, significant at K=3 and K=5 — noisier retrieval makes INT4 relatively more likely to hallucinate.
- **Degenerate generation is INT4-only.** Empty outputs or repetition loops reach 6% of generations on RGB and 3% on HotpotQA at K=5; BF16 and INT8 never degenerate. Numerical monitoring shows non-finite logits in <0.1% of INT4 runs (aborted before emission), confirming semantic rather than numerical explosion.
- **Refusal confound on HotpotQA.** INT4 drops the refusal rate from 0.518 (51%) to 0.317 (32%) at K=1 — the model guesses instead of refusing, which inflates containment-EM and NLI entailment; hence the LLM judge is the only valid primary signal in that regime.
- **Storage reality check.** At K=5, INT8 and INT4 reduce cache size by ~1.9× and ~3.6× (below the nominal 2× and 4×) due to per-block scale/zero-point overhead.

The methodology quality exceeds typical audit-paper standards — a round-trip fidelity gate, paired significance tests, a multi-signal faithfulness design, and an explicit power analysis for the underpowered H2 — suggesting careful execution, though the group has no visible prior track record in KV-cache compression specifically.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is genuine but narrow.** The claim of being the first offline KV-cache quantization audit in RAG holds up against the related work cited (TurboRAG and CacheBlend never compress stored caches; KVTuner establishes "4-bit as the safe floor for Qwen2.5-7B" purely on reasoning accuracy — this paper directly challenges that guidance by showing 4-bit breaks grounding). But the study is a diagnosis, not a solution: there are **no mitigation experiments at all** — no test of whether standard remedies the authors themselves cite (KIVI/KVQuant per-channel key quantization, high-precision attention-sink retention, error compensation, mixed-precision allocation) recover faithfulness, and no test of intermediate precisions, so there is no dose-response curve between bit-width and grounding beyond the two operating points (8-bit safe, 4-bit broken).

**Scope is thin for the strength of the conclusion.** Everything rests on one model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), two 300-example QA benchmarks, and three retrieval depths. The H2 "amplification" headline rests on a slope test the authors themselves admit is underpowered (p=0.12, only three K values); the abstract and intro state it more confidently than the appendix warrants, which is a mild overclaim despite honest labeling in Table 1. On HotpotQA at low K, the authors' own refusal confound invalidates two of their three faithfulness signals (HHEM, NLI), leaving a single LLM judge with only 76% human agreement as the sole support for H1 there — the strongest statistical evidence (p<10⁻¹⁰) comes from a judge whose label quality is weakest in exactly that regime.

**Missing baselines.** The paper quantizes the whole cache uniformly at INT8/INT4 but never runs the published "near-lossless" quantizers it reviews (KIVI, KVQuant, ZipCache, KVTuner) under the same offline-RAG protocol. So the practical question a deployer actually has — *does a smart mixed-precision quantizer also break faithfulness, or only naive uniform INT4?* — is left open, and the "faithfulness must be audited" imperative is demonstrated only for the least sophisticated compression setting. No code release is mentioned, which limits reproducibility of the 3,600-generation pipeline (though the config detail — g=64 groups, threshold 0.5, judge model, datasets, K values — is commendably complete).

**Genuine strengths worth crediting.** The Stage-0 round-trip gate (BF16 cache == Oracle on all 50 hold-outs), the single-prefill unified-cache design that removes the positional-stitching confound, and the self-discovered refusal artifact (a result that weakens the authors' own secondary metrics and is reported prominently rather than buried) are exactly the kind of rigor this subfield needs.

## Strengths

- Crisp, well-isolated causal question with a protocol (single prefill, unified cache, round-trip fidelity gate) that makes cache precision the only variable — a model of experimental control for compression audits.
- The hidden-harm finding (faithfulness flips 291:24 negative on accuracy-preserved examples, McNemar p<10⁻³⁸) is novel, statistically supported, and practically consequential for anyone deploying TurboRAG/CacheBlend-style offline caches.
- Three independent faithfulness signals with reported inter-signal agreement (r=0.44, 92%/76% judge-vs-human), rather than reliance on a single judge.
- Honest self-auditing: an explicit power analysis for the underpowered H2, numerical-stability monitoring separating semantic from numerical failure, and prominent reporting of the HotpotQA refusal confound that undermines its own NLI/HHEM results.
- Actionable engineering takeaway with a corrected storage accounting (~1.9×/~3.6× actual compression, not the nominal 2×/4×).

## Limitations

- Single model family and size (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct); the INT4 faithfulness cliff may shift substantially for larger models, other architectures, or different KV-cache statistics (acknowledged by the authors).
- Only two 300-example QA benchmarks and K∈{1,3,5}; the retrieval-depth amplification test is underpowered (p=0.12) and H3 is evaluated only on RGB where per-example noise ratios are available.
- No mitigation experiments and no comparison against the published per-channel/mixed-precision quantizers (KIVI, KVQuant, ZipCache, KVTuner) under the same protocol — the audit cannot say whether sophisticated quantization also breaks grounding, only that naive uniform INT4 does.
- On HotpotQA at low K, the refusal confound reduces H1's support to a single LLM judge with 76% human agreement.
- No code or data release mentioned; no 2-bit or 3-bit operating points, so the boundary of the safe-precision floor for faithfulness is unknown.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中"极端性能优化下的可靠性"关注点：它证明纯存储导向的 KV cache 低比特量化会引入 EM/F1 完全看不见的静默忠实性退化，本质上是推理侧"低精度计算引入隐性正确性损失"的一次系统审计，与量化误差、静默数据损坏检测的兴趣高度契合；唯一需注意的是其失效模式是模型输出层面的 grounding 而非硬件层 SDC。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do error-compensated or mixed-precision quantizers (KIVI/KVQuant-style per-channel keys, high-precision attention sinks, KVTuner-style query-aware allocation) preserve faithfulness at 4-bit, or is the grounding loss intrinsic to 4-bit KV storage?
- How does the faithfulness cliff scale with model size and family — do 70B models with lower-entropy KV caches tolerate INT4 grounding-wise even if 7B does not?
- Can the degenerate generations (empty output/repetition loops, 6% at K=5) be localized to specific layers or channels via per-layer quantization-error attribution, connecting faithfulness loss to internal representation geometry?
- Would faithfulness-constrained quantization (optimize scale/zero-points to minimize grounding drift on held-out evidence rather than weight reconstruction) recover INT4 at the same ~3.6× compression?
- Does the same hidden harm appear in multi-turn agentic RAG, where a compressed document cache is reused across many queries and errors compound?
