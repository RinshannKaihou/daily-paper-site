UE5M3 FP4 Block Scaling for Stable Language Model Pretraining: A Simpler FP4 Pretraining Recipe with Delayed Tensor Scaling ↗

Key Metric

+21.2%

Model-body throughput on one GB200 (3,212 → 3,894 tokens/s) from dropping RHT and the BF16 exemption — while final-window training loss lands at 2.3090 vs TE NVFP4’s 2.3474 on an 8B model over 188.7B tokens.

Why This Matters

Low-precision training stability is becoming everyone’s problem. This gives a free-range scale format, a reusable headroom rule, and rare numerical forensics on undocumented hardware.

Critical Edge

Every result rests on one seed per configuration, and the paper’s own best FP4 run is not the proposed recipe — UE5M3 with TE settings wins every aggregate.

Methodology

  • Format: all systems share the E2M1 4-bit payload (max magnitude 6); they differ only in block scales. NVFP4: 16-wide blocks with signed E4M3 scales (max 448). UE5M3: the sign bit becomes a fifth exponent bit — unsigned E5M3, max 61,440, smallest subnormal 2⁻¹⁷, same 3 fraction bits, every code a usable nonnegative scale.
  • Delayed tensor scaling: cache each operand’s amax and refresh every D=50 optimizer steps (sample-and-hold) instead of a per-quantization full-tensor reduction. Proposition 1: block scales avoid saturation iff the stale ratio ≤ U/T — T=448 default gives ~137× growth headroom; a targeted T=2048 override for the Wgrad-dY operands of the four final down-projections keeps 30× headroom while pushing small-gradient codes ~4.6× farther from zero.
  • Rounding: ties-to-even everywhere except unbiased stochastic rounding on the upstream-gradient dY payload — the same operand TE rounds stochastically.
  • Probe-matched GEMM emulator: UE5M3 has no native hardware on the evaluated stack, so training runs in software. Witness-driven reverse engineering identified Blackwell/TE numerics — 64-product groups, FP32 RNE partial sums, round-toward-zero cross-group adds — validated bit-exactly: equal logits/loss on a 1.29B model, zero relative L2 error across all 1,291,929,600 gradients, byte-exact parameters and AdamW state (2,583,859,363 tensor values) over 100 updates.
  • Setup: Nemotron-H 8B (4 attention + 24 FFN + 24 Mamba-2 blocks), 30,000 steps = 188.7B tokens on an OLMo-family mixture, matched field-by-field to NVIDIA’s 8B NVFP4 recipe; 32 GB200 ranks; seven single-seed trajectories compared.

  • Stability is the clearest isolation: after step 2,500 the proposed trajectory logs zero losses above 3 and zero pre-clip gradient norms above 1; the E4M3 no-RHT/all-linears ablation logs 205 and 89 respectively and finishes far worse — UE5M3 scales, not recipe simplification, buy stability.
  • Held-out NLL: proposed path lower than TE NVFP4 at 8 of 12 checkpoints, including all four final ones; UE5M3-with-TE-settings is lower at all 12.
  • Throughput: native-hardware ablation removes RHT and quantizes the 16 final-block BF16 projections → 100-step wall time 31.877s → 26.298s, +21.2% tokens/s on one GB200.
  • Emulator fidelity: 258/258 permutation witnesses (nearest alternative: 149/258), 7/7 BF16-boundary witnesses from real 8192×2048×6144 GEMMs, byte-exact 100-update control.
  • Scale-target control (350M): switching only Wgrad-dY to T=2048 in the final four layers: final-window loss 2.90904 → 2.90248 (−0.00656).

Strengths

  • Genuinely useful format insight: unsigned scales are free range (137× larger max, 256× smaller subnormal), and Proposition 1’s U/T headroom analysis is a clean, reusable design rule for delayed scaling.
  • The probe-matched emulator is a standout — witness-driven identification of undocumented reduction order and rounding, with falsification tables (0/7, 6/512, 149/258, 258/258) and a bit-exact end-to-end control. Numerical forensics rarely seen.
  • Honest, precise reporting: the best FP4 configuration is not the proposed one and is reported as such; single-seed, confounded-comparison, and emulation caveats are stated in the text, not buried.
  • Strong internal validity: the TE NVFP4 baseline is re-run under identical architecture, optimizer, schedule, seed, and data — a fair comparison even if not comparable to NVIDIA’s published 1T-token numbers.
  • Practical payoff quantified on native hardware, not extrapolated from theory.

Limitations

  • One seed per configuration; no variance estimates for any loss or accuracy delta.
  • 188.7B tokens is ~19% of NVIDIA’s 1T-token regime, on a substituted data blend — and prior work reports BF16 final layers remain necessary for 8B NVFP4 up to 1T, exactly where the no-exemption claim is untested.
  • Primary experiments are software-emulated; the +21.2% throughput comes from a native E4M3 ablation, not an actual UE5M3 implementation.
  • The T=2048 override is hand-placed on four named layers of one architecture, justified by a single 350M two-run control; no selection rule or sweep.
  • Operational friction: scale caches are not checkpointed (resume restarts the refresh phase) and D=50 inference is request-order dependent.
  • Emulator quirks characterized but not ablated at 8B: ~1.2 ppm RTZ gain contraction; 1/1024 snap dead zone. Downstream eval is likelihood-based multiple choice only.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the UE5M3 advantage hold at 1T tokens, where prior work reports 8B NVFP4 recipes still need BF16 final layers, or does the no-exemption recipe diverge at long horizons?
  • How much of the win is each factor in isolation — e.g., UE5M3 with D=50 but RHT retained, or UE5M3 with D=1 and no RHT — to cleanly separate format range from scale-lifecycle effects?
  • Would a systematic rule for choosing T per layer type or depth beat the hand-placed T=2048 override, and does it transfer to dense Transformers or MoE?
  • Do the ~1.2 ppm RTZ contraction and the 1/1024 snap dead zone matter at larger horizons or on small-gradient-sensitive tasks (fine-tuning, RLHF)?
  • What would native UE5M3 tensor-core support cost in silicon, and would it preserve the +21.2% throughput of the no-RHT/all-linears configuration?
  • Can the witness-driven emulator methodology be packaged as a general conformance test for FP4/FP8 accelerators — including silent-data-corruption screening?
