PaperGym: Rubric-Centered Evolution for Research-Plan Generation

Yuhan Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Yuchen Yan, Kaitao Song, et al. — Zhejiang University (corresp. Yongliang Shen)  ·  2026-08-31

AI-Scientists Research-Plan-Generation Rubric-as-Rewards GRPO Self-Distillation 2026-08-31

Key metric

3.73%

criterion leakage · PaperGym-20k (20,000 instances)

vs 11.90%–34.10% for HealthBench, RubricHub Science, and ResearchPlanGen — achieved structurally, by sourcing questions and rubrics from disjoint paper sections.

Why this matters

An end-to-end recipe for auto-building verifiable AI-scientist training environments — the leakage metric and reward-hacking caveats matter to any automated-reward designer.

⚠ Critical edge

The headline "beats Kimi K2.6" is a one-benchmark cherry-pick: on the five-benchmark average the trained 8B scores 38.55 against Kimi K2.6's 51.64.

Methodology

  • Leakage-decoupled pipeline: each arXiv paper is map-reduced into four sections — Research Goal / Background / Method / Experimental Design. The question is synthesized only from the first two; reference answer + rubric only from the last two (numerical results excluded to block memorization).
  • Rubrics: DeepSeek-V4-Flash proposes question-conditioned (RQ) and answer-grounded (RA) criteria; merged, semantically deduped, ranked, truncated to 10 atomic binary checks spanning innovation (63.8%) and experimental design (36.2%), plus a fixed general rubric set. Scorer self-consistency ≤ ~0.01 across rounds; ~80% verdict agreement between Qwen3-8B and Kimi K2.6.
  • Stage 1 — rubric-conditioned OPSD: the student's on-policy rollouts are rescored by a teacher holding the rubric as privileged context (withheld at inference); student minimizes per-token JSD (LoRA r=64, 200–400 steps). Rubric-conditioning beats answer-conditioned distillation by +1.08 / +0.88.
  • Stage 2 — GRPO with rubric-as-reward: a frozen base copy self-grades 8 rollouts per prompt with binary verdicts; reward r = 0.7·rspec + 0.3·rgen (7:3 wins the sweep). The 1.7B model is too weak to self-score — a 4B model serves as its scorer.
  • Evaluation: in-domain PaperGym-Innov / PaperGym-Design (post-Jan-2026 papers) + out-of-domain ResearchQA, ResearchPlanGen-ML, RubricHub Science.

Results & Highlights

Five-benchmark average gain from OPSD→GRPO on Qwen3-1.7B (22.24 → 27.81); +5.02 at 4B, +4.81 at 8B.

  • 8B vs larger baselines: ResearchQA 73.48 — above Kimi K2.6 (73.19) and S1-VL-RL (72.09), +6.85 over its own base. In-domain margins are large (Innov 24.47 vs Intern-S1-Mini's 7.48) but the overall average (38.55) still trails GPT-5.1 (54.53) and DeepSeek-V4-Flash (52.43).
  • Rubric quality is the bottleneck: downgrading the rubric generator costs −2.00/−1.18 (Innov/Design) vs only −0.97/−0.67 for downgrading the extractor; dropping either rubric source hurts (RA-only −1.87/−1.11).
  • Privileged information helps: adding the reference answer to the rubric hurts Design (9.17 vs 10.45) — one realized solution narrows the space of valid continuations.

Strengths

  • Root-cause fix: attacks rubric-reward contamination structurally — disjoint source sections, not post-hoc filtering — and quantifies the effect against five existing datasets.
  • Clean, motivated schedule: the same rubric as privileged OPSD context then GRPO reward, with an entropy widen-then-narrow analysis explaining why single-stage variants fail.
  • Unusually thorough evaluation: three model scales, three out-of-domain benchmarks, a recipe-fixed win-rate study isolating data contribution, a scaling law, and ablations over rubric source, generator scale, privileged information, reward mixing, and stage ordering.
  • Full release: code, pipeline, the 20k corpus, two held-out benchmarks, and the actual prompts in the appendix.
  • Clean temporal split: training papers 2015–2025, benchmarks post-Jan-2026; the Claude-Sonnet-5 refusal artifact is disclosed rather than hidden.

Limitations & Critique

  • LLM-judge circularity: DeepSeek-V4-Flash generates the rubrics and judges all scores — and even the 3.73% leakage labels come from the same judge. No human evaluation anywhere.
  • Self-grading verifier: the GRPO reward comes from a frozen base copy grading its own outputs; no adversarial audit of reward hacking is reported, and the 1.7B case already needs an external 4B scorer.
  • In-domain benchmarks share the training pipeline (same decomposition, rubric conventions, judge), so large in-domain margins partly measure rubric-format familiarity.
  • Domain mismatch: trains on the CS subset (~10k instances) only, while benchmarks span econ and physics; transfer unvalidated.
  • Plans graded on coherence, never on outcomes: quantitative results are excluded from reference answers, so "correct" plans are never checked against whether the proposed experiments would actually produce the paper's findings.
  • Qwen3-family only (1.7B–8B); binary verdicts give zero partial credit and borderline scoring is unmeasured.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the rubric-quality bottleneck hold with human-written rubrics, or with an ensemble of judge models voting per criterion?
  • Can the 3.73% leakage figure be confirmed by human annotation — and does a judge other than DeepSeek-V4-Flash change the ranking against HealthBench and ResearchPlanGen?
  • Is the 8B-vs-GPT-5.1 gap (38.55 vs 54.53) plan quality or rubric-style mismatch — and would distilling proprietary plans under PaperGym rubrics close it?
  • Do PaperGym-trained plans actually execute better (code runs, ablations complete), i.e., is rubric coverage correlated with downstream experimental success?
  • The 58.1% win-rate advantage is measured at 1.7B — does the data-construction advantage persist at 8B+, where models may rely less on rubric scaffolding?
