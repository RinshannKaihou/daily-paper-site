> **一句话总结：** 密歇根大学与滑铁卢大学作者在同一 Qwen2.5-1.5B 起始检查点上平行训练 9 个后训练分支（5 个 GRPO 变体 + 3 个 SFT + 1 个 DPO），并扩展到 Qwen 3B/7B、Llama-3.2-3B、Phi-3.5-mini 三个家族，在 CounterFact/ConFiQA 知识冲突协议下发现接地（context grounding）增益三极分化：GRPO 几乎不改善接地（最高 +.036 且 4 种子置信区间含 0，同一训练却把被奖励的 HotpotQA F1 抬高 +.120），冲突 SFT 中等（+.062），DPO 直冲天花板（CounterFact 更新率 .586→.964，3B 上 +.599）；机制审计显示训练前即可从起始模型估计出接地方向（与训练后模型方向余弦 ≥.915、top-8 因果头 8/8 保留），向起始模型注入该方向可恢复 DPO 增益的 35–40%，减去它则抵消冲突 SFT（−.405）与 DPO（−.276）的绝大部分增益——接地增益主要是放大既有机制，而非建立新机制。

> **TL;DR:** This paper audits nine post-training arms launched from one Qwen2.5-1.5B checkpoint (five GRPO variants, three SFT arms, one DPO; extended to Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Llama-3.2-3B, Phi-3.5-mini) and shows that context-grounding gains on the CounterFact and ConFiQA knowledge-conflict benchmarks dissociate sharply by recipe: GRPO adds at most +.036 update rate (4-seed CIs include zero) while the same training improves its rewarded HotpotQA F1 by +.120, conflict-SFT adds +.062, and DPO drives the CounterFact update rate from .586 to .964 (+.378; up to +.599 at 3B) across all five models. A grounding direction estimated from the starting model *before* training stays aligned with every trained arm (cosine ≥.915), recovers 35–40% of DPO's gain when injected into the base model, and suppresses most of the conflict-SFT (−.405) and DPO (−.276) gains when subtracted — supporting a mechanism-reuse account: post-training largely amplifies grounding machinery already present in the instruction-tuned starting model rather than building new circuits.

## 核心贡献

- **同一检查点上的 9 分支对照实验**：从 Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct 出发并行训练 5 个 GRPO 变体（A: answer token-F1 奖励；A′: 证据强调 prompt；B: citation set-F1 奖励；C: 对比式 context-utility 奖励；D: in-context counterfactual 答案奖励）、3 个 SFT 分支（E0 无冲突对照、E2 KAFT 式混合、E3 78% 冲突数据）和 1 个 DPO（4,500 对，β=.5），并将关键对比扩展到 Qwen 3B/7B、Llama-3.2-3B、Phi-3.5-mini——arms 是完整训练配方（目标、数据、预算同时变化），作者明确把结论限定在"被测配方"而非"优化目标"本身。
- **三极分化的行为学结果**：GRPO 的接地增益微小且不复现（两种子复现后 4 种子置信区间含 0；TOST 等价性检验表明 A/A′ 的效应落在匹配对照的冲突 SFT 增益 ±.044 内）；冲突 SFT 中等改善；DPO 在全部 5 个模型上接近天花板（McNemar p ≤ 2.5e−25）。
- **"奖励指标 vs 接地"解耦证据**：GRPO 把被奖励的 HotpotQA F1 提高 +.120（1.5B: .442→.563）和 +.104（3B: .566→.670），但接地迁移到 ConFiQA（.500）与 FaithEval（.680）与 base 持平——RL 确实优化了被奖励的能力，但那不等于接地鲁棒性。
- **机制复用的因果审计**：训练前用 DiM（follow-ctx 与 follow-mem 项的末位残差均值差）从起始模型估计接地方向；per-head knockout 显示所有被审计分支基本保留起始模型的 top-8 因果头（8/8 或 7/8，超几何 p ≤ 7.1e−13）；向 base 注入该方向提升接地 +.109 并恢复 DPO 增益的 40.0%（α=30；通过全部副作用检查的剂量恢复 35.0%），从训练后模型减去该方向抵消 DPO −.276、冲突 SFT −.405 的增益。
- **GRPO 小增益的覆盖度检验**：上下文答案覆盖本已很高（hit@8=.978，仅 6.0% 项 advantage-collapsed）；200 例 SFT 热启动把 hit@8 从 .380 提到 .453、接地 +.023，但在其上跑完全相同的 GRPO 配方只再加 +.001（p=.91）——修复覆盖度不足以让该 GRPO 配方改善接地（作者注明这不排除更强的覆盖度干预）。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled nine-arm audit from one checkpoint**: five GRPO variants, three SFT arms, and DPO all start from the same Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct checkpoint, with key comparisons replicated on Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Llama-3.2-3B, and Phi-3.5-mini — arms are complete training recipes (objective, data, and budget vary together), and the authors scope claims to the tested recipes rather than to optimization objectives in the abstract.
- **A three-way behavioral dissociation**: GRPO grounding gains are small and shrink under seed replication (both 4-seed CIs include zero; TOST equivalence tests place A/A′ effects within ±.044 of the matched-control conflict-SFT gain), conflict-SFT gains moderately (+.062; 4-seed +.044 [+.034,+.055] over the matched no-conflict control), and DPO reaches near-ceiling grounding on all five models across three families (McNemar p ≤ 2.5e−25).
- **Rewarded-metric vs grounding decoupling**: the same GRPO runs raise the rewarded HotpotQA F1 by +.120 at 1.5B (.442→.563) and +.104 at 3B (.566→.670) while grounding transfer to ConFiQA (.500) and FaithEval (.680) stays at base level — RL demonstrably optimizes what it is rewarded for, but that does not buy grounding robustness in this setting.
- **Causal auditing of mechanism reuse**: a DiM grounding direction estimated from the starting model before training remains aligned with all trained arms (cosine .915–.984 at 1.5B, .942–.987 at 3B/7B); per-head knockout recovers the starting model's top-8 causal heads in every audited arm (8/8 for GRPO-A/A′/DPO, 7/8 for SFT arms; hypergeometric p ≤ 7.1e−13, and a matched parametric-recall control shares zero heads); injecting the direction into the base model lifts grounding (+.109) and recovers 40.0% of DPO's gain (35.0% at the largest dose passing all side-effect checks), while subtracting it removes −.276 (DPO) and −.405 (conflict-SFT) of the trained gains.
- **A coverage test for the small-GRPO result**: context-answer coverage is already high (hit@8 = .978, 6.0% advantage-collapsed on eval items); a 200-example SFT warm start raises hit@8 .380→.453 and grounding +.023 (p=.012), yet running the identical GRPO recipe on top adds only +.001 (p=.91) — insufficient within-group reward variation, not just rollout coverage, limits this GRPO family (stronger coverage interventions explicitly not ruled out).

## Methodology

**Training arms.** All 1.5B arms start from the same Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct checkpoint. Five GRPO variants (Shao et al.'s Qwen recipe: 200 steps, lr 3e−6, KL β=1/2, group size 8 in TRL) differ only in reward: A (answer token-F1), A′ (same reward, evidence-emphasizing prompt), B (citation set-F1), C (contrastive context-utility: log p(ans|q,ctx) − log p(ans|q)), D (in-context counterfactual answer — the only arm trained on conflict data with a grounding-targeting reward). Three SFT arms (E0 no-conflict control, E2 KAFT-style mixture, E3 78% conflict / 22% standard) use two epochs over the same 1,500-example pool GRPO samples from; DPO uses β=.5, 3 epochs, 4,500 pairs (Rafaailov et al.). Replication: A, A′, E2, E0, E3, DPO × 4 seeds at 1.5B; A/E3/DPO additionally at 3B (4 seeds) and Llama-3.2-3B; DPO at 7B and Phi-3.5-mini; GRPO arms at 7B. Crucially, the arms compare *complete recipes* — objective, data, and budget vary together by design.

**Evaluation.** The CounterFact conflict protocol (Meng et al. 2022) is a two-pass design: pass 1 (closed-book) identifies items the base model answers correctly from memory, giving a *frozen known set* (n=1089 at 1.5B, computed once from the base model and reused for every arm, so all comparisons are paired on identical items); pass 2 presents a counterfactual context and records follow-ctx vs follow-mem, with update rate = follow-ctx/(follow-ctx+follow-mem). ConFiQA and FaithEval provide replication sets with distribution/format variants; HotpotQA measures the rewarded task. Statistics: exact two-sided McNemar on paired items, seed-level t-CIs (t.975, n−1, n=4), and TOST equivalence testing at ±.044 (the observed conflict-SFT gain over matched control). A metric-integrity audit flags that activation interventions can flip both the known-set gate and the "decisive" subset — this bias changed one effect estimate by a factor of four — so all intervention results use the *exclusive follow-ctx rate on the frozen no-intervention known set*, with the non-decisive fraction reported separately.

**Direction estimation and interventions.** The grounding direction is a difference-in-means (DiM) vector: mean last-position residual on follow-ctx items minus follow-mem items, computed on the base model at layer 21 (1.5B; full layer sweep at 3B with an even/odd item split guarding against overfitting). Every intervention is validated against matched-norm random directions, and steering magnitudes are norm-calibrated across families (Llama-3.2-3B mean residual norm 26.7 vs Qwen 103.7). Mechanistic audits: per-head knockout recovering the base model's top-8 causal heads per arm, cross-task asymmetry (conflict-task knockout drops mean logit difference 6.76 with barely any matched-recall change, while recall-task knockout shows the opposite pattern; a matched parametric-recall task shares 0/8 heads), probe AUROC (.818) vs Hewitt-Liang selectivity (.330), and cosine alignment of the base direction with each trained arm's own direction. Steering add/subtract experiments quantify recovery, defined as (m_steer − m_base)/(m_DPO − m_base), with side-effect checks: KL at the intervened position (threshold .1 after Arditi et al. 2024), MMLU and ARC drift, and generation-quality inspection.

## Results & Highlights

- **DPO produces near-ceiling grounding everywhere (Table 2).** CounterFact update rate, base → DPO: Qwen2.5-1.5B .586→.964 (+.378), Qwen2.5-3B .362→.961 (+.599), Qwen2.5-7B .446→.969 (+.524), Llama-3.2-3B .492→.980 (+.488), Phi-3.5-mini .586→.942 (+.356); paired McNemar p ≤ 2.5e−25, seed t-CIs +.376 [+.370,+.382] (1.5B) and +.596 [+.591,+.600] (3B). At 3B the gain also transfers across ConFiQA splits (QA +.599, MR +.471, MC +.535) — but HotpotQA F1 is flat at 1.5B (.444 vs .442), drops .566→.536 at 3B, and drops −.095 on Llama.
- **GRPO gains are small, non-robust, and decoupled from the rewarded metric (Table 1).** Against base update rate .538 (n=1089): GRPO A +.011 (p=.62), A′ +.036 (p=.007), B +.004, C +.018, D +.008; the two seed-replicated variants' 4-seed CIs include zero (A +.006 [−.000,+.012]; A′ +.017 [−.008,+.041]), and equivalence tests bound their effects within ±.044 of conflict-SFT's matched-control gain. Meanwhile the same training lifts HotpotQA F1 by +.120 at 1.5B (.442→.563) and +.104 at 3B (.566→.670), while grounding transfer to ConFiQA (.500) and FaithEval (.680) sits at base level — the largest GRPO effect (A′) does not transfer cross-dataset.
- **Conflict-SFT is intermediate; the no-conflict control already helps (Table 1).** E3 (78% conflict) +.062 over base (p=1.2e−7); E2 +.044 (p=.003) with 4-seed +.047 [+.041,+.054]; even E0 (no conflict) +.025. Against the matched E0 control, E3's 4-seed gain is +.044 [+.034,+.055]. E2 transfers best among SFT arms to ConFiQA (.904, 47 of 52 decisive items).
- **The starting model already contains the machinery (Tables 3–4).** Per-head knockout recovers the base top-8 causal heads in every audited arm (GRPO-A 8/8, A′ 8/8, DPO 8/8 at 1.5B/336 heads; DPO 8/8 at 3B/576 heads; SFT arms 7/8; DPO seeds 7/8 each; recall control 0/8; hypergeometric p ≤ 7.1e−13 at 1.5B, ≤ 3.5e−18 at 3B). The base-estimated direction stays aligned with trained arms (cosine .915–.984 at 1.5B, .942–.987 at 3B/7B). Injecting it into the base model lifts grounding +.109 over matched random (p=2.7e−4); subtracting it from trained models suppresses DPO −.276 (1.5B), −.162 (3B), −.175 (7B), conflict-SFT −.405, and Llama −.196 at L15 (though that last one is n.s., p=.375).
- **Steering the base model alone recovers 40.0% of DPO's gain with zero training (Figure 4).** Follow-ctx rate on the frozen known set (n=137) rises from .467 (α=0) to .584 at α=30 while matched-norm random directions stay flat (.453); recovery = (m_steer − m_base)/(m_DPO − m_base) = 40.0% (absolute rates .584/.467/.759). The largest dose passing *all* stated side-effect checks is α=20 (KL .076, below the .1 threshold of Arditi et al. 2024; KL .170 at α=30 is over), which recovers 35.0%; MMLU/ARC move within about one standard error. Steering stacked on context-faithful prompting adds nothing significant (.555→.606, p=.118), and DPO (.759) remains far stronger.
- **Gains arrive early on a stable direction (Figure 2).** During DPO training, ConFiQA grounding jumps from base .577 to .932 by step 160 of 800 (~90% of the final .961), while the DiM direction's cosine to the base-estimated direction stays ≥.968 at every checkpoint (steps 160–800: .980/.970/.972/.971/.968) — an amplification account, not rotation.
- **Metric validation.** The lexical-containment metric agrees only moderately with a Qwen2.5-14B LLM judge on 400 items across four intervention conditions (κ=.507 final iteration; agreement .73 on training-arm outputs vs .58 on intervention outputs), but the headline base-to-DPO contrast reproduces under judge labels: follow-ctx .45→.61 on CounterFact and .46→.70 on ConFiQA.

## Critical Assessment

- **The central contribution is an audit, not a method — and it is honest about that.** The authors explicitly disclaim novelty for mechanism reuse or the context-vs-memory direction; the value is the controlled cross-recipe comparison from a common checkpoint plus causal tests. That said, the headline "GRPO doesn't improve grounding" is carefully scoped (their reward family, their base policy with low context-answer rollout coverage), and they flag that synthetic-coverage GRPO (Si et al. 2026) and frozen-backbone gate-module recipes (Li et al. 2026) fall outside the bound — good hygiene, but readers who cite this as "RL can't fix grounding" will be overclaiming beyond what the paper supports.
- **The arms confound objective with data and budget, and the authors say so.** DPO trains on 4,500 pairs for 3 epochs while GRPO runs 200 steps over a 1,500-example pool; the dramatic DPO-vs-GRPO gap (+.599 vs ≤+.036 at matched scales) therefore cannot be attributed to the optimization objective alone. The paper's framing ("recipes differ sharply") is accurate, but the abstract-level temptation to read this as "DPO > GRPO as objectives" is not what was tested.
- **The lexical-containment metric is the weakest link, and the GRPO/SFT contrasts rest on it alone.** Agreement with an LLM judge is only moderate (κ=.507), and agreement drops to .58 on intervention outputs precisely where the suppression effects live; only the DPO contrast was re-validated under judge labels. The 100 blind hand-labels favored the judge, suggesting the lexical metric is the conservative side — but a κ of .507 means the small GRPO deltas (+.004 to +.036) are within the measurement's comfortable noise band, which the seed-level CIs (including zero) corroborate.
- **The causal-suppression evidence has soft spots the paper surfaces but does not fully resolve.** Suppression at Qwen scales requires intervention strengths of 75–190% of the residual stream norm and severely damages generation; the non-decisive fraction rises from .27 to .41 under DPO suppression, meaning part of the measured effect is reduced decisiveness rather than reduced grounding; and the Llama suppression at the chosen layer is not significant (p=.375), acknowledged via a cross-family layer-calibration caveat. These are exactly the kind of details that could erode the effect at other scales — 7B behavioral confirmation exists, but 7B mechanistic depth is thinner.
- **The mechanism-reuse account is supported but incomplete.** Steering recovers 35–40% of DPO's gain — not 100% — and the paper is forthright that one direction and one head set cannot rule out new machinery on unmeasured axes. The DPO seed-level head-instability discussion (7/8 recovery per seed, identity-vs-algorithm concerns after Tigges et al. 2024) is addressed via interventions that do not depend on head identity, which is the right call, but "large overlap" and "same mechanism" are not the same claim.
- **Reproducibility is plausible but not demonstrated.** The paper reports standard recipes (TRL GRPO, DPO β=.5), frozen known sets, and per-item paired statistics, all on public datasets; no code or artifact release is mentioned in the main text, and the sheer number of arms (9 at 1.5B, plus replication across 3 families) would be expensive to re-run for a lab without dedicated GPUs.
- **Significance.** For practitioners the actionable finding is real: if your starting checkpoint already contains a strong grounding direction (as these instruction-tuned models do), cheap steering gets you a third of the way to DPO-level grounding for free, and post-training gains should be audited against what was already there before crediting the recipe.

## Strengths

- **Controlled common-checkpoint design.** Running nine recipes from one checkpoint with a frozen known set (n=1089) and paired per-item statistics removes most of the confounds that plague post-training comparisons in the literature.
- **Causal, not just correlational, evidence.** The mechanism-reuse claim rests on add/subtract steering, per-head knockout, cross-task knockout asymmetry, a parametric-recall control sharing 0/8 heads, and matched-norm random-direction baselines — the direction is treated as a hypothesis to be causally tested, following the specificity discipline of Arditi et al. and Syed.
- **Statistical care unusual for interpretability papers.** Seed-level t-CIs, TOST equivalence testing (±.044), exact McNemar, and an explicit equivalence bound tied to the observed SFT effect make the null-ish GRPO result quantitative rather than hand-wavy.
- **Self-audited metric integrity.** Catching that the intervention flips the known-set gate and the decisive subset (biasing one effect estimate by a factor of four) and then re-basing all intervention results on the exclusive follow-ctx rate is exactly the kind of measurement honesty most steering papers skip.
- **Honest scoping throughout.** The limitations section names the lexical metric (κ=.507), the six-arm audit coverage, the instruction-tuned-checkpoint meaning of "pre-existing," the recipe confound, and the GRPO bound explicitly — the conclusions never outrun the evidence.

## Limitations

- **Metric ceiling.** The follow-ctx/follow-mem lexical-containment metric agrees with an LLM judge only at κ=.507, and the SFT and GRPO contrasts were not re-validated under judge labels — the small positive effects claimed there are the most exposed to measurement noise.
- **Scale and depth of the mechanistic audit.** The full mechanistic stack (head knockout, direction interventions) is deepest at 1.5B and covers six of nine arms (B, C, D are behavioral only); interventions are single-seed; head sets are claimed rather than complete circuits, and 7B/Phi/Llama are partially behavioral.
- **Recipe confound.** Objective, data, and budget vary together across arms by design, so the three-way dissociation identifies *recipes*, not *objectives* — the DPO-vs-GRPO gap cannot be decomposed into its ingredients.
- **The reuse account is partial by the paper's own numbers.** Steering recovers 35–40% of DPO's gain, suppression retains a rising non-decisive fraction (.27→.41), and the Llama suppression is n.s. at the calibrated layer — room remains for genuinely new machinery the audit does not measure.
- **Coverage explanation only weakly tested.** The warm-start experiment uses a single intervention strength (200 examples) and one GRPO family; the authors concede it "does not rule out stronger coverage interventions" — so the small-GRPO finding is a bound on this configuration, not a mechanism-level verdict on RL for grounding.
- **Domain narrowness.** All conflict evaluation is factual QA on public benchmarks (CounterFact, ConFiQA, FaithEval); no instruction-following, long-context, or multi-hop conflict settings, so transfer of the mechanism-reuse account beyond entity-style knowledge conflicts is untested.

## Relevance

这篇论文与你关注的「LLM Hidden States & Interpretability」方向高度契合：它把探测（DiM 方向 + probe 选择性）、因果头定位（per-head knockout）与激活转向（norm-calibrated steering + 副作用检查）组合成一套对后训练增益的机制审计流程，正好示范了"用表征几何和因果干预回答训练问题"的方法论；同时它对 GRPO/DPO 训练配方的可观测性审计（种子置信区间、等价性检验、指标完整性自检）也对「LLM Training Stability & Observability」有直接参考价值。需要注意的是它研究的是知识冲突/幻觉类模型侧行为，而非硬件故障或 SDC 检测，属于你明确划出的范围之外的那类 model-side 问题，但机制审计方法本身值得借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the mechanism-reuse account hold for *pretrained* (not instruction-tuned) starting checkpoints, or is the pre-existing grounding direction an artifact of instruction tuning itself?
- What explains the remaining 60–65% of DPO's gain that steering cannot recover — new machinery, distributed changes, or the non-decisive-fraction confound under suppression?
- Would a GRPO recipe with reward directly targeting grounding *and* a fix for within-group reward variation (e.g., synthetic coverage as in Si et al. 2026) break the +.017 ceiling observed here?
- Does the grounding direction and its causal head set remain stable under much longer DPO training or at 30B+ scale, where the Qwen-norm calibration (residual norm 103.7 vs Llama 26.7) may no longer transfer?
- Can the pre-existing direction be used prospectively — e.g., as a checkpoint-selection or data-mixing signal estimated *before* committing to an expensive DPO run?
