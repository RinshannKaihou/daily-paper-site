TreeFI: Value-Aware Statistical Fault Injection for Deep Neural Networks

TreeFI · Value-Aware Statistical Fault Injection

Noam Bires, Marcello Traiola, Angeliki Kritikakou, Elisa Fromont · 2026-09-04 · Inria / IRISA, Univ. Rennes
fault-injection statistical-fault-injection dnn-reliability bit-flip-errors stratified-sampling

Key Metric

44.9×

Average injection reduction vs data-aware SFI for activation faults (5.9×–72.1×); vs exhaustive ground truth on ResNet8, 270× fewer injections with MAE inside the 1% target.

Why This Matters

Hardware bit flips silently corrupt DNN outputs; TreeFI makes estimating how often far cheaper — reliability studies intractable on big models become practical.

Critical Edge

Ground-truth validation exists only on a tiny CNN; for larger models accuracy rests on agreeing with baselines that share the same fault model — a common bias can't be excluded.

Methodology

Setup — ResNet8 (CIFAR-10) · RepVGG-A0, DeiT-T/S/B (ImageNet) · FP32 single-bit flips · 1% error / 95% confidence · NVIDIA H100

  • Fault model. FP32 single-bit flips under the single-fault assumption; a fault is a "failure" when corrupted top-1 differs from the golden output. The goal is minimizing injections to reach the target statistical accuracy.
  • Phase 1 — offline characterization, zero injections. Per-layer weighted histograms (activations from validation forwards; weights read directly); per layer-bit 1-D regression trees split the value axis where flip severity changes sharply, trained on value-change magnitude — never model output. Each interval gets a sigmoid risk score r = σ(s·(avg_log − β)).
  • Phase 2 — Neyman-style stratified campaign. Injections per interval ∝ frequency × √(r(1−r)) — more samples where faults are common and variance is high; the final rate is the frequency-weighted mix of per-interval rates.
  • Only two knobs. Sigmoid sharpness s (default 2) and bias multiplier y; histogram preprocessing cuts distribution prep from days to minutes.

  • Exhaustive validation (ResNet8/CIFAR-10). TreeFI-10× reproduces exhaustive failure rates in 28 min; one-shot SFI needs 740,534 injections (21.2× more), iterative IFI 979,021 / 9 h 35 min (28.0× more). Wall-clock: 15.3× vs SFI, 20.3× vs IFI.

  • ImageNet scale. DeiT-Tiny first layer: 1,042 injections vs 124,089 (IFI) and 139,778 (SFI); IFI is not tractable for DeiT at all.
  • Ablation. 60 of 88 (s, y) configurations stay within the 1% margin; s ≤ 3 always passes, sharp sigmoids (s ≥ 16) mostly fail.

Strengths

  • Rare exhaustive ground-truth validation (9.44M injections), with MAE reported per layer and per bit, not just globally.
  • Statistically principled: Neyman-style variance-aware allocation and frequency-weighted aggregation preserve confidence guarantees by construction.
  • Genuine scalability: full-model results where SFI needs 111 h 30 min for three DeiT-Tiny layers and IFI is infeasible.
  • Honest, self-critical evaluation: wall-clock and count reductions both reported; ablation pinpoints over-sharp sigmoids; weight-fault headroom candidly smaller.
  • Fault-model-agnostic core idea plus open-source artifacts with per-figure reproduction documentation.

Limitations

  • Ground truth confined to one small CNN (ResNet8, CIFAR-10 activations); all larger-model results lack an exhaustive reference.
  • FP32 single-bit, single-fault only: no multi-bit or correlated faults; FP16/bfloat16/quantized formats unvalidated, where bit significance differs sharply.
  • Top-1 classification only: no confidence degradation, calibration, or other task metrics.
  • Offline overhead erodes wall-clock gains; activation histograms come from validation-set forwards and may not cover out-of-distribution inputs.
  • New tuning surface: ~32% of ablation configurations missed the 1% margin, mostly at high sharpness — a careless user can silently get a poor allocation.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does interval learning transfer to FP16/bfloat16/INT8-quantized models, where exponent/mantissa structure and value distributions differ sharply from FP32?
  2. Can the learned interval risk scores be reused as a protection signal — selective redundancy or exponent-aware hardening on high-risk value intervals?
  3. How does stratified allocation behave under multi-bit or temporally/spatially correlated faults, where the single-fault assumption breaks down?
  4. Could closed-form, exponent-aware binning replace the regression trees — removing the training step, the s/y knobs, and the 28 failing configurations?
  5. How robust are the offline histograms to out-of-distribution activation ranges — does the allocation remain statistically valid under distribution shift?
