Fewer verification FLOPs per candidate than the 7.6B Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B that HSRM nearly matches. Zero extra generator passes; the whole verifier is 4–7 MB in fp16.

Why this matters

Cached decoding states are a free verification signal — no extra generator passes — reshaping the test-time compute accuracy-cost trade-off.

Critical edge

The “text-only” 55M verifier HSRM beats is the authors’ own re-implementation — the published EORM itself mines rewards from hidden states, weakening the headline framing.

§01Methodologyread, don’t re-encode
  1. Step-boundary extraction — segment the chain-of-thought at delimiters (\n\n, .\n, ;\n…), take residual-stream states at positions before each boundary (last layer, or top-4 concat) → ≤100 step vectors. Zero extra generator passes: the states already exist during decoding.
  2. Tiny verifier — per-step linear projection (the only generator-dependent part, dgen = 2048–5120) → 2-layer Transformer encoder (d = 256, 4 heads) → mean-pool → linear scalar head. 2.12M–3.4M params by generator scale.
  3. Supervision-free training — 64 self-sampled candidates per problem cached with binary final-answer labels; a tie-safe pairwise (Bradley–Terry) ranking loss pushes every correct candidate above every incorrect one, imposing no order among same-label candidates. ~150–500 training problems, 1,000 steps, 5 seeds.
  4. Evaluation — best-of-8 accuracy (primary) + within-problem AUROC on GSM8K / MATH-500 / AIME / OlympiadBench; Qwen3 1.7B–14B (non-thinking, plus thinking on MATH-500) and Llama-3.2 1B/3B/8B.
  5. Labeling audit — deterministic answer matching, then an LLM judge for ambiguous cases (~3.1% of ~255K labels changed, mostly recovering correct answers in non-canonical formats).
  6. Baselines — EORM-55M text reader (authors’ re-implementation), Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B, and non-learned heuristics (log-prob, entropy, length) compared under an optimistic envelope that favors the baselines.

Matches or beats EORM in 15 of 16 generator–dataset settings
  • The win is the input, not capacity: step-boundary hidden states hit 86.86% Bo8 / 0.724 AUROC vs 82.23% / 0.519 for a text encoder of the same recipe (+0.205 AUROC); a DeepSet with 0.4M params performs comparably, and hybrid text+hidden input adds nothing — raw text contributes nothing on top of internal representations.
  • Thinking mode: don’t read the deliberation. Post-</think> extraction beats full-trace on AUROC — Qwen3-4B: 0.884 vs 0.672 — so thinking helps generation, but reading the trace itself hurts verification.
  • Where it loses: the 7B PRM keeps a clear edge only on AIME, where HSRM (20 train / 70 eval problems) even falls to cumulative log-probability (17.7% vs 19.3%).
  • Data efficiency & transfer: 25→500 training problems lift Bo8 83.4→86.2%; GSM8K/MATH-500-trained verifiers transfer zero-shot to OlympiadBench, still beating EORM.
  • Objective matters, modestly: tie-safe BT beats ListMLE (84.59%) and pointwise BCE (83.91%) on GSM8K.

+Strengths

  • Striking efficiency: a ~2M verifier (4–7 MB) matching a 55M text verifier on 15/16 settings and approaching a ~7.6B domain PRM, at ~10⁵× lower per-candidate cost with zero extra generator passes.
  • Architectural insight, not a scale play — validated by the capacity ablation (DeepSet d=128 ≈ Transformer d=256) that cleanly attributes the gain to the input signal.
  • Unusually thorough ablations: generator layers, input modality, encoder architecture, training objective, pool size, N-scaling, zero-shot transfer, cross-family, thinking-mode extraction position.
  • Honest negatives in-line: hybrid input doesn’t help; cheap heuristics win on AIME; gains shrink as generators saturate GSM8K.
  • Reproducible: code released, split indices, hyperparameters, and labeling statistics all published.

−Limitations

  • Math reasoning only; no evidence of transfer to open-ended or non-verifiable domains where outcome labels are harder to automate.
  • Requires white-box access to generator internals and per-generator projections — API-only models are out of scope by design; the verifier must be retrained (or re-projected) per generator family.
  • AIME results are weak and noisy (20 train / 70 eval problems) with a loss to a log-prob heuristic; output is a candidate-level scalar — no step-localized (PRM-style) error detection.
  • Baseline set omits majority-voting / self-consistency — free and strong when answers are verifiable — and a faithful as-published EORM; the LLM judge touches both train and eval labels.
  • Thinking-mode study covers only two model scales on MATH-500; the post-</think> finding needs broader validation. One cell reports 87.00 ± 0.00 accuracy across 5 seeds — suspiciously identical.
§03Questions for further exploration
  1. How would the original, as-published EORM (which mines rewards from hidden states) compare at matched parameter counts — is step-boundary granularity or hidden-state access itself the deciding factor?
  2. Does the post-</think> advantage (0.672 → 0.884) reflect self-correction dynamics in general — does it appear in R1-style deliberators or in earlier intermediate layers?
  3. Can step-boundary scores be localized into per-step error detection (PRM-style), or is the correctness information only available at the trajectory level?
  4. What happens when a verifier trained on one generator family’s hidden states is applied to another’s — the paper transfers across datasets but always within a generator?
  5. Would combining HSRM with majority voting close the remaining gap to the oracle on AIME and OlympiadBench, where pools often contain a correct candidate no verifier selects?
