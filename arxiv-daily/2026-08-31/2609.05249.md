> **一句话总结：** 在 20/40/58 MeV 质子束（6.19×10¹⁰ p/cm²、184.3 Gy）下对运行于 Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC 的开源 TCU 神经网络加速器（ResNet-20/CIFAR-10）做系统级辐照表征：记录 7 次 Linux-SEFI 工作负载中断与 2 次静默输出损坏——最严重一次是 100 个推理中前 61 个正确后 39 个连续输出恒为图像池中不存在的 "bird" 类，而内核日志、内存测试与电源轨监视全部正常；宽束场 Linux-SEFI 截面 3.6×10⁻¹⁰ cm²/system [1.5–7.5]×10⁻¹⁰，而 SoC 中心小束场 44 个完成块零事件（95% 单侧上限 <2.5×10⁻¹⁰ cm²/system）。

> **TL;DR:** Proton irradiation (20/40/58 MeV, 6.19×10¹⁰ p/cm²) of an open-source TCU NN accelerator (INT8, 16×16 systolic array) running ResNet-20 on a fixed 10-image CIFAR-10 pool under Linux/PYNQ on a Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC yields 7 Linux-SEFIs and 2 silent output-corruption events — the largest being 39 consecutive "bird" predictions (a class absent from the pool) at unchanged 23–24 ms cadence with every availability monitor nominal. Against the SoC-centered small-field baseline (0 onsets in 44 completed blocks, 95% upper limit <2.5×10⁻¹⁰ cm²/system), the wide-field Linux-SEFI cross section is 3.6×10⁻¹⁰ cm²/system — a nominal matched-energy exact p = 0.016 that the authors themselves show degrades to p = 0.086–0.46 under alternative readings of one ambiguous run record, so the field contrast is offered as exploratory only.

## 核心贡献

- **首个经 Linux 管理的 PS–PL 路径的开源 ML 加速器束流基线：** 开源 RTL 级 TCU 加速器（16×16 脉动阵列、INT8 16 位定点、AXI DMA 供数）此前没有任何公开的质子束数据（FINN 生成的设计有束流基线，但不是经 Linux 管理的 PS–PL 路径）；本文补上了这一空白，作为未来 COTS FPGA-SoC 神经网络硬件加固的未加固基线。
- **完整的"静默常类损坏"事件档案：** Run 5 块 10 中，前 61 个预测正确，第 62–100 个全部输出 "bird"（图像池中不存在该类），推理节奏（23–24 ms）不变，笔记本进程、内核日志、10-MB memtest、电源轨全部正常——top-1 oracle 与所有常规可用性监视器都抓不到该事件；且损坏状态跨块界 PL/模型重载仍然存在（下一块启动即失败），说明重载刷新的逻辑/PL 存储不在受影响路径上。
- **三条可迁移的报告规范：**（i）按 onset 计数——同一损坏态产生的连续错误输出算一个事件（39 个错误输出若按输出计会把事件数高估 39 倍）；（ii）每个截面分母必须与监视其 endpoint 的监视器匹配，并声明该监视器的盲区；（iii）记录恢复动作但不把恢复动作当作故障物理来源的证据。
- **事件分类法与统计口径：** F3–F9 症状类（F3 多错误后块完成 / F4 输出损坏持续到 endpoint / F8 处理器级失败 / F9 系统级失败=Linux-SEFI）与 R5–R8 恢复类（进程重启/重启/断电），以及按验证块比例分配的 operational fluence（式 1）和逐监视器分母的截面定义（式 2），便于跨辐照活动比较。
- **场对比（Q2，探索性）：** 全部 9 个主要 onset 与 3 次匹配能量 Linux-SEFI 都发生在覆盖 LPDDR4 与板级电路的宽束场（该场只占 45.0% 的操作曝光），SoC 中心小束场 44 个完成块零 onset；作者明确标注该关联与块运行顺序、累积剂量混杂，未预注册、未做多重性校正，属探索性证据。

## Key Contributions

- First published proton radiation baseline for an open-source, RTL-accessible NN accelerator (Xilinx TCU, INT8 16-bit fixed-point, 16×16 systolic array) executed through a Linux-managed PS–PL path on a Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC — prior campaigns had no published beam data for open accelerators through this path.
- A complete, monitor-by-monitor record of a silent constant-class corruption episode: 61/100 correct predictions then 39 consecutive "bird" outputs at unchanged cadence, invisible to the top-1 oracle and every availability indicator; the corrupted state survived the scheduled PL-configuration + model reload at the block boundary.
- Three reusable reporting practices (onset-based event counting, monitor-matched cross-section denominators with stated blind spots, recovery actions logged without causal attribution) plus an F3–F9 / R5–R8 event taxonomy and an operational-fluence definition for cross-campaign comparability.
- A field-contrast experiment: all 9 primary onsets and all 3 matched-energy Linux-SEFIs occurred under the wider beam field (45.0% of operational exposure) while the SoC-centered small field produced zero onsets in 44 completed blocks — honestly labeled exploratory (p = 0.016 nominal, 0.016–0.46 across defensible readings of the ambiguous record, 0.086 with all runs pooled).

## Methodology

**Platform.** Avnet Ultra96-V2 (Zynq UltraScale+ XCU3EG, 16-nm FinFET MPSoC). PS: PYNQ 2.7, Ubuntu 20.04, kernel 5.4.0-xilinx-v2020.2.2, no experiment-specific watchdog, no SEM controller, no redundant inference checker; 2 GB LPDDR4 (x32 device, no ECC) and 16 GB microSD. PL: the open-source Tensor Compiler Unit (TCU) — a 16×16 systolic MAC array at 100 MHz in INT8 16-bit fixed-point format with 20,480 local-memory and 4,096 accumulator-memory vectors, two 128-bit AXI4 masters and a 128-bit AXI DMA; Vivado 2021.2 implementation used 24,382 LUTs (34.6%), 180 BRAM tiles (83.3%), 273 DSP48E2 (75.8%) at +0.523 ns worst-case slack; the essential-bit mask marks 13,222,948 / 30,876,800 bit positions (42.8%) as essential.

**Workload and block protocol.** ResNet-20 classifying a fixed pool of ten CIFAR-10 test-set images (indices 10–19; six classes present; pre-irradiation validation classified all ten correctly, so dataset labels served as the online reference). Each block: 17-pattern memtester pass over 10 MB (0.5% of memory), 100 inferences (~2.34 s, outputs logged every 23–24 ms) with per-inference retained record = predicted class + reference class only (no logits, no intermediate tensors), power-rail samples, kernel-log monitoring, and corrected-OCM ECC telemetry; blocks separated by a median 40 s (23–127 s), each ending with a scheduled PL reconfiguration and model reload (which refreshes PL logic/memories and model buffers but not Linux, OCM, LPDDR4, or the page cache).

**Irradiation.** AIC-144 cyclotron at IFJ PAN, nominal 20/40/58 MeV protons, fluence uncertainty 3%. Two collimations: a nominal 2-cm field covering the SoC footprint and a 4-cm wide field covering SoC + LPDDR4 + additional board area. Six monitored intervals: 580.7 s beam-on, 6.19×10¹⁰ p/cm² monitored fluence (4.29×10¹⁰ p/cm² reconstructed over operational windows), 184.3 Gy.

**Event definitions and statistics.** Onset-based counting: the first abnormal observation after a normal state or recovery is one onset; a persistent corruption counts once regardless of how many outputs it affects. Endpoints: F3/F4 (output corruption, separated by whether the block completed) vs F8/F9 (Linux-SEFI: the prescribed notebook workload fails at process or system level). Recovery tiers R5 (process restart), R6/R7 (reboot/board reset), R8 (power cycle). Operational fluence Φ_op is allocated to each condition by its fraction of verified blocks (Eq. 1); system-level cross sections σ = N/Φ per endpoint (Eq. 2); Garwood 95% Poisson intervals, one-sided 95% upper limit = 2.996/Φ for zero events; matched-energy field contrast via a one-sided exact conditional binomial test (Eqs. 4–5). The test was not preregistered, no multiplicity adjustment was applied, and a sensitivity analysis over alternative readings of the ambiguous run-4 record (Table X) is reported.

## Results & Highlights

- **Silent constant-class corruption (run 5, block 10, 58 MeV wide field):** the first 61 predictions in the 100-inference CIFAR-10 block were correct, then all remaining 39 outputs read "bird" — a class absent from the ten-image pool — at the unchanged 23–24 ms cadence; the kernel log was silent, the 10-MB memtest passed, power rails stayed in range, and the only thing that ended the ~0.9 s episode was the scheduled bitstream reload (duration right-censored; the next launch then failed). No availability monitor flagged anything.
- **Linux-SEFI cross sections:** pooled over the wide field, 7 onsets over 1.93×10¹⁰ p/cm² give 3.6×10⁻¹⁰ cm²/system (95% CI [1.5, 7.5]×10⁻¹⁰); the 40-MeV wide-field point estimate is 6.9×10⁻¹⁰ (halving to 3.5×10⁻¹⁰ if run-4's two onsets are merged). The SoC-centered small field yields only upper limits from 0 onsets in 44 completed blocks: <2.5×10⁻¹⁰ cm²/system at 20 MeV and <2.6×10⁻¹⁰ at 40 MeV — below the wide-field point estimates but not distinguishable from them with these counts.
- **Field contrast (Q2):** at matched energies every onset fell in the wide field — 20 MeV: 1 wide vs 0 small events (fluences 1.19 vs 0.78×10¹⁰ p/cm²), 40 MeV: 2 vs 0 (1.17 vs 0.29×10¹⁰) — giving an exact one-sided p = 0.016. However, alternative defensible readings of run 4 (merged onsets, credited recovery minute, JEDSC-consistent counting) span p = 0.016–0.46 (Table X), and crediting all runs' exposure outside the operational window yields p = 0.086; the authors therefore report it as an exploratory association, not component attribution.
- **Availability:** the small-field runs kept the workload operational for 91–93% of the beam interval; runs 3 and 5 reached 75–76%; run 4 only 21%. Seven Linux-SEFIs spanned three recovery tiers: 2 process restarts (R5), 4 reboots/board resets (R6/R7, one documented by a console photograph of a multi-pitter kernel oops with a call trace through the clone/fork path while ipython was active), and 1 power cycle (R8).
- **Campaign totals:** 92 blocks launched, 83 completed; 8300 inferences executed with class records retained for 6200 inferences in 62 blocks; 7 Linux-SEFIs and 2 output-corruption events (one F3, one F4), 4 reboots/board resets, 1 power cycle. Record retention was thinnest at 40 MeV (19 of 28 launched blocks retained class records vs 43 of 43 at 20 MeV), and 8 of the lost blocks carried corrected-OCM reports — the authors warn this biases the output-event cross sections toward too much exposure.
- **OCM ECC telemetry:** corrected-error reports at the same OCM address 0xFFFEFBA0 appeared in nine blocks spanning one reboot under both fields; ECC corrected every read (so the corrupted value never reached software), and the recurrence is consistent with either a persistent cell or repeated upsets — the record cannot distinguish them.

## Authors & Credibility

The team combines SnT (Interdisciplinary Centre for Security, Reliability and Trust, University of Luxembourg — Memon, Graczyk, Papadakis) with IFJ PAN (Henryk Niewodniczański Institute of Nuclear Physics, Kraków — Śwakoń, Grzanka, Kuśyk), the institute operating the AIC-144 proton cyclotron used for eye-cancer radiotherapy; this gives genuine beam-time access, facility dosimetry procedure, and SRIM2013 dose conversion rather than borrowed facility logs. The work builds directly on the group's own prior line: kernel-level cross-architecture proton characterization of Linux on COTS SoCs (Memon et al., arXiv:2503.03724) and 60-MeV proton testing of LPDDR2/LPDDR4 SDRAM (RADECS 2025), and the F8/F9 definition is inherited from their workload-conditioned prior study. The manuscript is a preprint submitted to IEEE Transactions on Nuclear Science — the natural venue for this community — and notably discloses that GPT-5.6 Pro and Claude were used for language editing and consistency checking only, with authors verifying all numbers, equations, and citations; this level of transparency is commendable and consistent with the paper's generally careful self-auditing style.

## Critical Assessment

The novelty is real: there was genuinely no published beam data for an open accelerator executed through a Linux-managed PS–PL path, and the episode-duration-plus-monitor-record dimension (how long corrupted state persists, what each standard monitor recorded during the event) is something prior Zynq campaigns did not report — Table XI's comparison shows Stürk et al. power-cycled after each failure (destroying persistence information), Sabogal et al. grouped consecutive errors without durations, and Agiakatsikas et al. never reported which monitors fired during events. The rigor of the self-criticism is unusually high: onset-based counting, right-censoring flags, per-monitor denominators, Garwood intervals, and a full sensitivity table (Table X) for the ambiguous run-4 record.

That said, the headline field contrast is weaker than a casual reader of the abstract might infer. The nominal p = 0.016 rests entirely on counting run 4's two blocked launches (90.4 and 91.4 min) as two onsets; merge them and p rises to 0.079, credit the minute outside the run-4 denominator and it rises to 0.02–0.086, and the weakest defensible reading gives 0.46 — the authors admit this, but the design itself cannot support attribution because run order was not randomized (wide-field runs first), field is confounded with accumulated dose, five unmonitored same-day exposures added fluence outside all denominators, and 58 MeV was never tested in the small field. The physical question the wide field was meant to probe — LPDDR4 vs SoC — is also under-instrumented on the memory side: the memtest covers only 10 MB (0.5% of the LPDDR4) and ran in only 18 of 92 blocks, so the paper's own statement that it "neither establishes LPDDR4 integrity during an output event nor localizes one" is accurate. The oracle is top-1 class only — no logits or score vectors were retained — so corruption that preserves the winning class is undetectable by construction, and the checker cannot distinguish a stuck output buffer from genuine input corruption (they show the "bird" output is not the last-correct or the all-equal-argmax artifact, which is good forensics, but still cannot localize the holding state). Finally, the paper's constructive recommendation — content checks alongside availability monitors — remains an untested hypothesis: no checksum, known-answer probe, SEM scrubbing, or ECC-on-PL-memory mitigation is actually evaluated, and the small event count (7 SEFIs, 2 output events on a single board, single model, single 10-image pool) means all cross sections are system-specific and low-statistics by the authors' own admission. One more data quirk worth noting: at 40 MeV only 19 of 28 launched blocks retained class records and 8 of the lost blocks had corrected-OCM reports, which the authors correctly flag as a bias on the output-event cross section — but it also means the 40-MeV output numbers are the least trustworthy in the paper.

## Strengths

- Statistically disciplined for a small-count beam campaign: onset-based counting with an explicit demonstration that output-level counting would overcount the largest episode by 39×, monitor-matched denominators, operational fluence (Eq. 1) with its approximation stated, Garwood intervals, one-sided upper limits for zero-event cells, and an honest refusal to claim significance (exploratory, unpreregistered, no multiplicity adjustment).
- The scheduled per-block PL reload acts as a clean experimental probe of state persistence — the constant-class event surviving a reload, and the OCM address recurring across a reboot, are genuinely diagnostic observations about where corruption can live (outside refreshed PL logic and model buffers).
- Auditable record: campaign timeline (Fig. 3), per-block indicator table (Table III), archived console photographs of the kernel oops with call trace, EXT4 directory-entry corruption, and the corrected-OCM report; class-histogram reasoning (the stuck "bird" is not index-0 argmax or the last-correct class) rules out the trivial stale-buffer explanations.
- The three reporting practices are actionable beyond this paper and directly address inconsistencies in how prior campaigns counted and grouped events (Table XI).
- Transparent LLM-usage disclosure and explicit limitations section that correctly anticipates most external criticism.

## Limitations

- Single board (Ultra96-V2), single accelerator (TCU), single model (ResNet-20), fixed 10-image CIFAR-10 pool: the cross sections apply only to this exact hardware–software configuration and protocol; no device-to-device variation, no model variation, and no CIFAR-10 accuracy-under-irradiation or flight qualification quantification.
- No component-level attribution: the wide beam exposes SoC, memory traces, clocks, and power delivery simultaneously; field is confounded with run order and accumulated dose; 58 MeV lacks a small-field counterpart; five unmonitored exposures fall outside every denominator.
- Monitor blind spots are structural: top-1 class retained (no logits), memtest covers 10 MB in 18 of 92 blocks, no intermediate tensors, no LPDDR4 traffic capture, no physical fault localization, and the USB-gadget console link means console loss could itself perturb onset counts and tier assignment.
- Recovery causality is unassignable by design: multi-action recovery sequences (reboot during beam, then beam termination and power cycle in run 4) are reported as lower bounds; two power-rail samples per block cannot exclude a single-event latch-up.
- Record losses are material at 40 MeV (19/28 blocks with class records) and run 4's ±150 s timing resolution propagates into every headline number (cross sections and the field-contrast p-value).
- No mitigation is built or evaluated — deliberately, as an unhardened baseline — but this also means the paper's central recommendation (content checks + staged recovery) has no measured overhead, coverage, or detection-latency numbers attached.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中"Inference Reliability & SDC Detection"核心方向：它用真实质子束实验证明 top-1 输出检查和全部常规可用性监视器（内核日志、内存测试、电源轨）都无法发现硬件诱导的静默输出损坏——39 个连续错误类输出在所有监视器正常的情况下发生，且损坏状态能跨 PL 重载持续。按监视器匹配截面分母、按 onset 计数、声明监视器盲区这三条报告规范，可直接迁移到 SDC 检测管线、看门狗与故障归因系统的设计；对边缘/空间场景下 COTS 加速器的 content-check 需求论证也极具参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Which exact state held the constant class — a stuck LPDDR4 weight/instruction fetch, a corrupted accumulator or constant path bypassing layers, or PS-side driver state? Would retaining full score vectors plus DMA-status and accelerator-state checkers disambiguate the candidates the paper lists?
- Would per-inference known-answer probes or score-vector checksums bound such an episode to one probe interval, and at what cadence and performance overhead on this systolic-array design?
- Does enabling SEM scrubbing or PL-memory ECC eliminate the constant-class pattern while leaving Linux-SEFI rates unchanged — i.e., are the two endpoints independent?
- With randomized field order, dose-controlled small-field runs, and complete score-vector retention, does the wide-vs-small field contrast hold at component level (LPDDR4 vs SoC), and does it reproduce at 58 MeV?
- Do the Linux-SEFI and output-corruption cross sections transfer to other open accelerators (FINN, hls4ml) or transformer workloads over the same Linux-managed PS–PL path, or are they dominated by this TCU's memory organization?
