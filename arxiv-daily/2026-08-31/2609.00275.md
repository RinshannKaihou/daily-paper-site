> **一句话总结：** 提出"不可逆性预算"——把不可逆暴露当作 OS 一等资源，可信运行时按每个外部效应残余损失的 95 分位（0.52v）在提交前对 agent→workflow→tenant 三级账本做原子 VaR 预留、超支即拒绝；$250k 容限的采购舰队仿真（300 次种子运行）中，逐效应局部闸门 300/300 全部超支（平均 2.4×，千机舰队达 48×），而预算账本零超支并把敞口稳定在 0.48× 容限，同时比面值硬帽多执行 1.8× 的有效交易。

> **TL;DR:** Fleets of tool-using LLM agents can each pass their own per-effect gate yet jointly blow through the principal's risk limit under a shared trigger. This MPI-SWS workshop paper proposes the **irreversibility budget**: a trusted runtime charges each external effect its 95th-percentile residual loss (c_0.95(e) = 0.52v) and atomically reserves it on hierarchical agent→workflow→tenant ledgers *before* commit, denying the marginal effect that would overdraw. In a 300-run discrete-event procurement simulation ($250k tenant tolerance), the **local-gates baseline overdraws in all 300 runs (2.4× tolerance on average, rising to 48× at 1,000 agents) while the budget never overdraws (95% upper bound 1.3%)** at ~0.48× occupancy; typed charging executes 1.8× the value of a face-value cap and 1.7× the strongest pooled cap. A trace study of **38,452 τ-bench/AgentDojo trajectories** supports the shared-trigger premise (22% of tool calls are external effects; one planted prompt injection drives the same external effect in 84% of a fleet on average). Honest limitation: prices are constructed, not learned — misdeclaration reopens the cap ($1.0M, 77% overdraw) and correlated recovery breaks additive charging (59% overdraw), so conservative dependency-aware pricing is the open problem.

## 核心贡献

- **不可逆暴露的资源模型（§3）**：将"不可逆性"定义为可计量的一等资源——外部效应 e 的收费 c_t(e) 是补偿/恢复/撤销穷尽后残余损失分布的 q 分位数（可为标量金额或向量，用于不可公度的数据/可用性/法律损害）；每个主体 p（agent、workflow、tenant）获得窗口化预算 B_p，不变式为：当边际收费会超出所需置信度下的预算时，主体不得外化该效应。预算耗尽程度作为"风险压力"信号驱动准入调度（类比内存压力）。
- **可信运行时机制（§4）**：(1) 风险类型化效应——工具规范声明效应类别、暴露上界、可逆路径、依赖提示，收费来自规范与可信定价服务而非模型；(2) 规范化（canonicalization）——运行时通过可信注册表把效应类绑定到工具身份/签名、API 端点/请求模式与结算回执，被攻陷的 workflow 无法把最终转账"声明"为可退款，无法识别的效应按最贵匹配类计费（节流而非静默放行）；(3) 收费生命周期——保守初始界→离线重放校准→结算回执审计→检测到依赖类时上调边际收费；(4) 层级账本与 reserve/confirm/cancel 原语（Algorithm 1：沿 agent→workflow→tenant 路径跨账本原子预留或拒绝，保证不超支、幂等确认、崩溃恢复、层级汇总）；(5) 风险压力调度——收紧置信度、降级低价值支出者、过期陈旧预留、升级到限速审计授权（记为"已批准敞口"而非提高 B）。
- **受控可行性研究（§5）**：五个 RQ 下的离散事件采购仿真（300 次种子运行、95% CI 与 Wilson 区间），证明逐效应安全的组合失败与预算账本的封顶能力，并量化规模扩展（RQ2）、活性代价（RQ3）、定价敏感性（RQ4）；账本微基准给出记账开销上界。
- **真实轨迹佐证（RQ5）**：分析 τ-bench 与 AgentDojo 共 38,452 条轨迹，用固定词表把 pay/refund/delete/update/send/create 标记为外部效应，验证"共享触发导致跨 agent 相关损失"这一核心假设。
- **诚实的两层契约**：账本无条件保证的只是"声明收费单位内不超支"（四项假设：认证主体、强一致预算权威、幂等预留生命周期、可信效应规范），不保证已实现损失有界——已实现损失界依赖尚未解决的保守、依赖感知定价。

## Key Contributions

- **Irreversible exposure as a first-class, metered resource**: charge c_t(e) = q-quantile of the residual-loss distribution after compensation/recovery/revocation (scalar for money, vector for incommensurable harms); windowed hierarchical budgets per principal with a pre-commit invariant — deny the marginal effect whose charge would exceed the budget at the required confidence level; budget depletion doubles as a "risk pressure" scheduling signal.
- **A trusted runtime combining**: risk-typed effect declarations from tool specs; runtime-side canonicalization via a registry binding effect class to tool identity, API endpoint/schema, and settlement receipts (a compromised workflow cannot declare a final transfer refundable; unresolved effects are charged at the most expensive matching class); a charge lifecycle (conservative init → offline calibration → settlement audit → dependency-driven repricing); hierarchical reserve-confirm-cancel ledgers with atomic cross-ledger reservation (Algorithm 1); and risk-pressure scheduling up to rate-limited, audited authority escalation.
- **A controlled feasibility study (300 seeded runs, discrete-event procurement simulation)** that isolates the fleet-level composition failure and shows the budget bounding exposure at every fleet size, against local gates, face-value/pooled-quantile caps, static partitions, a reactive circuit breaker, and hierarchical sub-budgets.
- **Trace evidence from 38,452 public τ-bench/AgentDojo trajectories**: external effects are 22% of tool calls, effect timing is task-determined (z up to 198 vs. an independent-agent permutation null), and a planted prompt injection propagates the same external effect across 84% of a heterogeneous fleet on average (minimum 48% over 35 injections).
- **An honest two-layer contract**: an unconditional declared-charge bound (under four stated assumptions) versus a realized-loss bound that depends on open problems — conservative, dependency-aware pricing — with failure modes quantified rather than hidden.

## Methodology

**Resource model.** An external effect's charge c_t(e) is the q-quantile of its residual-loss distribution (loss surviving after compensation, recovery, and revocation are exhausted), supplied by a pricing service from the effect specification — never from the model. Reversibility enters as a coordinate in the price rather than a binary gate. Each principal holds a windowed budget B_p replenished by a budget authority (short windows bound bursts, long windows bound campaigns); admission is value-at-risk control: an effect commits only if reserved + committed exposure stays within allocation at *every* ledger on the agent→workflow→tenant path (Algorithm 1 reserves atomically across the path or denies).

**Runtime.** Tool specifications declare effect classes, exposure bounds, reversibility paths, authority requirements, and dependency hints. A trusted registry canonicalizes the effect type from tool identity/signature, the resolved API endpoint and request schema, and settlement receipts — so type cannot be chosen by the caller, and anything unresolvable is charged at the most expensive matching class (throttled, not silently admitted). Charges start from a conservative bound, are calibrated offline by replaying traces/stress cases against realized residual loss, audited against settlement receipts, and repriced upward when the pricer detects a dependency (a correlation class declared on a workflow or a shared trigger inferred from the proposal stream), instead of repeating the independent price. Scheduling under risk pressure tightens confidence, demotes low-value spenders, expires stale reservations, and escalates to a rate-limited authority that mints a separate *audited* allocation without raising B.

**Evaluation setup.** Five RQs over a discrete-event procurement simulation mirroring the running example (Table 2): 50 agents / 1,000 ticks; proposal probability 0.0005 calm / 0.05 burst for 50 ticks; log-normal purchase values (median ~$15k); recovery fraction r ~ Beta(6,2), mean 0.75; tolerance R = budget B = $250k; 95th-pct charge c_0.95(e) = 0.52v; local baseline $50k call cap + rate limit. Baselines beyond local gates: face-value and pooled-quantile caps, static per-agent partitions, a reactive circuit breaker, hierarchical sub-budgets. Metrics: overdraw (realized exposure no authority approved exceeds R in any window), realized exposure relative to R, admitted routine/burst traffic, attacker value moved, sibling-workflow throughput, reservation latency; means over 300 seeded runs with 95% CIs and Wilson intervals. Simulator, seeds, parameters, benchmark, and trace analysis are released at github.com/mpi-dsg/irreversibility-budget. A separate ledger microbenchmark measures the reservation lifecycle in isolation. The trace study (RQ5) reads τ-bench's 165 tasks with ≥3 trials as fleets of independent agents against a permutation null, and AgentDojo's 35 planted injections with ≥20 runs as shared-trigger fleets; a fixed vocabulary tags pay/refund/delete/update/send/create as external effects.

## Results & Highlights

- **Composition (RQ1, procurement simulation, 300 runs):** local gates approve every purchase yet overdraw in **all 300 runs at 2.4× the $250k tolerance on average**; the budget overdraws in **0 runs** (95% Wilson upper bound **1.3%** on overdraw probability) and settles near **0.48× tolerance** (occupancy ≈ mean-to-quantile ratio 0.25/0.52 of B). Under a colluding fragmentation attack the budget still bounds exposure below R (P(overdraw)=0.00) where local gates hit P(overdraw)=1.00.
- **Scale and alternatives (RQ2):** local-gate overdraw grows with the fleet — safe at 10 agents, **2.4× at 50, 9.7× at 200, 48× at 1,000** — while the budget holds near **0.48× at every size**. A fragmentation attack slips the full **$1.5M** transfer past local gates to **3.9× tolerance**, but the ledger admits only **$459k** because the charge attaches to exposure, not call count; a reactive circuit breaker ends at **1.03× R** with instant observation and **2.2× R** at a fifty-tick lag because it reads loss only after the irreversible effect committed.
- **Typing and pricing (RQ4):** with honest charges the typed ledger executes **1.8× the value of a face-value cap and 1.7× the strongest pooled cap** (gain shrinks to **1.2×** as effect classes converge). Mispricing is asymmetric: underpricing fails silently (**32% of runs overdraw at half the true charge**) while overpricing only throttles (ε=4 executes 5% of proposed value). Two failures bound the result: misdeclaring colluding transfers as refundable re-opens the cap to **$1.0M with 77% of runs overdrawing**; a shared trigger depressing recovery from 0.75 to 0.25 makes additive per-effect charging overdraw in **59% of runs** — and repricing at the burst quantile needs near-instant detection (a five-tick lag in a fifty-tick burst still overdraws **39%**; ten ticks is no better than no detector).
- **Liveness (RQ3):** at B=R the budget passes routine traffic in full and admits **14%** of the correlated burst; even **B=R/2 preserves 93% of routine traffic**; but B=2R reintroduces overdraw in up to **33%** of runs. Scheduling arms recover most liveness (Table 3): fair-share admission caps attacker value at **$459k → $200k** and holds the bound against 40 colluders; per-workflow ledgers lift an innocent sibling's throughput from **48% to 89%** with a 0.6B sub-budget; rate-limited re-authorization (H=50 effects/window) restores post-burst liveness to **93%** while converting the extra **$75k** of exposure into audited rather than silent spend.
- **Trace evidence (RQ5, 38,452 τ-bench + AgentDojo trajectories):** external effects account for **22% of tool calls**; individual agents' effects are sparse and end-loaded (no agent bursts alone), but effect timing is task-determined — **z up to 198** against the independent-agent permutation null — and one planted instruction propagates the same external effect across **84% of a heterogeneous fleet on average (minimum 48%)**, exactly the structure additive charges fail to price.
- **Ledger overhead (microbenchmark):** a reserve-then-confirm across the three-level path costs **2.6 μs at the median** and **240 bytes per live reservation**, with a shared tenant root under 32 threads sustaining a few ×10^5 reservation cycles/s — explicitly an in-memory, single-host, no-persistence bound on accounting work, not a durable authority's cost.

## Authors & Credibility

Bardia Mohammadi and Laurent Bindschaedler are at the Max Planck Institute for Software Systems (MPI-SWS), a top systems research group, and this paper is the continuation of a visible research agenda on trustworthy agentic systems: the first author is an author of Atomix (transactional tool use for agentic workflows, ICLR 2026 "Agents in the Wild" workshop, ref [17]) and of the AgenticOS 2026 position paper "Irreversibility in Agentic OSes" (ref [13]); Bindschaedler has prior work on trusted workflows ("Don't Trust the Cloud", PACMI 2026, ref [12]) and bounded-reserve settlement for smart-contract side effects (BCCA 2026, ref [5]). The mechanism design draws on solid systems lineage (escrow transactions, sagas/TCC, resource containers). Caveats: this is a **workshop paper** (2nd Workshop on OS Design for AI Agents / AgenticOS 2026, Prague — non-archival), so it has not been through full conference review; several of the closest related systems it contrasts against (Atomix, trusted workflows) are the authors' own, and the evaluation is a self-built simulator with a promised artifact release (github.com/mpi-dsg/irreversibility-budget), not yet an independently verified one.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline RQ1 result is partly built-in.** With honest 95th-percentile charges and reservation-before-commit, a ledger *must* hold the declared-charge bound — that is escrow accounting (the paper itself cites O'Neil's escrow method [19]) applied to risk units, so "0 overdraws in 300 runs" mostly confirms the arithmetic. The genuinely informative results are RQ2 (the gap grows with fleet size, which per-effect controls structurally cannot see) and RQ4 (where the bound breaks). To the authors' credit, RQ4 is presented prominently rather than buried.
- **Baseline strength is uneven.** The primary "local gates" baseline is definitionally blind to the running sum — close to a strawman for the composition claim. The stronger aggregate alternatives (pooled-quantile caps, hierarchical sub-budgets) appear mainly in Table 3 / RQ2 with thinner analysis, and there is no baseline with *learned* or externally calibrated prices, because none exists — which is itself the paper's honest open problem.
- **Safety is measured optimistically by construction.** Prices are "constructed rather than learned" (the authors say so in §6): residual loss is a Beta-distributed recovery fraction times purchase value with a known 95th-percentile multiplier (0.52v). Real charges would carry estimation error, adversarial misdeclaration (shown to reopen the cap to $1.0M, 77% overdraw), and unmodeled correlation (recovery 0.75→0.25 → 59% overdraw). The unconditional guarantee covers declared charges only — a narrow contract the abstract's "holds every correctly charged run" phrasing quietly depends on.
- **The trace study is the weakest quantitative link.** External-effect labeling uses a fixed keyword vocabulary (pay, refund, delete, update, send, create) with no reported label-noise estimate; τ-bench "fleets" are independent *trials of the same task*, so the striking "z up to 198" largely measures task-determinism of effect types, not adversarial shared triggers among concurrently running agents. The AgentDojo planted-injection result (84% average propagation, 35 injections) is the on-point evidence, but the corpus is small and curated (injections with ≥20 runs only).
- **Deployment feasibility is unproven.** The microbenchmark is in-memory, single-host, no persistence/replication/crash recovery; a durable budget authority needs a log write per spend and, across hosts, a round of coordination on any violating spend (the CALM-style trade-off the authors flag). The 2.6 μs number therefore says little about end-to-end latency or availability under partition, and the paper does not exercise refunds, capability delegation, vector charges, or the full reserve-confirm-cancel lifecycle.
- **Choice of VaR is asserted, not argued.** Value-at-risk is not subadditive (they cite Artzner et al. [2] themselves), so summing per-ledger VaR across principals can understate tail risk; coherent alternatives (expected shortfall) are named but untested, and budget-size selection ("as useful as the authority's choice of B") is left as a business problem.
- **Scale of the toy vs. the claim.** Everything is dollar-denominated procurement with ~$15k median purchases; the paper's own discussion concedes vector budgets for data/availability/reputational harm are needed but unimplemented. The "48×" headline comes from a 1,000-agent fleet, 20× the nominal setting.

## Strengths

- The composition failure is real, crisply stated, and quantified at multiple scales (2.4× → 9.7× → 48× as the fleet grows 50 → 200 → 1,000 agents), with a structural explanation (no per-call gate can see the running sum).
- RQ4 is exemplary honesty: it quantifies exactly when the mechanism fails — silent underpricing (32% overdraw at 0.5× charge), refundable misdeclaration ($1.0M, 77%), correlated recovery (59%), detection lag (39% at five ticks) — and derives the design rule that charges should fail conservative (visible throttling, not silent overdraw).
- The two-layer contract (unconditional declared-charge bound vs. conditional realized-loss bound) is stated explicitly, and §6 names its own biases (liveness measured pessimistically without refunds; safety optimistically with constructed prices).
- Practical liveness engineering: per-workflow ledgers (48%→89% sibling throughput), fair-share admission ($459k→$200k attacker value, holds vs. 40 colluders), and audited rate-limited re-authorization (93% post-burst liveness at +$75k audited exposure) show the budget is not just deny-everything.
- The shared-trigger premise is tested against 38,452 public benchmark trajectories rather than assumed, and the artifact (simulator, seeds, params, trace analysis) is released for verification.

## Limitations

- Establishes the accounting mechanism only — no deployed agent OS, no integration with a real tool-calling framework, and the full reserve-confirm-cancel lifecycle, capability delegation, vector charges, and refunds are not exercised.
- Pricing is the open core: charges are hand-constructed in a single financial domain; misdeclaration, correlated recovery, and even a five-tick repricing lag all break the realized-loss bound (77% / 59% / 39% overdraw); no calibration method or guarantee is offered.
- Distributed enforcement is unresolved: hard cross-host budgets require coordination on any violating spend; availability cost under partition is unreported; the 2.6 μs microbenchmark excludes persistence and replication.
- VaR is non-subadditive and unvalidated as the right measure; B selection is undefined; vector budgets for incommensurable harms (data, availability, legal) remain design notes.
- Evidence for the correlation premise is partial: keyword-based effect typing with no noise estimate, a permutation-null construction on τ-bench that mostly shows task determinism, and only 35 curated AgentDojo injections.
- Workshop-level peer review; the closest competing systems in Table 1 include the authors' own prior work, so comparative positioning is not independent.

## Relevance

这篇论文属于用户最高优先级方向中"长程自主智能体系统的可靠性与可信性"分支：它给出把智能体舰队的不可逆外部效应纳入 agent OS 记账与准入控制的机制框架，并用仿真与公开轨迹数据量化了"逐效应安全不等于舰队安全"的系统性风险，对理解自主智能体基础设施的故障面与资源治理很有价值。它与自进化智能体的训练/内省方法无直接交集，但若长期运行的自主研究智能体要对外执行不可逆操作，这类预算式准入是必要的底层保障。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can residual-loss charges be *learned* from settlement receipts with a calibration guarantee that pricing errors fail conservative (visible throttling) rather than silently (overdraw)?
- Does a coherent risk measure such as expected shortfall change the cross-principal aggregation result, given VaR's non-subadditivity across hierarchical ledgers?
- What is the measured latency/availability cost of a durable, coordinated budget authority across hosts — and where exactly does the CALM boundary fall between hard and advisory budgets in practice?
- Can shared-trigger detection run early enough to reprice *before* the burst commits exposure (five-tick lag already leaves 39% overdraw), or is dependency-aware pricing inherently reactive?
- How do vector budgets over incommensurable harms (money, data loss, availability, legal exposure) change the liveness trade-off observed in the scalar financial setting?
- How would the registry-based canonicalization behave against real prompt-injection defenses and tool-wrapper ecosystems — and what happens when the pricing service itself is compromised or slow?
