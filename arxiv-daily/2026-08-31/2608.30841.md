> **一句话总结：** 提出 HSRM——一个仅约 2M 参数的隐状态奖励模型：不再把候选解文本重新编码一遍，而是直接在推理步边界读取冻结生成器（Qwen3 1.7B–14B、Llama-3.2）的内部隐状态，经逐层投影 + 2 层小 Transformer 编码后打分做 best-of-N 选择；在 GSM8K/MATH-500/AIME/OlympiadBench 四个基准上以 ~2M 参数在 16 个生成器×数据集组合中的 15 个追平或超过 55M 参数的文本式能量验证器 EORM，验证计算量比 7B PRM 低约 5 个数量级。

> **TL;DR:** HSRM is a ~2M-parameter verifier for best-of-N math reasoning that scores candidate solutions by reading the frozen generator's hidden states at reasoning-step boundaries (projected through a 2-layer Transformer encoder), instead of re-encoding the generated text with a separate model. Trained only from self-generated trajectories with final-answer outcome labels (tie-safe pairwise ranking loss), it matches or beats the 55M-parameter text-only energy verifier EORM on 15 of 16 generator–dataset settings (e.g., 48.4 vs 40.3 best-of-8 accuracy on GSM8K with Llama-3.2-1B, +8.1 pts) and nearly matches the ~7.6B Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B while using roughly five orders of magnitude fewer verification FLOPs per candidate. Ablations show the win comes from the hidden-state input, not capacity: on GSM8K (Qwen3-1.7B) step-boundary hidden states reach 86.86% / 0.724 within-problem AUROC vs 82.23% / 0.519 for a text-only encoder of the same recipe.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 HSRM 架构**：一个轻量级隐状态奖励模型——在推理步分隔符（`\n\n`、`.\n`、`;\n` 等）处提取冻结生成器最后层（或多层拼接）的残差流隐状态，经线性投影 + 2 层 Transformer 编码器（d=256，4 头）+ 均值池化 + 线性读出，为每个候选解输出标量分数；每个生成器规模的验证器仅 2.12M–3.4M 参数。
- **免人工过程监督的训练方案**：仅用生成器自采样轨迹 + 最终答案对错标签，采用"tie-safe"成对排序损失（只要求正确候选得分高于错误候选，同一问题内多个正确候选互为平局，不施加任意全序）；每题采 64 个候选、150 题量级训练池即可训练，无需额外生成器前向。
- **系统性实证**：跨 4 个数学基准 × Qwen3（1.7B/4B/8B/14B）+ Llama（1B/3B/8B）共 16+6 个设置，~2M 参数在 15/16 个设置上追平或超过 55M 文本式验证器 EORM，并接近领域训练的 Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B（约 7.6B 参数），验证成本约低 5 个数量级。
- **消融定位收益来源**：输入模态消融显示收益来自隐状态输入本身而非验证器容量（DeepSet / d=128 / d=256 编码器性能相当）；层消融显示正确性信号分布在多个高层而非仅最终层，top-4 层拼接最佳；thinking 模式下只读 `</think>` 之后的表征显著优于读全程轨迹（AUROC 0.672→0.884）。

## Key Contributions

- **HSRM architecture**: a lightweight hidden-state reward model that extracts frozen-generator residual-stream states at reasoning-step boundaries, projects them per step, runs a 2-layer Transformer encoder (d_model=256, 4 heads, ~2M parameters total), mean-pools, and applies a linear readout to produce a candidate score — no re-tokenization or re-encoding of generated text.
- **Supervision-free verifier training**: trained on self-generated trajectories (64 candidates per problem) with binary final-answer outcome labels and a tie-safe pairwise (Bradley–Terry-style) ranking loss that treats all correct candidates as ties; no human-written process supervision, no pretrained verifier, no extra generator forward passes.
- **Broad empirical evidence**: across GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME, and OlympiadBench with Qwen3 (1.7B–14B) and Llama (1B–8B) generators, HSRM matches or outperforms a 55M-parameter text-only energy verifier in 15 of 16 settings and approaches the domain-trained ~7.6B Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B at roughly five orders of magnitude lower verification cost.
- **Ablation-driven attribution**: the advantage comes primarily from the input representation (hidden states) rather than verifier capacity; correctness information is distributed across multiple upper generator layers; for thinking-mode generators, post-`</think>` representations are far more predictive than the full deliberation trace; a hybrid text+hidden-state model does not improve over hidden-states-only.

## Methodology

**Step-boundary hidden-state extraction.** For a candidate solution y = (y1..yT) generated by a frozen generator, HSRM does not use all token-level states. It segments the chain-of-thought at step delimiters (`"\n\n"`, `".\n"`, `";\n"`, `"\n\n\n"`, `"\n\n\n\n"`), collects the last-layer residual-stream states at the S token positions immediately preceding each boundary (plus the final token), giving H ∈ R^{S×d_gen} with S ≤ 100. This both compresses the input (S step vectors instead of T tokens) and, crucially, requires zero additional generator forward passes since these states are already produced during decoding.

**Verifier architecture.** Each step state is linearly projected (W_in ∈ R^{d_model×d_gen}), the projected sequence Z^(0) is encoded by a small Transformer encoder (2 layers, d_model=256, 4 attention heads, dropout 0.1), representations are mean-pooled over steps and layer-normalized, and a single linear head maps to a scalar score. Only the input projection depends on the generator (d_gen = 2048–5120); the encoder body is shared. Total size: 2.12M/2.25M/2.65M/~3.4M parameters for Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B/14B.

**Training objective.** For each training problem, N=64 candidates are sampled once and cached with binary final-answer correctness labels. A tie-safe pairwise ranking loss L = (1/|P||N|) Σ_{i∈P} Σ_{j∈N} log(1 + e^{-(s_i - s_j)}) pushes every correct candidate above every incorrect one without imposing any ordering among candidates sharing a label; problems with no correct or no incorrect candidate are skipped. Training uses AdamW (lr 1e-4), 1000 gradient steps, problem-level early stopping, 5 seeds.

**Evaluation setup.** Generators: Qwen3 1.7B/4B/8B/14B in non-thinking mode (float16, temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9), plus Qwen3 thinking mode on MATH-500, plus Llama-3.2-1B/3.2-3B/3.1-8B for cross-family checks. Benchmarks: GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME, OlympiadBench, with per-dataset verifier training pools (e.g., 500 GSM8K / 150 MATH-500 / 20 AIME / 150 OlympiadBench problems) disjoint from eval splits. Metrics: verifier best-of-8 accuracy (primary), within-problem AUROC, oracle pass@8; mean ± std over 5 seeds. Candidate labels come from a two-stage procedure: deterministic regex/symbolic answer matching, then an LLM judge for ambiguous cases (judge changed 7,788 of ~255K labels, net +7,512, ~3.1%, mostly recovering correct answers in non-canonical formats). Baselines: EORM 55M (authors' re-implementation of the energy-based verifier as a text reader, 53.4M params), Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B (~7.6B off-the-shelf PRM), and non-learned heuristics (cumulative/mean log-probability, negative entropy/varentropy, length, step count).

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline comparison (Bo8 accuracy)**: HSRM (2.12–3.4M params) matches or outperforms EORM 55M in 15 of 16 generator–dataset settings across Qwen3 and Llama generators. Cross-family gains are largest at small scale: on GSM8K with Llama-3.2-1B, HSRM reaches 48.4% vs 40.3% for EORM (+8.1 pts, oracle 72.2%); Llama-3.2-3B 81.1% vs 75.1% (+6.0); on MATH-500, HSRM 30.1% vs 26.5% (+3.6), 47.9% vs 43.4% (+4.5), 56.8% vs 52.3% (+4.5). On GSM8K with Qwen3 generators, HSRM nearly matches Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B despite using ~2M vs ~7.6B parameters; the 7B PRM retains a clear advantage only on AIME, the hardest benchmark.
- **Input-modality ablation (GSM8K, Qwen3-1.7B, Table 1)**: step-boundary hidden states from the top-4 layers achieve 86.86 ± 0.74% Bo8 accuracy and 0.724 ± 0.009 within-problem AUROC, vs 82.23 ± 0.29% and 0.519 ± 0.009 for a text-only verifier that encodes the raw solution text with the same recipe (+4.63 pts, +0.205 AUROC). Even reading only the final step representation of the final layer gets 86.11% / 0.691. The hybrid text+hidden model (86.23% / 0.707) does not beat hidden-states-only — raw text adds nothing on top of internal representations.
- **Efficiency (Fig. 4, Table 11)**: HSRM lies on the upper-left accuracy-vs-cost frontier, matching the accuracy of EORM-55M and Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B across generator scales at roughly five orders of magnitude lower verification FLOPs per candidate. Concretely: HSRM 2.1–3.4M params (~4–7MB fp16, input = S ≤ 100 hidden vectors, 0 extra generator passes) vs EORM 55M (53.4M, ~107MB, re-encodes T tokens) vs Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B (~7.6B, ~15GB).
- **Thinking mode (MATH-500, Table 2)**: restricting extraction to post-`</think>` representations beats full-trace extraction on within-problem AUROC — Qwen3-1.7B: 0.736 ± 0.034 vs 0.669 ± 0.027 (accuracy 83.67% vs 82.00%); Qwen3-4B: 0.884 ± 0.023 vs 0.672 ± 0.044 (accuracy 87.00% vs 86.33%). Non-thinking baselines are far below (67.14% / 77.62% accuracy), so thinking helps generation, but reading the deliberation trace itself hurts verification.
- **Training-objective ablation (Qwen3-1.7B, Table 9)**: the tie-safe Bradley–Terry loss beats both alternatives — GSM8K: 85.93 ± 0.56% / 0.718 AUROC vs ListMLE 84.59 ± 1.41% / 0.694 vs pointwise BCE 83.91 ± 0.45% / 0.687; same ordering on MATH-500 (66.91% vs 66.69% vs 66.57%).
- **Scaling and transfer**: growing the verifier training pool from 25 to 500 GSM8K problems raises Bo8 accuracy 83.4% → 86.2% and AUROC 0.612 → 0.716; verifiers trained only on GSM8K or MATH-500 transfer zero-shot to OlympiadBench, consistently beating EORM 55M and staying close to Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B (the residual gap there is mostly to the oracle, not to other verifiers). HSRM also beats the best non-learned heuristic (chosen optimistically per cell) in 12 of 16 settings — but loses to cumulative log-probability on AIME 8B (17.7% vs 19.3%, −1.6) and ties on two AIME cells.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper comes from Xianzhi Li and Xiaodan Zhu of the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering & Ingenuity Labs Research Institute at Queen's University (Canada); Zhu's group has a track record in LLM reasoning, interpretability, and knowledge-in-model research. The work is methodologically careful for its scale: 5 random seeds with mean ± std everywhere, disjoint problem-level train/eval splits with indices published in the appendix, a documented two-stage labeling procedure with relabeling statistics (~3.1% changed by the LLM judge), honest reporting of where HSRM loses (AIME, saturated MATH-500 cells, 2 ties with cheap heuristics), and an optimistic-envelope design for the cheap-scorer comparison that favors the baselines. Code is released at github.com/JXL884/HSRM. Limitations are stated explicitly (math-only domain; thinking-mode study limited to two scales on MATH-500). This is a solid, self-critical workshop/conference-scale paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline dichotomy rests partly on a re-implementation.** The "55M text-only verifier" that HSRM beats in 15/16 settings is the authors' own reproduction of EORM as a chain-of-thought text reader ("we reproduce a Transformer energy model over chain-of-thought text trained with a Bradley–Terry pairwise loss"). The original EORM paper's title is "Mining intrinsic rewards from LLM hidden states for efficient best-of-N sampling" — i.e., the published EORM itself operates on hidden states. If the re-implementation diverges from the original method, the central "hidden states vs re-encoded text" framing is weakened; a faithful EORM comparison is the single most important missing experiment.
- **The text-only baseline is weak by construction.** The 82.23% / 0.519 text baseline uses a small from-scratch encoder — no pretrained language model reads the text. Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B partially covers this gap, and it wins on AIME, which suggests a sufficiently strong text-side model can still beat HSRM where the task is hard. There is also no majority-voting / self-consistency baseline despite verifiable final answers making it free and strong — the cheap-scorer table includes log-prob and entropy heuristics but omits the one non-learned ensemble that is standard practice for math BoN.
- **Some cells are small and noisy.** The AIME verifier is trained on only 20 problems and evaluated on 70 problems, which explains the 7.7–22.1% accuracy regime and the instability (HSRM actually loses to cumulative log-probability there). The MATH-500 Qwen3-4B thinking cell reports 87.00 ± 0.00 accuracy across 5 seeds — exactly identical accuracy with non-trivial AUROC variance (0.884 ± 0.023) is surprising and deserves explanation. N-scaling results (only +0.0–1.2 from N=8→64) indicate many eval pools are near their oracle ceiling, which compresses the differences between verifiers and flatters all methods.
- **Genuine strengths in rigor**: the labeling audit (7,650 incorrect→correct vs only 138 correct→incorrect recoveries, with the asymmetry explained), the encoder-capacity ablation showing DeepSet d=128 (0.40M) ≈ Transformer d=256 (2.12M) — which cleanly attributes the gain to the input signal — and the honest hybrid-doesn't-help result are all well-designed and increase trust. Eval-label dependence on the same LLM judge used for training labels is a minor circularity risk, mitigated by the judge being used only for a small fraction of labels.
- **Significance**: the structural insight — cached decoding states at step boundaries already contain rankable correctness information — is valuable and quantified (AUROC 0.519 → 0.724 on GSM8K). But the requirement of white-box access to generator internals (hidden states, specific layer choices, per-generator input projections) limits applicability to open-weight models only; API-based generators are out of scope by design, and this trade-off is not deeply discussed.

## Strengths

- Striking parameter efficiency: ~2M verifier (4–7MB fp16) matching a 55M text verifier on 15/16 settings and approaching a ~7.6B domain PRM, with ~5 orders of magnitude lower per-candidate verification cost and zero extra generator passes.
- The verification signal is read for free from states computed during decoding — a clean architectural insight rather than a scale play, validated by the capacity ablation (DeepSet d=128 performs comparably).
- Unusually thorough ablations: generator layer, input modality, encoder architecture, training objective (BT vs ListMLE vs BCE), training-pool size K, N-scaling, zero-shot transfer, cross-family (Llama), and thinking-mode extraction position.
- Honest negative results reported in-line: hybrid text+hidden input does not help; cheap heuristics win on AIME; gains shrink as generators saturate GSM8K.
- Reproducibility: code released, split indices, hyperparameters, and labeling statistics all published.

## Limitations

- Math reasoning only; no evidence the approach transfers to open-ended or non-verifiable domains where outcome labels are harder to obtain automatically.
- Main experiments use non-thinking mode; the thinking-mode study covers only two model scales on MATH-500, and the post-`</think>` finding needs broader validation.
- Requires white-box access to generator hidden states (and per-generator input projections), so it cannot serve API-only models; the verifier must be retrained (or at least re-projected) per generator family.
- AIME results are weak and noisy (20 training / 70 eval problems), and HSRM underperforms a simple log-probability heuristic there; no process-level (step-localized) error detection is attempted — the model outputs only a candidate-level scalar.
- Baseline set omits majority-voting/self-consistency and a faithful (as-published) EORM; LLM-judge relabeling touches both train and eval labels.

## Relevance

与用户的「LLM 隐状态与可解释性」方向高度契合：论文用系统消融证明生成器内部表征在步边界处编码了可用于排序的正确性信号（AUROC 0.519→0.724），并给出信号随层分布（top-4 层拼接最佳）与 thinking 轨迹中自我修正位置的定位分析，本质上是对 LLM 隐状态中"自我知晓正确性"信号的探测与工程化利用；对推理时验证的效率-精度权衡也有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would the original, as-published EORM (which mines rewards from LLM hidden states) compare against HSRM at matched parameter counts — is the step-boundary granularity or the hidden-state access itself the deciding factor?
- Does the post-`</think>` advantage (AUROC 0.672 → 0.884 on Qwen3-4B) reflect self-correction dynamics in general, and does the same effect appear in other reasoning models with explicit deliberation (e.g., R1-style models) or in earlier intermediate layers?
- Can step-boundary scores be localized into per-step error detection (PRM-style), or is the information only available at the trajectory level?
- What happens when the verifier is trained on one generator family's hidden states and applied to another's (the paper transfers across datasets but always within a generator)?
- Would combining HSRM with majority voting close the remaining gap to the oracle on hard benchmarks like AIME and OlympiadBench, where the pool often contains a correct candidate that no verifier selects?
