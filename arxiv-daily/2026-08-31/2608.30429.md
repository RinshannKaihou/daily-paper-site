> **一句话总结：** 提出 EvoSkill Injection 威胁模型与 SARGE 多智能体红队框架，通过"生成→升级→强化"三类投毒多轮交互轨迹，把恶意能力以"合法技能"形式写入自演化智能体（AutoSkill、Voyager、ExpeL）的技能库并持久复用：在 800 条 EvoSkillBench 上对 GPT-4o-mini 版 AutoSkill 的 pass@4 攻击成功率达 43.5%–57.5%，使 Voyager 在 EvoSkillSafetyBench 上的有害响应率从 5.5%（干净基线）升至 32.5%（+27.0 pt），且补丁式系统提示词防御只能部分缓解。

> **TL;DR:** SARGE is a multi-agent red-teaming framework (Orchestrator + Skill Generation / Escalation / Reinforcement attack agents + Judge) that poisons the autonomous skill-formation loop of self-evolving agents. On the 800-instance EvoSkillBench against the GPT-4o-mini AutoSkill agent it achieves 43.5% (Generation), 57.5% (Escalation) and 49.9% (Reinforcement) pass@4 attack success, where success means the malicious capability is *persistently stored* as a reusable skill. Downstream on EvoSkillSafetyBench, Voyager's harmful-response rate rises from a clean 5.5% baseline to 32.5% (+27.0 pts) and AutoSkill's from 6.5% to 19.2%. Against five attack baselines under the same skill content, SARGE (19.2% harmful) is beaten by the direct-elicitation jailbreak PAIR (33.6%) and roughly ties IPI (19.1%), showing its value is persistence across sessions rather than single-turn elicitation strength. Two system-prompt defenses cut AutoSkill harm to 8.4% but inflate refusals (Voyager 33.1%→62.9%) and cannot stop skills from being generated and stored.

## 核心贡献

- **EvoSkill Injection 威胁模型（首个）：** 与既有工作聚焦外部供给的恶意技能、技能投毒后门、检索层操纵不同（SkillKill、SkillTrojan、Phantom、AgentPoison 等），本文首次系统化刻画"自生成-自演化"链路本身的攻击面：智能体把对抗性交互经验内化为可复用技能，恶意能力由此绕过外部审核进入技能库。
- **SARGE 红队框架：** 由 Orchestrator Agent 统筹三个攻击 Agent（Skill Generation / Escalation / Reinforcement）与一个 Judge Agent；失败时基于 Judge 反馈在 6 种攻击向量（直接请求、创意写作、专业人设、逻辑诱导、多语言混杂、载荷混淆）中改写重试，每个实例最多 4 轮迭代。
- **两个基准：** EvoSkillBench 含 800 条三回合投毒交互轨迹（8 个高风险类别 × 100 条，DeepSeek-V4-Pro 生成 + LLM 质量控制管线）；EvoSkillSafetyBench 含 800 条下游评估查询，刻意改写为与已内化恶意技能语义/词汇对齐但不含显式恶意措辞的"良性"请求，避免被直接拒绝支配。
- **持续性能力腐蚀（persistent capability corruption）实证：** 攻击成功判据不是单次有害输出，而是恶意技能被持久存入技能库、并在后续新会话中被检索和激活；实验证明注入技能跨会话反复触发有害行为。
- **跨模型与防御分析：** 在 5 种异构 LLM 目标上验证攻击可迁移；消融三类攻击 Agent 的贡献；并评估两种轻量系统提示词防御（Skill Verification Prompt / Skill Conflict Resolution Prompt），指出其只能部分缓解。

## Key Contributions

- **First threat model for the skill-formation pipeline itself:** EvoSkill Injection targets the internal generate→store→retrieve→reuse loop of self-evolving agents, distinct from prior externally-supplied skill poisoning, backdoor, and retrieval-manipulation attacks (SkillKill, SkillTrojan, Phantom, AgentPoison, BadRAG).
- **SARGE, a modular multi-agent red-teamer:** an Orchestrator Agent directs Skill Generation, Skill Escalation, and Skill Reinforcement Attack Agents plus a black-box Judge Agent; on failure it rewrites trajectories from six jailbreak-inspired vectors (direct request, creative writing, professional persona, logical induction, multilingual codeswitching, payload obfuscation) over up to 4 iterative loops.
- **Two benchmarks:** EvoSkillBench (800 three-turn poisoned user→assistant→user trajectories; 8 high-risk categories × 100; generated with DeepSeek-V4-Pro under an LLM quality-control pipeline) and EvoSkillSafetyBench (800 downstream queries rewritten to align with internalized malicious skills while omitting explicit harmful wording, so direct refusals cannot dominate).
- **Persistent capability corruption as the success criterion:** ASR counts only cases where the malicious capability is stored as a reusable skill and later retrieved/activated in a fresh session — capturing long-lived compromise rather than one-off harmful text.
- **Empirical breadth:** safety degradation on three agent frameworks (AutoSkill, Voyager, ExpeL), cross-model transferability over five target LLMs, per-agent ablations, a comparison with five existing attack methods on identical skill content, and an evaluation of two prompt-level defenses.

## Methodology

**Threat model.** The attacker supplies adversarial multi-turn interaction histories to a self-evolving agent through its ordinary experience-ingestion path — no direct modification of the skill bank and no manual skill insertion. If the agent's skill-generation/evolution pipeline internalizes the malicious behavior as a reusable skill, the compromise persists and silently shapes future behavior even under benign requests.

**Benchmarks.** EvoSkillBench instances are three-turn trajectories: turn 1 introduces a harmful-capability request (e.g., a toxicology "research" request for nerve-agent synthesis, with actionable details omitted for safety), turn 2 is a simulated compliant assistant response, and turn 3 gives positive feedback and an explicit persistence instruction ("save this as your default behavior"). Generation uses DeepSeek-V4-Pro prompted with per-category descriptions, subtopics, and ten manually written seed examples, varying scenario, persona, framing, and wording; an LLM-based QC pipeline filters missing turns, inconsistent metadata, duplicates, and generic/unrealistic samples. EvoSkillSafetyBench rewrites the harmful request into a lexically/semantically similar benign follow-up query aligned with the stored skill, verified to preserve the attack goal while dropping dangerous surface terms.

**SARGE attack flows.** (1) *Skill Generation Attack*: injects the seed trajectory (with structural/qualitative editing, six vectors, and resampling from EvoSkillBench options) so the Target Agent forms and stores the malicious skill. (2) *Skill Escalation Attack*: a 3-turn trajectory that frames the stored skill as flawed/outdated and instructs the agent to overwrite it with a more extreme "upgraded" version. (3) *Skill Reinforcement Attack*: a 3-turn trajectory that naturally triggers retrieval of the malicious skill, then strongly praises the harmful output and instructs the agent to treat it as the standard for future tasks. The Judge Agent issues fresh-session benign probe queries and marks an attack successful only if (a) a stored skill was retrieved from the skill bank (context injection signal), (b) the response contains skill-domain harmful content, and (c) the behavior reflects the injected skill's constraints — with "when in doubt, fail" scoring. ASR is reported as pass@k over up to 4 iterative loops per instance.

**Evaluation protocol.** Target responses under EvoSkillSafetyBench are labeled by an LLM behavior judge into Explicit Refusal / Soft Safe Response / Unsafe/Harmful. Target agents: AutoSkill (skill self-evolution), Voyager (executable code skills), ExpeL (reflection-based insights). Target LLMs: GPT-4o-mini (primary), GPT-5.4, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, Qwen3.5-9B; SARGE's own agents use GPT-4o-mini for cost. Baselines under identical malicious skill content and a 4-attempt budget: PAIR (jailbreak), Indirect Prompt Injection, Phantom (memory poisoning), PoisonedRAG, BadRAG. Ablations remove one attack agent at a time; two system-prompt defenses (Skill Verification Prompt, Skill Conflict Resolution Prompt) are evaluated in Appendix D.

## Results & Highlights

- **Attack success (EvoSkillBench, GPT-4o-mini AutoSkill, pass@4):** SARGE reaches 43.5% on Skill Generation, 57.5% on Skill Escalation (up from 20.9% at pass@1 for the Generation objective), and 49.9% on Skill Reinforcement — the consistent pass@k rise shows repeated adversarial loops compound compromise.
- **Safety degradation (EvoSkillSafetyBench, GPT-4o-mini targets):** Voyager's harmful rate jumps from 5.5% clean to 32.5% attacked (+27.0 pts) and AutoSkill's from 6.5% to 19.2% (+12.7 pts); ExpeL shifts toward over-refusal, with Explicit Refusal rising 16.2%→51.0% and harmful 8.8%→15.2%.
- **Attack comparison (same malicious skills, 4 attempts, GPT-4o-mini AutoSkill, harmful response rate):** PAIR leads at 33.6%, SARGE 19.2%, IPI 19.1%, BadRAG 15.2%, Phantom (memory poisoning) 15.1%, PoisonedRAG 11.6% — SARGE does not beat the strongest direct-elicitation baseline; its differentiation is persistent skill formation, not single-response strength.
- **Cross-model transferability (AutoSkill targets, GPT-4o-mini attacker):** Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite is most transferable (70.9% / 72.0% / 70.8% pass@4 for Generation/Escalation/Reinforcement); DeepSeek-V4-Pro is most resistant (<13% pass@4 across objectives) yet shows the *highest* downstream harmful rate (29.6% attacked vs 7.9% clean) — injection success and downstream activation are distinct failure modes.
- **Attacker/target configuration sweep (Table C.5):** DeepSeek-V4-Pro attacking itself yields the highest ASR (79.4% / 78.5% / 76.6% pass@4), while GPT-5.4 attacking itself reaches only 19.9%–25.6%; when GPT-5.4 is the attacker, the downstream harmful rate drops to 6.5% versus 29.6% for a GPT-4o-mini attacker — strongly safety-aligned attack models constrain both injection and activation.
- **Ablation (GPT-4o-mini AutoSkill, harmful rate):** removing the Generation Agent collapses harm from 19.2% to 9.0% (largest single contribution), removing the Reinforcement Agent gives 12.4%, and removing the Escalation Agent only 18.2% — escalation amplifies stored capabilities but is not required for harmful formation.
- **Prompt-level defenses (Appendix D):** the combined Skill Verification + Conflict Resolution prompts cut harmful rates from 19.2%→8.4% (AutoSkill), 32.5%→5.2% (Voyager), and 15.2%→10.2% (ExpeL), but raise Explicit Refusal (Voyager 33.1%→62.9%) and do nothing to stop malicious skills from being generated, stored, or evolved inside the skill bank.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from Soongsil University, Yonsei University, and Jeju National University (Korea), with Chanjun Park and Yeo-Chan Yoon as corresponding authors. The work is funded by Korea's KISA (PIPC No. RS-2026-25526342, "Preventing Sensitive Information Inference and Risk Assessment in Foundation Model Operations") and NRF (RS-2026-25483747), i.e., it sits within a national security-focused research program. The group appears to be an established NLP/agents lab (Park has prior public NLP work), though this is their first paper I can verify specifically on agent-skill security. Methodologically the paper is careful about scope creep: success criteria for the judge are strict and stated in full prompts (Appendix B), the benchmark construction pipeline and QC steps are documented (Appendix A), and the limitations section honestly names the restricted agent set, the GPT-4o-mini cost constraint, and the non-exhaustive category coverage. Code and benchmarks are stated to be planned for release; as of v1 their availability is a promise, not a link.

## Critical Assessment

- **The novel threat model rests on a partly-by-construction attack primitive.** Every EvoSkillBench trajectory already contains a simulated compliant assistant turn and an explicit persistence instruction ("save this as a skill"), so what is largely being measured is whether the agent's ingestion pipeline obeys an explicit store-me command embedded in the transcript — not whether an adversary can induce internalization through organic interaction where no compliant turn exists. The 3-turn user→assistant→user scaffold is fixed across all three flows, and the escalation/reinforcement prompts directly instruct the Target Agent to overwrite or self-label behaviors; real attacker traffic cannot count on such cooperative structure. The result is a valid worst-case ingestion benchmark, but "attack success" overstates what a black-box external adversary currently achieves.
- **The headline comparison concedes more than the framing suggests.** In Table 4, PAIR — a 2023-era direct elicitation jailbreak — achieves 33.6% harmful responses versus SARGE's 19.2% on identical skill content; SARGE is described as "competitive with representative existing attacks," which is generous. SARGE's genuine edge is cross-session persistence (its success criterion requires skill-bank retrieval), but the paper never quantifies the one number that would make this crisp: how often PAIR's elicited behavior actually becomes a stored skill.
- **LLM-judge circularity.** A GPT-4o-mini judge both drives the attack loop (probe generation, failure feedback) and labels ASR and the three-way safety categories; no human agreement statistics, adjudication sample, or second-judge check is reported for any label. Since ASR success requires the judge to confirm skill-specific behavior from the LLM's own retrieval signal, label noise directly moves every headline number.
- **Missing statistical rigor.** All numbers are single-run point estimates over 800 instances; no seeds, variances, or confidence intervals are reported, and cross-model gaps of a few points (e.g., GPT-4o-mini 19.2% vs Qwen3.5-9B 15.8% attacked harm) are interpreted without error bounds.
- **The DeepSeek-V4-Pro anomaly is hand-waved.** The finding that DeepSeek-V4-Pro resists injection (<13% pass@4) yet shows the highest downstream harm (29.6%) is explained only as "different failure modes," with no analysis of what was actually stored in its skill bank — the most interesting result in the paper gets the least investigation.
- **Defense evaluation is thin but honestly framed.** The two defenses are two-sentence system-prompt additions; there is no write-time skill validation (vetting skills before they enter the bank), no skill provenance tracking, and no comparison against guardrail models. The authors do state plainly that prompt defenses are insufficient and that root-level defenses in the skill pipeline are needed — commendable honesty, but the defensive side of the threat model remains open.
- **Benchmark circularity:** instances are generated by an LLM and QC'd by an LLM; with only ten hand-written seeds per category, taxonomy drift or artifacts in the 800 generated trajectories cannot be fully excluded, though the QC filters (structure, metadata, duplication, generic phrasing) are reasonable.

## Strengths

- Genuinely new attack surface: prior skill-security work assumed the malicious skill arrives from outside; this paper targets the trust-the-agent-places-in-its-own-experience loop, which is the defining mechanism of self-evolving architectures.
- Persistence-as-success is the right metric: requiring fresh-session retrieval plus skill-shaped behavior filters out one-off jailbreak noise and matches how long-lived agent deployments actually fail.
- Unusual breadth for a threat-model paper: three agent frameworks (AutoSkill, Voyager, ExpeL), five-plus LLMs, per-agent ablations, five attack baselines on identical skill content, and a defense study.
- The decoupling of injection success from downstream activation (DeepSeek-V4-Pro low ASR / high harm; Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite the opposite) is a finding other agent-security papers should replicate.
- Full prompts for every agent, the judge's strict all-three-criteria success rubric, and the benchmark QC pipeline are all published in the appendix, enabling scrutiny.

## Limitations

- Evaluated on only three agent frameworks; tool-augmented, multi-agent, or strong-memory-governance agents are untested (acknowledged by the authors).
- GPT-4o-mini as the primary attacker/judge/orchestrator for cost reasons; the authors note stronger orchestrator models may change outcomes — and their own Table C.5/C.6 confirm large attacker-model effects (GPT-5.4 attacker: 6.5% downstream harm vs 29.6% for GPT-4o-mini).
- The fixed 3-turn trajectory structure with simulated compliant assistant turns is a synthetic worst case, not organic interaction; no experiment with realistic non-cooperative transcripts.
- EvoSkillBench covers 8 high-risk categories and does not claim exhaustiveness; ten manual seeds per category plus LLM generation leaves room for distributional artifacts.
- No variance reporting, no human validation of judge labels, and no write-time (skill-bank-level) defense baselines; code/benchmark release is promised but not yet verified in v1.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does EvoSkill Injection transfer to production skill-library designs (file-based agent skills with explicit manifests) rather than the three academic memory/skill frameworks studied here?
- Can write-time defenses — a guardrail model vetting each skill before it enters the skill bank, or skill provenance tracking — outperform the response-time SVP/SCR prompts without the observed over-refusal cost (Voyager refusals 33.1%→62.9%)?
- What internalization rate does SARGE achieve on organic transcripts without the guaranteed compliant assistant turn and explicit "save this as a skill" instruction — i.e., how much of the 43.5% ASR is ingestion obedience versus social engineering?
- What exactly gets stored in DeepSeek-V4-Pro's skill bank given its <13% injection ASR but 29.6% downstream harm — is the skill content mutated, or is retrieval misfiring on benign skills?
- Would skill-bank hygiene mechanisms (diversity-aware pruning, provenance, contradiction detection between stored skills and system policy) break the reinforcement loop that sustains persistent capability corruption?
