Critical edge

Evidence stops at 0.6B–1B models trained from scratch for 100M tokens — roughly two orders of magnitude below the production regimes where SDC actually bites; whether Q/K dominance holds at 7B–70B is untested.

Critical Assessment

01 Methodology — characterize, then protect each regime

  • Characterize. Inject transient faults (4 random bit flips per element) into every module's forward activations and backward gradients; score damage with D_Maximum / D_final, normalized by each clean run's nondeterminism envelope so a value >1 exceeds every clean-to-clean difference.
  • Observe two regimes. Forward vulnerability is path-dependent: Q/K-path outputs cause the only persistent final-loss deviation yet turn weakly observable after softmax/residual; V, MLP and block outputs are transient. Backward vulnerability is module-agnostic, governed by the gradient exponent distribution.
  • Protect each differently. Q/K path executed twice and verified with paired 64-bit raw/indexed XOR fingerprints (128 bits); a Residual Gain Guard compares each DDP rank's log-RMS residual gain to the median of other ranks (robust z-score, α=2.5) and discards + replays on alarm; backward, exponent-aware scaling multiplies loss by 215 and divides gradients by 215 — an algebraically identical update that relocates gradients out of bf16's bit-flip amplification region.

  • Sparse faults (10 elements/event): ΔPPL ≤ 0.004, Top-1 99.1%, and D_final lands below the clean nondeterminism envelope — statistically indistinguishable from fault-free training.
  • Exact where it matters: Q/K recomputation missed 0 direct corruptions of protected targets; all 797 missed forward events sit outside the recompute boundary.
  • Components are complementary: exponent scaling alone hits nonfinite ("found_inf") under dense faults — prevention without detection is not enough.
  • Operational hygiene: 0/200 clean false positives, 0.061% step-level FPR over full 814-step traces; k=15 stayed admissible for 74,000 consecutive Pythia-1B pretraining steps.

03 核心贡献

  • 首个跨模块、跨前向/反向的 SDC 表征，用清洁运行的非确定性包络区分瞬时损伤与持久损伤。
  • 两条关键观察：前向脆弱性路径依赖（Q/K 持久且弱可观测），反向脆弱性模块无关、由梯度指数分布主导。
  • 分区防护框架：Q/K 重算 + 128-bit XOR 指纹校验、跨 DDP rank 残差增益守卫、指数感知梯度缩放（k=15）。
  • 813 次注入协议下，稠密故障 ΔPPL 压到 ≤0.175，开销仅 1.65%–6.76%。

04 Strengths

  • Every protection component maps to a measured observation — a unusually clean characterization→design loop.
  • Q/K recomputation gives a crisp exact guarantee, not a statistical one, on the targets that matter most.
  • Deployability beyond the benchmark: calibrated thresholds, full-trace FPR accounting, 154-checkpoint stability study.
  • Exemplary honesty: missed-alarm taxonomy, clean-run envelopes, and a negative result (sparse recall) all disclosed.

05 Limitations

  • Sparse-fault recall is low (~41–43% forward; guard alone 35%/15%) — quality is carried by prevention, not detection.
  • Synthetic single-layer bit flips only; no production fault traces; bf16-specific analysis, no fp16/fp8.
  • No head-to-head with full duplication (DMR/SparTrain) despite the positioning claim; XOR collision probability unanalyzed.
  • Guard presumes DDP replication — rank-median reference does not obviously port to pipeline/tensor parallelism; Qwen overhead (6.76%) exceeds LLMFT (5.62%).

06 Questions for further exploration

  • Do Q/K dominance and the gradient-exponent story survive at 7B–70B with pretrained-weight continuation?
  • How should the Residual Gain Guard work under pipeline/tensor parallelism, where no peer rank computes the identical layer?
  • What is the analytical collision probability of the paired 128-bit XOR fingerprints — and would a third mixer close the gap?
  • Can exponent-aware scaling co-design with mixed-precision GradScaler instead of disabling auto scale adjustment?
  • How does the framework fare against real production fault traces (Meta LLMFIT) and correlated multi-bit faults?
