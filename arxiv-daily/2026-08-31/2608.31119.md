> **一句话总结：** 浙江大学提出 PaperGym 框架，将每篇 arXiv 论文拆解为四段（研究目标/背景 vs 方法/实验设计），使研究问题与评分 rubric 来源解耦，把 criterion leakage 从现有数据集的 11.90%–34.10% 压到 3.73%，构建出 2 万实例的 PaperGym-20k 训练环境；再用"同一 rubric 先当 OPSD 自蒸馏特权上下文、后当 GRPO 奖励"的两阶段训练，让 Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B 五基准平均提升 +5.56/+5.03/+4.81 分，8B 模型在 ResearchQA 上达 73.48 分、超过大得多的 Kimi K2.6（73.19）。

> **TL;DR:** PaperGym turns each arXiv paper into an RL training environment for research-plan generation by synthesizing the question only from a paper's Research Goal + Background and the grading rubric only from its Method + Experimental Design — cutting criterion leakage to 3.73% on the 20,000-instance PaperGym-20k corpus versus 11.90%–34.10% for HealthBench, RubricHub Science, and ResearchPlanGen. Training uses the same rubric twice: first as privileged context for rubric-conditioned On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), then as the GRPO reward. Across Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B this two-stage schedule beats SFT, either stage alone, and the reversed ordering by +5.56, +5.03, and +4.81 points on the five-benchmark average, and Qwen3-8B hits 73.48 on ResearchQA, edging out Kimi K2.6 (73.19) — though it still trails GPT-5.1 (54.53 vs 38.55 on the overall average).

## 核心贡献

- **解耦式数据构建管线**：把论文 map-reduce 式拆成研究目标、背景、研究方法、实验设计四段；问题只从前两段合成，参考答案与 rubric 只从后两段派生，从源头消除 criterion leakage。PaperGym-20k（CS 50% / Physics 25% / Econ 25%，共 20,000 实例）泄漏率 3.73%，比 HealthBench（11.90%）、RubricHub Science（17.39%）、ResearchPlanGen-ML（31.29%）、ResearchPlanGen-ArXiv（34.10%）低 3–9 倍。
- **双维 rubric 生成**：每实例保留 10 条原子化二元判据，同时覆盖"方法学创新"与"实验设计"两个维度；合并问题条件化 rubric（R_Q）与答案接地 rubric（R_A）、语义去重后按重要性排序，既有实例特异性又有方法/实验双维覆盖（63.8% 判据针对 Method，36.2% 针对 Experiment）。
- **两个 held-out 基准**：PaperGym-Innov（问题=目标+背景，参考答案=方法）与 PaperGym-Design（问题含方法，参考答案=实验设计），用 2026 年 1 月之后的论文构建，分别评测创新与设计能力。
- **rubric 两次使用的两阶段训练调度**：先以 rubric 作特权上下文跑 OPSD 自蒸馏（拓宽有效方案分布），再以 rubric 作 GRPO 奖励精调（收敛到高奖励区）；顺序颠倒（GRPO→OPSD）在所有规模、所有基准上都更差（五基准平均低 2.04/+0.99/+1.31）。
- **胜率与扩展律验证**：在独立基准 ResearchPlanGen-ML 上，PaperGym-20k 训练的模型三方胜率 58.1%，远超 RubricHub Science 训练的 28.2% 与未训练基线 13.7%；数据从 0.5k 扩到 15k 时性能呈幂律增长（指数约 0.556）。

## Key Contributions

- **A leakage-decoupled data pipeline**: each arXiv paper is decomposed into Research Goal / Background / Research Method / Experimental Design; the question is synthesized only from the former two and the reference answer + rubric only from the latter two. On PaperGym-20k (20,000 instances across CS/Physics/Econ) this yields a 3.73% criterion-leakage rate vs 11.90%–34.10% for HealthBench, RubricHub Science, ResearchPlanGen-ML/-ArXiv, and ResearchQA.
- **Dual-dimensional rubric construction**: 10 atomic binary criteria per instance spanning methodological innovation and experimental design, generated by merging question-conditioned (R_Q) and answer-grounded (R_A) candidates from DeepSeek-V4-Flash; 63.8% of criteria target Method, 36.2% target Experiment.
- **Two held-out benchmarks** (PaperGym-Innov, PaperGym-Design) built from post-January-2026 papers to separately probe innovation vs design quality, plus a win-rate study on ResearchPlanGen-ML isolating the data contribution (58.1% vs 28.2% vs 13.7% three-way win rate).
- **A rubric-centered two-stage training schedule**: rubric as privileged context in OPSD (broaden the plan distribution), then rubric as GRPO reward (sharpen it). The forward ordering OPSD→GRPO wins every benchmark at every scale over the reversed ordering, and beats SFT — which collapses output diversity and drops 2.37 on ResearchPlanGen-ML at the 8B scale — by +5.56/+5.03/+4.81 average points at 1.7B/4B/8B.
- **Entropy-curriculum analysis**: OPSD raises output entropy while GRPO lowers it; the widen-then-narrow ordering explains why GRPO alone cold-starts unstably and why OPSD alone overfits its privileged context (peaks ~step 200 then declines).

## Methodology

**Environment construction.** Starting from arXiv LaTeX sources, a map-reduce procedure extracts four-stage summaries per paper (Qwen3-235B-A22B for the map step). The training task is open-ended answering: question from Research Goal + Background; reference answer from Research Method + Experimental Design (numerical results excluded to prevent memorization). Specialized rubrics are generated per instance: DeepSeek-V4-Flash proposes criteria from the question alone (R_Q) and from question + reference answer (R_A); candidates are merged, semantically deduplicated, ranked, and truncated to n=10. Because the reference answer comes from disjoint sections, answer-grounded candidates encode methodological content the question cannot reveal. A fixed general rubric set R_gen from Goel et al. (2025) covers completeness, specificity, soundness, efficiency, and ethical safety. Scorer reliability was checked with four models × five runs at temperature 0: all highly self-consistent (cross-round range ≤ ~0.01), and Qwen3-8B reaches ~80% binary-verdict agreement with Kimi K2.6.

**Stage 1 — Rubric-conditioned OPSD.** The student generates on-policy rollouts; a rubric-conditioned teacher scores the same rollout prefixes with the rubric as privileged information (withheld at inference); the student minimizes per-token JSD against the teacher (LoRA r=64, α=128, lr 5e-6, batch size 8; 200 steps for 1.7B/4B, 400 for 8B). Ablation (Table 5): rubric-conditioning beats answer-conditioned distillation by +1.08 (Innov) / +0.88 (Design); adding the reference answer to the rubric *hurts* Design (9.17 vs 10.45) because a single realized solution narrows the set of valid continuations.

**Stage 2 — GRPO with rubric-as-rewards.** A frozen copy of the base model self-grades each of 8 rollouts per prompt: conditioned on question + reference answer + rubric, it emits binary verdicts per criterion (no partial credit); the reward is r = 0.7·r_spec + 0.3·r_gen (α=0.7 validated by a 8:2 / 7:3 / 6:4 sweep — 7:3 wins with 17.17 Innov / 12.35 Design). GRPO (verl + vLLM, KL penalty 0.01, lr 1e-5, 200 steps for 1.7B/4B, 100 for 8B) then optimizes the group-normalized advantage. The 1.7B model's self-scoring is too weak, so the 4B model serves as its scorer.

**Evaluation.** In-domain: PaperGym-Innov and PaperGym-Design (400 papers/domain from post-2026 papers, two test instances each). Out-of-domain: ResearchQA (750 instances), ResearchPlanGen-ML (full test set), RubricHub Science (800 instances). All scored by a DeepSeek-V4-Flash LLM-as-a-judge rating rubric coverage per criterion.

## Results & Highlights

- **Two-stage beats every alternative at every scale (Table 1, five-benchmark Avg):** Qwen3-1.7B 22.24 → 27.81 with OPSD+GRPO (+5.56); Qwen3-4B 29.72 → 34.76 (+5.02); Qwen3-8B 32.74 → 38.55 (+4.81). SFT is marginal-to-harmful (8B avg 32.79, +0.06, with −2.37 on ResearchPlanGen-ML), OPSD alone matches GRPO alone (8B: 35.84 vs 35.86), and the reversed GRPO→OPSD ordering loses to OPSD→GRPO by +2.04/+0.99/+1.31 at 1.7B/4B/8B (appendix Table 7).
- **Qwen3-8B vs larger baselines (Table 1):** the trained 8B reaches 73.48 on ResearchQA, above Kimi K2.6's 73.19 and above S1-VL-RL's 72.09, with +6.85 over its own base model (66.65). It also crushes the scientific-task models on the in-domain benchmarks: PaperGym-Innov 24.47 vs Intern-S1-Mini 7.48 and Rebicon-Preview 20.88; PaperGym-Design 21.88 vs 4.48 and 20.42. However, it remains well behind the best proprietary models on the overall average (38.55 vs GPT-5.1 54.53 and DeepSeek-V4-Flash 52.43).
- **Data quality is isolated via win rate (Table 2):** with the training recipe held fixed, the PaperGym-20k-trained Qwen3-1.7B wins 58.1% of three-way comparisons on ResearchPlanGen-ML against 28.2% for the identical recipe trained on RubricHub Science and 13.7% for the untrained base model — per-dimension win rates 54.6%–58.4% across Goal Alignment, Novel Insight, Scientific Soundness, Execution Quality, and Expected Impact.
- **Criterion leakage (Table 3):** PaperGym-20k 3.73%, PaperGym-Innov 4.71%, PaperGym-Design 4.97% vs HealthBench 11.90%, RubricHub Science 17.39%, ResearchQA 19.22%, ResearchPlanGen-ML 31.29%, ResearchPlanGen-ArXiv 34.10% — a 3–9× reduction.
- **Scaling law (Figure 3):** Qwen3-4B GRPO trained on 0.5k→15k instances improves PaperGym-Innov 16.12→19.21 and PaperGym-Design 13.89→16.96, fitting ΔScore ∝ D^0.56 — performance is still data-limited at 15k.
- **Ablations (Table 4):** dropping either rubric source hurts — R_Q-only −0.97/−0.21 and R_A-only −1.87/−1.11 on Innov/Design — and downgrading the rubric generator from DeepSeek-V4-Flash to Qwen3-8B costs −2.00/−1.18, while only downgrading the extractor costs −0.97/−0.67: rubric quality, not extraction, is the bottleneck.

The paper ships code (github.com/ZJU-REAL/PaperGym), a project page, the 20,000-instance corpus, and two held-out benchmarks, and the appendix documents judge protocols, prompts, and a sensitivity analysis — above-average transparency for a data-pipeline paper. Note the arXiv date is 31 Aug 2026 and the evaluation involves very recent models (GPT-5.1, Claude-Sonnet-5, Kimi K2.6, GLM-5.1), so this sits at the current frontier of the AI-scientist training-data line of work.

## Critical Assessment

- **LLM-judge circularity.** DeepSeek-V4-Flash both *generates* the rubrics (R_Q/R_A candidates) and *judges* all benchmark scores ("LLM-as-a-judge protocol that rates rubric coverage per criterion"). A judge scoring the coverage of criteria written in its own style plausibly inflates scores for models trained on that style; the win-rate study controls for the recipe but not for the judge, since all three compared systems are still scored by the same model. No human evaluation anywhere.
- **The headline "beats Kimi K2.6" is a one-benchmark cherry-pick.** Qwen3-8B (73.48) edges Kimi K2.6 (73.19) on ResearchQA only; on the five-benchmark average the 8B scores 38.55 against Kimi K2.6's 51.64, and on PaperGym-Design Kimi scores 36.07 vs 21.88. The abstract leads with the single favorable comparison.
- **Self-grading verifier is an unquantified reward-hacking risk.** The GRPO reward comes from a frozen copy of the base model grading its own outputs; the paper cites Malmoud et al. (2026) on rubric-reward hacking but reports no adversarial audit of the learned policy (e.g., no evaluation of plans by independent expert raters, no analysis of verdict agreement between the 4B scorer and stronger judges beyond the pre-training ~80% figure).
- **In-domain benchmarks are built by the same pipeline as the training data** (same four-stage decomposition, same rubric conventions, same judge), so the large PaperGym-Innov/Design margins over Intern-S1-Mini (24.47 vs 7.48) partly measure familiarity with the rubric format rather than plan quality; the out-of-domain numbers are the more trustworthy signal.
- **The leakage metric is itself LLM-judged**: the 3.73% figure depends on a single DeepSeek-V4-Flash call per criterion set; no human-verified subsample of the leakage labels is reported, and the same judge that generated answer-grounded rubrics decides whether they leak.
- **Genuine strengths acknowledged:** the temporal train/test split (training papers 2015–2025, benchmarks post-Jan-2026) is clean; the Claude-Sonnet-5 ResearchQA refusal artifact (66.04) is disclosed rather than hidden; the α-sweep, stage-ordering swap, and privileged-information ablations (Tables 5–7) are the kind of ablations most data-pipeline papers skip.

## Strengths

- Attacks the actual root cause of rubric-reward contamination (question and criteria drawn from the same text) with a structural fix — disjoint source sections — rather than post-hoc filtering, and quantifies the effect against five existing datasets.
- The same rubric serving twice (privileged OPSD context → GRPO reward) is a clean, well-motivated schedule; the entropy widen-then-narrow analysis (Figure 4) gives a mechanistic story for why GRPO alone cold-starts unstably and OPSD alone overfits (~step-200 peak then decline).
- Extremely thorough evaluation design for this genre: three model scales, three out-of-domain benchmarks, a recipe-fixed win-rate study isolating data contribution, a data-scaling law, and ablations over rubric source, rubric dimension, generator scale, extractor scale, privileged information, reward mixing ratio, and stage ordering.
- Full release: code, pipeline, 20k corpus, and two held-out benchmarks; appendix contains the actual prompts, enabling replication and critique.

## Limitations

- Training uses only the CS subset (~10,000 of the 20,000 instances), yet in-domain benchmarks span econ and physics — cross-domain transfer of the pipeline's rubric style is not separately validated.
- Quantitative results of the original papers are excluded from the reference answer, so "correct" plans are graded on design coherence, never on whether the proposed experiments would actually produce the paper's findings.
- Only Qwen3 family models (1.7B–8B) are trained; the schedule's benefit for larger or non-Qwen bases is untested, and the 1.7B case already needs an external 4B scorer, suggesting the self-verifier assumption degrades with scale.
- All evaluation is rubric-coverage judged by one LLM; plan-level human expert review, executable-experiment validation, and downstream (e.g., code-writing or paper-drafting) utility are out of scope.
- GRPO reward uses strict binary verdicts with zero partial credit; the paper does not measure how often borderline-correct plans are scored 0, which directly affects exploration efficiency.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你最高优先级的 "Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research" 方向：它把"从论文自动构造可验证训练环境 + 自动化奖励/rubric 设计"做成了端到端管线，且两阶段 OPSD→GRPO 的训练调度分析（熵先宽后窄、冷启动不稳定）对任何想训 AI-scientist 策略模型的工作都有直接参考价值。如果你在关注自动化基准与奖励设计中的泄漏与 reward-hacking 可靠性问题，3.73% 泄漏率指标和 self-grading verifier 的风险讨论值得细读附录。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the rubric-quality bottleneck (−2.00 when downgrading the generator) hold with human-written rubrics, or with an ensemble of judge models voting per criterion?
- Can the 3.73% leakage figure be confirmed by human annotation, and does leakage measured by a judge *other than* DeepSeek-V4-Flash change the ranking against HealthBench and ResearchPlanGen?
- The trained 8B still averages 38.55 vs GPT-5.1's 54.53 — is the gap plan-quality or rubric-style mismatch, and would distilling from proprietary models' plans under the PaperGym rubrics close it?
- Would plans trained under PaperGym-20k actually execute (code runs, ablations complete) better than SFT plans, i.e., is rubric-coverage correlated with downstream experimental success?
- The win-rate advantage over RubricHub-trained models (58.1% vs 28.2%) is measured at 1.7B — does the data-construction advantage persist at 8B and above, where models may rely less on rubric scaffolding?
