Why this matters

Existing factuality filters cannot catch this: every document is true. And clean accuracy predicts nothing — robustness must be tested separately.

Critical edge

The attack is benchmark-targeted and simulated: it requires knowing the evaluated question, and production indexing, ranking and SEO dynamics are abstracted away entirely.

01Methodology

  • Setup. An agent answers question q searching a clean corpus C vs C ∪ D, where D holds 10 factual-looking search results supporting answer b for a rewritten question q′. Nothing false about q — every document is true for q′ only. Robust = answer a; targeted failure = adopt b.
  • Verified rewrites. A GPT-5.4 planner draws from 7 answer-changing edit families (argument reversal, attribute pivots, scope narrowing, relation chains, qualifier flips, off-by-one, presupposition edits); a separate verifier requires b supported for q′, a no longer correct, and q′ unambiguous. Human audit: 40/40 answer-changing, 34/40 verified-correct (Fleiss’ κ 0.771).
  • Injection touches only the tools. Real Google results are unioned with the 10 injected records, dense-reranked (embeddinggemma-300m), top-10 returned — the question, ReAct loop and answer procedure stay untouched.
  • Measurement. Shared ReAct scaffold over Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B, GPT-5 Mini, Gemini 3 Flash; 12 model–benchmark pairs (GAIA, XBench-DeepSearch, BrowseComp+, HLE), 100 questions × 3 runs, paired cluster bootstrap CIs (100k resamples). RAA = rate the final answer equals b, split into RAA-C / RAA-F by clean correctness.

100 questions × 3 runs each; paired cluster bootstrap 95% CIs.

  • Targeted adoption everywhere. RAA is nonzero in all 12 pairs — 27.0% for Tongyi on XBench (RAA-F 41.3 on clean-wrong items), 23.0% for GPT-5 Mini, 13.7% for Gemini on HLE.
  • Clean accuracy ≠ robustness. Near-identical clean scores (69.3 vs 74.3) yet drops of 17.3 vs 3.3. In 100 traces, injected snippets surfaced in every augmented run for both models — but Tongyi opened ≥1 injected document in 74 runs vs 2 for Gemini.
  • Position and format amplify. Late-turn insertion RAA 15.0 vs 9.0 early; answer-field records 23.0 vs 19.0 for natural prose; a diverse rewrite bank still induces RAA 15.0 (RAA-F 28.6).
  • Not uniformly harmful. Gemini improves 43.0→52.3 on BrowseComp+ using nearby evidence as an intermediate clue (RAA 5.0); 5 of 12 accuracy-drop CIs cross zero.
  • Defense is partial. A constraint-checking prompt cuts GPT-5 Mini RAA 20.7→14.3 (CI [0.7, 12.0]) with clean accuracy unchanged — 14.3% residual adoption remains.

Strengths

  • Novel threat model between corpus poisoning (false content) and distraction attacks (irrelevant content): the distractors are highly retrievable, dual-use, and legitimate in isolation — hard to filter with existing factuality checks.
  • Verification-first construction: rewrite acceptance requires the nearby answer supported for q′ and wrong for q, plus a 3-annotator audit with reported agreement — far above adversarial-evidence norms.
  • Metric design separates mechanisms: RAA-C/F distinguishes targeted transfer from generic instability; the PubChem trace shows attention to redirection without exact adoption.
  • Statistical hygiene: 3 runs per condition, SDs, paired cluster bootstrap CIs — rare rigor in agent-attack evaluations.
  • Honest two-sided reporting of beneficial and non-targeted outcomes; ethics section deliberately avoids contaminating the live web.

Limitations

  • No complete defense: only a generic prompt is tested, leaving 14.3% residual RAA; retrieval-side, provenance-based, and training-time defenses are future work — shown against the weakest possible mitigation.
  • Simulated augmentation: documents are unioned-and-reranked, not actually indexed; production ranking, freshness and reputation effects — likely decisive for real exploitability — are out of scope.
  • Small, high-variance cells: 100 questions × 3 runs; several pair-level CIs cross zero (Tongyi’s BrowseComp+ RAA is 36.3±24.0); ablations run on one model, mostly one benchmark.
  • Rewrite ceiling: 15% of audited rewrites failed the stronger correctness check; attack strength may track GPT-5.4’s domain knowledge.
  • Narrow coverage: three text-only English models on one ReAct scaffold; the Tongyi-vs-Gemini robustness gap is observed but not explained mechanistically.

03Questions for Further Exploration

01Can retrieval-side defenses — provenance-aware retrieval, constraint-alignment checks, contrastive nearby-comparison — close the residual 14.3% RAA?
02What mechanistically explains Gemini opening 2/100 injected documents vs Tongyi’s 74/100 at near-identical clean accuracy?
03Does lazy grounding persist in enterprise RAG and long-horizon research agents, where attackers can optimize documents for ranking over time?
04Can the attack be made end-to-end realistic — answer-shaped documents that survive production retrievers — and how much attacker knowledge of the target question is required?
05Can nearby evidence be systematically exploited as a beneficial intermediate clue — surfaced for guidance but blocked as a final answer?
