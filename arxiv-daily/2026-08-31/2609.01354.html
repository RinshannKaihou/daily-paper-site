The published ~94% verifier accuracy describes one harness, not the task: identical inputs span 53.8%–95.2% across four graders.

“Where the Verifier Fails: A Category-Level Audit of Reward Signals in RLVR”

Esther Xin (Independent Researcher) · Sep 1, 2026 RLVR verifier reliability metamorphic testing reward noise

Key metric

41.3 pts

Self-validation spread across four math verifiers on the same 4,990 gold answers — and the two modes of the same library disagree on 49.9% of pairs.

Why this matters

Every RLVR reward and math benchmark score rests on these graders; the dominant failure is a one-line normalization fix, so verifier config must be reported.

Critical edge

All four audited graders are rule-based — the LLM-as-judge path, which fails by reward hacking rather than parsing, is entirely out of scope.

01 Methodology

  • ACertified equivalence. Apply a semantics-preserving transform T to a gold answer g: T(g) is correct by construction, so rejecting it is a provable false negative — no human adjudication, no stronger model. The dual certifies false positives.
  • B43 transforms in 14 strata, three classes: certified-equivalent (rejection scored), contract-dependent (specification ambiguity, never counted as defects), adversarial (acceptance scored).
  • CContract matrix + coverage split. Verifiers are scored only on strata they claim to handle; crashes and timeouts are distinct verdict classes — a crashing grader can never be credited with “correctly rejecting everything”.
  • DFour verifiers: math-verify in plain-expression and LaTeX-extraction modes, a DeepSeek-lineage string normalizer, and an exact → 1e-4 numeric → SymPy cascade. ANTLR4 pinned at 4.13.2; 5-second isolated subprocess budget.
  • EData: 4,990 golds from GSM8K, all seven MATH subjects, and Big-Math, plus 2,000 synthetic standard-form answers — 307,420 verdicts, CPU-only, Wilson intervals on every cell.

02 Results

Identical 4,990 certified-equivalent inputs; the published ~94% figure (Hugging Face, 2025b) sits near the top of this range.

  • String hygiene, not grammar. A trailing period or newline flips the verdict: “\frac{1}{2}” accepted, “\frac{1}{2}\n” rejected by both math-verify modes.
  • A deterministic, scale-dependent false positive. The 1e-4 relative tolerance is scale-invariant, so sympy-cascade accepts 0% of off-by-one answers below gold magnitude 104 and 100% at or above it (n = 131 / 75). The aggregate 16.0% false-positive rate completely conceals the exact threshold.
  • Same aggregates, opposite failures. sympy-cascade (12.7% residual) judges only 87.3% of inputs but is right on every one it judges; strip-string (4.8%) judges all and errs by rejecting.
  • Cross-paper accuracy is not comparable. Acceptance of identical inputs spans 0.0%–75.1% on boxed answers and 0.0%–100.0% on scientific notation — undocumented contract differences, not defects.
  • Robustness: dropping all 2,000 synthetic golds preserves the ordering (57.5 / 84.1 / 87.8 / 88.3%).

03 Strengths

  • Certified ground truth by construction eliminates the adjudication bottleneck; scales as golds × transforms × verifiers, CPU-only.
  • Actionable headline: trailing-period/whitespace normalization accounts for 93.0% of one verifier’s failures — directly contradicting the “LaTeX parsing is hard” attribution.
  • The magnitude-threshold false positive is a genuinely new, deterministic mechanism — maximally systematic, invisible in any aggregate rate.
  • Wilson intervals everywhere; the contract matrix and coverage split are methodological contributions; all artifacts released for re-analysis.

04 Limitations

  • Verifier-level only: no estimate of the effect on any model’s benchmark score, and no RLVR training runs.
  • Rule-based, open-source implementations only — no LLM judges, no closed frontier graders.
  • 40.1% of gold answers are synthetic; contract assignments are the author’s contestable judgement calls.
  • Real model outputs do not produce transform variants at equal rates, so per-stratum rates do not translate into reward-noise parameters (ρ0/ρ1).

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. How large is the end-to-end spread in reported MATH/GSM8K accuracy when a fixed corpus of real model outputs is re-scored under all four verifier configurations?
  2. Can the certified-equivalence suite be ported to LLM-based judges — and does the failure profile shift from string hygiene to semantic, reward-hackable modes?
  3. During RLVR training, does the 104 tolerance threshold create an exploitable reward niche — do policies learn to emit large-magnitude or near-tolerance answers?
  4. What fraction of the whitespace/punctuation error budget does a one-line normalizer patch remove, and do the verifier rankings survive?
  5. Do the same failure strata recur in execution-based settings — e.g., SWE-bench Verified, where 28.5% of tasks reportedly accept Docker-verified incorrect patches?
