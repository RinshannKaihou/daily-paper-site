Wrapping unmodified coding agents in a planner–coder–tester loop lifts performance +52.25% on average — without touching the harness itself.

一句话：不改 harness 内部实现，外层元 harness 组织规划–开发–测试循环，跨轮携带工件与证据双状态 —— 三基准 × 三配置平均相对提升 52.25%（最高 82.86%），70 轮自主开发出可游玩 FPS 游戏。

Harness-of-Harness: Multi-Day Autonomous Software Development with Continual Improvement

Haoyang Yan, Min-Le Su, Hangfan Zhang, Zhanhao Li, et al. · Shanghai AI Laboratory

  • coding agents
  • long-horizon autonomy
  • multi-agent orchestration
  • continual improvement
Key Metric +52.25%

mean relative gain of HoH@3 over Vanilla across 9 benchmark–configuration cells (max +82.86%); FrontierSWE Dominance 27.33% → 72.67% at HoH@10.

Why This Matters

A harness-external protocol tackles forgetting, regressions and false completion claims in long-horizon autonomous agents — adopted with zero changes to existing tools.

Critical Edge

Single runs, no significance tests on the headline tables — comparisons like +21.93 vs +22.08 points sit within run-to-run noise, and seeds control sampling, not model generation.

Methodology

  1. Unmodified hosts. HoH wraps a fixed harness–model pair — Codex CLI + GPT-5.5, OpenCode + DeepSeek-V4-Pro, or Pi + MiniMax-M3 — changing neither; it only orchestrates.
  2. Three roles per loop. The Planner reads the evidence state Et−1 and prior artifact read-only, emitting a bounded, locally complete objective. The Developer is the single writer, warm-starts from the artifact, shifts testing left. The QA Tester gets a frozen read-only candidate and runs black-box + white-box tests — acceptance never rests on the implementer's claim.
  3. Deterministic Runtime. Freezes each invocation's inputs, enforces per-role tool/write permissions, requires structured output schemas (violations trigger retries).
  4. Two-state continuity. Artifact At and evidence Et cross loop boundaries explicitly: verified behavior becomes a preservation constraint, failures become gaps. Progressive disclosure over an indexed filesystem replaces a memory module; versioned histories enable rollback.
  5. Constrain artifacts, not workflows. Roles must return schema-valid outputs but keep full autonomy over reasoning and tool use.

Results & Highlights

Consistent gains across all nine benchmark–configuration cells after three loops, with the weakest configuration benefiting most.

  • Not just more passes. Pass-controlled: HoH@2 scores 64.84 with 5.67M tokens vs 58.24 for 3-pass Vanilla Continuation at 6.33M tokens — the gain survives an equal-budget comparison.
  • Every mechanism earns its keep. Ablations (Codex, T=3): removing plan update −8.13, evidence feedback −6.28, warm-start −7.85 points; without warm-start, per-task tokens jump 8.41M → 11.12M from repeated reconstruction.
  • 70-loop deployment. From a PRD alone, HoH builds Fusepoint, a playable narrative FPS in Godot 4.7: 81 issues tracked, 65 closed, 17 reopened after regressions, 16 still open at loop 70; humans only intervened for network/API recovery.
  • Cost. 8.41M vs 2.59M tokens per GameCraft task (3.25×); the pass-controlled comparison blunts but does not erase this.

Strengths

  • The right control. Vanilla Continuation isolates the "more passes" confound — HoH@2 beating 3-pass continuation with fewer tokens is the paper's most persuasive single result.
  • Generality across model tiers. Gains hold for a frontier closed model, a strong open model, and a smaller open model; the weakest baseline benefits most — model-agnostic scaffolding, not a fit to one model's quirks.
  • Mechanism-level ablations quantify each cross-loop component, with a mechanistic token explanation rather than just a score drop.
  • Honest failure reporting: reopened and unresolved issues, Pi's non-monotonic ProgramBench peak, excluded-task reasons, and token-caching caveats are all disclosed.
  • Traceability and horizon. The full Fusepoint trajectory (commits, issue ledger, evidence packets) is public; HoH@10 shows the loop does not plateau.

Limitations

  • No variance quantification: single runs per condition, no significance tests on headline tables; the +21.93 vs +22.08 style comparisons are within noise of each other.
  • Case-study attribution gap. Fusepoint uses a newer model (GPT-5.6-Sol) and a richer tool stack than the benchmark configurations, with no controlled run — feasibility demonstrated, not attribution.
  • Small, partly judged subsets. 45 of 140 and 15 of 17 tasks; GameCraft's rubric rewards visual richness almost by construction, so visual gains partly reflect the metric.
  • Cost scaling unexamined: 3.25× token growth, and the marginal-utility curve beyond HoH@3 is explored only for FrontierSWE Dominance.
  • Proprietary dependence and partial release: results may not transfer without frontier reasoning quality; benchmark repos, raw runs, and hidden evaluators are not published. All tasks have executable acceptance — subjective domains untested.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Do the gains survive modest open-weights coders (7–30B), where the QA Tester may be too weak to produce trustworthy evidence states?
  2. Is there a principled early-stopping rule based on evidence-state saturation — declining new-issue discovery? Pi already peaks at HoH@2 on ProgramBench.
  3. Could a learned verifier replace the re-read evidence state and cut the ~3.25× token overhead?
  4. What would a 70-loop Vanilla or ablated control on the same PRD show — how much of Fusepoint is protocol vs model vs tools?
  5. How does the loop behave where acceptance is genuinely subjective (non-game product software), or purely unit-tested (Commit0-style), where richer artifacts cannot buy points?
  6. Does the single-writer boundary bottleneck at scale — would parallel developers with merge conflicts preserve the evidence-binding guarantees?
