> **一句话总结：** Broad Institute 团队提出"科学沙箱"评估框架（specimens–assays–密封 oracle 的闭环实验循环 + 保留 agent 的实验室笔记本），并实例化为 MPRAbox（调控基因组序列库设计）与 CodonBox（发明的遗传密码系统）两个沙箱：Claude Opus 4.7 一次性设计的 5 万条 MPRA 训练库中位 Pearson r=0.774、超过 14 个人类策略中最强的 r=0.763，但在 14 条隐藏"干"规则下 3 种任务框架共 42 次运行只正确发现 1 条规则（GC balance），且在 CodonBox j=6 字母表世界中完全未能发现密码子结构——揭示了 agent"优化指标的能力"与"理解系统规则的能力"的尖锐脱节。

> **TL;DR:** A Broad Institute team (Rao, Castro, ..., Lander, Sabeti) introduces "science sandboxes": closed-loop testbeds where an AI agent runs repeated experiment→feedback→hypothesis-revision cycles against a sealed oracle (wet = physical lab, damp = model trained on empirical data, dry = invented hidden rule), with the agent's lab notebook preserved so evaluators can tell hill-climbing from genuine understanding. In **MPRAbox** (designing a 50,000-sequence MPRA training library, scored by Pearson r on 14 hidden test sets built from Gosai et al. MPRA holdouts, UKBB/GTEx eQTL variants, DHS/Sei/genomic/synthetic sequences), Claude Opus 4.7's one-shot library reached median r=0.774 vs **0.763 for the best of 14 human-designed strategies** (GPT-5.5: 0.655, Gemini 3.5 Flash: 0.680), and after seeing human strategy scores GPT jumped +10.5 pts to 0.760. But under **14 invented dry rules** the agent correctly discovered the hidden rule in only **1 of 14** (GC balance, symbolic framing only), and in **CodonBox** (invented codon tables + Dill HP folding) it recovered codon structure for alphabet sizes j=4 at codon lengths k=2–4 but **never discovered it for j=6**, despite 500 rounds. Optimization ability does not imply scientific understanding.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"科学沙箱"框架**：由 specimens（可提交测试的实体）、assays（测定方法）、sealed oracle（密封反馈源）三部分组成；oracle 分为 wet（真实物理实验）、damp（基于实验数据训练的代理模型）、dry（设计者发明的隐藏规则）三类，可在不同"经验可验证性"层级上评测同一个实验循环。
- **核心评测对象是推理而非分数**：agent 被要求维护 append-only 的 `notebook.md` 实验室笔记本，记录假设、实验、解释与修正，使人（或独立的 AI judge）能区分"局部搜索找到高分序列"与"形成并检验了解释系统的假设"。
- **MPRAbox**：把"为训练 sequence-to-activity 模型选出最有信息量的 5 万条 200bp 调控序列库"形式化为序贯决策问题；damp oracle 用 Malinois（77.6 万条实测序列训练的 CNN）打标签、从零训练同架构下游模型、在 14 个隐藏测试集上报告 Pearson r；并构建了 14 个人类策展基线策略面板（各 5 个采样种子）。
- **Dry-oracle 与 CodonBox 的规则发现测试**：14 条发明的隐藏规则（GC balance、English words 密码、Fibonacci 位置、Game of Life、Collatz、parity 等）× 3 种任务框架（MPRA 框架 / 无框架 / 符号字母表 {0,1,2,3}）；CodonBox 把中心法则改造为可参数化的发明世界（字母表 j∈{4,6,8}，密码子长度 k∈{2,3,4}，隐表 + Dill 2D HP 折叠，适应度=最优折叠中非相邻 H-H 接触数，上限 9）。
- **关键实证发现**：agent 借助预训练生物先验能匹配/超过人类设计策略，但一旦系统规则落在熟悉先验之外（位置型、数学型、组合型逻辑），定量优化仍可继续、规则推断却崩溃；并记录了可复用的失败模式——暴力搜索耗尽预算、混杂实验（同时改 cCRE 和鸡序列）、用代理理论替代真实规则。

## Key Contributions

- A general **"science sandbox" framework** (specimens + assays + sealed oracle) for measuring scientific capability through repeated experimentation, with three oracle regimes — wet, damp, dry — spanning the spectrum of empirical verifiability.
- **Reasoning as the evaluation target**: agents keep an append-only lab notebook, so evaluators can distinguish metric hill-climbing from hypothesis formation, testing, and revision — something fixed-score benchmarks cannot do.
- **MPRAbox**, a regulatory-genomics sandbox where agents design a 50,000-sequence MPRA training library, scored via a sealed Malinois-based damp oracle on 14 hidden evaluation sets, benchmarked against a 14-strategy human-curated reference panel (5 sampling seeds each).
- **Rule-discovery probes**: 14 invented dry oracles under 3 task framings, and **CodonBox**, a family of invented genetic worlds (alphabet j ∈ {4,6,8}, codon length k ∈ {2,3,4}) coupled to a fixed Dill 2D HP folding model, where only the fitness score is returned.
- A sharp empirical finding: frontier agents match or beat human-designed strategies inside familiar biological priors, but their rule inference **collapses** for positional, mathematical, or combinatorial logic outside those priors — quantifying the gap between optimization and understanding.

## Methodology

**Framework.** A science sandbox couples an autonomous agent to a sealed oracle over M rounds: the agent proposes specimens, the oracle applies assays and returns a (possibly scalar-only) report, the agent records an interpretation in a lab notebook and chooses the next experiment. Oracles: *wet* (real experiments), *damp* (computational surrogate trained on empirical data), *dry* (invented rules known only to designers). A sandbox may have known physical meaning (agents may use published science), concealed physical meaning (e.g., DNA replaced by abstract strings to block familiar priors), or none.

**MPRAbox.** Task: submit a library of N=50,000 candidate 200-bp enhancers that maximally improves training of a generalizable sequence-to-activity model. The damp oracle pipeline: (1) score each submitted sequence with **Malinois** (deep CNN trained on 776,474 MPRA-measured 200-bp sequences in K562/HepG2/SK-N-SH, reported Pearson r ≈ 0.88); (2) train a fresh downstream model *from scratch* (same architecture as Malinois, no weight sharing; 3 conv layers of 300/200/200 filters with kernels 19/11/7, 1,000-unit FC layer, cell-type branches with 140 hidden units, Adam lr 3.27e-3, batch 512, up to 200 epochs with early stopping on a 10% validation split) on the submitted library + Malinois labels; (3) evaluate on **14 hidden test sets from 9 source distributions** — MPRA holdouts on chromosomes excluded from Malinois training (chr7/13: 60,053; chr19/21/X: 56,340), UKBB/GTEx fine-mapped cis-eQTL variants (59,084; both-alleles 62,966; one-allele 30,503), Sei classes, DHS index, random genomic windows, and uniform-random synthetic DNA (20,000 each); (4) return only the 14 Pearson correlations and their mean. Chromosome-level holdout was enforced; evaluation-set identities were hidden.

**Baselines and regimes.** Fourteen human-curated sampling strategies (DHS topic-weighted / random / component-stratified, Sei class-balanced / random, random-synthetic + oracle labels, Malinois training sequences with oracle vs real MPRA labels, and 2- and 3-source mixtures), each with 5 independent seeds; library-size sweeps from 10,000 to 300,000 with 50,000 as the primary comparison. One-shot regime: Claude Opus 4.7 (Claude Code), GPT-5.5 (Codex CLI), Gemini 3.5 Flash (Gemini CLI) in their native harnesses, 5 replicates each, with and without prior knowledge of the 14 human strategies' descriptions and scores. Long-horizon regime: M=30 rounds, Claude only, 4 runs (2 blind, 2 informed) with notebook/skill state carried across rounds.

**Dry oracles.** Each of 14 invented rules (GC balance, alternating purine, English-word cipher, compression, prime counts, Fibonacci positions, Game of Life, modular cross, Collatz, RLE/sine/XOR, substring, position 100, exact nucleotide count, parity) replaces Malinois as labeler; a 3-output Ridge regression (α=1.0) on normalized 6-mer frequencies is trained per submission and evaluated on the same 14 sets. Claude ran each rule under three framings (MPRA-framed, unframed, symbolic {0,1,2,3}) for 30 rounds each; rule discovery was assessed from the agent's stated hypotheses against ground truth.

**CodonBox.** Eight invented genetic worlds over four axes: codon length k ∈ {2,3,4}, alphabet size j ∈ {4,6,8}, silent positions, and additive vs interacting informative positions; hidden codon tables (built deterministically, balanced so exactly half of codons map to H) translate sequences into 16-residue H/P chains folded by Dill's 2D HP lattice model — fitness = non-consecutive H-H contacts in the optimal fold (ceiling 9; all 2^16 = 65,536 HP strings enumerated in advance). Claude Opus 4.7 played 500 rounds per world, one run each, told nothing about codons, translation, or folding.

## Results & Highlights

- **One-shot, no prior knowledge (n=5 replicates per agent):** Claude Opus 4.7 produced the best libraries with median Pearson r = **0.774** across the 14-set evaluation suite — every one of its five libraries met or exceeded the mean of the **best human-designed strategy (r = 0.763**, DHS topic-weighted, 5 seeds). GPT-5.5 reached median r = **0.655** and Gemini 3.5 Flash r = **0.680**; neither generated a library exceeding the best human reference. Strategically, Claude sampled real genomic regulatory DNA across ENCODE cCRE classes with rare-class up-weighting ("Negatives are critical for calibration"), while GPT's fully synthetic motif-perturbation libraries underperformed — lacking native genomic context.
- **One-shot with prior knowledge of human strategy scores:** all agents improved, more for the weaker ones — Claude 0.774 → **0.781** (+0.7 pts), GPT-5.5 0.655 → **0.760** (+10.5 pts, now above the human best 0.763), Gemini 0.680 → **0.751** (+7.1 pts). Despite identical prior information, agents did not converge on a single strategy or copy the best human one; notebooks show they inferred *why* strategies worked and modified them (e.g., Claude kept the DHS-topic backbone and added dinuc-shuffled DHS "negative-syntax" controls).
- **Multi-round (M=30, Claude, 4 runs):** all four runs exceeded the strongest human strategy, but quantitative gains over single-round were modest — the value was in observing hypothesis revision. Agents discovered distinct theories: motifs are most informative in native genomic context; enriching rare regulatory classes helps only with broad coverage; adding chicken sequences improved performance while more distant species did not; GC-content stratification improved on the DHS-based strategy. Agents spontaneously converged on one-variable-at-a-time experimentation despite no instruction to do so, after a confounded experiment (moving cCRE AND chicken simultaneously) produced a wrong conclusion.
- **Dry oracles — optimization without understanding:** across 14 rules × 3 framings (42 runs), agents raised the score above the random-library baseline on many rules (e.g., reaching roughly 0.65 mean Pearson on the substring rule vs ~0.4 random baseline), but correctly inferred the hidden rule in only **1 of 14 cases** (GC balance, and only in the symbolic framing). For the English-word cipher, no framing led the agent to consider character pairs encoding letters; for the Fibonacci rule it settled on "learning dinucleotide composition statistics" — a proxy that captured enough feedback structure to improve the score while being the wrong rule. Framing (MPRA vs unframed vs symbolic) changed exploration strategy and sometimes quantitative progress.
- **CodonBox — capability cliff:** the agent reached the maximum fitness score (9 H-H contacts) within the **first 10 rounds in every run** via repeated-character sequences, then used remaining rounds on rule inference. It correctly inferred codon structure for j=4 at k=2, 3, and 4 (16/64/256-entry tables; k=4 identified but did not fully recover the table), and for j=8, k=3 (512 codons) still found the 3-nucleotide codon structure. Strikingly, for **j=6 (216 codons) it never discovered the codon structure at all**, building an elaborate but wrong theory around individual nucleotides and short patterns. It recovered a middle-position rule (correctly inferring silent outer positions) and even a non-additive interaction between outer positions ("This breaks my 'B is poison' theory"), but in the hardest world (j=6, k=4; 1,296 codons; silent + interacting positions) it inferred grouping into fours yet never found the silent position or the general mapping, regressing to cataloging individual codons.
- **Supporting correlations (Fig. S3, n=30 one-shot runs):** performance correlated with tool calls (r = 0.55, p = 0.0016) and shell commands (r = 0.48, p = 0.0072) but **not** with input tokens (r = 0.12, p = 0.538) — doing more discrete experiments, not burning more context, predicted success.

## Critical Assessment

- **The damp-oracle setup is partly circular.** Malinois both supplies training labels for every submitted library and provides labels for 9 of the 14 hidden evaluation sets; the downstream model shares Malinois's exact architecture. An agent whose library resembles Malinois's training distribution is implicitly rewarded for agreement with the oracle's own inductive biases, so a large share of "performance" measures fit to the oracle's prior rather than to experimental reality. The paper acknowledges damp oracles "inherit the data biases," but no experiment isolates how much of the 0.774-vs-0.763 edge survives on the 5 empirical-label sets alone.
- **The evaluation suite has a high, composition-driven floor that compresses the dynamic range.** Even uniform-random synthetic DNA scores a mean Pearson r ≈ 0.721 (the "random floor" in Figs. S4–S5), and the agents themselves discovered this: "a substantial fraction of the eval signal is composition-driven and 'free'." The headline gap (0.655–0.781) therefore spans a range whose floor is already 0.72; per-set results (Fig. 3a) show swings like random DNA jumping to 0.83 while a CHiP-class-only library collapses to 0.54, so per-run differences of a few points of r are far less meaningful than the framing suggests.
- **Severe sample-size asymmetry across claims.** The robust claim (Claude beats human strategies one-shot) rests on 5 replicates vs 5 seeds with no significance test reported. But the most quotable claims — rule-discovery collapse in dry oracles and the j=6 CodonBox failure — rest on **single runs per rule-framing and per world**, so it is impossible to distinguish systematic failure from run-to-run variance; the j=6 vs j=8 non-monotonicity (structure found at 512 codons but not at 216) is especially suspicious as a one-run anecdote.
- **One model dominates the deep probes.** Long-horizon, dry-oracle, and CodonBox experiments all use Claude Opus 4.7 only ("because of computational cost"), so the paper's central narrative — optimization without understanding — is established on a single model from a single lab, while GPT-5.5 and Gemini are only characterized in the easier one-shot setting. Cross-model generalization of the failure mode is asserted in the Discussion but not measured.
- **The "human baseline" is a panel of 14 static sampling rules, not human experimentalists.** No human expert played the sandbox iteratively, so "matching or exceeding human-designed reference strategies" is a weaker claim than the agent-as-scientist framing implies.
- **Rule discovery is judged qualitatively by the authors** from notebook hypotheses, with no automated judge, rubric, or inter-rater reliability reported — and the paper itself proposes AI judges as future work. The binary 1/14 discovery matrix is thus a human coding of free-text reasoning.
- **No wet-lab instantiation** appears anywhere despite the framework being motivated by experimental science; all quantitative results are model-vs-model. CodonBox's physical layer is also deliberately easy (fitness ceiling 9 reachable by a monotonous sequence in 10 rounds), so it measures hypothesis quality only, with zero quantitative headroom after round 10.

## Strengths

- Conceptually clean and genuinely novel framing: separating "can the agent raise the score" from "did the agent learn the rules" is exactly the distinction fixed benchmarks (PaperBench, BixBench, LAB-Bench, DiscoveryBench) cannot make, and the wet/damp/dry oracle taxonomy is a useful design vocabulary for the field.
- The lab-notebook protocol yields unusually rich qualitative evidence — verbatim hypothesis revisions, a self-caught confounded experiment, and an explicit "operational lesson" — making the failure modes concrete and actionable rather than anecdotal.
- Careful, expert-built evaluation infrastructure: chromosome-level holdouts excluded from Malinois training, 14 hidden sets spanning empirical and oracle-labeled distributions, sealed `prepare.py` with verified agent compliance (session logs checked), and deterministic invented worlds with pre-enumerated ground truth.
- Honest reporting of negative results: the paper leads with agents' failure to discover rules, publishes the strategies agents used, releases all code and agent-facing files, and explicitly discusses the trade-offs of each oracle type.
- The observed behavioral patterns (brute-force budget exhaustion, confounded multi-variable experiments, proxy theories that fit feedback without matching the generator) are precisely quantified, reusable targets for improving agent harnesses.

## Limitations

- All deep-dive regimes (30-round, 14 dry oracles, 8 CodonBox worlds) use only Claude Opus 4.7, with one run per condition — findings about scientific reasoning failure may be model- and run-specific.
- Small replicate counts throughout (5 one-shot replicates per model-condition, 4 long-horizon runs, 1 run per CodonBox world) with no statistical testing or confidence intervals on the headline correlations.
- Damp-oracle circularity: Malinois is both label generator and architecture donor for 9/14 evaluation sets, inflating apparent library quality relative to purely empirical evaluation.
- High composition-driven floor (~0.721 for random synthetic DNA) means the suite barely rewards genuine regulatory-grammar learning over base-composition matching.
- No wet-oracle experiments; no human-in-the-loop baseline (only static expert-designed sampling strategies); CodonBox's HP-lattice physics is trivially maxed, capping quantitative resolution.
- Qualitative rule-discovery assessment is author-judged without an automated or blinded rubric; the paper's proposed AI-judge automation remains unimplemented.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你的第一优先方向 "Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research"：它给出了一个评测 agentic scientific discovery 的可复用框架（密封 oracle + 实验笔记本 + wet/damp/dry 反馈源谱系），并系统记录了长程自主 agent 的具体失败模式（暴力搜索耗尽预算、混杂变量实验、用代理理论冒充真实规则），对设计更可靠的自动化科研循环和 agent 评测基线都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the optimization-vs-understanding gap replicate across other frontier models (GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash were only tested one-shot), or is rule-discovery collapse a Claude-harness-specific artifact?
- How much of the one-shot Claude advantage (0.774 vs 0.763) survives when restricted to the 5 empirically-labeled evaluation sets, removing Malinois-oracle circularity?
- Would an explicit scaffold enforcing structured exploration (the paper observes agents waste budgets on brute-force search) close the dry-oracle discovery gap without being told the rule class?
- Can automated AI judges score lab notebooks against ground-truth rules reliably enough to scale sandboxes to hundreds of agent architectures, as the authors propose via BroadBox?
- The j=6 CodonBox failure despite success at j=8 suggests hypothesis-space search is non-monotonic in world complexity — is this a property of the agent's in-context theory building, and would tool-augmented systematic design-of-experiments fix it?
- Since ~9 H-H contact fitness ceilings in 10 rounds leave no quantitative signal, what CodonBox-like worlds with hard optimization surfaces would let researchers measure both dimensions simultaneously?
