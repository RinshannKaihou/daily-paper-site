> **一句话总结：** 提出 AutoTraceGT——首个把社会科学六十年历史的扎根理论（开放编码→主轴编码→理论编码，配理论饱和停止判据）用四角色多智能体流水线自动化、用于大规模归纳分析 LLM Agent 轨迹的框架；在 6 个数据集、4 个骨干模型、7500+ 条轨迹上，其编码本（gpt-5-mini 诱导、gpt-5 判分）复现专家人工失败分类中 73.7%（GAIA）到 90.9%（WebShop）的失败模式并发现人工分类遗漏的模式，同配置重复运行的编码本余弦相似度中位数 0.929（跨配置仅 0.791），作为特征供 FLAML 预测失败时在 SWE-Agent/GPT-5-mini 上把 MCC 从 few-shot 基线的 0.222 提升到 0.318。

> **TL;DR:** AutoTraceGT operationalizes Grounded Theory — inductive open coding → axial coding → theoretical coding, with an explicit theoretical-saturation stopping rule — as a four-agent LLM pipeline (OpenCode / AxialCode / codebook Manager / TheoreticalCode) over agent trajectories. Against the expert failure taxonomy of Zhu et al. (2025) its induced codebook recovers 90.9% of WebShop failure modes vs 63.6% for a one-pass LLM baseline (+27.3 pts), 75.0% on ALFWORLD vs 60.0% (+15.0), and 73.7% on GAIA vs 54.5% (+19.2), while surfacing emergent categories the human taxonomy lacks. Replicate runs of the same configuration reach 0.929 median cosine similarity versus 0.791 across configurations (Mann–Whitney p < 1e-20), and cross-LLM coverage beats three null baselines (permutation p ≤ 0.001). Used as deductive features for FLAML failure prediction from the first 50% of a trajectory, it lifts GPT-5-mini MCC on SWE-Agent from 0.222 (few-shot LLM) to 0.318 — but on TAU-Bench the standalone codebook (0.131) actually loses to a simple few-shot codebook baseline (0.257); the union of both wins (0.306). A complete gpt-5-mini codebook costs $0.74–$1.91.

## 核心贡献

- **方法论迁移**：首次论证扎根理论（Glaser & Strauss 1967；Charmaz 2014）可作为 ML 界分析 Agent 行为的可扩展归纳方法——相对预定义专家失败分类（刚性、难泛化到新任务）和纯定量元数据（长度/动作数/成功率，无法解释过程），它同时具备归纳开放性、有原则的饱和停止判据、以及从原始轨迹到理论论断的可审计链条。
- **AutoTraceGT 流水线**：四个角色化 Agent 实现三层语义压缩——OpenCode 逐条轨迹打概念编码（2–5 词行为标签 + 步骤区间 + 逐字引文证据，分段携带 memo），AxialCode 按批把编码归组为带类型关系和成败状态标签的类目，Manager 对照运行中的编码本执行 confirm/extend/add/contradict 与 add/merge/split/flag（merge-first 策略、append-only 修订日志），TheoreticalCode 在饱和后产出核心类目与理论叙事。饱和判据：连续 W 轮新增类目数 a_t < ε（默认 ε=0.2, W=2）。
- **实证验证（RQ1 过程可靠性）**：12 个数据集×模型单元各跑 3 次不相交子集——同配置编码本余弦相似度中位数 0.929，跨配置中位数 0.791，以跨配置分布 95 分位 0.901 为数据驱动的高可复现阈值，Mann–Whitney U 检验 p < 10^-20；固定数据集换骨干模型时，编码本覆盖率显著高于三个零假设基线（置换检验 p ≤ 0.001），说明恢复的是数据结构而非模型先验。
- **实证验证（RQ2 产出质量）**：对照专家失败分类（Zhu et al. 2025），编码本覆盖 73.7–90.9% 的人类失败模式（WebShop 90.9%），轨迹级推理匹配 58.0–87.9%；独立收敛到先前专家提出的"误差级联"理论；17 个未被人工分类覆盖的新类目经双人审计全部有效（0 个伪类目）。编码本还可作演绎特征空间：GLM 系数给出可解释的成败关联，FLAML 失败预测超过 few-shot 基线。
- **成本**：完整一次 gpt-5-mini 编码本仅 $0.74–$1.91，开放编码每条轨迹 <$0.01–$0.04，对比估算的人工编码 ~$3.33/条（8 分钟 × $25/时）。

## Key Contributions

- **A new analytic method for ML**: brings Grounded Theory — a six-decade-old inductive qualitative methodology — into ML agent analysis, offering what pre-defined failure taxonomies (rigid) and quantitative metadata (process-blind) cannot: openness to novel/emergent behaviors, a principled data-driven stopping criterion (theoretical saturation), and an auditable trail from raw trajectories to theoretical claims.
- **AutoTraceGT**: the first end-to-end multi-agent pipeline for automated grounded theory. OpenCode labels each trajectory with conceptual codes (code + step span + verbatim quote); AxialCode groups codes into categories with typed relations and success/fail status tags; a codebook Manager performs constant comparison via confirm/extend/add/contradict and add/merge/split/flag actions on a versioned codebook with an append-only revision log; TheoreticalCode distills a core category and narrative after saturation (stop when new-category additions a_t < ε = 0.2 for W = 2 consecutive rounds, batch B = 30).
- **Process reliability (RQ1)**: across 12 dataset×model cells × 3 runs, within-configuration codebooks reach 0.929 median cosine similarity vs 0.791 cross-configuration (data-derived reproducibility threshold 0.901 = 95th percentile of the cross-config null; Mann–Whitney p < 10^-20); cross-LLM codebook coverage on a fixed dataset exceeds cross-dataset, same-model, and 1,000-permutation label-shuffle nulls (p ≤ 0.001 on all three datasets), indicating dataset-specific rather than backbone-prior structure.
- **Artifact quality (RQ2)**: the codebook covers 73.7–90.9% of expert failure modes in the human taxonomy of Zhu et al. (2025) and matches 58.0–87.9% of per-trajectory expert reasoning, while surfacing emergent categories the module×failure-type schema cannot express (e.g., ALFWORLD "noncompliant inaction", GAIA "signaled-but-unrealized shifts", WebShop "mode oscillation without synthesis"); its theoretical narrative independently recovers the prior cascade-of-errors account. A human audit judged all 17 unmatched categories valid (0 spurious).
- **Deductive reuse + cost**: codebook presence/co-occurrence features feed interpretable GLMs (e.g., in GO-BROWSE, clicking a UI affordance alone predicts success β = −2.59, but clicking then declaring completion without verification predicts failure β = +3.28) and FLAML failure classifiers trained on the first 50% of trajectories; a full codebook costs $0.74–$1.91.

## Methodology

**Framework.** AutoTraceGT takes a corpus of N trajectories and runs Algorithm 2 (explicit state version): each round draws a batch of B = 30 trajectories under a sampling policy π (uniform random default), conditioned on the current codebook. Four agents at three scales:

1. **OpenCode (per trajectory)**: partitions the message sequence into chunks of 50 messages, codes each chunk with (conceptual code of 2–5 words in verb+noun form, step span, verbatim quote, analytic memo), carrying a segment memo across chunks for continuity. Prompts forbid AI/ML technical vocabulary and force behavioral-quality labels over literal action descriptions.
2. **AxialCode (per round)**: groups the batch's codes into categories — each with a definition, member codes linked to trajectory IDs, a status tag σ ∈ {SUCCESS, FAIL, BOTH} aggregated from member outcomes, and status differentiation — plus typed relationships with evidence, underdeveloped codes, and an axial memo.
3. **Manager (codebook management across rounds)**: reconciles the round's axial output against the versioned codebook K_t = (C_t, R_t, L_t): for categories confirm/extend/add/contradict; for relations add/merge/split/flag — every action appended to an audit-friendly revision log L_t. Merge-first policy aligns new categories to existing structure when a compatible match exists. Saturation is operationalized as a_t < ε (ε = 0.2) for two consecutive rounds.
4. **TheoreticalCode (global)**: after saturation, selects the core category and writes a theoretical narrative using only K_T and the revision log — no new local evidence, unresolved tensions preserved.

**Evaluation design.** Two corpora: (a) outcome-labeled — 1,980 TAU-BENCH (1,183 success / 797 fail), 2,000 GO-BROWSE (728/1,272), 2,000 SWE-AGENT (167/1,833) trajectories, 4 backbones (GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, GPT-OSS-120B), 12 cells × 3 replicate runs on disjoint subsets; (b) failure-annotated — ALFWORLD (100), GAIA (50), WEBSHOP (50) failed trajectories with expert failure reasoning from Zhu et al. (2025), human taxonomies |H| = 15/11/10. Coverage is measured by GPT-5 as judge (match/no-match with rationale) between the gpt-5-mini-induced codebook and the human taxonomy + per-trajectory reasoning; the judge is human-validated on 100 pairs (annotator–annotator Cohen's κ = 0.74; annotator–judge κ = 0.66/0.76). Downstream failure prediction truncates every trajectory to its first 50% of steps, induces codebooks only on the training split, annotates prefix-only presence/co-occurrence features, and fits FLAML AutoML — preventing label leakage. Baselines: few-shot LLM direct prediction, few-shot-prompted codebook, their union, and a semantic-clustering baseline (K = 10 k-means on text-embedding-3-large). Ablations: merge-first instruction, stopping threshold, sampling policies, prompt sensitivity (3 prompts × 2 orders), cross-family backbone (Gemini-3-Flash on SWE-AGENT), one-pass coding baseline, and a token-cost accounting from OpenAI Batch records.

## Results & Highlights

- **Coverage vs expert human taxonomy (Table 1; gpt-5-mini codebook, gpt-5 judge)**: recall of human failure modes — ALFWORLD 75.0% (15 human modes, 20 induced categories), GAIA 73.7% (11 → 17), WEBSHOP 90.9% (10 → 13); per-trajectory reasoning match 82.4% / 58.0% / 87.9%. Versus the one-pass baseline with the same backbone and judge (Table 7): ALFWORLD 75.0 vs 60.0 recall (+15.0 pts), GAIA 73.7 vs 54.5 (+19.2), WEBSHOP 90.9 vs 63.6 (+27.3). Note precision on ALFWORLD is only 60.0% because the codebook is larger (20 vs 15 categories).
- **Replicate reproducibility (Fig. 5a, App. A.3)**: in-cell (same dataset+model, disjoint subsets) median cosine similarity 0.929 / mean 0.923 across 594 cross-cell pairs at median 0.791 (range 0.57–0.94); adopting the cross-cell 95th percentile 0.901 as threshold, all 12 cell means exceed it (Mann–Whitney p < 10^-20). Within-config coverage 0.570–0.707 vs null mean 0.440 ± 0.075.
- **Cross-LLM stability (Fig. 5b)**: holding the dataset fixed, cross-backbone coverage is 0.543 (SWE-AGENT), 0.540 (TAU-BENCH), 0.539 (GO-BROWSE), above all three null distributions with permutation p = 0.0010 on every dataset — the codebooks reflect trajectory-specific structure, not model priors.
- **Downstream failure prediction (Table 3; MCC / ROC AUC on first-50% prefixes)**: the complementary AutoTraceGT+few-shot-codebook feature set wins broadly, e.g., GPT-5-mini: TAU-BENCH MCC 0.306 vs 0.061 for few-shot LLM direct (+0.245), SWE-AGENT 0.318 / 0.772 vs 0.222 / 0.702, GO-BROWSE 0.425 vs 0.291; GPT-5 on GO-BROWSE reaches 0.498 / 0.807 standalone vs 0.343 / 0.736 few-shot codebook. But the standalone AutoTraceGT codebook is not uniformly better: on TAU-BENCH it loses to the few-shot codebook for GPT-4.1-mini (0.153 vs 0.245) and GPT-5-mini (0.131 vs 0.257). The semantic-clustering baseline beats gpt-5-mini AutoTraceGT only on TAU-BENCH (0.268 vs 0.131) and never beats the complementary set.
- **Interpretable GLM layer (Table 2)**: on SWE-AGENT, "uses sweeping workaround" carries β = +4.10 (failure) while "builds minimal reproducer after reconnaissance" carries β = −1.82 (success); on GO-BROWSE, "clicks UI, closes without verification" β = +3.28 vs "clicks visible UI affordance" alone β = −2.59 — the decisive pattern is verification before closure, not clicking. GLM fit: Nagelkerke R² = 0.618 (TAU-BENCH, N=170), 0.431 (GO-BROWSE, N=734), 0.483 (SWE-AGENT, N=396), all LR p < .001.
- **Human validation of artifacts (Tables 8–9)**: two independent annotators judge all 17 codebook categories unmatched to the human taxonomy as valid failure-related/neutral behaviors (14 failure-related, 3 neutral, 0 spurious; complete agreement); the LLM judge is slightly conservative relative to humans (on the 87 pairs where annotators agree, they reverse 7 judge non-matches vs 1 judge match).
- **Cost (Table 12)**: open coding <$0.01–$0.04 per trajectory; one complete gpt-5-mini codebook: $0.74 (TAU-BENCH, 3 batches, 91 calls), $1.08 (GO-BROWSE, 4 batches), $1.91 (SWE-AGENT, 6 batches, 274 calls) — vs an estimated $3.33/trajectory for human coding at 8 min × $25/h.
- **Robustness ablations**: merge-first ablation changes mean ADD rate 0.125 → 0.129 with 0.957 cosine to the original codebook; prompt-sensitivity similarity 0.932 vs 0.929 for trajectory-order variation (cross-dataset null 0.777); a Gemini-3-Flash cross-family run on SWE-AGENT converges (median 4 batches, 11 categories) but is a single point.

## Critical Assessment

- **The downstream-prediction headline outruns the table.** The abstract says the codebook "outperforms zero-shot and few-shot LLM baselines on downstream failure prediction", but Table 3 shows the *standalone* AutoTraceGT codebook loses on TAU-BENCH MCC to the trivially simple few-shot codebook baseline for both GPT-4.1-mini (0.153 vs 0.245) and GPT-5-mini (0.131 vs 0.257); the consistent wins come from the *complementary union* of both codebooks. The paper flags this in Section 4.2.2 ("not always as a standalone replacement") — commendable honesty — but the abstract phrasing glosses it. Also, MCCs of 0.13–0.43 on highly imbalanced corpora (SWE-AGENT: 167/2,000 successes) are signal, not a deployable failure detector.
- **The mutual-coverage metric structurally favors the bigger codebook.** AutoTraceGT induces 17–20 categories against human taxonomies of 10–15; recall can exceed precision partly by construction (ALFWORLD precision is just 60.0%). The authors' strongest mitigation is real — a full human audit found all 17 unmatched categories valid, 0 spurious — but "surfacing additional patterns" is easy when you emit 1.5× more categories, and no category-count-matched baseline is tested.
- **Backbone homogeneity undercuts the generality claim.** All four principal backbones are OpenAI GPT-family models; the single Gemini-3-Flash run on one dataset (11 categories, median 4 batches) cannot rule out shared frontier-model bias, and the authors say so. The "cross-LLM stability" result is really a within-family stability result.
- **Judge-dependence and small reference corpora.** Every headline coverage number passes through a GPT-5 judge; the human validation is 100 pairs and 17 categories with two annotators — adequate but modest, and the disagreement asymmetry shows the judge is systematically conservative. The failure-annotated corpora are tiny (100/50/50 trajectories, all failures, inherited from Zhu et al. 2025), so "recovers 73–91% of failure modes" rests on 10–15 human failure modes per benchmark.
- **Saturation is operationalized, not proven.** ε = 0.2 with W = 2 is a heuristic; the median terminal ADD rate is 0.056 but the max is 0.167 — some runs stop while still adding categories at a 16.7% rate — and the paper candidly notes the convergence-to-1.0 similarity curve in Fig. 4 is measured against the terminal codebook, so it approaches 1 by construction. Tightening ε to 0.10 costs ~7 extra batches (gpt-4.1-mini).
- **Missing empirical baselines for the core claim.** LOGOS, AcademiaOS, and MAST are discussed in Related Work as prior automated/semi-automated pipelines, but the only coverage comparator is the authors' own one-pass baseline; there is no head-to-head against any existing automated grounded-theory or taxonomy-induction system.
- **Genuine strengths worth crediting**: the pipeline is a faithful — not cosmetic — operationalization of grounded theory (three coding stages, constant comparison, memos, revision log; Table 13's 10-version evolution of one category is a lovely audit artifact), the label-leakage protocol for failure prediction (train-split-only induction, 50% prefix truncation) is careful, and the ablation suite (merge-first, sampling policy, prompt sensitivity, stopping threshold) is well beyond what most agent-failure papers run.

## Strengths

- First principled, auditable bridge between a mature qualitative methodology and ML-scale agent analysis — with codebook codes traceable to step-level verbatim quotes, and an append-only revision log that makes the theory's evolution replayable.
- Statistically disciplined validation for an LLM-qualitative paper: a data-derived reproducibility threshold (0.901 = cross-config 95th percentile) instead of an arbitrary cutoff, Mann–Whitney and 1,000-permutation nulls, human validation of both the judge and the emergent categories.
- Robustness work that most peers skip: merge-first instruction ablation, stopping-threshold analysis, three sampling policies, prompt/order sensitivity, a cross-family spot check, and full cost accounting ($0.74–$1.91 per codebook).
- Honest reporting throughout: mixed downstream results shown in full; "hypotheses for human review, not ground truth" framing; limitations section is specific rather than boilerplate.
- Practical payoff: emergent categories (e.g., WebShop "mode oscillation without synthesis") are exactly the fine-grained, remediation-discriminating failure modes that pre-defined taxonomies collapse — and the GLM coefficients turn the theory into testable, signed behavioral claims.

## Limitations

- Every coding stage is LLM-performed, so codebooks inherit backbone blind spots; the all-GPT backbone suite (plus one Gemini run) leaves shared frontier-model bias unresolved.
- Coverage and match numbers are conditional on a GPT-5 judge's reading; human validation covers 100 pairs / 17 categories only, and the reference corpora are small, all-failure sets (100/50/50).
- Runs to saturation over a corpus — many LLM calls per trajectory — so it is an offline corpus-analysis tool, not a per-trajectory online monitor.
- Instantiates one methodology (grounded theory) on one behavioral surface (single-agent trajectories with binary success/failure outcomes), English-language, public benchmarks; multi-agent/dialogue settings and other languages are future work.
- The algorithmic saturation criterion (a_t < ε for W rounds) is an approximation of the methodological concept — some runs stop while still adding categories at up to a 0.167 rate, and richer criteria (category density, relationship stability) are explicitly future work.
- Downstream failure prediction is demonstrated, not solved: standalone-codebook losses to the few-shot codebook on TAU-BENCH and modest AUCs (0.57–0.83) mean the deductive feature space is currently more interpretive than predictive.

## Relevance

与你最关注的"自进化 Agent 与自动化科研"高度相关：AutoTraceGT 提供了一个可审计、可复现、每次不到 2 美元的自动化流程，把长程自主 Agent 的失败行为归纳成有证据链的行为理论——这正是提升长期自主 Agent 系统可靠性与可信度所缺的过程级诊断工具；其"多智能体流水线自动化归纳式分析"本身也是自动化科研闭环的一个具体实例。编码本特征可复用于过程级奖励信号与行为感知评估指标的设计（论文 Future work 已点名），对后续做自进化 Agent 的失败归因和定向训练数据构造有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does a codebook induced on one benchmark transfer to a related one (e.g., induce on SWE-AGENT, apply to WebArena), or is every corpus re-saturated from scratch in practice?
- Can the saturation criterion be enriched with category density and relationship stability (the authors' own proposal) so that runs stop at a principled, budget-independent point?
- The four principal backbones are all GPT-family: would a deep cross-family sweep (Gemini, Claude, Llama) reproduce category structure, or does each model family induce a different phenomenology of failure?
- Could codebook presence/co-occurrence features serve as process-level reward signals or targeted training-data selectors for self-improving agents, closing the loop from diagnosis to intervention?
- How does the pipeline behave on multi-agent trajectories (the MAST setting), where failures span coordination rather than single-agent cognition — do axial categories still emerge, or does the batch-level grouping saturate prematurely?
- What is the right human-in-the-loop interface: the paper's revision log is machine-auditable, but at what point should a qualitative researcher override MERGE/ADD decisions, and does that change the saturated theory?
