> **一句话总结：** 论文发现长程工具使用智能体在任务末期存在一种可从隐藏状态线性读出的"晚期压力"状态——它使模型在硬约束尚未满足时偏向提交看似完整、实则未解决的答案（探针 AUROC 0.916，随机基线 0.5）；据此提出的 PSPR 插件（线性探针在线监测 + CAA 式压力缓解转向 + 高压时显式状态整理）在 DeepPlanning-Travel 上将 Qwen3-32B ReAct 的综合分 CP 从 29.3 提升到 33.2，并在 3 个骨干模型 × 3 类基线上一致有效。

> **TL;DR:** On DeepPlanning-Travel with Qwen3-14B/32B and OLMo-3.1-32B, this paper shows that before final submission, an agent's hidden states linearly encode a "late-stage pressure" state that biases toward polished answers with unsatisfied constraints — a linear probe separates pressure-driven commits from healthy commits and productive continues at AUROC 0.916 (vs 0.5 random). PSPR, a lightweight plugin that senses pressure online and applies CAA-style pressure-relief steering (escalating to model-generated explicit state organization above a threshold), lifts ReAct's Composite Score from 29.3 to 33.2 on Qwen3-32B (+3.9) and Commonsense Score from 41.5 to 45.6 on Qwen3-14B (+4.2, paired bootstrap 95% CI [0.4, 8.0]), while strict case-level accuracy remains 0.0 for nearly all methods.

## 核心贡献

- **识别并定义"晚期压力"内部状态**：长程工具使用智能体临近收尾时，隐藏状态中存在一个可线性分离的状态，它与"提交看似完整但关键约束未满足"的失败模式（polished but unresolved）相关——这与以往只从行为层面研究此类失败的工作（幻觉式完成、知识整合衰减）形成互补。
- **严格的三类对比构造**：PresC（压力提交：约束满足度 S_sat ≤ 0.3 且交付打磨分 DPS ≥ 4）vs HealC（健康提交：S_sat ≥ 0.85 且 DPS ≥ 4，排除"仅检测提交动作"的捷径）vs ProdC（继续 productive 工作：排除"压力 = 普通难度/未完成"的混淆），并用线性概念擦除思想对转向方向做残差化，剥离通用的"提交 vs 继续"成分。
- **定位两个缓解因素**：约束清晰度（constraint clarity，把未验证约束显式重述）和行动映射（action mapping，把未解决约束映射为下一步具体工具调用），二者结合使 PresC-risk 节点的探针压力从 0.62 降到 0.13。
- **提出 PSPR 插件**：用线性探针在每个动作边界在线感知压力，按阈值 a=0.4、b=0.65 三档响应——不干预 / 加压力缓解方向 / 触发模型自生成的显式状态整理（每条轨迹最多两次），零微调、阈值跨骨干固定，即插即用于 CoT/ReAct/Reflexion。

## Key Contributions

- Identifies and operationalizes a **late-stage pressure state** in long-horizon tool-use agents: an internal state, linearly separable in hidden space, that biases the model toward submitting complete-looking answers while hard constraints remain unresolved (probe AUROC 0.916 on Qwen3-14B / DeepPlanning-Travel).
- Constructs a carefully controlled **contrast set** — PresC (pressure commit: S_sat ≤ 0.3, DPS ≥ 4) vs HealC (healthy commit: S_sat ≥ 0.85, DPS ≥ 4, controlling for submission form) vs ProdC (productive continue, controlling for generic difficulty) — and residualizes the steering direction against the generic commit-vs-continue component before intervening.
- Shows two trajectory factors that **mitigate** the pressure state: constraint clarity and action mapping; combined they cut PresC-risk probe pressure from 0.62 to 0.13 while raising continuation (0.28 → 0.87) and targeted repair (0.08 → 0.85).
- Proposes **PSPR (Probe-Sensed Pressure Relief)**, a lightweight inference-time plugin: a linear probe monitors first-token hidden states at every action boundary and selects among no intervention, activation addition of a pressure-relief direction, and model-generated explicit state organization. It consistently improves CoT, ReAct, and Reflexion1 across three backbones without any fine-tuning.

## Methodology

**Setting.** DeepPlanning-Travel (Zhang et al., 2026): 120 tasks where agents use offline tools to produce travel plans satisfying multiple verifiable hard constraints (location, budget, user constraints). Backbones: Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B (thinking mode), OLMo-3.1-32B-Instruct.

**Behavioral evidence.** Final submissions are scored on Commonsense Score (CS, feasibility/execution quality) and a Delivery Polish Score (DPS, 1–5, judged by Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview on whether the answer merely *appears* complete). The density of submissions concentrates in high-DPS regions even when CS is far from perfect — motivating the search for an internal correlate of "polished closure under unresolved constraints".

**Probe construction (Sect. 3).** At action boundaries (after prefill, before the next generated token), the first-token hidden state is collected. PresC boundaries (S_sat ≤ 0.3 and DPS ≥ 4) form positives; HealC (S_sat ≥ 0.85, DPS ≥ 4) and ProdC (boundaries where the model continues addressing unresolved constraints) form two negative controls. Labels come from the benchmark's constraint checker plus Gemini 3.1 Pro teacher annotation; a human audit (two annotators, 50 examples/class) shows 100%/96%/100% exact three-way agreement for PresC/HealC/ProdC, and an external GPT-5.5 + Claude-Opus-4.7 audit shows 100%/97%/98%. From 120 queries (90 train / 30 held-out), the balanced training set has 300 PresC, 110 HealC, 135 ProdC states; layer 26 of Qwen3-14B (layer 46 for Qwen3-32B) is used, chosen from middle-to-late layers.

**Steering (Sect. 4).** Following contrastive activation addition, directions v = μ(PresC) − μ(HealC) and μ(PresC) − μ(ProdC) are built from the first K=5 generated tokens at each boundary. The ProdC direction is residualized against the generic commit direction μ(HealC) − μ(ProdC) via linear-concept-erasure projection, so it isolates pressure beyond a submit-vs-continue tendency. Adding vs subtracting the direction at test nodes tests whether moving along the pressure direction causally shifts commitment behavior.

**Mitigation analysis (Sect. 5).** Two appended context spans are tested at action boundaries: a *constraint clarity* span (restating active hard constraints, current evidence, unresolved items) and an *action map* (mapping unresolved constraints to likely next tool calls). Effects are measured via probe pressure, continuation rate, and a teacher-judged repair rate; an attention analysis checks the model actually reads the appended span (row-normalized attention on the appended block vs its token share).

**PSPR (Sect. 6).** The probe runs online on first-token states at every action boundary with thresholds a=0.4, b=0.65: (i) s_t < a: no intervention; (ii) a ≤ s_t < b: add v_relief = μ(LP) − μ(HP) — the mean low-pressure (productive, post-organization) minus high-pressure activation over the first K=10 tokens; (iii) s_t ≥ b: explicit state organization — the model itself summarizes constraint status and plans follow-up actions, inserted into the prefill (max twice per trajectory). No gold labels are used at deployment; |α|=1, fixed across all backbones and baselines.

## Results & Highlights

- **Probe separability (Table 1, Fig. 2):** On DeepPlanning-Travel with Qwen3-14B, the linear probe separates PresC from all negatives at **AUROC 0.916 / PR-AUC 0.921** (80 vs 80 held-out states; random baseline 0.5). Against targeted controls it stays strong: vs healthy commit **0.890/0.918**, vs productive continue **0.929/0.914**. It replicates across scale and benchmark: **0.841/0.823** on Qwen3-32B, and **0.921/0.954** for Qwen3-14B on TravelPlanner (with a relaxed HealC threshold).
- **Main results (Table 4, pass@3, DeepPlanning-Travel):** PSPR improves every CS/PS/CP entry across all three backbones and all three base methods. Largest gains: Qwen3-32B **ReAct CP 29.3 → 33.2 (+3.9)** with CS 45.3 → 50.5; Qwen3-14B **ReAct CS 41.5 → 45.6 (+4.1)**, and a paired bootstrap over 3,000 task-level resamples confirms **ΔCS = +4.2, 95% CI [0.4, 8.0]** (Table 11). Gains also hold for CoT (Qwen3-32B CP 25.2 → 28.1) and Reflexion1 (Qwen3-32B CP 33.1 → 34.5), and for OLMo-3.1-32B (ReAct CP 27.0 → 28.9), though OLMo gains are smaller.
- **Mitigation diagnostics (Table 3):** On PresC-risk nodes, appending both constraint clarity and action map drops mean probe pressure from **0.62 to 0.13**, raises continuation from **0.28 to 0.87** and teacher-judged repair from **0.08 to 0.85**; ProdC pressure also falls from 0.19 to 0.02. Attention analysis: the appended span is only **11.9%** of prefill tokens on average but receives **19.1%** of prefill-normalized attention (~1.6× token share), confirming the model actually uses it.
- **Trigger controls validate probe timing (Table 6):** Applying explicit organization at a *random* selected boundary **hurts** CS (29.4 vs 32.2 for CoT alone, CP 18.4 vs 19.0); a fixed *Periodic-3* trigger improves CS to 34.2 / CP 20.8, still below full PSPR's **CS 36.0 / CP 22.6** — the pressure signal, not just extra text, drives the gain.
- **Generalization (Table 5, 7):** On DeepPlanning-Shop (Qwen3-32B ReAct), Match 65.9 → 68.0 and Acc 19.0 → 23.0; on TravelPlanner, micro pass 60.5 → 72.3 and hard-constraint macro 24.5 → 26.3, but the strict Final macro only moves 12.5 → 13.3 and Final Acc is unchanged at 2.5. On the Chinese version of DeepPlanning-Travel, Qwen3-14B CoT CP rises 19.4 → 23.2, so the effect is not English-specific.
- **Cost (Table 8):** PSPR increases average turns from 4.62 to 7.94 and tool calls from 7.57 to 8.95 under CoT (Qwen3-14B), with next-boundary pressure drops of −0.31 (relief direction) and −0.57 (explicit organization) — the extra execution is the intended behavioral shift from premature finalization to continued verification.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is genuine.** Prior studies of premature/illusionary completion in search agents are behavioral; this is the first to ask whether a distinct *internal state* corresponds to polished-but-unresolved submissions, and the three-way contrast design (HealC controls submission form, ProdC controls task status) is exactly the right way to rule out trivial probe shortcuts. The residualization of the commit direction and the deliberate choice to collect LP activations at the *next productive boundary* rather than right after appending the organizational text show real care about what the direction encodes.

**Weaknesses worth flagging:**

1. **LLM-teacher dependence in the labels.** Both the polish score (DPS) and the per-constraint annotations used to build PresC/HealC/ProdC come from Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview. The human audit is reassuring (96–100% three-way agreement), but a data quirk undermines confidence in cross-benchmark portability: on TravelPlanner the original HealC criterion (S_sat ≥ 0.85) yielded *too few examples* and had to be relaxed to S_sat ≥ 0.75 — i.e., the contrast set construction is fragile off the home benchmark.
2. **The causal analysis in Sect. 5 is oracle-assisted.** The constraint-clarity and action-map spans used to claim "pressure is mitigated by constraint clarity and action mapping" (the 0.62 → 0.13 result) are instantiated from the dataset's built-in constraint parsing plus Gemini structured interpretation — gold-ish information the deployed PSPR does not have (its organization step is model-generated). The mitigation numbers should be read as upper bounds, not as what PSPR achieves.
3. **No strict metric improves.** Case-level accuracy stays at 0.0 for essentially every method on DeepPlanning-Travel (except one 0.8 entry), and TravelPlanner Final Acc is flat at 2.5. All headline gains are on partial-satisfaction metrics (CS/PS/CP on a 0–100 scale) moving +2 to +4 points. PSPR reduces a specific failure mode; it does not make agents solve tasks.
4. **pass@3 inflates gains.** The best-of-3 protocol interacts favorably with PSPR's push toward longer, more complete trajectories. The single Avg@3 example in Table 12 shows a smaller gain (CS 34.8 → 36.9, +2.1, vs +3.8 at pass@3 for the same setting), and the bootstrap CI [0.4, 8.0] only barely excludes zero — the true effect could be half the headline number.
5. **Steering effects are modest and thresholds hand-set.** In the clean Sect. 4 tests, subtracting the direction changes CS from 0.21 to 0.25 — signs are stable but magnitudes are small. The thresholds a=0.4, b=0.65 come from "a small number of preliminary trials"; sensitivity is discussed qualitatively (lowering a hurts, raising delays intervention) but not systematically mapped.
6. **No code release is mentioned**, so reproducing the labeling pipeline (DPS prompt, S_sat checker wiring, rollout collection) requires reimplementation from the appendix prompts.

The authors' own limitations section is unusually honest: they explicitly disclaim a complete causal account (premature submission can also stem from earlier planning mistakes, missing evidence, tool errors, or benchmark-specific incentives) and note the hidden-state access requirement.

## Strengths

- Clean, falsifiable hypothesis with a well-controlled contrast set that rules out the two obvious probe shortcuts (detecting submission form; detecting generic difficulty).
- Strong validation discipline: label reliability audits, two-fold held-out splits for probe and directions, replication across model scale (14B/32B), language (English/Chinese), and three benchmarks; non-probe trigger controls showing probe timing matters.
- PSPR is genuinely lightweight and practical: no fine-tuning, no gold labels at deployment, fixed thresholds across backbones and baselines, and an explicit cost accounting of the extra turns.
- Honest framing — the paper claims an operationalized *correlate* of premature closure, not a cause, and positions itself as complementing behavioral studies.

## Limitations

- Restricted to textual, structured, verifiable long-horizon settings (travel/shopping planning); unclear whether the pressure state exists or is separable in open-ended, dynamically changing, or multimodal agent environments.
- Requires white-box access to hidden states plus an offline annotation/rollout phase per model and benchmark — not applicable to API-only models, and the relaxed TravelPlanner labeling threshold suggests per-benchmark re-tuning is sometimes necessary.
- Strict end-to-end success is untouched (Acc ≈ 0.0 everywhere); the method improves partial constraint satisfaction and delivery quality, not task solvability.
- Absolute plan quality remains low (CS ~36–50, PS ~9–16 out of 100), and PSPR adds ~3 turns and ~1.4 tool calls per trajectory on average.
- The Sect. 5 mitigation story relies on oracle-constructed spans, so the mechanism attribution ("clarity + mapping mitigate pressure") is stronger than the deployment evidence.

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Can the late-stage pressure state be detected in API-only models via behavioral proxies (e.g., online divergence between delivery polish and constraint progress), given the probe requires hidden-state access?
2. Since Acc stays at 0.0, is the pressure direction necessary-but-not-sufficient for actual task completion — would a "constraint-completion" direction (steering toward evidence gathering rather than away from commitment) compose better with PSPR?
3. Qwen3 backbones ran in thinking mode: does the probe read a pre-decision state inside the reasoning trace, and does pressure sensing transfer to non-thinking or RL-trained agents?
4. Could the probe score serve as a dense internal reward or curricula signal for self-evolving agents (e.g., fine-tuning on boundaries where pressure spiked but the correct action was to continue)?
5. How does the pressure state interact with benchmark-specific incentives — DeepPlanning-Travel rewards delivered plans; would the same state appear in settings where abstaining or returning "unsolved" is the correct closure?
