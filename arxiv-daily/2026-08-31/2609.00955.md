# ASSERT: Adaptive Stochastic Sampling for Robust Diffusion Models on Analog Compute-in-Memory Hardware

> **一句话总结：** 基于 100 颗物理 eFlash CIM 芯片实测数据校准噪声模型，发现扩散模型去噪的早期高噪声阶段对固定硬件权重扰动远比末段精修阶段脆弱（固定硬件映射导致跨步相关的预测误差持续传播），据此提出免训练、不增加任何网络评估次数的 ASSERT 采样器——早期保持最大随机性（η_max=0.8）、随后按余弦衰减过渡到确定性 DDIM 精修，在 CIFAR-10 步数实验中最高取得 7.68× 更低的 FID（500 步下 21.27 vs DDIM 的 163.38），在高分辨率数据集（噪声水平 0.08）上比 DDIM 降低 2.39–2.58× 平均 FID。

> **TL;DR:** Diffusion sampling is surprisingly fragile on analog compute-in-memory (CIM) hardware because a fixed spatial weight-perturbation map repeats across denoising steps, making prediction errors correlated. Grounded in a noise model validated on measurements from 100 physical eFlash CIM chips (R²=0.993), the authors show the early high-noise stage is most vulnerable and propose ASSERT: a training-free schedule that holds stochastic DDIM at η_max=0.8 early, then cosine-decays to deterministic refinement. On CIFAR-10 with hardware noise at 500 steps it reaches FID 21.27 vs 163.38 for deterministic DDIM (7.68× lower, same number of network evaluations), and on CelebA-HQ/LSUN at noise level 0.08 it cuts mean FID by 2.39–2.58× vs DDIM.

## 核心贡献

- **多芯片实证的鲁棒性刻画**：首次对扩散模型在模拟 CIM 硬件噪声下的行为做端到端研究，噪声模型用数百次实测芯片 MVM 样本拟合（权重噪声项聚合编程变差、漏电流、热噪声与随机电报噪声），并在 100 颗物理芯片的测量上验证（模型-实测误差 R²=0.993，KL 散度 0.0198）。
- **发现"早期最脆弱"的时间敏感性模式**：将同一带噪映射只施加于去噪早期（如 100 步调度中的 99–80 步）造成的 FID 恶化远大于施加于末段精修（20–0 步）；Figure 4/5 的分段实验清晰隔离了这一现象。
- **一阶轨迹误差分析**：将固定空间扰动 Δθ 经一阶线性化展开到最终样本（ε_S = Σ P_{S,t+1}·α_t·H_t·Δθ），说明所有步的误差共享同一方向性扰动、跨步正相关；并给出早期窗口传播增益不小 + 互项非负时的充分条件，以及随机性通过改变激活轨迹 H_t 来降低跨步对齐（E⟨u_i,u_j⟩ 项变负）的去相关机制。
- **ASSERT 采样器**：免训练、不改参数、不加网络评估次数——前 τ_trans 比例步数保持 η_max，之后按余弦调度 D(p)=(1+cos πp)/2 平滑衰减到 0（最后一步必为确定性）；η_max=0.8、20% 早期平台由 CIFAR-10 消融选出后跨数据集固定。
- **系统性实验**：5 个数据集（CIFAR-10 32×32 到 CelebA-HQ/LSUN 256×256）、多种噪声水平、多步数、4 种随机性调度（cosine/linear/step/reverse）对比 + η_max×onset 消融。

## Key Contributions

- **First multi-chip, measurement-grounded characterization of diffusion robustness on analog CIM**: the noise model (y = x^T(W+ε_W)+ε_y, σ²_W = α_W|W|+b_W) is fitted from several hundred measured chip MVM samples on a 28-nm eFlash platform (4×2.2-Mb arrays) and validated against 100 physical chips, achieving R²=0.993 and D_KL(p_chip||p_model)=0.0198.
- **Temporal sensitivity finding**: the early, high-noise denoising stage is substantially more vulnerable than the final refinement stage — applying the noisy mapping only at steps 99–80 degrades FID far more than applying it only at steps 20–0 — attributed to a fixed hardware mapping re-injecting directionally correlated prediction errors at every step (first-order analysis: ε_S = Σ_{t=1}^{S−1} P_{S,t+1} d_t, d_t = α_t H_t Δθ).
- **ASSERT sampler**: a training-free, zero-extra-NFE schedule — hold stochastic DDIM at η_max for the first τ_trans fraction of steps, then cosine-decay η_t to 0 for deterministic refinement. Injected stochasticity changes subsequent activation trajectories, decorrelating them from the persistent spatial error (E||ε_S||_stoch − E||ε_S||_det ≈ ΣΣ(E⟨u_i,u_j⟩_stoch − E⟨u_i,u_j⟩_det) < 0).
- **Comprehensive validation**: five datasets (CIFAR-10, Butterfly, CelebA-HQ, LSUN-Church, LSUN-Bedroom), three seeds, hyperparameters fixed across datasets after CIFAR-10 tuning, plus a η_max × onset ablation (best FID 5.95 at η_max=0.8, onset 90).

## Methodology

**Noise model (chip-calibrated).** Each CIM MVM is modeled as y = x^T(W + ε_W) + ε_y with weight noise ε_W ~ N(0, σ²_W) and output noise ε_y ~ N(0, σ²_y). The weight-noise variance follows σ²_W = α_W|W| + b_W, fitted from several hundred measured MVM samples on physical eFlash CIM chips (shared 28-nm platform, four 2.2-Mb arrays, 8.8-Mb total, 86 mW, 120/180 ns latency, 14.7 mm²). The fitted weight term aggregates programming variation, leakage, thermal noise, and random telegraph noise; the output term captures ADC error and short-timescale output fluctuation. Crucially, each mapped layer receives an independently sampled weight-perturbation map that is **fixed for the entire deployment** (the "persistent" spatial noise), while ε_y is redrawn for every MVM — so repeated denoising steps share the same dominant spatial perturbation. The model is validated on measurements from 100 chips.

**Persistent-noise error analysis.** Starting from the DDIM update, a prediction perturbation δε_t changes one update by δx_s = c_t δε_t; the cancellation in c_t limits *immediate* error, so end-to-end sensitivity requires propagating how a perturbed state alters all later evaluations. First-order linearization gives ε_{t+1} = J_t ε_t + α_t H_t Δθ, unrolled to ε_S = Σ_{t=1}^{S−1} P_{S,t+1} d_t with d_t = α_t H_t Δθ. Because Δθ is reused, all d_t share one underlying spatial perturbation, so propagated errors remain directionally aligned across steps. A sufficient condition for early-stage vulnerability is sketched (equal-length early/late windows where the propagation gain g_i = ||P_{i+1,t}||·||d_i|| is no smaller and cross terms ⟨u_i, u_j⟩ ≥ 0 within each window ⇒ terminal error no smaller for the early window). The authors are explicit that they do **not** empirically evaluate the implied Jacobian gains or cross-step cosines, leaving that to future work; the mechanism is instead tested at the output-distribution level via FID.

**ASSERT schedule.** ASSERT applies stochastic DDIM (η_t ∈ [0,1]) with a time-varying η_t: with normalized progress r_t = t/(S−1), η_t = η_max if r_t < τ_trans, otherwise η_max·D((r_t − τ_trans)/(1 − τ_trans)) with cosine decay D(p) = (1 + cos(πp))/2, ensuring η_{S−1} = 0. No retraining, no extra network evaluations; the σ_{t−1→t}(η_t) is recomputed per timestep pair. η_max = 0.8 and a 20% early plateau are selected from the CIFAR-10 ablation and kept fixed across all datasets. Baselines: deterministic DDIM, linear decay (step), and reverse step (stochasticity late rather than early, τ_trans = 0.4). Evaluation uses public Hugging Face checkpoints (BF16, native parameterization), FID over 50,000 images, three shared seeds with identical initial latents and deployment-noise realizations.

## Results & Highlights

- **CIFAR-10 step-count study (Table 1)**: under the calibrated hardware noise, noisy FID for deterministic DDIM explodes with step count — 33.53 at 100 steps and 163.38 at 500 steps — while ASSERT stays at 5.90 and 21.27 respectively (33.53/5.90 = 5.68×; 163.38/21.27 = 7.68× lower FID). Even at 10 steps ASSERT gives 20.16 vs DDIM's 29.37.
- **High-resolution datasets (noise level 0.08, three-seed mean FID)**: ASSERT achieves 2.39× (CelebA-HQ), 2.58× (LSUN-Church), and 2.54× (LSUN-Bedroom) lower mean FID than deterministic DDIM, and 1.36× / 1.22× / 1.51× lower than the linear-decay (step) schedule — the paper notes these are ratios of means, not absolute FID differences.
- **Noise model validation**: predicted vs measured error across 100 physical chips gives R² = 0.993 and KL divergence D_KL(p_chip||p_model) = 0.0198 (a polynomial fit, R² = 0.991, is reported only as an alternative diagnostic, not as the noise model). In the noise-source decomposition, ASSERT tracks the clean baseline closely while deterministic DDIM degrades most under persistent weight noise specifically.
- **Schedule comparison**: cosine decay (ASSERT) obtains the lowest FID among all stochastic schedules; reverse step (stochasticity concentrated in final refinement) performs far worse — confirming that *where* stochasticity is placed matters more than having it at all.
- **Ablation (Table 2, CIFAR-10)**: best FID is 5.95 at η_max = 0.8 with onset 90; too little stochasticity hurts badly (17.08 at η_max=0.6, onset 100) and so does too much (best value 7.58 at η_max=1.0, and 8.31–8.52 at long plateaus), supporting a short, strong stochastic phase followed by gradual deterministic refinement.
- **Honest trade-off reporting**: robustness is not free — 20-step ASSERT clean FID is 13.96 vs 11.08 for DDIM; noisy FID is non-monotonic in step count (500-step 21.27 vs 1000-step 80.59), and the 20-step noisy value (13.69) landing slightly below its clean value is flagged by the authors as requiring repeated-seed validation.

## Critical Assessment

- **Partially unvalidated theory**: the "sufficient condition for early-stage vulnerability" rests on assumptions (propagation gains no smaller for the early window, non-negative cross-step cosines) that the paper explicitly does not measure — "direct Jacobian-gain and cross-step-cosine measurements are left for future work." The decorrelation story is therefore verified only indirectly, through end-to-end FID; the mechanism could plausibly be a simpler stochastic-averaging effect.
- **Sampler-only baselines**: all comparisons are against variants of the same DDIM family (deterministic, linear, step, reverse). No comparison to the alternative mitigation axis the introduction itself cites — noise-aware fine-tuning [12,24] or hardware-level fixes such as noise-compensating ADC schemes [20] and robust CIM designs from the same groups [2,3] — so the claim that sampler-level robustness is the cheaper option is argued, not measured.
- **Simulation, not on-silicon end-to-end**: despite the chip grounding, the entire diffusion trajectory is executed in simulation with the fitted noise model ("the entire model executes on one mapping"), not by actually running sampling on the CIM chip; an end-to-end on-hardware demonstration is missing.
- **Tuning and dataset caveats**: η_max = 0.8 and the 20% plateau were tuned on CIFAR-10 (32×32) and the 7.68× headline number is from that small dataset; the ablation is CIFAR-10-only. Ratios at noise 0.08 are ratios of three-seed means, and single-seed behavior may be noisier given the authors' own repeated-seed caveat on the 20-step result.
- **Anomalies honestly but incompletely explained**: noisy FID is non-monotonic in step count (e.g., 1000-step noisy ASSERT 80.59 is ~4× worse than 500-step 21.27), attributed qualitatively to "repeated exposure to a fixed hardware perturbation," with no predictive model of where the optimum lies.
- **Clean-quality cost in low-NFE regimes**: at 20 steps — the regime where DDIM is typically used — ASSERT's clean FID (13.96) is ~26% worse than DDIM's (11.08), a real regression the summary numbers can hide.
- **Reproducibility gaps**: checkpoints are public, but the chip measurement data (Zhicun's 28-nm platform) is not available to outsiders and no code release is stated, so the calibrated noise model cannot be independently regenerated.

## Strengths

- Rare grounding in **real physical chip measurements** (fitted from hundreds of MVMs, validated on 100 chips with R²=0.993) instead of ad-hoc synthetic noise assumptions.
- The stage-sensitivity finding (Figure 4/5) is clean, well-isolated, and actionable: it converts directly into a concrete scheduling rule.
- The fix is **training-free, parameter-free, and NFE-neutral** — deployable to any existing DDIM-compatible checkpoint, unlike CIM fine-tuning approaches.
- Hyperparameters tuned once on CIFAR-10 and **fixed across five datasets** (32×32 → 256×256), which is a fair transfer protocol.
- Unusually candid error reporting: the paper flags its own anomalous result (20-step noisy < clean) and the ratio-vs-absolute-FID distinction.

## Limitations

- No end-to-end on-chip demonstration; everything runs in simulation against the fitted model on a single eFlash platform.
- The mechanistic theory (Jacobian gains, cross-step alignment) is asserted but not measured.
- Baselines limited to DDIM-family schedules; no retraining-based or hardware-based robustness comparisons, and no cost/Pareto comparison against them.
- Headline gains concentrated on CIFAR-10; high-resolution results are only shown as curve/ratio summaries at one noise level; no ImageNet- or text-to-image-scale (e.g., SD/DiT) evidence.
- Clean FID regression at short step counts; noisy FID non-monotonic in step count with no predictive account.
- Chip data and code are not released; results may not transfer to other CIM technologies (RRAM, PCM) without refitting.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the assumed propagation-gain and cross-step-cosine conditions be measured directly on real trajectories, and does the decorrelation mechanism survive that test — or is the gain mostly stochastic averaging?
- How does ASSERT compare quantitatively (FID *and* energy/cost) against noise-aware fine-tuning and hardware-level mitigations such as noise-compensating ADCs or robust CIM macro designs?
- Does the early-stochastic schedule transfer to transformer-based diffusion backbones (DiT) and text-to-image scale, where the "early stage = global structure formation" argument would need revalidation?
- Can η_max and the plateau length be auto-tuned online from chip telemetry (measured output-noise statistics) instead of a fixed CIFAR-10-tuned constant?
- What explains the non-monotonic noisy FID versus step count — can exposure to a fixed spatial perturbation be modeled well enough to predict the optimal step count per noise level?
- Does the schedule generalize to other analog technologies (RRAM, PCM) whose noise statistics differ from eFlash, given σ²_W = α_W|W| + b_W must be refitted?
