IoU — FALCON vs conventional binary IMC

3×3 morphological closing on a binary silhouette under 15% salt-and-pepper noise, N=1024 bit-streams per pixel.

Why this matters

A design-side route to tolerating silent data corruption: arithmetic that absorbs bit flips and voltage droop by construction — not detection.

Critical edge

“Functionally correct at 30% noise” hides degraded numbers — multiplication MAE still runs 17.8% there, and every result is simulation-only: no silicon, no measured MTJ endurance.

Methodology

  1. Deterministic encoding. Each bit Xi is replicated 2i times at predefined low-discrepancy positions of a 2n-length stream — the stream holds exactly X ones. No RNG hardware, no switching-probability calibration.
  2. Correlation by pattern choice. Same LD pattern → correlated streams (AND/OR → min/max); distinct patterns → independent streams (AND → multiply); XOR → absolute difference; a 2:1 MUX → scaled add/sub and division.
  3. LIM cell. 2 MTJs + 2 FinFETs per cell: a propagation phase writes resistance states, an evaluation phase senses resistance division against a reference MTJ through a dual-output amplifier.
  4. In-memory decoding. An LIM-based converter recovers weighted binary output in O(log N) steps.

Evaluation protocol

  • Behavioral MAE: 6 operations × 4 stream lengths × 0–30% injected noise.
  • 14nm FinFET HSPICE delay/power; ±3σ / 10% Monte Carlo over FinFET and MTJ parameters; supply scaling 0.9 → 0.7 V; morphological-closing case study.

  • Voltage scaling: at 0.8 V / 0.7 V every operation remains correct, cutting power ≈ 27% / 51% vs the 0.9 V nominal point.
  • Circuit (Table II): preparation delay 1.17–1.79 µs, evaluation delay 3.41–5.57 µs; power 48.4 µW for AND-based ops vs 144.5–144.8 µW for MUX-based ops.
  • ±3σ Monte Carlo across FinFET (Lg, FinH, TFin, ToxFin) and MTJ (free-layer area, TMR, RA): measurable delay/power spread, zero functional failures.
  • Energy: in-situ 256×256 closing costs 7.63 µJ — an estimated ~10× saving over a von Neumann sliding-window baseline (an unnumbered generic estimate, not a modern IMC accelerator).
  • Table III is qualitative — no identical-workload MAE/energy/area comparison against SCRIMP, SC-CRAM, Stoch-IMC or Ambit-class binary IMC.

Strengths

  • Genuinely eliminates RNG circuitry and probability calibration — the structural edge over intrinsic-stochasticity SC-IMC designs (SCRIMP, SC-CRAM, Stoch-IMC) whose stream statistics ride on uncontrollable device physics.
  • Principled, explicit inter-stream correlation control: min/max and multiplication coexist in one reconfigurable array.
  • Unusually complete reliability methodology — behavioral sweeps, 14nm HSPICE, ±3σ Monte Carlo spanning both FinFET and MTJ parameters, 3-point voltage scaling, each targeting a different failure mode.
  • Honest about the accuracy/latency tradeoff; Table I shows both trends and names the regime where shorter streams win.
  • Full arithmetic coverage — including division and absolute subtraction, which most SC-IMC designs omit — with all state kept in-memory.

Limitations

  • Simulation-only evidence: no silicon, no measured MTJ write endurance or write energy for deterministically encoding 1024-bit streams per pixel.
  • Low throughput by construction — N cycles per operation for N-bit serial streams; latency-critical suitability is argued, not benchmarked.
  • Arithmetic error under heavy noise stays large (MAE 15–18% at 30% noise even at 1024 bits); error accumulation over composed chains is unanalyzed.
  • Scaled semantics (MUX add returns (A+B)/2) complicate multi-operation pipelines — no treatment given.
  • Single case study (morphological closing); no DNN benchmark despite the Edge-AI framing, no mm² area figures, no head-to-head energy numbers vs named accelerators.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Head-to-head vs SCRIMP / Stoch-IMC on identical workloads and noise models — MAE, energy and area, not qualitative feature checklists?
  2. How does error accumulate when hundreds of SC operations compose (a full conv/FC chain), given the scaled MUX semantics?
  3. Can LD-pattern encoding extend to block-floating or adaptive-precision formats — and would a DNN inference benchmark substantiate the Edge-AI reliability claim better than closing?
  4. What is the measured MTJ write energy and endurance cost of the 1024-bit-per-pixel encoding — does 7.63 µJ survive at scale?
  5. Does deterministic correlation survive multi-operand reductions, where pairwise pattern assignments may conflict?
