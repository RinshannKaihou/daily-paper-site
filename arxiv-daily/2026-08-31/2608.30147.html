Same actor with prompted frontier critics: GPT-4.1 6.0% · Qwen3-235B 3.4% · Qwen2.5-72B 6.3% — fine-tuned critics win by up to +13.2 pts.

Why this matters

Anyone bolting an LLM judge onto an agent loop should note: prompted frontier critics erode reliability, while small fine-tuned critics make agents measurably more trustworthy.

Critical edge

The "+10.6 pts over GPT-OSS-120B" headline is measured against a soft anchor — Qwen3-235B posts 27.8% Retail pass⁴ and towers over every CAST model (best average 19.5%).

01Methodology

Problem. Tool-calling trajectories carry only a terminal 0/1 reward; one irreversible wrong action can sink the task, and failures surface across repeated trials. CAST learns a per-action verifier that outputs a structured rationale and a validity label — conditioned only on step-local context (never future observations; privileged info exists at annotation time only).

  • Stage 1 Qwen3-32B teacher (ReAct) runs 500 τ-Retail tasks × 5 rollouts, Qwen2.5-72B as user simulator.
  • Stage 2 Agentic annotation: rule-extractor + tool-extractor + three checkers (hallucination / domain violation / wrong tool) + verdict orchestrator → 2,500 step-level experiences.
  • Stage 3 CAST-Critic trained on rationale generation + verification classification; second rollout round verifies every teacher action in-loop; only reward-1 trajectories SFT the policy.
  • Setup Qwen3-4B/8B backbones, H100, 32k context, lr 5e-6, 3 epochs. Evaluated on τ-Retail (in-domain), Airline, Telecom, Telehealth with pass^k metrics.

  • Calibration is the mechanism: post-feedback correction success jumps from 43–46% (GPT-4.1 feedback) to 82–88% across all three error types.
  • Reliability, not single-shot: pass⁴ gains exceed pass¹ gains — critique SFT mainly buys repeated-trial robustness. Case study: CAST-Policy-8B goes 0/4 → 4/4 on Retail task 52 by verifying the irreversible exchange step first.
  • vs agentic frameworks: +14.9 pass⁴ over PALADIN, +4.3 over EvoTool; cheaper tokens/latency than IRMA/FAMA (though FAMA posts higher pass⁴ on several domains).
  • Behavior: optimal verification budget is actor-dependent and reliability is non-monotonic in refinement rounds (32B peaks at 3 rounds, 19.9%).

03Strengths

  • Deterministic recipe: sparse rewards → mechanically checkable action labels with a concrete failure taxonomy that transfers beyond τ-bench-style domains.
  • The calibration analysis is the paper's best contribution — a quantified explanation (46.8% → 11.4% false-flag rate) for why prompted judges hurt and a fine-tuned 8B critic helps.
  • A genuinely useful negative result: three frontier models as prompted critics all degrade the actor's pass⁴ (11.5% → 3.4–6.3%).
  • RFT is the right control, isolating +4.3 pass⁴ from critique-rationale supervision at 4B scale.
  • Practical serving: standalone policy needs no critic at run time; lower token/latency cost than IRMA/FAMA/PALADIN.
  • Unusual documentation: full prompts, hyperparameters, and an honest failure case in the appendix.

04Limitations

  • SFT only — the critic is not yet an on-policy RL reward model, so the policy never learns from its own exploration.
  • Verification is myopic: step-local validity, no forward-looking rationale about downstream consequences.
  • Critic-guided inference can hurt: Retail pass⁴ 9.6% vs 16.5% for the 4B policy alone; RFT-8B beats Policy-8B+Critic on Telehealth across the board.
  • RFT control is confounded by fresher, larger critic-filtered data; the multi-agent annotation pipeline itself is never ablated.
  • OOD evidence rests on 18–20-task τ-trait domains, no error bars or significance tests; several key comparisons exist only as figure bars; no code or data release.
  • All four environments are English customer-service simulations — user-simulator bias is unexamined.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Would CAST-Critic as an on-policy RL reward model (GRPO-style) close the gap to Qwen3-235B, instead of SFT on teacher trajectories?
  2. Can the critic do forward-looking verification — and would that fix the Telehealth and in-domain Retail regressions of critic-guided inference?
  3. Is the multi-agent annotation decomposition necessary, or would a single prompted Qwen3.5-122B verifier produce equally usable labels at lower cost?
  4. If RFT received the same second-round critic-in-the-loop data budget, how much of the +4.3 pass⁴ delta would survive?
  5. How do gains transfer where domain rules are not enumerable text (code agents, scientific-discovery loops), and are 18–20-task results stable under bootstrap resampling?
  6. What is the end-to-end annotation cost, and at what deployment scale does it amortize against plain rejection fine-tuning?
