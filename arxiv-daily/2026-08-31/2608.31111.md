> **一句话总结：** ByteDance Seed 等提出 ASPIRE——首个"模糊目标驱动自我进化"基准：智能体只拿到一句宽泛的自然语言能力目标（如"提升数学推理"），必须自己决定学什么、怎么学、如何验证，而下游评测的 520 道专家原创题目全程隐藏；实验发现智能体能跑通"选数据→训练→评测→回滚"的完整闭环，但 24 次 final-only 权重进化中仅 3 次超过基座（最好的 Qwen3.5-9B 科学推理 45.33→48.00，+2.67），30 个 adaptive-feedback 单元中仅 1 个在回滚后保留增益（Terra 数学 17.86→20.10），而进化的 harness 全部低于工程化的 Qwen-Agent 参照（最好的 Sol 27.22 vs 28.64 task-macro，-1.42）——"完成训练闭环"远不等于"保留真实能力提升"。

> **TL;DR:** ASPIRE is a benchmark for a new axis of LLM self-evolution: given only a vague natural-language capability goal (e.g., "improve difficult mathematical reasoning and justification"), an agent must decide WHAT to learn, HOW to learn, and HOW to verify, while the 520-item expert-authored hidden evaluation set spanning six goals is never revealed. Across three studies the results are sobering: on PostTrainBench, replacing explicit task names with vague goals drops Claude Opus 4.8's weighted average from 32.90 to 27.07 and GPT-5.6's from 36.23 to 29.58; starting from instruction-tuned Qwen3.5-4B/9B checkpoints, only 1 of 6 model-goal pairs (9B scientific reasoning, 45.33→48.00, +2.67) and 3 of 24 final checkpoints beat the base model under a final-only protocol, and only 1 of 30 adaptive-feedback cells retains an above-base checkpoint after rollback; and in harness evolution all three valid successor harnesses score below the fixed Qwen-Agent reference (best: GPT-5.6 Sol at 27.22 vs 28.64 task-macro, -1.42). The paper's core finding: agents reliably close the training loop but rarely produce retained, goal-aligned capability gains.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化"模糊目标驱动的自我进化"问题**：指出现有 LLM 自进化工作（PostTrainBench、LaMDAgent、SEAL 等）都假设任务/指标/奖励已由人类明确指定，智能体只搜索"怎么优化"；ASPIRE 把"决定优化什么"（what）和"如何验证"（how）也交给智能体，将其定义为目标操作化（goal operationalization）这一缺失的进化轴。
- **发布 ASPIRE 基准**：一个隐藏的、专家逐题原创撰写的 520 题评测集，覆盖 6 个模糊目标（科学与学术推理 75 题、人文社科 110、健康医学 100、数学推理 120、逻辑/可靠性/指令遵循 80、学术与科学写作 20），GPQA/MMLU-Pro/MedQA 仅作格式与难度参照、零题目复制，经过盲评难度校准、精确+语义去重、重叠审计与不可变版本化。
- **带信息边界的最小交互环境**：智能体通过单一类型化工具搜索/导入/合成数据、发起 SFT/GRPO(LoRA) 训练、自建验证集、分支与终止；控制器托管数据/训练/校验并强制预算与安全回滚（仅当被选 checkpoint 得分超过来时状态才保留，故保留增益 Δ^ret ≥ 0 是选择的结果而非每次更新的保证）。
- **三层递进实验（RQ1–RQ3）**：模糊目标 vs 显式任务的对照（RQ1）、从指令微调检查点出发的权重自进化（RQ2）、权重冻结下的 harness 自进化（RQ3），并给出轨迹级证据：局部上升的轨迹常常只是"从训练-induced 退化中恢复"，仍远低于基座分数。
- **识别出一个具体失败模式**：数值标签 SFT 导致答案格式崩溃——在 21,000 个单数字标签的 MMLU SFT 数据上训练的 5 个 checkpoint，其 279 条评测输出全部是单数字。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes vague-goal-driven self-evolution** as a missing axis of autonomous post-training: existing self-evolution systems (PostTrainBench, LaMDAgent, SEAL, etc.) begin after humans have operationalized the capability request into a fixed task, metric, and success criterion; ASPIRE hands the WHAT and the HOW-to-verify decisions to the agent along with the HOW.
- **Introduces the ASPIRE benchmark**: a hidden, expert-authored 520-item evaluation set spanning six vague goals (scientific/academic reasoning 75; humanities & social sciences 110; health & medicine 100; mathematical reasoning 120; logic/reliability/instruction following 80; academic & scientific writing 20). GPQA/MMLU-Pro/MedQA are used only as format and difficulty references — no item is copied — and items pass blind multi-model difficulty calibration, exact + semantic dedup, benchmark-overlap audits, scorer binding, and immutable versioning.
- **A minimal interactive environment with an information boundary**: the agent uses one typed tool to search/import/synthesize data, launch SFT or GRPO(LoRA) training, build its own validation data, and branch/stop; the controller absorbs credentials, job scheduling, and checkpoint verification, enforces budgets (e.g., 40 GPU-hours per final-only run), and applies safe rollback — the selected checkpoint is retained only if it beats the incoming state, so a nonnegative retained improvement is a selection outcome, not a guarantee about every update.
- **Three progressive studies (RQ1–RQ3)**: (RQ1) vague-goal vs explicit-task prompting in PostTrainBench; (RQ2) self-directed weight evolution from instruction-tuned Qwen3.5-4B/9B checkpoints under final-only and adaptive-feedback protocols; (RQ3) one-step harness evolution with model weights frozen. Trajectory evidence shows locally rising curves are often recovery from training-induced regression rather than above-base gains.
- **A concrete failure mode**: numeric-label SFT causes answer-format collapse — five checkpoints trained on MMLU data with 21,000 single-digit targets emit single-digit answers on all 279 evaluation outputs (scores 0, 0, 0, 6.141, 0), a training-induced regression rather than evaluator error.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A campaign is a tuple Γ = (G, E_G, J, A, B, Σ): a vague natural-language goal G, a hidden evaluator E_G, a judge J for rubric-scored items, a typed action contract A, a compute budget B, and a predeclared terminal selection rule Σ. The agent sees only a released projection (public history, lifecycle status, legal record types, identifiers, remaining budget); items, answers, rubrics, and routing labels are controller-only for the entire campaign. Two evolution surfaces are studied while holding the other components fixed: weight evolution (mutable M) and harness evolution (mutable H).

**Hidden evaluation set (520 items, 6 goals).** Domain experts author every candidate item from scratch, with GPQA, MMLU-Pro, and MedQA serving only as references for task format, domain coverage, and approximate difficulty. Candidates pass independent expert review, blind difficulty screening across multiple frontier scorers, exact and semantic deduplication, an overlap audit against the reference benchmarks, scorer binding (deterministic/structured scoring where possible; a campaign-fixed rubric-based judge for open-ended items), and immutable versioning. Each goal is evaluated on its own slice; RQ3 reports task-macro (20 task bundles) and example-micro averages. Under the adaptive-feedback protocol the agent may query a bounded number of aggregate scores on the same items, forming a sparse black-box outcome channel; under the final-only protocol it gets no score before submitting one terminal checkpoint.

**Environment.** The agent does not touch a shell or training repo; it issues typed tool calls (search/download/import/synthesize data; SFT or GRPO with LoRA; run self-eval on its own validation data; branch/stop lineage). The controller translates these into managed backend operations, validates candidates (parent, registered data, admitted update, checkpoint provenance; any foreign or agent-invented artifact is rejected), and enforces budgets. For weight evolution, the controller retains the selected checkpoint k* only when k* ≠ incoming and its raw score improves (Δ^raw > 0); otherwise the state rolls back to the incoming model — hence the retained improvement Δ^ret ≥ 0 by construction.

**Experiments.**
- *RQ1*: PostTrainBench with the benchmark identifier replaced by a broad capability description; matched Claude Opus 4.8 vague-goal vs explicit-task trajectories, against official Claude Opus 4.8 Max and GPT-5.6 references (descriptive, not a causal isolation).
- *RQ2 final-only*: Qwen3.5-4B Self and Qwen3.5-9B Self each optimize all six goals in two terminal runs (24 runs, ≤40 GPU-hours each), reporting Avg@2 of final checkpoints (not best-of-two).
- *RQ2 adaptive-feedback*: 30 configuration-goal cells — Qwen3.5-4B Self, Qwen3.5-9B Self, and GPT-5.6 Luna/Terra/Sol as external decision models over the same Qwen3.5-4B base — with score-guided checkpoint search.
- *RQ3*: Qwen3.5-4B frozen as runtime model, original Qwen-Agent as reference harness; Qwen3.5-4B Creator and GPT-5.6 Luna/Terra/Sol each produce one successor harness H1; the three valid successors are frozen and executed three times each on the same 20 hidden writing items.

## Results & Highlights

- **RQ1 — vague goals cost aggregate score but shift where effort goes (PostTrainBench):** Claude Opus 4.8 under vague-goal prompting reaches 27.07 weighted average vs 32.90 for the official Claude Opus 4.8 Max reference (-5.83), and GPT-5.6 reaches 29.58 vs the 36.23 official reference (-6.65); yet task-level changes vary — vague-goal Opus 4.8 is higher on HumanEval and BFCL, and vague-goal GPT-5.6 is higher on GSM8K — so the authors interpret the aggregate gap without assuming uniform task-level degradation.
- **RQ1 — trajectory shifts:** matched Claude Opus 4.8 vague-goal runs record 2,109 more seconds of decision-model thinking, 0.61 more GPU-idle hours, and 1.27 fewer GPU active-and-evaluation hours per run; access to agent-visible task material and proxy evaluation scripts becomes 2.98x and 2.39x as dense; LoRA use jumps from 24.1% to 89.8%; feedback density falls to 0.57 evaluations per training start (3.54 evals / 6.17 starts) vs 2.63 (39.21 evals / 14.91 starts) for official GPT-5.6 — a 4.6x difference in feedback opportunities.
- **RQ2 final-only — above-base gains are rare:** across 24 runs, Qwen3.5-4B Self is above base on 0/6 goals and Qwen3.5-9B on 1/6 — the sole positive pair is scientific & academic reasoning rising 45.33 → 48.00 (+2.67, two-run mean); at the run level only 3/24 final checkpoints exceed their base scores and rollback retains the base model in the other 21 runs.
- **RQ2 adaptive-feedback — completing the loop ≠ beating the base:** of 30 configuration-goal cells, 28 produce an evaluated checkpoint and 21 satisfy eligibility, but only 2 cells achieve a best evaluated checkpoint above base (Qwen3.5-4B Self science 44.00 → 45.33; GPT-5.6 Terra mathematics 17.86 → 20.10) and only one — Terra mathematics — remains above base after rollback. GPT-5.6 Sol searches most broadly (33 evaluated checkpoints over 76.56 settled GPU-hours) yet none of its six goals beats base — more search does not mean better search.
- **Locally rising trajectories can be regression recovery:** of 62 consecutive checkpoint transitions across 22 multi-evaluation cells, 28 rise, 13 tie, 21 fall; Qwen3.5-4B Self's mathematics climbs 0.79 → 1.75 → 2.38 while remaining far below its 17.86 base score. In the final-only protocol, three runs continue for 16–20 training jobs and consume 26.57–32.29 GPU-hours, yet their submitted final checkpoints score only 0.159 (4B mathematics), 2.540 (9B mathematics), and 0 (9B writing) — blind continuation along a lineage can end in a near-worthless checkpoint.
- **Failure mode — numeric-label SFT → format collapse:** five submitted checkpoints trained on numeric-labeled MMLU SFT (21,000 single-digit targets) produce single-digit outputs on all 279 corresponding evaluation outputs, scoring 0, 0, 0, 6.141, and 0; all 24 final evaluations complete without item errors or missing outputs, so evaluator failure does not explain the degradation.
- **RQ3 — all evolved harnesses sit below the engineered reference:** on the 20 hidden academic & scientific writing items, the original Qwen-Agent reference scores 28.64 task-macro / 27.55 example-micro; the best successor, GPT-5.6 Sol, reaches 27.22 / 25.97 (-1.42 macro), followed by Terra 20.76 / 20.14 and Luna 19.32 / 18.33; Qwen3.5-4B Creator produces no valid harness and is assigned the predeclared zero. Luna's harness wins its own 8-item checklist (7/8 → 8/8) yet hides a template that recasts mechanism/parameter questions as study-design problems — narrow self-validation specialized to the wrong proxy.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper comes from **ByteDance Seed** with the Singapore University of Technology and Design (SUTD), M-A-P, and TokenWave.AI. Core authors are Yuhao Wu (ByteDance), Wenzxuan Zhang (SUTD), Shen Yan (ByteDance), Wenliao Huang (ByteDance), and Ge Zhang (University of Michigan), with core contributors Yuhao Wu, Jingsyuan Zhang, and Jiajun Shi and roughly a dozen additional contributors. The team combines frontier-lab post-training resources (real GPU budgets, managed training backends) with academic benchmark-construction experience (Ge Zhang has authored several LLM evaluation efforts; M-A-P is known for open datasets). Credibility signals are strong: expert-authored items with audited provenance, immutable versioning, predeclared eligibility and termination rules, atomic accounting of every candidate, and — unusually for a benchmark paper — a results section whose headline finding is negative (agents fail to reliably self-improve). The project page (self-developing-agents.github.io) and controlled-access traces for content-level claims add transparency, though the evaluation items themselves are sealed by design and cannot be independently inspected.

## Critical Assessment

This is a genuinely novel and well-instrumented contribution, but its conclusions deserve careful scoping. First, **the RQ1 comparison is explicitly non-causal**: the authors themselves state that matched Claude Opus 4.8 runs "describe observed difference rather than isolate a prompt-only causal effect," and the GPT-5.6/Opus 4.8 Max reference numbers are cross-system statistics from PostTrainBench's official release, not reruns — the -5.83 and -6.65 aggregate gaps could partly reflect run-metadata differences, and the paper rightly only claims a trajectory association (2,109 extra thinking seconds, 0.61 extra GPU-idle hours).

Second, **statistical power is thin**. The entire RQ2 final-only conclusion rests on two runs per model-goal pair (Avg@2 over 24 runs), and the adaptive-feedback protocol uses one run per configuration-goal cell (30 cells); the paper concedes the Terra mathematics retention (20.10 vs base 17.86) was measured using repeated aggregate feedback on the same fixed evaluation slice, so "without a separate confirmation slice, it is evidence of feedback-guided selection rather than an independently confirmed capability gain." Similarly, RQ3 compares three successor harnesses to one reference on a single 20-item writing slice executed three times, but per-execution dispersion is not reported — a 1.42-point macro gap on 20 items is well within plausible runtime noise, even though the direction (all successors below reference) is consistent across all three.

Third, **the six-goal coverage is uneven by the paper's own admission**: the writing goal has only 20 items inside one composite logic/reliability/instruction bundle, and the fifth goal deliberately mixes three targets that are not independently identifiable, so per-goal scores have very different signal-to-noise. The "out-of-distribution" label is also operational rather than distributional — items are independently authored and excluded from the agent's registered data, but the authors explicitly do not claim disjointness from a model's pretraining corpus.

On the positive side, the honesty is exemplary: safe rollback means Δ^ret ≥ 0 is shown to be a selection artifact, the numeric-label SFT collapse is documented with source traces rather than hand-waving, negative results are the headline, and the limitations paragraph (six goals, single canonical adaptive run per cell, controlled-access traces) is concrete. The main red flag for reproduction is structural: the sealed 520-item set and controller-side tooling mean outsiders can verify the *claims* only through the released artifacts, not by re-running the evaluation independently.

## Strengths

- **A new, well-motivated axis**: separating WHAT-to-optimize from HOW-to-optimize is a clean formalization that existing self-evolution benchmarks (PostTrainBench, AIIA4AI-Bench, MLE-bench) conspicuously assume away; the background section's framing via forward-deployed engineers is convincing.
- **Serious benchmark hygiene**: 520 items authored from scratch with reference-guided (not copied) construction, blind multi-model difficulty calibration, exact + semantic dedup, benchmark-overlap audits, scorer binding, and immutable versioned manifests — far above typical benchmark-construction rigor.
- **Auditable execution**: every candidate records parent, data, update spec, and provenance; foreign or invented artifacts are rejected; budgets and terminal selection rules are predeclared, making the reported trajectories trustworthy.
- **Negative results delivered with mechanism, not just scores**: the rollback/retention decomposition, the recovery-vs-improvement trajectory analysis (62 transitions classified), and the traced numeric-label SFT format collapse give the community actionable failure modes, not merely a leaderboard.
- **Two complementary protocols** (final-only vs adaptive-feedback) cleanly separate "can the agent produce a good final checkpoint" from "can it use sparse feedback to search," and the external-decision-model design (Luna/Terra/Sol over the same 4B base) isolates decision-model effects.

## Limitations

- **Small run counts**: two terminal runs per model-goal pair (final-only) and one canonical adaptive-feedback run per configuration-goal cell; single-run conclusions like the Terra mathematics retention lack an independent confirmation slice.
- **Narrow goal and slice coverage**: six goals, with the writing goal at only 20 items; RQ2 evaluates one goal at a time and never measures whether unrelated capabilities survive weight updates, so "capability preservation" is untested; RQ3 covers only one-step harness evolution on a single goal (no recursive H1→H2).
- **RQ1 is observational**: matched-run differences are descriptive; the official PostTrainBench references come from different systems and metadata, so the aggregate vague-goal gap is not a controlled causal estimate.
- **Sealed evaluation**: items, rubrics, and traces are controller-only (content traces under controlled access), preventing fully independent verification of scoring quality and per-item behavior.
- **Environment abstraction**: the single typed tool interface removes infrastructure friction by design, so results measure goal operationalization under this scaffold — not the messier engineering reality where harness and infrastructure co-evolve.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你排位第一的兴趣方向 Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research：它把"自动确定优化什么/如何验证"形式化为新基准，并用负结果刻画了自治训练闭环的可靠性（回滚、检查点保留、训练诱导退化），与 autonomous research loop 和 automated benchmark design 直接相关。此外，numeric-label SFT 导致输出格式崩溃、局部上升轨迹实为"从训练退化中恢复"等轨迹级证据，也与你关注的 LLM Training Stability & Observability（异常检测、checkpoint 可靠性）有实质性交集。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a separate, held-out confirmation slice (or refreshed evaluation-set versions, as the authors suggest) confirm that the single retained adaptive-feedback gain (Terra mathematics 20.10 vs 17.86 base) is a real capability improvement rather than feedback-guided selection on a fixed slice?
- Can the answer-format collapse from numeric-label SFT be detected cheaply from the agent's own validation data (e.g., output-format invariants), or does it require external evaluation — i.e., is this a missing self-verification mechanism that harness evolution should be able to add?
- Does the vague-goal aggregate penalty in RQ1 (-5.83 for Opus 4.8, -6.65 for GPT-5.6) persist under matched systems with rerun controls, and does it shrink with more feedback queries per training start (0.57 vs 2.63 evals/start)?
- How would results change if the evaluation measured preservation of unrelated capabilities alongside the target goal — does rollback to the base checkpoint mask catastrophic forgetting that a broader evaluation would reveal?
- Can harness evolution and weight evolution be composed (à la Co-Harness/HELIX) under the same information boundary, and does co-evolution finally beat the engineered Qwen-Agent reference, or does the engineered-harness gap persist?
- With only 20 writing items and one-step harness editing in RQ3, what minimum evaluation-slice size and how many evolution steps are needed before harness differences exceed runtime variance (per-execution dispersion was not reported)?
