The Structure of Quantization Damage in LLMs: Why the Next Bit Should Be Spent Globally

Jundong Hu, Shekar Ramachandran · PayPal AI

  • quantization
  • mixed precision
  • causal intervention
  • LLM evaluation
Key Metric +21–52 pts

gap-recovery margin of global group-128 over oracle-selected local 8-bit repair at matched +0.146 eff. bits/weight — 8/8 models, P(global>local) ≥ 0.95.

Why This Matters

Spend small bit budgets on finer group scales everywhere instead of protecting “important” layers — and never trust interpretability localizers without causal validation.

Critical Edge

Tested only on models ≤8B under a nonstandard task mix, with no code release — the headline rule is unvalidated at the scales where quantization matters most commercially.

Methodology

  1. Causal instrument, not heuristics. Mixed-precision intervention as ground truth: raise a candidate layer to 8-bit, keep the rest 4-bit, and measure the recovered fraction of the 4-bit→8-bit gap — the marginal value of precision at that location.
  2. Coverage. 9 open-weight models across 4 architecture families and a 16× size range; CORE continuation-scoring harness, 22 tasks × 200 samples, seed 1337; damage = each model's fp16→4-bit CORE gap.
  3. Three hypotheses under test. H1 task-circuit drift (per-head activation deviation); H2 computation site via causal activation patching (protect the boundary MLP pair); H3 weight statistics (std, reconstruction error → “fragile” pair). A localizer counts only where the intervention shows a precision benefit.
  4. Diffuseness. Oracle-ordered top-k restoration curves; additivity validated (pair recoveries 0.91–1.10× the sum of singles).
  5. Matched-budget duel. +0.146 effective bits/weight spent either globally (per-row → group-128 scales everywhere) or locally (oracle top-k whole layers → 8-bit) — an upper bound that biases the comparison in local's favor.
  6. Statistics. 5,000 task-bootstrap resamples, TOST equivalence, within-model permutation, multi-seed calibration; a single 40GB A100.

Results & Highlights

Global granularity beats even an oracle local repair on every compatible model, and the damage structure explains why.

  • Damage is diffuse, 9/9 models: recovering 50/75/90% of the gap takes ~20/49/73% of layers; max single-layer damage runs from 43.9% (Mistral-7B) down to 8.1% (Qwen3-1.7B).
  • All three cheap localizers fail causally: circuit drift's raw r=+0.377 collapses to +0.054 (n.s., TOST-equivalent to zero) after model+category control; boundary-pair protection recovers ≤13% of the gap in 6/9 models; weight-std pairs find the true peak only in the LLaMA family (−0.1% on Qwen3-8B).
  • Granularity, not algorithm: per-row→g128 RTN gains +0.095 CORE; GPTQ adds only +0.020 and AWQ +0.017 over g128.
  • 8-bit is near-lossless: fp16 − RTN8 ≤ 0.005 on all 8 models — the 4-bit residual is budget-limited, not precision-limited.
  • How one-sided is it? Local repair would need 5.16–6.33 bits/weight — 7–15× the increment — to match global.

Strengths

  • The right instrument. Causal ground truth (mixed-precision intervention) instead of correlational heuristics — the correct test for a localization question, and the source of the most durable findings.
  • The comparison is biased against itself. The local arm is oracle-selected, so global's win is, if anything, understated.
  • Careful uncertainty treatment: task-level bootstrap, TOST equivalence, permutation tests, multi-seed calibration and eval.
  • Negative results are quantified, not asserted — each refutation (H1–H3) comes with effect sizes and controls.
  • Honest, specific limitations section that scopes every claim; transparent handling of the Qwen3-8B GPTQ>fp16 outlier.
  • Broad coverage for the budget: 9 models, 4 families, 16× size range, full RTN/GPTQ/AWQ 4-bit/8-bit ladder.

Limitations

  • Untested alternatives. Weight-level salience protection and Hessian-based methods (HAWQ-style) are untested; “no few-layer fix” is scoped to greedy, layer-granularity, weight-only interventions.
  • Maximal-damage regime is probed only under per-row RTN; whether the diffuse structure holds for the smaller gaps of calibrated GPTQ/AWQ g128 is unknown.
  • Scale ceiling: only ≤8B models actually tested (40GB A100, ≈58B ceiling); family-level generalization rests on n=1 for Mistral and OpenLLaMA.
  • Oracle selectors are scored on the same eval set and are not deployable; only Qwen3-8B's pair recovery is multi-seeded; smaller-model recoveries sit near the noise floor.
  • Nonstandard evaluation: 22 tasks × 200 samples with continuation scoring — no WikiText-2 perplexity or MMLU, so numbers are incomparable to the mainstream quantization literature; no code release mentioned; budget accounting is first-order, weight-only.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the diffuse-damage structure persist for calibrated methods (GPTQ/AWQ g128) and at scales beyond 8B, where per-layer outliers may concentrate damage differently?
  2. Would weight-level or Hessian-based salience protection (SpQR/HAWQ-style sub-layer allocation) change the “no few-layer fix” verdict?
  3. LLaMA-3.x peaks at layer 1 at every size (leave-one-out 3/3) — can a deployable, non-oracle selector be built from architecture family alone, and does it transfer to unseen families?
  4. How does the global-vs-local verdict change under exact byte-level accounting and a multi-bit rate-distortion allocation (e.g., 3-bit base with selective 6-bit)?
  5. Is 8-bit near-losslessness robust under longer-generation, chat-style, or agentic evaluation, where small per-token logit shifts could compound, rather than 200-sample continuation scoring?
