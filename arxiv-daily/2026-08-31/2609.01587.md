> **一句话总结：** 对 9 个开源 LLM（4 个架构家族、16 倍规模跨度）用逐层 8-bit 混合精度因果干预作为 ground truth，证明 4-bit 量化损伤是弥散的（平均恢复 50/75/90% 的精度差距需要 ~20/49/73% 的层），任务电路、计算位置、权重统计三类廉价定位器全部失效；而在等预算（+0.146 有效 bits/weight）下，把这点预算全局花在细化到 group-128 粒度上，比 oracle 挑选局部整层升 8-bit 的修复多恢复 21–52 个百分点（如 Llama-3-8B 78.8% vs 44.7%，Qwen3-8B 76.6% vs 54.9%）——"下一个 bit"应该全局花，而不是局部花。

> **TL;DR:** On 9 open-weight LLMs evaluated with the CORE harness (22 tasks × 200 samples, seed 1337), the authors test three intuitions about where 4-bit quantization damage lives — task circuits (H1), computation site via causal activation patching (H2), and weight statistics (H3) — against a causal ground truth: raising candidate layers to 8-bit and measuring the fraction of the 4-bit→8-bit gap recovered. All three localizers fail and the damage is diffuse (recovering 90% of the gap takes ~73% of layers on average; no single layer exceeds ~44% of the damage). At a matched budget of +0.146 effective bits/weight, spending globally on group-128 granularity beats oracle-selected local layer repair on all 8 group-128-compatible models by 21–52 points of gap recovery (Llama-3-8B: 78.8% vs 44.7%; even the most concentrated model Qwen3-8B: 76.6% vs 54.9%), and the 4-bit gain is driven by granularity (+0.095 CORE from per-row→g128 RTN) not by calibration algorithms (GPTQ +0.020, AWQ +0.017 over g128), while 8-bit RTN is near-lossless (fp16−RTN8 ≤ 0.005).

## 核心贡献

- **提出"因果干预作为 ground truth"的定位方法论**：不信任任何廉价定位器（电路漂移、激活修补、权重统计），而是用混合精度干预——把候选层升到 8-bit、其余保持 4-bit、测量恢复的精度差距比例（该位置的 precision 边际价值）——来直接检验"损伤在哪里"。
- **证伪三个直觉假设**：H1 任务电路漂移与损伤的原始相关 (+0.377) 在控制 model+category 后坍缩到 +0.054（n.s.，TOST 等价于零）；H2 因果激活修补定位的"边界层对"保护在 6/9 模型上只恢复 ≤13% 的差距；H3 权重标准差"脆弱对"只在 LLaMA 家族碰巧命中真峰（Qwen3-8B 上为 −0.1%）。
- **损伤是弥散的**：按 oracle 顺序逐层恢复 8-bit，平均需要 ~20/49/73% 的层才能恢复 50/75/90% 的差距；单层损伤最大 ~44%（Mistral-7B），"几层修复"不存在。
- **等预算下的资源分配法则**：+0.146 有效 bits/weight 时，全局细化到 g128 比.oracle 局部层修复在全部 8 个 group-128 兼容模型上多恢复 21–52 个百分点；局部修复要追平全局需要 5.16–6.33 bits/weight（是被替换增量的 7–15 倍）。
- **两个次级发现**：(a) 8-bit 是近无损的（RTN8 的 fp16−RTN8 ≤ 0.005，6/8 模型 CI 含零），残余差距是预算受限而非精度受限；(b) 恢复峰的位置在家族内随规模相关（LLaMA-3.x 各尺寸峰值都在 L1，leave-one-out 3/3 命中），但跨家族不迁移。

## Key Contributions

- A **causal ground truth** for localization claims: mixed-precision intervention (raise each candidate layer/set to 8-bit, keep the rest 4-bit, measure the recovered fraction of the 4-bit→8-bit gap) — the "marginal value of precision" at each location — used to test, rather than assume, where damage lives.
- **Refutation of three cheap localizers**: task-circuit drift (H1), computation site via causal activation patching (H2), and weight statistics — weight std and reconstruction error (H3) — none predicts layer-level recovery; correlational signals do not survive causal validation.
- **The damage is diffuse**: oracle-ordered top-k restoration needs ~20/49/73% of layers on average to recover 50/75/90% of the gap; every model has >44% of its damage spread beyond any single layer, so "no few-layer fix" holds at layer granularity.
- **A resource-allocation rule**: at matched +0.146 effective bits/weight, global granularity (per-row → group-128 scales everywhere) beats oracle-selected local layer repair by 21–52 points on all 8 group-128-compatible models, unconditional within the tested budget; the result is robust to task-bootstrap resampling (P(global>local) ≥ 0.95 for 8/8).
- **Granularity, not algorithm, drives the 4-bit gain**, and 8-bit is near-lossless: mean +0.095 CORE from per-row→g128 RTN vs only +0.020 (GPTQ) and +0.017 (AWQ) over g128; the fp16−RTN8 residual is ≤0.005 on all 8 models.

## Methodology

**Models and evaluation.** 9 open-weight models spanning 4 architecture families and a 16× size range: Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Llama-3-8B (Meta), Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/8B, Mistral-7B, and OpenLLaMA-3B. Each is evaluated on 22 tasks at 200 samples/task with a fixed continuation-scoring harness (CORE; Li et al., 2024) in a nanochat-style loop, seed 1337. Tasks (e.g., squad, hellaswag, arc_challenge, dyck_languages) cluster into 5 reproducible groups stable across all 9 models (Appendix A). Quantization damage is defined as a model's fp16→4-bit CORE gap.

**Three hypotheses, one causal test.** Each hypothesis comes with a cheap localizer computed on a common calibration set: (H1) each task's head-deviation profile (per-head activation relative to cross-task mean), clustered into 22 tasks → 5 groups; damage-location prediction = tasks whose circuits drift more (directional drift 1 − cos(c_fp16, c_int4)) should lose more accuracy. (H2) causal activation patching (Meng et al., 2022) localizes computation to the boundary pair (first and last MLP), suggesting protecting that pair. (H3) per-layer weight statistics (standard deviation; reconstruction error) identify a "fragility" pair to protect. All three are tested against the same causal instrument: **mixed-precision intervention** — raise the chosen layer or set to 8-bit in turn, leave the rest at 4-bit, and read the fraction of the 4-bit→8-bit gap recovered. A localizer counts only if it identifies locations where the intervention shows a precision benefit.

**Diffuseness measurement.** Layers are ranked by their own protect-one recovery (oracle ordering) and the top-k layers are restored to 8-bit; recovery curves vs k quantify how many layers must be restored (Appendix E). Additivity is validated: pair recoveries match the sum of the two single-layer recoveries with ratios 0.91–1.10.

**Matched-budget allocation (Section 5, Appendix G).** From per-row 4-bit RTN, a +0.146 effective bits/weight increment is spent in two ways: *globally* (per-row → group-128 scales everywhere) or *locally* (restore the top-k most recoverable whole layers to 8-bit, k ≈ 0.0365·n_L, interpolated along the top-k curve). The local arm is oracle-selected (an upper bound, not deployable). Recovery is scored as % of the per-row RTN4→RTN8 gap, making the two arms directly comparable. Equal-budget accounting is first-order, weight-only, with non-integer layer counts interpolated.

**Controlled ladder and statistics.** The 4-bit rung adds GPTQ and AWQ (via LLM-Compressor, W4A16, group-128) on top of per-row and g128 RTN; an 8-bit tier (RTN8, GPTQ8, AWQ8) and fp16 complete the ladder (Tables 6–8). Uncertainty: 5,000 task-bootstrap resamples for the global-vs-local margin (P(global>local), 95% CI), TOST equivalence testing (±0.2 band) for the H1 correlation, within-model permutation tests, partial correlations with model/category controls; GPTQ/AWQ use 3 calibration seeds; Qwen3-8B pair recoveries were replicated over 3 eval seeds. All runs on a single 40GB A100 (MIG partition).

## Results & Highlights

- **Global beats local at matched budget (Table 1, CORE, +0.146 eff. bits/weight, recovery = % of RTN4→RTN8 gap):** g128 recovers 78.8% for Llama-3-8B vs 44.7% for oracle local repair; 76.6% vs 54.9% for the most concentrated model Qwen3-8B; 72.9% vs 51.7% for Mistral-7B. The margin is +21.2 to +52.0 points across all 8 group-128-compatible models (largest on Qwen3-1.7B: 66.9% vs 14.9%; smallest-recovery model Qwen2.5-0.5B: 41.1% vs 3.4%). Task-bootstrap: P(global>local) ≥ 0.95 for 8/8, and the 95% margin CI excludes local for 6/8 (exceptions Qwen2.5-0.5B and Mistral-7B are near-noise). To match global, the local arm would need 5.16–6.33 bits/weight — 7–15× the +0.146 increment.
- **The damage is diffuse (9/9 models):** recovering 50/75/90% of the 4-bit→8-bit gap takes ~20/49/73% of layers on average; every model has more than ~44% of its damage outside any single layer. Max single-layer damage: Mistral-7B 43.9%, Llama-3-8B 30.7%, Llama-3.2-3B 28.6% — down to Qwen3-1.7B's 8.1%. Even Qwen3-8B, whose top-3 layers recover essentially the whole gap, has a single best layer at only ~40%.
- **Granularity, not algorithm, drives the 4-bit gain (Table 7, mean over 8 models, CORE):** per-row→g128 RTN gains +0.095 CORE, whereas GPTQ and AWQ add only +0.020 and +0.017 over g128 RTN; including the Qwen3-8B outlier, GPTQ is +0.032 and AWQ +0.016. Per-model example (Table 6): Qwen2.5-0.5B moves .185 (per-row) → .243 (g128) → .268 (GPTQ) against fp16 .325.
- **8-bit is near-lossless (Table 8, CORE @200):** fp16 − RTN8 ≤ 0.005 for every model (e.g., Llama-3-8B .556 vs .561; Qwen3-8B .411 vs .416), with the residual CI containing zero for 6/8 — the 4-bit residual is budget-limited, not precision-limited, across RTN, GPTQ, and AWQ.
- **All three localizers fail causally:** H1 circuit drift correlates +0.377 with damage over 198 task-model pairs but collapses to +0.054 (p=0.46) after controlling model+category, TOST-equivalent to zero within ±0.2; H2 boundary-pair protection recovers ≤13% of the gap in 6/9 models (Qwen3-8B 12.0%, Qwen2.5-0.5B −4.1%) with only Mistral-7B substantially helped (55.5%); H3 weight-std pairs find the true protect-one peak only in the LLaMA family (Llama-3.2-3B 57.0%) and miss entirely on Qwen3-8B (−0.1%) and Qwen3-1.7B (0.6%), and Qwen3-8B's most-recoverable layers are among the *lowest* reconstruction-error ones (top-3 cuts reconstruction error by only ~7% yet recovers nearly all the accuracy).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core move — treating "where does quantization damage live" as a causal question answered by mixed-precision intervention rather than by correlational heuristics — is genuinely valuable and, to my knowledge, not the framing used by prior interpretability-flavored quantization work (e.g., Qiao et al. 2025's task-circuit quantization or Xiao et al. 2025's salience-based protection, which the authors cite and deliberately distinguish themselves from). The negative results (H1–H3) are more informative than most positive results in this literature. However, the paper is a measurement study, not a method: it proposes no deployable algorithm, and the "global granularity as default" recommendation is close to common practitioner wisdom (group-wise scales are standard); the contribution is the *proof of when local repair cannot win*, not a new technique.

**Rigor.** Above average for an LLM-measurement paper. Strengths: the H1 confound analysis is exemplary (raw r=+0.377 → +0.054 under model+category control, with TOST equivalence, permutation p=0.024, and a per-model check showing 8/9 positive but 0/9 individually significant); the additivity check (0.91–1.10) validates the top-k decomposition; the local arm being oracle-selected means the headline comparison *favors* local, so global's win is if anything understated. Weaknesses the authors partly concede: (1) the "no few-layer fix" claim is scoped to greedy, layer-granularity, weight-only interventions — weight-level salience protection (Xiao et al. 2025) and Hessian-based methods (HAWQ) are untested, yet the abstract's phrasing "cheap signals that correlate with quantization damage do not necessarily identify where restoring precision improves accuracy" travels slightly beyond the evidence; (2) the maximal-damage regime is probed only via per-row RTN — whether the same diffuse structure holds for GPTQ/AWQ g128 (smaller gaps) is untested; (3) oracle selectors are scored on the same eval set used for reporting, and only Qwen3-8B's pair recovery is multi-seeded (38.1/44.2/36.7%) — smaller-model recoveries sit near the noise floor, which the authors flag; (4) family-level generalization rests on thin evidence outside LLaMA: Mistral and OpenLLaMA are n=1, and Qwen's concentration appears only at 8B.

**Reproducibility.** Mixed. Seeds, harness, sample counts, calibration-seed protocol, and the full bit ladder are documented in commendable detail, and the models are all public. But no code release is mentioned anywhere, and reproducing the full sweep (protect-one across ~all layers × 9 models × ~6 ladder rungs × 22 tasks) is a substantial engineering effort. The nonstandard task set (dyck_languages, operators, repeat_copy_logic alongside squad/hellaswag) also makes the numbers incomparable to the mainstream quantization literature (no WikiText-2 perplexity, no MMLU), so the "granularity not algorithm" claim cannot be cross-checked against published GPTQ/AWQ results — it is only relative within this harness, though that is the comparison the authors actually need.

**Red flags.** (a) The Qwen3-8B GPTQ score exceeds fp16 (.470 vs .411) — an obvious 200-sample eval-noise outlier that the authors handle honestly by excluding it from method means, but it underscores how tight the +0.020/+0.017 method deltas are relative to the noise floor at 200 samples/task; (b) the equal-budget match is first-order weight-only accounting — Qwen3-8B's local-vs-global tie at 4.206 bits/weight (+0.05 over g128) is within ~3–44% of the increment's own uncertainty, so the "unconditional" claim is safe for 7/8 models but the Qwen3-8B case is genuinely knife-edge; (c) the largest model tested is 8B despite a 58B hardware ceiling, so the headline rule is unvalidated at the scales where quantization matters most commercially.

**Significance.** For practitioners the actionable takeaway is real: at small bit increments, spend on finer group granularity everywhere rather than on selective layer protection, and don't trust interpretability-derived localizers without causal validation. For the quantization-research community, the diffuse-damage finding is a useful corrective to "protect the important layers" intuitions.

## Strengths

- Causal ground truth (mixed-precision intervention) instead of correlational heuristics — the right instrument for a localization question, and the source of the paper's most durable findings.
- Matched effective bits/weight design with an oracle-selected local arm, i.e., the comparison is biased *against* the paper's own conclusion, which strengthens it.
- Careful uncertainty treatment: task-level bootstrap (5,000 resamples), TOST equivalence, within-model permutation, partial correlations, multi-seed calibration/eval where it matters.
- Honest, specific Limitations section (§6) that scopes every claim, plus transparent handling of the Qwen3-8B GPTQ>fp16 outlier.
- The negative results (H1–H3) are rigorously instrumented and genuinely useful — each refutation is quantified, not just asserted.
- Broad coverage for the budget: 9 models, 4 families, 16× size range, full 4-bit/8-bit ladder across RTN/GPTQ/AWQ.

## Limitations

- Untested alternatives: non-greedy layer sets and weight-level salience protection (Xiao et al., 2025) are untested; H3 covers weight std and reconstruction error but not Hessian sensitivity (HAWQ-style).
- Maximal-damage regime: the diffuse-damage structure is established under per-row RTN; whether it holds for the smaller gaps of calibrated methods (GPTQ/AWQ g128) is untested.
- Scale ceiling: everything runs on a single 40GB A100 (MIG partition), capped ≈58B parameters — only ≤8B models are actually tested, so larger-scale behavior is unknown.
- Oracle selectors: top-k curves and the local arm are scored on the same eval set and are not deployable; only Qwen3-8B is multi-seeded for pairs; smaller-model recoveries sit near the noise floor; Mistral and OpenLLaMA are single models (n=1), and Qwen-family concentration appears only at 8B.
- First-order bit-accounting: the equal-budget comparison is per-layer and weight-only; exact byte-level per-weight allocation and a multi-bit rate-distortion sweep are left to future work.
- Evaluation: 22 tasks × 200 samples with a nonstandard task mix and continuation scoring; no standard quantization metrics (perplexity on WikiText-2/C4, MMLU) for external comparability; no code release mentioned.

## Relevance

与用户"极限性能优化下的可靠性"核心方向高度契合：论文直接研究 4-bit 量化误差的损伤结构、8-bit 混合精度修复的边际价值以及"8-bit 近无损/损伤弥散/粒度优先于算法"的量化结论，可操作性强。同时它以电路漂移和因果激活修补作为被证伪的假设来源，也正好触及"LLM 隐藏状态与可解释性"兴趣区——是少见的把可解释性信号放进因果检验框架的量化研究。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the diffuse-damage structure persist in the maximal-damage regime of calibrated methods (GPTQ/AWQ at g128) and at scales beyond 8B, where per-layer outliers may concentrate damage differently?
2. Would weight-level or Hessian-based salience protection (e.g., SpQR/HAWQ-style sub-layer allocation) change the "no few-layer fix" conclusion at sub-layer granularity, which this paper explicitly leaves untested?
3. The within-family concentration result (LLaMA-3.x peaks at L1 at every size, leave-one-out 3/3) suggests family-level priors — can a deployable (non-oracle) selector be built from architecture family alone, and does it transfer to unseen families?
4. How does the global-vs-local verdict change under exact byte-level accounting and a multi-bit rate-distortion allocation (e.g., 3-bit base with selective 6-bit), rather than uniform 4/8-bit mixed precision?
5. Is 8-bit near-losslessness (fp16−RTN8 ≤ 0.005) robust under longer-generation, chat-style, or agentic evaluation, where small per-token logit shifts could compound, rather than under 200-sample continuation scoring?
