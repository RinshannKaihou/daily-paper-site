In 23 agent-run experiments on a real flow-chemistry robot. The 50-experiment human-directed campaign reached 70.9% — the agent traded peak yield for material and cost efficiency.

Why this matters

Keeps LLMs out of the optimizer math and on the science — a working blueprint for reliable, auditable, long-horizon autonomous research systems.

Critical edge

The headline ablation gap rests on a synthetic benchmark; on real chemistry the proposed architecture doesn’t clearly win, and both physical campaigns are single runs.

Methodology

  • Two-agent division of labour. A BO specialist subagent writes a reusable, smoke-tested Python campaign program; the coordinating agent supervises, repairs, and intervenes only when scientific judgment is required.
  • BO-MCP service. Bayesian optimization as a service: 22 MCP tools over MCP/REST, pluggable BayBE/BoTorch backends, typed persistent campaign state, per-suggestion provenance, SHAP explainability. “No BO mathematics are ever performed by an LLM.”
  • Platform-neutral bridges. PySCF execution graph for quantum-chemistry evaluators, A2A for structure generation, MQTT for the RAISE formulation SDL, authenticated RobBridge HTTP for the RoboChem-Flex robot in Amsterdam.
  • Event-driven supervision. Campaigns run as monitored background tasks (up to 5 d 8 h, unattended overnight); output is filtered into compact steering events so logs never flood the LLM context; repairs carry over all prior observations.
  • Three-tier evaluation. 4-architecture × 2-benchmark ablation (60-evaluation budget, 3 runs, GPT-5.4), five digital discovery campaigns mirroring published studies, two physical closed loops with full cost accounting.

Strengths

  • Real multi-day closed loops on two physically distinct platforms, run unattended overnight across time zones (Toronto orchestrating an Amsterdam robot).
  • Principled architecture: typed persistent optimizer state, per-suggestion provenance, LLM never does BO math — directly targeting the documented failure mode of LLMs ignoring the posterior.
  • Rich taxonomy of real failure recoveries (evaluator misconfiguration, 180° fallback-label corruption, zero-yield spectral forensics), each with operator transcripts in the SI.
  • Negative results reported plainly: missed contact-angle target, initialization luck, LLM bill exceeding the chemistry cost.
  • Event-driven supervision keeps long-horizon campaigns out of the context window — a practical answer to the dominant deployment constraint.
  • Reproducibility: raw artifacts and BO-MCP open-sourced; 100+ page SI with full records.

Limitations

  • Ablations are 3 runs, end-to-end only — the causal contribution of specialist delegation vs programmatic execution vs BO-MCP state cannot be isolated.
  • Shields arylation saturates, leaving synthetic Ackley 6D as the only discriminating benchmark; no multi-task or multi-objective ablation.
  • Physical showcases are n=1, semi-autonomous — human approvals at constraint changes and one-shot experiment authorization.
  • The GPT-5.4 sweep is confounded by development bias (framework built against its failures); per-model run counts are small and unequal.
  • The agent’s best yield (58.8%) trails the human campaign (70.9%); the comparison is retrospective, not budget-matched.
  • $109.55 inference overhead would erase the cost advantage against more expensive experimental platforms.

Questions for further exploration

  • Does the Specialist-script advantage survive continuous or high-dimensional parameter spaces, or is the Ackley separation an artifact of low-dimensional categorical spaces?
  • Could typed validation schemas at the specialist→program interface have caught the 180° fallback-encoding bug before deployment?
  • How does event-driven supervision extend to non-BO planners (evolutionary algorithms, active learning), and what state must BO-MCP expose?
  • Would the eight-model ranking persist with prompts and tool descriptions neutralized of GPT-5.4 development bias?
  • Is the agent actually better per unit of starting material at matched stopping rules — a budget-constrained regret analysis?
