> **一句话总结：** 首次对公开模型仓库分发的 GGUF 量化代码模型做"功能性普查"——327 个工件(官方 Ollama 库 305 个 + HuggingFace 社区 22 个)逐一执行 15 任务冒烟测试:官方库有 5 个工件(1.6%)是"到货即坏"的静默缺陷(4 个 Qwen2.5-Coder-3B 低比特转换 + 1 个 phi3.5-mini q2_K,在 CUDA 和 Metal 两个后端上 164 题 HumanEval+ 全部得 0 分,而同模型同量化级别的独立发行商转换可得 8–14/15),另发现 2 个社区工件是"后端相关失败"(CUDA 上残废、Metal 上健康)——这类故障没有任何注册页、模型卡或单栈基准能暴露;作者据此发布 quantcheck 验收工具(中位数 30 秒/工件)并主张模型注册表需要补上软件包注册表早已普及的"发布前验收门"。

> **TL;DR:** The first execution-based census of distributed quantized LLM artifacts: 327 GGUF code models (305 from the official Ollama library, 22 community HuggingFace conversions) each ran a 15-task smoke suite calibrated so healthy artifacts pass 15/15 while a known-broken Qwen2.5-Coder-3B q3_K_M fails. Five official artifacts (1.6%) are silently defective — four Qwen2.5-Coder-3B low-bit conversions and one phi3.5-mini q2_K score 0/164 on HumanEval+ on both CUDA and Metal, versus 8–14/15 for independent distributors' conversions of the identical model and quantization used as referees. The adjudication chain (family baselines, second backend, cross-distributor referee) also exonerates near-zero scorers like llama3.2-1B at 2-/3-bit as genuine capability collapse, and reclassifies two TheBloke deepseek-coder-6.7B files (5–6/15 on CUDA vs 13–15/15 on Metal) as backend-dependent failure, a class no registry currently tests for. Output-statistics heuristics catch only 3 of 5 defects (2.3% false positives on 300 healthy artifacts) — execution is unavoidable. Tool and full dataset released (github.com/aditi-p31/quantcheck).

## 核心贡献

- **首次生态级功能性普查:** 不是看元数据,而是真正执行下载到的工件——305 个官方 Ollama 工件(15 条模型线、每个合规量化级别、≤8GB,是全量普查而非抽样)+ 22 个最热门社区 GGUF 转换 + 7 个跨发行商参照转换,总下载流量超过 1 TB。
- **确诊 5 个官方库静默缺陷(1.6%):** 4 个 Qwen2.5-Coder-3B 转换(q2_K/q3_K_S/q3_K_M/q3_K_L,连续低比特区间,疑似同一次坏转换批次)+ 1 个孤立的 phi3.5-mini q2_K;在双后端上 0/164 全军覆没,而参照转换可得 8–14/15——其中 q3_K_M 是 2024 年 BPE 预分词缺陷前就已知的校准缺陷,其余均为新发现。
- **三分法现象学(比缺陷本身更有价值):** 同样的低分可能是 (a) 转换缺陷(参照转换好→工件坏)、(b) 小模型极端量化下的能力坍缩(llama3.2-1B 2/3-bit 独立转换同样烂,与发行商无关)、(c) 后端相关失败(TheBloke deepseek-coder-6.7B 两个文件 CUDA 上 5–6/15、Metal 上 13–15/15)。朴素阈值会把后两类全部误判为缺陷。
- **证明廉价信号不够用:** 在 300 个健康工件上系统评估 4 类输出统计量,最好的规则(截断率≥0.3 或重复 6-gram 占比≥0.3)也只能抓 3/5 缺陷、2.3% 误报;另外 2 个缺陷产出流畅、结构良好但错误的代码,表面统计量完全落在健康区间内——只有执行代码才能暴露。
- **交付物:** 15 任务冒烟套件 + quantcheck 一条命令验收工具 + 全部逐工件转录数据集 + 每个确诊缺陷的披露报告(已向发行商提 issue),主张注册表应在发布时跑"15 道简单题"的验收门(单工件中位数 30 秒 GPU 时间)。

## Key Contributions

- **First ecosystem-scale functional census of distributed quantized artifacts:** execution-based rather than metadata-based — 305 official Ollama artifacts (a census of the entire eligible population: every instruct, code-capable tag with explicit quantization suffix at ≤8 GB across 15 model lines), 22 most-downloaded community GGUF conversions from HuggingFace, and 7 cross-distributor reference conversions; total network churn exceeded a terabyte.
- **Five confirmed silent defects in the official library (1.6% of artifacts):** a contiguous low-bit batch of four Qwen2.5-Coder-3B conversions (q2_K, q3_K_S, q3_K_M, q3_K_L — consistent with one bad conversion run) plus an isolated phi3.5-mini q2_K, all scoring 0/164 HumanEval+ tasks on both backends while independent conversions of the same models solve 8–14 of 15 smoke tasks; one (q3_K_M) is the pre-known 2024-era defect used for calibration, the rest are new discoveries.
- **A three-way phenomenology that a naive threshold cannot produce:** identical low scores can be (a) a conversion defect (referee conversion is fine), (b) capability collapse of a small model at extreme compression (llama3.2-1B at 2-/3-bit: independent conversions fail just as badly, 0 and 3 of 15 — the failure travels with model+precision, not distributor), or (c) backend-dependent failure (two TheBloke deepseek-coder-6.7B files score 5–6/15 on CUDA but 13–15/15 on Metal under the same runtime and template).
- **Evidence that cheap signals cannot replace execution:** over 300 healthy artifacts as negatives, the best low-noise rule (truncation ≥0.3 or repeated-6-gram share ≥0.3) catches 3 of 5 defects at a 2.3% false-positive rate; the two fluent-but-wrong defects sit at the 94th percentile of healthy repetition share and below the 3rd percentile on every other signal.
- **Deliverables:** the calibrated 15-task suite, the quantcheck one-command acceptance tool, the full per-artifact transcript dataset, and disclosure reports filed against every confirmed defect; the argument that model registries occupy the position package registries held before continuous integration, and that a fifteen-task gate at publication time (median 30 s per artifact) would have caught every defect found.

## Methodology

**Sampling frame (census, not sample).** For the official channel: every instruct, code-capable tag in the Ollama library with an explicit quantization suffix and file size at most 8 GB — 305 artifacts across 15 registry model lines. For the community channel: 22 representative files from the most-downloaded HuggingFace GGUF conversion repositories. The inventory was frozen with pull dates recorded before any testing. Seven cross-distributor conversions of the same models serve as referees.

**Calibrated smoke suite.** A full benchmark run of hundreds of artifacts is unnecessary for detecting broken ones. From prior ground truth (24 configurations of one family evaluated on the EvalPlus suite), the authors identified the 39 HumanEval+ tasks solved by every healthy configuration — including the weakest 0.5B 3-bit model — and selected the first 15 by task identifier. Every healthy configuration passes 15/15; the known defect (Qwen2.5-Coder-3B q3_K_M) fails. The suite is deliberately easy: it certifies basic functional competence (a smoke test), not capability.

**Two-stage screen.** Stage one flags any artifact passing fewer than 9 of 15 tasks (this flags 50 of 305 official artifacts, 16.4%). Stage two compares each flagged artifact against the median smoke score of its own model and size at high precision (q6_K, q8_0, fp16): at or above 60% of baseline → healthy; at or below 2/15 when the family baseline is ≥8 → defect suspect; low scores from families already weak at high precision → capability collapse, not defect. Seven suspects emerged and received full adjudication.

**Adjudication protocol.** Each suspect faces (1) the full 164-task HumanEval+ evaluation; (2) replication on a second inference backend — the bulk sweep runs on a Linux RTX 4090 (CUDA) server, confirmed defects are replicated on an Apple-Silicon laptop (Metal), and a defect claim requires the same verdict on both, which rules out GPU-specific kernel interactions; (3) a cross-distributor referee — an independent distributor's conversion of the same model at the same (or nearest available) quantization; a suspect is defective only if it scores 0/15 while the referee reaches ≥5/15 and exceeds it by ≥4 tasks; (4) manual transcript inspection. Decoding is greedy with a fixed 2048-token context (mostly 768 new tokens); outputs reproduce byte-for-byte across runs; both backends share one pinned Ollama release (v0.3.26, llama.cpp core). Generations are scored with EvalPlus against tests, and all transcripts are archived. Four cheap output statistics are recorded per generation: largest repeated 6-gram share, fraction of prompt lines echoed verbatim, fraction of generations hitting the token cap, and presence of a function definition.

**Community-channel controls.** Community artifacts run with the chat template copied verbatim from the corresponding official registry entry (so template choice cannot differ between channels), and additionally re-run under whatever template their own file embeds (so a verdict never rests on a template the auditor chose). Because full evaluation is only triggered by a flag, a stratified random sample of ten unflagged artifacts was evaluated on 49 tasks held out of the suite — the only way to observe what the screen would have missed. Every artifact labeled defective receives a disclosure report with reproduction instructions; registry issues are filed with the affected distributors.

## Results & Highlights

- **Five silently defective official artifacts, all dead on both backends.** Per Table II, the four Qwen2.5-Coder-3B conversions (q2_K, q3_K_S, q3_K_M, q3_K_L) score 0/15 smoke and 0/164 HumanEval+ while their independent-distributor referees score 14/15 (for q2_K) and 8/15 (for each q3 variant); the phi3.5-mini q2_K scores 0/164 while its independent q2_K referee scores 6/15. Overall: 5 of 305 official artifacts (1.6%) defective — 4 of 281 (1.4%) outside the calibration study; counted as defect events, 2 of 29 model-and-size conversion groups carry one incident (6.9%, Wilson 95% CI 1.9%–22.0%), and the per-artifact rate's Wilson interval spans 0.7%–3.8%.
- **The adjudication chain is what separates defects from lookalikes.** The naive stage-1 threshold alone flags 50/305 (16.4%); after family-relative scoring and the cross-distributor referee, only 7 suspects remain and 5 confirm. The referee exonerates llama3.2-1B at 2-/3-bit (near-zero scores, but independent conversions at the same levels fail equally badly — 0 and 3 of 15 — so it is capability collapse, not a distribution defect); without the referee these would have been recorded as defects.
- **Cheap output statistics catch only 3 of 5 defects.** On 300 non-defective official artifacts: truncation fraction at a 0.2 threshold catches 2 of 5 defects with 11 false positives; repeated-6-gram share at 0.2 catches 3 of 5 with 4 false positives; prompt-echo fraction and missing-function-definition rate are useless at any threshold. The best rule (truncation ≥0.3 or repetition share ≥0.3) catches 3 of 5 at a 2.3% false-positive rate; the remaining two (q3_K_M, q3_K_L) sit at the 94th percentile of healthy repetition share and at/below the 3rd percentile on every other signal — any threshold catching them also fires on at least 66% of healthy artifacts.
- **Backend-dependent failure is a distinct, previously untested class.** TheBloke's deepseek-coder-6.7B q3_K_M and q4_K_M score 5 and 6 of 15 on the CUDA server versus 13 and 15 of 15 on Apple Metal under the same pinned runtime, while Ollama's conversion of the identical model and quantization achieves 13 of 15; under the raw-prompt fallback (the files embed no prompt template), 87% and 94% of CUDA generations run to the token cap and the pair scores 6 and 4 of 15 (22 and 16 of the full 164 tasks). A defective file should fail everywhere — these do not, so they are not classified as defects.
- **Screen sensitivity check and cost.** Every artifact confirmed defective by the full 164-task evaluation had been flagged by the screen. Ten unflagged artifacts evaluated on 49 held-out tasks all solve at least 22% (median 44%), none resembling the 0/164 signature — but with ten artifacts the miss-rate bound is loose (Wilson 95% CI 0%–27.8%), and the paper reports it as such. Screening one artifact takes 12 seconds to 7 minutes on a commodity GPU (median 30 seconds; the slow tail is runaway defects filling the token budget), excluding download time.
- **The community channel is cleaner than expected — but the frontier is not.** 20 of 22 sampled community conversions are healthy. In a day-zero vignette, of five live community conversions of a 30B model released three days before the run, four failed to parse in the pinned stable runtime (architecture outpacing runtime support) and one could not be retrieved — conversions reach users before any pipeline exists that could validate them.

The paper discloses that Claude (Anthropic) AI assistance was used throughout preparation, with the author stating they designed the study, verified all reported results against raw data and primary sources, and take full responsibility. Credibility rests on unusually complete artifact release: the audit dataset (per-artifact transcripts, signature features, verdicts, scoring outputs), the quantcheck tool, and all harness code are public at github.com/aditi-p31/quantcheck, an archival DOI is promised, and disclosure reports were filed with affected distributors. The measurement discipline — deterministic greedy decoding, byte-for-byte reproducibility, dual-backend replication, pre-registered referee thresholds, and candid reporting of loose confidence intervals — reads as careful empirical work, though the single-author, non-institutional setting means no external group has yet independently validated the census; the released data makes that possible.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is genuine.** Prior model-hub studies inspect metadata or hunt malicious content (e.g., the MalHug scan of 705K Hugging Face models found no malicious payloads at serialization level; upstreamance/misleading-name studies target deceit), and the closest quantization work (Kurt's unified evaluation of llama.cpp quantization on a single model converted in-house under ideal conditions) never touches what users actually download. "Accidentally broken artifacts reaching far more users than malicious ones" is a real, precisely named gap, and this is the first execution-based census of it.
- **Calibration circularity is the main methodological soft spot.** The 15-task suite was calibrated on Qwen2.5-Coder using exactly the 3B q3_K_M defect that is also one of the five headline findings — the suite's separation properties are demonstrated on the same family-defect pair it was tuned on. The prospective check is thin: ten unflagged artifacts on 49 held-out tasks yield a miss-rate bound of 0%–27.8% (Wilson 95% CI), which the authors honestly flag as loose. "The screen catches every defect" holds on the census itself but is not strongly bounded beyond it.
- **The title overpromises relative to the detector result.** "How to Catch Them" resolves to: run the code. Two of five confirmed defects are statistically invisible to every surface signal tested (the q3_K_M/q3_K_L pair at the 94th percentile of healthy repetition share), so the only reliable discriminator is execution — which is exactly the thing registries do not do. The paper is upfront about this, but the cheap-signal path it analyzes tops out at 3/5 detection.
- **No root cause for either headline anomaly.** For the Qwen2.5-Coder-3B batch, "consistent with one bad conversion batch" is an inference from the contiguity of quantization levels, not a demonstrated conversion-error mechanism; for the TheBloke backend-dependent pair, the paper explicitly refuses to claim a mechanism, and its circumstantial timing argument (published months before the llama.cpp BPE pre-tokenization fix) is left as an open alternative reading. Two files from a single community repository is also a very small base for the entire backend-dependence class.
- **Scope restrictions are real but acknowledged.** The smoke suite tests Python code generation only, so defects invisible to code tasks escape and prevalence figures are explicitly lower bounds; everything runs on one pinned runtime (Ollama v0.3.26 / llama.cpp core), so a defect that only manifests under another runtime version is indistinguishable from a healthy file — the verdict is a property of the file under that runtime, not universally; the community sample is 22 popularity-weighted files, so the "community is cleaner" conclusion rests on thin data (group-level CI 1.9%–22.0%).
- **Red flags: none serious.** The paper is unusually resistant to overclaiming — it reports the embarrassing loose CI, labels the census a lower bound, declines mechanism claims, and distinguishes defect/degraded/collapsed/backend-dependent rather than lumping. Mild concerns: the "about a cent at cloud spot prices" cost estimate is back-of-envelope with no registry-integration prototype evaluated, and quantcheck's usability is asserted rather than studied with users.

## Strengths

- **A real denominator.** This is a census of the entire eligible official population (every compliant tag ≤8 GB across 15 model lines), not a convenience sample — the 1.6% figure is meaningful in a way most supply-chain measurements are not.
- **An adjudication chain that produces falsifiable verdicts.** Family-relative baselines, dual-backend replication, and a cross-distributor referee with pre-registered thresholds (0/15 vs ≥5/15, gap ≥4) correctly exonerate llama3.2-1B (capability collapse) and reclassify the TheBloke pair (backend-dependent failure) — the three-way taxonomy is itself a reusable contribution.
- **Reproducibility by construction.** Greedy decoding with byte-for-byte reruns, a pinned runtime, archived per-artifact transcripts, a released tool and dataset, and per-defect disclosure reports with reproduction instructions.
- **Practical weight.** Median 30 seconds per artifact on a commodity GPU; a one-command tool (quantcheck); practitioner guidance (test low-bit variants of small models, test on your deployment stack, never trust fluency) each backed by a specific census finding.
- **Statistical honesty.** Wilson CIs given even when unflattering (0.7%–3.8% artifact rate; 0%–27.8% miss-rate bound labeled loose), per-artifact vs per-incident rates separated to avoid overstating independent failures.

## Limitations

- **Construct:** the smoke suite covers Python code generation only; an artifact broken for other uses can still pass, and defects invisible to code tasks escape entirely — prevalence figures are lower bounds. The deliberately easy suite separates breakage from weakness, but a subject that degrades quality without collapsing might evade the screen.
- **Internal:** both backends share one pinned llama.cpp-based release, so a defect that only manifests under a different runtime version is indistinguishable from a healthy file; defect verdicts are file-under-that-runtime properties.
- **External:** one registry's code-capable instruct artifacts ≤8 GB plus a 22-file popularity-weighted community sample; chat models, larger artifacts, and other registries are unmeasured, and prevalence may differ for each.
- **Statistical power:** the held-out sensitivity check uses only ten artifacts (miss-rate CI 0%–27.8%), and the community-channel conclusion rests on 22 files.
- **Mechanism gap:** no root cause is established for the Qwen-3B batch, the phi3.5-mini q2_K, or the backend-dependent pair; upstream responses to the disclosure reports are not reported in the paper.
- **Detector ceiling:** cheap output statistics catch 3 of 5 defect classes at best, so the scalable-deployment story still requires executing every artifact.

## Relevance

与你的核心方向高度契合:这篇论文把"静默故障检测"从硬件层(位翻转、SDC)推进到模型工件分发层,证明量化这种极端性能优化手段会在不报错的情况下产出功能上彻底报废的模型,而且最危险的一类缺陷(流畅但错误的输出)在表面统计量上完全隐身、只有真正执行才能检出——正好落在"推理可靠性与 SDC 检测"与"极端优化下的可靠性(量化误差)"两大关注点的交汇处,其"用轻量功能性冒烟测试给量化工件做验收"的思路也可借鉴到训练/推理基础设施的健康检查中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the actual mechanism behind the four Qwen2.5-Coder-3B batch failures — a corrupted conversion run, a tokenizer/pretokenization regression, or tensor-mapping errors — and do the distributors' responses to the disclosure reports confirm it?
2. Why does the same TheBloke GGUF file behave healthily on Apple Metal but lose most of its capability on CUDA under the identical pinned runtime — quantization-kernel dispatch, memory layout, or something else — and does the effect persist across llama.cpp versions?
3. Should artifact verification be pinned to (file, runtime version, backend) triples rather than files alone, given that a "defective file" verdict here is explicitly runtime-relative?
4. Do the fluent-but-wrong defect signatures generalize beyond Python/HumanEval+-style tasks — e.g., to chat or math workloads where no test-based scorer exists — and can capability-collapse thresholds be calibrated without code tasks?
5. Can learned detectors trained on the released transcripts push cheap-signal detection past the 3-of-5 ceiling at acceptable false-positive rates, or is execution fundamentally irreplaceable for the fluent-but-wrong class?
