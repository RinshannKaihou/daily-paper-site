> **一句话总结：** 本文用三类互补证据论证 LLM 做机器翻译主要复用单语语言建模机制（"interlingua 假设"）：仅用各语言的 GlobalMMLU 准确率即可线性预测 FLORES BLEU（Llama-3.1-8B R²=0.739），加入语言对交互项几乎不提升预测力（嵌套 F 检验全部不显著，最大 ΔR²=0.0038）；GCM 定位的翻译相关注意力头集中在中间层且在全部 56 个翻译方向上复用（消融使 8 个目标语言 BLEU 下降约 2 倍于随机对照）；单语科萨语 LoRA 微调恢复平行语料微调 99% 的 BLEU 增益（16.84→24.81 vs 平行微调 24.88）。

> **TL;DR:** Three complementary lines of evidence support the claim that decoder-only LLMs translate by reusing monolingual language-modeling machinery through a latent task-agnostic ("interlingua") feature space. (1) On FLORES, a linear model using per-language GlobalMMLU accuracy alone predicts BLEU at R²=0.739 (Llama-3.1-8B) / 0.510 (Aya-23-8B), and adding a language-pair interaction term (bilinear baseline) is never significant (largest ΔR²=0.0038). (2) Generative causal mediation finds mid-layer attention heads (Llama layers 13–14, Aya 15–20) whose translation indirect effects exceed null controls by 3.0× (5.2× for top-10 heads on Llama); these top-20 heads are universal across all 56 translation directions, and mean-ablating them degrades BLEU in all 8 target languages at roughly 2× the random-head control. (3) Rank-16 LoRA fine-tuning on Xhosa: monolingual data recovers 99% of the parallel-data BLEU gain on Llama-3.1-8B (16.84→24.81 vs 24.88) and 73% on TinyAya-3B (23.46→26.64 vs 27.80).

## 核心贡献

- **将"interlingua 假设"操作化为三条可检验预测**：若 LLM 通过任务无关的潜在特征空间翻译，则 (1) 翻译质量应可由单语能力线性预测、无需语言对交互项；(2) 应存在同时因果影响单语任务与翻译任务的模型组件；(3) 单语微调应能复现大部分平行数据微调带来的翻译增益。
- **行为学证据**：在 FLORES 的 18–24 种语言上，仅用每语言单语能力代理（GlobalMMLU 准确率、MultiBLMP 语法裕度、FLORES 困惑度）即可预测翻译 BLEU；双线性交互模型相对线性模型在所有代理×模型组合上嵌套 F 检验均不显著（最大 ΔR²=0.0038）；按语言分解的边际 BLEU（μ+α_S+β_T）解释 Llama 93.2% / Aya 87.9% 的中心化方差，秩-1 重构恢复约 90%/83% 的矩阵能量。
- **因果证据（GCM）**：用生成式因果中介 + attribution patching 在 8 种语言、56 个翻译方向上定位翻译相关注意力头——|IE| 达 null 对照的 3.0×（全体头）/5.2×（top-10，Llama）；top-20 头在全部 56 个方向上都排前 20 且效应符号稳定；头集中在 Llama 13–14 层、Aya 15–20 层，与"推理阶段"假说（前半层读入抽象表征、后半层读出）吻合；消融 POS-10 头使两个模型全部 8 个目标语言 BLEU 下降约 2 倍于随机同层对照，并同时降低 MultiBLMP 语法裕度。
- **干预证据（微调）**：rank-16 LoRA 微调 Llama-3.1-8B 与 TinyAya-3B 学习科萨语，单语条件（80% 科萨语无对齐文本 + 20% 英语/法语/德语回放）即可恢复平行微调（OPUS MT560 科萨-英语句对）绝大部分增益（Llama 99%、TinyAya 73%），且法语/德语→英语翻译基本保持；德语/泰语等较高资源语言上两种条件均无一致提升。
- **实践含义**：低资源翻译的瓶颈更多在单语读写能力（目标语侧尤甚：目标语言 BLEU 方差是源语言侧的 9.3×/3.9×），无需平行语料、仅靠无对齐单语文本继续预训练即可部分解锁翻译能力。

## Key Contributions

- Operationalizes the **interlingua hypothesis** into three falsifiable predictions: translation quality should be linearly predictable from monolingual competence with no language-pair-specific interaction terms; task-agnostic components should causally influence both monolingual tasks and translation; and monolingual fine-tuning should recover a large share of parallel-data fine-tuning gains.
- **Behavioral evidence**: across 18–24 FLORES languages, per-language monolingual proxies (GlobalMMLU accuracy, MultiBLMP margin, FLORES perplexity) predict translation BLEU; a bilinear model adding a language-pair interaction term never significantly improves fit (nested F-test, largest ΔR²=0.0038); a per-language marginal decomposition BLEU_ST ≈ μ + α_S + β_T explains R²=0.932 (Llama) / 0.879 (Aya) of centered variance, and rank-1 reconstruction recovers ≈90%/83% of the BLEU matrix energy (faithfulness 89.8%/83.4%).
- **Causal evidence (GCM)**: generative causal mediation with attribution patching over 8 languages and 56 translation directions identifies attention heads concentrated in Llama layers 13–14 and Aya layers 15–20; mean |IE| exceeds the null cross-language control by 3.0× over all heads and 5.2× over the top-10 (Llama; 2.5×/4.3× for Aya); the top-20 heads by |IE| rank in the top-20 for all 56 directions with stable signs; mean-ablating the POS-10 set degrades BLEU in all 8 target languages for both models at roughly 2× the random same-layer control, and lowers MultiBLMP grammaticality margins in all 8 targets.
- **Intervention evidence (fine-tuning)**: rank-16 LoRA on Xhosa shows monolingual fine-tuning (80% unaligned Xhosa + 20% English/French/German replay) recovers 99% (Llama-3.1-8B) and 73% (TinyAya-3B) of the parallel-fine-tuning BLEU gain, while largely preserving French/German→English translation.
- **Practical implication**: the bottleneck for low-resource translation is largely monolingual (especially target-side) competence — target-language BLEU variance is 9.3× (Llama) / 3.9× (Aya) source-language variance — so improving monolingual modeling on unaligned text can unlock translation capability without parallel corpora.

## Methodology

**Part 1 — Behavioral regression (does translation need language-pair-specific terms?).** For language pairs (S,T) among the 24 FLORES languages, translation competence t_ST is BLEU (2-shot prompts sampled from the dev split, sacreBLEU; shots never overlap the test example). Per-language monolingual competence proxies: FLORES perplexity, MultiBLMP accuracy/margin (multilingual linguistic minimal pairs, ~200 randomly sampled pairs per language, log-probability margin of grammatical over ungrammatical completions), and GlobalMMLU accuracy. A linear model β_S·l_S + β_T·l_T + β_0 = t_ST is compared to a bilinear model adding β_ST·(l_S·l_T); if the interlingua hypothesis holds, the interaction term should add no predictive power. Evaluated by nested F-tests on the common 18-language subset and a 17-language no-English subset, for both Llama-3.1-8B and Aya-23-8B. Complementary analyses: a per-language marginal decomposition BLEU_ST ≈ μ + α_S + β_T; rank-1 multiplicative reconstruction of the 24×24 BLEU matrix via masked alternating least squares (552 off-diagonal cells, self-translation masked); logit-log functional-form checks; MultiBLMP margin restricted to subject–verb agreement phenomena; MMLU subject-subset breakdown; and MultiBLIMP terms added on top of the MMLU-only regression.

**Part 2 — Causal mediation (are translation components also monolingual components?).** Generative causal mediation (GCM) with attribution patching approximates the indirect effect (IE) of every attention head on a preference metric M = log π(r | p_orig) − log π(r | p_cf), where r is the FLORES-2022 gold translation and p_cf swaps the final source sentence (counterfactual activation cached by rerunning the model; IE approximated as a first-order gradient attribution). Four control conditions separate "selective for correct translation" from generic fluency: null cross-language, real same-language, null same-language (real_cross vs these three). The setup spans 56 ordered cross-language pairs among {English, Spanish, German, French, Turkish, Arabic, Hindi, Hebrew} with n=100 uniformly sampled pairs per direction. The top-10 positive-IE heads (POS-10) are then mean-ablated (replaced by their average activation over translation prompts at all token positions), translations regenerated, and BLEU change measured against size-matched random same-layer controls; the same ablated heads' effect on monolingual capability is measured as the change in the MultiBLMP acceptability margin Δ = log p(r_correct|p) − log p(r_incorrect|p) on 400 examples per language (200 heb, 100 hin). Parallel SAE-feature decompositions and ablation dose–response checks are in the appendix.

**Part 3 — Intervention (monolingual vs parallel fine-tuning).** Llama-3.1-8B and TinyAya-3B (Aya-23-8B itself is instruction-tuned and hard to fine-tune) are LoRA-adapted (rank 16, lr 3e-5, seq len 512, 100M tokens per epoch) on Xhosa under two conditions: monolingual (unaligned text, 80% Xhosa + 20% English/French/German replay against catastrophic forgetting) vs parallel (paired Xhosa–English sentences from OPUS MT560, both directions). Both use next-token prediction loss only; evaluation is few-shot prompting on the FLORES devtest split with sacreBLEU. Appendix C extends this to German and Thai adaptation, translation into Xhosa, and MultiBLIMP preservation checks.

## Results & Highlights

- **Monolingual competence alone predicts translation quality.** GlobalMMLU accuracy predicts FLORES BLEU with R²=0.739 (Llama-3.1-8B, MAE 2.91 BLEU) and R²=0.510 (Aya-23-8B, MAE 2.56), far above MultiBLMP accuracy (R²=0.294/0.235 — MultiBLMP saturates at low BLEU). Adding MultiBLIMP terms on top of the MMLU-only regression gives only a small but significant gain (partial R²=0.048, F-test p=5.8×10⁻⁴ Llama; 0.094, p=3.7×10⁻⁷ Aya).
- **No language-pair interaction anywhere.** The bilinear interaction term is non-significant in every proxy×model cell (nested F-test; largest ΔR²=0.0038 across all 14 tests). The per-language marginal decomposition explains R²=0.932 (Llama) / 0.879 (Aya) of centered BLEU variance; rank-1 reconstruction recovers ≈90%/83% of matrix energy with 89.8%/83.4% faithfulness over the 552 observed cells.
- **Target competence matters more than source.** Variance of mean BLEU across target languages is 9.3× (Llama) / 3.9× (Aya) that across source languages; fitted target coefficients are 1.7–5× source coefficients (GlobalMMLU 2.98/1.67; MultiBLMP margin 4.88/2.30). Restricting the MultiBLMP margin to subject–verb agreement improves prediction (R² 0.324→0.363 on Llama; SV-Person alone reaches R²=0.688).
- **Translation heads are real, sparse, selective, and universal.** Mean |IE| under translation exceeds the null cross-language control by 3.0× over all heads and 5.2× over the top-10 heads on Llama (2.5×/4.3× on Aya); the top Llama head (L13H18) has real_cross |IE|=1.076 vs null_cross 0.204. The top-20 heads by |IE| rank in the top-20 for all 56 translation directions with stable signs; per-direction, the top head exceeds the median head's mean |IE| by 60–162× (median 116, Llama). Heads concentrate in layers 13–14 (Llama) / 15–20 (Aya), consistent with the stages-of-inference account.
- **Ablations confirm causal relevance to both tasks.** Mean-ablating the POS-10 heads degrades BLEU in all 8 target languages for both models, roughly twice the random same-layer control. It also lowers the MultiBLMP margin in all 8 targets (Llama mean Δ=−0.092 vs random-control −0.069; the informative signal is the direction, not the magnitude), while ablating NEG-10 heads raises it (+0.179 Llama); the effect is monotone in the number of heads ablated.
- **Monolingual fine-tuning recovers most of the parallel-data gain.** Xho→Eng BLEU: Llama-3.1-8B 16.84 (base) → 24.81 (monolingual) vs 24.88 (parallel) — 99% recovery; TinyAya-3B 23.46 → 26.64 vs 27.80 — 73% recovery. High-resource translation is largely preserved and monolingual fine-tuning retains it better (Llama Fra→Eng: 43.27 base → 42.28 mono vs 38.87 parallel; Deu→Eng: 42.69 → 42.76 mono vs 39.80 parallel). On higher-resource German/Thai adaptation neither condition yields a consistent improvement, and parallel fine-tuning reduces performance in several directions.
- **Caveat the authors report:** only the monolingual condition includes high-resource replay data, so the retention advantage may come from replay rather than the absence of parallel data; MultiBLIMP accuracy after fine-tuning stays near ceiling (Eng 99.35→99.48/99.22), making small differences uninterpretable.

The team has strong standing in mechanistic interpretability: Aaron Mueller is a co-author of the sparse feature circuits line of work, the nnsight/NDIF demo paper, and the "Quest for the Right Mediator" causal-mediation survey; Brinkmann co-authored the ACL 2025 study showing latent grammatical concept representations shared across typologically diverse languages, which directly motivates this paper (Croody et al. 2025). ## Critical Assessment

- **The triangulation is the paper's real strength — but each leg alone is thinner than the framing suggests.** The behavioral leg rests on regressions with only 18–24 data points (languages) and 2–3 predictors; headline R² values are in-sample with no cross-validated error reported, and with so few languages the result could be sensitive to which languages are included (the 17-language no-English robustness check is a good start, but language families are still heavily imbalanced).
- **BLEU is the only translation metric throughout.** The authors themselves concede that the striking target-beats-source asymmetry (9.3× variance ratio) may be an artifact of n-gram matching — fluent target-side n-grams are exactly what BLEU rewards. A learned metric (e.g., COMET) or chrF/human evaluation is absent, and this is the single result most likely to shift under a better metric.
- **The causal-overlap evidence is only partial, and the paper is honest about it.** In the MultiBLIMP ablation, the POS-10 effect magnitude (Llama mean Δ=−0.092) is comparable to its random same-layer control (−0.069) — the argument for task-agnostic reuse rests on sign consistency (all 8 languages negative) rather than effect size, and Appendix B admits the heads are "not completely task-agnostic." The translation ablation (~2× random control) is much stronger than the monolingual one.
- **Methodological scope of the mediation analysis is narrow.** Attribution patching is a first-order gradient approximation (can miss higher-order interactions), and GCM only measures mediation at the final source-token position — earlier-position computations that move content across languages are invisible by design. SAE-feature analyses show the same REAL_CROSS-dominant pattern but with visibly weaker separation than heads (a few top features are same-language features).
- **The fine-tuning claim is established on essentially one low-resource language.** The 99%/73% recovery headline comes from Xhosa→Eng alone (into-Xhosa directions are near-zero BLEU and mixed: for Llama Eng→Xho, parallel 4.25 beats monolingual 2.49); on German and Thai there is no consistent improvement at all, so "monolingual recovers most parallel gains" is not yet shown to generalize beyond one language pair. The replay-data confound (present only in the monolingual condition) is flagged but not controlled — a parallel+replay condition was not run.
- **Model scope is limited and slightly awkward.** Two 8B models and one 3B model; the instruction-tuned Aya-23-8B is dropped from fine-tuning experiments (replaced by TinyAya-3B) because the authors found instruction-tuned models hard to fine-tune in pilots — so the intervention leg says little about the chat models people actually deploy, and nothing about encoder-decoder MT systems or frontier-scale models where dedicated translation mechanisms might emerge.
- **No direct representation-level demonstration.** Despite "feature space" in the title, there is no cross-lingual activation patching at the representation level (e.g., patching the source-side latent into a same-language run and observing translation-quality-consistent outputs); the interlingua remains an inference from component reuse and linear predictability. The authors acknowledge mixture-of-mechanisms views (e.g., word-translation-selective components found by Told et al. 2024) and do not claim exclusivity — appropriately.

## Strengths

- Three independent methods (correlational regression, causal mediation, training intervention) converge on the same conclusion; the design is falsifiable and each prediction is stated in advance.
- Careful control conditions: four-condition GCM control set that cleanly separates "selective for correct translation" from generic fluency or setup effects; size-matched random same-layer ablation controls; dev-shot/test-overlap guards; ablation dose–response and NEG-10 sign-flip checks.
- The universality finding is striking and concrete: ~20 heads with stable effect signs shared across all 56 translation directions, localized in mid layers consistent with the independent stages-of-inference literature.
- Unusually candid limitations and discussion; the paper claims "a significant mechanism that controls a large proportion of MT performance," not exclusivity.
- Practically actionable takeaway for low-resource MT: target-side monolingual competence is a real bottleneck, backed by a 99% recovery number on Llama-3.1-8B.

## Limitations

- BLEU-only evaluation; the target/source asymmetry — one of the headline findings — may partly be an n-gram-metric artifact.
- Small-n regressions (18–24 language observations); no cross-validated fit statistics for the headline R².
- Mediation analysis covers only last-source-token mediation with a first-order attribution-patching approximation; no representation-level cross-lingual patching to directly exhibit the shared feature space.
- Model scope: two 8B and one 3B decoder-only models; instruction-tuned models and encoder-decoder systems excluded from fine-tuning; GCM language coverage limited to 8 languages (56 directions).
- Fine-tuning confound: high-resource replay data exists only in the monolingual condition; no parallel+replay control, so retention differences cannot be cleanly attributed.
- Generality of the recovery claim rests on a single low-resource language (Xhosa); TinyAya recovers only 73%, and higher-resource German/Thai show no consistent effect.
- MultiBLMP saturates near ceiling for strong models, limiting its usefulness as both a regression feature and an ablation readout (the paper notes base accuracies of ~99% post-fine-tuning are uninterpretable).

## Relevance

该论文直接命中用户关注的核心方向「LLM 隐状态与可解释性」：综合运用因果中介分析（GCM/attribution patching）、注意力头定位与消融、SAE 特征分析来刻画多语言模型中任务无关的潜在表征（interlingua），并把它与低资源机器翻译这一实际痛点连接起来，对理解多语言内部表征几何与跨语言 circuits 都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the "no language-pair interaction" and target-dominance results survive a learned MT metric (COMET) or chrF/human evaluation, or is the target-beats-source asymmetry largely a BLEU n-gram artifact as the authors suspect?
- Is there a direct representation-level test of the interlingua — e.g., cross-lingual activation patching of the source-side latent into a same-language run — and does it localize the same mid-layer heads found by GCM?
- Would a parallel+replay fine-tuning control eliminate the retention advantage of the monolingual condition, making the two conditions fully comparable?
- Do larger or instruction-tuned models develop dedicated language-pair-specific translation mechanisms (e.g., word-translation-selective components), eroding the monolingual-recovery effect at scale?
- Does monolingual fine-tuning recover parallel-level gains across many low-resource languages simultaneously, and what predicts when it fails (script, pretraining exposure, morphological complexity)?
