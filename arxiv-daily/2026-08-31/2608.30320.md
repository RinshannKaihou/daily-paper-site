> **一句话总结：** Qwen 团队公开 Qwen3.8-Flash-Next（125B 总参 / 6B 激活 / 额外 51B off-accelerator n-gram 嵌入）的完整架构与消融报告：GDN+全局注意力 3:1 混合、续训阶段把全部注意力层换成压缩索引的 Qwen Sparse Attention（1M 上下文 kernel 级 prefill 提速 7.6×、decode 提速 4.9×）、4 分支加宽残差 + 门控残差（GR），配合 Muon 优化器和重拟合的 scaling law——以 1/3 激活参数、1/3 训练 token、约 1/9 训练 FLOPs 在 14 项基座评测中的 8 项超过 397B-A17B 前代旗舰（其余落后至多 2.6 分），并且在 4× 最优学习率压力测试下零 loss spike。

> **TL;DR:** Qwen3.8-Flash-Next replaces the 397B-A17B flagship with a 125B-total/6B-activated sparse MoE (+51B host-memory n-gram embeddings). On 14 base-model benchmarks it beats Qwen3.8-27B-Base on all 14 and the much larger Qwen3.7-Plus-Base on 8 of 14 (e.g., MMLU-Pro 73.23 vs 70.90 for Plus; trailing by at most 2.6 pts on MultiPL-E), at 1/3 the activated parameters, 1/3 training tokens and ~1/9 the FLOPs. The four ingredients: a 3:1 Gated DeltaNet / global-attention hybrid (ablation avg 53.81 vs 49.87 for full attention and 51.15 for SWA on nine benchmarks at the 25B-A3B scale); Qwen Sparse Attention with a compressed lightweight indexer (kernel-level 7.6× prefill and 4.9× decode speedup over dense GQA at 1M context, while MRCR@1M rises 20.71→26.44); a Gated Residual over four widened branches (benchmark avg 56.60 vs 50.91 pre-norm and 54.47 mHC at equal final loss); and Muon plus a refit hyperparameter scaling law that shifts the optimum to larger batch (25.2M vs 12.6M tokens) and learning rate (+4.14 avg downstream vs the old recipe at 156B-A7B scale). Crucially for stability work: at 4× optimal LR, the old AdamW recipe spikes 183 times per 10k steps and rides the clipping threshold on 213 of 19,942 steps, whereas the GR+Muon configuration records zero loss spikes — and the full-scale production run finished without a single loss spike or qk-clip/SwiGLU-clip.

## 核心贡献

- **总体配方**：以 125B 总参 / 6B 激活（另有 51B n-gram 嵌入表放在主机内存、异步预取）复刻前代 397B-A17B 旗舰的质量：14 项基座评测 8 项领先、其余最多落后 2.6 分，训练成本约为 1/3 激活参数、1/3 token、1/9 FLOPs。
- **GDN 混合 + QSA 稀疏注意力**：token 混合层按 3 GDN : 1 全注意力分块；续训时把所有全注意力层换成 QSA——用压缩轻量索引器在 micro-block 粒度打分，索引开销从 O(n²) 降到 O(n²/r)，1M 上下文 kernel 级 prefill 7.6× / decode 4.9× 提速，长上下文检索反而更好。
- **Gated Residual（GR）**：把 GatedNorm 与 4 分支加宽残差（AltUp/Hyper-Connections 一脉）合并成单一算子——按分支归一化、elementwise 数据依赖门控读取、每分支一个数据依赖标量写入；等最终 loss 下平均分大幅领先 pre-norm 与 mHC，并成为稳定性的主要来源（gate 把 3× LR 下的 spike 率从 32.0/10k 步降到 3.2/10k 步）。
- **Muon + 新 scaling law**：Muon 只用于二维线性映射权重（q/k/v、GDN 投影等），fused 参数先拆分再正交化，自研 Cananza 做分布式 Muon；重拟合的 scaling law 把最优 batch 和学习率显著上移（batch 12.6M→25.2M），并证明 Muon 之下 batch-size warmup 不再必要（ramp 白花 18.8% 优化器步数）。
- **方法论层面的贡献**：确立"loss、benchmark、成本三轴 + 压力测试 + 生产规模验证"的设计评估协议，显式记录 loss 与下游指标背道而驰的多处案例（加宽残差 loss 差 0.002 但 benchmark 差 1.98 分；稀疏读 pre-training 无损但 post-training 明显变差），以及 GR 分支的精确路径分解（4 分支中一条专门承载长程路径）。

## Key Contributions

- **Efficiency flagship recipe**: a 125B/6B-activated sparse MoE with an additional 51B n-gram embedding table held off-accelerator matches or beats the 397B-A17B predecessor on 8/14 pre-training benchmarks while trailing by at most 2.6 points, using 1/3 activated parameters, 1/3 training tokens, and ~1/9 training FLOPs.
- **Qwen Sparse Attention (QSA)**: a compressed lightweight indexer (MQA, average-pooled key blocks, partial RoPE, block-causal scoring) scores context at micro-block granularity, cutting indexing complexity from O(n²) to O(n²/r); trained in two stages (dense distillation of the full-attention distribution via KL, then sparse joint training) with total loss gap vs full attention at the order of 1e-4.
- **Gated Residual (GR)**: merges GatedNorm with the widened 4-branch residual stream — per-branch RMSNorm, a data-dependent elementwise read gate predicted from all branches, and one data-dependent scalar write per branch; the H_res mixing operator and static learned terms are ablated away as useless, making the dominant inference cost just the widened-stream memory traffic (stored in FP8).
- **Optimizer + scaling-law co-design**: Muon on 2D linear-map weights only (router, output head, embeddings, low-rank GR projections stay on AdamW/Adam), fused parameters split before Newton-Schulz, distributed via the Canzna framework; a refit scaling law predicts substantially larger optimal batch (25.2M vs previous 12.6M) and learning rate, both verified separately at 4T-token (10.8B-A0.89B) and 419B-token (156B-A7B) budgets; batch-size warmup is shown to be unnecessary under Muon.
- **Stability as a first-class axis**: stress tests that hold LR at 2×/4× optimal reproduce large-scale instabilities at moderate scale; the gate in GR is isolated as the key stability margin provider, and the criterion "new recipe at least as stable as the previous under equal stress" gates the design.

## Methodology

The paper describes Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and the ablation protocol that selected it. **Token mixing** alternates three Gated DeltaNet (GDN) layers with one attention layer per block of four; GDN keeps a recurrent matrix state S_t updated by a gated delta rule (decay α_t, write gate β_t, L2-normalized q/k after short convolutions), and at continued pretraining every full-attention layer (backbone and MTP) is replaced by QSA. QSA's indexer compresses keys into non-overlapping blocks of r=4 via average pooling, scores block-causal query-block similarity with ReLU-aggregated MQA logits (4 query heads, 1 shared key head, partial RoPE on 64 of 128 dims), and selects top-K_B blocks (token budget K=2048) for sparse core attention; training proceeds in two stages — dense distillation of the token-level teacher attention distribution into block-level scores (max-pool + L1 normalization, KL loss, ~2B tokens), then joint sparse training (~200B tokens, LR 2.5e-5). **Residual**: the stream is widened to n_r=4 branches (round-robin writes); GR normalizes each branch, predicts an elementwise gate G from all branches via a low-rank bottleneck (r = d/8), averages gated branches, and writes with one learned scalar per branch; ablations start from static operators (worth +1.58 avg points over pre-norm) and add data-dependence (+1.98 further) while dropping H_res. **N-gram embeddings**: a single n-gram embedding layer (Layer 2, 51B params, 300 tokens per active parameter) augments each token's embedding with retrieved n-gram vectors through contextual gating; tables are addressed deterministically and prefetched from host memory. **Optimization**: Muon with Nesterov momentum (μ=0.95), Polar-Express NS schedule (8 steps), γ(A,B)=0.2√max(A,B) scaling, Frobenius norm constant 1e-4; fused matrices (qkv, fc1/fc2, GDN in_proj) are split before orthogonalization; Canonza decouples logical from physical layout for distributed Muon with All-to-All. **Hyperparameters**: a refit Kaplan-style scaling law predicts batch size and LR; predictions validated on a 20-layer 10.8B-A0.89B MoE over a 4T-token budget (batch) and a 48-layer 156B-A7B MoE over 419B tokens (LR), each compared against the previous Qwen3.5 recipe. **Stability**: stress tests hold LR constant at 2×/4× optimal on a 28-layer 25B-A3B MoE, measuring loss spikes (excursions >0.1 above the 201-step rolling median), p99.9 pre-clip gradient norm, clipping-threshold crossings, and per-block max activation; the single-variable gate ablation toggles GatedNorm at 3× LR; finally the first 276B tokens of the production run are compared across three recipes sharing data order and schedule.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline base-model comparison (Table 11)**: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-Base (125B/6B) outperforms Qwen3.8-27B-Base on all 14 benchmarks and Qwen3.7-Plus-Base (397B/17B) on 8 of 14 — e.g., MMLU-Pro 73.23 vs 68.60 (27B) and 70.90 (Plus); SuperGPQA 51.36 vs 44.86 / 48.42; MATH 72.78 vs 60.54 / 74.38; MGSM 89.33 vs 86.37 / 85.42; SWE-Bench-Pretrain 50.99 vs 41.66 / 49.24 — while trailing Plus by at most 2.6 points (MultiPL-E 79.09 vs 81.68), at 1/3 activated parameters, 1/3 tokens, ~1/9 FLOPs.
- **GDN hybrid ablation (Table 1, 28-layer 25B-A3B, 400B tokens)**: the GDN hybrid averages 53.81 across nine benchmarks vs 49.87 for the full-attention Transformer and 51.15 for the SWA hybrid, winning 8/9 benchmarks (e.g., MATH 55.98 vs 49.40, MultiPL-E 47.48 vs 39.73, BBH 68.72 vs 63.78); the paper notes this does not isolate which component causes each improvement.
- **QSA quality and speed (Tables 2-3, Fig. 6)**: short-context average 76.8 vs 75.9 for full attention (matches/outperforms on 7 of 8 benchmarks; overall training-loss gap ~1e-4); long-context RULER 512K-1M improves 90.08→93.00 and MRCR 512K improves 30.66→40.53 (+9.87) and 1M 20.71→26.44 (+5.73), though MRCR at 128K/256K is slightly worse (95.98 vs 97.14, 93.00 vs 94.20); at 1M context the QSA attention module is 7.6× faster than dense GQA in prefill and 4.9× faster in decode at the kernel level, and QSA reuse in MTP leaves mean accepted length unchanged (4.07 vs 4.06).
- **Residual ablation (Table 5, 25B-A3B, 560B tokens)**: GR reaches 56.60 average benchmark score vs 54.47 for mHC (dynamic), 52.49 for mHC (static), and 50.91 for pre-norm, at essentially equal final loss (1.596 vs 1.594/1.596) — widening alone is worth 1.58 points and data-dependent read/write adds 1.98 more while the loss gap is only 0.002, a case where "loss would have underestimated the value of the change."
- **Stress tests (Figs. 10-12, 28-layer 25B-A3B)**: at 4× optimal LR, the Qwen3.5-structure AdamW baseline spikes 183 times per 10k steps and crosses the gradient-clipping threshold on 213 of 19,942 steps, while both Muon runs never cross the threshold and the GR configuration records zero loss spikes; at 2× LR, AdamW spikes 4.3 per 10k steps vs 0.2 for Muon configs; the single-variable gate toggle at 3× LR cuts the spike rate from 32.0 to 3.2 per 10k steps and threshold crossings from 256 to 20.
- **Production-run verification (Fig. 13, first 276B tokens)**: GR lowers loss by 0.026 over Qwen3.5+Muon and the full Flash-Next recipe by a further 0.032 (total −0.058); p99.9 pre-clip gradient norm drops from 0.097/0.109 to 0.043/0.066, and the full-scale training proceeded without a single loss spike or anomalous gradient fluctuation, with no qk-clip or SwiGLU-clip.
- **Scaling-law verification**: the new batch prediction (B=25.2M) ends at loss 1.5702 vs 1.5714 for the previous recipe (−7.2e-3) on the 4T-token run, with a further 1.5× batch increase costing only 4.3e-4; batch-size warmup ramps end 2.5e-4 to 3.5e-4 worse and need 18.8% more optimizer steps; at 156B-A7B/419B tokens the old recipe ends 7.8e-3 above the predicted LR optimum, and the predicted setting posts the best downstream average 60.55 vs 56.41 for the old recipe (+4.14).
- **Kernel and n-gram results**: the TileLang-based FlashQA library achieves 2-3× forward and ~2× backward speedup over the FLA Triton kernel (open-sourced at github.com/QwenLabs/FlashQA); scaling the n-gram vocabulary from 20× to 200× the 250K tokenizer vocabulary lowers loss monotonically (1.585→1.526) and consistently improves Chinese benchmarks (C-Eval 66.91→74.94) while other downstream scores saturate or fluctuate.

## Critical Assessment

The methodology is unusually honest, but several specifics warrant caution. First, the flagship comparison conflates architecture with data budget: Flash-Next-Base is trained on ~1/3 the tokens of Qwen3.7-Plus-Base, so "beats the flagship on 8/14 at 1/9 FLOPs" is a cost-adjusted claim, not a like-for-like architecture comparison — and the 8/14 tally is helped by a team-constructed SWE-Bench-Pretrain benchmark (footnote 1: patch-similarity scoring against golden diffs, not real SWE-Bench), where Flash-Next scores 50.99 vs Plus's 49.24 while genuinely established coding suites (MultiPL-E 79.09 vs 81.68) still favor Plus. Second, the QSA "matches or outperforms full attention" claim is carried by long lengths: at MRCR 128K and 256K QSA is measurably worse (95.98 vs 97.14 and 93.00 vs 94.20), and the abstract does not surface this regression. Third, ablations are single runs with no error bars; the authors themselves concede that the ~0.1-0.5-point margins among top settings in Table 10 "likely fall within standard evaluation noise," yet similar-magnitude margins elsewhere (e.g., GR's 0.008-0.011 loss ordering vs Block AttnRes) are used to justify design decisions. Fourth, the 51B-parameter n-gram tables live in host memory: the "negligible additional per-token FLOPs and latency" claim is argued from deterministic addressing and asynchronous prefetching, but no end-to-end serving throughput or tail-latency measurement of the host-memory path appears in the paper — the efficiency claim most exposed to real-world memory-bandwidth contention is exactly the one asserted rather than measured. Fifth, the GDN-hybrid headline (Table 1) is, by the authors' own admission, a three-checkpoint comparison that "does not by itself isolate which architectural component causes each improvement." Finally, the paper transparently documents two cases where pre-training metrics would have led to wrong decisions (the sparsified read that degraded post-training quality; loss-vs-benchmark divergence on data-dependent residual operators) — commendable, but it also means several shipped decisions rest on late-stage or post-training evidence that is summarized only qualitatively here.

## Strengths

- Three-axis evaluation discipline (loss + benchmarks + cost across training, prefill, decode) with explicit, instructive documentation of where the axes disagree — a template other labs' reports do not follow.
- Stability is treated as a measurable design constraint: stress tests amplify instabilities at moderate scale (constant LR at 2×/4× optimal, following Wortsman et al.), a single-variable gate ablation isolates the causal factor, and the finding is then verified on the actual production run (first 276B tokens) rather than assumed to extrapolate.
- Scaling-law predictions are validated separately per hyperparameter in the regime where each is most sensitive (batch on a 4T-token budget, LR at 156B-A7B scale), not just fitted and trusted.
- Genuine negative results reported: batch-size warmup unnecessary under Muon, multi-layer n-gram placement yields no consistent benefit, sparsified residual reads pass pre-training but fail post-training, IndexShare loses to per-layer compression, vocabulary-compression strategies show no gains.
- The GR path decomposition (Eq. 35-37) is an exact, machine-checkable attribution showing one branch specializes to long-range paths (median skip 10.9 vs 3.4-3.9 layers) — rare mechanistic depth in an architecture report.
- FlashQA kernels open-sourced; implementation details (NS steps, splitting granularity, Canonza partitioning, CUDA-graph capture) are concrete enough to guide practitioners.

## Limitations

- No post-trained/instruct model results, no decoding-quality evaluations beyond a mention of NoPE endless-generation issues; the report covers the base model only.
- The host-memory n-gram path lacks end-to-end serving measurements (throughput, tail latency under bandwidth contention); the claim of negligible latency is a design argument.
- All controlled ablations are single-run with no variance estimates; some design decisions hinge on margins the authors themselves classify as within evaluation noise.
- QSA slightly regresses short-context MRCR retrieval (128K/256K); the trade-off boundary is not mapped beyond the reported lengths.
- Training data mixture, tokenizer, and the exact continued-pretraining corpus composition are not specified; SWE-Bench-Pretrain is a self-defined similarity metric, limiting external comparability.
- The evaluation-throughput bottleneck is acknowledged as the open problem ("a cheaper mid-scale probe that reliably predicts post-training ordering" is future work), meaning the loss/benchmark divergence problem is diagnosed but not solved.

## Relevance

与你的"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"核心方向高度相关：报告用恒定 2×/4× 最优学习率的压力测试量化 loss spike 率、p99.9 梯度范数与裁剪阈值穿越次数，并把门控残差隔离为稳定裕度的单一来源（3× LR 下 spike 率 32.0→3.2/10k 步），还显式记录了 pre-training 指标误导决策的两个案例。同时它也是 Qwen 官方架构技术报告（Model System Cards 关注点），其 GR+Muon 在生产运行中零 loss spike、免 qk-clip 的结论值得归档。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- The widened residual stream is stored in FP8 — how does 4-branch redundancy interact with hardware-level silent data corruption (bit flips, DRAM errors)? Does the widened/gated read path damp or amplify propagated corruption compared to a single-stream residual, and could GR's gate serve as an anomaly sensor for SDC detection?
- What is the end-to-end serving cost of the 51B host-memory n-gram tables under realistic memory-bandwidth contention and batched decode — does the "negligible latency" claim survive multi-tenant inference?
- Is the gate-as-rescaling stability mechanism specific to Muon's orthogonalized updates, or does GR confer the same spike suppression on AdamW-only training of other architectures?
- Could the constant-LR stress test (2×/4× optimal) be standardized as a pre-production stability gate with a quantitative acceptance threshold (spikes per 10k steps), independent of model family?
- The QSA indexer distills full-attention distributions via KL on LM data — does block selection remain faithful for agentic/retrieval workloads where attention mass concentrates differently, and does the 128K/256K MRCR regression close with more indexer capacity?
