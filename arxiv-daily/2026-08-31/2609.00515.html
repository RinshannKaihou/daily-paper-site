The Interlingua Hypothesis: LLMs Translate via a Latent Task-agnostic Feature Space

Jacob Brinton · Jannik Brinkmann · Mark Crovella · Aaron Mueller•Boston University / TU Clausthal•2026-08-31

mechanistic interpretability machine translation causal mediation low-resource

Key metric

99%

of the parallel-data BLEU gain recovered by monolingual-only Xhosa LoRA fine-tuning on Llama-3.1-8B (16.84 → 24.81, vs 24.88 with parallel data).

Why this matters

The bottleneck for low-resource translation is monolingual competence, not parallel corpora — unaligned text alone can partially unlock translation ability.

⚠ Critical edge

The headline target-vs-source asymmetry may be a BLEU n-gram artifact, and the 99% recovery claim rests on one language with an uncontrolled replay confound.

Methodology— triangulated in three legs

01

Behavioral regression. Across 18–24 FLORES languages, predict pairwise translation BLEU from per-language monolingual proxies (GlobalMMLU accuracy, MultiBLMP margin, FLORES perplexity). A linear source+target model is compared against a bilinear model adding a language-pair interaction term (nested F-tests); a rank-1 reconstruction of the 24×24 BLEU matrix checks additivity directly.

02

Causal mediation (GCM). Attribution patching estimates every attention head’s indirect effect on the log-probability of the gold translation across 8 languages / 56 directions, under a four-condition control set separating translation selectivity from generic fluency. Top heads are mean-ablated; BLEU and MultiBLMP are re-measured against size-matched random same-layer controls.

03

Training intervention. Rank-16 LoRA adapts Llama-3.1-8B and TinyAya-3B to Xhosa under two matched conditions: monolingual (unaligned text + high-resource replay) vs parallel (OPUS MT560 pairs) — next-token loss only, evaluated with sacreBLEU on FLORES devtest.

  • Monolingual competence alone predicts BLEU. GlobalMMLU accuracy reaches R²=0.739 (Llama) / 0.510 (Aya); a language-pair interaction term never helps — max ΔR²=0.0038 across all 14 nested F-tests.
  • Target-side competence dominates. Target-language BLEU variance is 9.3× (Llama) / 3.9× (Aya) source-side; the additive μ+α_S+β_T decomposition explains 93.2% / 87.9% of centered variance.
  • Translation heads are sparse, mid-layer, universal. Mean indirect effect exceeds the null control 3.0× over all heads and 5.2× for the top-10 (Llama); the top-20 heads rank top-20 in all 56 directions with stable signs, concentrated in layers 13–14 (Llama) / 15–20 (Aya).
  • Ablations confirm causal relevance to both tasks. Mean-ablating the top-10 positive heads cuts BLEU in all 8 target languages at roughly 2× the random control, and lowers MultiBLMP grammaticality margins in all 8 targets.
  • Retention favors monolingual FT. Fra→Eng holds at 42.28 (mono) vs 38.87 (parallel) on Llama; on higher-resource German/Thai, neither condition improves consistently.

Strengths

  • Converging evidence: three independent methods — regression, causal mediation, training intervention — with predictions stated in advance and falsifiable.
  • Careful controls: four-condition GCM control set, size-matched ablations, dose–response curves, and NEG-10 sign-flip checks.
  • Striking universality: ~20 heads with stable effect signs shared across all 56 directions, localized in mid layers consistent with the stages-of-inference literature.
  • Unusually candid limitations; the paper claims “a significant mechanism,” not exclusivity.
  • Actionable: target-side monolingual competence as a concrete low-resource MT bottleneck.

Limitations

  • BLEU-only evaluation; the headline target/source asymmetry may partly be an n-gram-metric artifact — no COMET, chrF, or human eval.
  • Small-n regressions (18–24 languages, 2–3 predictors); headline R² is in-sample with no cross-validated error.
  • Narrow mediation scope: last-source-token only, first-order attribution approximation; no representation-level cross-lingual patching to exhibit the feature space directly.
  • Replay-data confound: high-resource replay exists only in the monolingual condition; no parallel+replay control was run.
  • Generality rests on Xhosa; TinyAya recovers only 73%, into-Xhosa is near-zero, and German/Thai show no consistent effect.
  • Model scope: two 8B + one 3B decoder-only models; instruction-tuned, encoder-decoder, and frontier systems excluded from the intervention leg.

Questions for further exploration

  • Do the “no language-pair interaction” and target-dominance results survive a learned metric (COMET) or chrF/human evaluation?
  • Is there a direct representation-level test of the interlingua — e.g., cross-lingual activation patching — localizing the same mid-layer heads?
  • Would a parallel+replay control eliminate the retention advantage of the monolingual condition?
  • Do larger or instruction-tuned models develop dedicated language-pair-specific translation mechanisms, eroding the effect at scale?
  • Does monolingual fine-tuning recover parallel-level gains across many low-resource languages, and what predicts failure (script, exposure, morphology)?
