Spawn Freely, Act Sparingly: Progressive Risk Vesting for Recursive LLM-Agent Trees

Molly Wang · Imperial Business School, London · single-author theory paper · all evidence synthetic

llm-agents agent-safety recursive-delegation branching-process probabilistic-guarantees
Key Metric
+5.75%

Held-out net utility 0.7302 (progressive vesting, search width n = 14) vs 0.6971 (spawn charging, n = 5), 200,000 episodes, 95% CI [+0.0391, +0.0411] — same δ = 0.05 budget, wider search because only activated branches pay.

Why This Matters

A provable permission layer for recursive agents — separating sandbox exploration from real-world execution — though certificate learning and deployed evidence remain open.

Critical Edge

Certificates are inputs, not outputs: no method to learn or calibrate valid allowances is given or tested — without one, the guarantee is vacuous.

Methodology escrow accounting + branching theory

Model. A governor tracks the sandbox tree, the order branches receive real-world authority, and a root risk escrow δ that delegation cannot copy. Activation = proposing an action or capability that could realize a catastrophic predicate; every approved activation carries a certificate E[Hᵢ | Fᵢ₋₁] ≤ aᵢ conditioned on the full pre-activation history, including the information used to select the branch, with Σaᵢ ≤ δ a.s.

  • Theorem 1 (anytime bound P(∪Hᵢ) ≤ δ) follows from Boole’s inequality + tower property + monotone convergence — an online alpha-spending analog; adaptive topology and dependent outcomes are allowed.
  • Proposition 1 (option value): delayed vesting weakly dominates spawn charging under fixed gates and compute; a half-zero / half-2r counterexample shows marginal calibration fails once a parent selectively escalates (conditional risk rises to 2r).
  • Galton–Watson analysis: pgf φ(z) = 1 − s + sz, authority reproduction number Rₖ = ms; no-harm probability is the fixed point of h = (1−p)φ(h).
  • Occupancy LP duality yields risk price λ and compute price ν; nested fanout modes get a base-stock-type largest-k threshold rule.
  • Governor (Algorithm 1): bind identity/lineage → deny or narrow scope → compute certificate → check uncommitted lineage escrow → atomic debit, one execution; every retry re-gates, debits are kept after exposure.
Status: correct but elementary probability; the intellectual content is the accounting design. All numerics are synthetic (fixed seed 20260901, fixed-point tolerance 10⁻¹⁴).

Results beyond the headline

  • Phase transition at Rₖ = ms = 1 (Poisson-branching grid 131 m × 120 s values, p = 0.005): trajectory harm is 1.23×10⁻³ at Rₖ = 0.6 vs 9.68×10⁻² at Rₖ = 1.4 — a ~79× jump across criticality at identical local risk.
  • Supercritical harm ignores local-risk fixes: cutting p from 0.005 to 10⁻⁵ leaves harm stuck at the 51.10% nonextinction floor (Rₖ = 1.4); the subcritical case falls to 2.50×10⁻⁵, matching the analytic 1/(1−0.6) = 2.5 multiplier. Below criticality, reliability buys safety; above it, only topology matters.
  • Closed-form admission rule (Corollary 1): expected authority nodes 1/(1−Rₖ); the union bound r/(1−Rₖ) ≤ δ is equivalent to Rₖ ≤ 1 − r/δ — spending 20% of escrow per activation forces Rₖ ≤ 0.8.

Strengths

  • Clean, implementable split: spawning is a compute decision, activation is a risk decision; Algorithm 1 is concrete (identity binding, atomic debit, retries re-gated).
  • The conditional-certificate formulation targets a real failure mode — selection-induced miscalibration — and the anytime guarantee correctly permits adaptive topology and dependent branch outcomes, which heuristic budget schemes ignore.
  • Assumption-light dominance argument (Proposition 1) plus a directly actionable budget rule (Rₖ ≤ 1 − r/δ).
  • Shadow prices λ, ν connect agent governance to inventory theory (base-stock fanout thresholds) with interpretable knobs.
  • Unusually honest scoping: synthetic-only disclaimers, a concrete evaluation roadmap, an ethics paragraph, and full reproducibility detail (seed, splits, tolerance).

Limitations

  • Certificates are inputs, not outputs: no learning/calibration/auditing method for valid aᵢ — the guarantee is vacuous without one, and the offered “stratified calibration” remedy is never evaluated.
  • Conditional on sandbox enforcement: data exfiltration, shared-state modification, and compute exhaustion sit outside the bound — the safety work is relocated to the enforcement layer, where most real difficulty lives.
  • i.i.d. branching assumptions: shared models/tools make branches fail together; the phase-transition numerics are diagnostic, not predictive.
  • No empirical evaluation: the “baseline” is the author’s own accounting variant — no deployed framework, benchmark, or competing safety system (LACUNA, NIST harness cited but never run).
  • Single-setting numerics: one budget, one charge, one noise level, one seed; no sensitivity sweep, no public code URL; the union bound is conservative and strict budgets may render recursive authority infeasible.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • How would a learned risk monitor be trained and audited to emit selection-conditional certificates that remain valid under routing and escalation — the exact failure mode the half-zero / half-2r counterexample constructs?
  • Do real recursive frameworks (Webswarm, THREAD, AgentSpawn) exhibit the Rₖ = ms criticality — can m and s be measured from deployed traces, and does catastrophic-action rate scale like p, √p, or hit a floor?
  • Does the +5.75% option-value gain survive when sandbox compute draws on the same budget as activations, rather than the “compute charged separately” assumption?
  • What certificate mechanism handles correlated failures from shared models/tools — does conditioning on Fᵢ₋₁ suffice, or does escrow need joint per-lineage charges?
  • Would an AgentDojo-style deployment study show escrow overhead keeps task completion competitive at δ levels where the safety guarantee is meaningful?
