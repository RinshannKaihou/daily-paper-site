> **一句话总结：** 提出面向遥感视觉任务的自进化多智能体框架 RingMoClaw：用异构 LLM Critic（Minimax 2.7）在"方案/数据/结果"三阶段做独立量化评审，配合双流动态经验总线（外部文献/代码 RAG + 内部实验记忆）在 23 个算子的结构化搜索空间中剪枝寻优，并辅以"五分钟敏捷验证"筛选候选策略；在 FAIR1M 检测、NWPU-RESISC45 分类、iSAID 分割、LEVIR-CD 变化检测四个任务上分别提升 +1.84 mAP₅₀、+1.79%、+2.70 mIoU、+1.01 F1/+2.87 IoU，且进化轮数比 AutoResearchClaw/AutoResearch 减少 40% 以上（7 轮 vs 18/20 轮），GPU 时从 26.90 降到 12.14 小时。

> **TL;DR:** RingMoClaw turns a remote sensing research loop into a closed, self-evolving multi-agent system: three worker agents (Main/Data/Vision, all GLM-5 Turbo) propose plans, preprocess data, and train/evaluate models, while an independent Critic (Minimax 2.7, deliberately a different LLM) scores every stage with a weighted formula Score = w₁C_t + w₂(1−B_t) + w₃H_t and a dual-stream experience bus injects RAG'd literature/code plus internal failure memory into a 23-operator structured search space. On FAIR1M object detection the system lifts mAP₅₀ from 47.30 to 49.14 (+1.84), beating AutoResearchClaw (+0.57 in 18 rounds) and AutoResearch (−3.50 in 20 rounds) while using 7 rounds, 12.14 GPU-hours vs AutoResearch's 26.90, and a five-minute screening protocol whose rankings correlate with full training (Spearman ρ=0.64, p=0.03).

## 核心贡献

- **统一的自进化研究闭环**：突破现有遥感 agent "一次性任务执行"的范式，将 Main Agent（规划编排）、Data Agent（数据检查与预处理）、Vision Agent（训练与评测）组成研究分支，把"模型改进背后的研究过程"本身自动化，优化轨迹由实验反馈与积累经验动态修正。
- **异构 Critic 机制**：Critic 由与执行链不同的 LLM（Minimax 2.7 vs 执行链的 GLM-5 Turbo）驱动，仅负责评审与诊断、不参与生成与执行；在方案、数据、结果三个阶段做分阶段评审，输出任务完成度 C_t、瓶颈影响 B_t、历史经验一致性 H_t 三维量化评分 Score_t = w₁C_t + w₂(1−B_t) + w₃H_t（三阶段权重各异），并按显式规则升级/接受/打回（Score ≥ 0.75 且 B_t ≤ 0.30 才接受）。
- **双流动态经验总线**：外部流通过 RAG 从论文与开源仓库抽取高层方法论与低层算子 ω={I,O,F,P}（输入输出张量、核心算子逻辑、关键超参分布），内部流维护任务内负记忆 M_loc（剪枝不可行超参组合）与跨任务全局经验池 M_glb（按 Score_t 门槛 Filter 后增量并入），共同支撑结构化搜索空间 S={S_arch,S_train,S_data,S_intg} 的动态扩展与剪枝。
- **五分钟敏捷验证协议**：用 f_agile = ΔAcc/Δt · β·exp(−σ‖∇²J‖)（单位时间精度增益 × 训练稳定性因子，稳定性用最后 50 个 loss 的变异系数代理）对候选策略做低成本预筛，进化在 20 轮上限或连续 2 轮无改进时终止。
- **四任务实证 + 双组件消融**：在目标检测/场景分类/语义分割/变化检测四个任务上验证，并与 AutoResearch、AutoResearchClaw 两个研究自动化框架做跨任务对比（精度增益 + 进化轮数 + GPU 时 + token 消耗），并分别消融 Critic 与双流经验总线。

## Key Contributions

- A **unified closed-loop research pipeline** for remote sensing vision tasks (Main Agent for planning/orchestration, Data Agent for preprocessing, Vision Agent for training/eval), automating the research process *behind* model improvement rather than just one-pass task execution.
- A **heterogeneous Critic mechanism**: an independent reviewer LLM (Minimax 2.7) distinct from the executor LLM (GLM-5 Turbo) performs staged Plan/Data/Result reviews and emits a Multi-Dimensional Score over task completion C_t, bottleneck impact B_t, and historical experience influence H_t, with explicit accept (Score ≥ 0.75, B_t ≤ 0.30) / revise (≥ 0.45) / escalate rules.
- A **dual-stream dynamic experience bus** that converts external knowledge (RAG over papers and repos, extracted into reusable operators ω = {I, O, F, P}) and internal experimental trajectories (local negative memory + score-gated global memory) into a structured search space S = {S_arch, S_train, S_data, S_intg} with 23 predefined operators, enabling pruning of unpromising directions.
- An **agile five-minute verification protocol** (f_agile = ΔAcc/Δt · β·exp(−σ‖∇²J‖)) that screens candidate strategies cheaply using accuracy gain per unit time and a training-stability factor (coefficient of variation of the final 50 training losses).
- **Four-task empirical validation** with cross-framework comparison on both accuracy gain and research efficiency (evolution rounds, GPU-hours, tokens), plus separate ablations of the Critic and the experience bus.

## Methodology

**Overall architecture.** RingMoClaw (built on the RingMo remote sensing backbone family) comprises three components: a Self-Evolving Research Pipeline, a Critic mechanism, and a Dual-stream Dynamic Experience Bus. All agents are driven by fixed, version-controlled system prompts with declared inputs and JSON output contracts; every output is schema-validated before entering the next stage. The Main Agent decomposes the task description T into a staged evolution roadmap; the Data Agent handles dataset checking/preprocessing; the Vision Agent performs training and evaluation. Execution chains run on GLM-5 Turbo, while the Critic is powered by Minimax 2.7 — a deliberate heterogeneity in pretraining, reasoning style, and knowledge preference to reduce self-evaluation bias; functionally the Critic participates in neither plan generation nor execution.

**Critic mechanism.** The Critic reviews three nodes of the chain: (a) Plan review — technical-rationality check of the plan (task alignment, loss-function/data fit, consistency with historical records and literature priors) to intercept clearly unreasonable plans before wasted computation; (b) Data review — title-size fit, class balance, integrity/format compliance, and preprocessing-plan coherence; (c) Result review — whether performance reaches the domain baseline in the experience pool, per-category balance, overfitting, and substantive improvement over the previous round. Each review produces a Multi-Dimensional Score Score_t = w₁C_t + w₂(1−B_t) + w₃H_t with stage-dependent weights: (0.30, 0.50, 0.20) for plan, (0.35, 0.40, 0.25) for data, (0.50, 0.30, 0.20) for result. Missing critical evidence triggers "escalate"; otherwise accept at Score ≥ 0.75 and B_t ≤ 0.30, revise at Score ≥ 0.45, reject below. All feedback is routed to the Main Agent as diagnosis reports (Eq. 2: o_{t+1} = Π_update(o_t, {c_t, Score_t})), and each iteration record is serialized into the experience bus, whose admission into global memory M_glb follows score-based filtering (M_glb ← M_glb ∪ Filter(M_loc, Score_t)).

**Dual-stream experience bus.** The external stream runs online RAG-based retrieval-augmented generation: high-level method extraction from papers/READMEs (novel loss functions, learning-rate schedules, connection patterns mapped into heuristic guidance vectors), and low-level operator extraction from code (strip I/O/logging scaffolding, retain core operator ω = {I, O, F, P}, then automatically search for candidate mounting positions matching ω). The internal stream keeps (a) local experience per task — negative memory M_loc recording hyperparameter combinations causing severe accuracy fluctuation or resource overflow, enabling real-time path pruning; and (b) global experience across tasks — never cleared, retrieved by weighted task-similarity matching over task description, current bottleneck, dataset characteristics, model, and primary metric. The bus continuously prunes unpromising paths and refines optimization directions in the structured search space.

**Search space and decision evolution.** S = {S_arch, S_train, S_data, S_intg} covers model architecture, training strategy, data processing, and system integration with 23 predefined base operators, each with applicable-bottleneck tags and constrained parameter spaces; new operators from retrieval/generation are incorporated after filtering and structured parameterization. Every candidate strategy first passes the five-minute verification: f_agile(m) = (ΔAcc/Δt)·β·exp(−σ‖∇²_J‖), where Δt is normalized to one verification window and stability is the coefficient of variation of the final 50 training losses. A candidate is promoted if it achieves a positive performance gain, completes verification without training failure, satisfies f_agile > 0.55, passes the result-stage Critic check, and is not rejected by negative memory. Evolution stops at a maximum of 20 rounds or when neither the max-20 bound nor two consecutive full-experiment rounds improve the best-so-far result.

**Instantiated trajectory (Algorithm 1, FAIR1M detection).** Stage 1: diagnostic planning of the baseline (YOLO11x-OBB head); Stage 2: iterative policy optimization over augmentation (rotation/flip/mixup) and few-shot handling, with the Critic rejecting directions that decrease overall accuracy and recording the failure in E; Stage 3: automated module evolution (ClassAwareSampler, focal loss, reused BFPA attention module), plus two newly designed modules — Multi-Scale Feature Refinement (MSFR, replaces the FPN neck with an interface-compatible multi-scale refinement module) and Contrastive Prototype Refinement (CPR, prototype-based logit refinement replacing the RoI classification head) — instantiated on Oriented R-CNN and validated by the Critic before admission into E.

## Results & Highlights

- **Object detection (FAIR1M, mAP₅₀):** the evolution trajectory reaches **49.14 vs 47.30 for the system's own YOLO11x-OBB baseline (+1.84)**; mAP₅₀:₉₅ rises 36.99 → 37.10. For context, published FAIR1M detectors in Table I score far lower (Oriented R-CNN 44.30, OFA-Net 43.73, Gliding Vertex 29.92). Per-stage: data augmentation 48.18, training-config update 47.77 (a regression that the Critic catches), few-shot 47.69, multi-model fusion 49.11, module innovation 49.14. MSFR+CPR on Oriented R-CNN attains 49.14/37.10 for only +0.730 M params and +0.876 G FLOPs over the 119 M / 167 G baseline. Note the trajectory is the **best of three independent runs**.
- **Scene classification (NWPU-RESISC45, Top-1):** soft TTA fusion reaches **94.97% vs the 93.18% baseline (+1.79 pts)**, fusing three candidate classifiers the system selected itself (ConvNeXt-T 92.25%, EfficientNet-B4 92.08%, ResNet50 82.10%); CAM analysis shows the candidates attend to complementary regions, motivating score-level fusion (93.18 → 93.71 hard → 94.48 soft → 94.97 soft+TTA).
- **Semantic segmentation (iSAID, mIoU):** **69.9 vs 67.2 for UperNet-RingMo (+2.70)**, via larger backbone (69.0) + single-scale upsampling (69.5) + multiscale TTA (69.8) + upscale-only TTA (69.9); prior methods in Table III sit at 62.1–64.8 (RANet, HMANet, FarSeg, FaceSeg, GRDL).
- **Change detection (LEVIR-CD):** the evolved EvoChangeStar achieves **90.54 F1 / 89.97 IoU vs the 89.53/87.10 baseline (+1.01 F1, +2.87 IoU)**, edging ChangeFormer (90.40/88.80) by only +0.14 F1; the winning composition is Bidirectional ChangeStar (89.18/88.89) plus deep change interaction (89.63/88.57).
- **Cross-framework comparison (Table V, gains over each task's own baseline):** RingMoClaw gains **+1.84 mAP₅₀ in 7 evolution rounds vs AutoResearchClaw +0.57 in 18 rounds and AutoResearch −3.50 in 20 rounds**; on the other tasks RingMoClaw posts +1.79 Acc (4 rounds), +2.70 mIoU (4 rounds), +1.01 F1/+2.87 IoU (6 rounds). AutoResearch actually degrades two tasks (−3.50 mAP₅₀, −0.98 F1).
- **Cost (Table VII, detection):** RingMoClaw uses **12.14 GPU-hours and 10.61 h wall-clock vs 26.90/13.45 h (AutoResearch) and 18.28/13.28 h (AutoResearchClaw)**, with 0.72 M input / 0.38 M output tokens (AutoResearchClaw: 2.68 M / 0.11 M); all LLM calls ran through CSTCloud with no commercial API cost.
- **Ablations (FAIR1M):** removing the Critic raises convergence from **7 to 11 rounds and drops final mAP from 49.14 to 47.43** (Table VIII); removing the experience streams gives 9 rounds/48.44 (w/o external), 11 rounds/47.62 (w/o internal), 14 rounds/47.53 with 6 failed iterations (w/o both) — the internal stream matters more for search efficiency (Table IX).
- **Five-minute verification protocol (Table X, 10 candidates):** Spearman ρ = 0.64 for mAP₅₀:₉₅ (permutation p = 0.03) and ρ = 0.59 for mAP₅₀ between five-minute and full-training gains; detrimental-candidate screening reaches **precision 1.00 (5/5 flagged candidates truly harmful) and recall 0.83 (5 of 6 harmful candidates caught)**.

## Critical Assessment

- **Best-of-N reporting on the headline task.** For FAIR1M the optimization "was independently repeated three times, and Table I reports the trajectory of the run with the highest final mAP₅₀." Reporting the best of three stochastic LLM-agent runs overstates expected performance; no variance across the three runs is given, and the other tasks do not state run counts at all — for a paper whose core claim is *reliable* closed-loop optimization, this is the most significant statistical weakness.
- **The cross-framework comparison leans on weak baselines.** Table V's headline (fewer rounds, higher gain) is partly a function of AutoResearch being brittle: it *loses* 3.50 mAP₅₀ on detection and 0.98 F1 on change detection relative to its own baseline, and generates only one candidate per round by construction (Table VII note). With n = 2 comparison frameworks, both from the same research lineage (AutoResearchClaw shares an author ecosystem), the ">40% fewer evolution steps" claim has thin external validity; no comparison to ML-agent systems like MLE-bench-style agents or MetaGPT/AutoML baselines is attempted.
- **Beating task SOTA is marginal where it matters.** On LEVIR-CD, EvoChangeStar's 90.54 F1 is only +0.14 over ChangeFormer (90.40), and BIT already reports 89.57 IoU vs 89.97; on FAIR1M the compared detectors (RetinaNet, Cascade R-CNN, 2020-era Gliding Vertex) are stale, and the strongest contextual baseline (Oriented R-CNN 44.30) is the authors' own architecture family. The honest contribution is delta-over-own-baseline, not domain SOTA.
- **Hand-tuned Critic weights and thresholds are never ablated.** The stage-dependent weights (0.30/0.50/0.20 etc.), the 0.75/0.45/0.30 accept-revise-escalate thresholds, and f_agile's β, σ, and the 0.55 promotion bar are all fixed by fiat; a sensitivity analysis over any of them is missing, leaving the sensitivity of the whole control loop unknown.
- **The verification protocol is validated on a tiny, single-task sample.** Table X's ρ = 0.64/0.59 and 1.00/0.83 precision/recall come from exactly 10 candidate strategies on one task (FAIR1M detection); recall 0.83 on n = 6 positives means a single missed candidate flips the estimate. The protocol is honestly framed as a screening proxy rather than a predictor, but its generality across tasks is untested.
- **Reproducibility is partial.** Prompts (Appendix E), operator definitions (Appendix B), and similarity/retrieval thresholds (Appendix C) are documented, but no code or experience-pool release is mentioned; results also depend on two specific proprietary LLM endpoints (GLM-5 Turbo, Minimax 2.7 via CSTCloud), so exact replication is not possible even in principle without the same models, and no alternative-LLM ablation is provided.
- **Novelty is orchestration, not methods.** Every leaf technique the system selects (focal loss, class-aware sampling, mixup, TTA, soft fusion, BiFPN-style fusion, BFPA) is established; MSFR/CPR are simple neck/logit-head tweaks worth +0.11 mAP₅₀:₉₅ combined. The genuine novelty — heterogeneous critic + score-gated experience admission + structured operator space — is well-motivated but evaluated only on four single-dataset tasks, with no test of whether the evolved artifacts or the accumulated M_glb transfer to a new dataset.

## Strengths

- The closed-loop design is unusually disciplined: schema-validated JSON contracts, version-controlled prompts, an independent Critic that is barred from both generation and execution, and explicit accept/revise/escalate rules make the loop auditable rather than vibes-driven.
- The heterogeneous-executor/critic split (GLM-5 Turbo vs Minimax 2.7) is a concrete, testable answer to self-evaluation bias, and the functional separation (Critic diagnoses, never commands) avoids the failure mode of reviewer-agents overwriting workers.
- Cost accounting is far more thorough than typical agent papers: GPU-hours, wall-clock, input/output tokens, explored candidates, and failed iterations are all reported per framework (Table VII), and the no-commercial-API detail aids cost reproducibility.
- Both core components are ablated separately (Critic: 7→11 rounds, 49.14→47.43; experience streams: 7→14 rounds, 49.14→47.53), and the internal stream is correctly identified as the bigger search-efficiency contributor.
- The five-minute verification protocol is a practical answer to the dominant cost of autonomous research (full training per candidate) and comes with an actual statistical validation (Spearman ρ with permutation test) instead of anecdote.
- Four diverse task types (detection, classification, segmentation, change detection) with per-stage evolution trajectories (Table I) give an unusually transparent view of what the loop actually did, including a stage that regressed (training-config update 47.77 < 48.18) being caught and routed around.

## Limitations

- Single dataset per task; no cross-dataset evaluation of evolved models or of the global experience pool M_glb, so cross-task transferability — the stated point of the bus — is asserted rather than measured.
- Hardware and cost scope: all experiments on 2× RTX 4090 with storage/server assumptions for long-running multi-agent execution; the authors themselves flag multi-agent communication and model-invocation overhead.
- The Critic's three review stages depend on LLM judgment for qualitative checks (plan rationality, data suitability); no human agreement study or critic-error analysis is reported.
- Evolution is capped at 20 rounds with termination after two no-gain rounds; behavior beyond small search spaces (23 base operators) is unexplored, and the operator catalog is remote-sensing-specific.
- The five-minute protocol requires gradient-norm estimates ‖∇²_J‖ and loss-variance tracking inside training runs, adding instrumentation not present in vanilla pipelines.
- Reporting best-of-three on the flagship detection task without variance; no significance testing on any headline delta.

## Relevance

该论文与用户最高优先级方向 Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research 几乎完全重合：它是一个真实的自治研究闭环（假设→实验→独立评审→经验积累），并且其 Critic 评分、经验准入门槛、五分钟验证协议直接涉及长时自治系统的可靠性与质量控制，对设计自动化研究框架有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How sensitive is the closed loop to the hand-set Critic weights and thresholds (0.75/0.45/0.30, stage-dependent w₁w₂w₃)? Would a learned or calibrated critic outperform the fixed linear score?
- Does the global experience pool M_glb actually transfer: does a system pre-seeded with experiences from the other three tasks converge faster on a held-out dataset (e.g., DOTA detection or a second change-detection set)?
- The five-minute protocol was validated on 10 candidates from one task — does its ρ = 0.64 rank correlation hold for segmentation/change detection, and how does it compare against simpler proxies (e.g., 5-epoch loss slope alone)?
- How much of the gain comes from the heterogeneous LLM choice versus the staged review structure itself? An ablation swapping in the same model for Critic and executors is missing.
- The three FAIR1M runs showed enough variance to justify best-of-3 reporting — what is the run-to-run variance of final mAP, and does the Critic reduce it, not just the mean?
- Can the framework escape its 23-operator catalog in practice, i.e., how often did retrieved external operators (the ω = {I, O, F, P} extraction path) actually get admitted and win versus the predefined base operators?
