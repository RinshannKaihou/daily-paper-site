> **一句话总结：** 作者提出"权重范数临界性"这一被忽视的损失尖峰机制——权重衰减持续收缩归一化层前的尺度不变权重，使其范数趋于零并令 Hessian 曲率按 α⁻² 量级爆炸，由此推导出可在逐层层面预测尖峰并定位到具体模块（如 MLP）的稳定性边界，并在 ResNet-50/CIFAR-100 与 187M LLaMA-style Transformer 上验证。

> **TL;DR:** Beyond the usual Edge-of-Stability learning-rate criticality, this paper identifies a complementary **weight-norm criticality**: weight decay shrinks the norms of scale-invariant weights (those feeding BatchNorm/LayerNorm), which amplifies Hessian curvature by α⁻² and pushes training past the stability boundary. On a 5-layer FNN with BN on MNIST, the top Hessian eigenvalue λmax grows from 15 (wd=0) to 3863 (wd=0.03), and on a 187M-parameter LLaMA-style Transformer pretrained on a 100B-token corpus, disabling weight decay on MLP modules removes the dominant curvature direction and substantially reduces spike frequency (final loss 2.548 vs 2.720).

## 核心贡献

- 提出并形式化**"权重范数临界性"（weight-norm criticality）**这一新机制：归一化引入的尺度不变性 + 权重衰减的持续范数收缩共同作用，会在尺度不变参数趋于零时令 Hessian 曲率爆炸，从而产生损失尖峰。该视角与传统的学习率临界性（Edge of Stability）互补。
- 给出理论结果 **Theorem 5.1（曲率爆炸）**：对正尺度不变的损失 L(αu,v)=L(u,v)，缩放尺度不变参数 u 时，λmax(H(αu,v)) ≥ α⁻²·λmax(Huu(u,v))。
- 推导出**逐权重范数稳定性边界** c* = √(ηρ/2)（ρ=‖u‖²·λmax(Huu) 为内禀曲率）与对应的**尖峰边界** c*_spike = √(ηρ_grad/2)（基于梯度方向曲率 λ_grad）。该边界可按各尺度不变组件分别计算，从而把尖峰定位到具体层/模块，而非整网。
- 通过受控实验从最小合成回归（y=x₁+2x₂）、MNIST FNN、CIFAR-100 ResNet-50，到 100B-token 语料的 187M LLaMA-style Transformer 一致验证：当 ‖W₁‖ 跌破 c*_spike 时与实测损失尖峰对齐。
- 给出**实用缓解策略**：在 Transformer 中只关闭 MLP 模块的权重衰减，即可显著降低尖峰频率并取得更低训练损失，验证了机制分析对实际训练的指导价值。

## Key Contributions

- Identifies and formalizes **weight-norm criticality** as a complementary driver of loss spikes: weight decay persistently contracts scale-invariant weights (those preceding BN/LN), driving their norms toward zero where curvature explodes.
- Proves **Theorem 5.1 (Curvature Explosion Induced by Scale Invariance)**: for positively scale-invariant L(αu,v)=L(u,v), λmax(H(αu,v)) ≥ α⁻²·λmax(Huu(u,v)), i.e. shrinking scale-invariant parameters by α amplifies the curvature in the u-subspace on the order of α⁻².
- Derives a per-component **Weight-Norm Stability Boundary** c* = √(ηρ/2) (Proposition 5.2) and a sharper **Weight-Norm Spike Boundary** c*_spike = √(ηρ_grad/2) (Proposition 5.3) based on the gradient-direction curvature λ_grad, which predicts one-step loss increases rather than only worst-case instability.
- Shows the analysis **decomposes across individual scale-invariant components**, so the instability can be attributed and localized to specific layers/modules instead of the whole network—extending prior globally scale-invariant theory (Li & Arora 2019; Kodryan et al. 2022) to architectures mixing scale-invariant and non-scale-invariant parameters.
- Validates the boundaries empirically across five settings (synthetic 2-D regression, MNIST FNN, CIFAR-100 ResNet-50, synthetic 3x→x Transformer, 187M LLaMA-style Transformer on a 100B-token corpus), and demonstrates a practical mitigation: turning off weight decay on MLP modules in a Transformer removes the dominant curvature direction and substantially reduces spike frequency.

## Methodology

The paper combines a theoretical Hessian-homogeneity analysis with controlled experiments and a module-wise diagnostic procedure.

1. **Scale-invariance setup.** Parameters are partitioned into scale-invariant u (e.g. weights feeding into a normalization layer, where L(αu,v)=L(u,v) for any α>0) and non-scale-invariant v. The block Hessian H(u,v) is decomposed accordingly.
2. **Curvature explosion theorem.** Under positive scale invariance, the largest eigenvalue of the full Hessian satisfies λmax(H(αu,v)) ≥ α⁻²·λmax(Huu(u,v)). This predicts that as weight decay shrinks u, the curvature in the u-subspace explodes.
3. **From curvature to weight-norm threshold.** Substituting the α⁻² scaling into the standard EoS stability criterion η·λmax(H) ≤ 2 yields a *weight-norm* boundary c* = √(ηρ/2), where ρ = ‖u‖²·λmax(Huu) is a norm-invariant "intrinsic curvature". A second-order Taylor expansion gives the sharper spike criterion η·λ_grad > 2 and the corresponding boundary c*_spike = √(ηρ_grad/2).
4. **Per-component decomposition.** Because the boundary is defined on Huu (the Hessian restricted to the scale-invariant component) rather than the full Hessian, each scale-invariant layer admits its own c*_spike, enabling layer-wise monitoring and attribution.
5. **Empirical validation.** (i) Parameter-scaling probes rescale only scale-invariant weights and confirm λmax follows the α⁻² lower bound (Fig. 5 on both a 3-layer FNN+BN and ResNet-50 on CIFAR-100). (ii) PCA visualizations (Fig. 3, Fig. 4b) show that stronger weight decay displaces the converged solution along the principal direction toward a "zero-limit" reference point where λmax is huge. (iii) Per-iteration monitoring of ‖W₁‖ vs c*_spike shows that sustained excursions below the boundary align with observed loss spikes. (iv) Module-wise top-Hessian-eigenvector decomposition (Fig. 8) identifies the MLP subspace as the dominant curvature carrier, motivating the wd-off-MLP ablation.
6. **Settings.** LLaMA-style 16-layer/16-head 187M Transformer (d_model=1280) trained one epoch on a 100B-token corpus with AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.999), peak LR 1e-3, grad-clip 1, wd swept over {0, 0.5, 1}. ResNet-50 on CIFAR-100 with SGD, batch 256, LR 0.003. MNIST FNN (4 hidden layers × 512 width) with optional BN/LN. Synthetic 3x→x next-token task with a 4-layer single-head Transformer (d_model=400).

## Results & Highlights

- **Curvature explosion on MNIST FNN+BN (Fig. 3):** as weight decay goes from wd=0 → wd=0.001 → wd=0.01 → wd=0.03, the endpoint λmax of the Hessian grows from **15 → 28 → 383 → 3863** (a ~258× increase) — direct quantitative support for Theorem 5.1.
- **Curvature explosion on synthetic 3-layer FNN+BN (Fig. 4):** endpoint λmax grows from **91 (wd=0)** to **113 (wd=0.01)**, to **205.76 (wd=0.05)**, to **281 (wd=0.1)** as weight decay increases, again following the predicted α⁻² trajectory.
- **Parameter-scaling validation of the α⁻² lower bound (Fig. 5):** on ResNet-50/CIFAR-100 (LR=0.002) and on the FNN+BN (LR=0.03), scaling only the scale-invariant weights by α∈[0.8,0.95] causes λmax of the full Hessian to rise sharply and to track the α⁻²·λmax(Huu) curve, empirically confirming the theoretical lower bound.
- **Spike-boundary alignment (Fig. 7):** in both the MNIST FNN+BN and the synthetic regression, the first layer's weight norm ‖W₁‖ persistently drops below the predicted c*_spike precisely during the iteration windows where pronounced loss spikes occur (marked by green/red dashed lines).
- **Module-wise attribution (Fig. 8) and mitigation on a 187M Transformer (Fig. 9):** the MLP blocks (fc1, fc2) dominate the top-Hessian-eigenvector contribution, with their share increasing around spikes. Disabling weight decay only on MLP modules (wd=3 for all-except-MLP) removes the steadily-rising MLP contribution and yields a far more stable loss curve with final loss **2.548 vs 2.720** for wd=3 applied to all modules.
- **Scale-invariant block dominates global sharpness mid-training (Fig. 6):** in the synthetic FNN+BN (η=0.03, wd=0.1), the λmax of the scale-invariant Hessian block starts below the non-scale-invariant block at epoch 0 but rapidly surpasses it and becomes the dominant contribution to the global λmax from mid-training onward.
- **Ablation: wd only on non-scale-invariant layer (Fig. 12–13, Appendix A.3):** applying weight decay exclusively to the fc-out (non-scale-invariant) layer removes the systematic λmax-increase trend and suppresses spikes even under large LR (0.01, 0.03) and large wd (up to 0.5), confirming scale-invariant parameters are the necessary ingredient.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The central α⁻² curvature-explosion result (Theorem 5.1) is essentially a sharpening/restatement of observations already made by Li & Arora (2019) on globally scale-invariant networks and by Kodryan et al. (2022) on sphere dynamics. The genuinely new contribution is the **extension to mixed scale-invariant + non-scale-invariant networks**, the per-component decomposition that enables layer-wise attribution, and the translation of the curvature threshold into a directly monitorable **weight-norm** threshold rather than a curvature threshold. This is a meaningful but incremental theoretical step.
- **Rigor.** The theory is clean and the controlled synthetic/MNIST experiments are well matched to the assumptions (full-batch GD, PSD local Hessian, strict layer-wise scale invariance). The extension to ResNet-50 is only approximate (residual connections preclude strict scale invariance — the authors honestly acknowledge this and rescale only Conv→BN weights), and the LLM setting uses AdamW where the EoS criterion is heuristic.
- **A specific weak point is the spike-boundary evaluation protocol.** The authors apply an *ad hoc* filtering procedure (merge intervals <30 iterations apart, discard intervals shorter than 200 iterations) before declaring that predicted-unstable windows align with spikes. No sensitivity analysis to these thresholds is provided, and the criterion (c*_spike) itself depends on the gradient-direction curvature λ_grad = g⊤Huu g/‖g‖², which is essentially as expensive to compute as the Hessian action itself — practical online monitoring cost is not discussed.
- **Missing baselines.** The paper does not directly compare its wd-off-MLP mitigation against competing spike-mitigation strategies from its own reference list: β2 reduction (Bai et al. 2025), checkpoint-resumption + bad-batch skipping (Chowdhery et al. 2023 / PaLM), or gradient clipping tuning. The mitigation is shown to help, but not shown to dominate alternatives.
- **Scale.** The largest model is a 187M-parameter Transformer pretrained for one epoch on 100B tokens — adequate to demonstrate the mechanism but small relative to current frontier LLMs; generalization to 7B+ scale where sharpness/instability dynamics can differ is untested.
- **Generalization impact.** The paper motivates the analysis partly by the generalization benefits of weight decay, but reports only *training* loss curves for the wd-off-MLP configuration; no test/validation perplexity or downstream evaluation is given, so the practical regularization trade-off is not quantified.
- **Red flags.** None severe. No cherry-picked external benchmarks, no overclaiming of a "solution" — the paper is framed as a mechanistic explanation and is appropriately hedged ("may exhibit loss spikes", "remains incompletely understood").

## Strengths

- Provides a unified, quantitative framework that bridges the Edge-of-Stability (learning-rate criticality) literature with the normalization × weight-decay literature, and is honest about the relationship rather than competing.
- The per-scale-invariant-component decomposition is genuinely useful: it turns a global phenomenon into a per-layer diagnostic, which the module-wise Hessian-eigenvector analysis (Fig. 8) and the wd-off-MLP ablation (Fig. 9) exploit directly.
- Empirical coverage is broad and consistent across five distinct settings (synthetic regression, MNIST FNN, CIFAR-100 ResNet-50, synthetic 3x→x Transformer, 187M LLaMA-style Transformer), with the same mechanism showing up in each.
- The mitigation recipe (don't decay scale-invariant/MLP weights) is simple, actionable, and compatible with existing training stacks.
- The PCA "zero-limit reference point" construction (Fig. 3, Fig. 4b) is a nice intuitive device showing *where* in parameter space weight decay pushes the solution.

## Limitations

- Theorem 5.1's α⁻² lower bound is, mathematically, a refinement of known scale-invariance results; the truly novel theoretical content is concentrated in the per-component boundary translation.
- The spike-boundary (Proposition 5.3) requires gradient-direction curvature, which is costly to compute at scale; the paper does not propose an efficient estimator or monitoring recipe.
- The interval-filtering protocol (30/200 iteration thresholds) is arbitrary and lacks sensitivity analysis.
- Linear/second-order local analysis assumes locally PSD Hessian; behavior *after* boundary crossing (nonlinear dynamics, self-stabilization per Damian et al. 2023) is not treated.
- 187M-parameter / 1-epoch pretraining is small; no results at 1B+ parameters or with longer training that would test whether the mechanism persists where other instabilities (e.g. Adam adaptivity, Bai et al. 2025) dominate.
- No guidance on *which* modules to exclude weight decay from beyond the empirically-observed MLP dominance; attention subblocks are not ablated individually.
- No test-set / downstream evaluation of the wd-off-MLP configuration, so the regularization trade-off is unquantified.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does weight-norm criticality persist at 7B–70B parameter scale, or do Adam-adaptivity spikes (Bai et al. 2025) and data-driven spikes dominate there, masking this mechanism?
- Can c*_spike be approximated cheaply (e.g. via Hutchinson estimators or power iteration on Huu restricted to a single layer) so it becomes a practical online monitor in production training runs?
- How does the analysis interact with RMSNorm (which has no learned scale and is strictly scale-invariant) vs. LayerNorm with affine parameters — does the predicted boundary tighten?
- What is the generalization trade-off of wd-off-MLP / wd-off-scale-invariant-layers on downstream benchmarks — does removing decay from MLPs hurt final perplexity or task accuracy at fixed compute?
- Can the framework be combined with β2-reduction (Bai et al. 2025) and gradient clipping into a unified spike-prevention schedule, and which dominates at a fixed compute budget?
- Does the module-wise curvature attribution extend to other commonly spiking modules (e.g. embedding layers, output projection) and to architectures without MLP dominance (e.g. Mamba / linear-attention models)?
