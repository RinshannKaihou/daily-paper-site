> **一句话总结：** 提出 CM-LRS —— 一个在"工作流产出层"评估 LLM 可靠性的七维度打分框架（事实准确性、证据可追溯、数值一致性、流程完整性、来源纪律、决策有用性、可审计性），并在 DCM/ECM 条款抽取、先例检索、发行人画像、并购可比推理五类资本市场任务上对 Claude Opus 4.7 / GPT-5.5 / Sonnet 4.6 / Llama 3.3 70B 进行打分，发现三家闭源前沿模型在 5 分制上仅差 0.22 分（Sonnet 4.31 / Opus 4.30 / GPT-5.5 4.09），开源基线落后约 1.16 分，差距集中在检索与综合而非单文档抽取，并以"决策有用性"维度区分度最大。

> **TL;DR:** CM-LRS is a workflow-output-layer reliability metric for LLMs in regulated capital-markets deployments, scored 0–5 across seven dimensions (factual accuracy, evidence traceability, numerical consistency, workflow completeness, source discipline, decision usefulness, reviewability). On five capital-markets workflows built from SEC EDGAR filings, a public UK takeover release, and synthetic Nordic-style term sheets, the three frontier closed-source models cluster within 0.22 points on four-judge-averaged CM-LRS (Sonnet 4.6 = 4.31 vs Opus 4.7 = 4.30 vs GPT-5.5 = 4.09), while Llama 3.3 70B trails at 3.15 — a ~0.94–1.16-point gap concentrated on retrieval/synthesis (W2: −2.23 vs Sonnet) rather than extraction (W1: −0.84). Decision Usefulness (D6) is the cleanest dimension-level production-readiness signal, with a 4.0-point cross-model spread on the issuer-profile workflow.

## 核心贡献

- **七维度可靠性框架**：定义了专门面向受监管资本市场工作流的七维评估体系（D1 事实准确性、D2 证据可追溯、D3 数值一致性、D4 流程完整性、D5 来源纪律、D6 决策有用性、D7 可审计性），每维 0–5 分打分并提供加权聚合公式 `CM-LRS = Σ wi·si`。
- **工作流分类法与默认权重**：提出 Extraction / Retrieval / Synthesis / Comparison & Reasoning / Drafting 五类资本市场工作流分类，并为每类给出维度权重默认值，使评估对工作流类型敏感而非一刀切。
- **四裁判跨家族去偏协议**：使用 Sonnet 4.6、GPT-5.5、Haiku 4.5、Gemini 2.5 Pro 四个裁判（横跨三个模型家族、含两个出组裁判）对同一批 104 个输出打分，以控制自评偏置与家族偏置；GPT-5.5 与 Gemini 之间 Spearman ρ = 0.03 是"家族偏置存在"的最强经验信号。
- **全公开、可复现的示范任务集**：五类工作流（DCM 抽取、先例检索、发行人画像、并购可比、ECM 抽取）全部基于 SEC EDGAR、公开 UK 收购公告与虚构 Nordic 合成条款表，无任何专有数据；rubric、prompt、原始输出、打分与"从原始数据重算每个数字"的脚本以 CC BY 4.0 发布。
- **面向治理的部署门槛**：将 CM-LRS 嵌入 NIST AI RMF 的 measure/manage 函数、EU AI Act 第 9/13 条合规证据、ISO/IEC 42001 监控要求，并提出"CM-LRS ≥ 4.0 且无维度 < 3 且 0/1 直接淘汰"的部署就绪复合门槛。

## Key Contributions

- **A seven-dimension workflow-output reliability rubric** (D1 factual accuracy, D2 evidence traceability, D3 numerical consistency, D4 workflow completeness, D5 source discipline, D6 decision usefulness, D7 reviewability/auditability), each scored 0–5 with worked anchors, plus a tunable weighted aggregate `CM-LRS = Σ wi·si ∈ [0,5]`.
- **A capital-markets workflow taxonomy** (Extraction / Retrieval / Synthesis / Comparison & Reasoning / Drafting) with per-class default dimension weights, so evaluation weighting follows the workflow rather than treating all LLM tasks identically.
- **A four-judge, three-family cross-validation protocol** (Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.5 in-panel; Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro out-of-panel) over 104 outputs to control self- and family-bias — the weakest pair GPT-5.5↔Gemini ρ = 0.03 is the strongest empirical evidence that family-bias matters and single-judge protocols would not surface it.
- **Five fully-public, reproducible demonstration workflows** (DCM extraction, precedent retrieval, issuer profile, M&A transaction-comparable reasoning, ECM extraction) built on SEC EDGAR filings, the public Darktrace UK takeover IR release, and synthetic Nordic-style supplements; rubrics, prompts, raw outputs, scores, and a deterministic verification script released under CC BY 4.0.
- **A governance-aligned deployment gate**: CM-LRS is positioned inside the NIST AI RMF measure/manage functions, as evidence under EU AI Act Articles 9 and 13, and within an ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system, with a composite deployment threshold of CM-LRS ≥ 4.0, no dimension < 3, and any score of 0 or 1 disqualifying.

## Methodology

CM-LRS evaluates an LLM output at the *workflow-output layer* (not the QA-pair layer). For each (workflow × model) cell, the output is generated once at temperature 0 (or the lowest deterministic setting) and then scored against the seven-dimension rubric by an LLM-as-judge protocol (Zheng et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023) with the bias controls of Wang et al. 2023.

- **Panel**: Claude Opus 4.7 (frontier, via Radical gateway), GPT-5.5 (frontier, via Codex CLI `codex exec` at reasoning effort high), Claude Sonnet 4.6 (production workhorse), Llama 3.3 70B Instruct (open-weights, via Groq). Gemini is deliberately a judge but not a panel member.
- **Input normalisation**: every document is preprocessed to a fixed 6,000-token budget of cleaned text so all four models receive identical inputs regardless of native context-window size. This is the single biggest external-validity threat the paper itself flags (Section 10, limitation 5): it biases toward cover-page/front-matter content and does not exercise buried covenants 90 pages deep.
- **Judges**: Sonnet 4.6 (primary, also in-panel), GPT-5.5 (in-panel, OpenAI family), Haiku 4.5 (out-of-panel, Anthropic family), Gemini 2.5 Pro (out-of-panel, Google family — fully external). All four use identical prompt and rubric, score the same 104 outputs, and write to parallel score files.
- **Aggregate**: equal-weighted mean across the seven dimensions per cell (the headline choice, justified because the frontier-vs-open-weights gap is large enough that no plausible weight vector inverts it). Default workflow-class weights and five calibration methods (AHP, BWM, PCA, entropy, revealed-preference regression) are offered for deployment-specific use.
- **Scoring instance record**: workflow id, input documents (with public URLs or synthetic-document ids), prompt, model version, raw output, per-dimension scores with one-sentence justifications, aggregate, and timestamp.
- **Reproducibility**: a deterministic verification script recomputes every numeric claim in the paper from the raw scoring data; corpus is frozen-snapshotted at publication.

The guiding principle is "predict with the LLM, calculate with code, decide with the human" — D3 (numerical consistency) and D7 (reviewability) are first-class precisely because numbers in regulated output should trace to deterministic computation, not token prediction.

## Results & Highlights

- **Frontier closed-source models cluster within a 0.22-point band** on four-judge-averaged CM-LRS (5-point scale): Claude Sonnet 4.6 = 4.31, Claude Opus 4.7 = 4.30, GPT-5.5 = 4.09. The in-cluster first place is judge-dependent (Sonnet under Sonnet/Haiku judges, GPT-5.5 under self-judge, Opus under Gemini-judge) — evidence the within-cluster ranking should not be over-read.
- **Open-weights baseline (Llama 3.3 70B) trails at 3.15**, a 0.94–1.16-point gap to the frontier cluster, and is ranked last by all four judges spanning three model families. Under the primary judge the gap is 1.40 points (Llama 3.24 vs Sonnet 4.64).
- **The open-weights gap is concentrated on retrieval and synthesis, not extraction**: under the primary judge (Table 5), W2 precedent retrieval shows a 2.23-point gap (Llama 2.51 vs Sonnet 4.74), W3 issuer profile shows a 2.15-point gap (Llama 2.33 vs GPT-5.5 4.48), but W1 single-document DCM extraction shrinks to a 0.84-point gap (Llama 4.11 vs Sonnet 4.95).
- **Decision Usefulness (D6) is the cleanest production-readiness signal**: on the W3 issuer-profile workflow alone, D6 ranges from Llama = 1.00, Opus = 2.67, Sonnet = 2.67 up to GPT-5.5 = 5.00 — a 4.0-point cross-model spread, the widest of any dimension in any cell — while still sitting in the top tier of inter-judge agreement (mean Pearson r = 0.52, within 0.02 of D1/D5 at 0.54).
- **Inter-judge agreement (n=104 outputs)**: Sonnet↔Haiku ρ = 0.94 (same family, very strong), Sonnet↔GPT-5.5 ρ = 0.66 (cross-family, moderate-strong), GPT-5.5↔Gemini ρ = 0.03 (effectively independent rankings — the strongest single empirical signal that family-bias matters).
- **Cost/latency trade-off (Table 4)**: Opus 4.7 ~$0.20–0.30/call at 18s; GPT-5.5 high-reasoning ~$0.02–0.04/call at 84s; Sonnet 4.6 ~$0.04–0.06/call at 21s; Llama 3.3 70B ~$0.004–0.008/call at 10s. Sonnet is highlighted as the cost/latency/CM-LRS sweet spot; GPT-5.5 is materially slower due to high reasoning effort.
- **Worked example (Appendix C)**: on the HCA 2024 $3.0bn senior-notes prospectus, Sonnet 4.6 scored CM-LRS = 4.86 = (5+5+5+5+4+5+5)/7, with the judge noting all figures reconcile ($750M + $1,250M + $1,000M = $3,000M; issue prices 101.864% / 99.854% / 99.935% match the pricing table exactly).

## Authors & Credibility

- **Sole author: Prerit Ahuja**, independent researcher, self-described director-level practitioner in investment banking focused on AI, data, and decision systems; London-based with practice across UK, Nordic, and US capital markets. ORCID provided (0009-0004-0147-4192) and a personal site. The author explicitly states his positionality (Section 5.5) and treats practitioner perspective as "load-bearing for the rubric design."
- **Credibility strengths**: a coherent, internally consistent framework with a genuine attempt at bias control (four judges, three families), public reproducibility commitments (CC BY 4.0 release of prompts, rubrics, raw outputs, scoring, and a verification script), explicit positionality statement, and an unusually candid limitations section (12 numbered limitations including the single biggest external-validity threat).
- **Credibility caveats**: single author with no institutional affiliation, no human-rater data (all judging is automated LLM-as-judge, calibrated only against other LLMs), and no peer review indicated (May 2026 arXiv preprint). The findings about specific model versions (Opus 4.7, GPT-5.5, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Llama 3.3 70B) are dated to "May 2026 submission" and the author flags that provider-side rebranding may diverge.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel in *layer* rather than *concept*. The seven dimensions individually are familiar (factual accuracy, faithfulness, hallucination, completeness all appear in FActScore, RAGAS, TrustLLM, Mohsin 2025). The contribution is operating at the workflow-output layer rather than the QA-pair or RAG-component layer, and spanning DCM/ECM/retrieval/synthesis/M&A-comparable under one rubric. The four-judge, three-family cross-validation protocol is a real methodological step beyond single-judge finance-LLM studies.
- **Rigor**: Mixed. The four-judge protocol and explicit bias acknowledgment (including the embarrassing GPT-5.5↔Gemini ρ = 0.03) are commendable. But every numeric score derives from LLM judges, with zero human-rater validation — and the primary judge (Sonnet 4.6) is itself a panel member. The author flags this honestly as the priority extension, but it remains a structural weakness for any deployment-readiness claim.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong on paper. CC BY 4.0 release of prompts, rubrics, raw outputs, scores, a deterministic verification script, and frozen-snapshotted public corpus is exactly the right commitment. The caveat is that "production APIs at temperature 0" are not bit-reproducible across time — Opus 4.7, GPT-5.5, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5 are moving targets and the author's own footnote acknowledges provider-side rebranding may diverge post-submission.
- **Significance**: Practically significant for the narrow audience of regulated capital-markets buyers choosing among frontier models. The headline finding ("the three frontier closed-source models are statistically indistinguishable; choose on cost/latency/workflow-fit, not headline reliability") is useful and counter to vendor marketing. Broader scientific significance is limited by the absence of human ground truth.
- **Red flags and overclaims**:
  - **6,000-token truncation bias** (Section 10, limitation 5): every document is truncated to 6,000 cleaned tokens, biasing all per-cell scores toward cover-page/front-matter content. The paper itself calls this "the single biggest threat to external validity of the per-cell scores." A "buried covenant 90 pages in" failure mode — arguably the highest-stakes failure mode in real prospectus review — is *not exercised at all* by this evaluation. This materially weakens any deployment-readiness claim for long-document workflows.
  - **n is thin per cell**: W2 has 5 queries, W3 has 5 issuers, W4 has 1 target deal with a 20-deal comparable universe. Per-cell means like "Llama W3 = 2.33" rest on 5 documents; the 4.0-point D6 spread on W3 rests on 5 issuer outputs × 4 models. The paper reports no confidence intervals, no significance tests, and no variance estimates.
  - **Depth heterogeneity in W4** (limitation 4): two of twenty comparable cells (Adobe/Figma, Thoma Bravo/Darktrace) are announcement-class HTML, not full merger proxies, and are scored "without penalising" missing content — a scoring leniency that is acknowledged but not quantified.
  - **Self/family-bias is only partially controlled**: even with four judges, the headline "0.22-point band" among frontier models is well within the documented judge-strictness variation (GPT-5.5 grades 0.3–1.1 points stricter than Sonnet; Gemini grades GPT-5.5 at 3.38 vs 4.19–4.44 by others). The claim that the three frontier models are "statistically indistinguishable" is asserted, not tested.
  - **Equal weighting is a convenience**: the author concedes default weights are "practitioner-informed but not empirically derived" and sketches five calibration methods but uses none of them.
  - **Cost numbers in Table 4 are "indicative ranges as at submission"** for a 5K-in/2K-out exchange — useful as order-of-magnitude only.

## Strengths

- Targets a real, repeatedly-observed practitioner failure mode (surface-fluent output that fails reviewer gates on traceability, reconciliation, completeness) that QA-pair benchmarks structurally cannot surface.
- The seven-dimension rubric is intuitive, the 0–5 anchors in Appendix A are concrete and scorable without specialist training, and the per-class default weights make the framework immediately deployable.
- The four-judge, three-family protocol with explicit reporting of the weakest pair (GPT-5.5↔Gemini ρ = 0.03) is a model of honest bias reporting.
- Fully public artefacts (SEC EDGAR URLs, synthetic Nordic supplements, prompts, raw outputs, scores, verification script) under CC BY 4.0 — the reproducibility commitment is exemplary for a single-author preprint.
- The deployment-readiness composite gate (CM-LRS ≥ 4.0, no dimension < 3, any 0/1 disqualifies) is a sensible guard against aggregate-score masking of a single critical-dimension failure.
- Positionality statement (Section 5.5) and 12-item limitations section are candid and load-bearing for the rubric's scope claims.

## Limitations

- **No human-rater validation**: every score is LLM-judge-derived; inter-rater reliability against practising bankers/compliance reviewers is the priority extension and is not done.
- **Document truncation to 6,000 tokens** biases scores toward front-matter content and does not exercise buried-covenant failure modes — the highest-stakes real-world failure class for long prospectuses.
- **Tiny per-cell n**: W2 = 5 queries, W3 = 5 issuers, W4 = 1 target deal; no confidence intervals or significance tests reported, yet "statistically indistinguishable" is asserted for the frontier cluster.
- **Single-turn, non-agentic**: no tool-use, no retrieval-tool calls, no code-execution for numerical computation — real deployments increasingly use agentic patterns the harness does not exercise.
- **Narrow model panel**: no Gemini in-panel, no Mistral/Qwen/DeepSeek; only one open-weights model.
- **Drafting workflows absent**: pitch evidence, compliance memos — a stated workflow class — are not demonstrated.
- **Failure-cost asymmetry unmodelled**: equal-weighted aggregate treats a wrong coupon and an omitted ESG paragraph as commensurate; calibration against revealed-preference data is sketched but not done.
- **Static, dated benchmarks**: model versions and frozen corpus are pinned to May 2026; the diagnostic value will decay as models advance.
- **Single author, no institutional affiliation, no peer review** — the work's credibility rests entirely on the public artefacts and the reader's trust in the practitioner's positionality.

## Relevance

对评估 LLM 在金融/受监管场景下"可上线性（bankability）"的研究方向高度相关：CM-LRS 提供了一个可直接借鉴的"工作流产出层"七维评估模板，特别适合希望为 RAG/金融抽取/合规工作流建立部署门槛、或评估 LLM-as-judge 跨家族偏置的实践者；其四裁判去偏协议对任何依赖 LLM 自动评估的下游任务都有方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do CM-LRS scores change when documents are *not* truncated to 6,000 tokens — i.e. when the buried-covenant-90-pages-deep failure mode is actually exercised via chunking or full-document retrieval?
- What is the inter-rater reliability between the four LLM judges and a panel of practising bankers/compliance reviewers on the same 104 outputs? Does D6 retain its "cleanest separator" status under human scoring?
- Does the "frontier cluster within 0.22 points" finding survive a proper significance test (paired bootstrap or similar across the 104 outputs), given that judge-strictness variation alone (0.3–1.1 points) exceeds the cluster band?
- How does CM-LRS behave under agentic/tool-use deployments (model calls a search tool, a code interpreter for numerical computation, then composes) — does D3 numerical consistency collapse toward 5 when arithmetic is offloaded to code, as the "predict with LLM, calculate with code" principle predicts?
- Can the D6 Decision Usefulness dimension be operationalised into a binary reviewer accept/reject classifier via revealed-preference regression on real operational data, turning CM-LRS from a score into a calibrated probability of admissibility?
- Does the framework generalise beyond capital markets to adjacent regulated domains (credit risk, asset management, insurance underwriting) without domain-specific rubric re-tuning?
