C-PTQ: Fisher-weighted Channel-wise Sensitivity for Post-training Quantization of MLLMs↗

AuthorsJiameng Li · Han Zhou · Matthew B. Blaschko Affil.KU Leuven · Tiangong University
Key finding

Existing MLLM quantizers measure sensitivity on one axis (modality / token) but search scaling on another (channel). C-PTQ closes that gap with a diagonal-Fisher-weighted channel-wise MSE — derived from the second-order Taylor expansion of the task loss — hitting SOTA without LoRA.

01 / METHODOLOGY

From task-loss perturbation to a Fisher-weighted residual

Prior CWS methods (MBQ, QIG, MASQuant) measure sensitivity along modality/token axes while the scaling search itself lives on the channel axis — a structural mismatch.

Starting from the second-order Taylor expansion of the SFT loss and dropping the first-order term under the OBD local-optimum assumption, the Hessian is approximated by the empirical Fisher Information Matrix F = E[gᵢgᵢ⊤] — which encodes global task sensitivity, unlike GPTQ's activation-derived XX⊤.

ΔL ≈ ½ · ΔY⊤ · H · ΔY   →   ½ Σᵢ ‖ √f ⊙ Δyᵢ ‖₂²

A diagonal simplification turns dense O(N²) Fisher into per-channel scalar weights fⱼ. Mean-normalization fixes the magnitude gap between gradient² and MSE — collapsing the objective to an element-wise weighted residual.

min_s ‖ √f · ( WX − Q(W·s)·Q(s⁻¹·X) ) ‖₂²

Calibration. 128 COCO-caption image–text pairs (ShareGPT4V subset), fixed seed 42, shared across all baselines.

1
Compute diagonal Fisher weights
Backprop the SFT loss to each linear-layer output on the calibration set; take per-channel fⱼ = E[gᵢⱼ²], then mean-normalize.
2
Initialize channel-wise scales
From activation magnitudes, s = x₀^α, …, x_Nc^α — the AWQ-style parameterization, now Fisher-aware.
3
40-step grid search over α
Minimize the Fisher-weighted MSE. Pure CWS — no LoRA, no low-rank matrices, no mixed bit-widths at inference.
✓
Deployable on stock INT kernels
Diagonal Fisher matches dense Fisher (74.3 vs 73.5) at ~5× the speed (~11 min vs ~50 min).

— avg over 8 benchmarks (MMMU, MMBench, MMStar, etc.). FP16 baselines: Qwen2.5VL-7B 81.6, 32B 77.7, InternVL2-8B 77.0. Aggressive activation quant (W4A6) is where C-PTQ's lead widens most.

Strengths＋
  • Theoretically grounded. Cleanly derives the Fisher-weighted MSE from the second-order Taylor expansion of the task loss — closing the activation-error vs task-loss gap AWQ/GPTQ leave open.
  • Practical efficiency. Diagonal Fisher matches dense Fisher at ~5× the speed; matches MBQ's quant time while beating QIG's 32-step integrated gradients by ~4×.
  • Hardware-friendly. Pure CWS with grid search — no low-rank matrices, no mixed bit-widths at inference; runs on stock INT kernels.
  • Rigorously stress-tested. Calibration subsets, sizes, modalities all ablated; transfers cleanly to pure LLMs and 2B–72B scales.
  • Honest reporting. Explicitly acknowledges AWQ stays competitive in easy settings, W4A4 fails, and the vision encoder remains FP16.
Limitations!
  • Weight-only gains within noise. 0.1 pt over MBQ on Qwen2.5VL-7B W3A16 — SOTA claim rests mainly on aggressive W4A6 and the 32B model.
  • Partial memory savings. Vision encoder and projection stay FP16; only LLM decoder layers benefit.
  • No extreme W4A4. Left as future work requiring LoRA-like compensation — undercutting the “no auxiliary modules” claim there.
  • Baselines are model-dependent. SQ vs MBQ swap ranking across families; single-benchmark SOTA claims should be read cautiously.
  • Under-specified calibration loss. Fisher requires backprop on an SFT loss; whether captions come from the model or ground-truth COCO is not crisp.
  • No end-to-end latency benchmark vs LoRA-based competitors — only quantization-time cost.
03 / OPEN QUESTIONS

Where this leads next

Q1 · OUTLIER COMPENSATION
Could diagonal Fisher weights merge with a low-rank head only for the few highest-gradient outlier channels (e.g. the ~3200-th in Qwen2.5VL-7B), recovering W4A4 without full LoRA cost?
Q2 · THEORETICAL PUZZLE
Diagonal Fisher beats dense (74.3 vs 73.5) — but dense should be a strict superset of information. Noise artefact? Overfitting on 128 samples? Or are off-diagonal terms actively harmful?
Q3 · LOSS FORMULATION
How does C-PTQ behave when Fisher uses a multimodal instruction-tuning loss with interleaved image–text tokens, rather than plain captioning cross-entropy?
Q4 · UNIFIED PIPELINE
Can channel-wise Fisher weights double as pruning saliency (à la Supernode) for a unified quantize-then-prune MLLM pipeline? Does the signal survive grouped-quantization granularity?
Q5 · VISION SIDE
Applying C-PTQ to the ViT too (with image-reconstruction Fisher) — would it close more of the memory gap, or does vision sensitivity differ structurally from the LLM decoder's?
Q6 · GPTQ CONTRADICTION
GPTQ scores worse than even RTN on Qwen2.5VL-7B (71.5 vs 75.4). Does this generalize, or expose a pathology specific to activation-derived Hessians on MLLMs?
