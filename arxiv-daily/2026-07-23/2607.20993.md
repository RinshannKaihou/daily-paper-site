> **一句话总结：** 提出无需训练的"概念通道探针"(CCP)，发现冻结的 3D CT 视觉编码器将每种放射学发现压缩在约 10 个稀疏通道中；通过因果消融证明这些通道对各自发现是必要的，配合语料模板还能在 CT-RATE 上以 22× 更低延迟超越 CT-CHAT 的报告生成效果。

> **TL;DR:** This interpretability paper probes frozen 3D CT vision-language encoders (Pillar-0 chest, Merlin abdomen) and shows each radiological finding lives in a sparse set of ~10 embedding channels that match full-feature AUROC (CCP-10 = 0.798 vs. full-D 0.793 on CT-RATE) and are causally necessary (on-target ablation drop 0.0060 vs. 0.00003 off-target, ~20×). A training-free probe + corpus-derived template beats the trained CT-CHAT report generator on clinical F1 (0.549 vs. 0.184) and BLEU-1 (0.483 vs. 0.373) at ~22× lower latency, and the sparse-channel recipe replicates across an architecturally unrelated abdominal VLM.

## 核心贡献

- **发现稀疏性结构**：在冻结的 Pillar-0 (D=1152) 上证明每种放射学发现由约 10 个通道编码，top-10 的 AUROC (0.798) 与全部 1152 维 (0.793) 持平，远超零样本文本提示 (0.682)；相比全特征用约 100× 更少的坐标。
- **因果可定位性**：采用因果中介分析式消融，将某个发现的 top-K 通道置零后，该发现的 AUROC 下降 (0.0060) 约为其他发现下降 (0.00003) 的 20×，证明选择性必要而非泛共享信号；部分 off-diagonal 高值对应临床相关发现（如动脉与冠脉钙化）。
- **跨主干/跨解剖迁移**：同一套 CCP 配方 (K=10) 在腹部 Merlin (30 发现) 上达到 AUROC 0.839、F1 0.832，超过 Merlin 零样本 (0.647) 和有监督 (0.641)；并提出三种迁移体制（跨机构、跨解剖/主干、解剖错配），指明"排名过程跨主干迁移，但编码器解剖需对齐"。
- **训练无关且可解释的报告生成**：把检测 (CCP) 与语言化 (语料模板) 解耦，在 CT-RATE 上同时赢得临床 F1 (0.549) 与 BLEU-1 (0.483)，超越训练过的 CT-CHAT (0.184 / 0.373)，且每卷延迟 0.24 s vs. 5.5 s（约 23× 更快）。
- **可复现、轻量的审计工具**：整套探针只在校准集上拟合闭式 mean-difference 方向与阈值，推理时冻结，为部署的医学基础模型提供"哪里编码了什么"的可审计通道级解释。

## Key Contributions

- **Sparsity of clinical findings**: Each radiological finding is encoded by ~10 channels in a frozen Pillar-0 encoder (D=1152); top-10 AUROC (0.798) matches the full 1152-dim embedding (0.793) and beats zero-shot text prompting (0.682), using ~100× fewer coordinates.
- **Causal localization, not just correlation**: Following causal-mediation and ROME-style intervention, zeroing a finding's top-K channels drops its own AUROC by 0.0060 vs. only 0.00003 for off-target findings (~20× selectivity), with elevated off-diagonal entries corresponding to clinically related pathologies (e.g., arterial/coronary calcification).
- **Backbone- and anatomy-portable**: The identical recipe (K=10) reproduces on an architecturally unrelated abdominal VLM (Merlin, D=512), reaching AUROC 0.839 / F1 0.832 versus Merlin zero-shot 0.647 and supervised 0.641, and a three-regime transfer study (cross-institution, cross-anatomy/backbone, anatomy mismatch) clarifies when the probe transfers and when it breaks.
- **Training-free report generation that beats a trained VLM**: Decoupling detection (CCP) from verbalization (a deterministic corpus template), the pipeline wins on both clinical efficacy and NLG surface metrics on CT-RATE (F1 0.549 vs. CT-CHAT's 0.184; BLEU-1 0.483 vs. 0.373) at ~22× lower latency.
- **A practical auditing recipe**: Channel ranking (prevalence-invariant per-coordinate AUROC), closed-form mean-difference direction, calibrated thresholds, and causal ablation are all fit on a small calibration set (600 volumes) and frozen at inference — providing channel-level explanations of deployed medical foundation models without retraining.

## Methodology

The paper takes a deliberately **training-free, probe-based** approach to interpretability. No weights are updated; the frozen vision encoder is treated as a black box that produces a cached embedding `x_i ∈ R^D`.

**Concept Channel Probe (CCP-K).** For each finding `c` in a multi-label task (C findings), the method:
1. Ranks every channel coordinate `j` by a selectivity score `Ã_{c,j} = A_{c,j} − 0.5`, where `A_{c,j}` is the pairwise win rate of channel `j` over calibration positives vs. negatives — equivalent to a per-channel AUROC centered so chance = 0. This is prevalence-invariant, which matters because CT-RATE/RadChest-CT findings span ~7%–45% prevalence.
2. Forms the top-K set `S^c_K` and fits a closed-form **mean-difference direction** `w_c` (à la representation engineering, Zou et al. 2023): `w_c = (μ_{S^c_K}^P − μ_{S^c_K}^N) / ||...||`. This is robust to class imbalance unlike least-squares or pooled fits.
3. Computes `score_c(i) = x_{i,S^c_K} · w_c` and applies a per-finding threshold `τ_c` calibrated on a held-out split (F1-maximizing for reports; ROC top-left operating point for classification, matching CT-CLIP's protocol).
4. K is selected on a disjoint holdout as the smallest K within 0.005 AUROC of full-D, yielding `K* = 10` for Pillar-0; the same K=10 is reused for Merlin without separate tuning. All of `{S^c_K, w_c, τ_c}` are fit on calibration only, so the test volume never enters the means — no data leakage.

**Causal ablation.** To test necessity, the top-K dev-ranked channels `S^c_abl = S^c_K` of a source finding are zeroed (`x'_{i,j} = 0`) and the drop matrix `Δ_{c→c'}` recorded; a selectivity index `s_el` averages the gap between on-target and off-target drops. Large `s_el` means a finding's channels are necessary for its own score but not for others'.

**Training-free report generation.** Detections `d_i` are verbalized deterministically: from training reports (calibration excluded) they extract a most-frequent "normal chest" skeleton `T0` and the most common positive sentence `u_c` per finding. At test time they split/tag/negate `T0`, drop sentences for absent findings, and append `u_c` for present ones — the verbalizer can never hallucinate a finding outside `d_i`.

**Backbones & data.** Pillar-0 (D=1152, chest CT) and Merlin (D=512, abdominal CT, Clinical-Longformer text head). Datasets: **CT-RATE** (18 findings, 1564 test volumes, 600-volume calibration), **RadChest-CT** (3630 external volumes, 84 labels mapped to the 18-finding ontology), and **Merlin Abdominal CT** (30 findings). Findings with <5 positive calibration volumes are abstained. Report metrics: RadBERT-CT micro/macro-F1, BLEU-1, ROUGE-L, RadGraph-F1, RadEval; uncertainty via paired bootstrap (B=2000).

## Results & Highlights

- **Sparsity recovery (CT-RATE test, Pillar-0, n=1564)**: Mean test AUROC climbs from top-1 = 0.752, to **top-10 = 0.798**, saturates at top-50 = 0.804, and full-D (1152) = 0.793. So ~10 channels match full-embedding AUROC while using ~100× fewer coordinates, and exceed zero-shot text prompting (0.682). On individual findings, top-10 even exceeds full-D (e.g., lung nodule 0.734 vs. 0.683; atelectasis 0.746 vs. 0.697) by dropping noisy dimensions.
- **Multi-abnormality classification (Table 1, CT-RATE test)**: Training-free **CCP-10 reaches AUROC 0.798 / weighted-F1 0.790**, approaching supervised Pillar-0 ClassFine (0.833 / 0.800, trained on ~39k volumes) and well above Pillar-0 zero-shot (0.682 / 0.693) and CT-CLIP zero-shot (0.731 / 0.707). On the external **RadChest-CT (n=3630)**, CCP-10 stays the best training-free method (AUROC 0.686, F1 0.622), retaining ~86% of in-domain AUROC.
- **Causal selectivity (CT-RATE, Pillar-0)**: Mean on-target AUROC drop after channel zeroing = **0.0060 vs. 0.00003 off-target (~20×)**, establishing that each finding's ~10 channels are selectively necessary for its own score; off-diagonal elevations align with clinically related pathologies (arterial/coronary calcification; emphysema with atelectasis/lung opacity).
- **Report generation (Table 2, CT-RATE test, n=1564)**: CCP-10 + corpus template achieves **RadBERT-CT micro-F1 0.549 / macro-F1 0.492, BLEU-1 0.483, ROUGE-L 0.379, latency 0.24 s/vol**, versus the trained **CT-CHAT at F1 0.184, BLEU-1 0.373, ROUGE-L 0.326, latency 5.5 s/vol** — i.e., +0.365 F1 and ~23× lower latency. Retrieve-then-edit matches clinical F1 (0.540) but drops BLEU (0.439); structured template and a frozen Qwen3-VL-8B LLM collapse on prose (BLEU ≤ 0.064 / ~0.01).
- **Cross-backbone (Table 4, Merlin abdominal, 30 findings)**: CCP-10 reaches **AUROC 0.839 / F1 0.832**, beating Merlin zero-shot (0.647) and supervised (0.641) F1. For abdominal report generation it improves BLEU-1 (0.214) and BERTScore (0.677) over Merlin+RadLlama (0.102 / 0.588), but trails on RadGraph-F1 (0.262 vs. 0.293).
- **Transfer regimes (Table 4)**: (A) Cross-institution RadChest→CT-RATE test = AUROC 0.726 / F1 0.462; CT-RATE→RadChest = 0.686 / 0.622 (retains ~82% of in-domain report F1). (C) Anatomy mismatch (abdominal Merlin on chest labels) stays in-domain (0.733) but lags chest-native Pillar-0 (0.798) and degrades under hospital shift (0.605 vs. 0.686), showing the ranking procedure transfers across backbones but the encoder anatomy must align.
- **Statistical rigor**: Pairwise bootstrap (B=2000, shared resampling indices) confirms all verbalizer and classification gaps with p < 0.001; 95% CIs reported.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine. Applying network dissection (Bau et al. 2017) + representation engineering (Zou et al. 2023) + causal mediation (Michel et al. 2019; ROME, Meng et al. 2022) to *frozen 3D medical VLMs* is fresh; the combination of sparsity + causal selectivity + a template verbalizer gives a coherent "where is the finding" story that prior 3D medical VLM work (CT-CHAT, Merlin, Pillar-0) does not address.
- **Rigor**: Strong on the interpretability claims. K=10 is selected on a disjoint holdout (not test), scoring directions are fit on calibration only with explicit no-leakage argument, the per-channel selectivity is prevalence-invariant, and uncertainty is handled with paired bootstrap (B=2000) and 95% CIs. The causal-ablation design (zeroing dev-ranked top-K and reading the off-diagonal) is the right way to move from correlation to necessity.
- **Weakness — the headline CT-CHAT comparison**: The "F1 0.549 vs. 0.184" result is eye-catching but should be read carefully. The CT-CHAT numbers are **extracted from Hamamci et al. (2026), not re-run** — the authors explicitly state "the public [CT-CLIP] checkpoint is not available," and the CT-CHAT row is cited. CT-CHAT's 0.184 RadBERT-CT micro-F1 is a known degenerate mode (it drifts toward normal wording and misses abnormalities, which the paper itself notes), so the delta partly reflects CT-CHAT's weakness under the labeler rather than CCP's superiority as a generator. This is a fair *detection-grounded* comparison (CCP is fed the same GT-derived labels), but it is not a like-for-like generator bake-off.
- **Weakness — latency claim**: The "~22×/23× lower latency" is structurally trivial — comparing a hash/template lookup to an autoregressive LLM. It is a consequence of the method choice, not a research feat, and overstates efficiency as a contribution.
- **Weakness — report fluency / entity richness**: On abdominal CT, CCP+template trails Merlin+RadLlama on RadGraph-F1 (0.262 vs. 0.293); the deterministic template cannot emit variable prose or rich entity descriptions. The paper's own sanity check — "unrelated reports score BLEU-1 0.385 against each other" — is honest about BLEU reflecting boilerplate, which somewhat undercuts BLEU as evidence for report *correctness*.
- **Reproducibility**: Good in principle (public datasets, standard metrics, K fixed before evaluation, small calibration sets), but the CT-CHAT/CT-CLIP baselines are not reproduced — they are cited from the original papers — which limits independent verification of the most marketable result.
- **Red flags**: None fatal. No evidence of test-set tuning; prevalence handling and the abstention floor for rare findings (≥5 positives) are disclosed. The main caveat is overclaiming risk in the report-generation framing; the interpretability framing stands on its own merits.

## Strengths

- Clean, principled interpretability methodology that fuses network dissection, representation engineering, and causal mediation into one coherent pipeline tailored to frozen 3D CT encoders.
- The sparsity finding is crisp and well-supported: ~10 channels recover full-D AUROC, with a per-finding table (Table 5) listing exact channel IDs and AUROCs.
- Causal ablation is the right experiment and is executed well — it converts a correlational probe into a necessity claim, which is rare in medical-VLM interpretability.
- Strong generalization story: three explicit transfer regimes (cross-institution, cross-backbone/anatomy, anatomy mismatch) honestly delineate where the probe transfers and where it fails.
- Training-free and lightweight — a 600-volume calibration set and K=10 channels make the recipe cheap to deploy and audit repeatedly.
- Honest about its own metrics (e.g., the BLEU boilerplate sanity check, the RadGraph-F1 deficit vs. RadLlama).

## Limitations

- Only two backbones evaluated (Pillar-0 chest, Merlin abdomen); whether the sparse-channel phenomenon holds for other 3D medical VLMs or modalities (MRI, X-ray) is untested.
- Report generation is confined to deterministic corpus templates — it cannot produce fluent, variable, or entity-rich prose; RadGraph-F1 lags trained generators on abdomen.
- Rare findings have high-variance channel estimates (few positive calibration volumes); the authors mitigate by abstention and prevalence-invariant metrics, but rare-pathology circuits remain under-powered.
- No human reader study — every evaluation is against automated labelers (RadBERT-CT, RadGraph, RadEval) or radiologist-annotated GT; clinical utility is not directly assessed.
- The CT-CHAT and CT-CLIP baselines are cited rather than reproduced, so the most dramatic delta rests on published numbers under a labeler known to disadvantage CT-CHAT.
- The latency advantage is an artifact of choosing a template verbalizer over an autoregressive LLM, not an efficiency innovation.

## Relevance

对关注大模型可解释性、探针分析与医学影像基础模型的研究方向高度相关：它示范了如何用无需训练的稀疏通道探针 + 因果消融去审计冻结的 3D 医学 VLM，既能定位"信息编码在哪里"，又能产出可用的检测与报告。其探针-读出范式对通用的视觉-语言模型机理可解释性、以及多智能体流水线中的轻量感知模块设计同样有借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the ~10-channel sparsity pattern hold for other 3D medical VLMs (e.g., CT-CHAT's own encoder, CT-FM, RadFM) and other modalities (brain MRI, X-ray), or is it specific to contrastive chest/abdomen CT pretraining?
- How stable are the identified channel circuits under finer-grained pathology labels (e.g., subtypes of consolidation, nodule morphology) and under longitudinal follow-up of the same patient?
- Could CCP channel circuits be used for *steering* (amplifying or suppressing a finding) rather than just detection/ablation, analogous to representation-engineering control vectors?
- What happens when CCP detections are fed into a modern autoregressive report LLM as structured grounding — does the detection/verbalization decoupling still beat end-to-end generation, and does it reduce hallucination?
- Is the per-finding selectivity gap (0.0060 vs. 0.00003) stable across calibration-set sizes and seeds, and can a theoretical account (e.g., from superposition/polysemanticity theory) predict the K≈10 regime?
- Can channel-level explanations be validated against radiologist annotations of *where* in the volume a finding appears, bridging feature-level interpretability with spatial localization?
