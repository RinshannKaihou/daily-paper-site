> **一句话总结：** V-DEAL 提出一个行为-理解-表征三层诊断框架，揭示 Video LLM 在「有害视频+良性查询」设置下攻击成功率反而高于「有害视频+有害查询」（48.33% vs 35.84%），且该漏洞并非源于视觉理解不足，而是已识别的有害视觉证据未能可靠转化为拒绝行为；作者提出的免训练 prompt 注入干预将平均 ASR 降至 0.80%。

> **TL;DR:** On three video-safety benchmarks (Video-SafetyBench, VA-SafetyBench, Omni-SafetyBench) covering 5,020 samples and six Video LLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVideo2.5-8B, InternVL3.5-8B, MiniCPM-o-4.5, MiniCPM-V-4.5), the authors show that pairing a harmful video with a topically-aligned benign query (VH-BQ) yields a higher attack success rate (avg 48.33%) than pairing it with an explicitly harmful query (avg 35.84%), even though the models recognize harmful video content with >81% structured-label accuracy. A training-free prompt-injection intervention that forces the model to summarize the video's harmful visual evidence before deciding reduces average ASR from 48.33% to 0.80% (zero-shot) / 0.09% (8-shot), matching the prior fine-tuning-based SEA baseline (0.34% SFT, 3.56% DPO) on the matched VA-SafetyBench setting.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 V-DEAL 三层诊断框架**：首次从行为、理解、表征三个层级联合分析 Video LLM 的「视频安全去校准」现象，将 4 种攻击条件（T-HQ / T-BQ / VH-HQ / VH-BQ）与视觉理解质量、内部拒绝方向联系起来。
- **揭示反直觉的 VH-BQ 漏洞**：有害视频配上语义相关但良性的查询，比配上显式有害查询的攻击成功率更高（平均 48.33% vs 35.84%），而拒绝率从 53.05% 跌至 14.22%，说明漏洞并非源于视觉感知失败。
- **表征层面的因果证据**：通过从外部安全数据（XSTest、MM-SafetyBench）学习层级化「拒绝方向」并投影样本隐状态，发现视觉理解激活的拒绝倾向弱于文本理解——在 5/6 模型中 VH-BQ 的拒绝得分低于 T-HQ。
- **免训练 prompt 注入重对齐方法**：强制模型在安全决策前显式进行「视觉证据文本化」推理，配合上下文示例，无需任何参数更新即可显著降低 ASR。
- **跨基准统一评测**：在 Video-SafetyBench、VA-SafetyBench 视频子集、Omni-SafetyBench 单模态视频子集共 5,020 个样本上对六大开源 Video LLM 进行统一对比。

## Key Contributions

- A three-stage diagnostic framework (V-DEAL) that jointly analyzes behaviour, understanding, and internal representation to explain *why* harmful-video + benign-query attacks succeed, rather than merely showing that they succeed.
- Empirical identification of a robust cross-architectural anomaly: VH-BQ yields a higher ASR than VH-HQ for 5 of 6 models (average ASR 48.33% vs 35.84%, refusal rate collapsing from 53.05% to 14.22%), despite models achieving >81% structured-label accuracy on harmful content.
- A representation-level finding: a refusal direction learned from external text/image-text safety data projects harmful-video samples (VH-BQ) to a weaker refusal-alignment score than explicitly harmful text (T-HQ) in most models, providing a mechanistic (not merely behavioural) explanation.
- A training-free prompt-injection realignment that forces explicit textual reasoning about harmful visual evidence, reducing average VH-BQ ASR from 48.33% to 0.80% (zero-shot) / 0.09% (8-shot), competitive with prior fine-tuning-based SEA alignment.
- A unified evaluation across 5,020 samples from three public video-safety benchmarks and six representative open-source Video LLMs spanning the Qwen2/2.5-VL, InternVL, InternVideo2.5, and MiniCPM families.

## Methodology

V-DEAL couples three diagnostic stages with a final intervention stage, evaluated on 5,020 videos (8 uniformly-sampled frames each) merged from Video-SafetyBench, the video subset of VA-SafetyBench, and the unimodal video subset of Omni-SafetyBench.

**Stage 1 — Behavioural analysis.** For each video the authors construct four attack conditions that disentangle textual harmfulness from harmful visual evidence: text-only harmful query (T-HQ), text-only benign query (T-BQ), harmful video + harmful query (VH-HQ), and harmful video + benign query (VH-BQ). A fixed GPT-4o-mini judge labels each output as HARMFUL / REFUSAL / SAFE; ASR, Refusal Rate, and Safe Rate are the category proportions.

**Stage 2 — Understanding analysis.** To test whether the VH-BQ vulnerability is merely weak perception, each model is re-run under a unified benign prompt and asked to emit (i) structured JSON labels over five attributes (main_environment, visible_weapon, physical_contact, num_visible_people, primary_focus) scored by exact-match Accuracy and Macro-F1, and (ii) a 3–5 sentence neutral summary scored by an LLM-judge for correctness, detail, and overall similarity against a stronger model's reference. A supplementary harmful-intent recognition test runs on a shared 300-video Video-SafetyBench subset.

**Stage 3 — Representation analysis.** The authors define a layer-wise refusal direction `w_ℓ = (μ_refusal^ℓ − μ_other^ℓ) / ||·||` from the hidden state of the last text token, estimated on Refusal/Other samples from external safety data (XSTest + MM-SafetyBench). Layers are ranked by ROC-AUC on the external corpus and the top-m=4 layers are averaged (m fixed a priori to avoid post-hoc tuning). Each attack sample is projected onto this axis to produce a scalar refusal-alignment score, computed on a balanced 1,250-sample subset per condition.

**Intervention — prompt-injection realignment.** Based on the diagnosis (the failure is downstream of perception), the authors add a prompt that explicitly instructs the model to first summarize the video's harmful visual evidence, then make its safety decision jointly with the query, optionally with 8 in-context refusal demonstrations. Model weights, inputs, and decoding settings are unchanged. A stricter benchmark-aligned comparison is run against the SEA method on Qwen2-VL-7B over the full VA-SafetyBench.

## Results & Highlights

- **VH-BQ is the highest-ASR condition for 5 of 6 models.** Average ASR jumps from 35.84% (VH-HQ) to 48.33% (VH-BQ), a +12.49 percentage-point increase, while the average refusal rate collapses from 53.05% to 14.22% (Behavioural analysis, Table 1/Figure 3). The lone exception, MiniCPM-V-4.5, still posts a 55.2% ASR under VH-BQ with only a 2.9% refusal rate.
- **Models do understand the harmful videos.** Structured-label accuracies range from 0.811 (MiniCPM-V-4.5) to 0.955 (InternVL3.5-8B); on the 300-video harmful-intent subset, 1,619 / 1,800 responses (89.94% overall) partially or exactly match the reference harmful intent, with model-level rates from 82.33% to 95.67% (Table 8). Yet InternVL3.5-8B and InternVideo2.5-8B, the two best-perceiving models, still reach VH-BQ ASRs of 49.1% and 36.1%.
- **Refusal-direction projection explains the failure mechanistically.** VH-BQ refusal scores are typically below T-HQ (and often below VH-HQ); Qwen2-VL-7B's VH-BQ score is −0.1516, far below its T-HQ score of 0.0803 (Table 3). External-to-video transfer AUCs for Refusal-vs-Other range 0.8534–0.9647, all above the random-direction 95th-percentile control (Table 9).
- **The training-free intervention is highly effective.** On the VH-BQ half-subset (2,510 examples), average zero-shot ASR drops to 0.80% and 8-shot ASR to 0.09%; InternVL3.5-8B drops from 49.1% to 0.08% under 8-shot, and three models reach 0.00% (Table 4). Across six models the prompt intervention reduces ASR by an average of 48.24 percentage points.
- **Competitive with fine-tuning baselines.** On the matched SEA-style setting (Qwen2-VL-7B, full VA-SafetyBench), zero-shot and 8-shot prompting achieve 0.14% and 0.24% ASR respectively, both below the prior SEA SFT result (0.34%) and well below SEA DPO (3.56%).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High. The framing of video safety de-calibration as an *understanding–refusal coupling* failure — diagnosed jointly via behaviour, proxy perception tasks, and a latent refusal axis — is more mechanistically grounded than prior video-safety work that only constructs attacks or reports ASR. The training-free prompt intervention is straightforward but is positioned as a *diagnostic validation* rather than a primary methodological contribution, which is intellectually honest.
- **Rigor:** Mixed. Strengths include controlled four-condition disentanglement, a balanced 1,250-sample-per-condition protocol for representation analysis, and an external-to-video transfer audit with bootstrap CIs and a random-direction P95 control (Table 9). Weaknesses: (a) the behavioural ground truth rests on a *single* LLM judge (GPT-4o-mini) with no human validation or inter-judge agreement reported; the authors themselves caveat that ASR 0.00% means "no outputs labeled HARMFUL by the fixed judge," not absolute safety. (b) The number of top layers `m=4` is fixed a priori from pilot runs but never sensitivity-analysed. (c) The matched SEA comparison on VA-SafetyBench is run on a *single* model (Qwen2-VL-7B), so the headline claim of beating SEA is narrower than the six-model framing suggests.
- **Reproducibility:** Reasonable. Benchmarks are public, models are open-source, the unified manifest (`all_video_manifest.jsonl`), exact prompts, layer indices, and parsing rules are documented in appendices. However, no code/model-release commitment is stated, and the refusal-direction estimation depends on the specific external XSTest/MM-SafetyBench splits which are not pinned down.
- **Significance:** The core behavioural finding (VH-BQ > VH-HQ) replicates and extends a known result; the mechanistic reframing (visual evidence activates weaker refusal than textual evidence) is the genuinely new and useful contribution for alignment researchers. The intervention is practical but adds inference cost (an extra summarisation pass plus 8 demos) that is not quantified.
- **Red flags:** The SEA-baseline comparison is slightly oversold — the difference between their 0.14% and SEA-SFT's 0.34% is within plausible judge noise given a single GPT-4o-mini judge. Qwen2.5-VL-7B's intervention result is also worth scrutiny: under 8-shot it reports 0.32% ASR but only 26.2% refusal and 73.5% "safe" outputs, raising the question of whether the model is genuinely refusing or merely rephrasing answers into non-harmful-looking text — a softer safety claim than the table headline implies.

## Strengths

- Genuinely mechanistic diagnosis that links three independent evidence types (behaviour, perception quality, internal refusal axis) rather than reporting a single ASR number.
- Broad and fair model coverage (six open-source Video LLMs across four families) and three public benchmarks with a unified manifest.
- Honest framing: the authors explicitly state that strong understanding does *not* imply strong refusal, and use the representation analysis to support rather than override the behavioural result.
- Training-free intervention is lightweight and broadly applicable, with a 0.00% ASR achievable on three of six models.
- Strong appendix detail on prompts, layer selection, parsing, and a transfer audit with bootstrap confidence intervals and random-direction controls.

## Limitations

- Single LLM judge (GPT-4o-mini) with no human evaluation or second-judge cross-check; ASR=0 is a judge-relative, not absolute, claim.
- Only 8 frames sampled per video — temporal evidence in long videos could be missed, and the impact of frame count is not ablated.
- The `m=4` top-layer choice and the choice of the last text token for hidden-state extraction are not sensitivity-analysed.
- The SEA-baseline comparison on VA-SafetyBench is limited to Qwen2-VL-7B, so "competitive with fine-tuning" is a one-model claim.
- Inference cost of the intervention (extra summarisation + few-shot context) and its effect on over-refusal / benign-task utility are not reported.
- Representation analysis relies on external text/image-text safety data to estimate the refusal direction; transfer to the video modality is validated but imperfect, with wider CIs for models with few refusal examples.

## Relevance

对研究多模态/视频大模型安全对齐、越狱攻防与机制可解释性的方向高度相关：V-DEAL 的「理解-拒绝解耦」诊断视角与隐状态拒绝方向分析方法，可直接迁移到图像/音频 LLM 的安全对齐评估、轻量化推理时防御设计，以及安全子空间的可解释性研究中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a stronger or ensemble LLM judge (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude) change the absolute ASR numbers, and in particular the 0.00% ASR claims and the narrow margin over SEA-SFT?
- Does the prompt-injection intervention cause over-refusal on benign video-question pairs, and what is its latency/cost overhead in practice?
- How sensitive is the representation analysis to `m`, to the choice of the last text token vs. visual tokens, and to which external dataset is used to estimate the refusal direction?
- Is the "visual evidence activates weaker refusal than text" finding specific to current safety-training recipes (which are text-heavy), and would vision-centric safety fine-tuning close the VH-BQ vs VH-HQ gap?
- Does the VH-BQ anomaly extend to longer videos (>8 frames) and to audio-bearing videos where harmful intent is carried by speech rather than pixels?
- Can the latent refusal-direction score be used as an online detector/trigger for the intervention, rather than applying the heavier prompt to every query?
