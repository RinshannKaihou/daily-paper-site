§ 01

Methodology

A parameterized/structural-complexity treatment: the architecture is input, but structural parameters — input dimension n, hidden-neuron count w, treewidth t — are fixed constants. Three reduction engines do the work.

§4 · ReLU tractability

Sign-branch + partition-branch

Normalize each single-out-edge hidden neuron to weights in {−1,+1} (Lemma 1–2 → 2w choices). Enumerate hyperplane-induced bipartitions of D in |D|O(n) via an extremal-point LP (Lemma 3 — repairs Arora et al.). Combine to solve |D|O(n·w·2w) QPs by ellipsoid.

§5 · Blow-up transform

Split high out-degree neurons

Replace a hidden neuron feeding d outputs with d parallel copies, each carrying one original weight (Lemma 7). Yields a same-depth, ≥-expressive, polynomially-trainable architecture — an improper-learning-style guarantee, at the cost of up to o·λℓ+1 hidden neurons.

§6 · Untanglings (LIN-NNT)

Block-decompose the graph

An untangling partitions nodes into single-source input-blocks and single-sink output-blocks whose adjacency mirrors reachability. Collapse free parameters to an n×d matrix + biases, solved as one QP. Detection: FPT branching or MSOL/Courcelle.

§ 03

Strengths & Limitations

✓

Strengths

  • Breaks the single-output cage. Exact-optimal ReLU training extends to multi-output, multi-layer, and mixed-activation nets — with a matching lower bound.
  • Reusable structural criterion. Untangling gives a clean tractability condition for linear networks plus a complete NP-hard / FPT complexity picture.
  • Self-contained proofs. Lemma 3 fills a genuine gap in Arora et al. (2018)'s enumeration step; the cited 2|D| choose n bound was imprecise.
  • Intellectual honesty. Flags improper-learning generalization risk and nonelementary treewidth dependency; poses three concrete open problems.
!

Limitations

  • Theory only. The |D|O(n·w·2w) and blow-up growth make the algorithms usable only on tiny constant-size architectures.
  • Untangling is conditional. LIN-NNT tractability assumes an untangling is given; detection is NP-hard in general and restricted in the poly cases.
  • No free lunch on blow-up. Preserves training loss and depth but can grow size exponentially and may raise generalization error.
  • No empirics. No experiments, ablations, or baseline comparison on real or even small toy instances.
  • Narrow scope. ReLU + linear only; sigmoid/tanh out of reach, and only squared ℓ₂ loss is handled.

Questions for Further Exploration

Q1 · OPEN
Can the ReLU enumeration bound |D|O(n·w·2w) separate the 2w factor from the dataset exponent, or is the stacked dependency inherent to multi-hidden-layer nets?
Q2 · OPEN
Is LIN-NNT polynomial-time tractable on constant-treewidth architectures without an explicit untangling? (authors' Open Question 1)
Q3 · OPEN
Do the techniques extend to ReLU hidden neurons with out-degree > 1, closing the gap to fully general constant-size networks? (Open Question 2)
Q4 · OPEN
Do the results port to other losses (cross-entropy, hinge) or to convolutional/attention-style architectural restrictions? (Open Question 3)
Q5 · TRADEOFF
Can a finer-grained size/expressivity tradeoff characterize when the blow-up is "worth it" relative to generalization?
Q6 · EMPIRICAL
Is there an empirical crossover point — however small — where these exact algorithms outperform gradient descent on toy instances, and what does it say about the theory-practice gap?
