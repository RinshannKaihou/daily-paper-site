> **一句话总结：** 针对高召回候选生成中"被漏掉的相关项不可恢复"这一非对称错误，本文证明仅靠候选集内部标签无法对漏检质量给出任何非平凡上界（命题3），并给出匹配的下界：任何在零漏检情形下以高概率认证"M0(g)≤m−1"的有效审计（即使自适应、可随机化、可标注整个纳入池）平均仍需检验 Ω(N0/m) 个排除池标签（定理13），使排除池审计成为零漏检体制下的极小化极大最优；在此基础上用精确的二项/超几何反演（而非渐近近似）构建一整套可部署的认证工具包——漏检质量、双池召回、前缀族同时认证、压力测试逃逸率——并在 N=100,000 语料的确定性算例中用 n0=300 个排除池标签、零观察到漏检即认证 r(g)≤0.853%（超几何 MU=853）、召回≥85.3%，而等价的全总体审计需 431 个标签。

> **TL;DR:** This paper studies how many audit labels are required, with finite-sample validity, to certify that a high-recall candidate generator misses little relevant mass — the irrecoverable error in pipelines from e-discovery and systematic-review screening to RAG retrieval. It proves two structural results: (1) included-pool labels alone can never certify a non-trivial bound on missed mass (Proposition 3 — the audit must sample the excluded pool), and (2) a matching lower bound shows any valid audit certifying fewer than m misses in the zero-miss regime must inspect Ω(N0/m) excluded-pool labels, even if adaptive and allowed to label the whole included pool, making uniform excluded-pool auditing minimax rate-optimal (Theorem 13 / Corollary 15, Θ(N0/m)). On the constructive side it builds an exact toolkit using Clopper–Pearson binomial and hypergeometric inversion (not asymptotic approximation) for missed mass, two-pool recall, simultaneous prefix-family certification, and stress-test escape rates. In a deterministic worked example on an N=100,000-sentence corpus (burden 0.30, excluded mass 0.70), auditing n0=300 excluded-pool items with zero observed misses certifies r(g)≤0.00853 (exact hypergeometric bound 853 missed sentences) and, combined with n1=500 included labels, recall ≥85.3% at joint 95% confidence — whereas a whole-population audit needs 431 labels for the same bound (a 1/p0≈1.43× overhead).

## 核心贡献

- **不可能性结果（命题3）**：首次严格证明，仅用候选集内部（纳入池）的标签、生成器取值与无限无标签数据，无法对漏检相关质量 r(g)=P(Y=1,g(X)=0) 给出任何非平凡的分布无关上界——最好的通用界退化为排除池质量 p0(g) 本身。这从根本上论证了"必须审计排除池"。
- **匹配的下界与极小化极大最优（定理13/推论15）**：在零漏检体制下，任何有效审计（即便自适应、随机化、可标注整个纳入池）平均须检验 E_A[T]≥(1−β−δ)·N0/m 个排除池项；当 β≤1/2、δ<1/4 时为 N0/(4m)。简单的非自适应零计数排除池审计以 (N0/m)log(1/δ)+1 达到同一尺度，故排除池审计在零漏检体制下极小化极大最优（Θ(N0/m)），而不仅是"方便"。
- **精确有限样本认证工具包（第4节）**：用 Clopper–Pearson 二项反演与超几何反演（而非正态近似）给出漏检质量上界、有限语料漏检计数上界、双池召回下界、整总体基线上界。定理7/8/10/12 覆盖各采样设计，全部为精确有限样本覆盖保证。
- **前缀族同时认证与停止规则（推论16/定理17/19）**：对预指定的嵌套前缀生成器族，用联合审计样本或共享参考样本一次性认证全部前缀，并在满足漏检质量目标的前缀中选出负担最小者；联合界的代价仅随前缀数 M 对数增长。
- **可审计诊断与压力测试证书（第5、6节）**：定理24 把条件漏检率 L(gJ) 分解为各组件检测器增量覆盖 γj 的乘积（L(gJ)=∏(1−γj)≤exp(−∑γj)），用审计到的正例估计每个组件贡献；定理29 给出针对预指定扰动机制 A（释义、OCR 损坏、拼写变体、语义对抗）的簇逃逸率证书。
- **设计与认证分离原则（假设1）**：明确要求候选生成器/前缀族在认证标签被查看前固定，否则采样分布失效；第7节给出设计阶段（有限类、VC、稀疏并、Neyman–Pearson）的选择保证，与独立留出审计配对。
- **可部署的工作流与算例（第8节）**：给出六步审计配方，并在 N=100,000 语料上用确定性计算演示负担/漏检/召回/前缀选择的完整认证流程。

## Key Contributions

- **Impossibility of included-pool certification (Proposition 3).** A clean argument shows that any procedure using only included-pool labels, generator values, and unlimited unlabelled data returns an upper bound T with P_A(T≥p0(g))≥1−δ; the best distribution-free bound collapses to the excluded-pool mass itself. This formally justifies why the audit must look outside the candidate set.
- **Matching lower bound and minimax optimality (Theorem 13 / Corollary 15).** In the zero-miss regime M0(g)=0, any valid level-δ audit that certifies M0(g)≤m−1 with probability ≥1−β must inspect E_A[T]≥(1−β−δ)·N0/m distinct excluded-pool items on average — even if adaptive, randomised, and permitted to label the entire included pool. The non-adaptive zero-count excluded-pool audit attains the matching upper bound (N0/m)log(1/δ)+1, so excluded-pool auditing is minimax rate-optimal (Θ(N0/m)) for zero-miss certification, not merely convenient.
- **Exact finite-sample toolkit (Section 4).** All certificates use exact Clopper–Pearson binomial inversion and hypergeometric inversion rather than asymptotic approximation, giving guaranteed finite-sample coverage. Theorems 7/8/10/12 cover excluded-pool missed mass (i.i.d. and finite-corpus), two-pool recall, and whole-population baselines.
- **Simultaneous prefix-family certification and stopping (Corollaries 16/17, Theorem 19).** A pre-specified nested family of prefix generators can be certified simultaneously from one shared reference sample; the union-bound cost grows only as log M in the number of prefixes, and a valid stopping rule selects the least-burdensome prefix meeting a missed-mass target.
- **Auditable diagnostics and stress-test certificates (Sections 5–6).** Theorem 24 decomposes the conditional miss rate as L(gJ)=∏_j(1−γj)≤exp(−∑γj), attributing coverage to each component detector via audited positives; Theorem 29 gives finite-sample escape-rate certificates against a declared perturbation mechanism (paraphrase, OCR, spelling, semantic-adversarial).
- **Design–certification separation (Assumption 1) and design layer (Section 7).** The candidate generator (or family) must be fixed before certification labels are examined; Section 7 supplies finite-class, VC, sparse-union, and Neyman–Pearson design-stage guarantees paired with independent held-out certification.
- **Deployable workflow and deterministic worked example (Section 8).** A six-step audit recipe instantiated on an N=100,000 corpus, demonstrating burden/missed-mass/recall/prefix-choice certification end to end.

## Methodology

The paper is a learning-theoretic/statistical methodology paper with no machine-learning training experiments; every number is an exact deterministic inversion of binomial or hypergeometric tail probabilities.

**Two sampling models.** An i.i.d. population model (X,F,P) with latent relevance indicator φ⋆ and candidate generator g:X→{0,1}, and a finite-corpus model over fixed N items where probability statements refer only to the random audit sample. The central decomposition is r(g)=p0(g)·η(g), where the excluded-pool mass p0(g)=P(g(X)=0) is observable *without* labels and the excluded-pool relevance rate η(g)=P(Y=1|g(X)=0) requires labels from {g=0}.

**Exact inversion.** Upper/lower one-sided bounds U_n(k,α), L_n(k,α) are the exact Clopper–Pearson inversions of binomial tails; the finite-corpus analogue uses hypergeometric inversion (Theorem 8). A conditional binomial-inversion lemma (Lemma 6) handles random effective audit sizes. "Exact" means guaranteed finite-sample coverage, not shortest intervals — the procedures are deliberately conservative.

**Core certificate pipeline.** (1) Proposition 3 proves included-pool labels are information-theoretically insufficient. (2) Theorem 7 draws n0 i.i.d. from the excluded pool; with zero observed misses, r(g)≤p0(g)(1−(δ/|G|)^{1/n0})≤p0(g)log(|G|/δ)/n0, with a union bound over the pre-specified family G. (3) Theorem 13 proves the Ω(N0/m) lower bound by coupling a zero-miss labelling with a planted m-subset labelling under the same random seed. (4) Corollary 15 matches it with the non-adaptive zero-count audit. (5) Theorem 10 combines an excluded-pool upper bound on M0(g) with an included-pool lower bound on M1(g) to certify recall. (6) Sections 5–6 add the component-decomposition diagnostic (Theorem 24) and the perturbation stress-test certificate (Theorem 29), the latter drawing one variant per cluster to keep the certificate exactly binomial.

**Validity discipline.** Assumption 1 (Design–Certification Separation) forbids using certification labels to construct/tune/order/select the generator; Assumption 2 (Noiseless Audit Labels) is required for all certificates, with a limited Section 9 extension to a known sensitivity floor for the i.i.d. missed-mass certificate only. A bicriteria (Neyman–Pearson-shaped) formulation minimises burden B(g) subject to U(g)≤ε.

## Results & Highlights

*This is a theory/methodology paper; it reports no experiments on real benchmark datasets. All quantitative claims below are exact deterministic audit calculations from the paper's worked example (Section 8) on a synthetic corpus of N=100,000 sentences, with whole-population auditing as the natural baseline.*

- **Excluded-pool missed-mass certificate (synthetic N=100,000 corpus, burden 0.30, excluded mass p0=0.70).** Auditing n0=300 excluded-pool items with zero observed misses gives the binomial bound U_300(0,0.025)=1−0.025^{1/300}≈0.01222, so r(g)≤0.70×0.01222≤0.00856 (≈856 missed sentences). The exact finite-corpus hypergeometric inversion (Theorem 8) tightens this to MU=853, i.e. r(g)≤0.00853.

- **Excluded-pool vs whole-population label efficiency (same target r(g)≤0.00853, δ=0.025, zero observed events).** Whole-population auditing requires n≥log(1/0.025)/log(1/(1−0.00853))≈431 labels, whereas the excluded-pool design achieves the same bound with n0=300 — a ratio 300/431≈0.70=p0(g), matching the planning identity n_whole≈n_excluded/p0(g). For a hypothetical generator with p0=0.20, the whole-population overhead would be ~5×.

- **Two-pool recall certificate (n1=500 included items, K1=100 relevant).** The exact finite-corpus lower inversion gives M_L(100,0.025)=4,983 included relevant items; combined with the excluded upper bound MU=853, Recall(g)≥4,983/(4,983+853)≥0.8538. Reported joint 95% statement: burden 30%, absolute missed mass ≤0.853% of corpus, recall ≥85.3%.

- **Sensitivity of the certificate to the observed audit count (n0=300, p0=0.70, N0=70,000, δ0=0.025).** Exact hypergeometric MU and certified r(g) scale sharply with the count of excluded-pool positives: K0=0→MU=853 (r≤0.00853); K0=1→1,287 (0.01287); K0=2→1,668 (0.01668). A single observed miss pushes the certificate above a 1%-of-corpus target.

- **Zero-count audit-size planning table (δ=0.05, zero observed misses, single candidate generator).** Labels required to certify η(g)≤ε: ε=0.10→29; ε=0.05→59; ε=0.02→149; ε=0.01→299; ε=0.005→598. Certifying 10 pre-specified prefixes simultaneously costs only 51 / 104 / 263 / 528 / 1,058 labels per prefix (log M overhead).

- **Prefix choice (3 pre-specified prefixes, burdens 0.15/0.30/0.38, all K=0, δm=0.05/3).** U_300(0,0.05/3)≈0.01356; certified r(g_m): g1≤0.0116, g2≤0.00949, g3≤0.00841. The valid post-audit choice is g2 — the least-burdensome prefix whose simultaneous certificate meets the 1% missed-mass target.

- **Stress-test exactness (Theorem 29 / Remark 31, Q=300, error budget 0.025, zero escapes).** The exact binomial bound U_300(0,0.025)=0.0122 is far sharper than a Hoeffding additive term √(log(1/0.025)/(2Q))=0.0784 — the exact one-draw-per-cluster certificate avoids any additive concentration penalty near zero.

- **Lower-bound scaling (Theorem 13).** Any valid audit must inspect E_A[T]≥(1−β−δ)·N0/m excluded-pool items in the zero-miss regime; for β≤1/2, δ<1/4 this is at least N0/(4m), matched by the zero-count audit's (N0/m)log(1/δ)+1.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — high and genuine on the structural results.** The included-pool impossibility (Proposition 3) and the matching Ω(N0/m) lower bound with minimax optimality (Theorem 13 / Corollary 15) are specific to the candidate-generation geometry and have no analogue in general risk-control frameworks (Learn then Test, Conformal Risk Control) or in the hypergeometric stopping-rule lineage (Callaghan & Müller-Hansen [7]). The lower bound's proof technique (coupling a zero-miss labelling with a planted m-subset under a shared random seed) is clean and the right tool. The constructive toolkit, by contrast, is a competent specialisation of Clopper–Pearson inversion rather than a new inferential idea.

- **Rigour — high.** Proofs are given in full; the lower bound carefully models adaptive, randomised procedures via a filtration (F_t) and a stopping time τ, and the coupling argument is tight. Assumptions are stated explicitly and their necessity discussed (Assumption 1 is the validity condition; Section 2.4 explains how design exploration can coexist with held-out certification).

- **Reproducibility — strong for the theory, weak for empirical validation.** Every certificate is an exact deterministic inversion, so any reader can reproduce the worked-example numbers from the stated audit outcomes. However, there are *no experiments on real corpora* — no e-discovery dataset, no systematic-review corpus (e.g., CLEF/TAR), no RAG retrieval benchmark. The paper never demonstrates the certificates on a real high-recall pipeline or compares against existing TAR stopping rules (Webber [48], Callaghan & Müller-Hansen [7]) on actual data. This is the single biggest gap: the theory is exact, but its operational value is asserted, not empirically shown.

- **Significance — solid within its niche.** For practitioners of technology-assisted review and systematic-review screening, the excluded-pool + exact-inversion recipe is directly usable and improves on the normal-approximation intervals Webber [48] flagged as covering poorly. The RAG-retrieval framing (Section 1.1) is a reasonable extrapolation but is not substantiated by any retrieval experiment.

- **Red flags / overclaiming.** (a) The minimax optimality is proved *only* for the zero-miss regime M0(g)=0; the paper is explicit about this scope but the abstract's phrasing ("minimax rate-optimal ... for missed-mass certification in the zero-miss regime") could be misread as broader. (b) Assumption 2 (noiseless audit labels) is strong and the paper itself notes the noise extension covers *only* the i.i.d. excluded-pool missed-mass certificate — recall, component-decomposition, and stress-test certificates all break under label noise and need additional assumptions (specificity floor or adjudication) that are not developed. (c) The excluded-pool label-efficiency gain is modest when the excluded pool is large (the worked example's 0.70 excluded mass gives only a 1.43× saving); the dramatic gains arise only for high-burden generators with small excluded pools, which the paper acknowledges. (d) The bicriteria/prefix-choice machinery assumes the prefix family is genuinely pre-specified — easy to violate in practice and the paper's repeated warnings, while correct, offer no computational guardrail against the violation.

## Strengths

- The impossibility result (Proposition 3) is short, general, and resolves a foundational question: it proves *why* the audit must spend labels in the excluded pool, closing the door on any included-pool-only shortcut.
- The lower bound (Theorem 13) is the strongest contribution: it shows excluded-pool auditing is not just a heuristic but information-theoretically necessary at the optimal rate, and the proof handles fully adaptive/randomised procedures rather than only fixed designs.
- Exact (non-asymptotic) certificates are the right tool in a regime where normal approximations are known to cover poorly (Webber [48]); the zero-count identity and the one-draw-per-cluster stress-test design avoid unnecessary conservatism.
- The design–certification separation (Assumption 1) is articulated clearly and tied to a concrete workflow (audit-and-stop vs audit-and-iterate), which is rare and valuable for practitioners who might otherwise invalidate their own certificates.
- The paper honestly scopes its guarantees (zero-miss regime, noiseless labels, declared perturbation mechanism) rather than overgeneralising.

## Limitations

- **No real-data experiments.** Every quantitative result is a deterministic calculation on a synthetic corpus; the paper validates no certificate on an actual e-discovery, systematic-review, or RAG-retrieval dataset, nor benchmarks against existing TAR stopping rules on real data.
- **Lower bound is zero-miss-only.** The Θ(N0/m) minimax result covers M0(g)=0; the practically important regime where misses are present but few is characterised only by upper-bound certificates, not by a matching lower bound.
- **Noiseless-label assumption.** Assumption 2 underpins all certificates; the Section 9 extension is limited to the i.i.d. excluded-pool missed-mass case with a known sensitivity floor, and does not extend to recall, component-decomposition, or stress-test certificates without extra assumptions the paper does not provide.
- **Modest efficiency gain for large excluded pools.** The headline excluded-pool advantage shrinks to the p0(g) factor when the excluded pool dominates the corpus (1.43× in the worked example); large gains require high-burden generators.
- **Operational fragility of pre-specification.** The validity of simultaneous prefix certification and the bicriteria choice depends entirely on the family being frozen before labels are examined; the paper warns against violation but supplies no automated mechanism to enforce or detect it.
- **Stress-test scope is mechanism-specific.** Theorem 29 certifies escape only for the *declared* perturbation generator A; it is explicitly not a general semantic-robustness guarantee over continuous perturbation sets.

## Relevance

这篇论文属于分布无关有限样本推断与学习理论范畴，与高召回检索/筛选流水线（法律 e-discovery、系统综述筛查、RAG 检索）的认证直接相关。若你的兴趣在检索评估、RAG 召回保证或检索增强系统的可验证覆盖性上，其"排除池审计 + 精确反演"的认证框架和零漏检极小化极大下界提供了把"召回估计"升级为"有限样本召回证书"的直接方法论；但论文本身不含任何真实检索基准实验，需要自行在具体检索任务上落地验证。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the excluded-pool certificates behave empirically on a real high-recall pipeline (e.g., a CLEF/TAR e-discovery corpus or a RAG retrieval benchmark with graded relevance), and does the exact hypergeometric bound materially beat the normal-approximation intervals used in current TAR practice?
- Can the Ω(N0/m) lower bound be extended beyond the zero-miss regime to the operationally central case where a small but non-zero number of misses is present — i.e., is there a matching lower bound for certifying M0(g)≤m−1 when M0(g) is small but positive?
- The Section 9 noise extension covers only the i.i.d. excluded-pool missed-mass certificate under a known sensitivity floor; what minimal assumptions (e.g., a known specificity floor, or adjudicated dual-review labels) suffice to recover finite-sample recall and stress-test certificates under label noise?
- The stress-test certificate (Theorem 29) is relative to a declared perturbation generator A; can it be extended to structured or continuous perturbation neighbourhoods via a covering/approximation argument to yield a genuine semantic-robustness guarantee for RAG retrievers?
- Does stratified excluded-pool auditing (sketched in Section 9) deliver a meaningful label-efficiency gain over uniform excluded-pool auditing when strata are defined by the generator's own score, and how should the strata be chosen pre-specification to respect Assumption 1?
