> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 CertiFOX 框架——包含一个新的证明格式（CertiFOX proof format）、一个可认证的 grounding 工具 GroundFOX 与独立的检查器 CheckFOX——通过在 grounding 的每一步记录机器可查的等价保持重写规则，首次弥合了一阶逻辑模型扩展（FOX）问题中用户高层规约与求解器底层输入之间的"信任鸿沟"；在 DIRT benchmark 上与 IDP-Z3、PYCLINGO 相比 grounding 性能大致相当，证明检查开销仅为 grounding 时间的 2–3 倍常数因子。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces **CertiFOX**, a certifying grounding framework for first-order logic model expansion (FOX) over finite domains, comprising a new proof format, the **GroundFOX** certifying grounder (operating on a novel Grounding Normal Form, GNF, with guarded binary quantifiers), and the independent **CheckFOX** proof checker. On the DIRT benchmark suite (515 instances across 8 problem sets), GroundFOX grounds 505/515 instances with negligible proof-logging overhead and is broadly comparable to IDP-Z3 and PYCLINGO (PYCLINGO OOMs on 30 PPM instances vs. GroundFOX's 4; IDP-Z3 times out on 117 and OOMs on 112 vs. GroundFOX's 6 timeouts / 4 OOMs), while CheckFOX verifies proofs within a small constant factor (2–3×) of grounding time, closing the trust gap left open by the proof-logging revolution that previously only certified the solving phase.

## 核心贡献

- **首次认证 grounding 阶段**：在 SAT/MaxSAT/SMT/CP/ASP 等领域的 proof logging 革命以往只覆盖求解阶段，作者将 certifying algorithms 的思想扩展到 grounding（把高层理论翻译为等价的无量词公式）这一步骤，弥合"求解器答案正确 vs. 求解器输入正确"之间的信任鸿沟。
- **CertiFOX 生态系统三件套**：定义了一个新的 **CertiFOX proof format**；实现了 **GroundFOX** —— 一个能产出机器可查证明的 certifying grounder；以及 **CheckFOX** —— 一个独立、体量小到可被形式化验证的 proof checker。
- **Grounding Normal Form (GNF)**：提出新的正规形式，借助"二元量词"（binary quantifiers，带 guard 的 ∀/∃，受 [33] 启发）利用输入结构的领域知识做紧致的、domain-aware 的 grounding，且可直接 grounding 到 CNF，避免额外的 CNF 转换步骤。
- **基于 Sₙ-equivalence 的可靠证明系统**：设计了 SNAND / SNOR / STN（化简）、EPRED / EPROP（按输入结构 Sᵢₙ 解释）、IQ（带 guard 的实例化）、TRIVIAL / UNSAT / SPLITC（理论级）等少量等价保持重写规则；证明 checker 只需线性时间（在证明和公式大小上）回放每一步。
- **可行性实验验证**：在 DIRT benchmark 上证明 certifying grounding 在实际规模下可行，GroundFOX 与成熟工具性能可比，且 checking 仅带来常数因子开销。

## Key Contributions

- **Certifies the grounding phase for the first time.** While the proof-logging revolution (DRAT, VeriPB, eDRAT, ASP-DRUPE) has covered SAT, MaxSAT, SMT, CP, planning and ASP, it only certified the solver's answer with respect to its low-level input — never that the input faithfully reflects the user's specification. CertiFOX closes this exact gap for FOX (first-order model expansion) over finite domains.
- **A complete three-part ecosystem.** (1) The **CertiFOX proof format** for grounding derivations, with a header/body/footer syntax and a positioning system that pinpoints any subformula via a tuple ⟨N, [i₀,…,iₙ]⟩. (2) **GroundFOX**, a proof-of-concept certifying grounder emitting a proof alongside the CNF output. (3) **CheckFOX**, an independent checker that replays each rule in linear time and confirms the final theory is syntactically identical to the claimed grounding.
- **Grounding Normal Form (GNF)** and **binary quantifiers** (∀x[ϕ]:ψ ≜ ∀x:ϕ⇒ψ, ∃x[ϕ]:ψ ≜ ∃x:ϕ∧ψ) that let guards over the input vocabulary Vᵢₙ be evaluated against Sᵢₙ during grounding, skipping irrelevant instances. GNF admits direct grounding to CNF with no auxiliary-symbol introduction by the grounder.
- **Sᵢₙ-equivalence as the correctness invariant**: every rule application preserves equivalence over structures extending Sᵢₙ; the checker's acceptance therefore guarantees T ≡_{Sᵢₙ} T′.
- **Empirical feasibility** on the DIRT benchmark suite, demonstrating the approach is broadly competitive with mature grounders and that proof checking adds only a constant-factor overhead — plus a clean separation-of-concerns architecture (Fig. 1) that means a grounder bug cannot compromise soundness.

## Methodology

**Problem.** A FOX problem ⟨V = Vᵢₙ ∪ Vₒᵤₜ, Sᵢₙ, T⟩ asks to find a structure S extending Sᵢₙ that models theory T. Solving is normally split into (i) grounding — translating T into an equivalent quantifier-free (CNF) theory T′ — and (ii) solving T′. Prior proof logging covers only (ii).

**GNF.** The framework restricts to a fragment where every sentence is in Grounding Normal Form: a (possibly nested) universal quantifier over a body in Grounding Disjunctive Form (literals or existentially quantified subformulas). Guards reference only Vᵢₙ so the grounder can evaluate them. Any FOL formula can be made equisatisfiable with a GNF formula, sometimes requiring auxiliary symbols (illustrated on the "node connected to all others" example, where a ToAll/1 predicate is introduced).

**Proof system.** Built on a minimal set of sound, equivalence-preserving rewrite rules organised at two levels:

- *Formula-level (Table 1):* SNAND, SNOR, STN (collapse conjunctions/disjunctions containing t/f), EPRED/EPROP (replace an input predicate/proposition by its truth value in Sᵢₙ), IQ (instantiate a binary quantifier only over domain elements satisfying the guard).
- *Theory-level (Table 2):* TRIVIAL (drop a formula that simplified to t), UNSAT (early-terminate with unsatisfiability when a formula simplifies to f — this certifies the original problem has no model extending Sᵢₙ), SPLITC (split a conjunction into separate formulas).

The invariant proved (informally, per rule) is **Sᵢₙ-equivalence**: for every S ≥p Sᵢₙ, S ⊨ ϕ ⇔ S ⊨ ψ.

**Proof syntax.** Header (format/grounder version), body (one line per rule application targeting a position via the ⟨N, [i₀,…,iₙ]⟩ syntax tree indexing system, Table 3), and footer (`FINAL IDS : <N>, ...` or `FINAL IDS : -` for an empty grounded theory).

**GroundFOX algorithm.** Parse via ANTPLR4; for each GNF sentence apply IQ to the outermost ∀, SPLITC into one top-level formula per domain element, recurse on nested quantifiers; instantiate ∃ via IQ, evaluate ground atoms with EPRED/EPROP (and STN for negation), collapse with SNOR (short-circuiting to t or dropping f literals), emit UNSAT on the first f and TRIVIAL on t.

**CheckFOX algorithm.** Replays the proof sequentially; each step needs at most one traversal of the targeted subformula tree (linear in proof+formula size); confirms the final theory is syntactically identical to the claimed grounding. The deliberate simplicity/smallness makes future formal verification feasible.

## Results & Highlights

- On **515 total instances** of the DIRT benchmark suite across 8 problem sets (CommonItem-SAT/UNSAT, CompleteSets-SAT/UNSAT, GraphColouring, PPM, RamseyNumbers, stablemarriage): **PYCLINGO hit the time limit on 2 instances and the memory limit on 30** (all PPM); **IDP-Z3 timed out on 117 and ran out of memory on 112**; **GroundFOX (with or without proof logging) timed out on 6 and ran out of memory on only 4 instances**.
- On the 30 PPM instances where **PYCLINGO runs out of memory, GroundFOX runs out of memory on only 4**; GroundFOX also grounds all stablemarriage instances whereas PYCLINGO times out on 2.
- The **overhead of proof logging in GroundFOX is minimal**, and the **overhead of CheckFOX checking is within a factor of 2–3 in most cases** of grounding time.
- CheckFOX is the most memory-intensive step: it **timed out on 3 instances and ran out of memory on 66 of the 505 successfully grounded instances** — explicitly flagged by the authors as open work.
- Proof sizes (Fig. 3) span **from a few hundred lines to millions of lines**, varying widely by benchmark family; **RamseyNumbers proofs are disproportionately large** because its IDP encoding has no types, so GNF yields unguarded, deeply nested quantifiers that produce an exponential number of formulas to simplify — in contrast to all other families where IDP's typed multi-sorted logic translates directly into GNF guards.
- GroundFOX is "respectable overall" but is **outperformed by PYCLINGO on all benchmarks and by IDP-Z3 on about half**; IDP-Z3 plateaus near 200 instances solved, performing particularly well on PPM (small typed integer domain with arithmetic constraints that Z3 handles efficiently).
- The source code is released at `https://gitlab.kuleuven.be/krr/software/groundfox-checkfox` (GroundFOX 0.1.0, CheckFOX 0.1.0).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — high.** Certifying the grounding phase (as opposed to the solving phase) is a genuine gap: the authors correctly observe that IDP-Z3 and CLINGO "neither currently produces certificates of correctness for their translations," and that SMT quantifier-instantiation proofs differ because SMT instantiation is non-exhaustive while grounding is exhaustive. The framing is honest about what is new vs. adapted.
- **Rigour — medium-high.** The Sᵢₙ-equivalence invariant and the per-rule soundness arguments are given informally rather than as formal proofs ("we will not provide a formal proof of this guarantee for each rule"). This is defensible for a foundations paper but leaves the central correctness claim resting on informal sketches; formalisation is explicitly deferred to future work.
- **Reproducibility — strong.** Versioned tools (GroundFOX 0.1.0, CheckFOX 0.1.0), public GitLab repository, fixed benchmark suite (DIRT), and clearly stated hardware (Intel Xeon Platinum 8468, Rocky Linux 8.10, single-core, 8 GiB RAM, 1-hour limit) and baselines (IDP-Z3 0.12.0, PYCLINGO 5.8.0).
- **Significance — high if it matures.** The contribution is foundational: a trustworthy end-to-end certified pipeline for declarative languages needs this exact piece. But the current GNF-only scope materially limits immediate applicability.
- **Red flags / specific concerns:**
  - **Benchmark cherry-picking.** The authors select "all benchmarks that can be translated into GNF **without introducing auxiliary predicates**" — this explicitly removes the harder cases (where GNF transformation needs auxiliary symbols, i.e. exactly the cases where grounding is most error-prone and certification would be most valuable). The headline "feasible" claim is therefore on the easy subset.
  - **Losing benchmark selection bias.** For stablemarriage, with multiple IDP encodings, "we used the encoding on which IDP-Z3 was the most efficient" — i.e. they picked the encoding most favourable to a competitor, which is an unusual and somewhat self-undermining choice for a paper claiming competitiveness.
  - **Weak baseline coverage.** Only two baselines, justified by "time constraints" — GrinGo and the broader ASP/CP grounding literature are absent.
  - **Competitiveness is real but partial.** GroundFOX is outperformed by PYCLINGO on *all* benchmarks and by IDP-Z3 on *about half*. The "broadly comparable" framing is fair, but the system is not yet at the frontier.
  - **Checker memory blowup.** CheckFOX OOMs on 66/505 grounded instances — the most memory-intensive step in the pipeline — undermining the "small constant factor overhead" headline for a non-trivial fraction of cases. No ablation on checker implementation choices is given.
  - **Missing ablations.** No ablation on the effect of guards vs. unguarded quantifiers (the RamseyNumbers blowup is described but not remedied); no ablation isolating the cost of each rule family; no analysis of how often UNSAT early-termination fires.
  - **Proof-size scaling risk.** The unguarded-quantifier → exponential-proof-size phenomenon on RamseyNumbers is an early warning that GNF-as-defined is not robust to encodings users will actually write.
  - **Checker not formally verified.** The paper's own stated goal — a "trust anchor" small enough to formally verify — is aspirational; CheckFOX is currently a normal OCaml-style prototype, so the trust claim rests on the same kind of trust the SAT community explicitly tries to eliminate.

## Strengths

- Identifies and cleanly formalises a real, under-recognised gap (the grounding trust gap) in an otherwise mature proof-logging literature.
- The Sᵢₙ-equivalence abstraction is elegant: it lets the grounder exploit domain knowledge (evaluate guards, skip irrelevant instances) while still certifying equivalence.
- Binary quantifiers / GNF is a well-motivated design that ties typed high-level encodings directly to compact groundings, and the RamseyNumbers counter-example honestly exposes its boundary.
- Clean separation of concerns (grounder vs. checker) with a positioning system that keeps proofs "unambiguous but brief."
- Honest reporting: the authors openly disclose that GroundFOX loses to PYCLINGO everywhere, that the checker is memory-heavy, and that the checker is not yet formally verified.
- Public, versioned artefacts and a real benchmark suite (DIRT) rather than toy examples.

## Limitations

- Restricted to the GNF fragment of FOL; arbitrary FOL sentences, cardinality constraints, aggregates and other high-level constructs are out of scope (explicitly future work).
- Benchmark selection excludes instances needing auxiliary predicates during GNF translation — i.e. precisely the harder grounding cases.
- Soundness arguments for each rule are informal; no machine-checked or paper-formal proof of the Sᵢₙ-equivalence preservation is provided.
- CheckFOX is the memory bottleneck (66/505 OOMs) and is not yet formally verified, so the "trust anchor" property is not yet delivered.
- Only two baselines (IDP-Z3, PYCLINGO); no comparison to GrinGo or other ASP grounders.
- No end-to-end integration with a certifying SAT solver is demonstrated; the paper "sets the stage" but does not yet close a full certified pipeline.
- Unguarded encodings (e.g. RamseyNumbers) blow up exponentially in proof size, limiting practical applicability to well-typed input.

## Relevance

This paper is highly relevant to research on **trustworthy and certifiable combinatorial solving**: it extends the proof-logging methodology (DRAT/VeriPB/eDRAT lineage) from the solving phase into the previously uncertified grounding phase, which is the key missing link for end-to-end certified declarative-solving pipelines (IDP-Z3, CLINGO/ASP, CP). For anyone working on SAT/MaxSAT/SMT/CP/ASP solvers, formal methods, or verified compilers for declarative languages, this is a foundational contribution worth tracking as it matures toward formal verification of the checker and broader language coverage.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does GroundFOX+CheckFOX perform on benchmarks that **require auxiliary predicates** to reach GNF — the subset deliberately excluded here, and arguably where certification matters most?
- What is the realistic path to a **formally verified CheckFOX** (e.g. in Lean, Coq, or Isabelle), and how much does the rule set need to shrink to make that tractable?
- Can the **exponential proof blowup on unguarded quantifiers** (RamseyNumbers) be tamed — e.g. by introducing a guarded re-encoding pass that itself produces a certificate, or by adding a guarded-quantifier-introduction rule?
- How does certifying grounding compose with **certifying SAT solving** (DRAT) to give an end-to-end guarantee from FOL specification to SAT answer, and what is the combined overhead?
- Could the GNF restriction and the Sᵢₙ-equivalence framework be extended to cover **cardinality constraints, aggregates, and FO(·)** features used in real IDP-Z3 / ASP models without breaking linear-time checkability?
- Is the 66/505 checker OOM rate a fundamental property of replay-based checking, or an artefact of the current CheckFOX implementation that better engineering (streaming, sharing) could remove?
- How sensitive are the results to the **choice of stablemarriage encoding** (the authors picked the one most favourable to IDP-Z3) — does the competitiveness picture change under a neutral or GroundFOX-favourable encoding?
