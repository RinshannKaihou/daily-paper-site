Critical Edge

The headline F1 0.549 vs. 0.184 over CT-CHAT is a detection-grounded comparison, not a like-for-like generator win: CT-CHAT's number is cited, not re-run, and reflects a known degenerate mode under the RadBERT-CT labeler.

→ Dramatic delta partly exposes CT-CHAT's weakness, not CCP's prose.

01 Methodology

A frozen encoder, treated as a black box. No weights move.

The Concept Channel Probe (CCP-K) ranks every coordinate of a cached embedding x ∈ R^D by a prevalence-invariant per-channel AUROC, fits a closed-form mean-difference direction on the top-K set, and calibrates a per-finding threshold.

  • Selectivity score Ã = A − 0.5 per channel — chance-centered, robust to CT-RATE's ~7–45% prevalence spread.
  • Direction w_c = (μ_pos − μ_neg)/‖·‖ (à la representation engineering), fit on 600 calibration volumes only.
  • Causal ablation: zero the top-K channels of a source finding, read the on- vs. off-target AUROC drop matrix — necessity, not just correlation.
  • Training-free reports: deterministic corpus template can't hallucinate findings outside the CCP detections.
  • K=10 picked on a disjoint holdout (smallest K within 0.005 AUROC of full-D), reused as-is on Merlin.

03 Strengths

What holds up
  • Principled: fuses network dissection + representation engineering + causal mediation into one coherent pipeline for frozen 3D CT.
  • Crisp sparsity: per-finding channel IDs and AUROCs listed (Table 5); ~10 recover full-D.
  • Causal, not correlational: ablation converts a probe into a necessity claim — rare in medical-VLM interp.
  • Honest transfer study: three regimes delineate where the probe transfers vs. breaks.
  • Cheap to audit: 600-volume calibration + K=10 → repeatable deployment checks.
  • Self-critical metrics: BLEU boilerplate sanity check (0.385 unrelated reports) disclosed openly.

04 Limitations

Where it frays
  • Only two backbones (Pillar-0, Merlin); other 3D VLMs and modalities (MRI, X-ray) untested.
  • Deterministic templates can't emit fluent, entity-rich prose — RadGraph-F1 lags RadLlama on abdomen (0.262 vs. 0.293).
  • Rare findings have high-variance channel estimates; abstention mitigates but under-powers rare circuits.
  • No human reader study — every metric comes from automated labelers or GT.
  • Headline CT-CHAT/CT-CLIP baselines are cited, not reproduced — the biggest delta rests on published numbers under a labeler that disadvantages CT-CHAT.
  • "~22× lower latency" is structural: template lookup vs. autoregressive LLM, not an efficiency innovation.

05 Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the ~10-channel sparsity pattern generalize beyond contrastive chest/abdomen CT — to CT-CHAT's own encoder, CT-FM, RadFM, brain MRI, or X-ray?
  2. How stable are the identified circuits under finer-grained labels (e.g., consolidation subtypes, nodule morphology) and longitudinal follow-up of the same patient?
  3. Could CCP channel circuits be repurposed for steering (amplify/suppress a finding), à la representation-engineering control vectors — not just detection/ablation?
  4. If CCP detections ground a modern autoregressive report LLM, does the detect/verbalize split still beat end-to-end generation and reduce hallucination?
  5. Is the 0.0060 vs. 0.00003 selectivity gap stable across calibration sizes and seeds — and can superposition/polysemanticity theory predict the K≈10 regime?
  6. Can channel-level explanations be validated against radiologist annotations of where in the volume a finding appears, bridging feature- and spatial-level interpretability?
