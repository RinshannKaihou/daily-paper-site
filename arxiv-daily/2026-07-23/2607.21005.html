Weight-norm Criticality: A Mechanism for Loss Spikes Induced by the Normalization and Weight Decay · arXiv:2607.21005 · 2026-07-23

Weight decay drives scale-invariant weights toward zero, exploding Hessian curvature by α⁻² and triggering predictable, layer-localizable loss spikes.

Xiaolong Li · Zhangchen Zhou · Zhi-Qin John Xu  —  Institute of Natural Sciences, School of Mathematical Sciences, Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Key metric · empirical proof of Thm 5.1

258×

growth in top Hessian eigenvalue λmax on an MNIST FNN+BN as weight decay rises wd = 0 → 0.03: 15 → 28 → 383 → 3863.

LLM final loss 2.548 vs 2.720

Why this matters

A drop-in per-layer weight-norm training monitor for LLM loss spikes — and a one-line robustness fix (turn off weight decay on MLP modules).

Directly actionable · aligns with training-observability stack

Critical edge

Validated only at 187M params / 1 epoch; the wd-off-MLP fix is never benchmarked against β₂-reduction, gradient clipping, or bad-batch skipping.

Mitigation shown to help — not shown to dominate
Methodology

From scale-invariance to a weight-norm you can monitor

A Hessian-homogeneity argument, collapsed into a norm threshold — so a global instability becomes a per-layer diagnostic you can attribute to a specific module.

  1. Split the parameters. Scale-invariant u (weights feeding BN/LN, where L(αu,v)=L(u,v)) vs. non-scale-invariant v; decompose the block Hessian accordingly.
  2. Curvature explosion (Thm 5.1). λmax(H(αu,v)) ≥ α⁻²·λmax(Huu) — shrinking u amplifies u-subspace curvature on the order of α⁻².
  3. Collapse to a norm threshold. Plugging α⁻² into the EoS criterion η·λmax≤2 gives the weight-norm boundary c* = √(ηρ/2) (ρ = ‖u‖²·λmax(Huu) is intrinsic curvature); a 2nd-order Taylor sharpening yields the spike boundary c*spike = √(ηρgrad/2).
  4. Per-component decomposition. Because the bound lives on Huu, every scale-invariant layer gets its own c*spike — instability is localized, not whole-network.
  5. Empirical cross-check. Parameter-scaling probes confirm the α⁻² lower bound (ResNet-50/CIFAR-100, FNN+BN); ‖W₁‖ excursions below c*spike align with measured spikes; module-wise top-eigenvector decomposition fingers the MLP subspace — motivating the wd-off-MLP ablation.
Results & highlights

One mechanism, five settings

  • MNIST FNN+BN — endpoint λmax vs weight decay 15 → 3863 (258×)
  • Synthetic 3-layer FNN+BN — λmax, wd 0 → 0.1 91 → 281
  • α⁻² lower bound tracked by scaling only scale-invariant weights ResNet-50 · FNN
  • Spike alignment — ‖W₁‖ dips below c*spike at spike windows MNIST · synth
  • Scale-invariant block dominates global sharpness from mid-training Fig. 6
  • 187M LLaMA-style Transformer, wd-off-MLP vs wd=3 all loss 2.548 vs 2.720
  • Ablation — decay only the non-scale-invariant fc-out layer spikes suppressed

Settings: 16-layer / 16-head 187M Transformer (dmodel=1280), one epoch on a 100B-token corpus, AdamW, peak LR 1e-3, grad-clip 1. ResNet-50/CIFAR-100 with SGD.

Assessment

What lands — and what doesn’t

+ Strengths

  • Unifying bridge between the Edge-of-Stability literature and the normalization × weight-decay literature — framed as complementary, not competing.
  • Per-component decomposition turns a global phenomenon into a per-layer diagnostic, exploited directly by the module-wise Hessian analysis and the wd-off-MLP ablation.
  • Broad, consistent empirics across five settings — the same mechanism shows up in each.
  • Simple, actionable mitigation compatible with existing training stacks.

− Limitations

  • Thm 5.1 is mathematically a refinement of known scale-invariance results; the novel content is the boundary translation.
  • c*spike needs gradient-direction curvature — costly at scale; no efficient estimator proposed.
  • Arbitrary interval filtering (30/200-iter thresholds) with no sensitivity analysis.
  • No test / downstream eval of wd-off-MLP — the regularization trade-off is unquantified.
Open questions

Where the mechanism has yet to be tested

01Does weight-norm criticality persist at 7B–70B scale, or do Adam-adaptivity and data-driven spikes mask it there?
02Can c*spike be approximated cheaply (Hutchinson / power iteration on a single layer’s Huu) for online monitoring?
03How does the analysis interact with RMSNorm (strictly scale-invariant) vs. affine LayerNorm — does the boundary tighten?
04What is the generalization trade-off of wd-off-MLP on downstream benchmarks at fixed compute?
05Can it combine with β₂-reduction and gradient clipping into a unified schedule — and which dominates?
06Does module-wise attribution extend to embeddings, output projections, and MLP-light architectures (Mamba, linear-attention)?
