§ 01

Methodology

A FOX problem ⟨V = Vin ∪ Vout, Sin, T⟩ asks for a structure extending Sin that models T. Solving splits into grounding (T → quantifier-free CNF T′) then solving T′. Prior proof logging covered only the second step — CertiFOX certifies the first.

Grounding Normal Form

Every sentence is a guarded universal over a body in Grounding Disjunctive Form. Binary quantifiers ∀x[ϕ]:ψ and ∃x[ϕ]:ψ let guards over Vin be evaluated against Sin during grounding — skipping irrelevant instances, grounding directly to CNF with no auxiliary symbols.

Proof system

A minimal set of equivalence-preserving rewrites: formula-level SNAND / SNOR / STN, EPRED / EPROP (interpret against Sin), IQ (guarded instantiation); theory-level TRIVIAL / UNSAT / SPLITC. Invariant: Sin-equivalence.

Two-tool split

GroundFOX emits a proof alongside the CNF, targeting subformulas via ⟨N, [i₀,…,iₙ]⟩ positions. CheckFOX independently replays each rule in linear time and confirms the final theory is syntactically identical to the claimed grounding — a grounder bug cannot break soundness.

Proof-logging overhead

Negligible for GroundFOX. CheckFOX verifies within a 2–3× constant factor of grounding time in most cases — the headline feasibility result.

Honest disclosure

GroundFOX is outperformed by PYCLINGO on all benchmarks and by IDP-Z3 on about half. CheckFOX is the memory bottleneck: 3 timeouts + 66 OOMs on the 505 grounded instances.

Proof-size scaling risk

RamseyNumbers proofs reach millions of lines — its untyped IDP encoding yields unguarded, deeply nested quantifiers and an exponential number of formulas. All other (typed) families translate cleanly into GNF guards.

Artefacts

GroundFOX 0.1.0 + CheckFOX 0.1.0, public on GitLab. Funded by ERC Starting Grant CertiFOX (101122653).

§ 03

Strengths & Limitations

▲ Strengths

  • Identifies and cleanly formalises a real, under-recognised gap — the grounding trust gap — in an otherwise mature proof-logging literature.
  • The Sin-equivalence abstraction is elegant: lets the grounder exploit domain knowledge while still certifying equivalence.
  • Binary quantifiers / GNF tie typed high-level encodings directly to compact groundings; the RamseyNumbers counter-example honestly exposes the boundary.
  • Clean separation of grounder vs. checker; positioning system keeps proofs "unambiguous but brief."
  • Honest reporting — openly discloses losses to PYCLINGO, the memory-heavy checker, and the absence of formal verification.
  • Public, versioned artefacts and a real benchmark suite rather than toy examples.

▼ Limitations

  • Restricted to the GNF fragment; arbitrary FOL, cardinality, aggregates and FO(·) are out of scope (future work).
  • Benchmark selection excludes instances needing auxiliary predicates — precisely the harder, more error-prone grounding cases.
  • Soundness arguments per rule are informal; no machine-checked or paper-formal proof of Sin-equivalence preservation.
  • CheckFOX is the memory bottleneck (66/505 OOMs) and is not yet formally verified — the "trust anchor" claim is aspirational.
  • Only two baselines (IDP-Z3, PYCLINGO); no GrinGo or broader ASP/CP grounder comparison.
  • No end-to-end integration with a certifying SAT solver — "sets the stage" but does not yet close a full certified pipeline.
  • Unguarded encodings (RamseyNumbers) blow up exponentially, limiting applicability to well-typed input.
§ 04

Open questions

How does GroundFOX + CheckFOX perform on benchmarks that require auxiliary predicates to reach GNF — the subset deliberately excluded here, where certification matters most?

What is the realistic path to a formally verified CheckFOX (Lean, Coq, Isabelle), and how much must the rule set shrink to make that tractable?

Can the exponential proof blowup on unguarded quantifiers be tamed — via a guarded re-encoding pass that itself produces a certificate, or a guarded-quantifier-introduction rule?

How does certifying grounding compose with certifying SAT solving (DRAT) for an end-to-end FOL→answer guarantee, and what is the combined overhead?

Can GNF + Sin-equivalence extend to cardinality, aggregates, FO(·) without breaking linear-time checkability?

Is the 66/505 checker OOM rate fundamental to replay-based checking, or an artefact fixable by streaming / sharing?
