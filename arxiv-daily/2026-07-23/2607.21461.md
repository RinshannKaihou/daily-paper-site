> **一句话总结：** BAAI 提出的 AREX 将深度研究建模为"递归自改进"过程：内层研究循环产出带置信度的候选答案，外层自改进循环按约束逐条审计并启动定向后续研究，配合一个自学的上下文更新工具压缩长程历史；其 10B 激活参数的 MoE 模型在 BrowseComp 上达 82.5、WideSearch-en 上达 82.0（全场最高），并在六项基准上整体超越同规模开源模型，能与激活参数大得多的前沿模型竞争。

> **TL;DR:** AREX reframes multi-constraint deep research as a Recursively Self-Improving (RSI) process built on a discovery–verification asymmetry: an inner loop gathers evidence and emits a confidence-scored provisional answer, while an outer loop audits it constraint-by-constraint and either accepts, refines, or restarts. A learned, self-invoked `update_context` tool compresses long interaction histories into a compact improvement state (verified findings + unresolved constraints + next-step plan). Trained via agentic mid-training plus step-aware RL with key-step reward shaping, the 10B-active-parameter 122B MoE (AREX-Base) reaches 82.5 on BrowseComp and the best reported 82.0 on WideSearch-en, beating Qwen3.5-397B and trailing only MiroThinker-H1 (88.2) on BrowseComp and GAIA; full-system ablation shows ACU + outer loop together add +22.9 BrowseComp points over the bare inner loop.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"递归自改进"深度研究范式**：基于"发现-验证不对称性"（发现一个同时满足多约束的答案昂贵，而逐约束验证候选答案可分解为简单检查），将验证从"事后过滤器"改造为"研究轮次之间的转移函数"——把部分验证的解递归地转化为更聚焦的下一个研究问题。
- **双层循环架构（AREX）**：内层研究循环（搜索/浏览/证据整合/构造候选答案并给出置信度）+ 外层自改进循环（按约束审计，依据置信度阈值 τ 决定 Accept / Refine / Restart），让验证成为主动控制信号而非终点过滤。
- **自学的上下文更新工具（ACU）**：模型自主决定何时调用 `update_context`，把不断膨胀的交互轨迹压缩为紧凑的"改进状态"，保留已验证发现、被拒候选、未决约束、下一步计划，丢弃冗余观测——区别于固定 token 阈值或外部模型做摘要的启发式做法。
- **多阶段智能体中期训练 + 步感知强化学习**：渐进式多轮能力训练（先浏览密集型工具使用，再推理密集型）+ 关键步骤聚焦监督（基于规则检测器标注"证据发现/路径否定重定向/关键上下文更新"步骤，仅对这些决策关键步骤施加损失）+ 步感知 GRPO（逐轮策略比、分层归一化、关键步奖励整形）。
- **双规模实例化与广泛评测**：稠密 4B（Turbo，Qwen3.5-4B 主干）与 122B-A10B MoE（Base，Qwen3.5-122B-A10B 主干），在 BrowseComp、WideSearch、DeepSearchQA、GAIA、xbench-2510、HLE 六个基准上系统超越同规模开源基线，部分指标上可与激活参数显著更大的前沿模型竞争。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulates deep research as Recursively Self-Improving (RSI) search**, grounded in a discovery–verification asymmetry: producing a joint-constraint-satisfying answer is costly, but verifying a candidate decomposes into cheap per-constraint checks. Verification is therefore promoted from a post-hoc filter into the transition function between research rounds, converting a partially verified solution into a sharper next research problem.
- **Introduces the AREX bi-level architecture**: an inner research loop (search/browse/integrate evidence, then emit a provisional answer with supporting evidence and a confidence score) plus an outer self-improvement loop (constraint-wise audit; decide Accept / Refine / Restart from a confidence threshold τ), with Refine preserving useful findings and Restart discarding a noisy trajectory.
- **Learns an Autonomous Context-Update (ACU) tool**: the model itself decides when to invoke `update_context` to collapse the trajectory into a compact improvement state that preserves verified findings + source IDs, rejected candidates, unresolved constraints, validity concerns, and the next-step plan — explicitly contrasted with fixed-token-threshold or external-summarizer heuristics.
- **Develops a multi-stage training recipe**: progressive multi-round capability training (browse-intensive tool use before reasoning-intensive), key-step focused supervision (offline, rule-based detectors flag evidence-discovery / path-rejection-and-redirection / key-context-update steps; loss applied only to those steps under matched budgets), and step-aware GRPO with hierarchical step-balanced normalization plus bounded key-step reward shaping.
- **Instantiates a dense 4B (Turbo) and a 122B-A10B MoE (Base)**, evaluating across BrowseComp, WideSearch-en, DeepSearchQA, GAIA, xbench-2510, and HLE-with-tools; AREX-Base achieves the best reported WideSearch-en score and stays competitive with frontier closed models.

## Methodology

**Bi-level recursive self-improvement.** Given a query x, AREX derives an initial research objective q⁽¹⁾. The **inner research loop** maintains an interaction trajectory hₜ⁽ᵏ⁾ = ⟨mᵢ, aᵢ, oᵢ⟩ (intermediate analysis, action, observation) and produces the next step from the research policy πθ(x, q⁽ᵏ⁾, ·). When the objective is sufficiently investigated it calls a structured `finish` interface that externalizes (provisional answer y⁽ᵏ⁾, supporting evidence E⁽ᵏ⁾, confidence score s⁽ᵏ⁾ ∈ [0,100]).

**Autonomous Context Updating (ACU).** At any point the model may call `update_context` to map the accumulated trajectory hₜ⁽ᵏ⁾ into a refreshed research state zₜ⁽ᵏ⁾ = fθ(hₜ⁽ᵏ⁾) that retains verified findings + source identifiers, current candidates, unresolved constraints, validity concerns, rejected candidates, and the next-step plan, while discarding redundant observations and obsolete plans. The effective context thereafter conditions only on z plus post-update steps, not the full history. Crucially the update is produced by AREX itself (not an external summarizer), organized around its current research objective.

**Outer self-improvement loop.** If s⁽ᵏ⁾ ≥ τ the answer is accepted. Otherwise a trajectory assessment g⁽ᵏ⁾ = (v⁽ᵏ⁾, P⁽ᵏ⁾, I⁽ᵏ⁾, q⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁾) is produced: v flags recoverability, P holds preserved information, I lists remaining issues, and q⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁾ is the next targeted objective. The decision rule is Accept if s ≥ τ, Refine if s < τ ∧ v = 1 (next round initialized from Refresh(·)), Restart if s < τ ∧ v = 0 (next round re-initialized from Init(x) only). The process is bounded by a maximum number of rounds and falls back to the highest-confidence completed answer.

**Training data.** Two stages: (1) recursive research task synthesis from real sources (web pages, literature, knowledge bases, repos) — each task extracts a latent answer y and a constraint set C(y) whose joint satisfaction uniquely identifies y, then abstracts constraints into indirect multi-hop descriptions; tasks with ambiguous answers, unverifiable constraints, or shallow-retrieval-solvable instances are filtered out. (2) Teacher trajectory collection with quality control — strong teachers roll out in AREX's tool environment; trajectories are filtered for genuine iteration, valid tool use, evidence-grounded answers reconstructable from collected evidence, and confidence ≥ τ_conf.

**Training pipeline.** (a) Progressive multi-round capability training: browse-intensive multi-round trajectories first (tool use, web navigation, evidence acquisition), then reasoning-intensive data (long-form thinking, hypothesis verification), then mixed-capability consolidation replaying difficult intermediate decisions plus capability-expanding academic/knowledge tasks. (b) Key-step focused supervision: rule-based detectors offline-annotate evidence-discovery steps, path-rejection-and-redirection steps, and key context-update steps; a step-level loss analysis confirms these annotated steps have materially higher loss (0.277 / 0.298 / 0.300 vs 0.232 for ordinary steps). Loss is applied only to the selected step while masking prefix/user/tool tokens. (c) Step-aware GRPO: turn-level policy ratio via geometric-mean length normalization, hierarchical averaging over steps then trajectories (to stop long trajectories dominating), KL penalty to a reference policy, and a bounded key-step bonus ẽ_{i,j} = 𝟙[Rᵢ > 0]·B_{i,j} added to the group-relative outcome advantage, so final-answer correctness remains the primary signal.

## Results & Highlights

- **Best reported WideSearch-en score (AREX-Base 82.0)**, ahead of Kimi-K2.6 (80.8), GPT-5.4 (77.5), DeepSeek-V4-Pro (78.0), and Gemini-3.1-Pro (66.4) — a head-to-head win against frontier closed and open systems on the broad-coverage retrieval/synthesis benchmark.
- **BrowseComp 82.5 (AREX-Base)**, trailing only MiroThinker-H1 (88.2) in Table 1 but beating Qwen3.5-397B (78.6), Kimi-K2.6 (83.2 comparable), DeepSeek-V4-Pro (83.4 comparable), and far above Qwen3.5-122B (63.8), Qwen3.5-35B (61.0), Quest-35B (64.6), and Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B (43.4); on GAIA AREX-Base reaches 85.4, beating Qwen3.5-397B (83.5) and Kimi-K2.6 (80.6).
- **DeepSearchQA 89.9 (AREX-Base)**, behind only Gemini-3.1-Pro (93.3) and beating GPT-5.4 (88.5), DeepSeek-V4-Pro (88.7), DeepSeek-V4-Flash (90.6), and Qwen3.5-397B (82.1).
- **ACU + outer loop together add +22.9 BrowseComp points** (Table 3): w/o ACU + w/o outer loop = 59.6; w/o ACU + w/ outer loop = 69.8 (+10.2); w/ ACU + w/o outer loop = 71.4 (+11.8 from ACU alone under matched single-round setting); full system = 82.5 (+11.1 from outer loop on top of ACU).
- **Key-step focused supervision is the single most important training component** (Table 4): replacing it with equal-budget random-step replay drops BrowseComp from 82.5 to 74.1 (−8.4, the largest degradation); replacing progressive multi-round capability training with mixed training drops to 77.5 (−5.0); replacing step-aware RL with standard GRPO drops to 79.4 (−3.1).
- **Confidence score is well-calibrated enough to drive the decision rule** (Figure 3): with ACU, 95.9% of correct final outputs land in the 90–100 confidence bin, while 55.2% of incorrect outputs fall below 60.
- **ACU is used proactively, not as a hard-limit safety net** (Table 2): invoked in 80.3% of BrowseComp episodes at a mean of 25,721 / median 25,386 tokens — far below the 128K cap — with only 0.01% of updates triggered at the limit; 66.9% of calls are for search-strategy revision, and ACU preserves unresolved constraints (95.5%) and next-step plans (96.4%) most consistently.
- **At the 4B scale, AREX-Turbo beats Qwen3.5-35B on 5 of 6 benchmarks** (BrowseComp 70.7 vs 61.0, DeepSearchQA 78.5 vs 68.5, WideSearch-en 68.5 vs 57.1, HLE 40.6 vs 47.4 lose, GAIA 81.6 vs 80.0, xbench 57.0 vs 50.3).

## Authors & Credibility

Authored by a 24-person team at the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI), including established researchers in retrieval, search, and foundation models (e.g., Zheng Liu, Zhongyuan Wang, Qiwei Ye, Kang Liu, Zhicheng Dou, Di He). BAAI is a reputable institution with a track record of large-scale model releases. The paper discloses both model variants with backbone attribution (Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B), publishes models on HuggingFace (BAAI/arex collection), and provides a GlobeApp plus project homepage — a level of artifact release above the average workshop report. Evaluation spans six recognized benchmarks (BrowseComp, WideSearch, DeepSearchQA, GAIA, xbench-DeepSearch-2510, HLE-with-tools) with explicit metric definitions (Item-F1 for WideSearch, F1 for DeepSearchQA, accuracy elsewhere).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — genuinely useful framing, not a wholly new primitive.** The discovery–verification asymmetry is a sound and clearly articulated motivation, and turning verification into an inter-round transition (rather than a post-hoc ranker) is a meaningful architectural contribution. However, the bi-level outer/inner loop and confidence-gated Accept/Refine/Restart pattern resembles prior self-refine and process-reward work; the distinctive piece is really the *autonomous, model-internal* `update_context` tool and its tight coupling to the outer loop.
- **Rigor — strong controlled ablations, but confined to BrowseComp.** Table 3 and Table 4 isolate ACU, the outer loop, progressive training, key-step supervision, and step-aware RL under matched budgets — the methodology is taken seriously. The weakness is that every single ablation is run only on BrowseComp; the paper never shows whether, say, the +22.9-point ACU/outer-loop gain replicates on GAIA, HLE, or DeepSearchQA, so the generality of the mechanism is under-evidenced.
- **Reproducibility — partial.** Models are released on HuggingFace, which is commendable. But several load-bearing hyperparameters are under-specified: the confidence threshold τ (the literal gate of the whole system), the maximum number of outer rounds, τ_conf for teacher filtering, λ_key for reward shaping, and the sizes of the synthetic task/trajectory datasets are all either absent or only gestured at. The "300 inner turns + 5 outer operations" inference budget is given but training compute is not reported at all.
- **Significance — high if the gains are real at the claimed scale.** A 10B-active MoE beating a 397B dense Qwen on five benchmarks and achieving the best WideSearch-en score is a strong efficiency result. The step-aware RL + key-step supervision recipe (−8.4 from removing the latter) is a clean, transferable training insight.
- **Red flags — (a) headline-vs-table discrepancy on BrowseComp.** Figure 1's BrowseComp leaderboard conveniently lists Kimi-K2.6 (83.2) and AREX-Base (82.5) at the top while omitting MiroThinker-H1, which Table 1 reveals actually scores 88.2 on BrowseComp and 88.5 on GAIA — both above AREX-Base. This is a presentation choice that flatters AREX. (b) HLE column mixes full-HLE results (asterisked: GPT-5.4 52.1, Opus-4.6 53.0, Gemini-3.1-Pro 51.4, Kimi-K2.6 54.0) with text-only-subset results for everyone else including AREX-Base (52.4), so cross-row HLE comparisons are not strictly apples-to-apples. (c) Several baselines are conspicuously weak at this scale (Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B at 43.4 BrowseComp; Quest-35B at 37.2 HLE) which inflates the "outperforms baselines" narrative. (d) No ablation on synthetic-data scale/quality, and no analysis of failure modes when the confidence score is wrong (the 33.0% of incorrect outputs still landing in the 90–100 bin under ACU is a non-trivial miscalibration the paper glosses over).

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated theoretical framing (discovery–verification asymmetry) that directly motivates each architectural component rather than being decorative.
- The autonomous, model-internal `update_context` tool is a genuinely good idea: replacing fixed-token-threshold heuristics with objective-aligned, self-timed state compression is a real advance for long-horizon agents, and the runtime analysis (80.3% usage at ~25K tokens, far below the 128K cap) empirically shows it is used as a research operation, not a memory crutch.
- Key-step focused supervision is the strongest single training result (−8.4 when ablated) and is well-justified by the step-level loss analysis showing annotated steps are 19–29% harder to learn than ordinary steps.
- Strong efficiency story: a 10B-active MoE competitive with 397B dense models and frontier closed systems, plus a 4B model that beats its 35B backbone sibling on 5/6 benchmarks.
- Artifacts released (HuggingFace models, GlobeApp, homepage), making the work verifiable in principle.

## Limitations

- All ablations are BrowseComp-only; the magnitude and even the direction of component contributions on other benchmarks (GAIA, HLE, DeepSearchQA, xbench) are unknown.
- Several critical hyperparameters undisclosed: confidence threshold τ, max outer rounds, λ_key, τ_conf, dataset sizes, training compute/FLOPs.
- MiroThinker-H1 beats AREX-Base on the two flagship deep-search benchmarks (BrowseComp 88.2 vs 82.5; GAIA 88.5 vs 85.4), so "substantially outperforms comparable-scale baselines" is only true for a selected subset of baselines — AREX is competitive, not dominant.
- HLE comparisons conflate full-HLE and text-only-subset protocols.
- Confidence-score miscalibration is non-trivial: even with ACU, 33.0% of incorrect outputs carry confidence 90–100, and the paper does not analyze what happens when the outer loop trusts these wrong high-confidence answers.
- The "verified synthetic tasks" pipeline depends on the quality of human-authored templates and rule-based key-step detectors; no sensitivity analysis is provided for how detector precision or task-distribution shift affects the trained policy.
- No analysis of inference cost: 300 inner turns × up to 5 outer rounds per episode is a large budget, and no latency/token-cost comparison against single-pass baselines is reported.

## Relevance

高度相关。AREX 直接命中深度研究智能体（deep research agent）、长程强化学习（long-horizon RL）与智能体上下文/记忆管理三个研究方向，其"递归自改进 + 自学上下文更新 + 关键步骤监督"的训练范式对构建可靠的长程研究型 Agent 具有直接的方法论参考价值，也呼应了用户对 agentic RL 与推理时扩展（inference-time scaling）的兴趣。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the ACU and outer-loop gains (Table 3) replicate on GAIA, HLE, and DeepSearchQA, or is the +22.9-point effect BrowseComp-specific? A per-benchmark ablation table is the most important missing evidence.
- How sensitive is performance to the confidence threshold τ, to λ_key, and to the precision/recall of the offline key-step detectors? Is there a regime where a miscalibrated τ causes the outer loop to over-Restart and waste budget?
- Given that 33% of incorrect outputs still score 90–100 in confidence under ACU, what would a calibration-aware Accept/Refine/Restart rule (e.g., ensemble over multiple finish calls, or external verifier) buy in accuracy?
- How does AREX's inference cost (tokens, latency, $/query at 300 inner turns × 5 outer rounds) compare against a single-pass Qwen3.5-397B or Kimi-K2.6 baseline on the same benchmarks?
- Can the key-step-focused supervision recipe transfer to non-research agentic domains (coding agents, web-navigation agents), where "decisive step" has a different operational definition?
- How robust is the synthetic-task pipeline to distribution shift at test time — e.g., on BrowseComp-style questions whose constraint structure differs from the templates used during task synthesis?
