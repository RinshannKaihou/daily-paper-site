Why simple baselines still win at 3D point-cloud segmentation under class imbalance.

02 Why this matters

Uniform cross-entropy is a safe default for point-based 3D LiDAR segmentation — and this is the first mechanistic explanation for why 2D-proven imbalance methods barely transfer.

03 Critical edge

The "CE is enough" verdict may rest on under-tuned specialized baselines.

  • Single fixed hyperparameters — Focal γ=1, LDAM, LADJ τ=0.3, CB β=0.9 — never swept per dataset.
  • Collapses may be tuning artifacts: invf & BalSoft drop to 67–68% on DALES, but were not re-tuned.
  • Mechanism only checked on KPConv — no voxel (Cylinder3D) or transformer architectures.

Methodology

A broad benchmark of 11 methods across two imbalance regimes, fused with three mechanistic lenses — confusion matrices, decision-boundary variability, and loss-landscape topology — that converge on one explanation.

What was evaluated

Six reweighting schemes on the per-class CE weight w_c: uniform (baseline), inverse-frequency N/n_c, class-balanced (1−β)/(1−β^n_c) (β=0.9), inverse-log, inverse-power 1/n_c^γ (γ=0.1), complementary-frequency.

Five loss functions: Focal Loss (γ=1), LDAM (margin Δ_c ∝ n_c^(−1/4)), Logit-Adjustment (τ=0.3), Balanced-Softmax, Seesaw Loss.

Architectures: KPConv and RandLA-Net. Datasets: DALES (outdoor aerial LiDAR, 8 classes, 641:1 extreme) and S3DIS (indoor, 13 classes, 56:1 moderate). Seed 42, 10-vote test-time aggregation.

The three mechanistic analyses

  • Confusion matrix — precision/recall plus majority→minority and minority→majority error rates, exposing the precision-recall trade-off.
  • Decision-boundary variability (DB-var) — fraction of test points whose prediction differs from uniform CE; high DB-var ⇒ boundaries heavily altered.
  • Loss-landscape topology — (i) flatness via filter-normalized weight perturbations (K=20 directions); (ii) top-10 Hessian eigenvalues via Lanczos to characterize local curvature.

The mechanism · data sets the landscape

The decisive finding: Hessian eigenvalue spectra are near-identical across all methods within each dataset — the geometry is set by the data, not the loss formulation. Extreme imbalance carves a narrow basin; moderate imbalance leaves a flat plateau. Either way, loss-level edits cannot escape it.

Strengths & limitations · side by side

Strengths

  • First mechanistic explanation for the 2D→3D imbalance-method gap — far beyond a benchmark-and-rank paper.
  • Three converging lenses (confusion matrix, decision boundaries, Hessian) mutually reinforce one conclusion.
  • Practitioner-actionable: uniform CE is a safe default; mild methods (invp, LDAM) give small safe gains; aggressive ones (invf, BalSoft) are risky — all with quantified deltas.
  • Honest scoping — explicitly names voxel/transformer architectures and combined data+loss strategies as future work.
  • Stability-checked — multi-seed and dual-architecture validation for the core performance claim.

Limitations

  • Under-tuned specialized losses — single fixed hyperparameters (Focal γ=1, LDAM, LADJ τ=0.3, CB β=0.9), never swept per dataset; some collapses may be tuning artifacts.
  • Mechanistic analyses only on KPConv — DB-var, Hessian, and perturbation are not verified on RandLA-Net, weakening the generality claim.
  • No voxel/transformer architectures (Cylinder3D, MinkowskiNet, Point Transformer) — the "fails to transfer to 3D" conclusion is strictly about point-based 3D.
  • Single-run per method (3-seed check on only 2 methods) — no error bars; S3DIS's 1.93% range is within plausible run-to-run variance.
  • Correlational, not causal — the implicit-balancing hypothesis (local aggregation densifies minority classes) is proposed but not ablated.

Open questions for further work

  1. Do voxel-based (Cylinder3D, MinkowskiNet) and transformer-based (Point Transformer) architectures show the same narrow-basin / flat-plateau topology, or does regular-grid attention change imbalance-method effectiveness?
  2. Would per-dataset hyperparameter tuning of Focal/LDAM/LADJ/CB reverse the DALES collapses of invf and BalSoft — or is the precision-collapse trade-off fundamental?
  3. Can the implicit-balancing hypothesis be isolated by ablating the local-feature aggregation module in KPConv / RandLA-Net?
  4. Does combining data-level resampling with loss-level mitigation escape the landscape constraints, or do both converge to the same basin?
  5. Are the Hessian-eigenvalue and DB-var signatures predictive on larger outdoor benchmarks (SemanticKITTI, nuScenes) where class counts and point densities differ?
  6. Does 10-vote test-time aggregation interact with imbalance methods in ways that mask or amplify true per-method differences?
