> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 APEX 框架，通过设计多项式 GNN 架构 PolyGIN（用 PolyActivation 与 PolyScaleNorm 替换 ReLU 和标准归一化层），使模型输出对输入特征保持有界多项式形式，从而用 $2^{L-1}$ 个 Gauss–Legendre 求积点对 Aumann–Shapley 归因路径积分进行**精确（截断误差为零）**计算，在五个图基准上以仅 8 个求值点（4 层网络）达到 FP32 数值精度，并显著优于 GNNExplainer、PGExplainer、FlowX、IG 等基线的归因保真度。

> **TL;DR:** APEX co-designs a polynomial GNN (PolyGIN) with the attribution algorithm: by replacing ReLU/standard-norm with PolyActivation and PolyScaleNorm, the pre-softmax logit becomes a bounded-degree multivariate polynomial, so the Aumann–Shapley / Integrated-Gradients path integral can be evaluated *exactly* (zero quadrature error) with a fixed $2^{L-1}$-point Gauss–Legendre rule. On five graph benchmarks (BA-Shapes, BBBP, BACE, Graph-SST2, Mutagenicity), PolyGIN matches standard GIN accuracy within ~1 point while APEX reaches FP32 completeness error at only 8 evaluation points and beats GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, GradCAM, FlowX, and IG on Fidelity+ / Fidelity−.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 **PolyGIN**，一种保留 GIN 风格邻域聚合、但用多项式/线性算子（PolyActivation $z+\theta\odot z^2$、PolyScaleNorm $s\odot z$）替换 ReLU 与标准归一化层的图网络，使标量得分（pre-softmax logit）成为输入节点特征的有界多元多项式。
- 证明 **度上界定理**：对含 $L$ 个多项式变换块的 PolyGIN，输出多项式度数至多 $2^L$，沿归因路径的偏导数度数至多 $2^L-1$，因此 $m=2^{L-1}$ 点 Gauss–Legendre 求积即可精确积分。
- 提出 **APEX 框架**，将 GNN 路径归因从「依赖采样分辨率 $N_\text{step}$ 的数值近似」转变为「架构确定的有限精确计算」，彻底消除截断误差（仅需 FP32 浮点舍入），相对传统 IG 的 $\Theta(N_\text{step})$ 求值开销给出 $2^{L-1}$ 点的常数级预算。
- 通过**可加聚合**将特征级归因扩展为带符号的节点级归因，并在定理上保持完备性（$\sum_v\Phi_v = f(X)-f(X')$）。
- 在五个图基准上系统评估：预测性能、保真度（Fidelity+/-）、效率与完备性误差，并给出 PolyGIN 在 1-WL 框架下的表达能力分析（附录 B）。

## Key Contributions

- **PolyGIN architecture**: a GIN variant whose message-passing, normalization (PolyScaleNorm), and transformation (PolyActivation) preserve a bounded multivariate polynomial form on scalar scores, replacing ReLU and standard normalization with polynomial/linear operators.
- **Provable degree bound (Theorem 1)**: for an $L$-block PolyGIN, the output degree is at most $2^L$ and the attribution-path partial derivative degree is at most $2^L-1$, which is exactly the regime where Gauss–Legendre quadrature is exact.
- **APEX co-design framework**: converts path attribution from an $N_\text{step}$-dependent numerical approximation into a finite exact computation using $2^{L-1}$ deterministic evaluation points, eliminating quadrature truncation error under exact arithmetic.
- **Completeness-preserving node aggregation**: signed node attributions are obtained by summing feature attributions over a node's features, with $\sum_v\Phi_v = f(X)-f(X')$ guaranteed up to floating-point precision.
- **Empirical validation** on five benchmarks spanning synthetic (BA-Shapes), molecular (BBBP, BACE, Mutagenicity), and text-graph (Graph-SST2) domains, plus a WL-expressivity analysis (Appendix B) clarifying that exactness and 1-WL discriminative power are independent architectural properties.

## Methodology

APEX is a **model–attribution co-design**: instead of approximating the Aumann–Shapley path integral on an arbitrary nonlinear GNN, the architecture is constrained so the integral becomes exactly computable.

**Attribution problem.** For a trained graph model $f$ producing a scalar pre-softmax logit, input features $X$, and baseline $X'$, the straight-line path is $\gamma(\tau)=X'+\tau(X-X')$ and the Aumann–Shapley / Integrated-Gradients attribution of feature $x_i$ is $\phi_i=(x_i-x'_i)\int_0^1 \frac{\partial f(\gamma(\tau))}{\partial x_i}\,d\tau$. A key design choice is attributing the **logit, not the post-softmax probability**, because softmax is non-polynomial and would break the exactness guarantee.

**PolyGIN building blocks.**
- *PolyActivation*: $\sigma_\text{poly}(z)=z+\theta\odot z^2$ — an identity path plus a learnable quadratic interaction, keeping the map polynomial.
- *PolyScaleNorm*: $\text{Norm}_\text{poly}(z)=s\odot z$ — a learnable per-dimension linear scaling that avoids the data-dependent division of standard normalization (which would destroy polynomiality) and helps control magnitude growth.
- *PolyGIN layer*: GIN injective sum-aggregation $a_v=(1+\epsilon)h_v+\sum_{u\in N(v)}h_u$, followed by $h_v=W_2\,\sigma_\text{poly}(\text{Norm}_\text{poly}(W_1 a_v))$.

**Degree bound (Theorem 1).** By induction: $h^{(0)}$ has degree 1; aggregation, linear maps, and PolyScaleNorm are degree-preserving; PolyActivation maps degree $d$ to at most $2d$. After $L$ blocks the output degree is $\le 2^L$, so $\partial y/\partial x_i$ has degree $\le 2^L-1$ along the affine path, making $g_i(\tau)$ a univariate polynomial of degree $\le 2^L-1$.

**Exact quadrature.** An $m$-point Gauss–Legendre rule integrates every polynomial of degree $\le 2m-1$ exactly on $[-1,1]$; mapping to $[0,1]$ and choosing $m=2^{L-1}$ yields the closed-form attribution $\phi_i=\frac{x_i-x'_i}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{L-1}} w_k\,\frac{\partial f(X'+\frac{t_k+1}{2}(X-X'))}{\partial x_i}$, exact up to FP32 roundoff.

**Node-level aggregation.** $\Phi_v=\sum_{i\in F_v}\phi_i$ is kept as a *signed* score (positive = supporting evidence for the target class, negative = opposing), and summation over nodes equals summation over all features, preserving completeness.

## Results & Highlights

- **Predictive parity with GIN (Table 1, mean ± std over 10 seeds).** PolyGIN matches a standard GIN at nearly identical capacity (only +0.12%–0.17% parameters):
  - BACE: PolyGIN **84.34±1.86%** vs GIN 83.62±1.19% (PolyGIN *higher*).
  - BBBP: PolyGIN **89.85±0.81%** vs GIN 89.90±1.09% (Δ ≈ −0.05).
  - Graph-SST2: PolyGIN **90.23±0.17%** vs GIN 90.95±0.20% (Δ ≈ −0.72).
  - BA-Shapes: PolyGIN 96.43±0.71% vs GIN 97.29±0.77% (Δ ≈ −0.86).
  - Mutagenicity: PolyGIN 80.98±0.61% vs GIN 81.78±0.40% (Δ ≈ −0.80). All gaps are below 1 point.

- **Exactness with a tiny evaluation budget (Fig. 5, completeness error vs NFE).** For the 4-block PolyGIN used, Theorem 1 gives an integrand degree bound of $2^4-1=15$, so $2^{4-1}=8$ Gauss–Legendre points suffice. APEX's completeness error drops **directly to the FP32 numerical-precision limit with only 8 evaluations**, whereas standard IG, Trapezoidal-IG, and Simpson-IG still show visible error at 8–32 points and only approach machine precision at hundreds of points (the gap spans several orders of magnitude on the log-scale plots across all five datasets).

- **Higher attribution fidelity than baselines (Figs. 3 & 4).** Against GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, GradCAM, FlowX, and 50-point IG on the same correctly-classified test graphs, APEX achieves **higher Fidelity+** (selected nodes more *necessary* — bigger drop when removed) and **lower Fidelity−** (selected nodes more *sufficient* — better preserve the prediction when kept alone) across sparsity levels 0.5–0.9 on BBBP, BACE, Graph-SST2, Mutagenicity, and BA-Shapes.

- **Quadrature-error = completeness-error equivalence (Appendix C).** The paper proves that for Riemann-sum IG the completeness error equals the numerical quadrature error, formally grounding why APEX's exact quadrature exactly recovers the completeness property of continuous Aumann–Shapley attribution.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine):** The core move — constraining the full input-to-logit map to bounded-degree polynomial form so Gauss–Legendre quadrature is *provably exact* — is a clean, well-articulated co-design idea that is distinct from polynomial graph filters (Chebyshev propagation) and from Π-Nets (which do not exploit degree for attribution). The separation of "exactness guarantee" vs "1-WL expressivity" in Appendix B is an honest and non-trivial clarification.
- **Rigor (strong on theory, lighter on empirical breadth):** Theorem 1 and the completeness-error proof (Appendix C) are solid. However, the degree bound is an *upper* bound; the paper does not report the *realized* degree empirically, so the actual required quadrature size could in principle be smaller than $2^{L-1}$. No test of whether $m<2^{L-1}$ already achieves machine precision is provided.
- **Reproducibility (mixed):** Good signs — fixed seeds (10 runs), hidden dim 300, batch 32, RTX 4090, dataset-specific LR/epochs in Table 1, and the GraphFramEx protocol. But several hyperparameters that matter for magnitude stability (PolyScaleNorm initialization, the $\theta$ init of PolyActivation, how "moderate" $L$ must be) are under-specified, and no code/release link is given in the manuscript body.
- **Significance (promising but conditional):** The exactness guarantee is the strongest selling point, but it is strictly conditional on (a) the polynomial architecture and (b) attributing a pre-softmax logit. It **does not transfer** to ReLU GNNs, softmax probabilities, batch norm, or attention softmax — explicitly acknowledged by the authors. Because PolyActivation is only quadratic, expressiveness per layer is limited, and the framework's applicability to deeper/larger architectures is unclear given the paper's own warning about magnitude growth and the use of only $L=4$ blocks.
- **Red flags / cherry-picking concerns:**
  - The **strongest fidelity baselines are not the strongest possible**: only a single IG setting (50 points) is compared, and modern Shapley- and GNNExplainer-derivatives (e.g., SubgraphX, RG-Explainer, GraphMASK) are omitted; the "state-of-the-art" claim rests largely on FlowX and PGExplainer.
  - **Fidelity metrics caveat:** the authors themselves note removal-based Fidelity+/− can be distorted by distribution shift from graph perturbation, so the fidelity wins should be read as comparative model-faithfulness, not semantic correctness — a caveat they handle responsibly.
  - **No comparison against exact-but-different attribution** (e.g., Hesse et al.'s single-pass method for homogeneous nets), so it is unclear where APEX sits on a cost/exactness Pareto frontier.
  - Predictive gaps are small but consistently *slightly worse* than GIN on 4/5 datasets, so "competitive" is accurate but not a free lunch.

## Strengths

- A clear, provable exactness guarantee with a concrete, deterministic evaluation budget ($2^{L-1}$ points) — no $N_\text{step}$ sweep, no convergence tuning.
- Completeness-preserving *signed* node attributions give directional (supporting/opposing) information that nonnegative importance scores cannot.
- Minimal capacity overhead (+0.12%–0.17% parameters) and essentially no accuracy loss relative to GIN.
- Honest scoping: the paper is explicit about what the guarantee covers (polynomial architecture, logit attribution) and what it does not (arbitrary GNNs, softmax outputs).
- Clean separation of three architectural concerns: polynomial exactness, 1-WL expressivity, and magnitude stability.

## Limitations

- **Architecture-restricted:** exactness holds only for polynomial GNNs; it does not apply to standard ReLU/attention/softmax GNNs. Adopting APEX means retraining with PolyGIN.
- **Expressiveness ceiling:** PolyActivation is purely quadratic (degree-2) and only $L=4$ blocks are used; deeper PolyGINs face exponential degree growth and potential numerical instability (the paper flags this but offers no principled remedy beyond PolyScaleNorm).
- **Baseline coverage:** omits several strong contemporary GNN explainers and uses only one IG configuration; "state-of-the-art" fidelity claims would be stronger with a broader slate and with exact-Shapley methods.
- **No semantic ground-truth validation:** exactness is mathematical completeness, not agreement with human-interpretable explanations — explicitly a non-goal, but it limits practical trust.
- **No released code** mentioned in the main text, and some stability-critical hyperparameters are under-specified.

## Relevance

对于关注 **GNN 可解释性 / XAI** 与 **Aumann–Shapley 归因 / Integrated Gradients** 的研究者，本文提供了一条「架构-归因协同设计」的全新精确化路径，其度上界定理与完备性误差等价证明具有方法论参考价值；若你的工作涉及分子图、文本图等结构化数据的特征/节点级归因，APEX 的 PolyGIN + 精确求积思路值得作为基线或改进对象。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the *realized* (not just upper-bound) polynomial degree of a trained PolyGIN be measured post-hoc, enabling an adaptive, potentially smaller Gauss–Legendre budget than $2^{L-1}$?
- How does APEX scale to deeper or attention-based graph architectures (e.g., GAT, GraphTransformer) — can polynomial attention or higher-degree PolyActivation recover expressiveness without exploding the quadrature budget?
- How do APEX attributions align with human/chemical ground truth (e.g., known toxicophores in Mutagenicity, functional groups in BBBP), beyond the model-faithfulness Fidelity metrics?
- What is the cost/exactness trade-off versus Hesse et al.'s single-pass axiomatic attribution for positively homogeneous networks, and can the two approaches be combined?
- Is the small but consistent accuracy drop versus GIN fundamental to the quadratic-only activation, or can learnable higher-degree or piecewise-polynomial activations close the gap while preserving exactness?
