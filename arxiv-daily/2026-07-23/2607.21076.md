> **一句话总结：** C-PTQ 通过对 SFT 损失做二阶泰勒展开、用对角 Fisher 信息矩阵作为 Hessian 的可计算近似，把"任务敏感性"与"通道级缩放因子"对齐到同一维度，在 Qwen2.5-VL / InternVL2 / LLaVA-OV 上以纯 CWS 流程在 W3A16/W4A8/W4A6 设置下达到 SOTA，且无需 LoRA。

> **TL;DR:** C-PTQ fixes a fundamental misalignment in multimodal LLM post-training quantization: prior methods (MBQ, QIG, MASQuant) measure sensitivity at the modality/token level but optimize scaling per channel. By deriving a Fisher-weighted channel-wise MSE from the second-order Taylor expansion of the SFT loss (using a diagonal Fisher matrix as a tractable Hessian approximation), C-PTQ aligns importance and scaling on the channel axis. On Qwen2.5VL-32B W3A16 it reaches avg 77.0 (-0.9% vs FP16) vs AWQ 76.4 (-1.7%), and on Qwen2.5VL-7B W4A6 it reaches 72.4 (-9.2%) vs the strongest baseline MASQuant 69.0 (-13.4%) and MBQ 63.1 (-20.8%) — without LoRA, at MBQ-level runtime (43s + 10m42s vs QIG's 41min).

## 核心贡献

- 揭示了一个根本性的"维度错位"问题：现有 MLLM PTQ 方法（MBQ 在 modality 级、QIG 在 token 级、MASQuant/Jia hybrid 级）在调制级别上度量敏感性，却在 channel 级搜索缩放因子，导致优化目标与重要性度量本身不一致。
- 在理论上把 SFT 损失扰动 ΔL 与通道级量化误差显式连接：基于二阶 Taylor 展开，丢掉一阶项（OBD 局部最优假设），用经验 Fisher 信息矩阵近似 Hessian，从而把复杂的损失扰动化为对角加权的逐通道 MSE。
- 通过对角近似将 dense Fisher 降为 channel-wise 权重，避免了大矩阵的 O(N²) 开销；实验证明对角化版本性能与 dense 版本持平甚至略好，但耗时从 ~50min 降到 ~11min。
- 一个统一、轻量、无 LoRA 的 PTQ 框架，在 weight-only（W3A16）和 weight-activation（W4A8、W4A6）两个设定下跨 Qwen2.5VL / InternVL2 / LLaVA-OV 三大模型族均取得 SOTA。
- 大量消融揭示了 C-PTQ 对校准集子集（COCO128a vs COCO128b 几乎一致）、校准样本数量（64/128/256 波动 ±0.3%）、以及校准模态（多模态 COCO vs 纯文本 Pile）都高度鲁棒，并扩展到 Llama2-7B / Llama3-8B 纯 LLM 量化与 2B–72B 模型规模。

## Key Contributions

- Identifies and attacks a fundamental dimension-mismatch in MLLM PTQ: prior channel-wise-scaling (CWS) methods (MBQ modality-level, QIG token-level, MASQuant/Jia hybrid) measure sensitivity along non-channel axes while the per-channel scaling search itself lives on the channel axis, producing a structural misalignment.
- Theoretically bridges task-loss perturbation ΔL with per-channel quantization error: starting from the second-order Taylor expansion of the SFT loss, dropping the first-order term under the OBD local-optimum assumption, and approximating the Hessian with the empirical Fisher Information Matrix, yielding a clean element-wise Fisher-weighted residual.
- A practical diagonal simplification of the Fisher matrix turns the dense O(N²) computation into channel-wise scalar weights; ablations show the diagonal version matches or slightly beats the dense version (W3A16 avg 74.3 vs 73.5 on Qwen2.5VL-7B) while cutting runtime from ~50 min to ~11 min.
- A unified, LoRA-free, grid-search-only CWS framework that achieves SOTA across weight-only (W3A16) and weight-activation (W4A8, W4A6) settings on Qwen2.5VL, InternVL2, and LLaVA-OV.
- Extensive robustness ablations: nearly identical results on two COCO subsets, ±0.3% fluctuation across 64/128/256 calibration samples, graceful behaviour on text-only Pile calibration (only 0.2–0.5 avg drop), and clean transfer to pure LLMs (Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B) and to 2B–72B model scales.

## Methodology

**Motivation.** Through visualization (Fig. 1) the authors show that the round-to-nearest squared quantization error and the squared SFT gradients along the channel axis are misaligned: the ~3200-th channel in Qwen2.5VL-7B has near-zero quantization error but very large gradient. Thus minimizing ∥ΔY∥² (vanilla AWQ) is not the same as minimizing downstream task degradation.

**From loss perturbation to Fisher-weighted residual.** For a linear layer with output Y = WX, quantized output Ŷ = Y + ΔY, and SFT loss L, the second-order Taylor expansion is

  L(Ŷ) ≈ L(Y) + g⊤ΔY + ½ ΔY⊤HΔY,

where g = ∂L/∂Y and H = ∂²L/∂Y². Under the OBD local-optimum assumption the expected gradient g ≈ 0, so the loss perturbation ΔL ≈ ½ ΔY⊤HΔY. The authors approximate the Hessian with the empirical Fisher Information Matrix F = E[gᵢgᵢ⊤] computed on the calibration set, which intrinsically encodes global task-loss sensitivity (unlike GPTQ's activation-derived H = XX⊤).

**Diagonal simplification.** The dense Fisher is expensive, so they take only the diagonal, fⱼ = E_{:,j}[gᵢⱼ²], assigning a single Fisher weight fⱼ to all tokens within channel j. After mean-normalization (fⱼ = fⱼ / f̄) to fix the magnitude gap between gradient² and MSE, the loss perturbation reduces to an element-wise weighted residual:

  ΔL ≈ ½ Σᵢ ∥ √f ⊙ Δyᵢ ∥₂².

**Unified channel-wise scaling.** Substituting the AWQ-style scaled residual ΔY = WX − Q(W·s)·(s⁻¹·X) into the Fisher-weighted objective gives the final optimization target:

  min_s ∥ √f · (WX − Q(W·s)·(s⁻¹·X)) ∥₂²  (weight-only),

or with weight-activation quantization:

  min_s ∥ √f · (WX − Q(W·s)·Q(s⁻¹·X)) ∥₂².

**Algorithm.** Three steps: (1) compute per-channel diagonal Fisher weights from output gradients; (2) initialize channel-wise scales from activation magnitudes as s = x₀^α, ..., x_Nc^α; (3) run a 40-step grid search over α minimizing the Fisher-weighted MSE. Code released at https://github.com/renaissanceee/cptq.

**Calibration.** 128 image-caption pairs from COCO captions (the ShareGPT4V subset), fixed seed 42, same subset for all baselines.

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen2.5VL-32B, W3A16 (Table 1):** C-PTQ reaches avg 77.0 (-0.9% vs FP16=77.7), beating MBQ at 76.6 (-1.4%) and AWQ at 76.4 (-1.7%) — a 0.6-point average gain on the largest weight-only setting where competitor gaps are typically sub-0.3.
- **Qwen2.5VL-7B, W4A6 (Table 1, aggressive weight-activation):** C-PTQ reaches avg 72.4 (-9.2%) vs MASQuant 69.0 (-13.4%) vs QIG 67.7 (-15.1%) vs MBQ 63.1 (-20.8%) — a 3.4-point avg gain and a 9.3-point gain over MBQ. On MMMU specifically, C-PTQ scores 41.8 vs SQ's catastrophic 29.0 (Δ +12.8) and MBQ's 38.4.
- **InternVL2-8B, W4A6 (Table 2):** C-PTQ avg 71.6 (-7.1%) vs MBQ 70.8 (-8.2%) vs QIG 67.2 (-12.8%) — the only method to stay above 70 avg under aggressive activation quantization.
- **LLaVA-OV-7B, W3A16 (Table 2):** C-PTQ is the only CWS method to beat AWQ here, with avg 72.2 (-2.4%) vs AWQ 71.5 (-3.4%) vs QIG 72.1 (-3.6%).
- **Efficiency (Table 3, Qwen2.5VL-7B W3A16):** C-PTQ takes 43s (gradient collection) + 10m42s (CWS search) — essentially identical to MBQ (40s + 10m42s) and dramatically faster than QIG (41min15s, owing to its 32-step integrated-gradient integration), while delivering better accuracy.
- **Diagonal vs dense Fisher (Table 6, Qwen2.5VL-7B W3A16, 4-benchmark avg):** diagonal 74.3 in 11 min vs dense 73.5 in 50 min — the cheap diagonal is actually 0.8 point better at ~5× the speed.
- **GPTQ comparison (Table 5):** on Qwen2.5VL-7B, GPTQ scores 71.5 avg (-10.3%) — worse than even naive RTN at 75.4 (-5.4%) — while C-PTQ scores 77.1 (-3.3%), corroborating the central thesis that activation-derived Hessian objectives misalign with task loss.
- **Calibration robustness (Table 4):** COCO128a vs COCO128b give 77.1 vs 77.0 (W3A16); varying sample count 64/128/256 fluctuates within ±0.3%; switching to text-only Pile only costs 0.2–0.5 avg, whereas modality-aware MBQ cannot use text-only calibration at all.
- **LLM transfer (Table 8, Llama3-8B W4A16):** C-PTQ avg 69.9 vs AWQ 69.5, with PPL 6.49 vs 6.53 and ARC-challenge 50.3 vs 49.9 — confirming the framework generalizes beyond MLLMs.
- **Scale generalization (Fig. 6):** SOTA maintained at 2B (Qwen2VL-2B) and 72B (Qwen2VL-72B) under W3A16.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core insight — that channel-wise scaling should be guided by channel-wise importance, and that a diagonal Fisher matrix is the right tractable proxy for task-loss sensitivity — is genuinely clean and well-motivated. The dimension-mismatch framing (Fig. 2) is sharp. However, Fisher-weighted quantization objectives and the OBD framework are classical (LeCun et al. 1989), so the novelty lies in the specific diagonal-Fisher + CWS combination rather than in any single component; the paper is honest about this and explicitly differentiates from Supernode (Cherilyn & Safaai 2026), which uses Fisher for pruning rather than quantization, and from mixed-precision schemes like High-Impact-Ratio (Pham et al. 2026).

**Rigor.** The empirical study is unusually thorough: three model families, three quantization settings (W3A16, W4A8, W4A6), eight benchmarks, calibration-subset/sample-size/modality ablations, diagonal-vs-dense Fisher, first-order-term ablation, RTN/GPTQ comparison, and pure-LLM transfer. The diagonal-approximation ablation is the most convincing piece — showing the cheap version matches dense Fisher undermines a likely reviewer critique.

**Reproducibility.** Strong: fixed seed 42, public LMMs-Eval harness, COCO/ShareGPT4V calibration (all public), per-model GPU counts given, code released. One minor gap: the Fisher weights require backpropagating the SFT loss to the layer output on the calibration set — the exact SFT loss formulation and whether the calibration captions are captioned by the model itself or from ground-truth COCO captions is not crisply stated.

**Significance.** Real but bounded. The improvements over the strongest CWS baselines are often in the 0.1–0.6 avg-point range in W3A16 (e.g., Qwen2.5VL-7B W3A16: 77.1 vs MBQ 77.0 — a 0.1 gap that is within noise), so the headline SOTA claim is mostly supported by the harder W4A6 regime and the larger 32B model, where gaps widen to 0.5–3.4 points. The claim is honest that AWQ remains "a highly competitive baseline under multimodal calibration data", which somewhat tempers the practical impact at the low-compression end.

**Red flags / weak points.** (1) On Qwen2.5VL-7B W3A16, C-PTQ's 0.1-point gain over MBQ is below plausible evaluation noise, yet is bolded as SOTA — readers should weight the W4A6 and 32B results more. (2) SQ and MBQ swap ranking across model families (SQ > MBQ on Qwen2.5VL, MBQ > SQ on LLaVA-OV), which suggests baselines are unstable and somewhat model-dependent — a single SOTA winner should be read cautiously. (3) All quantization is restricted to the LLM decoder; vision encoder and projection stay full precision, so the practical memory/latency gains are partial. (4) No results on extreme W4A4 — the authors explicitly flag this as a limitation requiring future compensation. (5) No wall-clock or memory end-to-end inference comparison against the LoRA-based methods (MASQuant, Zhong et al.); only quantization-time cost is reported, so the "high efficiency" claim against LoRA methods is asserted rather than measured.

## Strengths

- Elegant and theoretically grounded: cleanly derives the Fisher-weighted MSE from the second-order Taylor expansion of the actual task loss, closing the activation-error vs task-loss gap that AWQ/GPTQ leave open.
- Practical efficiency win: diagonal Fisher matches dense Fisher at ~5× the speed and matches MBQ's quantization time while beating QIG's 32-step integrated gradients by ~4×.
- Genuinely hardware-friendly: pure CWS with grid search, no extra low-rank matrices, no mixed bit-widths at inference — deployable on stock INT kernels.
- Robustness is rigorously stress-tested (calibration subsets, sizes, modalities), and the framework extends cleanly to pure LLMs and 2B–72B scales.
- Honest reporting: explicitly acknowledges AWQ remains competitive in easy settings, acknowledges W4A4 fails, acknowledges vision encoder is left in FP16.

## Limitations

- Weight-only (W3A16) gains over the best baseline are often within noise (0.1 pt on Qwen2.5VL-7B), so the SOTA claim rests mainly on aggressive W4A6 and on the 32B model.
- Vision encoder and projection layers are not quantized; only LLM decoder layers benefit, so peak memory savings are partial.
- Severe degradation under extreme W4A4 is explicitly left as future work — extra compensation (likely LoRA-like) is needed there, undercutting the "no auxiliary modules" selling point in that regime.
- Baseline behaviour is model-dependent (SQ vs MBQ swap), suggesting the field's baselines are not yet stable and that single-benchmark SOTA claims should be read with caution.
- Fisher computation requires running backprop on the calibration set with an SFT loss, adding implementation complexity vs vanilla AWQ; the precise calibration loss formulation is under-specified.
- No end-to-end inference latency / memory benchmarking vs the LoRA-based competitors; only quantization-time cost is compared.

## Relevance

对于关注高效多模态大模型部署与模型压缩的研究者，C-PTQ 提供了一个理论清晰、工程友好的后训练量化新基线，其"通道级敏感性 = 通道级缩放"的思想以及对角 Fisher 近似对低比特（W4A6 / W4A4）场景下的量化器设计、激活感知重要性度量、以及 LoRA-free 轻量补偿方向都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Could the diagonal Fisher weights be merged with a low-rank compensation head only for the few highest-gradient outlier channels (e.g., the ~3200-th channel in Qwen2.5VL-7B), recovering W4A4 performance without full LoRA cost?
- How does C-PTQ behave when the SFT loss used to compute Fisher is itself a multimodal instruction-tuning loss (e.g., with interleaved image-text tokens) rather than a plain captioning cross-entropy — does that change the per-channel Fisher distribution?
- The diagonal-vs-dense ablation shows the diagonal is *better* than dense, which is theoretically suspicious (dense should be a strict superset of information). Is this a noise artefact, an overfitting effect on the small 128-sample calibration set, or evidence that off-diagonal Fisher terms are actively harmful?
- Can the channel-wise Fisher weights be reused as pruning saliency (à la Supernode) to build a unified quantize-then-prune pipeline for MLLMs?
- How does C-PTQ interact with grouped quantization (e.g., per-group AWQ)? Does the Fisher signal survive group-level granularity changes?
- The vision encoder is left in FP16; would applying C-PTQ to the ViT as well (with image-reconstruction Fisher) close more of the memory gap, or does vision-side sensitivity differ structurally from the LLM decoder's?
