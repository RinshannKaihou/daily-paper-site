> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 SciExplore 基准，用 103 个专家标注、覆盖 10+ 学科的四类任务（数据库导航、模糊文献检索、缺失引用补全、跨源结构化知识综合）评估 LLM 与自主智能体；评测 12 个 SOTA 系统后发现，最强的 OpenAI Deep Research 总分仅 49.39%，多数系统低于 20%，跨源结构化综合任务（T4）上最佳系统单元级准确率也仅约 30%，暴露当前科学信息检索智能体在广度、抗模糊性与结构化输出上的严重缺陷。

> **TL;DR:** SciExplore is a new expert-curated benchmark (103 PhD-level tasks across 10+ scientific disciplines) with a four-task progression—from entity-level database navigation to cross-source structured knowledge synthesis—that probes realistic scientific information-seeking. Evaluating 12 SOTA systems, even the best agent (OpenAI Deep Research) only reaches **49.39% overall** vs. most foundation LLMs below 20%, and the hardest task (cross-source structured synthesis) sees the top system fill just **30.14% of item-level cells** and **18.59% of complete rows**, revealing that retrieval/tool use alone is insufficient for reliable autonomous scientific assistance.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 **SciExplore** 基准，把科学信息检索建模为一个"由实体到文档、再到证据、最终到领域综合"的渐进式认知过程，定义了四类互补任务（T1 科学数据库导航、T2 模糊文献检索、T3 缺失引用补全、T4 跨源结构化知识综合），覆盖 10+ 学科的 103 道专家标注题。
- 设计了一套严格、全流程专家驱动的数据构造与验证框架：逆向轨迹构造（T1）、特征去噪与模糊化+验证约束注入（T2）、引文密集段落改写（T3）、专家设计比较表（T4），并用三阶段质控（查询难度校准、人类交叉验证、答案唯一性验证）保证高推理深度、强领域特异性与答案唯一性，有效防止捷径与记忆驱动解法。
- 通过对 12 个 SOTA 系统（基础 LLM、检索增强 LLM、Deep Research 智能体）的广泛评测，定量揭示了当前系统在真实科研信息检索场景下的显著缺陷，尤其在结构化综合、广度检索与抗模糊性方面。

## Key Contributions

- **SciExplore benchmark**: A new expert-curated benchmark of 103 PhD-level tasks across more than ten scientific disciplines (materials science, pharmacology, organic chemistry, proteomics, ecology, crystallography, physical chemistry, computer vision, microbiology, neuroscience, geology, etc.), designed around scientific databases (PubChem, Materials Project, Open Targets, UniProt, DrugBank, ChEMBL, AFLOW, dbSNP, SDBS, etc.) and high-impact literature.
- **Progressive four-task taxonomy** that operationalizes scientific information seeking as a cognitive chain: T1 Scientific Database Navigation (entity-level reasoning, 39 tasks), T2 Ambiguous Literature Retrieval (document-level identification, 32 tasks), T3 Missing Reference Completion (evidence-level grounding, 14 tasks), T4 Cross-Source Structured Knowledge Synthesis (domain-level synthesis, 18 tasks).
- **Rigorous expert-driven construction + three-stage quality control** (Query Difficulty Calibration with explicit index blocking and long-tail entity preference; Human Cross-Validation with a 10-minute search limit; Answer Uniqueness Verification using search-enabled LLMs like Gemini-3-Pro and o3), explicitly engineered to defeat shortcut retrieval and parametric memorization.
- **Large-scale empirical exposure of agent weaknesses**: 12 SOTA systems evaluated across three paradigms (foundation LLMs, LLMs with search tools, Deep Research agents), demonstrating a sharp capability cliff as task complexity rises and isolating the roles of parametric knowledge, retrieval augmentation, and agentic planning.

## Methodology

SciExplore frames scientific information seeking as a four-step cognitive progression, each step instantiated as a task type with a dedicated construction strategy and metric:

- **T1 — Scientific Database Navigation (entity-level, 39 tasks, Exact-Match accuracy).** Tasks are built with a *Reverse Trajectory Construction Strategy*: explicit entity identifiers are progressively replaced by nested attribute-based constraints, forcing multi-hop reasoning over interconnected database records and preventing direct lookup. An EM-based score `F_em = 2C / (2C + 2I + N)` symmetrically penalizes incorrect (I) and not-attempted (N) answers.
- **T2 — Ambiguous Literature Retrieval (document-level, 32 tasks, EM accuracy).** Experts apply *Feature Denoising and Fuzzification* to obscure surface cues, while *Validation Constraint Injection* preserves answer uniqueness and enables post-retrieval verification without leaking hints into the query.
- **T3 — Missing Reference Completion (evidence-level, 14 tasks, accuracy).** Citation-dense paragraphs from high-impact literature are rewritten to remove textual overlap; predictions are grouped into citation slots and judged by an LLM in two steps (core cited work correct, then bibliographic attributes correct).
- **T4 — Cross-Source Structured Knowledge Synthesis (domain-level, 18 tasks, multi-level recall).** Built from expert-curated comparison tables in review/survey papers, requiring agents to fill a strict schema by integrating heterogeneous details scattered across multiple source papers. Evaluation is hierarchical: item-level recall (correctly predicted cells / total ground-truth cells) and row-level recall (rows correct only if primary key + all attributes are correct).

**Quality control (three stages):** (1) *Query Difficulty Calibration* — Explicit Index Blocking + Long-tail Entity Preference to defeat memorization; (2) *Human Cross-Validation* — annotators must fail to solve a question via search within 10 minutes or the question is discarded; (3) *Answer Uniqueness Verification* — search-enabled LLMs (Gemini-3-Pro, o3) generate candidate solutions; any alternative valid answer causes the task to be discarded.

**Overall score** is a difficulty-aware weighted aggregate: `Score_overall = 0.2·T1 + 0.2·T2 + 0.3·T3 + 0.3·T4`, up-weighting the harder evidence-grounding and synthesis tasks. Run-to-run variance was assessed by repeating the full benchmark four times per model (mean overall variance = 0.78, indicating stability). Systems evaluated span three paradigms — foundation LLMs (DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen2.5-72B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking, GPT-5.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3-Pro), LLMs with search tools (DeepSeek-V3.2, GPT-5.1, Gemini-3-Pro), and Deep Research agents (Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B, Gemini Deep Research, OpenAI Deep Research).

## Results & Highlights

- **Best overall is still under 50%.** OpenAI Deep Research tops the leaderboard at **49.39% overall**, while most foundation LLMs sit below 20% (e.g., Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking at 4.50%, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct at 4.99%); the strongest foundation LLM, Gemini-3-Pro, reaches only **19.08%** — a ~30-point gap to the best agent on SciExplore.
- **Deep Research agents dominate retrieval-augmented LLMs.** On the T4 row-level structured-synthesis metric, OpenAI Deep Research scores **18.59%** vs. Gemini-3-Pro-w/-search at **11.49%** and GPT-5.1-w/-search at **5.12%**, and foundation LLMs at 0.94–7.29%. On T3 Missing Reference Completion, OpenAI Deep Research reaches **59.91%** vs. Gemini-3-Pro-w/-search's 49.40% and Gemini-3-Pro (no search) at 13.69%.
- **Search augmentation can *hurt*.** DeepSeek-V3.2-w/-search (11.12% overall) is essentially flat vs. DeepSeek-V3.2 (11.06%), and on T1 Scientific Database Navigation GPT-5.1-w/-search (15.38%) underperforms GPT-5.1 (17.95%) — search amplifies "hypothesis-first" reasoning rather than correcting it (illustrated by the SCN10A / Nav1.8 case where the no-search model got the right answer by honoring a 604-aa constraint while the search-enabled model fabricated a non-existent "604 aa SCN10A construct").
- **The synthesis task (T4) is the cliff.** Even the best agent (OpenAI Deep Research) fills only **30.14%** of item-level cells and reconstructs only **18.59%** of complete rows; primary-key recall stays below 60% for every system, meaning essential row entities are simply never retrieved, making downstream table alignment infeasible.
- **Formatting/instruction-following failures on T4.** Despite strong retrieval, Gemini Deep Research achieves only **55.56% Format Accuracy** (a "report-generation bias"), versus 88.89–100% for most other systems — required columns are omitted or collapsed into summary-style tables.
- **Hop length does *not* predict difficulty.** On T1 multi-hop tasks, accuracy does not monotonically decline with hop count; e.g., Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B scores 25.0% at 2-hop but **62.50% at 5-hop** — because agents use a "guess-and-verify" strategy that diverges from how humans plan multi-step search. The human baseline does decline cleanly (66.70% at 2-hop → 25.00% at 6-hop).
- **Search intensity correlates with success.** Models averaging fewer than ~2 searches per question (e.g., GPT-5.1-w/-search) get low scores, while Deep Research agents that spend a larger search budget consistently outperform across T1–T4.
- **Run-to-run stability is acceptable.** Mean overall variance across four repeated full-benchmark runs is **0.78**, with most models under 1.0, supporting the reliability of the ranking.

Joint work between **University of Science and Technology of China (USTC)** and **Shanghai AI Laboratory**. Lead authors Yinhao Tang and Youqing Fang are co-equal contributors; corresponding authors are Yanan Sun and Kai Chen (Kai Chen leads the OpenOceanLab/Open-compass line of agent evaluation work at Shanghai AI Lab, which lends strong credibility to the benchmark methodology and evaluation rigor). ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine):** The four-task progression from entity → document → evidence → domain synthesis is a real taxonomic contribution that neither deep-search benchmarks (BrowseComp, WideSearch, DeepResearchBench — all general-domain, open-web, free-form) nor scientific QA benchmarks (SciBench, GPQA, SuperGPQA, HLE — all closed-book parametric reasoning) cover. The structured-synthesis task with strict schema and row-level recall is a notably harder target than free-form report scoring. The negative findings (hop-length uncorrelated with difficulty; search can degrade performance via hypothesis-first bias) are interesting and paper-specific.
- **Rigor (mostly strong, with caveats):** The three-stage QC (10-minute human cross-validation, answer-uniqueness check via Gemini-3-Pro/o3) and four-run variance analysis are commendable and uncommon in benchmark papers. However, the dataset is small — **T3 has only 14 tasks and T4 only 18 tasks** — so per-task scores (especially T3/T4 cell-level numbers) carry real sampling noise despite the low variance reported; a single misannotated task can move T3 by ~7 points. The evaluation metrics on T3/T4 rely on **LLM judges** whose own error rates are not separately reported.
- **Reproducibility (moderate):** Prompt templates and metric formulas are provided, and commercial agents (OpenAI/Gemini Deep Research) are clearly named; however, the task instances themselves are not released (and arguably cannot be, to preserve difficulty), and the closed-source agents are black boxes whose behavior may change over time. The "answer-uniqueness verification by Gemini-3-Pro/o3" is itself a moving target.
- **Significance (high if it sticks):** The headline finding — the best commercial Deep Research agent at 49.39% overall and <20% on full structured rows — is a credible, sobering signal that retrieval+tools alone do not make a scientific assistant. The failure-mode taxonomy (premature abandonment, post-stagnation hallucination, long-context information loss, schema non-compliance, premature hypothesis commitment) is concrete and actionable.
- **Red flags / things to probe:**
  - **Very small T3/T4 strata** (14 and 18 tasks) — confidence intervals around cell/row recalls are wide; the paper reports variance but not CIs.
  - **LLM-judge dependence** for T3/T4 grading — no human spot-check of judge accuracy is reported.
  - **Cherry-picked failure examples** (Fig. 8–12) are illustrative but anecdotal; the frequency of each failure mode across the dataset is not quantified, so the reader cannot tell whether "hallucination after search stagnation" affects 5% or 50% of attempts.
  - **No release of task instances** limits third-party reproduction and stress-testing of new models — the benchmark is effectively trust-us-to-run-it.
  - The **human baseline is only reported on T1 multi-hop** (and only loosely); no human overall score is given, so "how far from human" is under-specified for T2–T4.
  - The "search can hurt" claim is supported by aggregate dips of a few points that are within the reported run-to-run variance for some models (e.g., DeepSeek-V3.2 11.06 vs. 11.12), so the effect is real but smaller than the qualitative case study implies.

## Strengths

- Genuinely fills a gap between general deep-search benchmarks and closed-book scientific QA, with a well-motivated cognitive progression.
- Expert-curated, anti-shortcut construction (index blocking, long-tail preference, fuzzification) is materially harder to game than template-generated or LLM-synthesized tasks.
- Negative results are reported honestly — including the counterintuitive hop-length finding and search-degrades-performance cases — and supported with concrete trajectories.
- Difficulty-aware overall weighting (T3/T4 weighted 0.3 each) correctly emphasizes the capabilities that matter for real research assistance.
- Multi-run variance analysis and detailed qualitative failure-mode taxonomy (with five concrete case studies in the appendix) go beyond typical benchmark papers.

## Limitations

- **Scale:** only 103 tasks total, with T3 (14) and T4 (18) so small that per-task metrics are noisy; broader confidence intervals are not reported.
- **LLM-judged metrics** for T3/T4 without reported judge-accuracy spot checks.
- **No public task release**, limiting independent reproduction and longitudinal tracking as the closed-source agents evolve.
- **Human baseline is thin** — only on T1 multi-hop, not an overall human reference.
- **Coverage skew:** disciplines and databases are concentrated where structured DBs exist (chemistry, materials, biology); social-science and humanities scientific workflows are essentially absent.
- **Failure-mode frequencies are not quantified** — only illustrated — so the relative severity of each defect is unclear.
- **Closed-system dependence:** three of the strongest systems (OpenAI Deep Research, Gemini Deep Research, Gemini-3-Pro) are black-box commercial products whose behavior can shift between runs.

## Relevance

该工作直接面向 LLM/智能体评测这一核心研究方向，提出了一个比通用 Deep Search 基准更贴近真实科研工作流的硬基准，对评估与改进具备"数据库导航 + 文献检索 + 证据级引用 + 跨源结构化综合"能力的科研助手智能体具有高参考价值；其负面结论（最强智能体总分仍不到 50%、结构化综合行级召回不足 19%）对设计下一代检索增强与长程推理智能体具有明确的指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the T4 collapse is due to retrieval breadth (primary-key recall <60%) vs. schema-following vs. cross-source alignment, and which failure mode dominates when retrieval is fixed (e.g., by giving the agent a curated candidate-paper set)?
- Would scaling T3/T4 to a few hundred tasks change the ranking, or do the per-task noise concerns wash out at scale?
- Can a human spot-check of the LLM judge on T3/T4 quantify judge error, and does it differ by discipline?
- Is the "search-degrades-performance / hypothesis-first bias" effect robust under controlled retrieval, or is it specific to noisy open-web retrieval? Could a verification-then-commit protocol (forcing the agent to re-check constraints before answering) close the gap, as the SCN10A case suggests?
- Does the "hop length ≠ difficulty" finding generalize beyond T1, and can a difficulty predictor based on agent behavior (search calls, guess-and-verify cycles) replace hop count as a proxy?
- How do open, hackable agents (e.g., WebSailor, WebThinker, xbench-style systems) perform when given the same search budget as the closed Deep Research products, to separate model capability from product-level orchestration?
