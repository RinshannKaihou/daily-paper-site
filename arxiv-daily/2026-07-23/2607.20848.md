> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一种基于行为的医疗 LLM 诊断"证据使用审计"方法：将病例拆分为证据单元、在受控证据子集上对候选诊断打分、挖掘诊断边际上的低阶交互，并通过诊断相对角色、鲁棒性检验与盲法临床评审区分"合理的鉴别诊断交互"与"可疑的捷径式证据滥用"，在 DDXPlus/CupCase/MedCase 上对 5 个开源模型评估，发现大部分交互强度来自合理的支持或冲突/抵消，而真正被裁定为无效的病例集中在否定/缺失证据与临床局部证据上。

> **TL;DR:** A behavioral audit that goes beyond diagnostic accuracy: it decomposes each case into evidence units, scores candidate diagnoses (via option-letter log-probs) under controlled evidence subsets, mines low-order margin interactions, and then uses diagnosis-relative evidence roles + robustness checks + blinded 5-reviewer clinical review to separate plausible differential-diagnosis interactions from genuine shortcut-like failures. On DDXPlus (500 cases), CupCase (200), and MedCase (200) across five open-weight LLMs (Qwen2.5-7B, OpenBioLLM-8B, MedGemma-4B, BioMistral-7B, Med42-8B), faithful support + differential conflict/cancellation account for most interaction strength (e.g., 86.6–89.6% on DDXPlus across models), while the 11 adjudicated invalid/shortcut-like cases concentrate in negated/absent and clinically local evidence; stability filtering raises adjudicated precision on the candidate-failure queue from 0.55 to 0.80 while cutting the false-positive rate from 0.20 to 0.08.

## 核心贡献

- 提出首个面向医疗诊断 LLM 的**行为式证据使用审计协议**：将病例拆分为证据单元，在受控证据子集上对每个候选诊断打分（option-letter log-prob），并用 Shapley/Taylor 风格的低阶交互指标刻画"证据组合对诊断边际的联合影响"。
- 引入**诊断相对（diagnosis-relative）证据角色框架**：同一证据对一个诊断是支持、对竞争者是支持、对第三个诊断可能是排除——因此明确把"交互发现"与"失败归因"分开，避免把负交互或大交互自动判为错误。
- 设计**多层级验证管线**：自动机制标注 → prompt 措辞/候选顺序/删除vs中和的鲁棒性过滤 → 目标性反事实检验 → 5 位博士生医学评审盲法裁定（Fleiss' κ = 0.82），只有稳定且临床评审无效的交互才作为强失败主张。
- 在 3 个数据集、5 个开源模型上系统刻画证据使用结构：合理支持与冲突/抵消主导交互强度，而真正可疑的机制集中在否定/缺失证据与临床局部证据（如把"未旅行""皮疹"字段错误加权到无关诊断）。
- 提供可复制的"精度过滤"证据：稳定性过滤将候选失败队列的裁定精度从 0.55 提升到 0.80，假阳性率从 0.20 降到 0.08，随机对照仅 0.10 精度/0.75 假阳性。

## Key Contributions

- A behavioral (not introspective) audit protocol that scores diagnoses under evidence interventions and mines low-order interactions in diagnostic margins, complementing accuracy-only benchmarks.
- A diagnosis-relative evidence-role taxonomy (supporting / competitor-supporting / negated-or-absent / background-risk / likely-irrelevant-or-local) that treats evidence roles as directional expectations, not global good/bad labels — crucial because the same field (e.g., skin findings) is suspicious for inguinal hernia but appropriate for SLE.
- A principled separation of **interaction discovery** from **failure assignment**: large or negative interactions are first screened as plausible mechanisms; only stable, clinically-reviewed incoherent interactions become failure claims.
- A layered validation stack (prompt/order robustness, deletion-vs-neutralization semantics, targeted counterfactuals, blinded 5-reviewer clinical adjudication) that turns broad suspicious signals into high-confidence candidates.
- Empirical evidence that accuracy rankings do not match evidence-use rankings, and that most mined interactions reflect legitimate differential diagnosis rather than misuse.

## Methodology

The audit converts each clinical case into controlled observations of model preference through a four-stage pipeline.

**1. Diagnostic scoring with evidence interventions.** A case is decomposed into evidence units `E = {e1,…,en}` (symptoms, signs, risk/history items, labs, explicitly absent findings) and a candidate set `C`. Each prompt shows the visible units plus the same candidate list and asks the model to return only the option letter. Instead of using only the generated answer, the authors score every candidate via the next-token log-probability of its option letter (`sT(cj)`). For any audited diagnosis `d`, its **margin** under subset `T` is `vd(T) = sT(d) − max_{cj≠d} sT(cj)` — a within-prompt preference difference. Evidence interventions vary `T`; in the main setting unselected units are hidden, and in sensitivity analyses they are replaced with a "not specified" statement (important for negated evidence: hiding "no chest pain" makes it unavailable, not present).

**2. Diagnosis-relative evidence roles.** Units are categorized relative to the audited diagnosis: supporting evidence (raises `d`'s plausibility), competitor-supporting (raises an alternative), excluding/negated (reports absence or rules against `d`), background/risk (changes prior plausibility), and likely irrelevant/clinically local. These roles are directional expectations that guide interpretation of interaction sign and strength, not automatic error labels.

**3. Interaction mining.** Using the value function `vd(T)`, the interaction effect for a set `S` is the discrete Möbius/Shapley-style form `Id(S) = Σ_{T⊆S} (−1)^{|S|−|T|} vd(T)`. Singletons give one-unit effects; larger `S` captures synergy/suppression beyond lower-order effects. The audit targets low-order (mainly order-3) interactions because they are interpretable and cheap to estimate, and ranks high-strength interactions by `|Id(S)|`.

**4. From interactions to audit labels (provisional → confirmed).** Automatic rules assign provisional labels in a deterministic priority order: candidate-misuse conditions (positive interactions containing explicitly negated/excluding evidence; interactions with clinically local evidence lacking a role-supported path) take precedence; otherwise interactions fall through to mechanism labels (differential conflict/cancellation, target redundancy, faithful target evidence, background/history influence). Only stability-checked and clinically-reviewed cases become strong failure claims. Stability filters test prompt wording (clinical-summary vs. questionnaire variants), candidate option order (permutations), and deletion-vs-neutralization semantics.

**Setup.** Datasets: DDXPlus (500 validation cases, 8 audited units, 255 subset interventions/case), CupCase (200 test cases, 6 units, 63 interventions), MedCase (200 test cases, 6 units, 63 interventions). Models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, OpenBioLLM-8B, MedGemma-4B, BioMistral-7B, Llama3-Med42-8B, all scored with vLLM at temperature 0, one generated token, option-letter log-probs. DDXPlus supports structured role labels so fine-grained failure claims are anchored there; CupCase/MedCase serve as coarser external checks for the faithful-support vs. conflict/cancellation structure.

## Results & Highlights

- **Accuracy baseline (Table 2).** OpenBioLLM-8B has the highest average accuracy (72.2%) and the best DDXPlus score (74.0%); Med42 is close on DDXPlus (73.4%). CupCase is easiest (OpenBio 87.5%), MedCase hardest (best model OpenBio only 55.0%, BioMistral 44.5%). Crucially, the model ordering by accuracy does **not** match the ordering by faithful target-evidence strength or conflict/cancellation strength — a qualitative observation that accuracy and evidence-use structure are not interchangeable.
- **Most interactions are clinically plausible (Finding 2).** Differential conflict/cancellation accounts for **47.1%** of interaction strength on DDXPlus, **45.8%** on CupCase, and **52.2%** on MedCase. Faithful/plausible support plus conflict/cancellation together account for **86.6–89.6%** of DDXPlus strength across the five models, while negated/absent misuse stays at only **1.2–3.0%** (Table 14). The authors stress this argues against treating negative interactions as automatic errors.
- **Low-order interactions carry the signal (Table 3).** On DDXPlus (8 units → 256 subsets), singleton/leave-one-out scoring uses only 9 subsets (3.5% budget) and keeps 18.0% of top-interaction strength while retrieving **0/11** adjudicated invalid items; pairwise (37 subsets, 14.5%) retrieves **4/11**; order-3 (93 subsets, 36.3%) keeps 100% and retrieves **11/11**. Third-order interactions carry the largest share of count (59.5%) and strength (54.4%) in the high-strength set (Table 17).
- **Clinical validation (Finding 3, 130-item enriched sample).** Five blinded PhD-trained reviewers reached Fleiss' **κ = 0.82** with 77% full agreement. After adjudication: **111 valid, 8 questionable, 11 invalid/shortcut-like**. Faithful target evidence (29 valid / 1 questionable / 0 invalid) and conflict/cancellation (36/4/0) were almost always valid; **all 11 invalid cases came from the negated/absent-misuse bucket** (9 valid / 0 questionable / 11 invalid, Table 5). Invalid patterns are concrete: "no recent travel" increasing support for allergic sinusitis/asthma/Guillain-Barré, and skin/rash/no-peeling fields supporting unrelated diagnoses like inguinal hernia or influenza.
- **Counterfactuals separate local artifacts from global reliance (Finding 4, Table 6).** Removing the exact disease-name family-history cue for myasthenia gravis drops the target margin by **−9.50** (Qwen) and **−6.13** (BioMistral, also flipping rank 1→2) — a confidence-amplification shortcut. Removing skin fields for inguinal hernia *increases* margin by **+2.25** (rank unchanged → local incoherence, not reliance); for influenza **+3.75** (rank 2→1); for the SLE control **−7.50** (rank 1→2), confirming the same evidence family can be clinically appropriate. An 86-record stratified check shows skin-hernia (29.3%) and skin-other (32.1%) strong-change rates, much larger than the no-travel polarity effect (11.6% vs. 5.6% control).
- **Stability filtering turns suspicious noise into high-confidence candidates (Finding 5, Table 7).** The enriched candidate-failure queue shrinks from 300 (precision 0.55, FP 0.20) → 220 prompt-stable (0.65/0.15) → 180 +order-stable (0.75/0.10) → **120 +neutralize (0.80/0.08)**; a random control reaches only precision 0.10 with FP 0.75. Neutralization sharply reduces suspicious signal for OpenBioLLM and BioMistral but does not eliminate it (Table 9).
- **Selection sensitivity (Table 16).** Faithful+conflict accounts for 89.0% (main balanced selector), 94.0% (target-salient), 94.0% (random-balanced); suspicious strength remains small (2.2–2.8%) and negated/absent misuse 2.1–2.7%, supporting mechanism-level (not exact-interaction) stability.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from **Junchi Liao, Jiawen Deng, and Fuji Ren\*** (corresponding author) at the **University of Electronic Science and Technology of China (UESTC), Chengdu**. The methodology follows established behavioral/counterfactual evaluation traditions (Ribeiro CheckList, Gardner contrast sets, Sundararajan/Shapley interaction indices, ERASER) and is unusually careful about epistemics: it explicitly states that margins are within-prompt preference differences (not calibrated probabilities), that option-letter priors and position effects can bias scores, and that the clinical-review sample is enriched and therefore descriptive rather than population-estimated. The clinical validation uses a genuine blinded 5-reviewer protocol with a reported inter-rater agreement (Fleiss' κ = 0.82), which is above the bar for most NLP evaluation papers and lends credibility to the failure-claim discipline.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core move — combining Shapley/Taylor-style feature interactions with diagnosis-relative evidence roles and a discovery-vs-assignment separation — is genuinely novel for the medical-LLM space. Prior attribution/counterfactual work (LIME, SHAP, CheckList, ERASER) is cited and correctly distinguished; the paper is explicit that it does not recover latent reasoning or validate rationales.
- **Rigor.** High. The layered validation (prompt wording × candidate order × deletion-vs-neutralization × counterfactual × blinded review) is more thorough than typical "explainability" papers, and the authors report Wilson/bootstrap intervals rather than overclaiming. The honesty about the order-3 row in Table 3 being a reference set (not an independent sufficiency proof) rather than hiding it is a positive sign.
- **Reproducibility.** Moderate-to-good. Models, datasets, and the vLLM scoring protocol (temperature 0, one token, max-logprobs 100, option-letter token-ID validation) are specified. Prompt templates are given in Appendix C. However, no code/data release is mentioned, evidence-unit selection rules are only partially formalized, and the 130-item clinical-review labels (the paper's strongest asset) are human annotations that cannot be regenerated from code alone.
- **Significance.** The "accuracy ≠ evidence-use fidelity" message is timely and well-motivated for medical AI safety; the concrete finding that invalid mechanisms cluster in **negated/absent and clinically local evidence** (e.g., "no travel," "skin/rash," "no peeling") is actionable for dataset curators and red-teamers.
- **Red flags / weaknesses.**
  1. **Only open-weight, mostly 7–8B models.** No GPT-4/Med-Gemini/Med42 large proprietary models — the strongest clinical models are absent, so the failure profile may not transfer.
  2. **Multiple-choice / option-letter scoring only.** Free-form diagnostic outputs (the realistic clinical deployment mode) are explicitly out of scope; token-prior and position bias are acknowledged but only mitigated, not removed.
  3. **Enriched, non-probability sample.** The 11 invalid cases come from a high-strength, candidate-failure-enriched review queue; the authors are honest that prevalence estimates would require probability sampling, but readers may still over-read "11 failures" as a base rate.
  4. **No baseline comparator audit.** The paper introduces its own protocol but does not run a competing behavioral-audit baseline (e.g., a pure CheckList or SHAP pipeline) head-to-head on the same cases, so the marginal value of the interaction-mining step over cheaper singleton attribution is argued via the Table 3 retrieval gap rather than a direct method comparison.
  5. **Order-3 sufficiency is only checked on DDXPlus** and the authors flag this themselves.

## Strengths

- Conceptually clean separation of **interaction discovery** from **failure assignment**, which prevents the common overclaim that "any sensitivity = unreliability."
- Diagnosis-relative roles correctly handle the fact that the same evidence family (skin findings) can be a red flag for one diagnosis and legitimate for another — a subtlety most feature-attribution work misses.
- Strong validation discipline: prompt/order robustness, deletion-vs-neutralization semantics, targeted counterfactuals, and a blinded 5-reviewer clinical adjudication with κ = 0.82.
- Actionable, concrete failure taxonomy (negated/absent misuse, clinically local evidence) rather than vague "bias" claims.
- Honest epistemics throughout — the paper repeatedly tells the reader what the numbers do *not* prove.

## Limitations

- Measures prompt-conditioned preferences, not latent reasoning or clinical safety; interaction scores are behavioral signals, not internal causal explanations.
- Results depend on hand-constructed evidence units, candidate sets, and role annotations; CupCase/MedCase only support coarse labels, so fine-grained failure claims are anchored to DDXPlus alone.
- Clinical review is enriched for high-strength and candidate-failure items, so precision/mechanism shares/filtering values are descriptive, not population estimates.
- Order-ablation is a DDXPlus retrieval check, not proof that order-3 suffices generally.
- Only five open-weight models and multiple-choice option scoring; option-letter log-probs may reflect token/position bias that permutations reduce but cannot calibrate away.
- No proprietary frontier medical models evaluated; free-form diagnostic output mode not covered.

## Relevance

对关注医疗 LLM 评估与可解释性的研究者高度相关：本文提供了一个可操作的"超越准确率"的审计框架，明确指出模型排序与证据使用结构不一致，并揭示了否定/缺失证据和临床局部证据上的捷径式滥用——这些发现对医疗 AI 安全评估、对齐评测设计以及红队测试直接有用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the audit profile change for frontier proprietary models (GPT-4/5, Med-Gemini, Med42 large) and for **free-form** diagnostic outputs rather than multiple-choice option letters?
- Can the negated/absent-misuse cluster (no-travel, skin/rash, no-peeling) be reproduced under probability sampling, and what is its true base rate across a broad case distribution?
- Does targeting the discovered shortcut mechanisms (e.g., disease-name family-history cues) with targeted fine-tuning or decoding-time interventions reduce invalid interactions without hurting accuracy?
- How does the interaction-order budget trade-off generalize beyond DDXPlus's 8-unit structure — is order-3 still sufficient for cases with 12–20 evidence units?
- Could the diagnosis-relative role taxonomy be automated (e.g., via a separate clinical-role LLM) to remove the dependence on dataset-specific structured role labels and extend fine-grained failure claims to narrative datasets?
