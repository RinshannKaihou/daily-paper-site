> **一句话总结：** 本文把压缩式记忆度量（Morris et al. 2026b）扩展到冻结底座的 LoRA 场景，用比特（bits）直接测量 adapter 写进了多少信息：每个可训练参数仅约 1.7–2.8 比特，远低于全量微调的 ~3.6；且容量取决于参数"放在哪"（MLP 比 attention 多近一倍）和"写进什么样的底座"（随机底座只剩 29%），并据此证明在等精度下 GRPO 几乎不写、而 SFT 会逐字记下私钥——把"adapter 是无害 diff"的共识改成了可计算的事前量。

> **TL;DR:** By adapting the compression-based memorization meter of Morris et al. (2026b) to the frozen-base setting, this paper weighs LoRA adapters in bits and finds they store only 1.7–2.8 bits per trainable parameter (vs. ~3.6 for full fine-tuning of the same base), with capacity driven by placement (MLP vs. attention) and base substrate rather than parameter count. On a Qwen2.5-0.5B secret-sharer probe, moving LoRA rank from 16 to 64 raises canary exposure by ~11 bits while rank 4→16 moves it barely at all ("the step that writes is the step that leaks"); and at matched accuracy a rank-4 GRPO adapter writes ~0 excess bits on 3-digit arithmetic where SFT writes +271±42, settling the SFT-vs-RLVR debate with bits rather than behavior.

## 核心贡献

- **首个冻结底座下的比特级容量计**：把"压缩=记忆"框架（Morris et al. 2026b）从全量模型迁移到 PEFT 场景，用 64 个均匀随机 token（共 704 比特）作为定标砝码——无内容可学，因此每个被恢复的比特都是被记忆的比特——给出一个在任何冻结底座上都可计算的事前量。
- **三读数协议（artifact / behavior / memorization）**：同一框架返回三种读数——CL(Δθ) 是文件物理传输的比特，ΔW 是从最终策略读出的行为比特（让 SFT 和 RL 能在同一把尺上比较），α̂_ad 是单位参数记忆平台。三者回答不同问题，差异本身即故事。
- **"容量不等于参数量"的两条经验分离**：(a) 参数量固定在 ~37K，仅改放置位置，MLP-only 比 attention-only 多近一倍（2.43 vs 1.30 bits/param）；(b) 同一 LoRA 在随机底座上只记 29%，在 WikiText 预训练底座上记 98%——容量属于"adapter + 底座"的联合属性。
- **把比特读数桥接到真实隐私审计**：在 Qwen2.5-0.5B 上植入金丝雀，证明漏出随"写进的比特"而非"参数量"上升；rank 16→64 那一步既写又漏，而 rank 4→16 既不写也不漏，MIA 则全程接近随机。
- **用比特裁决 SFT vs RLVR 之争**：等精度下 GRPO 的 excess bits 在 binary/graded/noisy reward 上都 ≈0，而 SFT 在 800 步时已达 +271±42 比特；植入 8 位数字"秘密"后，SFT 达到 rank-1 提取（exposure 12.0–13.3 bits），GRPO 保持随机水平（1.1 bits），为"RLVR 是 elicitation 而非 injection"提供了非行为性的直接证据。

## Key Contributions

- A first compression-based memorization meter for the frozen-base/PEFT regime: extends Morris et al. (2026b) to LoRA, using uniformly random 704-bit sequences as a calibration weight so that every recovered bit is unambiguously a stored bit, and producing a quantity computable before a single gradient step.
- A three-reading protocol — artifact codelength CL(Δθ), behavior-write bits ΔW, and normalized writability α̂_ad — each answering a different question (what a file ships vs. what a policy reveals vs. a clean storage floor), and deliberately reported as a lower bound at a declared 16,000-step budget.
- Two clean dissociations showing capacity is shape/substrate, not size: fixing the trainable count at ~37K and moving weights from attention to MLP nearly doubles per-parameter capacity (1.30 → 2.43 bits/param); and the same LoRA memorizes 29% on a random base vs. 98% on a WikiText-pretrained base.
- A leakage audit on Qwen2.5-0.5B that ties measured bits to real attacks: canary exposure and (to a much lesser degree) MIA track the bits the adapter writes, and the rank step that writes is the step that leaks.
- The first bit-level head-to-head of SFT vs. GRPO at matched accuracy: GRPO writes essentially zero excess bits across binary/graded/noisy rewards, while SFT keeps writing after it has already learned the task; planted secrets that SFT memorizes verbatim (exposure 12–13 bits) GRPO leaves at chance (1.1 bits), giving non-behavioral evidence for the elicitation reading of RLVR.

## Methodology

The paper instruments adapters with a compression-based memorization framework. For a tuned model θ̂ and reference θ_ref, the codelength of a sequence x is its negative log-likelihood in bits H_K(x|θ) = -log2 p(x|θ), realizable by arithmetic coding (Witten, Neal, Cleary 1987). Unintended memorization is the reference codelength the tuned model undercuts: mem_U(x) = H_K(x|θ_ref) - min(H_K(x|θ_ref), H_K(x|θ̂)).

The random-string probe makes the reference exact. Sequences are 64 uniform tokens drawn from a 2,048-symbol vocabulary after a start token, so H_K(x|θ_ref) = 704 bits by construction and nothing in x can be predicted — every saved bit is a memorized bit. Held-out random sequences must read mem_U ≈ 0, a check the authors verify across their whole model spine (Appendix A).

Three readings are reported per configuration. (1) Writability α̂_ad is peak memorization normalized by trainable parameter count — a capacity floor. (2) The artifact-write cost CL(Δθ) is the entropy-coded size of the per-tensor-quantized trainable weights, minimized over precision — an upper bound on what shipping the file physically communicates. (3) The behavior-write bits W and their signed excess ΔW = W(D_train) - (n_train/n_held)·W(D_held) read off the final policy likelihoods on canonical answers, the memorization left once ordinary generalization is subtracted off. Because W reads a policy rather than its weights, it lets SFT and RL adapters be weighed on the same scale.

Protocol choices matter more at adapter scale than at full-model scale, so the authors fix a 16,000-step budget with no early stopping, sweep the learning rate and report the envelope, and place dataset sizes relative to estimated capacity so the informative band is sampled rather than the budget-starved tail. Every value is therefore a lower bound at a declared budget (extending to 40,000 steps lifts it by 10–24%). Bases are GPT-style decoders (2M and 8M non-embedding parameters) pretrained on WikiText-103 then frozen; adapters are LoRA and weight-tied TinyLoRA. Full fine-tuning of the same architecture bounds readings from above; the untouched base bounds them from below.

For the real-text audit, the authors fine-tune Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on a carrier corpus with the secret-sharer construction (Carlini et al. 2019): short random secrets at controlled repetition, with exposure = the log-rank of the true secret against 10,000 decoys (a clean ceiling at rank 1). For SFT vs. RLVR, the base and rank-4 LoRA are held fixed and both arms are trained to matched accuracy on 3-digit arithmetic and key-value extraction; SFT on gold answers, GRPO (Shao et al. 2024) on a verifiable reward.

## Results & Highlights

- **Adapters plateau well below the full-model line.** Full-model fine-tuning stores about 3.6 bits per parameter (Morris et al. 2026b), and full fine-tuning of the same base under this paper's own 16K-step budget climbs past 3 (Table 1: ≥3.07 bits/param, 6.81M bits across 2.2M parameters). LoRA adapters given the same budget stop at 1.7–2.8 bits per trainable parameter (e.g., 2M base, r=1: 1.71 bits/param over 12,288 params; 2M base, r=16: 2.75 bits/param over 196,608 params). The gap survives the matched budget and does not close with more compute.
- **Capacity depends on placement, not parameter count.** Fixing the trainable count at ~37K and changing only where the weights live moves per-parameter capacity from 1.30 (attention-only) to 2.43 (MLP-only) bits/param — a near-doubling with parameter count held flat (Figure 3, left, three seeds).
- **Capacity depends on the frozen base, not the adapter alone.** The same LoRA memorizes 29% of its data on a randomly initialized base, 79% on a base pretrained on structureless synthetic text, and 98% on one pretrained on WikiText; full fine-tuning of that same random base reaches 99%, so the shortfall is specific to writing through frozen weights (Figure 4).
- **Precision barely matters.** Casting a trained adapter to bf16 costs about 1% of its memorization while halving the file; fp16 costs essentially nothing (Figure 3, right).
- **Leakage follows written bits, not parameter count.** On Qwen2.5-0.5B, going from rank 4 to rank 16 quadruples trainable parameters but barely changes written bits, canary exposure, or MIA AUC; going from rank 16 to rank 64 quadruples the count again but written bits jump by ~50% and canary exposure climbs 11 bits, with frequently repeated secrets becoming rank-1 extractable among 10,000 candidates. Loss-based MIA and Min-K% stay near chance (AUC ≈ 0.5) across the entire rank sweep — extraction, not membership inference, resolves the difference.
- **At matched accuracy, GRPO writes ~0 bits; SFT keeps writing.** On 3-digit arithmetic (Table 2), SFT at 100 steps carries +106 excess bits and +271±42 by 800 steps; GRPO binary/graded/noisy at 300 steps carries -46, -56±9, and -37±10 bits respectively — zero within the estimator's noise. On key-value+canary, SFT reaches exposure 12.0 (300 steps) and 13.3 (800 steps) bits while GRPO binary sits at 1.1 bits.

## Authors & Credibility

- Kaizhen Tan (Carnegie Mellon University), Heqing Du and Yang Feng (Columbia University). Yang Feng directs Columbia's NLP group; the work extends a recent ICML measurement paper (Morris et al. 2026b, "How Much Do Language Models Memorize?") rather than reinventing the framework.
- The methodology is built on peer-reviewed machinery (compression-based memorization, secret-sharer canaries, GRPO/DeepSeekMath) and the authors release a harness. The framing is unusually careful about declaring every capacity number a lower bound and explaining where protocol choices that are harmless at full-model scale become dominant at adapter scale.
- This is an arXiv preprint (v1, 23 Jul 2026); it has not yet been through conference peer review. Claims rest on small-to-mid models (2M/8M synthetic decoders plus Qwen2.5-0.5B) rather than frontier-scale adapters.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. This is the first paper to bring the compression-based memorization meter into the frozen-base regime, and the three-reading protocol (artifact vs. behavior vs. writability) plus the placement test and the bit-level SFT-vs-GRPO comparison are not obvious recombinations of prior work — they address open questions raised directly by Morris et al. (2026a,b).
- **Rigor:** Strong on internal validity — random-string calibration, held-out zero check, parameter-matched placement, three seeds with reported spread, declared lower-bound status, and an honest leave-one-out regression caveat. But there are real gaps. The leakage claim rests almost entirely on the canary *extraction* metric: loss-based MIA and Min-K% sit at chance across the whole rank sweep, which the authors attribute (fairly) to corpus size, but it means the membership-inference link is asserted, not demonstrated. The "capacity depends on substrate" result rests on exactly three base conditions (random / synthetic-text / WikiText), which is suggestive rather than a scaling study — the authors concede the placement triple plus four planted-secret configurations are "not a scaling study." The 8M-base, r=16 reading (1.85 bits/param) is flagged with a dagger (†) as an uncertain reading, yet is included in the headline 1.7–2.8 range; readers should treat the upper end cautiously. TinyLoRA configurations (4,096 params) never repay the base's own surprise at random data and are reported as "below the measurement floor rather than as zero" — a principled choice, but it means the smallest-adapter regime is effectively unmeasured.
- **Reproducibility:** Good. Fixed 16,000-step budget, declared learning-rate envelope, named bases (WikiText-103-pretrained 2M/8M GPT decoders), named probe (64 uniform tokens / 2,048-symbol vocab = 704 bits), named downstream model (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) and reward algorithm (GRPO). A harness is promised, which materially helps. Missing details pushed to appendices (the base-surprise deficit in Appendix C, the 40K-step sensitivity in Appendix B) are at least signposted.
- **Significance:** High if the framing catches on. Reframing a shared adapter as a data artifact with a measurable capacity — and giving practitioners placement (not just rank) as a privacy knob — is actionable. The SFT-vs-GRPO result lands squarely in a live debate (Chu et al. 2025 "SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes"; Morris et al. 2026a "13 parameters") and gives it non-behavioral evidence.
- **Red flags / overclaiming:** Mostly contained. The authors repeatedly label their numbers lower bounds and admit the secret-injection test is a stress test, not an accuracy-matched comparison (SFT hits 100% where GRPO is in the high nineties). The reward-entropy dial the conjecture predicts would move GRPO's write rate "does not measurably raise" it — which slightly undercuts the very conjecture they are testing (the bound is slack on these tasks). The opening "twenty megabytes of training text" framing for a 7B rank-16 adapter is an extrapolation from 2M/8M and 0.5B bases to 7B; it is presented as a worked example rather than a measurement, which is honest but worth noting.

## Strengths

- A clean, transferable instrument: three cheap readings computable before a checkpoint is shared, with random-string calibration that earns trust on real text.
- Two genuinely surprising empirical dissociations (placement, substrate) that overturn the default "more parameters = more memorization" reading and give practitioners new privacy knobs.
- The SFT-vs-GRPO comparison at matched accuracy, on the same base and rank, with a direct secret-injection stress test, is the most decisive bit-level evidence yet for the elicitation reading of RLVR.
- Intellectual honesty throughout: declared lower bounds, acknowledged corpus-size limits for MIA, the measurement-floor caveat for TinyLoRA, and explicit admission that the reward-entropy dial does not move.

## Limitations

- Scale. Synthetic-decoder bases are 2M/8M parameters; the only real-model audit is Qwen2.5-0.5B. The headline 7B "ten megabytes of text" figure is an extrapolation, not a measurement.
- Optimization budget. Every capacity is a lower bound at 16,000 steps; extending to 40,000 steps lifts readings by 10–24%, so absolute numbers are soft.
- Membership inference is unresolved. MIA AUC stays near 0.5 across the entire rank sweep; the paper's privacy story leans on canary *exposure*, not membership, and the authors concede their corpus is too small to test whether bits predict membership at larger scale.
- Narrow task suite for SFT vs. GRPO (3-digit arithmetic, key-value extraction). The "GRPO writes ~0" result is shown on two tasks where the reward operates far below any entropy bound; whether it generalizes to richer reward signals is open.
- Random-string probe is a calibration instrument, not a threat model — it isolates storage by stripping away everything learnable, so the random-sequence numbers should not be read as leak rates on real text.

## Relevance

This paper is directly on-target for anyone studying PEFT privacy, memorization measurement, or the SFT-vs-RL post-training debate: it turns the "is a shared LoRA file safe?" question into a pre-training quantity and gives concrete (rank, placement, base) levers for controlling leakage. It is also a methodological reference for future work that wants to weigh any frozen-base adaptation — quantized adapters, mixture-of-experts fine-tunes, or federated updates — in bits rather than in post-hoc attack AUC.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the writability plateau (1.7–2.8 bits/param) hold at 7B–70B scale, or does the MLP-vs-attention gap and the substrate effect change with model width and depth?
- Can a calibrated MIA or a larger corpus confirm that behavior-write bits predict membership inference, or is extraction genuinely the only attack that resolves adapter leakage?
- How does the picture change for non-LoRA PEFT methods (VeRA, LoRA-XS, DoRA, prefix tuning) — do they sit above or below the LoRA writability line at matched parameter count?
- For RLVR with denser / higher-entropy rewards (process rewards, tool-use traces), does the "GRPO writes ~0" result survive, or does richer reward signal start depositing bits into the adapter?
- Can the substrate-capacity finding be exploited as a defense — e.g., deliberately choosing a base or a placement that lowers writability below the corpus information content — without sacrificing task accuracy?
