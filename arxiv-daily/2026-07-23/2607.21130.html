▌ Critical edge — read before believing

The headline numbers rest on simulation, not silicon. Treat the strongest claims as promising-but-unproven.

  • All accuracy is from the ISA Simulator only — no FPGA/silicon wall-clock, throughput, or energy.
  • The 4× Zvfh VPU speed-up is an architectural projection, never synthesized or measured.
  • F16-vs-F32 deltas (+0.41% to −0.68%) sit within plausible run-to-run noise — no variance, seeds, or significance reported.

01 Methodology

A co-design across two open-source foundations

Software. The base is AIfES, a modular embedded DNN training/inference library, extended with RISC-V Zfh (scalar) and Zvfh (vector) float16 math primitives sourced from NMSIS-DSP. Its vector-length-agnostic kernels keep the code portable across any Zvfh core. Manual vectorization is preferred over auto-vectorization; an enhanced AIfES-Converter imports PyTorch/TensorFlow models with optional layer-freezing for transfer learning. Float16/Zvfh kernels are selected at compile time.

Hardware. Target is NaxRiscv — an open-source SpinalHDL RV64GC super-scalar out-of-order core, configured dual-issue with a 64-entry ROB and two 16 kB L1 caches. Zfh extends the decoder, LSU, and FPU across all 36 instructions, reusing the float64-widened compute path with Nan-Boxing. A proposed 4-lane VPU (VLEN=64-bit) merges the vector and float register files and handles register grouping (LMUL) by micro-op splitting at issue/renaming — except data-dependent ops like vrgather.vv, which would blow up the micro-op count.

Evaluation: software training on the RISC-V ISA Simulator; Zfh hardware cost from Vivado 2021.1 synthesis on Xilinx XC7K325T-2FFG900C.

03 Strengths & Limitations

What it proves — and what it doesn't

Strengths

  • First open-source unification of RISC-V + full ODT + float16 + batch>1, filling the gap left by PULP-Trainlib (batch 1) and AIfES (no F16/RISC-V).
  • Low, well-quantified Zfh cost (+1.15% LUT6, no Fmax loss) — a concrete, verifiable result for HW designers.
  • Clean modular build on AIfES + NMSIS-DSP, with PyTorch/TF import and layer-freezing for transfer learning.
  • Honest scoping — VPU evaluation is explicitly deferred rather than overclaimed.
  • Vendor-neutral, standards-based (Zfh/Zvfh/RVV) — portable, not a custom-ISA accelerator.

Limitations

  • Software results come only from the ISA Simulator — no on-device time, throughput, or energy.
  • The Zvfh VPU is not synthesized; its area cost and real speed-up are future work.
  • Shallow model zoo — only MLPs on MNIST/FashionMNIST/EMNIST; no CNNs, transformers, or larger data.
  • Accuracy comparisons lack variance, multiple seeds, or significance; deltas are within noise.
  • No head-to-head benchmark vs quantized/hybrid ODT baselines — Table 1 is a feature checklist only.
  • No discussion of float16 instability mitigations (loss scaling, stochastic rounding).

04 Open questions

Where this needs to go next

  1. Does float16 training stability scale to CNNs, transformers, or larger datasets (CIFAR, ImageNet subsets) where the f16 range is more strained?
  2. What are the actual on-FPGA wall-clock times and energy/epoch once the Zvfh VPU is synthesized — does the projected 4× survive training being backward-pass/memory-bound?
  3. Would loss scaling or stochastic rounding close the small accuracy gap to float32, especially for Adam?
  4. How does it compare head-to-head against PULP-Trainlib (multi-core) and quantized/hybrid ODT baselines on the same workload?
  5. What is the synthesized area and timing cost of the full VPU, and how does it scale with VLEN and lane count?
  6. Can layer-freezing + float16 support federated / personalized learning loops on MCU-class RISC-V parts with severe RAM limits?
