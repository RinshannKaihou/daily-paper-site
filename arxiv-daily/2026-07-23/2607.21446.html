§ 01

Methodology

DiTs interleave Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) between every block, so the LLM “absorption trick” fails — an activation transform T cannot fold into the previous layer and must run online at every layer, every denoising step. Online cost is the binding constraint. KroQuant resolves the expressivity-vs-cost trade-off by constraining T to block-diagonal 32×32 blocks matching the MXFP4e2 quantizer.

1Kronecker construction

Each 32×32 block = Kronecker product of five 2×2 factors. Invertible overall ⟺ each factor invertible → inversion is five cheap 2×2 inversions.

2Unit-determinant LU

Each factor is |det|=1 via LU with 3 free scalars → 15 params/block. Contains the normalized Hadamard as a valid init.

3Per-layer calibration

Adam, 100 steps, lr 1e-2 on 128 activations; STE through the quantizer. Joint multi-layer optimization is unstable, so layers are optimized independently.

4Kernels + LoRaQ

LoRaQ’s offline rank-128 weight correction is kept intact. Two Triton kernels apply each block as one tensor-core GEMM: K1 (precomputed) & K2 (on-the-fly, 68× compression).

§ 03

Strengths & Limitations

✓Strengths

  • Clean formalization of why LLM rotation tricks break in DiTs — AdaLN non-commutativity — and the online-cost constraint that follows.
  • 15 params/block, < half of per-channel scaling, yet strictly generalizes the Hadamard — an elegant sweet spot.
  • Two kernel variants give deployment flexibility; K2’s bit-indexed on-the-fly reconstruction is a clever register-level optimization.
  • Honest reporting — mixed FLUX results and the n=32 ceiling are disclosed, not hidden.
  • Drop-in: composes directly with SVDQuant / LoRaQ weight-splitting pipelines.

!Limitations

  • Block size hard-locked to n=32; O(log n) params cannot scale to larger GL blocks — the main ceiling on further gains.
  • Per-layer only — joint multi-layer optimization unstable under STE + unit-determinant LU; no cross-layer error redistribution.
  • Speedups are MI350 / CDNA-4 only; no NVIDIA H100 / Blackwell numbers.
  • Loses on FLUX/MJHQ: IR 0.880 vs SVDQuant 0.952, LPIPS 0.336 vs LoRaQ 0.331 — double-stream heterogeneity, unfixed.
  • No comparison to learned full-d transforms (SpinQuant / FlatQuant), even as a quality upper bound.
§ 04

Open Questions

  • Q1Does KroQuant scale as the MX block size grows beyond 32 (64 / 128) without breaking the O(d) parameter budget?
  • Q2Would a per-stream / per-block-type transform recover the IR / LPIPS deficit on FLUX’s double-stream architecture?
  • Q3Can a differentiable or half-quadratic alternative to the STE stabilize joint multi-layer optimization?
  • Q4Do the MI350 speedups transfer to NVIDIA H100 / Blackwell tensor cores, or are they CDNA-4 artifacts?
  • Q5Is the higher-contrast output a systematic bias of the unit-determinant LU factors — and can it be regularized?
  • Q6How does KroQuant compose with sub-4-bit activation quantization (W4A3 / INT3), where outlier suppression is critical?
