> **一句话总结：** 提出 GABench——首个在生产级框架（LangChain/LlamaIndex/Vectara）上评测 LLM 智能体安全性的基准（580 场景、6 领域、5 种对抗攻击），发现最强配置（Claude Opus 4.5 on Vectara）整体准确率仅 74.8%，揭示"强模型漏调用工具、弱模型乱调用工具"两种失效模式，并提出一种在工具执行前拦截的轻量级 guardrail，相对系统提示词防御在所有 6 个模型上稳定提升 +2.8~+7.7 分、以 0.5% 误报率挽回 19.9% 的失败。

> **TL;DR:** GuardianAgentBench (GABench) is the first agent-safety benchmark run on production frameworks (LangChain, LlamaIndex, Vectara) rather than simulated sandboxes: 580 scenarios across 6 domains with 5 adversarial attack modes and multi-stage human validation. Six frontier models (Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2 Pro, GPT-OSS-120B, Gemini-3-Pro, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-Max) are tested, and the best configuration (Claude Opus 4.5 on Vectara) reaches only 74.8 Overall accuracy on GABench (Calendar domain is hardest, capped at 62.0). The analysis exposes two failure regimes—stronger models under-call required tools (MTC 52–57% of failures) while weaker models mis-select and over-call (ITS up to 25%, RTC 29–33%)—and shows performance drops monotonically with tool-set size (78.2→62.3, −15.9 pts) and turn depth (82.3→51.2, −31.1 pts). An execution-time guardrail (3 checks, Claude-Sonnet-4.5-powered) beats a system-prompt baseline on every model by +2.8 to +7.7 points (e.g., Claude Opus 4.5 +5.7 vs +0.4 for the prompt baseline), recovering 19.9% of failures at a 0.5% false-positive rate.

## 核心贡献

- **首个面向生产级框架的智能体安全基准（GABench）。** 覆盖 580 个场景、6 个领域（Customer Service 118、Email 117、Calendar 105、Financial 99、Business Intelligence 77、Internal Knowledge 64）、81 个工具、1,177 个串行 turn；398 个 happy-path（68.6%）+ 182 个对抗场景（31.4%），在 LangChain、LlamaIndex、Vectara 三个真实部署框架上端到端执行。
- **提出可操作的失效分类与两种失效模式。** 将工具调用错误归为 5 类（ITS/IMP/MTC/RTC/ITO），并实证发现"强模型漏调工具（MTC 52–57%）、弱模型选错/重复调（ITS 14–25%、RTC 29–33%）"，ITO（顺序错误）最罕见（0.6–4.4%）说明多步顺序已基本解决。
- **量化复杂度对安全的衰减。** 工具数从 1 增到 7，准确率从 78.2 跌到 62.3；turn 深度从 1 增到 7，从 82.3 跌到 51.2——长程规划是比工具数量更陡的瓶颈（−31.1 vs −15.9）。
- **执行时结构性 guardrail 显著优于系统提示词防御。** 三个 guardrail（Argument Validation / Tool Coverage Check / Relevance & Cost Check）在所有 6 个模型上稳定 +2.8~+7.7，对强模型差距尤为明显（Claude Opus 4.5 上 guardrail +5.7 vs prompt +0.4），并在 Claude Opus 4.5/LlamaIndex 上以 19.9% 挽回率、0.5% 误报率精确介入。
- **严谨的多阶段验证流程。** 五阶段 LLM 生成流水线 + 自动校验 + 双标注员人工审核 + 3 轮反馈修正；Claude Sonnet 4.5 judge 在 60 个抽样上与人类 93.3% 一致（56/60）。

## Key Contributions

- **First production-platform agent-safety benchmark.** GABench runs 580 scenarios (6 domains, 81 tools, 1,177 sequential turns) end-to-end on LangChain, LlamaIndex, and Vectara—the deployed frameworks practitioners actually use—rather than bespoke Python sandboxes, closing the gap between benchmarks and practice.
- **Actionable failure taxonomy and two empirically distinct failure regimes.** Five failure types (ITS, IMP, MTC, RTC, ITO) reveal that stronger models fail by *omitting* required calls (MTC 52–57%) while weaker models fail by *mis-selecting and repeating* (ITS up to 25%, RTC 29–33%); tool-ordering errors (ITO) are nearly solved (0.6–4.4%).
- **Quantified complexity scaling.** Overall accuracy falls monotonically with tool-set size (78.2 at 1 tool → 62.3 at 7 tools, −15.9 pts) and turn depth (82.3 at 1 turn → 51.2 at 7 turns, −31.1 pts), establishing long-horizon planning as the steeper bottleneck.
- **Execution-time structural guardrails beat prompt-based defenses.** A proof-of-concept of three guardrails (Argument Validation, Tool Coverage Check, Relevance & Cost Check) outperforms a system-prompt defense on all six models (+2.8 to +7.7), with the widest margins on the strongest models, and recovers 19.9% of failures at a 0.5% false-positive rate.
- **Rigorous validation pipeline.** Five-stage LLM generation + automatic checks + dual-annotator human review + three refinement cycles; the Claude Sonnet 4.5 judge achieves 93.3% agreement (56/60) with human annotations.

## Methodology

**Scenario construction** is a two-stage automated pipeline with human validation. Stage 1 builds a *happy-path* scenario through five LLM-driven sub-stages: (i) **User Intent Generation** from the agent's system prompt + tool specs; (ii) **User Prompt Generation** producing both *self-contained* and *incomplete-info* variants per intent; (iii) **Tool Response Simulation**, where parameterized tools get a mapping of argument combinations to responses (with `exact` or `semantic` match strategies and `is_implicit` flags for inferred args); (iv) **Ground Truth Trace Construction** producing the dependency-annotated execution DAG; and (v) **Response Evaluation Criteria** defining response-quality, acceptable proactive-offering, and forbidden-behavior rubrics. Stage 2 derives *adversarial variants* by mutating tool responses along five modes—**Massive Data**, **Error Conditions**, **Multiple Matches**, **Prompt Injection**, **Partial Data**—each targeting a distinct robustness failure, regenerating the ground truth after each mutation.

**Execution.** Each scenario runs on three production frameworks through a unified adapter interface that standardizes tool registration, invocation, and trace collection, so behavioral differences are attributable to the framework rather than evaluation artifacts: LlamaIndex `FunctionAgent` with `FunctionTool` objects (streaming-event grouped by step), LangChain `create_react_agent` via LangGraph with `StructuredTool` wrappers (parsed from message history), and fully cloud-based Vectara (Python-lambda tools deployed via REST API, structured events parsed from platform responses). Every agent receives the same system prompt, tool set, and user query across frameworks. Six frontier models are evaluated at default API parameters (temperature, top-p, extended-thinking where applicable).

**Scoring.** A Claude Sonnet 4.5 judge scores each scenario on two independent dimensions—**response correctness** (final answer faithfully synthesizes tool outputs without hallucination) and **action correctness** (tool-call sequence matches the ground-truth DAG in selection, arguments, and ordering); Overall requires both. Failed executions are classified into the five taxonomy types. The judge was validated against human labels on 60 sampled cases (93.3% alignment, 56/60).

**Guardrails.** Three lightweight, rule-based checks (Argument Validation, Tool Coverage Check, Relevance & Cost Check) are integrated into LlamaIndex's `FunctionAgent` via a custom `llama-index-core` extension. Before each tool step all three run concurrently (`asyncio.gather`) over the agent's full context and issue one of three verdicts—Pass / Provide Corrective Feedback / Block. Feedback is folded into a single corrective message; the agent retries up to two iterations; persistent violations permanently block the call and raise a human-in-the-loop alert. All three guardrails are powered by Claude Sonnet 4.5 at default settings.

**Statistics.** 580 scenarios over 6 domains (Customer Service 118, Email 117, Calendar 105, Financial 99, Business Intelligence 77, Internal Knowledge 64); 398 non-adversarial (68.6%) and 182 adversarial (31.4%); mean tool-set size 2.74 (range 1–7); mean turns 2.09 (range 1–8).

## Results & Highlights

- **Best configuration reaches only 74.8 Overall.** Claude Opus 4.5 on Vectara tops the leaderboard at 74.8 Overall (Response 82.6 / Action 82.8), meaning agents still fail roughly one in four scenarios on GABench; the weakest model, DeepSeek-V3.2 on Vectara, sits at 63.0 Overall.
- **Calendar is the hardest domain, capped at 62.0.** No model exceeds 62.0 Overall on Calendar (Claude Opus 4.5/LangChain reaches it), while Financial and Customer Service are the most tractable (Claude Opus 4.5 hits 83.0 Customer Service on LlamaIndex, 82.0 Financial on LangChain).
- **Clear model hierarchy mirrors general capability rankings.** Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini-3-Pro lead at 71–75 average Overall; GPT-5.2 Pro and Qwen3-Max at 68–72; DeepSeek-V3.2 and GPT-OSS-120B trail at 63–68.
- **Two failure regimes are qualitatively distinct.** Stronger models fail mainly via Missing Required Tool Call—GPT-5.2 Pro and GPT-OSS-120B at MTC 55–57%, Gemini-3-Pro at 52–55%—while weaker DeepSeek-V3.2 and Qwen3-Max show MTC only 36–40% but compensate with high RTC (29–33%) and ITS (14–25%).
- **Tool ordering is essentially solved.** Incorrect Tool Order (ITO) is the rarest failure type across all models and frameworks, at just 0.6–4.4%.
- **Performance degrades monotonically with complexity (Claude Opus 4.5).** Tool-set size: 78.2 (1 tool) → 62.3 (7 tools), a 15.9-point drop; sequential turns: 82.3 (1 turn) → 51.2 (7 turns), a 31.1-point drop—long-horizon planning is the steeper bottleneck.
- **Framework choice has minimal impact.** Within each model, Overall scores vary by at most 2–3 points across LlamaIndex, LangChain, and Vectara, confirming the gaps are model-driven, not framework-driven.
- **Guardrails beat system-prompt defense on every model.** Boosts range from +2.8 (GPT-5.2 Pro) to +7.7 (DeepSeek-V3.2); system-prompt gains are negligible or negative for the strongest models (Gemini-3-Pro +0.3, Claude Opus 4.5 +0.4, GPT-5.2 Pro −0.3) but larger for weaker ones (DeepSeek-V3.2 +5.5).
- **Guardrail precision is high.** On Claude Opus 4.5/LlamaIndex, of 151 originally-failing scenarios the guardrail recovers 30 (19.9% recovery rate) while of 429 originally-passing scenarios only 2 are wrongly blocked (0.5% false-positive rate).

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from **Vectara, Inc. (Palo Alto)** with Chenyu Xu of **Iowa State University**; corresponding author Vishal Ishwar Naik is at Vectara. The paper is **accepted at the ICML 2026 Workshop on Failure Modes in Agentic AI** (Seoul), a directly topical venue, lending it credible peer-reviewed status. The authors have a clear practitioner background—Vectara is itself a commercial agent platform, which both motivates the production-framework focus and creates an alignment-of-interest the paper does not explicitly disclose. The empirical scope is substantial: six frontier models × three frameworks × six domains × five adversarial modes, with a disclosed judge-validation protocol (93.3% human alignment on 60 cases).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-positioned. Being the first to evaluate agent safety on *production* frameworks (LangChain/LlamaIndex/Vectara) rather than simulated Python sandboxes is a real contribution, and the two-regime failure characterization (under-call vs. mis-call) is a crisp, actionable diagnostic. The taxonomy itself is fairly conventional, however, echoing prior work (AgentSafetyBench, ToolEmu, MT-AgentRisk).
- **Rigor:** Mostly sound. The validation pipeline (multi-stage generation + dual annotators + three refinement cycles) is more careful than most, and the unified adapter interface isolates framework effects. But the headline 74.8 figure and all deltas are reported as point estimates with **no confidence intervals or variance**, and the "sequential reasoning is solved" claim rests solely on ITO being rare—a judge artifact or coarse ordering metric could mask subtle ordering errors.
- **Reproducibility:** Mixed. Frameworks and models are named and defaults are specified, and prompt templates are fully disclosed in the appendix, which is commendable. However, the 580 scenarios and their ground-truth traces are not clearly stated to be released, the guardrail code is LlamaIndex-specific and not described as open-sourced, and several models are proprietary APIs whose behavior may drift—reproducing exact numbers will be hard.
- **Significance:** High for practitioners building tool-using agents; the production-platform framing and the guardrail-vs-prompt comparison directly inform deployment. The finding that added system-prompt safety text can *hurt* strong models is a valuable corrective to common practice.
- **Red flags:**
  - **Vendor conflict of interest.** Vectara is both (a) an author affiliation and commercial product and (b) one of the three benchmarked platforms; the single best configuration (Claude Opus 4.5 on Vectara, 74.8) happens to run on the authors' own platform. The paper does not disclose this interest.
  - **Judge–leaderboard vendor alignment.** The judge is Claude Sonnet 4.5 (Anthropic) and the top model is Claude Opus 4.5 (Anthropic); same-vendor judging a same-vendor winner invites bias even if unintentional, and judge validation is only on 60 cases.
  - **Apples-to-oranges defense comparison.** The guardrails are backed by an additional Claude Sonnet 4.5 LLM making per-call decisions, whereas the baseline is a static system-prompt string—the headline "+2.8 to +7.7" is partly a comparison of "extra LLM reasoning" vs "a sentence of text," not purely guardrail-vs-prompt. The paper frames it as the latter.
  - **Narrow generalization of the guardrail claim.** Guardrails are implemented only in LlamaIndex, yet Table 7's precision analysis (19.9% / 0.5%) is reported for a single model on a single framework (Claude Opus 4.5 / LlamaIndex); the recovery rate is not validated across models or frameworks.
  - **Missing ablations.** No breakdown of which of the three guardrails contributes what, and no ablation isolating each adversarial mode's contribution to the failure regimes.

## Strengths

- First benchmark to test agent safety on actual production frameworks, materially increasing ecological validity over sandbox-only predecessors.
- Clean, well-controlled experimental design via a unified adapter interface that isolates model vs. framework effects.
- The two-regime failure decomposition (under-call for strong models, mis-/over-call for weak ones) is intuitive, well-supported by Table 6, and immediately actionable for model-specific remediation.
- Honest, somewhat counterintuitive finding that system-prompt safety instructions provide near-zero or negative gains for the strongest models—useful guidance that challenges default practice.
- Disclosed, falsifiable methodology: full prompt templates in the appendix, named models at default settings, disclosed judge and its human-alignment rate.
- High guardrail precision (19.9% recovery at 0.5% FPR) demonstrates that execution-time interception can improve safety without breaking correct behavior.

## Limitations

- **Scope is bounded:** 580 scenarios, 6 domains, English only, and only 5 adversarial attack modes; coverage of long-horizon (7+ turn) scenarios is thin (only a handful at 6–8 turns), undercutting confidence in the steepest part of the turn-depth curve.
- **Simulated, not real, tool execution.** All tool responses are mocked by the generation pipeline, so the benchmark cannot expose failures arising from genuine API latency, auth drift, schema evolution, or real-world data noise.
- **Single judge model (Claude Sonnet 4.5).** No cross-model judge agreement study beyond the 60-case human alignment, so judge bias cannot be ruled out—especially given the same-vendor top model.
- **No statistical significance or variance reporting** on any headline number or delta.
- **Guardrails are a proof of concept on one framework (LlamaIndex) and one judge LLM;** their cost (extra LLM calls per tool step), latency, and transferability to LangChain/Vectara are not measured.
- **Adversarial scenarios are LLM-generated, not hand-crafted by red-teamers,** so they may miss the most creative or worst-case attacks; the benchmark measures robustness to *plausible* perturbations, not adversarial optimization.
- **No released dataset/code commitment** is clearly stated, threatening reproducibility and independent follow-up.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 智能体（agentic AI）工具使用与安全性的研究高度相关：它把安全评测从模拟沙箱搬到 LangChain/LlamaIndex/Vectara 等真实生产框架，并给出"强模型漏调用、弱模型乱调用"的失效画像与可落地的执行时 guardrail，直接服务于构建更鲁棒的工具型智能体与 agent 安全护栏这一方向。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the judge-vendor alignment (Claude judging Claude) inflate the leaderboard? Would a cross-vendor or human panel re-rank models, especially on the 6–8 turn scenarios where scores are lowest?
- How much of the "+2.8 to +7.7" guardrail gain survives if the guardrail is stripped of its backing LLM and reduced to pure schema/rule checks—i.e., what is the marginal value of the LLM-powered verdict versus simple argument validation?
- Which of the three guardrails (Argument Validation, Tool Coverage, Relevance/Cost) drives the recovery, and does Tool Coverage Check alone account for most of the MTC-regime fix?
- Does the under-call vs. mis-call dichotomy hold on genuinely long-horizon tasks (15+ turns) and with real, non-simulated tool APIs that fail in correlated ways?
- How do the failure regimes and guardrail efficacy change under optimized adversarial attacks (e.g., gradient-based prompt injection) rather than LLM-generated perturbations?
- Can the guardrail's 0.5% false-positive rate be maintained when tools have overlapping functionality, where "redundant" calls may actually be legitimate fallbacks?
