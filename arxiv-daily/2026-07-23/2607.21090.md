> **一句话总结：** 本文首次将反事实忠实度指标（Phi-CCT）改造为 GRPO 强化学习奖励，直接优化 LLM 自我解释的忠实度，使 Llama3.1-8B / Qwen3-8B 的 Phi-CCT 从近 0 提升至 0.664（用户偏置，分布内）乃至 0.691（StrategyQA 等 OOD 任务），且未出现逐字复制等奖励黑客行为。

> **TL;DR:** The authors turn the Phi-CCT faithfulness metric (correlation between whether a counterfactual intervention flips the model's decision and whether the explanation mentions it) into a per-sample binary reward and train LLMs with GRPO. On Llama3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B, in-distribution (e-SNLI / Social-IQA) Phi-CCT rises from near-zero baselines (e.g., 0.038) to 0.664 for user-bias interventions, and OOD transfer on StrategyQA/ComVE reaches 0.691 (Qwen3-8B, +0.643 over a 0.048 base). They explicitly analyze and rule out reward-gaming (no overlap-ratio spikes, no silent collapse), and honestly disclose that influence labels are frozen off-policy and that cross-intervention transfer is asymmetric and model-dependent.

## 核心贡献

- **首个直接优化"解释忠实度"的训练框架**：将 Siegel 等人提出的 Phi-CCT（干预是否改变决策 与 解释是否提及干预 之间的相关性）从纯评测指标改造为逐样本二值奖励 `r = 1{M ⇔ I}`，并用 GRPO 直接更新模型参数，而非仅在推理时做提示工程或对齐外部金标签。
- **揭示"检测"与"披露"的分离现象**：SFT 能教会模型检测哪些干预翻转了它的决策（相关性可达 0.66+），但这些被检测到的影响因素并不会自然出现在模型的自解释里——说明"知道"不等于"说出来"，只有 RL 才能把二者对齐。
- **跨数据集的良好泛化**：仅在 e-SNLI / Social-IQA 上训练，模型在 StrategyQA / ComVE 等 OOD 任务上 Phi-CCT 仍可达 0.691，提示模型学到了抽象的"影响→披露"规则而非模板记忆。
- **系统的奖励黑客排查**：通过 Completion Length 和 Overlap Ratio 两个指标分析，证明提升并非来自"逐字复制 prompt"或"全部沉默"等退化策略，Phi-CCT 的设计本身对两类静态作弊不敏感。
- **诚实的局限披露**：明确指出训练所用的影响标签来自冻结的初始模型（off-policy），随着策略漂移可能失效，并将其列为后续 on-policy 工作的关键方向。

## Key Contributions

- **First training framework that directly optimizes explanatory faithfulness.** Prior work either evaluated faithfulness (Phi-CCT, CCT, Atanasova-style tests) or improved *causal* faithfulness / plausibility via inference-time prompting (FLARE, question decomposition) or external-gold rewards (MARIO, FRODO). None directly optimized the counterfactual-correlation notion of *explanatory* faithfulness, which has no static ground truth because "is this explanation faithful?" depends on the acting model's own decisions.
- **A per-sample reward derived from a dataset-level metric.** Phi-CCT is a dataset-level correlation `Corr(I, M)`; the authors decompose it into a per-instance binary signal `r = 1{M ⇔ I}` covering all four influence×mention cases (TP/FN/FP/TN), which penalizes both indiscriminate mentioning and indiscriminate silence.
- **Empirical dissociation of detection vs. disclosure.** SFT on influence introspection lifts predicted-vs-true-influence correlation substantially but fails to make models *verbalize* those factors; only RL with the mention-alignment reward closes the gap.
- **Cross-dataset generalization and a partial cross-intervention signal.** Training on one intervention type (random-word insertions) yields a non-trivial 0.178 Phi-CCT when Llama3.1-8B is evaluated on the unseen user-bias type — suggestive evidence of an abstract "influence → disclose" rule rather than template memorization.
- **Explicit reward-gaming analysis.** Completion-length and overlap-ratio diagnostics confirm improvements come from selective integration of interventions into reasoning, not from parroting prompt segments or collapsing to silence.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** For a model with parameters θ taking prompt X to decision Y and explanation Z, latent factors U drive both. Without access to U, the authors use a *single-factor intervention* Δ inserted into X to form X′ = X ⊕ Δ. Because Δ is the only controlled difference, any decision flip Y ≠ Y′ is attributable to it. A faithful counterfactual explanation Z′ should mention Δ iff Δ actually flipped the decision.

**Faithfulness definition.** Two binary variables: influence `I = 1{Y′ ≠ Y}` and mention `M = 1{Δ mentioned in Z′}`. Phi-CCT = `Corr(I, M)` over the dataset (Siegel et al., 2025). The training reward is the per-sample agreement `r = 1{M ⇔ I}`, which covers all four influence×mention cells and is robust to the two simplest static hacks (always-mention → Corr 0; never-mention → Corr 0).

**Data construction.** Faithfulness is model-specific, so a separate dataset is built per (model × dataset) pair. For each factual prompt x, candidate interventions are generated, ranked by an LLM for naturalness/semantic cohesion, and the best is kept. Two intervention families:
- *Random-word insertions (RI):* neutral adverbs/phrases inserted at POS-constrained slots (NLTK POS-tagging), semantically arbitrary.
- *User-bias insertions (UB):* explicit social-appeal hints (e.g., "My teacher believes the answer is A").

Datasets are **class-balanced** (I=1 vs I=0 at parity via iterative sampling + majority subsampling) so that a fixed strategy like "always output the intervention" yields reward mean 0.5 and Phi-CCT 0, forcing genuine discrimination. Train/Test come from e-SNLI and Social-IQA; OOD from ComVE and StrategyQA.

**Training stages.**
1. *Influence Introspection (SFT):* model fine-tuned with cross-entropy to output Yes/No on whether Δ flips its decision; evaluated by predicted-vs-true influence correlation.
2. *Influence-Mention Alignment (RL):* GRPO with 16 generations per prompt, accumulated over 4 generation steps before each update; reward applied only to the counterfactual explanation tokens z′.

**Evaluation.** Phi-CCT with 95% percentile bootstrap CIs (5,000 resamples, ~2,000 examples/split). Reward-gaming monitored via Completion Length (word count of Z′) and Overlap Ratio (distinct shared words / length of Z′).

**Important off-policy caveat (acknowledged).** Influence labels I are computed once from the frozen T0 model and never resampled. As the policy shifts, labels go stale: the reward targets alignment with T0's decisions while evaluation targets alignment with the *current* trained model's decisions. Reported gains may partly reflect decisions drifting toward stale labels rather than purely improved disclosure.

## Results & Highlights

- **In-distribution Phi-CCT (RL, user-bias, Llama3.1-8B):** rises from a Base of 0.038 ± 0.038 to **0.664 ± 0.026** on the Test split (e-SNLI / Social-IQA) — a delta of +0.626.
- **In-distribution Phi-CCT (RL, user-bias, Qwen3-8B):** rises from Base 0.058 ± 0.037 to **0.607 ± 0.027**, delta +0.549.
- **In-distribution Phi-CCT (RL, random insertions, Llama3.1-8B):** rises from Base 0.048 ± 0.042 to **0.536 ± 0.037** (Test), delta +0.488; Qwen3-8B from 0.210 ± 0.040 to **0.404 ± 0.038**, delta +0.194.
- **OOD generalization (RL on UB, Qwen3-8B, StrategyQA/ComVE):** reaches **0.691 ± 0.036** Phi-CCT vs Base 0.048 ± 0.051 — a delta of +0.643 on tasks the model was never trained on.
- **SFT influence-introspection (random insertions, Llama3.1-8B):** predicted-vs-true-influence correlation rises from **-0.013 to 0.537** (Test); Qwen3-8B from **0.110 to 0.552**; user-bias from 0.036 → 0.664 (Llama) and 0.032 → 0.607 (Qwen). But these SFT models then *fail* to verbalize the detected factors when asked to explain.
- **Cross-intervention transfer (asymmetric):** Llama3.1-8B trained on random insertions and evaluated on user bias scores **0.178 ± 0.044** Phi-CCT (vs near-zero base) — non-trivial generalization from a harder, semantically arbitrary signal to a social-template one. The reverse direction and all Qwen3-8B cross-intervention cells do *not* replicate this.
- **Reward-gaming ruled out:** no significant increase in Overlap Ratio for True-Positive cases after RL (no parroting); RL does shorten explanations for predicted-negatives (a "risk-averse" conciseness that raises precision) but does not collapse to silence, and Phi-CCT on balanced data is provably robust to both always-mention and never-mention strategies.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel as a *training* contribution. FRODO (DPO on causal dependency) and MARIO (RL with an external-judge consistency reward) optimize related-but-different targets; this is the first to directly optimize the counterfactual-correlation (Phi-CCT) notion of *explanatory* faithfulness, which is harder precisely because it has no reusable ground truth. The decomposition of a dataset-level correlation into a per-sample binary reward is clean and the balanced-class construction is a sensible anti-hack design.
- **Rigor:** Strong on the faithfulness metric itself (bootstrap CIs, four-cell reward, explicit reward-gaming diagnostics). Weaker on the training-time vs. evaluation-time mismatch: the authors *honestly* concede that influence labels are frozen off-policy, so part of the reported Phi-CCT gain could be the trained model's decisions drifting toward stale T0 labels rather than improved disclosure. This is the single most important threat to the central claim and it is acknowledged, not hidden — but it is also not quantified or ablated away.
- **Baseline realism:** The strongest "baselines" are the un-fine-tuned Base models (Phi-CCT ≈ 0.03–0.21), which trivially under-disclose. There is **no comparison against FRODO, MARIO, FLARE, or question-decomposition prompting** on the same Phi-CCT benchmark. Given that those are the obvious competing faithfulness-improvement methods, their absence is the most conspicuous gap.
- **Reproducibility:** Model families (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B), intervention rules (NLTK POS-constrained insertion, user-bias templates), balancing procedure, GRPO config (16 gens × 4 accumulation steps), and metric (Phi-CCT with 5,000-resample bootstrap) are described in enough detail to reproduce. Datasets are public. The mention-detection is morphological-variant matching only, which is a stated simplification that could undercount mentions; reproducers should note this.
- **Significance:** A credible proof-of-concept that explanatory faithfulness is an optimizable target, with honest scope. The cross-intervention asymmetry (random→bias transfers 0.178; bias→random and all Qwen cells do not) is genuinely interesting but under-explained and rests on a single model family, so it should be read as suggestive, not established.
- **Red flags:** (a) Only two 8B models, no instruction-tuning-variant or scale sweep — generalization to larger/frontier models is unverified. (b) 6k-sample balanced subsets of four datasets is a narrow slice of "natural language." (c) SFT models "often providing generic explanations (the word does not affect my decision)" — a possible premature-`<eos>` failure mode the authors flag but neither confirm nor rule out. (d) No plausibility or human-preference scores are reported, so we cannot tell whether the more faithful explanations are also acceptable to humans.

## Strengths

- Cleanly converts a dataset-level evaluation metric into a per-sample training signal without trivially gameable shortcuts.
- The detection-vs-disclosure dissociation (SFT detects but doesn't verbalize; RL does both) is a sharp, instructive finding.
- Balanced-class dataset construction explicitly removes the two easiest reward-hacking equilibria.
- Transparent, non-defensive limitations section, including the off-policy label staleness issue.
- Cross-dataset OOD transfer (up to 0.691) is the most practically encouraging result and is reported with confidence intervals.
- Honest handling of asymmetric/non-replicating results (Llama random→bias transfers, the reverse does not, Qwen doesn't replicate) rather than cherry-picking.

## Limitations

- **Off-policy influence labels:** I is computed once from the frozen T0 model; as the policy drifts the reward and the evaluation target diverge, and the drift is not quantified.
- **Mention detection is morphological only:** synonyms or paraphrases are missed, underestimating M and possibly inflating apparent faithfulness gaps.
- **Random insertions restricted to single words**, and naturalness ranking cannot guarantee zero semantic shift.
- **Narrow model and data scope:** only Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B; 6k balanced subsets of four datasets; no scale or model-family sweep beyond these two.
- **No comparison to competing faithfulness methods** (FRODO, MARIO, FLARE, decomposition prompting) on Phi-CCT.
- **No plausibility/human-judgment scores** and no analysis of how task accuracy or calibration changes after training.
- **Workshop-paper scope:** short, single-GPU, proof-of-concept rather than a full-scale study.

## Relevance

与 LLM 可解释性、推理忠实度（CoT faithfulness）及 RL 后训练高度相关——本文提供了把"忠实度"从评测指标变成可优化训练目标的具体范式，对关注 chain-of-thought 是否可信、反事实探针、以及 GRPO 奖励设计的研究者都是有价值的参考；其"检测≠披露"的发现也提示在做可解释性监督时不能只靠 SFT。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a fully **on-policy** variant that resamples I each GRPO step preserve the gains, or do the reported numbers shrink once labels track the current policy?
- How do **FRODO, MARIO, FLARE, and question-decomposition prompting** compare on the same Phi-CCT benchmark under matched compute?
- Does the method **scale** beyond 8B (e.g., 70B, frontier instruction-tuned models), and does the random→bias cross-intervention transfer asymmetry persist or invert?
- Can the reward be extended to **multi-factor / multi-token interventions** instead of single-word Δ, and to free-form paraphrased mentions rather than morphological matching?
- What is the **plausibility–faithfulness trade-off**: are the more faithful RL explanations still judged acceptable by humans, and does task accuracy / calibration degrade?
- Does faithful-disclosure training improve **adversarial robustness** to prompt-injection / jailbreak hidden tokens, as the authors speculate in §5.2?
- Can **multi-objective rewards** (faithfulness + task accuracy + calibration) be combined without inducing the training instability or reward hacking the authors warn about?
