Methodology

A compression-based memorization meter, ported to the frozen-base regime. For tuned model θ̂ vs reference θ_ref, memorization is the reference codelength the tuned model undercuts.

The random-string probe makes the reference exact: 64 uniform tokens from a 2,048-symbol vocab after a start token give H_K(x|θ_ref) = 704 bits by construction — nothing is learnable, so every saved bit is a stored bit. A held-out zero check (mem_U ≈ 0) is verified across the whole model spine.

Three readings per config, each answering a different question: writability α̂_ad (peak memorization per param, a floor), artifact cost CL(Δθ) (entropy-coded quantized weights, an upper bound on what the file ships), and behavior-write ΔW (policy likelihoods with generalization subtracted — the scale that lets SFT and RL be weighed together).

Protocol is fixed and declared: a 16,000-step budget, no early stopping, learning-rate envelope reported, every value a lower bound (40K steps lifts readings 10–24%). Bases are GPT-style decoders (2M / 8M non-embedding params) pretrained on WikiText-103 then frozen; adapters are LoRA and weight-tied TinyLoRA.

Results & highlights

01Adapters plateau below the full-model line

Full FT of the same base climbs past 3 bits/param; LoRA stops at 1.7–2.8 under the matched 16K-step budget. The gap survives compute and does not close.

02Capacity depends on placement, not count

Fixing trainable params at ~37K and moving weights attention→MLP nearly doubles per-param capacity (1.30 → 2.43). Parameter count held flat.

03Capacity depends on the frozen base

Same LoRA memorizes 29% on a random base, 98% on WikiText; full FT of that random base hits 99%. The shortfall is specific to writing through frozen weights.

04Precision barely matters

Casting a trained adapter to bf16 costs ~1% of memorization while halving the file; fp16 costs essentially nothing.

05Leakage follows written bits, not count

Rank 4→16 quadruples params but moves nothing; rank 16→64 quadruples again and canary exposure jumps ~11 bits. Loss-based MIA and Min-K% stay near chance throughout.

06The step that writes is the step that leaks

SFT memorizes planted secrets verbatim (exposure 12.0–13.3 bits); GRPO leaves them at chance (1.1 bits) — non-behavioral evidence for the elicitation reading of RLVR.

Strengths & limitations

Strengths
  • A clean, transferable instrument: three cheap readings computable before a checkpoint is shared, with random-string calibration that earns trust on real text.
  • Two genuinely surprising dissociations (placement, substrate) that overturn “more params = more memorization” and hand practitioners new privacy knobs.
  • The most decisive bit-level evidence yet for the elicitation reading of RLVR — SFT vs. GRPO on the same base and rank, with a direct secret-injection stress test.
  • Intellectual honesty throughout: declared lower bounds, acknowledged corpus-size limits for MIA, the measurement-floor caveat for TinyLoRA.
Limitations
  • Scale. Synthetic decoders are 2M/8M; the only real-model audit is Qwen2.5-0.5B. The headline 7B “ten megabytes of text” figure is an extrapolation, not a measurement.
  • Optimization budget. Every capacity is a lower bound at 16,000 steps; 40,000 steps lifts readings 10–24%, so absolute numbers are soft.
  • Membership inference unresolved. MIA AUC stays near 0.5 across the rank sweep; the privacy story leans on canary exposure, and the corpus is too small to test larger-scale membership.
  • Narrow task suite for SFT vs. GRPO (3-digit arithmetic, key-value). Whether “GRPO writes ~0” survives denser, higher-entropy rewards is open.
  • The reward-entropy dial predicted to move GRPO's write rate does not measurably raise it — slightly undercutting the conjecture being tested.
04

Open questions

Does the 1.7–2.8 bits/param writability plateau hold at 7B–70B, or do the MLP-vs-attention gap and substrate effect shift with width and depth?
Can a calibrated MIA or a larger corpus confirm that behavior-write bits predict membership inference, or is extraction genuinely the only resolving attack?
Where do non-LoRA PEFT methods (VeRA, LoRA-XS, DoRA, prefix tuning) sit relative to the LoRA writability line at matched parameter count?
For RLVR with denser, higher-entropy rewards (process rewards, tool-use traces), does the “GRPO writes ~0” result survive?
Can the substrate-capacity finding be weaponized as a defense — deliberately choosing a base or placement that lowers writability below corpus information — without sacrificing accuracy?
