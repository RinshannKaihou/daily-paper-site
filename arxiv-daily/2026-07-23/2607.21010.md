> **一句话总结：** 本文首次系统量化零样本 LLM 摘要生成器在静态输入下的"稳定性"问题，提出两层 Stability Analysis Protocol（文档级 + 语料级）对 Gemma3-4B、Llama-3.2-3B、Qwen-2.5-3B 在 CNN/DM、PubMed、IN-Abs 三种文体上进行基准评测（>28K 条摘要），发现 BERTScore 远比 AlignScore 稳定、Gemma3 是三者中最可信的摘要器。

> **TL;DR:** The paper proposes a two-level Stability Analysis Protocol (SAP) that runs each LLM-summarizer 100 times per document on 32 stratified documents across CNN/DM, PubMed, and IN-Abs, then quantifies stability via a pessimistic stability coefficient, Gini/combined-CV, Levene's test, and Friedman-Nemenyi rank tests. Across >28K summaries, Gemma3-4B is the most trustworthy summarizer (e.g., CNN/DM BERTScore stability 0.7957 vs. Llama 0.6461), and BERTScore is far more stable than AlignScore, whose corpus-level stability collapses to ~0.003 on CNN/DM — exposing factuality as the weak link of zero-shot LLM summarizers.

## 核心贡献

- 首次系统形式化"LLM 摘要稳定性"问题：把零样本摘要器在静态输入下反复生成时表现出的随机波动，定义为可信度的代理指标，弥补了传统单次评估的盲区。
- 提出两阶段 Stability Analysis Protocol (SAP)：文档级计算 stability coefficient ϵ（基于 100 条摘要得分的最大成对差，pessimistic 估计）+ Gini、CV、IQR 等离散度；语料级用 combined CV、Levene 方差齐性检验、Shapiro-Wilk 正态检验、Friedman + Nemenyi 后检验等统计工具做基准比较。
- 大规模实证：3 个 < 4B 开源 LLM (Gemma3-4B-Instruct、Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct、Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct) × 3 种文体 (CNN/DM 新闻、PubMed 医学、IN-Abs 法律) × 32 篇分层抽样文档 × 100 次重复生成 = 超过 28K 条摘要，得到统计可靠的稳定性估计。
- 关键发现：(i) BERTScore 是最稳定的评价指标，AlignScore 最不稳定（事实一致性维度是 LLM 摘要器短板）；(ii) Gemma3 在所有文体和指标上稳定性最高，Llama 的 AlignScore 中位数趋近 0，事实对齐能力堪忧；(iii) 短文档 (CNN/DM) 的事实可靠性最低。
- 倡导把"稳定性 + 置信区间"纳入 LLM 摘要器评测框架，作为继相关性、连贯性、事实性之后的新维度。

## Key Contributions

- First formal problem definition and systematic quantification of *stability* for zero-shot LLM-summarizers under a controlled (static) input environment, framing stability as a proxy for trustworthiness/robustness.
- A two-stage Stability Analysis Protocol (SAP): document-level stability coefficient ϵ = 1 − Δ (Δ = max pairwise score difference over k summaries) plus dispersion measures (Gini, CV, IQR); corpus-level generalization as the min stability coefficient over documents, with Shapiro-Wilk, Levene's, Friedman, and Nemenyi non-parametric tests plus bootstrap CIs for variance.
- Large-scale empirical study: 3 small open LLMs (Gemma3-4B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct) × 3 genres (CNN/DM news, PubMed medical, IN-Abs legal) × 32 length-stratified documents × 100 repeated generations = >28K summaries.
- Three takeaways: (i) BERTScore is the most stable evaluation metric while AlignScore is the least stable and merits further scrutiny; (ii) Gemma3 is the most reliable summarizer of the three; (iii) factuality of summaries for short documents (CNN/DM) is the least reliable.
- Advocacy for broadening summarizer evaluation beyond point estimates to include stability and confidence-interval-based (distribution-based) reporting.

## Methodology

**Problem framing.** Given an LLM-summarizer S, a document D in genre G, and a metric m, the goal is to quantify trust in S's output as measured by m. The authors argue that variation in metric scores across repeated generations reveals the model's inherent stochasticity (from probabilistic decoding), which point-estimate single-run evaluation misses.

**Summary generation.** For each sampled document, k = 100 summaries are generated in zero-shot mode using one fixed prompt (Fig. 2 in paper) and default model parameters, so all variation is attributable to decoding stochasticity rather than prompt/parameter confounds.

**Evaluation metrics (3 dimensions).**
- BERTScore — semantic alignment of each summary to the gold reference.
- AlignScore — factual faithfulness of each summary against the source document.
- Semantic Consistency (SC) — average pairwise cosine similarity over the k summaries (Eq. 1).

**Document-level stability.** Stability coefficient ϵ = 1 − max_ij |x_i − x_j| (Definition 1) — a pessimistic lower bound on agreement. Reinforced by descriptive dispersion: IQR, std, coefficient of variation (CV), Gini coefficient (GC), skewness, kurtosis.

**Corpus-level stability.** Aggregate ϵ over N documents by taking the minimum → stability coefficient E (Definition 2, also pessimistic). Combined coefficient of variation CV_c (derived in Appendix A) captures both within-document stochastic dispersion and between-document structural heterogeneity. Levene's test checks homogeneity of variances across documents. Bootstrap (10,000 resamples of size 32) yields 95% CIs for the mean document-level variance.

**Benchmarking.** Document-wise rank heatmaps produce win/loss counts (rank 1 = win) for average (quality) and std/Gini (dispersion). A non-parametric Friedman test validates rank differences at α = 0.05; Nemenyi post-hoc test with critical-difference (CD) diagrams identifies which summarizer pairs differ significantly.

**Sampling design.** Length-aware stratified sampling: each corpus is split into 4 quartiles by document length, 8 documents sampled per quartile → 32 docs/genre. Z-test (means) and χ² test (variances) confirm sample representativeness vs. the population, with IN-Abs requiring outlier removal to pass the variance test.

## Results & Highlights

- **Motivating example (Fig. 1):** 100 Llama-3.2 summaries of one CNN/DM document yield BERTScore values spanning **[0.41, 0.71]** against the gold reference — a 0.30 spread that the authors argue invalidates single-run evaluation.
- **Document-level stability (Table 5, doc_424 from CNN/DM):** Gemma3 ϵ = **0.9118** on BERTScore vs. Llama **0.7883** and Qwen **0.8436**; on AlignScore all three collapse (Gemma3 **0.0058**, Llama **0.2185**, Qwen **0.1903**), showing factuality is unstable regardless of model.
- **Corpus-level BERTScore stability E (Table 6, CNN/DM):** Gemma3 **0.7957** > Qwen **0.7857** > Llama **0.6461**; for AlignScore E: Qwen **0.0100** > Gemma3 **0.0031** > Llama **0.0021** — factuality stability is effectively zero corpus-wide.
- **Cross-genre BERTScore stability (Table 8):** PubMed Gemma3 **0.8036** > Llama **0.6834** > Qwen **0.3925**; IN-Abs Gemma3 **0.6668** > Qwen **0.6477** > Llama **0.5660** — Gemma3 ranks #1 on BERTScore stability for every genre.
- **AlignScore stability (Table 8):** PubMed Gemma3 **0.4345** > Qwen **0.1624** > Llama **0.0893**; IN-Abs Gemma3 **0.2725** > Qwen **0.2352** > Llama **0.1618** — factuality stability is much higher on long documents than on CNN/DM.
- **Win-count analysis (Table 9, 32 docs/genre):** Gemma3 dominates — e.g., on PubMed it wins average-AlignScore on **26/32** documents and average-SC on **32/32**; Llama wins AlignScore-average on only **1/32** PubMed and **0/32** IN-Abs documents.
- **Combined CV (Table 8):** BERTScore CV_c ≈ 0.075–0.088 across all summarizers/genres, whereas AlignScore CV_c reaches **0.3658 (Gemma3, CNN/DM)** and up to **0.4261 (Llama, CNN/DM)** — AlignScore variability is 4–5× BERTScore's.
- **Statistical significance:** Friedman test rejects H₀ (equal summarizers) at α = 0.05 for all datasets/metrics; Nemenyi post-hoc shows Gemma3 vs. Llama and Gemma3 vs. Qwen are significantly different on AlignScore and SC ranks for CNN/DM (CD diagram, Fig. 10), while Llama vs. Qwen is not significant.
- **Confidence intervals (Fig. 8):** Gemma3 consistently produces the narrowest bootstrap CIs for variance; Llama produces the widest, especially on AlignScore for CNN/DM.

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors (Vasudha Bhatnagar, Purnima Bindal, Vikas Kumar, Raj Kumari Bahl) are affiliated with the Department of Computer Science and Department of Statistics at the University of Delhi, with the corresponding ORCIDs provided. The cross-departmental CS + Statistics composition is well-matched to a paper that is heavily statistical-methodological (Gini, combined CV, Shapiro-Wilk, Levene, Friedman-Nemenyi, bootstrap CIs). The work is framed as an "Empirical investigation" and the experimental setup (workstation spec, Python/PyTorch versions, model checkpoints, prompt) is documented in reproducible detail. The paper claims to be the first to systematically investigate stability of LLM-summarizers; the related-work section (Sec. 1.2) surveys adjacent areas (LLM trustworthiness benchmarks, LLM-as-judge reliability, prompt sensitivity, output variability in IR/QA) and cleanly identifies the gap, lending credibility to the novelty claim.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. While output variability of LLMs is a known concern (Mizrahi et al. on prompt sensitivity; Wang et al. on multi-prompt medical QA), the application to *summarization under static input* — with a formal stability coefficient, SAP, and Friedman/Nemenyi benchmarking — is, as far as the related-work survey shows, new. The framing of stability as a pessimistic min-of-document-coefficients (Def. 2) is a defensible, conservative choice.
- **Rigor:** Strong on the statistical side — normality testing (Shapiro-Wilk) before applying non-parametric tests, Levene's for variance homogeneity, bootstrap CIs with 10,000 resamples, Friedman + Nemenyi with CD diagrams. The authors honestly flag that the optimistic/pessimistic estimates depend on k and that single-document stability is "of little practical utility" (a candid limitation of their own metric).
- **Reproducibility:** Mixed. Document lengths, sample sizes, sampling protocol, prompt text, model HuggingFace links, and hardware are fully specified. However: no mention of code release, random seeds, exact temperature/decoding defaults ("default model parameters" is under-specified — a key omission given that the paper's entire thesis is about decoding stochasticity), or which documents were sampled (apart from doc_424). Without seed/decoding-config disclosure, the 100-summary runs cannot be exactly replicated.
- **Significance:** High practical relevance for evaluation methodology — the paper makes a compelling case that single-run summarization benchmarks (including popular LLM-summarization leaderboards) systematically under-report variance. The recommendation to report stability + CIs is actionable.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:**
  - *Model coverage is narrow.* Only three small (< 4B) open-weights models — no GPT-4/Claude/ proprietary LLMs, no larger open models (7B/8B/70B). The authors attribute this to budget, but the headline "trustworthiness of LLM-summarizers" overgeneralizes from 3 small models.
  - *Sample size of 32 documents/genre* limits the power of corpus-level claims; combined with model-specific decoding defaults, the absolute stability numbers may not transfer to other LLMs.
  - *AlignScore is used as the sole factuality metric.* Given that the central finding ("AlignScore is unstable") is metric-driven, the absence of a second factuality metric (e.g., FactCC, DAE, QAG-based) is a notable confound — the instability may be in AlignScore itself rather than in the summaries.
  - *The stability coefficient's pessimistic form* (max pairwise difference, then min over documents) is very sensitive to a single outlier summary; the paper does not report sensitivity of E to k or to outlier summaries.
  - *No human evaluation* of summary quality, so the link between low stability and actual user-perceived trustworthiness is asserted rather than measured.
  - *Decoding-default ambiguity* (see reproducibility above).

## Strengths

- Formal, principled statistical framework with named tests and clearly stated null hypotheses at each stage; rare rigor for an LLM-evaluation paper.
- Length-aware stratified sampling with explicit Z-test and χ²-test validation that the 32-document sample represents the population (Table 2, Table 3) — including honest handling of the IN-Abs outlier issue.
- Multi-dimensional evaluation: semantic (BERTScore, SC) and factual (AlignScore) stability analyzed separately, exposing that factuality is the unstable dimension even when semantics are stable.
- Cross-genre generalization (news / medical / legal) with consistent trends reported in appendices, plus pre-registered statistical significance testing (Friedman/Nemenyi) rather than eyeballed comparisons.
- Actionable recommendations: distribution-based / multi-run evaluation with CIs, and including stability as a new evaluation dimension — concrete guidance for the community.

## Limitations

- **Genre coverage:** Only three genres; dialogues, conversations, meetings, code, multi-document summarization are not covered, and decisions based on those genres' summaries may differ in stability.
- **Model coverage:** Restricted to free, small (< 4B) open-weights LLMs due to budget; proprietary and larger open models are untested, and results will likely shift with newer model versions.
- **No human/expert validation:** SAP relies on embedding-based automatic metrics; user or expert evaluation would be needed to confirm that measured stability corresponds to perceived trustworthiness.
- **Computational cost:** Multi-run evaluation (100 summaries/doc) is expensive; the authors note the number of repetitions must be balanced against available compute in practice.
- **Decoding configuration opacity:** "Default model parameters" is not fully disclosed; temperature, top-p, and seed values are missing — problematic given decoding stochasticity is the very phenomenon under study.
- **Single factuality metric:** AlignScore is the only factuality measure; its well-known noise sensitivity could be driving the "factuality is unstable" conclusion rather than the summaries themselves.

## Relevance

与用户对 LLM 摘要/可信度评测的兴趣高度相关：论文提出的 stability coefficient 与 SAP 协议可直接用于审视日常使用的 LLM 摘要器（含 RAG/Agent 流水线中的摘要环节）在重复调用下的稳健性，提示在摘要评测基准（如 LLM-summarization leaderboard）中应报告置信区间与多轮稳定性，避免被单次点估计误导。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the stability coefficients change when temperature, top-p, and seed are explicitly varied? Is the instability driven by high-entropy decoding defaults, and can a low-temperature configuration recover AlignScore stability?
- Does the "AlignScore is unstable" finding replicate under alternative factuality metrics (FactCC, DAE, QAFF, SummaC)? If those are also unstable, the problem is in the summaries; if not, it may be in AlignScore.
- Do the conclusions transfer to larger / proprietary models (Llama-3.1-70B, GPT-4-class, Claude-class)? The authors' "LLM-summarizers" framing needs validation beyond 3B–4B models.
- What is the sensitivity of the pessimistic stability coefficient E to k (number of repetitions) and to outlier summaries? Is there a cheaper robust estimator (e.g., IQR-based, or trimmed) that converges faster?
- Does stability correlate with downstream-task robustness when summarization is an intermediate step in an NLP/RAG pipeline (i.e., does low stability actually cascade into downstream performance variance)?
- Can expert/user judgments validate that low measured stability corresponds to perceived untrustworthiness, especially in the high-stakes legal (IN-Abs) and medical (PubMed) domains?
- How does stability interact with prompt rephrasing? This study holds the prompt fixed; combining the SAP protocol with multi-prompt evaluation would give a fuller trustworthiness picture.
