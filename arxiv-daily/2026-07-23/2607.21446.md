> **一句话总结：** KroQuant 提出一种可学习的 Kronecker 结构块对角激活变换，在扩散 Transformer 的 W4A4 量化中用 32×32 张量核 GEMM 替代 SmoothQuant 的逐通道缩放，在 PixArt-Σ/SANA/FLUX.1-schnell 上同时获得更接近 FP 参考的质量（LPIPS 最高相对降低 17.5%）和更低的推理开销（MI350 上量化核比 SmoothQuant 快至 14%）。

> **TL;DR:** KroQuant replaces the per-channel SmoothQuant activation transform in W4A4 (MXFP4e2) post-training quantization of diffusion transformers with a learned block-diagonal Kronecker-factored 32×32 transform that costs less than half the parameters of scaling yet runs as tensor-core GEMMs. On MJHQ-30K and SDCI across PixArt-Σ, SANA, and FLUX.1-schnell, it reduces LPIPS by up to 17.5% relative to the LoRaQ baseline (e.g., 0.347 vs 0.381 on PixArt-Σ/MJHQ-30K), and its quantizer kernel is up to 14% faster than SmoothQuant on an AMD MI350 while being 1.3–2.45× faster than the Fast Walsh–Hadamard Transform.

## 核心贡献

- **首个面向 DiT PTQ 的可学习非对角在线激活变换**：以 32×32 块对角 Kronecker 结构构造 T ∈ GL(d)，比固定块 Hadamard 更具表达力，又比满维 SpinQuant/FlatQuant 的 d×d GEMM 在线代价小得多——这是 DiT 中因 AdaLN 无法做离线权重吸收而被迫在线运行时的关键约束。
- **Kronecker-LU 参数化 GL(32)**：用 5 个 2×2 单位行列式 LU 因子的 Kronecker 积参数化每个 32×32 块，仅 15 个参数/块，比稠密 GL(32) 小 68×，比逐通道缩放还省参数，并以归一化 Hadamard 作为天然初始化。
- **融合 W4A4 Triton 核**：块对角结构映射为单遍张量核 GEMM；提供 K1（预算稠密块）与 K2（15 参数即时重建，68× 参数压缩）两种实现，在 MI350 上 K1 比逐通道缩放核快至 14%，两变体均比 FWHT 快 1.30–2.45× 且无需 power-of-two 填充。
- **即插即用替换 LoRaQ 前端**：保持 LoRaQ 的离线低秩权重校正不变，仅用学习到的 Kronecker 变换替换其 SmoothQuant 前端；消融证明 15 个可学参数相对固定 Hadamard 在 LPIPS 上改善 0.035、PSNR 改善 0.8 dB。
- **三模型两基准一致提升**：在 PixArt-Σ、SANA、FLUX.1-schnell 的 MJHQ-30K/SDCI 上多数指标达到或超过 SVDQuant 与 LoRaQ，缩小与 FP 参考的 FID 差距。

## Key Contributions

- **First non-diagonal online activation transform for DiT PTQ**: A learned block-diagonal T ∈ GL(d) with 32×32 blocks, more expressive than a fixed block-Hadamard and far cheaper at inference than the full-d rotations of SpinQuant or the dense invertibles of FlatQuant, both of which are infeasible to run online in DiTs where AdaLN prevents offline weight absorption.
- **Kronecker-LU parametrization of GL(32) blocks**: Each block is the Kronecker product of five 2×2 unit-determinant LU factors (15 scalars total), a 68× reduction over a dense GL(32) block and smaller than per-channel scaling, with the normalized Hadamard as a natural initialization.
- **Fused W4A4 tensor-core kernels**: Two custom Triton kernels (K1 precomputed-dense, K2 on-the-fly 15-parameter reconstruction with 68× compression) apply each block as a single tl.dot; on MI350, K1 runs up to 14% faster than the SmoothQuant quantizer kernel and both variants beat the Fast Walsh–Hadamard Transform by 1.30–2.45× without power-of-two padding.
- **Drop-in replacement for the SmoothQuant front-end of weight-splitting PTQ**: KroQuant keeps LoRaQ's offline low-rank weight calibration unchanged and only swaps the activation transform, yielding the headline LoRaQ-with-KroQuant comparison; ablation shows the 15 learned parameters strictly improve over a fixed Hadamard (ΔLPIPS = 0.035, ΔPSNR = 0.8 dB on PixArt-Σ).
- **Consistent gains across three DiT architectures and two benchmarks**: Matches or beats SVDQuant and LoRaQ on most metrics on PixArt-Σ, SANA, and FLUX.1-schnell at MXFP4e2 W4A4, narrowing the FID gap to the full-precision reference.

## Methodology

KroQuant targets the standard PTQ problem of inserting an invertible activation transform T ∈ GL(d) that leaves the linear map invariant (A·T · (W·T⁻ᵀ)ᵀ = AWᵀ) while reducing blockwise quantization error. The core difficulty in DiTs is that Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) sitting between every transformer block prevents the "absorption trick" used in LLM quantizers — T cannot be folded into the previous layer's output because Norm(xT)·T⁻¹ ≠ Norm(x) for AdaLN. Consequently every activation transform must run online, at every linear layer of every denoising step, making per-step compute the binding design constraint.

KroQuant resolves the expressivity-vs-cost trade-off by constraining T to be **block-diagonal with 32×32 blocks**, matching the block structure of the MXFP4e2 quantizer:

1. **Kronecker construction (Section 3.2)**: Each n×n block K is the Kronecker product of log₂n small 2×2 matrices, Kₙ = G₁ ⊗ G₂ ⊗ … ⊗ G_{log₂n}. For n = 32 this yields five factors; since the Kronecker product of invertibles is invertible, inversion reduces to five independent 2×2 inversions.
2. **Unit-determinant LU parametrization**: To avoid degenerate rescaling, each factor is constrained to |det Gₖ| = 1 via an LU decomposition Gₖ = LₖUₖ with three free scalars (aₖ, bₖ, cₖ), giving 15 parameters per 32×32 block (vs. 1024 for unconstrained GL(32)). The negative-reciprocal (2,2) entry lets the family include orthogonal reflections/rotations, so the normalized Hadamard is a valid initialization.
3. **Layer-wise optimization (Section 3.3)**: Each layer's T is optimized independently with Adam (100 steps, lr 10⁻²) on 128 calibration activations, minimizing the output MSE L_out plus two auxiliary terms L_A, L_W that keep each operand individually well-quantized, with regularization weights auto-scaled to match L_out's magnitude at initialization. Gradients flow through the non-differentiable quantizer via a straight-through estimator (STE). Optimization is kept at the layer level because joint multi-layer optimization is unstable under the Kronecker+STE composition.
4. **Offline LoRaQ weight calibration (Section 3.4)**: After T is fixed, LoRaQ decomposes the transformed weight into a 4-bit quantized residual and a rank-128 low-rank correction (itself quantizable below 16 bits), optimized data-free.
5. **Hardware-efficient kernels (Section 3.5)**: Two Triton kernels apply T as a single tensor-core GEMM per 32-element slice: K1 loads precomputed dense 32×32 blocks (2 KB/block); K2 reconstructs each block on-the-fly from 15 scalars via a bit-indexed product over the five factors (68× compression), fusing the MXFP4e2 quantizer in the same kernel.

## Results & Highlights

- **LPIPS vs LoRaQ on MJHQ-30K**: KroQuant reduces LPIPS by 0.034 (8.9% relative) on PixArt-Σ (0.347 vs 0.381) and by 0.023 (10.0%) on SANA (0.206 vs 0.229), measuring closeness to the FP reference.
- **FID vs the full-precision reference**: On PixArt-Σ/MJHQ-30K, KroQuant reaches FID 16.9 vs the FP16 reference of 16.6, the LoRaQ baseline's 17.5, and SVDQuant's 19.3; on SANA/MJHQ-30K it matches the reference FID of 16.6 exactly while LoRaQ sits at 17.4 and SVDQuant at 16.6.
- **FLUX.1-schnell / SDCI LPIPS**: KroQuant achieves the best LPIPS of all quantized methods at 0.283, vs LoRaQ's 0.343 (a 17.5% relative reduction) and SVDQuant's 0.589, with PSNR 16.6 vs LoRaQ's 15.8.
- **PSNR on SANA/MJHQ-30K**: KroQuant reaches 18.4 dB vs LoRaQ's 17.7 dB and SVDQuant's 17.1 dB.
- **Kernel latency (Table 3, MI350)**: The K1 precomputed-dense kernel matches or exceeds per-channel scaling (Scale) at 1.00–1.14× speedup on every tested shape, and is up to 14% faster than the SmoothQuant quantizer kernel; both K1 and K2 beat the Fast Walsh–Hadamard Transform by 1.30–2.45× while handling arbitrary d divisible by 32 (no power-of-two padding).
- **K2 parameter compression**: The on-the-fly Kronecker variant stores 15 scalars per block vs K1's 1024 values — a 68× compression — at a throughput cost of only 5–15% relative to K1, and still stays within 25% of the Scale kernel (0.75–0.96×).
- **Ablation (Table 2, PixArt-Σ/MJHQ-30K)**: Standalone (no LoRaQ), KroQuant reduces FID from 130.6 (Smoothing) to 41.8; with LoRaQ fixed, the learned Kronecker transform (Hadamard init) improves LPIPS by 0.035 and PSNR by 0.8 dB over a fixed Block Hadamard-32, which itself matches Smoothing on FID (both 17.5) but slightly degrades PSNR (15.7 vs 16.4) — confirming the 15 learned parameters capture per-layer structure a fixed orthogonal transform cannot.
- **Bandwidth (Figure 3)**: K2 reaches 85–88% of the Scale kernel's effective HBM bandwidth while applying a full-rank 32×32 tensor-core GEMM per block, and is ~1.4× faster than FWHT across all token counts.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — genuinely fills a known gap, not incremental polish.** The paper cleanly identifies that the absorption trick fails in DiTs (AdaLN does not commute with T) and therefore the only viable online transforms are diagonal (SmoothQuant) or fixed-Hadamard — leaving an empty middle ground between cheap-but-weak and expressive-but-infeasible. The Kronecker-LU parametrization of GL(32) at 15 parameters/block is a non-obvious construction that contains the Hadamard as a special case, and the on-the-fly K2 kernel that reconstructs a 32×32 block from 5 bit-indexed scalar lookups is a genuine systems contribution.
- **Rigor — ablations are appropriate but narrow.** The Table 2 ablation isolates the transform contribution (Smoothing vs KroQuant, both with/without LoRaQ) and isolates the learned-vs-fixed contribution (Block Hadamard-32 vs Kronecker-Hadamard-init). However, there is **no ablation over block size** (n is fixed at 32 throughout), **no ablation over the number of Kronecker factors or the unit-determinant constraint**, and the auxiliary-regularization weights λ_A, λ_W are only described via auto-scaling, not swept. The straight-through estimator is used but its accuracy vs. the true quantization gradient is not discussed.
- **Reproducibility — strong on paper.** Appendix D specifies exact hyperparameters (Adam, 100 steps, lr 10⁻² for KroQuant; rank 128; lr 10⁻³/500 steps for LoRaQ; Cayley SGD lr 0.5 for QAT), the 128-prompt COCO calibration set, the TensorCast library, and the hardware (H100/H200 for calibration, MI350/ROCm 7.2.2/Triton 3.6.0 for kernels). The block Hadamard baseline is reproduced faithfully (matching Smoothing on FID). Caveat: the headline kernel speedups are MI350/CDNA-4-specific; NVIDIA H100/Blackwell numbers are absent, so the "up to 14% faster than SmoothQuant" claim does not necessarily transfer to the dominant deployment platform.
- **Significance — real but bounded by the n=32 ceiling.** The Kronecker construction has only 3·log₂n parameters per n×n block, which the authors honestly note grows too slowly to approximate GL(n) at larger blocks; KroQuant is therefore "tied to n = 32 in practice." This caps how much further the method can improve as MX block sizes evolve.
- **Red flags / overclaiming — minor.** The abstract's "produces outputs closer to the FP reference than SVDQuant and LoRaQ" is supported on PixArt-Σ and SANA but is **not uniformly true**: on FLUX.1-schnell/MJHQ-30K, KroQuant loses to SVDQuant on IR (0.880 vs 0.952) and to LoRaQ on LPIPS (0.336 vs 0.331). The paper does disclose this honestly in Section 4.2 and attributes it to FLUX.1's double-stream joint-attention heterogeneity, which is a fair and paper-specific explanation — but the abstract overstates the uniformity of the win. There is no per-layer analysis of where the Kronecker transform helps most, which would have strengthened the double-stream hypothesis.

## Strengths

- Identifies and cleanly formalizes a structural reason (AdaLN non-commutativity) why LLM rotation tricks do not transfer to DiTs, motivating the online-cost constraint.
- The Kronecker-LU parametrization is parameter-efficient (15/block, <half of per-channel scaling) while strictly generalizing the Hadamard — an elegant sweet spot.
- Two kernel variants (K1 throughput-optimal, K2 68× memory-compressed) give deployment flexibility, and the bit-indexed on-the-fly Kronecker reconstruction in K2 is a clever register-level optimization.
- Honest reporting: the mixed FLUX.1/MJHQ-30K results and the n=32 limitation are disclosed rather than hidden.
- Drop-in design makes it directly composable with existing weight-splitting PTQ pipelines (SVDQuant, LoRaQ).

## Limitations

- **Block size is hard-locked to n = 32.** The Kronecker construction's parameter count grows only as O(log n) per block, so it cannot scale to larger GL blocks; the authors flag this as the main ceiling on further gains.
- **Joint/multi-layer optimization is unstable** under the STE + unit-determinant LU parametrization, so optimization is confined to the per-layer level, forgoing cross-layer error compensation.
- **Platform-specific kernel claims.** All speedups are on AMD MI350/CDNA-4; no NVIDIA results are reported, limiting generalizability of the "14% faster than SmoothQuant" headline.
- **Mixed results on FLUX.1-schnell/MJHQ-30K** (worse IR and LPIPS than the strongest baselines), attributed to double-stream joint-attention heterogeneity but not fixed by a per-stream or per-block-type transform variant.
- **Qualitative side effect**: KroQuant outputs tend to have higher contrast than the FP16 reference, an effect the authors note but do not explain theoretically.
- **No comparison against learned full-d transforms (SpinQuant/FlatQuant) on DiTs**, even as a reference point — these are dismissed as infeasible online, but an offline/quality-upper-bound comparison would contextualize the gains.

## Relevance

对关注高效推理与模型压缩的研究者，KroQuant 直接落在扩散模型 W4A4 部署的痛点上：它给出了一种既比逐通道缩放更准、又比满维旋转更省的在线激活变换，并提供了 MI350 上的实测加速核，对扩散 Transformer 的低比特落地与硬件感知量化算法设计有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does KroQuant behave as the MX block size n grows beyond 32 (e.g., to 64 or 128 in future MX revisions), and can the Kronecker factor count be adaptively increased without breaking the O(d) parameter budget?
- Would a per-stream or per-attention-block-type Kronecker transform recover the IR/LPIPS deficit on FLUX.1's double-stream architecture, where text and image tokens have heterogeneous activation distributions?
- Can a differentiable or half-quadratic alternative to the STE stabilize joint multi-layer optimization, unlocking cross-layer error redistribution?
- Do the MI350 speedups (14% over SmoothQuant, 1.3–2.45× over FWHT) transfer to NVIDIA H100/Blackwell tensor cores, or are they artifacts of CDNA-4's matrix-instruction shape?
- Is the empirically observed higher-contrast output a systematic consequence of the unit-determinant LU factors biasing certain channel-mixing directions, and can it be regularized away?
- How does KroQuant compose with activation quantization below 4 bits (e.g., W4A3 or INT3), where outlier suppression becomes even more critical?
