Auditing Provenance Sensitivity in LLM Agent Action Selection

Junchi Liao · single-author preprint · 23 Jul 2026 · arxiv.org/abs/2607.20827 ↗
Key finding

Open-weight agents respond to source-authority cues, yet still follow unauthorized competing evidence in 2.4% of cases — a guardrail cuts that error only 14.9% → 9.5%.

02 Why this matters

A reusable 450-task authorization audit and three-instrument diagnostic stack, filling the gap prompt-injection and tool-use benchmarks leave open.

For agent-safety, tool-trust, and provenance-tracking researchers: a baseline method and protocol that asks does relevant-but-unauthorized evidence still steer a decision?

03 Critical edge

These are stress-set rates, not deployment prevalence — and roughly 1 in 5 target-factor labels would change under independent adjudication.

Coalition (Shapley) result does not isolate authorization from semantic competition; near-null full-context transform bounds the claim to partial-evidence regimes. Headline numbers reproduce only on open-weight models.

§ 01

Methodology

4-label target audit

For a fixed next-action decision the action interface is frozen; context is decomposed into semantic factors. Each factor is labeled Valid / Invalid / Neutral / Excluded per target — a record may authorize the send_email tool yet be invalid for its cc slot.

  • Instrument 1 · matched source intervention Cross source authority a ∈ {T,U} with support/compete relation; only the "Source status:" frame changes. Primary endpoint: within-relation authority gap δS, δC and difference-in-differences ΔΔ = δC − δS.
  • Instrument 2 · controlled valid-evidence degradation full / mixed (drop one Valid) / clean (drop that Valid + all Invalid). Strict retained-invalid pattern cF=1, cM=0, cC=1 isolates residual unauthorized influence.
  • Instrument 3 · exact coalition interactions All 28=256 subsets, exact Harsanyi interactions over 28 non-empty coalitions of the 8 factors. Shapley fractional excess s_frac = primary partial-evidence summary; label-count-matched null over 500 permutations.
  • Datasets & models 450 instances → 1,350 targets → 10,800 target-factor labels. AgentAudit-250, Tau2Audit-100, BFCLAudit-100. Models: Qwen3-4B/30B, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B, Llama-3.3-70B; + DeepSeek-V2-Lite. 95% CIs cluster-bootstrapped.

§ 03

Strengths & Limitations

side by side

Strengths

  • Clean separation of relevance, truthfulness, and authorization — never conflates "unauthorized" with "false".
  • Three estimands with explicitly bounded scopes, each with its own primary/secondary statistic and CI.
  • Honest negative results in main text: near-null full-context transform, role-matched reversal (−.018), DeepSeek guardrail reversal.
  • Genuine external annotation study with κ reported, plus a consensus-label sensitivity check that preserves the aggregate pattern.
  • Practitioner caution: an authority-policy guardrail is not a provenance defense — it raises pooled correctness heterogeneously.

Limitations

  • Deliberate stress-set: 2.4% / 5.4% are sensitivity rates, not field error rates.
  • Human-authored factor decomposition; ~21% of labels would change under adjudication; case-level explanations are unstable.
  • Exact enumeration only at 8 factors; pairwise screening at 12/16 is an approximation; long trajectories unproven.
  • Open-weight only — closed APIs (GPT-4o, Claude) get a weaker forced-choice proxy (Appendix Table 55).
  • Source cue is a textual "Source status:" marker, not operational retrieval/memory/tool-transport provenance.
  • Shapley diagnostic localizes interactions containing unauthorized competitors, not authorization-specific dependence.
§ 04

Open Questions

for further work
  1. Do the 5.4% competing-discordance and 2.4% retained-invalid rates survive when source authority is carried by an operational channel (retrieval tags, tool-transport metadata, signed memory) instead of a textual marker?
  2. Would closed-weight frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude) show higher or lower provenance sensitivity under the forced-choice proxy, and does the open/closed gap change the guardrail recommendation?
  3. Can the audit extend to multi-turn trajectories where memory, repeated tool calls, and retrieval evolve — given exact subset enumeration is intractable beyond ~8 factors?
  4. The retained-invalid pattern is an intervention, not causal mediation: can a formal mediation or counterfactual analysis name the specific retained invalid factor, not just the invalid set?
  5. Does training-time instruction-hierarchy alignment reduce the matched authority gap (+0.481) and retained-invalid rate (2.4%), or only the gross error rate?
  6. Would an authorization-aware verifier/LLM-as-judge repair the residual 1.2% harmful trusted→untrusted transitions the action model fails to suppress?
