Future Rendering ≠ Future Surface: A Benchmark and Dataset for Dynamic Surface Reconstruction Beyond the Observed Window

Yukun Shi  ·  Minglun Gong ● University of Guelph, Computer Science ● 2026-07-23 ● arxiv.org/abs/2607.21471 ↗

Key finding

Dynamic-Gaussian backbones amplify future-surface Chamfer error 2.0–6.6× — and the rendering metrics the field reports are statistically decoupled from geometry.

Why this matters

Future-time NVS claims (PSNR / LPIPS) don’t measure future geometry. The field’s standard metrics can’t evidence surface quality at held-out times.

— linear fit explains <6% of surface-error variance

⚠ Critical edge

Only two backbones tested — both share the deformation-MLP family. The “future-surface gap” is strictly a deformation-MLP finding, not a statement about dynamic reconstruction writ large.

— authors flag this in §8; a temporally-distinct backbone is still untested

02

Methodology

Task. Given observed frames t ∈ [0, T], reconstruct the surface mesh at held-out future times t > T.

Split: 75% obs (0–149) / 25% future (150–199) Robust at: 60/40, 85/15

Metric. Per-frame bidirectional Chamfer distance in one shared canonical frame at GT scale, with no per-frame alignment. Nearest-point matching means no vertex correspondence or shared topology is needed — any method that exports meshes can be scored.

Diagnostics. (i) gap = future-CD / observed-CD; (ii) Sim(3)-aligned gauge-removed CD separating rigid drift from non-rigid shape error; (iii) per-vertex error maps.

Dataset — 8 controlled motions on a base UV sphere (r=0.9), each an analytic map Fm(x,t):

  • 5 factor-isolating: wave (periodic), compound (period+trend), stretch (decelerating), bulge (traveling Gaussian), accel (constant-accel; paired with stretch).
  • 3 falsification controls: twist (surface-invariant), rotate (rigid gauge), stop (frozen future) — these probe the protocol, not any method.

Backbones. DG-Mesh (primary; surface extraction) and Deformable-3DGS (deformation-MLP; asset cross-check). Training is unchanged — the benchmark is purely an evaluation protocol. CPU-only scoring, minutes.

Cross-backbone (Deformable-3DGS). Comparable 2.1–6.6× gaps on assets, with small observed CDs (0.012–0.026) ruling out gross misalignment.

GT-side recoverability oracle (Tab. 6). Four of five futures recovered nearly exactly from GT trajectories — accel future CD ≈ 1e-5 (~1200× better than freeze); wave 47×, compound 15×, stretch 13×. bulge (spatial transport) defeats every per-vertex rule. This separates “the future is unknowable” from “the backbone can’t extrapolate.”

04

Strengths

  • First standardized, released protocol scoring extracted surface meshes at held-out future times — closes a real measurement gap (Tab. 1) with a method-agnostic metric.
  • Three falsification controls test the protocol itself — a broken metric or alignment cannot pass silently. Exemplary benchmark hygiene.
  • GT-side recoverability oracle turns the philosophical “unknowable future” objection into a measurable quantity — the conceptual highlight.
  • Rendering–surface decoupling (|ρ|=0.13) is a field-relevant negative result that should discipline future-time NVS claims.
  • Honest benchmark card enumerating supported/unsupported claims; versioned, CPU-only reproducible artifacts (CC BY 4.0).
05

Limitations

  • Family-confounded breadth. Only two backbones, both deformation-MLP — the gap is strictly a deformation-MLP-family finding until a temporally distinct backbone is tested.
  • Deformable-3DGS unscoreable on analytic surfaces (Gaussian centers 2–7× off the surface), so the deepest controlled diagnostics are DG-Mesh-only.
  • Synthetic analytic data is required (real captures lack true future meshes); real-world deployment is explicitly out of scope.
  • Small dataset (8 motions, one base sphere) — scene diversity traded for exact future GT and factor isolation.
  • Gap ratio inherits denominator variance; correctly demoted to a diagnostic, but dramatic ratios (6.6× on torus→sphere) partly reflect a small denominator.
  • Diagnoses the gap, proposes no fix — explicitly a benchmark, not a method paper.
06

Open questions for further work

  • How does the gap behave under a temporally distinct backbone — physics-integrated (PhysGaussian, GIC), explicit 4D-grid, or neural-SDF flow — outside the deformation-MLP inductive bias?
  • Can a learned spatial-transport rule recover the bulge traveling-bump future that defeats every per-vertex extrapolator?
  • Does adding temporal inductive bias (periodicity priors, physical constraints, drift regularization on static futures) close the controlled-suite gap without harming observed-window fit?
  • Does the decoupling (|ρ|=0.13) generalize to topologically varying (HyperNeRF-style) or fluid/elastic dynamics?
  • Is there a motion-type-independent safe extrapolation horizon below which current backbones stay within a fixed multiple of observed-window error?
  • Could a hybrid metric (rendering + cheap silhouette/normal-consistency proxy) recover the predictive power LPIPS alone lacks for future geometry?
