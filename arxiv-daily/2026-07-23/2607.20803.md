> **一句话总结：** 本文将大模型的"人格"建模为荣格八维认知功能（Ti/Te/Fi/Fe/Ni/Ne/Si/Se）而非静态的大五特质，在 Llama-3.1-8B 上用 difference-in-means 提取并注入激活向量，实现对全部八个维度的单调可控调节，并发现人格信息集中在第 7–12 层、理性功能与 irrational 功能在激活空间中呈现可区分的几何结构、且多维 steering 方向不能由单维方向线性组合恢复。

> **TL;DR:** Instead of the static Big Five (OCEAN) trait model, this paper casts LLM personality as the eight Jungian Cognitive Functions (Ti/Te/Fi/Fe/Ni/Ne/Si/Se) and extracts activation steering vectors on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct using difference-in-means over a new 2,100-character role-play dataset (NarrationDB). All eight functions are monotonically steerable (e.g., Se swings from 39.06 to 95.77, an effectiveness of 56.70; Ne from 41.60 to 98.07), personality computation concentrates in middle layers 7–12, and multi-dimensional directions (e.g., Ne+Ti) are NOT recoverable as linear combinations of single-function directions (Least-Squares residual r = 0.23–0.65 across 12 combinations).

## 核心贡献

- **首次将荣格八维认知功能（Ti/Te/Fi/Fe/Ni/Ne/Si/Se）作为大模型人格的过程化表示**，区别于以往基于静态大五（OCEAN）特质的研究（Frising & Balcells 2026；Chen et al. 2025；Bhandari et al. 2026），提供了一个更贴近"信息感知 / 决策 / 注意力调节"动态过程的视角。
- **构建并发布 NarrationDB**：基于 SWCPQ 数据集（Jorgenson 2020），用 Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct 对 2100+ 虚拟角色做角色扮演自述，并标注每个角色的八维认知功能分数，覆盖整个八维空间。
- **设计了一套可扩展的 Jungian 评估协议**：将开源人类量表（Felmonon 2026，132 条 Likert 1–5 题目）改造为 LLM 测试，用 30 个随机种子组评估，并以 `S = 100·(w−1)/4, w = 0.7P + 0.3(6−N)` 计算分数。
- **在 Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct 上对全部八个维度实现单调可控的激活 steering**，并通过逐层扫描定位了"高有效性体积"在中间层 7–12。
- **给出激活空间的几何证据**：理性功能（Te/Ti/Fe/Fi）与其外倾/内倾同型更近，非理性功能（Ne/Ni/Se/Si）距离更远，与荣格原始区分吻合。
- **提出多维 steering 的"回溯"分析**：直接从样本中抽取如 `V_Ne+Ti` 的复合方向后，用最小二乘反推其在 `V_Ti, V_Ne` 张成子空间中的最佳线性近似，残余 r = 0.23–0.65，证明多维方向不是单维方向的线性叠加。

## Key Contributions

- First to model and steer LLM personality as a **dynamic cognitive process** using the eight Jungian Cognitive Functions rather than the static Big Five trait space, complementing the OCEAN-based steering literature.
- Releases **NarrationDB**, a synthetic corpus of 2,100+ role-playing character self-narrations generated by Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct with human-aligned SWCPQ tag injection, each annotated with eight-dimensional Jungian scores that densely populate the function space.
- Adapts an open-source human Jungian typology questionnaire (132 Likert items) into a scalable **LLM evaluation protocol** that averages over 30 random question-shuffle seeds to mitigate ordering effects.
- Demonstrates **monotonic, effective steering for all eight functions** on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct via difference-in-means vectors, and localizes the "high-effectiveness volume" for personality manipulation in middle layers 7–12.
- Provides **geometric/mechanistic evidence** that rational vs. irrational functions have distinguishable activation-space structure, consistent with Jung's original distinction.
- Introduces a **backtracking analysis** showing multi-dimensional steering directions (e.g., Ne+Ti) are NOT linear combinations of single-function directions — a non-trivial finding about entanglement in activation space.

## Methodology

The paper is organized as an encode→compute→decode pipeline study of personality in LLM activations:

1. **Jungian Evaluation Protocol.** The authors take the 132-item open-source Jungian typology test (Felmonon 2026), group items into 4 batches of 33, shuffle them with 30 random seeds, and prompt Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct under a PsychoBench-style prefix. Each function score is computed as `S = 100·(w−1)/4` where `w = 0.7·P + 0.3·(6−N)`, with P (resp. N) the mean Likert score on positively (resp. negatively) contributing items.

2. **NarrationDB Construction.** They prompt Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct to role-play as virtual characters from the SWCPQ dataset (Jorgenson 2020). To inject human-aligned knowledge, they compute the 95% margin of error of each SWCPQ tag score, keep the 20 most agreed-upon tags per side of the slider, then select the 10 closest to the extremes as role-play descriptors. The 8B model then plays the character consistent with the generated narration, and is scored on the Jungian test. The result is 2,100+ characters covering the eight-dimensional space (visualized by t-SNE in Figure 1).

3. **Steering Vector Extraction (difference-in-means).** For each function Cf and layer n, characters are sorted by S_Cf; the top-k and bottom-k (k=10) form high and low clusters HCf, LCf. Each cluster character is prompted with T=5 open-ended Jungian questions, the response-token latents are averaged, and the steering vector is `V_{Cf,n} = Lat(HCf)_n − Lat(LCf)_n`. This averages over 50 dialogues per cluster to cancel target-unrelated signal.

4. **Activation Injection.** Following Rimsky et al. (2024), the vector is normalized to unit length and added at a single layer n with strength λ: `Lat_{n,steered} = Lat_n + λ·V_n / ||V_n||`. λ is swept from −6.875 to +6.875 (step 0.25). Combinations with task-completion rate <80% are discarded. Effectiveness score = S_max − S_min over the viable λ range. The per-function most effective layer n* is recorded from a full 31-layer scan.

5. **Layer-Based Analysis.** All 31 layer outputs are scanned for each function; the resulting "high-effectiveness volume" at layers 7–12 is interpreted via the encode-compute-decode hypothesis (Skean et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2025).

6. **Activation-Space Geometry.** Since all eight single-dim vectors work at layer 12, PCA (to 2D) is performed on V_12. Rational functions cluster nearer their E/I counterparts; irrational functions are farther apart.

7. **Multi-Dimensional Steering.** A pivot score `S_pivot = OddSq(NS_Ne) + OddSq(NS_Ti)` (with `OddSq(x)=x·|x|` over z-scored normalized scores NS) is defined to select clusters that are jointly extreme on two functions. Direct difference-in-means on these clusters yields e.g. `V_{Ne+Ti,n}` without combining single-dim vectors. A Least-Squares backtrack then fits `V' = w1·V_Ti + w2·V_Ne` and measures residual `r = ||V_{Ne+Ti} − V'||` (all vectors unit-normalized).

## Results & Highlights

- **All eight Jungian functions are steerable monotonically** on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Single-dimension effectiveness scores (S_max − S_min) range from 19.20 (Fi, weakest) to 56.70 (Se, strongest), with Se moving from 39.06 → 95.77 and Ne from 41.60 → 98.07 at their most effective layers (Table 4).
- **Baseline Jungian profile of unsteered Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct** (Table 1): Ti 90.77, Ne 88.38, Ni 87.77, Fi 87.20, Fe 81.60, Se 76.37, Te 75.21, Si 72.53 — i.e., the model is strongly introverted-thinking-biased and weakest on sensing functions.
- **Most-effective layers cluster in the middle.** The per-function n* values fall in layers 7–12 (Fe: 12, Fi: 7, Ne: 12, Ni: 7, Se: 9, Si: 8, Te: 12, Ti: 8), supporting the encode-compute-decode interpretation where personality is "computed" in middle layers and "decoded" to next-token space afterwards.
- **Fi steering is asymmetric/weak**: only the negative direction works (λ ∈ [−2.38, −0.12], effectiveness 19.20). The authors hypothesize post-training pins Fi high because low Fi is associated with low self-control and moral identity.
- **Geometry matches Jungian theory.** PCA of V_12 shows rational functions (Te/Ti/Fe/Fi) positioned closer to their same-type E/I counterparts, while irrational functions (Ne/Ni/Se/Si) are farther apart — a distinction Jung (1971) drew between rational and irrational functions. This structure is stable across layers 7–12 (Appendix D).
- **Multi-dimensional steering works directly.** A jointly extracted V_{Ne+Ti} produces monotonic control on both axes simultaneously (Figure 6, scores climb together as λ increases) with minimal cross-interference.
- **Multi-dim directions are NOT linear combinations of single-dim directions.** Across 12 common function pairs (Table 5), Least-Squares residuals r range from 0.23 (Ni+Te) to 0.65 (Si+Ne); e.g., Ti+Ne gives w1=0.93, w2=0.28, r=0.37. This supports the hypothesis (Bhandari et al. 2026) that personality vectors are inherently entangled.

## Authors & Credibility

- **Liu Zai, Junchen Fu, Joemon M. Jose** (University of Glasgow) and **Yumeng Wang** (Leiden University). Jose is a well-established IR/ML researcher with a long publication record; the group has prior form in conversational and LLM evaluation.
- The methodology builds carefully on canonical activation-steering work: difference-in-means (Marks & Tegmark 2024), contrastive activation addition (Rimsky et al. 2024), persona vectors (Chen et al. 2025), and the layer-role hypothesis (Skean et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2025). Citations are appropriate and recent.
- The work is positioned honestly as exploratory/mechanistic rather than as a state-of-the-art personality-control benchmark, which is consistent with what the evidence supports.
- Code/data are referenced via the open-source Jungian test (Felmonon 2026) and SWCPQ (Jorgenson 2020); NarrationDB is described as released, though the paper does not give a repository URL.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel framing. Reframing personality from static Big Five traits to a process model (Jungian Cognitive Functions) is a non-trivial conceptual move, and the multi-dimensional backtracking experiment is, to my knowledge, new in the personality-steering literature.
- **Rigor:** Adequate for an exploratory paper but with clear gaps. The single-model design (only Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, with Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct only as a data generator) means no generalization claim can be made — the layer-localization and PCA-geometry findings could be Llama-specific artifacts. There is **no head-to-head quantitative comparison with Big Five steering baselines** (Frising & Balcells 2026; Chen et al. 2025); the paper argues conceptually for Jungian framing but never shows it is better on a common metric. The Fi weakness is acknowledged but not ablated — e.g., no test of whether it persists in a base (non-instruct) model where post-training pinning would be absent.
- **Reproducibility:** Mostly reproducible. Hyperparameters (λ range, k=10, T=5, 30 seeds, 80% completion threshold) are fully specified, and the test questionnaire is itemized in Appendix A. However, no code or dataset URL is given in the paper, and NarrationDB generation depends on the closed Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct sampling, which has its own nondeterminism. The 80% completion-rate filter is described only qualitatively (no definition of "task completion" is provided).
- **Significance:** Moderate. The empirical contributions (monotonic 8-D steering, layer 7–12 localization, multi-dim non-linearity) are useful and reusable; the conceptual contribution (Jungian framing) will appeal mainly to researchers already interested in personality modeling and is unlikely to displace OCEAN, partly because **Jungian typology itself is contested in modern psychology** — the paper treats it as ground truth without addressing its empirical standing.
- **Red flags:**
  - **Effectiveness metric is ad hoc**: S_max − S_min rewards functions with low baseline scores and can be inflated by floor/ceiling shifts rather than true control. No normalization by score range or baseline is performed.
  - **Question-order sensitivity is acknowledged but only averaged, not bounded** — no variance reported across the 30 seeds for the steering results themselves.
  - **PCA conclusions from a single layer with only 8 points** are visually suggestive but not statistically tested; "rational vs. irrational" clustering is asserted from a 2D projection of a high-dim space.
  - **No downstream behavioral evaluation**: the only success criterion is the Jungian test score itself, which is the same instrument used to extract the steering vector — a partially circular evaluation. No independent behavioral task (e.g., decision-making, dialogue style) confirms the steered personality manifests downstream.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated pipeline from questionnaire adaptation to dataset construction to vector extraction to geometric analysis — each step is traceable and parameter-light.
- Honest reporting: per-function effectiveness is given for all eight functions including the weak Fi result; residuals and λ ranges are published.
- The multi-dimensional backtracking experiment is the most interesting conceptual contribution and is supported by a clean Least-Squares analysis across 12 combinations.
- Releases a new dataset (NarrationDB) and an LLM-usable Jungian evaluation protocol, both reusable by the community.
- Connects mechanistic findings (layer roles, entanglement) back to a named psychological theory rather than stopping at "the vectors work."

## Limitations

- Single architecture (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct); no transfer to other model families or scales.
- No quantitative comparison with Big Five / persona-vector baselines on a common benchmark.
- Fi steering is ineffective in the positive direction and the explanation (post-training pinning) is plausible but unverified.
- Evaluation is partially circular: the test used to extract the steering vector is also the test used to score it.
- Jungian typology's empirical validity in psychology is contested; treating it as ground truth is a conceptual risk.
- Code/data release URL not given in the paper; the 80% "task completion" filter and question-shuffle variance bounds are under-specified.
- No downstream task or human evaluation to confirm that steered personalities manifest in actual behavior.

## Relevance

对从事 LLM 可解释性与激活 steering 的研究者，本文提供了一个新的、过程化的人格维度（荣格八维）以及多维方向不可线性分解的直接证据，这与 persona vector、steering vector 几何独立性的工作形成互补。如果你关注"激活空间中概念如何被表示与组合"，论文的多维 backtracking 方法和 NarrationDB 数据集是直接可复用的素材。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the layer 7–12 localization and rational/irrational geometric structure replicate in other model families (Qwen, Mistral, Gemma) and at other scales, or are they Llama-3.1-specific artifacts?
- How do Jungian-steered personalities compare quantitatively against Big Five (OCEAN) steering on a shared downstream behavioral benchmark (dialogue style, moral-dilemma responses, decision-making)?
- Does the Fi positive-direction failure disappear in a base (non-instruct / non-RLHF) Llama model, which would confirm the post-training-pinning hypothesis?
- Can the multi-dimensional non-linearity result be exploited — i.e., are there steering tasks where directly extracted joint vectors outperform any single-dim combination by a measurable margin?
- How sensitive are the results to the seed averaging and to the 80% task-completion filter — would tightening or loosening these change the ranking of most-effective layers?
- Does an independent, non-Jungian behavioral evaluation (e.g., decisions on moral dilemmas, conversational style markers) actually move in the theoretically predicted direction when a given function is steered, breaking the circular evaluation?
