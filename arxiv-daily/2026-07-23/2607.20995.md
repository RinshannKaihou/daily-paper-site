> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了一个受控的 animacy（有生性）最小对数据集，用 EAP-IG 电路发现方法在 GPT-2 Small、Llama 3.2 3B、Gemma 3 4B、Qwen 3 4B 四个开源模型上找到了既充分又必要的 animacy 电路，但该电路比其他已知电路更分散、跨任务迁移不均一，证实了 animacy 概念的分布式、上下文依赖和梯度特性。

> **TL;DR:** Using a controlled minimal-pair dataset of passive prefixes (e.g., "The victim was examined by the" vs. "...blinded by the"), the authors apply EAP-IG circuit discovery to four open-weight models (GPT-2 Small, Llama 3.2 3B, Gemma 3 4B, Qwen 3 4B) and identify causally sufficient and necessary "animacy circuits." Qwen 3 reaches 85% faithfulness and 97% accuracy using only 1.74% of its edges, while other models need 7-13% of edges; however, the recovered circuits are larger and more distributed than known circuits for tasks like Greater-Than or IoI, and they transfer unevenly (e.g., Qwen drops to 33% accuracy under a BLiMP prefix shift), arguing against a single universal animacy mechanism.

## 核心贡献

- 首次对 LLM 中 animacy（有生性）理解进行因果机制层面的研究：之前的工作多为行为评估或基于表征的分析，本文填补了"是否存在专责 animacy 的因果电路"这一空白。
- 构建了一个新的受控最小对数据集：使用模板 "The [patient] was [verb p.p.] by the"，通过 GPT 生成语义框架 + Llama 3.3 70B 合理性过滤 + 人工校验，最终保留 16,305 / 20,000 (81.5%) 高质量对，并构造约 500 个 animate / inanimate 目标名词集合。
- 在四个开源模型（GPT-2 Small、Llama 3.2 3B、Gemma 3 4B、Qwen 3 4B）上运行 EAP-IG 电路发现，并通过充分性、必要性、随机边对照、跨种子稳定性、跨任务迁移五个维度系统评估电路。
- 发现 animacy 电路主要由 MLP 主导（尤其是 MLP0），且分布式地跨越从首层到中后层，而非集中在单一区域；attention heads 仅承担位置路由作用。
- 通过三组迁移实验（换数据 / 换目标类型 / 两者都换）证明电路并非"万能 animacy 电路"，迁移不均匀，揭示了 animacy 作为多因素、分级、上下文依赖语义现象的本质。

## Key Contributions

- First causal, mechanistic-interpretability study of animacy comprehension in LLMs, going beyond prior behavioral (Coll Ardanuy et al., 2020) and surprisal/representation-based analyses (Hanna et al., 2023a; Kauf et al., 2023) to ask whether a dedicated circuit exists.
- A new controlled minimal-pair dataset of 16,305 pairs (81.5% retention from 20,000 generated) built from the template "The [patient] was [verb p.p.] by the", with Llama 3.3 70B plausibility filtering at the 0.90 Yes-probability threshold and manual frame inspection, plus ~500-noun animate/inanimate target sets derived from WordNet.
- Systematic EAP-IG circuit discovery across four open-weight models (GPT-2 Small, Llama 3.2 3B, Gemma 3 4B, Qwen 3 4B), validated through a five-axis protocol: sufficiency, necessity, random-edge controls, seed stability, and transfer.
- Empirical demonstration that animacy circuits are MLP-dominated (with MLP0 prominent) and distributed across early-to-late layers, unlike compact localized circuits found for Greater-Than, IoI, or Country-Capital.
- A nuanced transfer study (Setup 1: BLiMP prefixes + original targets; Setup 2: original prefixes + named entities; Setup 3: BLiMP prefixes + named entities) showing partial but uneven generalization, refuting the "single universal animacy circuit" interpretation.

## Methodology

**Data construction.** Existing datasets are ill-suited for circuit discovery: BLiMP `subject_passive` puts the contrastive noun in the input (no patchable target), and Gregorio et al. (2025) lack minimal-pair structure. The authors therefore build a custom dataset using the template `"The [patient] was [verb p.p.] by the"`. GPT 5.4 generates joint patient+verb "semantic frames" across categories, which are manually inspected. For each frame the template is instantiated with every patient and every (animate_verb × inanimate_verb) pair, producing minimal pairs like *"The victim was examined by the"* (clean, favors animate continuation) vs. *"The victim was blinded by the"* (corrupt, favors inanimate continuation). Llama 3.3 70B with a zero-shot Yes/No prompt scores plausibility; pairs are retained only if both clean and corrupt prefixes exceed 0.90 Yes probability. Animate targets are restricted to unambiguous `noun.person` words (officer, manager, captain, ...); inanimate targets are non-person nouns from WordNet categories `noun.event`, `noun.phenomenon`, `noun.state`, `noun.artifact`, `noun.substance`, `noun.object`. After model-specific single-token filtering, a shared cross-model subset of 9,532 sentence pairs remains.

**Task and metric.** Because there is no single ground-truth next token, the authors define a set-based **average logit difference** over the animate target set A and inanimate target set I: `avgLD(x) = mean_a logit(a|x) − mean_i logit(i|x)`. A pair is "correct" when `clean avgLD > 0` AND `corrupt avgLD < 0` (random baseline = 25%). A per-pair **margin** = `clean_avgLD − corrupt_avgLD` measures separation strength. Only pairs the model solves are retained for circuit discovery, and a margin > 0.5 threshold selects 500 high-confidence discovery examples per model.

**Circuit discovery.** The authors apply **Edge Attribution Patching with Integrated Gradients (EAP-IG)** (Hanna et al., 2024) to rank directed edges in four models. Faithfulness is the primary metric: `Faithfulness(x) = (m_patched − m_corrupt) / (m_clean − m_corrupt)`, averaged over pairs. A greedy increasing-budget procedure selects the smallest circuit reaching ≥85% faithfulness. Discovery is followed by necessity tests (cumulative top-k ablation), size-matched random-edge controls, seed-stability IoU checks (seeds 0, 1, 42), and a backup-circuit (shadow-circuit) rediscovery test.

**Transfer evaluation.** Three setups test generalization: (1) BLiMP `subject_passive` prefixes with the original animate/inanimate target sets; (2) original prefixes with named-entity continuations (animate/inanimate proper names); (3) BLiMP prefixes + named entities. The original 85%-faithfulness circuit is applied to each, and where possible (Setup 2) a new circuit is independently discovered and its IoU with the original is reported.

## Results & Highlights

- **Behavioral task success (random baseline 25%):** GPT-2 retained 4,721/9,532 pairs (49.53%); Llama 3.2 3B retained 4,734 (49.66%); Gemma 3 4B retained 4,371 (45.86%); Qwen 3 4B retained 4,300 (45.11%) under the sign-based avgLD metric.
- **Circuit compactness:** Qwen 3 reaches the 85%-faithfulness threshold (with 97% accuracy) using only **1.74% of its collapsed edges**, whereas GPT-2, Llama 3.2 3B, and Gemma 3 4B require circuits in the **7-13% of total collapsed edges** range — substantially larger than known circuits for Greater-Than, IoI, or Country-Capital.
- **Robustness vs. random edges:** At the budget where the discovered circuit first reaches 85% faithfulness, the faithfulness gap between the discovered circuit and a random-edge baseline is **87.6% (GPT-2), 93.5% (Llama 3.2 3B), 87.9% (Gemma 3 4B), and 85.6% (Qwen 3 4B)**, and across-seed discovery runs show IoU values between **0.8 and 1.0**.
- **Necessity:** Ablating only the top-20 EAP-IG edges drops faithfulness **below 0.1 for all four models**, while disjoint 20-edge sets sampled from ranks 21-200 do not produce comparable damage (Appendix Table 4); no strong backup circuit is rediscovered (max reduced-graph faithfulness 0.000433 for GPT-2 and similarly near-zero for the others, Appendix Table 5).
- **Component composition:** MLPs dominate the induced scores — in GPT-2, MLP0 shows an induced-score gap of ~1.18 over the next MLP; the gap is ~0.11 in Llama 3.2 and ~0.06 in Gemma 3 (where MLP0 and MLP5 tie at 0.13), indicating the circuit is MLP-driven and distributed rather than localized.
- **Transfer (Setup 3, both data and target type changed):** the original circuit breaks — e.g., GPT-2 faithfulness stays at 0.86 but **accuracy collapses to 0.05**, Llama 3.2 3B to **0.17**, Qwen 3 4B to **0.52**, and Gemma 3 4B to **0.47** — even though full models still solve the task (>0.84 accuracy).
- **Setup 2 named-entity transfer:** Llama 3.2 3B is strongest (faithfulness 0.910, accuracy 0.892) while GPT-2 and Qwen 3 drop sharply (faithfulness 0.526 and 0.596); the independently discovered Setup-2 circuits share IoU ~0.4-0.7 with the original common-noun circuits.

## Authors & Credibility

- **Samuele Punzo** (University of Amsterdam, Graduate School of Informatics), **Giovanni Cinà** (Amsterdam UMC, Medical Informatics; ILLC), and **Sandro Pezzelle** (ILLC, University of Amsterdam), with Cinà and Pezzelle sharing equal supervision. Pezzelle has a directly relevant prior publication line on animacy in LMs (Hanna, Belinkov, Pezzelle 2023a "When language models fall in love"; Hanna, Pezzelle, Belinkov 2024 "Have faith in faithfulness"), so the team has deep domain expertise in both animacy semantics and the EAP-IG method they use.
- The methodology closely follows best-practice circuit-discovery evaluation (Hanna et al., 2024; Heimersheim & Nanda, 2024; Mueller, 2024; Zhang & Nanda, 2024), including the explicit warning that single-setting faithfulness does not imply a task-general mechanism — a caveat the authors take seriously in their transfer experiments.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine first-mover work — no prior study has performed causal circuit discovery for animacy; existing work was behavioral or representational. The contribution is conceptual (animacy is mechanistically localizable but not via a universal circuit) rather than a new method; the EAP-IG technique is borrowed wholesale from Hanna et al. (2024).
- **Rigor:** Strong by interpretability-community standards: five-axis evaluation (sufficiency, necessity, random controls, seed stability, transfer), explicit margin thresholds, shared cross-model subset of 9,532 pairs, and honest reporting of model-specific tokenization differences (450/450 targets for GPT-2 vs. 401/434 for Llama/Qwen).
- **Reproducibility:** Good — four open-weight models, a documented dataset-construction pipeline, and a stated public code/data release ("Datasets and code are available at this link"). Caveats: dataset depends on a GPT 5.4 generator and a Llama 3.3 70B plausibility filter, introducing undocumented lexical/event-level regularities the authors themselves flag as a limitation.
- **Significance:** Moderate-to-high for the mechanistic-interpretability and lexical-semantics communities; the negative result (no universal animacy circuit; circuits are large, MLP-dominated, and transfer poorly) is itself valuable and tempers over-claiming seen in earlier single-task circuit work.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:**
  - **Task narrowness:** Only one construction (passive `by`-prefixes) and only person-vs-inanimate animacy (not the full Human → Animal → Plant → Inanimate hierarchy) are tested, so "animacy circuit" is arguably an overstatement for "agent-vs-cause circuit in passive `by`-frames." The authors acknowledge this.
  - **Data circularity risk:** Semantic frames generated by GPT 5.4, plausibility filtered by Llama 3.3 70B — neither is among the four evaluated models, which mitigates but does not eliminate generator-specific regularities; no human-validated evaluation split is used.
  - **High-confidence cherry-picking:** Circuit discovery uses 500 sign-correct pairs with margin > 0.5, while models only solve ~45-50% of pairs to begin with, so the circuit describes a successful subset (~half the data) and explicitly does not explain model failures.
  - **GPT-2 as a "baseline model" is weak:** A 124M model from 2019 sitting beside three 3-4B 2025 models; the resulting compactness comparisons (Qwen at 1.74% vs. others at 7-13%) are confounded by model scale and architecture.
  - **Functional interpretation is preliminary:** The MLP0 "routing vs. writing" story is supported by PCA and logit-contribution plots but not by causal feature-level identification.

## Strengths

- First mechanistic, causal account of animacy in LLMs, filling an explicit gap in the literature.
- Methodologically careful: multi-axis validation, random-edge controls, seed-stability IoU, shadow-circuit tests, and three distinct transfer setups — exactly the controls recent critiques (Mueller 2024; Zhang & Nanda 2024) demand.
- Honest, non-overclaiming framing: the paper repeatedly stresses that the circuit is task-relative, not a universal animacy mechanism, and lists concrete limitations.
- A reusable, controlled minimal-pair dataset (16,305 pairs) that solves the patching incompatibility of BLiMP and the non-minimal-pair nature of Gregorio et al. (2025).
- Cross-model consistency of the qualitative finding (MLP-dominated, distributed, early verb information routed via MLP0) across four architecturally diverse models lends credibility.

## Limitations

- Single syntactic construction (passive `by`-prefixes) and person-vs-inanimate contrast only; no active sentences, coreference, agreement, animal/personified agents, or discourse-level animacy.
- Dataset relies on LLM-generated frames and automatic plausibility filtering; no large human-validated or lexically/frame-disjoint evaluation split.
- Circuit discovery is conditioned on successful model behavior (~45-50% of pairs) and on a margin > 0.5 high-confidence subset, so it does not explain model failures or estimate prevalence over the full dataset.
- EAP-IG gives only an approximate edge ranking; results depend on task metric, clean-corrupt contrast, graph representation, and intervention distribution — different schemes could surface different structures.
- Functional interpretation of the necessary core (what features MLP0 routes, how later MLPs transform them) remains preliminary; no feature-level causal characterization.
- GPT-2 Small is an order of magnitude smaller and six years older than the other three models, complicating cross-model compactness comparisons.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 可解释性（mechanistic interpretability / circuit discovery）的研究者高度相关：本文提供了一套"充分性 + 必要性 + 迁移"的电路评估范式，可作为研究其他抽象语义概念（如 agency、causality、thematic roles）因果定位的模板；对关注 lexical/semantic concept 在神经网络中如何编码的工作亦有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does an animacy circuit recovered from active-voice subject-verb-object frames (e.g., "The dog chased the ball" vs. "The storm damaged the bridge") overlap with the passive `by`-frame circuit found here, or do active and passive animacy rely on distinct mechanisms?
- How does the circuit change when the animate target set is expanded down the animacy hierarchy (animals, imaginary beings) rather than restricted to `noun.person`?
- Can a feature-level interpretability method (e.g., SAEs, dictionary learning on MLP0 outputs) identify the specific verb-level features that MLP0 routes, moving beyond the current "routing vs. writing" speculation?
- Would circuits discovered on human-validated, lexically-disjoint evaluation sets look structurally different from the GPT-generated, Llama-filtered dataset used here?
- Why is Qwen 3 4B's circuit an order of magnitude more compact (1.74% of edges) than the other three models — is this an artifact of EAP-IG on Qwen's architecture, a genuine mechanistic difference, or evidence of a less distributed representation that also explains its poor transfer?
- Do the discovered animacy circuits align with the "attention sink" / early-layer MLP patterns (Gu et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2024) reported in recent architecture studies, suggesting a generic early-layer routing role rather than an animacy-specific one?
