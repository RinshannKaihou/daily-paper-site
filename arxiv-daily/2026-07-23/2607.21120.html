Key
Finding

Only value differences — not absolute magnitudes — are needed for control; a pairwise Bellman operator provably γ-contracts, and the reconstructed R-GAE is an unbiased drop-in PPO critic.

PPO+RV beats standard PPO on final mean scores across the 49-game Atari/ALE benchmark — and beats DAE on 37/49 (75%). 10 seeds per game, 40M frames each.

Why this matters

Drops the behaviorally meaningless absolute value scale at the model level — a drop-in PPO critic that opens principled relative-value RL.

Advantages and greedy action selection are invariant to additive shifts of Vπ; RV removes that gauge degree of freedom by construction.

Critical edge caveat

Motivation invokes preference-based RL / RLHF and reward shaping — but no such experiment is run. Only PPO on discrete-action Atari, with 19/49 games still lost.

Baselines are PPO and DAE only — no dueling, distributional, or modern tuned PPO. "Competitive with PPO" is a modest bar; DAE is itself weak here.

02

Methodology

RV reframes value learning around the fact that, for γ<1, the absolute scale of Vπ is behaviorally irrelevant: advantages and greedy action selection are invariant to additive shifts. Only value differences matter for control — so RV makes differences the primary learning objective.

Representation

An antisymmetric function Δθ(si,sj) = Φ(fenc(si) − fenc(sj)) with a bias-free learned vector w. Antisymmetry and zero self-difference hold by construction — no target networks, no stop-gradients.

Δθ(si,sj) = −Δθ(sj,si), Δθ(s,s) = 0

Theory core · Thm 3.1

On the Banach space of bounded antisymmetric pairwise functions, the pairwise Bellman operator is a γ-contraction; by Banach, its unique fixed point equals the true value differences Δπ.

(TπΔ)(si,sj) = (ri−rj) + γ·E[Δ(s′i,s′j)]

Well-posed targets

Absolute values are inaccessible, so naive bootstrap targets go ill-posed when a successor is terminal. Targets are rearranged into observable-reward + non-terminal-pair form via a four-case (di, dj) branch — covering 1-step, n-step, and λ-return.

Relative GAE · Lem 3.2 / Cor 3.3

GAE is reconstructed from pairwise differences by anchoring each rollout to zero and telescoping. Ãt = At + Bt (Bt a trajectory constant); replacing A with Ã leaves the policy gradient unbiased.

Ṽθ(s0)=0, Ṽθ(st)=Σk<t Δθ(sk+1,sk)

Results & Highlights

On the 49-game Atari/ALE benchmark (10 seeds × 40M frames = 490 runs), PPO+RV is competitive as a drop-in critic, not strictly dominant. Under the Agarwal et al. (2021) protocol with 95% stratified-bootstrap CIs, it achieves higher median / IQM / mean human-normalized score and a lower optimality gap than both PPO and DAE. Compute: ~65 min/run on a single A100; 490 runs total ≈ 530 GPU-hours.

04

Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths

  • Theoretically clean. Genuine γ-contraction result and a crisp proof that R-GAE is an unbiased policy-gradient estimator, with the exact (Bt) relationship to standard GAE.
  • Principled motivation. The gauge-freedom argument (only differences matter for control) is correct; the antisymmetric-by-construction critic removes an unnecessary degree of freedom.
  • Well-posed targets. Four-case handling of terminal successors is a non-trivial detail that makes the framework actually deployable.
  • Reproducible. Open code, modest compute (~530 GPU-hours), identical hyperparameters across all 49 games, 10 seeds, field-standard Agarwal aggregate protocol.
  • Honest reporting. Candid about the Δγ alternative performing worse and about per-game losses vs PPO.

− Limitations

  • O(B²) pairwise cost. Naive pairwise training scales quadratically in batch size; relies on subsampling with no throughput numbers given for the critic step.
  • Critic expressivity. Enforced antisymmetry plus a near-linear difference head may underfit complex value differences; the non-linear-head ablation is asserted but not shown.
  • Variance from Bt. Gauge fixing injects a trajectory constant that inflates variance for long horizons or as γλ→1, only partially mitigated by ranking.
  • Narrow evaluation. Only PPO on discrete-action Atari — continuous control, off-policy regimes, and the motivation's preference-based / RLHF settings remain undemonstrated.
  • Thin baselines. No comparison to dueling networks, distributional critics, or modern tuned PPO configs; "competitive with PPO" is a modest bar.

Open Questions

Q1

Does RV transfer to continuous control (MuJoCo / DM Control) and off-policy actor-critic (SAC, TD3), where pairwise targets and replay-buffer pairing raise new design questions?

Q2

Can the pairwise critic serve preference-based / RLHF learning where only pairwise comparisons are observable — the setting the motivation invokes but never tests?

Q3

How sensitive are results to the n-step horizon and the O(B²) subsampling ratio? The authors supply no ablation tables for either.

Q4

Would a non-linear antisymmetric head (tanh + bias-free layers) recover the losses on Centipede, Zaxxon, and Tennis where the linear head underperforms PPO?

Q5

Can RV combine with distributional or dueling critics, or does the antisymmetry constraint conflict with those representations?
