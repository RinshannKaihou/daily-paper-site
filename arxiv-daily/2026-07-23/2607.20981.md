> **一句话总结：** 这是一篇综述论文，论证了视觉 token 压缩、MoE 路由、量化、KV-cache 管理与边缘硬件执行不能作为独立优化来对待——它们构成一条"失败传播链"，上游干预会变成下游的分布偏移、路由错误、量化故障或证据丢失；论文提出统一的失败链分类法、形式化了一个用于视频 MoE 的"时序路由一致性 (TRC)"诊断指标，并指出压缩-路由交互是当前最显著的研究空白。

> **TL;DR:** A survey arguing that the efficiency techniques used in multimodal/VLM/MoE inference — visual token compression (ToMe, ATM, MustDrop, ZipVL), MoE routing, low-bit quantization (W4A4/W3A3), KV-cache eviction (SnapKV, PyramidKV, PagedAttention), and edge deployment — form a coupled *failure-propagation chain* where each stage's output distribution feeds the next, so locally optimal choices can cause routing instability, miscalibrated quantization, evidence loss, or memory-movement blowups. It synthesizes 100+ works (2021–2026) around three interfaces (compression→routing, routing→quantization, hardware feedback), introduces **Temporal Routing Consistency (TRC)** as a Jaccard-overlap diagnostic for expert jitter in video MoE models, and quantifies the stakes with cited numbers such as routing-aware PTQ recovering **1.15%–2.28%** average score on low-bit MoE benchmarks (Fu et al., 2026), modality-aware VLM quantization retaining **93.5% of FP16 at W3A3 (69.5 vs 74.3)** on SplitQ (Zhong et al., 2026), and ZipVL attaining **2.3× prefill / 2.8× decoding at only 0.5% VQAv2 loss** (He et al., 2024). The TL;DR upshot: on benchmarks like VQAv2, OpenVLM/MMBench V1.1, HotpotQA/MuSiQue and edge suites (PalmBench, MLPerf Power), no current method or benchmark jointly measures all five axes, making the compression–routing interaction (Interface B) the largest open gap.

## 核心贡献

- **统一的失败传播链分类法（Failure Propagation Chain）**：首次将压缩、MoE 路由、量化、KV-cache、硬件执行串联成 `P = H ∘ Qπ ∘ Rθ ∘ Cϕ` 的复合管线，明确指出公式 (2) 中各阶段独立优化的假设在统计上几乎不成立，并形式化了三个关键接口（A: 视觉输入→压缩；B: 压缩→路由；C: 路由→量化）。
- **时序路由一致性 TRC 诊断指标**：针对 Transformer-MoE 缺乏循环状态这一结构性缺陷，提出用相邻帧激活专家集合的平均 Jaccard 重叠 `TRC = (1/(T−1)) Σ |E_t ∩ E_{t+1}| / |E_t ∪ E_{t+1}|` 来度量视频 MoE 的"专家抖动"，使原本被默认的时间连续性变得可测量、可惩罚。
- **压缩-路由交互（Interface B）被识别为最大研究空白**：明确指出 routing-stability 从未作为 token 压缩方法的标配评测指标；论文用放大关系不等式 `Δ_CQ ≥ Δ_Q + Δ_C − Δ_Q·Δ_C` 刻画了压缩与量化联合引起的路由变化下界，并主张用压缩/非压缩输入下的专家分配 KL 散度作为路由感知压缩目标。
- **不可分解 Pareto 前沿与硬件协同设计视角**：在边缘硬件上，由于内存搬运（而非算术）主导能耗，压缩、路由、量化、cache 驻留与硬件容量构成"互 constrained"变量，独立优化后再组合不能保证端到端最优；作者主张报告 `⟨accuracy, latency, energy, peak memory, KV footprint, expert traffic, routing stability⟩` 的完整部署元组。
- **系统化文献覆盖**：覆盖 2021–2026 年 100+ 篇工作，跨越 ViT 冗余、MLLM pipeline 压缩、视频时序冗余、KV-cache、MoE 路由/服务、量化、边缘部署与硬件感知基准，并通过表 1（主优化矩阵）、表 2（压缩方法对比 + 接口影响列）、表 4（量化失败模式分类）将文献映射到失败链的具体接口。

## Key Contributions

- **A unified failure-chain taxonomy** that folds compression-induced routing distribution shift, low-bit routing inconsistency, and modality-heterogeneous quantization degradation into a single propagation chain `P = H ∘ Qπ ∘ Rθ ∘ Cϕ`, explaining why per-stage optimization leaves downstream failures unaddressed.
- **Temporal Routing Consistency (TRC)** — a formal diagnostic (mean Jaccard overlap of expert sets across adjacent frames) that makes the *absence of recurrent state* in Transformer-MoE measurable, converting implicit temporal continuity into a first-class metric that can be jointly optimized with compression for video MoE.
- **Identification of Interface B (compression→routing) as the dominant research gap**: the survey shows that routing stability is not reported by any token-compression method as a standard metric, formalizes a joint amplification lower bound `Δ_CQ ≥ Δ_Q + Δ_C − Δ_Q·Δ_C`, and proposes a KL-divergence routing-stability objective analogous to routing-consistent quantization.
- **A non-decomposable Pareto / co-design view**: on edge devices, where memory movement (not FLOPs) dominates energy (Chung et al., 2026), compression ratio, routing behaviour, quantization precision, KV residency, and hardware capacity are mutually constrained; the paper calls for reporting the full deployment tuple rather than a single accuracy number.
- **Systematic 2021–2026 coverage** of 100+ works across ViT redundancy, MLLM pipeline compression, video temporal redundancy, KV-cache, MoE routing/serving, quantization, edge deployment, and hardware-aware benchmarking, mapped to specific chain interfaces via Table 1 (master optimization matrix), Table 2 (compression methods with an "interface impact" column), and Table 4 (quantization failure taxonomy).

## Methodology

This is a **survey/synthesis paper**, so the methodology is analytical and taxonomic rather than empirical. The authors proceed in four steps:

1. **Architectural grounding (§2).** They fix the vocabulary: ViTs treat patches as tokens with substantial redundancy (MAE pretraining works with 75%–85% masking; VideoMAE with ~90%); MLLMs process visual and text tokens identically (the root of token-budget asymmetry); MoE replaces one FFN with N experts and a Top-k router, formalized as `Router(x) = TopK(softmax(W_r x), k)` with the load-balancing target `E_x[1{i ∈ TopK(x)}] ≈ k/N`. They characterize edge constraints as a hierarchy: SRAM (few–tens of MB on mobile SoCs) → DRAM bandwidth → energy/thermal envelope.

2. **The failure-propagation framework (§3).** They model inference as the composition `P = H ∘ Qπ ∘ Rθ ∘ Cϕ` and argue the independence assumption in equation (2) — `ϕ* = argmin L(Cϕ)`, etc. — rarely holds. They define three interfaces (A: visual input→compression; B: compression→routing; C: routing→quantization) plus a hardware feedback loop, and for each give: what it transfers, how it can fail, and the literature state. They introduce TRC in §3.5 as `TRC = (1/(T−1)) Σ_{t} |E_t ∩ E_{t+1}| / |E_t ∪ E_{t+1}|` and derive the joint amplification lower bound for routing changes.

3. **Per-stage literature synthesis (§4–§12).** Sections are organized along the chain: token redundancy evidence (§4), ViT compression with the dropping-vs-merging dichotomy (§5), MLLM pipeline sensitivity (MustDrop, HiRED, ZipVL, SparseVLM, EvoComp, CS-VLM) and positional integrity (§6), video/temporal redundancy and the TRC discussion (§7), KV-cache from PagedAttention → H2O/SnapKV/PyramidKV → ChunkKV → graph-based (GraphKV, MadaKV) → state-space/semantic (§8), MoE routing design space, expert collapse diagnosis (utilization < 1/N = 1.5625% for 64 experts; routing-Gini > 0.7) and VQ codebook collapse (§9), high-throughput MoE serving (ExpertFlow, D2MoE, Janus, disaggregated serving) (§10), quantization failure taxonomy (signal degradation vs computation collapse vs routing inconsistency vs modality-heterogeneous degradation vs speculative-decoding interaction) (§11), and edge on-device deployment with hot/cold expert storage and lossless compression (§12).

4. **Benchmarking critique and open problems (§13–§14).** They catalogue current suites (ImageNet, OpenVLM MMBench V1.1, LLMInferenceBench, PalmBench, HW-GPT-Bench, CarAML, MLPerf Power, MoE-CAP, LocateBench) and argue none jointly measures compression + routing + KV + quantization + hardware under one protocol; they propose a deployment-tuple benchmark and enumerate nine open directions including routing-aware compression, theoretical foundations of collapse, proactive failure detection, hardware-aware co-design, federated edge inference, and hallucination-under-compression.

## Results & Highlights

Because this is a survey, the "results" are quantitative claims **synthesized from the cited primary literature** rather than new experiments. The most load-bearing numbers the paper foregrounds:

- **Routing-aware post-training quantization recovers 1.15%–2.28% average score across three MoE architectures under W4A4/W3A4 settings** (Fu et al., 2026, EAQuant) — evidence that quantization-induced routing perturbations are non-negligible and that preserving the full-precision Top-k assignment structure (Park et al., 2026, VSRAQ) yields further gains. This is the central empirical anchor for Interface C.
- **Modality-aware VLM quantization (SplitQ) retains 93.5% of FP16 performance at W3A3 (69.5 vs 74.3)** on LLaVA-class benchmarks, vs naive joint quantization that loses **2%–4% VQA accuracy at W4A8** with losses concentrated in spatial-reasoning tasks (Zhong et al., 2026) — quantifying modality-heterogeneous degradation. The paper also reports visual-token std is up to **4× higher** than text-token std and outlier probability is **~2.3% vs ~0.6%**.
- **ZipVL delivers 2.3× prefilling acceleration and 2.8× decoding-throughput improvement at only a 0.5% VQAv2 accuracy reduction** (He et al., 2024) — the strongest cited single number for stage-coordinated KV sparsification.
- **RetentiveKV achieves 5× KV compression with 1.5× decoding acceleration** on MLLM benchmarks (Liu et al., 2026a); modality-adaptive eviction yields **1.3×–1.5× decoding latency reduction** (Li et al., 2025b, MadaKV).
- **ToMe token merging: ~2× throughput with only 0.2%–0.3% accuracy loss on ImageNet** for DeiT/MAE/CLIP (Bolya et al., 2023); ATM reduces DeiT FLOPs **>30% with exact accuracy preserved** (Lee & Choi, 2025); MaMe/MaRe cuts Stable Diffusion latency by **31%** (Huo & Li, 2026).
- **High-throughput MoE serving: up to 25.5× speedup (4.6× average)** vs unsophisticated dense baselines (Yuan et al., 2025); **3.9× per-GPU throughput** for disaggregated attention/expert serving (Janus, Zhang et al., 2026); **2–10× inference speedup** from predictive routing paths (ExpertFlow, He et al., 2024); **25% fewer activated experts and 1.35× speedup** from sensitivity-based gating (Zhong et al., 2024); **up to 72.77% inference-latency reduction** from lossless expert compression + cache-aware scheduling (ZipMoE, Yang et al., 2026).
- **Speculative decoding under quantization: 2.78× speedup and 1.31× over EAGLE-2 on 4-bit Llama-3-70B** via hierarchical drafter–target design (Zhang et al., 2025c); W8A8 quantization drops drafter acceptance by **3%–5%**, mitigating speculative gains by **1.5–2×**.
- **Expert collapse diagnostics: utilization threshold 1/N (=1.5625% for 64 experts); ~one-third of sparse MoE layers drift to single-expert routing** under weak balancing (Sha, 2026); **selective deadlock causes 8%–15% FID loss** in class-conditioned generation; **routing-probability Gini > 0.7** predicts collapse regardless of topology (Delibasoglu, 2026).
- **Self-supervised redundancy evidence: 75%–85% patch masking (MAE) and ~90% video masking (VideoMAE)** still yield competitive features (He et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2022) — the quantitative justification for "≥75% of visual tokens can be removed without information loss."
- **OpenVLM MMBench V1.1 vs edge-throughput Pareto frontier (Fig. 7)**: high-accuracy models cluster upper-left (large footprint, low throughput); the upper-right "high accuracy + high throughput + low memory" region is essentially empty — the empirical "edge hardware wall."

No quantitative results reported *by this paper itself* (it performs no new experiments); all numbers above are attributed to cited works.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core thesis — "compression, routing, quantization, and cache are coupled, not independent" — is not a new insight per se; practitioners have long known this. The genuine novelty is (a) formalizing it as a four-stage composition with three named interfaces, (b) the **TRC diagnostic** (a concrete, computable Jaccard metric for video MoE expert jitter, which to my knowledge no prior work defines), and (c) the inclusion-exclusion lower bound `Δ_CQ ≥ Δ_Q + Δ_C − Δ_Q·Δ_C`. TRC is the survey's most citable, reusable contribution.
- **Rigor**: Mixed. The framework is conceptually rigorous, but several load-bearing claims are **undersupported by direct evidence**:
  - The compression→routing interaction (Interface B) is repeatedly called "the most significant research gap," yet the only quantitative support is borrowed from **quantization** studies (Fu et al.; Park et al.) and argued by analogy: "compression can modify both representations and sequence structure, [so] these observations suggest that compression may induce routing shifts comparable to, or potentially larger than, those observed under quantization." The authors admit this "remains a hypothesis rather than an established result." A survey whose central gap is itself unmeasured is intellectually honest but leaves the framework's predictive power unvalidated.
  - The amplification inequality `Δ_CQ ≥ Δ_Q + Δ_C − Δ_Q·Δ_C` is presented as a lower bound *under the assumption that perturbation sources affect disjoint subsets* — but the text immediately concedes that in practice they affect the *same* marginal assignments, so the bound is loose in the conservative direction and "a tighter bound would require empirical measurement… which does not yet exist." Useful as a pointer, weak as a theorem.
- **Reproducibility**: Not directly applicable (survey), but the **TRC metric is fully specified** (a Jaccard average over adjacent-frame expert sets) and trivially computable from any Top-k MoE model, which makes it genuinely reproducible as a community diagnostic. By contrast, the speedup numbers in Table 2 are explicitly flagged as "not directly comparable across different base models and hardware" — a caveat the survey makes visible but does not resolve.
- **Significance**: High if the community adopts TRC and the deployment-tuple benchmark proposal; moderate otherwise. The compression→routing-co-design framing is a real agenda-setter. The cataloguing value is high for new entrants.
- **Red flags / overclaiming**:
  - The paper leans heavily on a small cluster of **2026 arXiv preprints** (Fu et al. EAQuant; Park et al. VSRAQ; Zhong et al. SplitQ; Do et al.; Delibasoglu) that are themselves not peer-reviewed; the failure-chain argument rests disproportionately on this thin 2026 slice.
  - Some specific numbers are quoted without the original context (model, dataset, protocol). For example, "25.5× speedup" (Yuan et al., 2025) is reported against an "unsophisticated dense baseline," which inflates the headline; the survey passes it through with only the parenthetical "(4.6× on average)" as a hedge.
  - The proposed "unified multimodal edge benchmark" is sketched at the tuple level but **no concrete benchmark, protocol, or reference implementation is provided** — it remains an aspirational Table 5 gap analysis.
  - Minor: the Tanwar/Wang email field in the author block contains a duplicated Marwadi address (rajesh.gupta@...) where a Singaporetech address likely belongs, suggesting a rushed proofread.
- **Overall**: A genuinely useful synthesis with one strong, reusable novel artifact (TRC) and a clear agenda. It overclaims slightly by presenting the compression→routing failure chain as more established than the cited evidence supports, but is unusually transparent about its own gaps.

## Strengths

- The **failure-propagation-chain formalism** (`P = H ∘ Qπ ∘ Rθ ∘ Cϕ`) is a clean organizing lens that turns a sprawling literature into a single causal story; the interface-by-interface template (transfer / failure mode / state of literature) is consistently applied.
- **TRC is a concrete, low-cost, high-value contribution**: a Jaccard diagnostic that any video MoE team can compute today, addressing a real gap (Transformer-MoE has no recurrent state guaranteeing temporal continuity).
- Intellectual honesty about **what is and isn't measured**: the paper explicitly names Interface B as unmeasured, flags the amplification bound as loose, and repeatedly distinguishes established results from hypotheses — a refreshing trait for a survey.
- Strong **design-tension synthesis** (accuracy vs token budget; static vs content-adaptive; sparse efficiency vs expert collapse; low-bit vs modality-specific degradation; benchmark convenience vs edge realism), each backed by named methods.
- The **"interface impact" column in Table 2** and the **quantization failure taxonomy in Table 4** are reusable reference artifacts that go beyond mere cataloguing.
- Coverage is broad and current (through mid-2026), spanning compression, KV-cache, MoE, quantization, serving, and hardware in one place — a genuine one-stop map.

## Limitations

- **No original experiments**: the entire framework rests on cited numbers; the central Interface B gap is, by the authors' own admission, unmeasured. The predictive content of the failure chain (e.g., that compression-induced routing shift exceeds quantization-induced shift) is conjectural.
- **Heavy reliance on unrefereed 2026 preprints** for the load-bearing routing-quantization claims; if EAQuant/VSRAQ/SplitQ shift under peer review, the framework's empirical anchors shift with them.
- **Speedup figures in Tables 2 and the text are non-comparable** (different base models, hardware, compression ratios), yet are presented in close proximity; a reader could over- or under-weight them. The caveat is present but easy to miss.
- The **proposed unified benchmark** is specified only as a 7-tuple; there is no released protocol, workload, or reference implementation, so the community cannot immediately act on it.
- **TRC is proposed but never empirically demonstrated** on a real video MoE model — no baseline TRC values, no sensitivity analysis, no evidence that low TRC actually correlates with downstream temporal-reasoning failures. The diagnostic is plausible but unvalidated.
- **Edge/hardware discussion is largely conceptual**: the SRAM/DRAM/energy hierarchy is described but the paper does not engage deeply with specific SoCs, measured bandwidths, or thermal traces; the "memory movement dominates energy" claim rests on one citation (Chung et al., 2026).
- The **hallucination-under-compression** open problem is raised but barely developed — a hook rather than an analysis.
- Some **redundancy across sections**: Interface B and TRC are each discussed three times (§3.2, §3.5, §7, §9, §14), which inflates length without always adding new content.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does **compression-induced routing shift actually exceed quantization-induced shift** on a controlled MoE benchmark? The survey hypothesizes yes but provides no measurement; a direct `Δ_C` vs `Δ_Q` ablation on Mixtral/DeepSeek-MoE-class models with ToMe/ATM upstream would resolve Interface B.
- What is the empirical **TRC distribution** of current video MoE models (e.g., on NextQA, EgoSchema, LongVideoBench), and does low TRC correlate with degraded temporal-reasoning accuracy? The diagnostic is defined but never measured.
- Can a **routing-stability KL term** be added to existing compression losses (analogous to Fu et al.'s routing-consistent PTQ) without harming the accuracy-FLOPs frontier, and does it also improve TRC?
- How does the amplification inequality's actual joint rate `Δ_CQ` compare to the inclusion-exclusion lower bound? This requires measuring the correlation between compression- and quantization-induced assignment changes, which the paper identifies as missing.
- Does **calibrating expert-importance quantization on compressed inputs** (rather than full-resolution inputs) close the miscalibration gap identified in §3.3.1? This is a concrete, testable fix for the compounding failure on rare, high-information experts.
- What does a **deployment-tuple benchmark** look like in practice — what prompt mixes, vision-token budgets, precision formats, and edge targets would actually surface Interface B/C failures that VQAv2/MMBench miss?
- Does aggressive compression/eviction measurably increase **hallucination rate** on infrequent-but-informationally-dense tokens, as §14 speculates? This is a safety-relevant open question the survey raises but does not pursue.
