QuantiBias: Benchmarking Quantization-Induced Bias in LLMs · arXiv:2607.21063 · 2026-07-23

Quantized LLMs pass every standard safety audit — yet volunteer a stereotype in roughly one in four open-ended answers.

Emilio Ferrara  —  Thomas Lord Dept. of Computer Science, University of Southern California (USC)

Key metric · independent judge, MBTP probe

1in 4

Why this matters

Standard quantization safety audits systematically miss open-ended bias; this supplies a probe, scoring harness, and baseline for a stricter post-training evaluation pipeline.

Deployable · production LLMs ship quantized

Critical edge

The “bias rises with compression” slope rests on the in-family judge; under the independent judge it is explicitly flat (0.238 / 0.267 / 0.266) — the author concedes the slope is “not established.”

Level robust · dose-response provisional
Methodology

Short-form controls vs. an open-ended probe, on a measured-precision ladder

Eight instruments split into controls and a generative probe. Every weight tensor’s real size is read from the artifact — so compression is plotted against measured bits-per-weight, not the nominal labels that overstate it 11–40%.

  1. MBTP probe. 40 canonical English stereotype items rendered into 7 more languages by a frontier model with back-translation QC; each paired with a group-swapped counterpart so only the language of presentation varies. 320 items total.
  2. Judges, idempotent. Primary scorer: in-family Qwen3-8B lean judge (~94% vs reference). Validated against two never-quantized out-of-family judges (Claude Sonnet-5, Gemini-2.5-pro). Every number traces to a job id + content hash; Wilson 95% intervals throughout.
  3. Mechanism (§7 + App. C). Quantization error Δ_b with ‖Δ_b‖² ~ 2⁻²ᵇ shifts each behavior’s margin by ε = ⟨∇_W m, Δ_b⟩; endorsement rate becomes a tail probability R(b) = ∫ ρ(m) Φ(−m/σ(b)) dm — the same object that governs flip rates under Gaussian smoothing.
  4. Why controls diverge. Alignment-reinforced behaviors (refusal) sit behind wide logit margins and survive; the never-directly-trained “don’t volunteer a stereotype” sits behind a narrow margin and degrades first. Standard quantizer calibration data (Prop. 2) provably under-protects those bias-critical weights.

Assessment

What holds — and what doesn’t yet

+ Strengths

  • Sharp, falsifiable central claim (selective gap) that the controls genuinely isolate — standard audits read “unchanged” exactly where open-ended bias is high.
  • Measurement hygiene: measured effective bpw instead of nominal labels, decoding frozen, content hashing, Wilson intervals, two never-quantized independent judges.
  • Honest Limitations naming exactly what a follow-up would need (more paired cells at 1-bit, more families, an activation-precision axis) to upgrade the provisional slope.
  • Mechanistic supplement (App. C) ties the empirics to a testable margin-crossing model rather than a black box.
  • Usable artifact: released probe, scoring harness, and severity rubric, positioned for downstream re-evaluation of released builds.

− Limitations

  • The compression slope is judge-dependent and provisional; under the independent judge it is flat (0.238/0.267/0.266). Only the level — the gap itself — is robust.
  • Full-sample bias rests on one in-family judge whose absolute level is known to understate; absolute rates need re-scoring with the independent ensemble across the full ladder.
  • One primary backbone for the full ladder; second-backbone and CEB replications are real but underpowered.
  • Power analysis (Cohen’s h ≈ 0.15) needs several hundred to >1000 paired prompts per cell to resolve the per-family slope; current pools are being expanded.
  • Confound: the multilingual MBTP is a diagonal design entangling language, national target, and content; activation precision is held constant; the screen sees only the fast (reasoning-off) path.
Open questions

Where the selective gap has yet to be tested

01Would replacing the quantizer’s capability-only calibration data with bias-signal-aware calibration (or critical-weight protection à la Al Hakim 2026) close the gap, as Proposition 2 predicts?
02Does the compression slope resolve under the independent judges at the one-bit rung on a full-ladder parallel run — or does open-ended bias truly flatten once measured correctly?
03Does the frequency-vs-severity split (Prop. 4) hold empirically — does mean severity s̄(b) also rise with compression, growing total harm even when frequency looks flat?
04Is the reasoning-remedy’s family dependence (works on Qwen, not Gemma) a function of reasoning quality/length, of whether deliberation revisits the stereotype decision, or of backbone architecture?
05Can the margin-crossing model’s predicted R(b)−R₀ ~ 2⁻²ᵇ onset law be quantitatively confirmed with a denser ladder, and does a per-behavior precision floor (Prop. 3) translate into a practical bit-width certification scheme?
06Model names (“Qwen3.6-27B”, “Gemma-4-31B”) use future version numbers for a 2026-07-23 paper — are these real public checkpoints or synthetic labels, and how does that bear on reproducibility of the artifact identifiers?
