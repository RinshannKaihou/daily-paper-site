01

Methodology

A learning-theoretic / statistical methodology paper with no ML training; every number is an exact deterministic inversion of binomial or hypergeometric tail probabilities.

r(g) = p₀(g) · η(g)
excluded-pool mass (label-free, observable) × excluded-pool relevance rate (needs labels from {g = 0})
  • Two sampling models. An i.i.d. population model (X,F,P) with latent relevance φ⋆, and a finite-corpus model over fixed N items where probability statements refer only to the random audit sample.
  • Exact inversion. Clopper–Pearson binomial Thm 7 + hypergeometric Thm 8 inversion — guaranteed finite-sample coverage, deliberately conservative, not shortest intervals.
  • Certificate pipeline. Prop 3 impossibility → Thm 7 zero-count cert → Thm 13 Ω(N₀/m) lower bound → Cor 15 matched upper → Thm 10 recall → Thm 24/29 diagnostics & stress test.
  • Lower-bound proof. Couples a zero-miss labelling with a planted m-subset under a shared random seed; handles fully adaptive, randomised procedures via a filtration (Fₜ) and stopping time τ.
  • Validity discipline. Assumption 1 (design–certification separation) forbids reusing certification labels to tune the generator; Assumption 2 (noiseless audit labels) underpins all certificates.

+ Strengths

why the contribution lands

  • Prop 3 closes the door on any included-pool-only shortcut — short, general, foundational.
  • Thm 13 is the strongest result: excluded-pool auditing is information-theoretically necessary at the optimal rate, and the proof handles adaptive / randomised procedures, not just fixed designs.
  • Exact (non-asymptotic) certificates are the right tool where normal approximations are known to cover poorly (Webber [48]).
  • Design–certification separation (Asm 1) is articulated and tied to a concrete audit-and-stop workflow — rare and valuable for practitioners.
  • Honest scoping — zero-miss regime, noiseless labels, declared perturbation — rather than overgeneralising.

! Limitations

where it falls short

  • No real-data experiments — no e-discovery, systematic-review (CLEF/TAR), or RAG-retrieval benchmark; not compared to existing TAR stopping rules on actual data.
  • Lower bound is zero-miss-only. The few-but-nonzero-miss regime has upper-bound certificates but no matching lower bound.
  • Noiseless-label assumption underpins all certificates; the Section 9 extension covers only i.i.d. missed-mass with a known sensitivity floor.
  • Modest gain for large excluded pools — 1.43× at p₀ = 0.70; large gains need high-burden generators with small excluded pools.
  • Pre-specification is operationally fragile — no automated guardrail detects a prefix family being tuned after seeing labels.
  • Stress-test scope is mechanism-specific — certifies only the declared perturbation generator A, not a general semantic-robustness guarantee.
03

Open questions for further work

01
How do excluded-pool certificates behave on a real high-recall pipeline (CLEF/TAR e-discovery, or a RAG benchmark with graded relevance) — and does the exact hypergeometric bound materially beat current normal-approximation TAR intervals?
02
Can the Ω(N₀/m) lower bound extend beyond the zero-miss regime to the operationally central case of a small but non-zero number of misses?
03
What minimal assumptions (known specificity floor, adjudicated dual-review labels) recover finite-sample recall and stress-test certificates under label noise?
04
Can the declared-perturbation stress test extend to continuous perturbation neighbourhoods via a covering argument — yielding a genuine semantic-robustness guarantee for RAG retrievers?
