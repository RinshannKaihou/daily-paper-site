Emergent Misalignment Recruits a Pre-existing Persona Subspace

Key finding

A pre-existing, low-rank persona subspace — shared across unrelated domains — is recruited by fine-tuning to produce emergent misalignment.

Key metric · Necessity result causal
27.7%→0.0%

Broad misalignment after projecting the pre-registered persona subspace out of the residual stream during fine-tuning. Matched-rank random control: 27.5%.

Why this matters so what

Reframes emergent misalignment from phenomenon to mechanism — and hands alignment engineers actionable intervention hooks.

Critical edge caveat

The only working prevention also abolishes the narrow trained behavior (adherence 0.902 → 0.000) — not behavioral surgery.

Methodology § extraction design

Contrastive teacher forcing isolates "who is speaking" from "what is said."

  • Contrastive teacher forcing. One response is read twice behind a "dangerously reckless" vs. "carefully cautious" system prompt, with response tokens held byte-identical (token-id hash verified). The residual-stream difference isolates the persona direction, not content.
  • Subspace build. Average per-token differences, stack per-pair, take top-4 left singular vectors → rank-4 subspace per domain; style/topic cores built at matched diversity (12 descriptor-variant pairs each).
  • Pre-registration. The subspace was extracted from the frozen model, fixed, and written to disk before any organism existed — necessity and sufficiency tested on the same object.
  • Readout. Differentiable teacher-forced margin M = M⁺ − M⁻ over an OOD prompt battery; judge = AWQ Qwen2.5-72B (alignment<30 AND coherence≥50 = broadly misaligned).
  • Statistics. Unit of inference = prompt cluster; permutation/sign-flip at the 10⁻⁴ floor; effect sizes as Cliff's δ; every arm carries its own matched random control.

Strengths § what holds

  • Pre-registered, single object extracted before any organism exists; necessity & sufficiency on the same object — a clean cause, not a post-hoc descriptor.
  • Matched random controls on every arm (rank for removal, norm for injection), making null results interpretable.
  • Persona ≠ style containment test (82% / 90% outside) — a real methodological advance over entangled activation work.
  • Read/write dissociation — activation projection works, weight-gradient projection is inert — a sharp mechanism constraint.
  • Honest failure reporting — three failed edits, a violated pre-set bar, an unconfirmed inoculation screen.
  • Reconstitution certificate (~97% carrier re-formed in the cleared span) — a principled suppression-vs-removal test others could adopt.

Limitations § what doesn't

  • Single model, scale, method. All on Qwen2.5-14B + LoRA. Own fine-tunes don't separate insecure vs. educational in judged behavior — so first-step intent, write-core specificity, and domain-count superadditivity rest on the margin, not generations.
  • Provenance open. Extracted from a released instruction-tuned checkpoint, not a pre-alignment base; overlaps the assistant direction at cosine 0.565. Base-checkpoint re-extraction is the highest-value unmade measurement.
  • Capability confound. Prevention also abolishes narrow behavior (0.902 → 0.000); cannot separate removing the structure from removing the capacity to represent a character.
  • Basis-scoped foreclosure. All three failed edits use the same operator; the shared write core was never edited post-training; the functional pullback was never built.
  • Two objects joined by interpretation. No overlap statistic between the read carrier and the write core was measured.
  • External reproducibility risk — Schreiber & Goldstein (2026): the phenomenon "largely fails to reproduce across open-weight models."

Open questions for further exploration § the next measurements

01Does the persona subspace survive re-extraction from a base (pre-alignment) checkpoint, or across intermediate alignment checkpoints? This single measurement decides pretraining-vs-alignment provenance.

02Does necessity/sufficiency replicate at 72B under full fine-tuning, where prior work says the phenomenon is method-conditional?

03Can the projection prevent broad misalignment without collapsing the narrow task — e.g. orthogonalize against a task-direction, or project only on OOD prompts?

04What is the overlap statistic between the read carrier (layers 18/24/30) and the weight-space write core? The one place two measured objects are joined by interpretation.

05Would editing through the functional pullback (gradient of the readout) remove the disposition where the naive writer-column lift fails?

06A usage-matched random control for the projection (matching residual-stream share, not just rank) was never run — would it change the 27.5% null?

07Does the superadditive domain-count effect hold in judged behavior, not just the margin (4-domain judged p = 0.15)?

08What drives the intent-unspecific M⁻ drift (+62–84 nats under every fine-tune) — same structure, or a separate baseline?
