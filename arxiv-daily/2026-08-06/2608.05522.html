01 Methodology

One observable, log E(h), fit for the opposite slope — no Jacobian, no surrogate.

An ensemble of N=5000 short post-transient scalar trajectories (70/30 split) feeds a K=3 kNN predictor. Per-horizon forecast error is aggregated as the geometric mean E(h); in a contracting regime log E(h) ≈ C + λ₁h with λ₁ < 0, so the fitted slope is the dominant negative exponent. Two adaptations make negative-LLE estimation work at all:

  • Period-aware horizons. A recurrence statistic Q_p(T) = median|uᵢ(n+p) − uᵢ(n)| detects the orbit period p; candidate profiles are sampled at h_m = m·s with s ∈ {1, p}, preferring s = p so the same orbit phase is compared — collapsing an alternating / multi-branch error profile into a single contraction envelope.
  • Stable-transient consensus. Slopes are fit across several transient lengths Tⱼ; ≥3 consistent transients → "reliable" median, a consistent pair → "acceptable", an isolated slope → rejected. This rule exists specifically to kill high-R² early-transient fits with the wrong asymptotic slope.
  • Equation-free by construction. Neither governing equations nor an analytical Jacobian is needed — only complete short realizations. The reliability class (reliable / acceptable / unreliable) is part of the estimator, not a post-hoc label.
  • Benchmarks. Logistic map x_{n+1}=r·x_n(1−x_n), r∈[3.5,4.0] (analytical reference); 2D no-fixed-point map of Huynh/Pham et al. 2019 (reference from QR accumulation of the Jacobian).

02 Results & detection limits

Strongest where it is hardest to fool — weakest where it is hardest to measure.

  • 2D no-fixed-point map (the headline). Three independently processed scalar observables agree to MAE 0.00909 (xₙ), 0.01145 (yₙ), 0.00879 (xₙ²+yₙ²), R² 0.9858 / 0.9830 / 0.9852, coverage 83.2–90.4%. Cross-observable agreement is a non-trivial internal check, since no coordinates are combined during period detection or estimation.
  • Clean-room confirmation (logistic, r=2.7). Theoretical λ₁ = log|2−r| = −0.35667494; estimator returned λ̂₁ = −0.35667476 — an absolute deviation of 1.8×10⁻⁷, R² indistinguishable from unity.
  • Logistic-map negative branch — the weak spot. Recovers 92 / 112 negative-exponent values (82.14% coverage), MAE 0.0253, RMSE 0.0586, R²=0.886. RMSE is dominated by a few deep/narrow stable windows and the strongly contracting interval start — not typical error.
  • Positive-branch context (not the contribution). The same ensemble formulation on the chaotic logistic regime gives MAE 0.00709, R²=0.9964, within rounding of the authors' prior single-long-trajectory R²=0.998 — i.e. the short-trajectory protocol preserves prior positive-LLE performance.
  • Identified failure mode. Residual bias is positive (estimates slightly less negative than references); strongly stable / superstable neighborhoods are underestimable or missing because the ensemble collapses to a numerical floor before a long asymptotic profile can be measured.

03 Strengths ↔ Limitations

▲ Strengths

  • Conceptual unification. One observable (log E(h)) and one short-trajectory pipeline serve both chaotic and stable regimes, differing only in horizon step and profile selection.
  • Consensus rule attacks a real failure mode — high-R² early-transient fits with the wrong asymptotic slope — and embeds the accept/reject decision into the estimator itself, more disciplined than a single point estimate.
  • The 2D three-observable test is a clever internal-consistency check that never combines coordinates, yet the three pipelines agree tightly.
  • Unusual intellectual honesty. Explicit that the output is a "dominant observable contraction rate," not a full spectrum and not interchangeable with modal damping; repeatedly warns against equating it with the asymptotic LLE of continuous-time limit cycles (which is zero by phase neutrality).

▼ Limitations

  • No experimental validation — only deterministic discrete-time maps with known reference exponents. The structural-vibration / electromechanical-recovery motivation is stated but never demonstrated.
  • No head-to-head baselines. Comparison with Yang–Wu nonlinear mapping, Sun–Wu RBF surrogate, Koopman/EDMD decay eigenvalues, and empirical Floquet multipliers is explicitly deferred to future work.
  • No quantitative noise study. Negative exponents drive forecast errors to the observational floor earlier than the positive case, so the recoverable magnitude under noise is unknown.
  • Logistic coverage only 82%, R²=0.886, degrading exactly where the exponent is most negative. No code/data release; consensus logic is prose, not pseudocode.

04 Questions for further exploration

  1. How does the method perform under additive measurement noise of controlled amplitude, and at what floor does the recoverable negative-exponent magnitude saturate? (Flagged by the authors as the most pressing open question.)
  2. What is the marginal contribution of period-aware horizons (s=p) versus the consensus rule, when ablated independently on the logistic-map narrow periodic windows and the 2D map?
  3. How does it compare head-to-head (MAE, coverage, reliability-class calibration) with Yang–Wu, Sun–Wu, and Koopman/EDMD decay-eigenvalue methods on identical short-trajectory ensembles?
  4. Does it extend to continuous-time experimental ring-down (bridges, electromechanical recovery), where the autonomous limit-cycle LLE is zero and the estimator must target a transverse / Floquet decay rate?
  5. What is the minimum ensemble size N and perturbation amplitude needed to keep the contraction interval measurable before the numerical/noise floor truncates it?
  6. Could the consensus rule be replaced by an information-theoretic model-selection criterion (one- vs. two-line fits to the log-error profile) to reduce the residual positive bias without lowering coverage?
