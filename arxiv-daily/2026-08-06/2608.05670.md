> **一句话总结：** 作者构建 RENDEQ——通过重绘同一份数据生成的"渲染等价集"(语义等价、答案精确已知、风格因子可独立切换)，在三个开源 VLM 上精确度量"一致性–正确性"耦合：重绘优于重采样，跨渲染一致性(REA)在 3 个模型中的 2 个上击败 token 对数概率基线，且基于自身共识的微调反而使准确率在每个(共 5 个)复现 run 中都下降。

> **TL;DR:** On the RENDEQ benchmark (synthetic render-equivalence figure sets with exact programmatic ground truth, K=8 styles, pooled over 3 independent instantiations), re-rendering beats resampling at matched call count on Qwen2.5-VL-7B/3B and InternVL2-8B: REA beats fixed-image self-consistency (B3) by Δ=+0.102 AUROC (CI [+0.083, +0.121], p<10⁻⁴), and RM aggregation beats self-consistency by +4.8 to +6.3 accuracy points. REA beats the token-logprob baseline B1 on 2/3 models (+0.094 and +0.033 AUROC; tied on the 3B). Strikingly, fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on its own K=8 majority consensus *inverts*—accuracy drops in all 5 replication runs—contradicting a published positive result on natural images.

## 核心贡献

- **RENDEQ 测量仪器**：一种"渲染等价集"(render-equivalence set) 生成器与协议。同一份数据 D 用 K 个不影响编码信息的风格(绘图库、配色、主题、网格线、长宽比、标记、字体、刻度、图例、分辨率)重绘，所有渲染图共享一个程序可计算出的精确答案 a*。等价性是构造保证的、答案钥匙精确、风格因子可单独切换，从而把"正确性"变成可精确观测、把"集中度"变成可无混杂估计的量。
- **耦合的精确测量与归因**：在三个开源 VLM 上、跨三次独立生成实例化(种子 0/1/2，每个 N=350，7 个图族，K=8)，度量一致性(REA)与正确性(RM)之间的耦合，并对每个风格因子的贡献做因果归因(因风格因子可独立切换)。
- **共识自训练的边界条件**：经验性地反转了一个已发表的正向结果——在自身 K=8 多数共识上做 LoRA 微调，5 个复现 run 中准确率全部下降，且给出由定理 5 解释的机制(奖励一致性恰好摧毁了使一致性能够证明正确性的"错误扩散度")。
- **配套理论分析**：给出三条结果——REA 在有限 K 下对集中度有向上的选择偏差(命题 4ii)，并给出无偏的成对替代 REApair；一致性能证明正确性仅当错误足够扩散(命题 5，阈值 1/L)；渲染色散可分解到风格因子上(命题 7 的上界)。

## Key Contributions

- **RENDEQ, a measurement instrument**: A generator and protocol producing render-equivalence sets. Same data D is rendered under K styles that do not change the encoded information, so all renderings share an exact, programmatically known answer a*. Equivalence holds by construction, the answer key is exact, and style factors are individually togglable—making correctness exactly observable and concentration estimable without confounding.
- **Coupling measurement with per-factor attribution**: Estimates the agreement–accuracy coupling on three open-weight VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, InternVL2-8B), checked across three independently generated RENDEQ instantiations (seeds 0/1/2, N=350 each, 7 figure families, K=8 renderings), with causal attribution of dispersion to each style factor.
- **A boundary condition on consensus self-training**: An empirical inversion of a published positive result—LoRA fine-tuning on the model's own K=8 cross-render majority drops accuracy in every one of five replication runs, with a mechanism (Theorem 5) explaining why rewarding agreement destroys exactly the error diffuseness that makes agreement certifying.
- **Accompanying analysis (Sec. 4)**: Three formal results—REA is upward-biased for concentration at finite K (Prop. 4ii) with an unbiased pairwise alternative REApair (Prop. 4iii); agreement certifies correctness only above a threshold 1/L set by how diffuse the model's errors are (Prop. 5); and render dispersion decomposes over style factors via an upper bound D ≤ Σⱼ Dⱼ (Prop. 7).

## Methodology

The core construct is a **render-equivalence set**: given data D underlying a figure and a quantitative question q with exact answer a*(D,q), a renderer R maps (D, style θ) to an image x = R(D; θ). Drawing K styles that do not change the encoded data yields K images that all share a*(D,q) by construction. Any answer change across the set is therefore attributable to the model, not to a meaning change—the property that natural-image perturbations only satisfy by assumption.

Two quantities are defined on this set:
- **REA (render-equivalence agreement)**: the modal answer's share among K parsed answers. Label-free, upward-biased at finite K. The pairwise variant REApair is unbiased for the collision probability A = Σₐ P(a)².
- **RM (render marginalization)**: mode (categorical) or median (continuous) aggregation over K renderings. Label-free, costs K forward passes.

Because the answer program also exposes intermediate reads, each instance is partitioned into perception failures (at least one read wrong) and reasoning failures (all reads correct but answer wrong).

RENDEQ axes (Table 1): plot family (bar, grouped bar, stacked bar, line, scatter, pie, log-axis), question type (value read, comparison, extremum, trend sign, arithmetic), style Θ (10 individually togglable groups), and difficulty controls. Every headline number is pooled over three independently generated instantiations (3150 instances total; 29.5% numeric). Decoding is greedy (temperature 0) for main runs; SC uses temperature 0.7. Baselines: B1 mean token log-probability, B2 verbalized confidence, B3 response self-consistency over K=8 samples at a fixed rendering, B4 Khan & Fu's consistency signal, B5 prompt ensemble.

The analysis (Sec. 4) derives four pre-registered-style predictions (Table 2): aggregation helps wherever dispersion exists; the perturbation with the largest single-factor disagreement Dⱼ wins at matched budget; agreement need not beat evidence-carrying signals (Corollary 6); and an objective that raises π while lowering diffuseness L destroys its own certificate (Prop. 5).

## Results & Highlights

- **Re-rendering beats resampling on reliability (all 3 models)**: Pooled over three instantiations, REA beats the fixed-image self-consistency control B3 by Δ = +0.102 AUROC (95% CI [+0.083, +0.121], p < 10⁻⁴); per-model lifts range +0.061 to +0.157. Figure 2a reports +0.06 to +0.16 AUROC for REA−B3 across the three models.
- **Re-rendering beats resampling on accuracy (all 3 models, Table 4)**: RM improves on single-render decoding by 5.5–6.1 accuracy points and on self-consistency (SC) by 4.8–6.3 points on every model. Per-model RM−SC: Qwen2.5-VL-7B +0.057 (RM 0.701 vs SC 0.644), Qwen2.5-VL-3B +0.063 (RM 0.626 vs SC 0.563), InternVL2-8B +0.048 (RM 0.624 vs SC 0.576); every interval excludes zero.
- **REA vs token-logprob evidence (Table 3)**: REA beats B1 (mean token log-probability) on AUROC against correctness on 2 of 3 models and ties on the third. Qwen2.5-VL-7B: REA 0.864 vs B1 0.771 (Δ +0.094, CI [+0.066, +0.121]); Qwen2.5-VL-3B: REA 0.858 vs B1 0.867 (Δ −0.009, CI [−0.030, +0.011], statistical tie); InternVL2-8B: REA 0.907 vs B1 0.875 (Δ +0.033, CI [+0.013, +0.053]).
- **Dispersion concentrates in one style factor (Sec. 6.3)**: The plotting library (matplotlib vs plotly) dominates with a lift of 0.102 (CI [+0.084, +0.120])—more than double the next factor (bar_labels 0.043, CI [+0.025, +0.060]) and roughly an order of magnitude above the noise floor. Every other factor's CI includes zero.
- **Consensus self-training inverts (Sec. 6.4, 5/5 runs)**: LoRA (r=8, 3 epochs) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with the model's own K=8 majority as the label drops accuracy under RM in every run—by 3.6–21.2 points on training families and 10.4–27.2 on held-out families. REA's AUROC rose on held-out families (0.009–0.100) while falling on training families (0.014–0.124): the model grew more confidently self-consistent on families it now answers worse.
- **Per-instance diagnostics**: Render-flip rate (RFR) is 0.59–0.73 across models and strongly negatively correlated with per-family accuracy (r = −0.69). Roughly four-fifths of instances have at least one bad intermediate read; pure reasoning failures are rare. Estimated diffuseness median L̂ is 1.33–1.50, placing the certifying threshold 1/L̂ typically well above the midpoint.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The conceptual distinction between concentration and correctness is not new—the authors explicitly disclaim it and cite the self-consistency literature (Wang et al., Estornell & Liu). The genuine contribution is the *measurement instrument*: render-equivalence sets make one side exactly observable and the other estimable without confounding, which natural-image perturbation studies cannot do. This is a legitimate and well-scoped methodological advance rather than a new method.
- **Rigor**: Strong on replication (three independent instantiations, bootstrap CIs, McNemar's test, a mechanism-control B3 isolating render-induced vs sampling-induced dispersion). However, an earlier version of the headline REA-vs-B1 comparison reported the *opposite direction* on every model because a silent library-export failure collapsed all three "independent instantiations" to matplotlib-only images—a serious reproducibility scare that the authors handled honestly but which underscores that the pipeline itself is a load-bearing, fragile component. The fact that the library factor is also the dominant dispersion source makes this especially pointed.
- **Reproducibility**: Excellent on paper—pinned seeds, public generator, MIT-licensed code, CC-BY-4.0 data. But the dominant style factor (matplotlib vs plotly) is "less purely cosmetic than a palette change," and the authors flag that the library-held-fixed ablation their own Sec. 6.3 finding motivates is *missing* because too few instances have all eight renderings in one library to power it. That ablation could narrow the paper's central scope and is conspicuously absent.
- **Significance**: The negative consensus-self-training result is the most consequential finding and is well-motivated by Theorem 5. But it rests on one model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), one LoRA config (r=8, 3 epochs), and one generator; the direction held across 5 runs but the magnitude did not (3.6–27.2 point range), so it identifies a regime of failure rather than a generalizable law. The authors are careful not to claim a replication of Kaya et al. (different augmentation, aggregation level, models).
- **Red flags / missing pieces**: (1) The key delta the paper argues for—exact render-equivalence vs ordinary pixel augmentation—is reasoned rather than measured (no approximate-perturbation arm). (2) The numeric tolerance τ=5% is load-bearing and never varied; on one model it changes the direction of the REA-vs-B1 comparison. (3) ~18 paired comparisons are reported without multiple-testing correction. (4) All three models date to 2024/early 2025 and two share the Qwen2.5-VL family—no third-family current-generation model. (5) External validity is untested: only a single in-style synthetic split was run; the out-of-style split and a real-data anchor built from a public benchmark were designed but not executed, so the results characterize behavior on RENDEQ rather than on chart QA generally. (6) The perception-vs-reasoning split rests on an intermediate-read query whose own reliability is never validated.

## Strengths

- A genuinely well-motivated measurement instrument: render-equivalence sidesteps the two obstacles (assumed meaning preservation, no exact answer key) that have prevented directly measuring the agreement–accuracy coupling in prior natural-image work.
- Pre-registered predictions tested against outcomes, with honest accounting (Table 2 marks which predictions held and at what replication level).
- Per-factor causal attribution (enabled by individually togglable style factors) is more informative than the usual "apply many augmentations and average" style of robustness study.
- Striking, mechanism-explained negative result on consensus self-training that contradicts a published positive finding—a valuable boundary condition for the test-time-scaling literature.
- Full code and data release with pinned versions and seeds.

## Limitations

- Single in-style synthetic split; the out-of-style split and real-data anchor were designed but not run, so generalization to real charts is untested.
- The dominant dispersion factor (plotting library) is the least "cosmetic" of the style axes, and the library-held-fixed ablation that would isolate this is missing.
- No approximate-perturbation arm, so the central claim (exact equivalence beats ordinary pixel augmentation) is reasoned rather than measured.
- Standard label-free baselines omitted: no semantic entropy, no P(True), no direct run of Zhang et al. [1], and Jiang & Luo (the closest label-free test-time chart method) is discussed but not benchmarked against RM.
- Model coverage is narrow (3 models, 2024/early 2025, two from the same family); the negative fine-tuning result is one model, one LoRA config, with direction stable but magnitude unstable across runs.
- τ never varied; ~18 paired comparisons without multiple-testing correction; intermediate-read reliability unvalidated.

## Relevance

Highly relevant to anyone studying vision-language model reliability, self-consistency / test-time aggregation, and calibration. The render-equivalence methodology is a reusable evaluation primitive for chart/figure reasoning VLMs, and the negative consensus-self-training result is a direct caution for test-time-scaling work that treats agreement as a safe training target.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the consensus-self-training inversion persist with token-level aggregation (which Kaya et al. report as stronger) rather than answer-level RM, or under a larger / different model family?
- How much of the library effect survives a library-held-fixed ablation powered with enough single-library instances, and is the remaining dispersion still certifying on its own?
- Does the diffuseness threshold 1/L measured on RENDEQ transfer to real chart benchmarks (the real-data anchor the authors designed but did not run)?
- How does REA compare to semantic entropy and P(True)—the two standard label-free signals the authors omitted—on the same render-equivalence sets?
- Can an evidence-carrying signal (B1) and REA be combined at deployment to recover the per-model AUROC gains the cross-validated logistic combination shows, and does that combination still hold under the held-out threshold selection the authors did not perform?
