> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 Synthetic Query Probing (SQP) 方法，通过 LLM 从文档生成不同相关度级别的合成查询（PARAPHRASE / RELEVANT / IRRELEVANT）来构建 query–chunk 对，进而在 SciFact 和企业语料上学习 Amazon Titan（256/512/1024 维）与 OpenAI Ada-002 之间的余弦相似度转换函数，发现跨维映射近无损（R² ≥ 0.97）、跨模型映射呈 S 型非线性且 isotonic regression 在 12 个方向对上均表现最优。

> **TL;DR:** The authors introduce Synthetic Query Probing (SQP), a reference-free method that uses an LLM (Claude Sonnet 4.6) to generate PARAPHRASE/RELEVANT/IRRELEVANT queries from sampled chunks, producing 3,000 labeled query–chunk pairs per corpus to compare cosine-similarity distributions across Titan 256/512/1024-d and OpenAI Ada-002 on SciFact and a 114k-chunk Pega enterprise corpus; learned isotonic-regression maps achieve R² up to 0.945 on the hardest Titan→Ada pair (SciFact, vs 0.841 for linear OLS) and ≥0.97 on all intra-Titan dimensional pairs, while a fixed precision-≥0.95 threshold that sits at 0.063 for Titan-1024 on SciFact must shift to 0.718 for Ada — demonstrating that similarity thresholds and scores are not portable across embedding models without explicit calibration.

## 核心贡献

- **问题形式化**：首次将"嵌入模型间相似度分数不可比"这一现象系统化为研究问题，指出它同时影响对嵌入空间的基础理解，以及阈值校准、模型迁移等工程实践。
- **Synthetic Query Probing (SQP) 方法**：提出可扩展、免人工标注的方法论——从语料中随机采样 chunk，用 LLM 为每个 chunk 生成 PARAPHRASE / RELEVANT / IRRELEVANT 三类查询，构成带相关性标签的 query–chunk 对，从而能在无 ground-truth 评测集的情况下对任意模型对进行配对回归分析。
- **跨模型相似度分布的实证刻画**：在两个语料、四个嵌入配置上揭示——类别排序（PARAPHRASE > RELEVANT > IRRELEVANT）在所有配置上都保持，但绝对分数存在系统性扭曲：Titan 低维分数整体偏高、Ada 分数被压缩到窄高区间（标准差比 Titan 小 2–6 倍）。
- **分数/阈值校准与迁移**：给出实用的跨模型分数转换方法（线性 OLS、isotonic regression、quantile/CDF mapping），其中 isotonic regression 在全部 12 个方向对、两个语料上 R² 最高、MAE 最低，特别适合拟合 Titan↔Ada 的 S 型非线性关系。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the non-comparability of cosine similarity scores across embedding models as an under-studied problem that blocks threshold reuse and complicates model migration in RAG.
- Introduces Synthetic Query Probing (SQP): an annotation-free, scalable protocol that generates PARAPHRASE / RELEVANT / IRRELEVANT queries from sampled chunks via an LLM, yielding controlled query–chunk pairs usable to fit cross-model similarity transfer functions without a human-labeled benchmark.
- Empirically characterizes similarity distributions across Titan 256/512/1024-d and OpenAI Ada-002 on SciFact and a 114,648-chunk enterprise corpus, showing preserved class ordering but systematic scale distortion (e.g., Ada compresses all classes into a 0.626–0.930 band on SciFact vs Titan's near-zero IRRELEVANT means).
- Provides and benchmarks practical calibration tools — linear OLS, isotonic regression, and quantile mapping — over all 12 directional model pairs, with isotonic regression winning every pair and a clear two-regime finding: near-lossless intra-family (Titan↔Titan, R² ≥ 0.97) vs noisy, S-shaped, corpus-dependent cross-family (Titan↔Ada) mappings.

## Methodology

The paper's framework, **Synthetic Query Probing (SQP)**, is deliberately simple and reference-free:

1. **Corpus and chunking.** Two corpora are used: SciFact (5,183 biomedical/life-science abstracts, homogeneous) and a Pegasystems enterprise corpus (114,648 chunks from 33,492 documents — product guides, API refs, KB articles, marketing, community content — heterogeneous). Each is chunked with a chosen strategy.
2. **Synthetic query generation.** A uniform random subset of 100 chunks per corpus is sampled. For each chunk, Claude Sonnet 4.6 generates 10 queries in each of three relevance classes: PARAPHRASE (fully answerable from the chunk), RELEVANT (topically related, needs external context), and IRRELEVANT (unrelated domain). This yields 3,000 labeled query–chunk pairs per corpus.
3. **Scoring.** Four embedding configurations are compared: Amazon Titan Text Embeddings V2 at 256, 512, and 1024 dimensions, plus OpenAI text-embedding-ada-002 (1,536-d). Cosine similarity is computed for every (query, chunk) pair under every configuration, so each row carries all four scores.
4. **Threshold analysis.** For each model independently, a 500-point sweep over cosine thresholds defines a binary classifier (PARAPHRASE∪RELEVANT = positive, IRRELEVANT = negative), reporting precision/recall/F1 at precision operating points 0.93 / 0.95 / 0.97.
5. **Cross-configuration conversion.** All 12 directional model pairs are fit with three transfer functions: linear OLS (slope + intercept), isotonic regression (monotone step function / knot set), and quantile (empirical CDF) mapping. Accuracy is reported as MAE and R².

The design choice of mapping between **score distributions** rather than between embedding vectors avoids assumptions about geometric alignment and keeps the method agnostic to dimensionality.

## Results & Highlights

- **Cross-dimension (Titan↔Titan) conversions are near-lossless and corpus-invariant.** On SciFact, R² ≥ 0.967 (MAE ≤ 0.035); on the enterprise corpus, R² ≥ 0.980 (MAE ≤ 0.023). The tightest pair is 512↔1024 with R² = 0.990 (SciFact) / 0.996 (enterprise) and MAE ≈ 0.010–0.019, confirming the two dimensionalities are nearly identical.
- **Cross-model (Titan↔Ada) conversions are noisy, non-linear, and corpus-dependent**, exhibiting an S-shaped relationship. With isotonic regression, Titan→Ada reaches R² = 0.901–0.945 on SciFact (e.g., 1024→Ada: R² = 0.945, MAE = 0.014) but only 0.820–0.830 on the enterprise corpus. Linear OLS is markedly worse (e.g., 1024→Ada SciFact R² = 0.841 vs isotonic 0.945).
- **Isotonic regression is the best method on every one of the 12 directional pairs and both corpora** (bolded winners in Table 3), because it captures the monotonic-but-non-linear Titan↔Ada curve that linear OLS misses.
- **Directional asymmetry in absolute error.** MAE for Ada→Titan conversions is roughly 3–4× higher than the reverse Titan→Ada direction (e.g., enterprise 1024↔Ada: 0.073 vs 0.019; SciFact: 0.060 vs 0.014), because Titan's wider score range amplifies absolute prediction error even when R² is identical in both directions.
- **Thresholds are corpus- and model-specific.** To reach precision ≥ 0.95, Titan-1024 needs threshold 0.063 on SciFact but 0.393 on enterprise (~6× higher), while Ada needs 0.718 on SciFact vs 0.821 on enterprise. Naively reusing a Titan-1024 threshold on Ada, or a SciFact threshold on enterprise, would catastrophically break precision.
- **Per-class distribution confirms class ordering preserved but scale distorted.** Titan's IRRELEVANT mean rises from 0.018 (1024-d) to 0.061 (256-d) on SciFact as dimensionality drops; Ada compresses everything into a 0.626–0.930 band with std 2–6× smaller than Titan, shrinking the IRRELEVANT↔RELEVANT mean gap to 2–4× smaller than Titan's.
- **False-positive counts are stable within a corpus despite very different threshold scales.** At precision 0.95, all configurations produce 96–104 FPs on SciFact and 72–79 on enterprise, indicating comparable noise rejection once each model is calibrated to its own scale.
- **Recall is corpus-dependent.** On SciFact, Ada achieves perfect recall (1.000) at precision 0.95 vs Titan's 0.981–0.996; on enterprise the situation reverses and Titan retains higher recall at equivalent precision, suggesting Titan's wider class separation gives a more favorable precision-recall trade-off in heterogeneous content.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** is genuine but incremental: the paper is the first to explicitly frame and empirically study cross-model *similarity-score* transferability (rather than embedding-space similarity, retrieval overlap, or human-judgment calibration). However, the core tools — isotonic regression and quantile mapping — are textbook, and the closest prior work (Tacheny, ref. 13) already uses linear + isotonic regression to calibrate similarity to human STS judgments; the delta is shifting the calibration target from "human judgment" to "another model's score distribution."
- **Rigor** is sound but bounded by the experimental design. Only **two corpora** and **two model families** (Titan, Ada) are evaluated; generalization to other families (Cohere, Voyage, Mistral, GECKO, open BGE/E5) is untested. The 100-chunk × 30-question sampling yields 3,000 pairs per corpus — adequate for stable distributions but small relative to the 114k-chunk enterprise corpus, and the authors flag this trade-off themselves.
- **A significant methodological gap: train/test leakage.** The conversion functions in Table 3 are "fitted on the full dataset" — the authors acknowledge this in the Discussion and note that "proper train/test protocols for reusable calibration" are future work. As reported, the R²/MAE numbers are in-sample fit quality, not held-out transfer accuracy, so the practical conversion accuracy a practitioner would experience is likely lower than Table 3 suggests.
- **Missing ablations.** There is no ablation on (a) the choice of query-generation LLM (only Claude Sonnet 4.6 is used), (b) the number of synthetic queries per chunk, (c) chunk size, or (d) sensitivity to the IRRELEVANT query distribution (drawn from "unrelated domains" but the construction procedure is underspecified). Given that synthetic-query bias is a known concern, at least one robustness check against a human-labeled relevance set would strengthen the claims.
- **Weak/absent baselines for the conversion task.** The three conversion methods are compared only against each other; there is no comparison to a trivial baseline (e.g., z-score normalization, percentile rank, or a "do nothing / reuse the source threshold" reference) that would quantify how much the learned mappings actually buy over cheaper normalization tricks.
- **Scope of embedding models is narrow for a paper titled "across embedding models."** All four configurations come from essentially two model families, and three are dimensionality variants of the same model. The "cross-model" claim rests largely on a single Titan↔Ada contrast; calling this "systematically related across embedding models" slightly overreaches the evidence.
- **Reproducibility** is moderate: SciFact is public, the methodology is simple, and full experimental protocol (chunk count, query counts, threshold sweep formula) is given. But the enterprise corpus is proprietary, the synthetic queries are not released, and the conversion-function parameters are omitted "to keep the paper readable," so exact numerical replication is not possible from the paper alone.
- **Significance** is real for RAG practitioners: the threshold-portability failure mode (a 0.063 threshold becoming catastrophic when reused at 0.718-scale) is a concrete, deployable insight, and the two-regime finding (intra-family ≈ linear, cross-family ≈ non-linear isotonic) is actionable guidance for model-migration projects.
- **Red flags**: none severe. Claims are appropriately hedged; no overclaiming of downstream retrieval-quality gains (the work explicitly stays orthogonal to ranking quality). The main risk is that readers treat the in-sample R² values as deployable accuracy.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated problem with immediate practical value for anyone doing embedding-model migration or threshold calibration in production RAG.
- Methodology is deliberately simple, annotation-free, and reproducible in principle — a practitioner can run SQP on their own corpus with any LLM and any embedding model.
- The two-regime finding (near-linear intra-family vs S-shaped cross-family) and the directional-asymmetry observation (Ada→Titan has 3–4× higher MAE than Titan→Ada) are non-obvious and useful.
- Quantitative results are reported comprehensively: per-class distribution statistics (Table 1), threshold/recall/F1 operating points (Table 2), and full 12-direction × 3-method conversion accuracy (Table 3), with KDE/box/scatter visualizations.
- Honest about limitations: the authors explicitly call out the two-corpus scope, synthetic-query bias risk, the static-threshold assumption, and the in-sample fit issue.

## Limitations

- Only two corpora and two model families (effectively one cross-family contrast: Titan↔Ada) are evaluated; generalization to the broader embedding-model zoo is unproven.
- Conversion functions are fitted and evaluated on the **same full dataset** — no train/test split, so reported R²/MAE overstate deployable accuracy.
- No ablations on query-generation LLM, number of synthetic queries, chunk size, or IRRELEVANT-query construction.
- No comparison against cheap normalization baselines (z-score, percentile rank) that might close part of the gap without learned mappings.
- Assumes normalized embeddings, static thresholds, and single-stage retrieval without re-ranking — real RAG systems with hybrid search, rerankers, or learned sparse retrieval are out of scope.
- Proprietary enterprise corpus and unreleased synthetic queries limit exact replication.
- Only cosine similarity is studied; the authors note the method is open to other distance metrics but do not test any.

## Relevance

与用户对 RAG / 嵌入模型 / 检索系统的兴趣高度相关：本文直接解决"换嵌入模型后旧相似度阈值失效"这一生产级痛点，提供的 SQP 方法和 isotonic regression 校准可作为模型迁移与持续监控的轻量工具，方法学也容易移植到自有语料上复现。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the conversion accuracies change under a proper train/test (or train/validation/test) split, and how stable are the learned isotonic functions when the target corpus drifts over time?
- Does SQP generalize beyond Titan and Ada to other families (Cohere, Voyage, Mistral, BGE, E5, GECKO)? Does the "intra-family ≈ linear, cross-family ≈ S-shaped" two-regime pattern hold universally?
- How sensitive are the per-class distributions and threshold operating points to (a) the choice of query-generation LLM, (b) the number of synthetic queries per chunk, and (c) the definition/construction of the IRRELEVANT class?
- Can SQP-derived class-separation statistics (e.g., the IRRELEVANT↔RELEVANT mean gap) serve as a *prior* predictor of expected cross-model conversion R² before any mapping is fitted?
- How do learned similarity mappings interact with hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense) and with reranking stages that compress or sharpen the score distribution?
- Could SQP be used for continuous corpus-drift monitoring — detecting when a deployed calibration has gone stale and re-calibration is needed — and what would the detection threshold be?
