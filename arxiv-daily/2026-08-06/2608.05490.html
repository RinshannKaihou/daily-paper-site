Why this matters

Foundational statistical theory for unsupervised, success-only LLM-agent failure attribution — its FDR and selection-sensitivity procedures are drop-in error-control modules for deployable auditing pipelines.

Critical edge

Exact localization rests on assumptions — (A0) cost non-degeneracy and (A2a) on-support correctness — that are precisely the conditions most likely to fail with real lossy embeddings, and are never verified for any deployed model.

01 Methodology

Axiomatic-probabilistic. No model is trained; no benchmark is run.

A trajectory is a sequence of operations o₁…o_T with embeddings x_t ∈ ℝᵈ under a budget. Two filtrations are defined — F_t and G_t = F_{t-1} ∨ σ(c_t) — the latter being what is known just before each embedding realizes. The machinery:

  • Generalized residuals. A predictable conditional law Q_t yields Rosenblacht-uniform residuals u_t ∈ [0,1]ᵈ; the audit statistic is a_t = S(u_t). Gaussian bridge instance recovers a_t = ‖ε_t‖².
  • Corruption model. For corrupted steps t ∈ I the true law is Q̃_t ≠ Q_t with a predictable mean shift. The set I must itself be predictable.
  • Proof toolkit. Peeling / sequential maximal coupling (Thm 1, 5); Grönwall-type two-sided comparison for the propagation envelope (Thm 2); DKW + Massart for simultaneous FDP (Prop 6); Pinsker / chain-rule arguments for the confusability floor (Thm 9).
  • Concrete instantiation (§6). A neural-bridge drift with heteroscedastic covariance floored at σ_min·I, Euler-discretized — but only as the object the theory is built for; nothing is fitted here.

02 Results & detection limits

Where success-only auditing can go — and where it provably cannot.

  • Exact localization (Thm 1). Under one-step scoring, a benign step executed after a corruption has a null generalized residual — one mechanism error generates exactly one flag. This dissolves the "symptoms mask the source" problem.
  • Confusability floor (Thm 9). At d=256, halving the floor requires 2^{d+2} ≈ 10⁷⁸ more trajectories. The exact corrupted set is not testable at any sample size without a minimum-effect threshold (Thm 8).
  • Dimension is the lever (Cor 8). Dropping d: 256 → 8 turns a near-flat floor into one decaying as n⁻¹ᐟ⁵ — the stronger argument for dimension reduction than any finite-dim power table.
  • One-step vs. k-step. For a gradual perturbation spread over k steps, one-step loses a factor k in noncentrality; the dyadic ladder {1,2,4,…,2^J} closes it to within √2 (Thm 10).
  • Sparse power. Max-coordinate (Bonferroni) channel needs λ*=24.5 vs. 80.0 for dense χ² at 1-sparse — a 3.3× reduction; wins only for sparsity k* ≤ 7 (<3% of dims).
  • Late shifts are 9× harder (Prop 15). A shift at s₀=0.89 has a damage-to-detectability ratio 9× that of the same shift at the start — the bridge anchor contracts early perturbations.
  • Per-report bounds are weak (Prop 6). Additive slack is 3.9 flags at T=10, 5.5 at T=20, 8.7 at T=50 — so only a long-run FDR guarantee is delivered, not a per-report probability statement.

03 Strengths ↔ Limitations

▲ Strengths

  • Solves the masking problem by construction: Thm 1's conditional nullity of inherited residuals is the precise statement prior reconstruction-based auditors only approximated.
  • Complete error-control toolkit — BY, e-BH, simultaneous FDP, TV-robustness, selection Γ* — each with explicit dependence assumptions, so a deployer picks the procedure whose assumptions actually hold.
  • The negative identifiability results (Thm 8–9) are the most valuable part: they tell the field where success-only auditing cannot go, independent of any particular model.
  • Honest about scope: §12 enumerates three structural limits and explicitly recommends E-detectors / conformal test martingales for the regime-change case it cannot handle.

▼ Limitations

  • Zero empirical validation. No model is fit, no real trajectories are audited; the four §9 diagnostics are prescribed but never executed. The gap to Yeh et al. (2026) is never closed.
  • Headline result needs strong assumptions. Predictable conditioning forces an explicit joint factorization of cost and embedding; benign-step null requires competence preservation (Prop 14) — if the agent's mechanism changes post-error, the framework does not apply.
  • Per-report guarantees weak at realistic T (3.9 flags slack at T=10): a single audit report gets only a long-run FDR + point estimate.
  • The floor result cuts both ways. At deployed dimensions more calibration data is nearly useless; the remedy (model in intrinsic dimension) is asserted, not demonstrated.

04 Questions for further exploration

  1. Does any actually deployed neural-CDE / bridge auditor satisfy (A0) cost non-degeneracy and (A2a) on-support correctness — and what does the §9 QQ/KS diagnostic show on held-out valid trajectories stratified by s_{t-1}?
  2. How large is the intrinsic dimension of real agent-trajectory embeddings? Cor 8 changes the rate from flat to n⁻¹ᐟ⁵ at d=8, but no method is given to estimate it or fit dynamics in a representation that exposes it.
  3. Can the selection-sensitivity value Γ* (Thm 6) be estimated from reviewer-score data in a real deployment — and how often do flags survive at, say, Γ=2?
  4. For gradual cohort-narrowing / covariate-drift attacks, how much power does the dyadic ladder recover over one-step scoring on a real bridge — and does the matched-horizon gain survive the O(log T) multiplicity cost?
  5. Step-level attribution is declared inadmissible under post-error regime change (Prop 14). Can the FDR-controlled step set and the changepoint estimate be reported jointly with E-detectors / conformal test martingales?
