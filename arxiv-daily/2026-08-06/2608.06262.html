Hypothesis Testing with Conditional Queries: Learnability and the Value of Interaction

A pure-theory paper pinning down exactly how much interaction helps when testing distributions with conditional samples.
Key finding

Interaction cuts conditional hypothesis-testing queries by exactly a quadratic Θ(N²) factor — never exponential.

交互式测试相对静态测试的查询数增益恰好为 Θ(N²) ——平方级节省，而非指数级。

N²Key metric
GN(ε) = Θε(N²)

The exact worst-case adaptivity gap for conditional-distribution hypothesis testing, holding for every N ≥ 8.

L = 8M·(T + log 1/ρ) fixed pair queries, M = C(N,2)
★Why this matters

Quantifies how much interaction can cut evaluation tests — a tight Θ(N²) ceiling for adaptive benchmark sampling, though idealized assumptions block direct LLM deployment.

!Critical edge

Methodology

方法

A binary hypothesis test on a finite space X=[N]: an unknown full-support distribution P belongs to one of two classes P₀ or P₁, and a tester must identify the label using conditional queries — each query is an event A ⊆ X, the oracle returns a fresh sample X ~ P(·|A).

The whole question turns on timing: a non-adaptive tester commits the entire query vector before seeing responses; an adaptive tester picks each query from observed history. Three techniques carry every result:

T1Pairwise representation

The map Θ(P) of all pairwise conditionals P(i|{i,j}) fixes P up to normalization — echoing Bradley–Terry. Learnability ⟺ positive uniform separation Δ_pair > 0.

T2Coupling from the past

Read-once CFTP (Propp–Wilson ’96 / Wilson ’00) turns a pre-committed pair-sample stream into exact draws from P(·|A) for any later event A — the move that beats exponential enumeration.

T3Hard family on N=4m

Partition into I,J,U,V; hide the label signal in one of m² light pairs B_{jk}. Adaptive testers locate (j,k) in O(1) queries; non-adaptive ones must spread T over all m² pairs.

Results & Theorem Cards

结果与定理

No benchmarks — the load-bearing output is four theorem statements with closed-form constants. The two extremes meet in the middle to pin down the gap.

Thm 3.1 · Learnability

Exact characterization via pairwise separation

Two classes are learnable under conditional queries ⟺ Δ_pair(P₀,P₁) > 0. At zero separation the minimax risk is exactly ½ at every finite budget — adaptive or not.

If Δ > 0T*_na ≤ M·(8/Δ²)·log(2M/ε)
Thm 4.1 · Simulation

Universal non-adaptive simulation

Any T-round adaptive policy can be simulated non-adaptively, transcript within TV distance ρ, uniformly over all P — replacing the naïve 2^N−1 enumeration baseline.

Pair queriesL = 8M·(T + log 1/ρ)
Thm 5.1 · Lower bound

Matching Ω(N²) hard family

On N=4m outcomes: adaptive complexity is O_ε(1) (≤ 3⌈8 log(3/ε)⌉), while any non-adaptive tester needs Ω_ε(m²). Off-target query KL ≤ 32 log 9 · m⁻⁴.

Constantc_ε = 2(1−2ε)² / (2 log 3 + 8 log 9)
Cor 4.2 · Gap

Tight worst-case adaptivity gap

Combining upper + lower bounds gives the exact rate G_N(ε) = Θ_ε(N²) for all N ≥ 8. Interaction helps by a quadratic factor — the apparent exponential branching yields no exponential advantage.

Coalescence costE[τ_A] < 2M, E[exp(τ_A/8M)] ≤ e

The Balance Sheet

优势 / 局限

+ Strengths

  • Exact, not just bounded. Both the learnability criterion and the adaptivity gap are tight — the rare paper that closes a question.
  • Coupling-from-the-past is elegant. Produces exact P_A samples from pre-committed pair data, with a clean exponential-moment bound.
  • Honest scope. Sec. 6 lists every assumption (finite full-support, exact conditionals, worst-case-only) instead of overclaiming.
  • Real conceptual gain. Replaces the only prior generic upper bound — exponential enumeration of all 2^N−1 events — with a tight quadratic one.
  • Usable constants. c_ε, n_ε, the 8M factor are all closed-form — no asymptotic hand-waving.

− Limitations

  • No experiments. Pure theory; no evidence the quadratic gap matters in real evaluation pipelines.
  • Strong model assumptions. Full support + exact conditional sampling from arbitrary events are not primitives in LLM benchmarking — implications are aspirational.
  • Worst-case gap only. The hard family is adversarial; typical tasks may show much smaller (or zero) adaptivity gains. No average-case analysis.
  • No bounded-adaptivity analysis. Fully-adaptive vs. non-adaptive only; the practically interesting 1-to-log-N-round regime is left open.
  • Counts queries, not event-specification cost — the dominant expense in real benchmarks, explicitly excluded.

Open Questions

待探索
What is the gap for k-round testers (k=1,2,…,log N) interpolating between extremes? The Chakraborty ’24 bounded-adaptivity framework suggests this is tractable.
Can the lower bound be matched by a distribution-specific or average-case gap rather than this adversarial worst case?
What survives under noisy / approximate conditional oracles — e.g. LLM-as-judge labels with non-zero error?
Does the Θ(N²) gap extend to tolerant testing (ε-close vs. ε-far), where the sharp threshold may differ?
Is there a data-dependent simulation achieving o(N²) on easy distributions while retaining the worst-case O(N²) guarantee?
If specifying the conditioning event is the real cost, what is the gap under a combined query + specification budget?
