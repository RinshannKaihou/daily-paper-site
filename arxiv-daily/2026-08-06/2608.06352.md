> **一句话总结：** CalibForge 提出一种对抗式"作者-求解器"循环来合成终端任务——利用多个求解器的验证过/失败结果作为构造期反馈，把候选任务迭代修订到"可解但不被一致求解"的可学习区间，由此合成 5,431 个任务，让 Qwen3-30B/3.5-35B 在 Terminal-Bench 2.0 上分别达到 32.58%/47.57%，并显著迁移到 SWE-bench Pro 和 Doc2Repo。

> **TL;DR:** CalibForge turns terminal-task construction into a constrained adversarial author–solver loop where verified solver pass/fail outcomes and trajectories drive multi-round task revision toward a "solver-relative learnable zone" (solvable but not uniformly solved). Its multi-solver (cross-model disagreement) and contrastive (strong-pass/weak-fail) calibration strategies produce 5,431 tasks; fine-tuning Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct on them lifts Terminal-Bench 2.0 from 7.87% to 32.58% (+24.71pp over base), beating the strongest baseline TerminalTraj (26.22%) by 6.36pp, with the same gains transferring to SWE-bench Pro (30.94%, +27.68pp over base) and Doc2Repo (35.98%, +30.04pp over base). Matched ablations isolate the gain to calibration (contrastive +8.62pp, multi-solver +6.74pp vs single-solver +1.87pp at equal task counts), not trajectory volume.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"环境级行为校准"（environment-level behavioral calibration）这一构造原则**：把任务的可学习性视为相对于求解器设置的属性，用已验证的求解器行为（通过/失败 + 完整轨迹）来决定任务保留并指导多轮修订，而非仅用可执行性来判定任务是否合格。
- **设计 CalibForge 系统，实现受约束的对抗式"作者-求解器"循环**：从一条线索出发，作者代理研究真实工程问题（版本相关 bug、依赖冲突、配置陷阱、可复现边缘案例），联合构造任务指令、Docker 执行环境与验证测试，并经过结构校验 + 自求解两阶段验证后进入校准循环（最多 Rmax=50 轮）。
- **提出两种对抗式校准策略**：多求解器校准（多个不同模型独立尝试，保留"至少一个通过且至少一个失败"的候选）与对比式校准（指定强/弱求解器，保留"强者通过、弱者失败"的候选），两者都把候选任务锚定到"可解但不被一致求解"的可学习区间。
- **合成 5,431 个校准任务并系统证明校准优于单纯撰写/校验与单求解器反馈**：匹配任务数（各 1,300 个）的消融显示，对比式/多求解器校准比"无求解器"分别提升 Terminal-Bench 2.0 +8.62/+6.74 个百分点，远超单求解器反馈的 +1.87 个百分点——且多求解器产生的 SFT 轨迹更少却仍更准，排除了"数据量"解释。
- **验证可迁移性**：在两个 Qwen 主干上，CalibForge 任务带来的能力可迁移到训练分布外的仓库级软件工程基准 SWE-bench Pro 和 Doc2Repo，30B 主干在二者上的提升分别达 +27.68 和 +30.04 个百分点。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulates "environment-level behavioral calibration"** — a construction principle that treats task learnability as solver-relative and uses verified solver behavior (pass/fail outcomes plus full trajectories) to determine retention and guide multi-round revision, rather than treating executable validity as sufficient.
- **Builds CalibForge**, an autonomous terminal-task synthesis system realizing a constrained adversarial author–solver loop: from a clue, the authoring agent researches real engineering problems (version-specific bugs, dependency conflicts, configuration pitfalls, reproducible edge cases), jointly authors the instruction, Docker environment, and verification tests, and validates via a two-stage process (structural checks + self-solving) before entering calibration.
- **Introduces two adversarial calibration strategies**: multi-solver calibration (a heterogeneous solver pool; retain when at least one passes and at least one fails) and contrastive solver calibration (a designated stronger/weaker pair; retain on strong-pass/weak-fail). Both operationalize a solver-relative "learnable zone" anchored in demonstrated solvability.
- **Constructs 5,431 calibrated tasks and isolates the calibration effect**: matched ablations (1,300 tasks each on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct) show contrastive (+8.62pp) and multi-solver (+6.74pp) calibration far exceed single-solver feedback (+1.87pp) on Terminal-Bench 2.0; crucially multi-solver yields *fewer* SFT trajectories than no-solver yet improves accuracy, ruling out a trajectory-volume explanation.
- **Demonstrates cross-benchmark transfer**: the learned terminal capability transfers out-of-distribution to SWE-bench Pro and Doc2Repo, with the 30B backbone gaining +27.68pp and +30.04pp over base respectively.

## Methodology

**Overall loop.** CalibForge starts from a clue `c` and a calibration specification `γ` (defining the solver setting and retention criterion `C_γ`), then runs Algorithm 1: (I) author and validate a candidate task `τ`; (II) for up to `Rmax=50` rounds, probe `τ` with solvers, check `C_γ`, and if unmet, revise `τ` using feedback and revalidate. A task is retained once `C_γ` is satisfied or discarded if unmet after `Rmax` rounds.

**Authoring & validation.** The authoring agent (DeepSeek-V4-Pro) works in a draft sandbox with web research, shell, and file-edit tools. It searches official docs, GitHub repos/issues, and Stack Overflow for concrete engineering problems, selects a direction by relevance/distinctiveness/feasibility, and writes a task specification (tools/dependencies, inputs/edge cases, expected behaviors, target failure modes). It pre-tests package installability and resource accessibility, then jointly authors the task instruction, Dockerfile + initial files, and verification tests. Before calibration, each candidate passes **two-stage validation**: (1) *structural validation* — required build files present, environment builds and initializes, verifier runs, and all tests fail in the initial state (fail-first); (2) *self-solving* — the authoring agent itself solves the task in an isolated sandbox and confirms the intended solution executes and that instruction, environment, and tests are mutually consistent. Only `V(τ)=1` candidates proceed.

**Multi-solver calibration (γ_multi).** K solver subagents using different models attempt `τ` independently; with outcomes `y=(y_1,…,y_K)`, the retention criterion is `0 < Σ y_i < K` — disagreement across solver models. All-pass suggests too easy (shallow solution path); all-fail suggests too hard, underspecified, or broken. Feedback summaries + trajectories guide revision.

**Contrastive solver calibration (γ_con).** A designated stronger solver (DeepSeek-V4-Pro) and weaker solver (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) attempt `τ`; retain on `y_s=1 ∧ y_w=0`. Both-pass → look for shortcuts/insufficient difficulty; both-fail → check solvability, spec quality, verification; inverted (weaker passes, stronger fails) → inspect for leakage, nondeterminism, or misleading formulation.

**Trajectory collection & training.** Retained tasks are stored as Harbor-style instances. SFT trajectories are distilled with DeepSeek-V4-Pro (reasoning effort high) under CALIBFORGE-EVAL — a minimal bash/file-edit/finish scaffold matching the DeepSeek-V4 eval setting. Each task is attempted twice (200-step limit, 1-hour timeout); test-passing trajectories are retained and filtered for length, invalid tool calls, and tokenizer-unsafe tokens. Baseline task sets are re-distilled under the identical teacher protocol. Full-parameter SFT for 10 epochs on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B.

**Decontamination.** Training tasks are decontaminated against all three eval benchmarks: 14-gram prompt overlap removal, then 5-shingle Jaccard similarity over normalized instructions + verifier/test code, combined with shared output paths, overlapping test functions, and high-risk task-family matches; any flagged candidate is discarded.

## Results & Highlights

- **Terminal-Bench 2.0 (primary, in-distribution):** CalibForge-30B-A3B reaches **32.58 ± 1.12%**, beating the strongest baseline TerminalTraj (26.22 ± 0.75%) by **+6.36pp**; CalibForge-35B-A3B reaches **47.57 ± 0.99%**, beating TerminalTraj (40.82 ± 0.75%) by **+6.75pp**. The largest improvement over the base model is **+24.71pp** (30B: 7.87% → 32.58%).
- **SWE-bench Pro (OOD, 731-task public set, Resolved Rate):** CalibForge-30B-A3B reaches **30.94%** (best in its block, vs SETA-Env 29.91%), a **+27.68pp** gain over the 30B base (3.26%); CalibForge-35B-A3B reaches **44.32%** (vs TerminalTraj 43.91%), a **+3.03pp** gain over its base (41.29%).
- **Doc2Repo (OOD, full-repo generation, Pass Rate):** CalibForge-30B-A3B reaches **35.98 ± 1.82%** (best in block, vs TermiGen 34.11%), a **+30.04pp** gain over the 30B base (5.94%); CalibForge-35B-A3B reaches **48.77 ± 0.90%** (vs TerminalTraj 47.20%), a **+3.85pp** gain over its base (44.92%).
- **Calibration ablation (Table 3, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct, 1,300 tasks/variant):** No Solver **22.47%** → Single Solver **24.34% (+1.87pp)** → Multi Solver **29.21% (+6.74pp)** → Contrast Solver **31.09% (+8.62pp)**. Notably, Multi Solver produces *fewer* SFT trajectories (2,425) than No Solver (2,466) yet still improves accuracy substantially, ruling out trajectory volume as the cause.
- **Contrastive calibration first-probe analysis (Fig. 8):** Only **19%** of candidates initially satisfy the strong-pass/weak-fail target relation (despite all passing structural validation + self-solving); 61% are both-pass (too easy), 16% both-fail, 4% inverted. After feedback-driven revision + re-probing, **96%** ultimately satisfy the criterion (only 4% discarded).
- **Calibration funnel (Fig. 9):** 15% of candidates are retained within 1 probe, **53% within 5 probes**, 76% within 10, 93% within 20, 96% final — showing many mismatches are correctable early but a long tail needs sustained probing.
- **Data diversity:** 5,431 tasks = 1,263 multi-solver + 4,168 contrastive, spanning all 16 domain categories. Largest category is software engineering (25.5%) — far more balanced than prior sets skewed to one category (SETA-Env 74.6% sysadmin, TerminalTraj 49.9% sysadmin, CLI-Gym 67.0% debugging). 3,885 distinct capability tags (median 5/task), long-tailed (51.6% of tags appear in only one task).

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from the **Gaoling School of Artificial Intelligence, Renmin University of China** (a top Chinese AI lab), with two independent researchers and the AweAI Team. Several authors (Guoxin Chen, Fanzhe Meng, Wayne Xin Zhao, Ruihua Song, Ji-Rong Wen, Kai Jia) are associated with prior agent/long-horizon engineering work cited in the paper (e.g., "Toward autonomous long-horizon engineering for ml research", arXiv:2604.13018; "BeyondSWE"). The work is positioned squarely against a very recent (2026) wave of terminal-task synthesis systems (Endless Terminals, SETA-Env, CLI-Gym, TermiGen, TerminalTraj) and uses current frontier models (DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Flash, GLM-5, Kimi K2.5). A dataset and GitHub link are advertised on the title page, and the experimental protocol is notably careful: identical teacher-distillation and SFT recipe across all data sources, mean ± SEM over 3 runs for TB2/Doc2Repo, and explicit multi-rule benchmark decontamination. The matched task-count ablation (1,300 tasks/variant) and the "multi-solver yields fewer trajectories yet higher accuracy" control are methodologically sound ways to isolate the calibration effect.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine).** The core reframing — treating solver pass/fail as *construction-time* feedback that revises the task, rather than as a post-hoc filter or as feedback to the solver (as in Reflexion) — is a real conceptual contribution. The evidence that only 19% of valid+self-solved candidates meet the contrastive criterion on first probe, rising to 96% after revision, convincingly shows that "validity ≠ learnability" and that the loop actively reshapes rather than filters tasks.
- **Rigor (mostly strong, with gaps).** The matched 1,300-task ablation controlling trajectory volume is the right experiment and is well executed. However, several things weaken confidence: (a) **SWE-bench Pro is evaluated only once** (no variance reported), so the 35B's +0.41pp edge over TerminalTraj on SWE-Pro (44.32 vs 43.91) is within noise; (b) the 35B OOD gains are small and arguably not clearly separated from baselines — on SWE-Pro the 35B improvement over the best baseline (TerminalTraj 43.91) is just +0.41pp, and on Doc2Repo +1.57pp over TerminalTraj, so the headline "transfers" claim is much weaker for the stronger backbone; (c) there is no explicit ablation isolating the *number* of solver rounds `Rmax` or the solver pool composition (e.g., what if the "weaker" solver in contrastive calibration were redefined?).
- **Reproducibility (mixed).** The protocol is described in detail and a dataset/GitHub is promised, but full reproduction is gated behind access to multiple frontier models (DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Flash, GLM-5, Kimi K2.5) that may not be openly available, plus substantial compute (each candidate runs up to 50 rounds × multiple solvers × 100 steps × 30-min cap). The exact decontamination thresholds and task-family matching rules are deferred to supplementary material.
- **Significance (high, if it holds).** Terminal-agent training data is a current bottleneck, and a principled "learnable zone" criterion is a useful primitive. The 30B gains are large and consistent across benchmarks; the 35B gains are the more honest test and are modest.
- **Red flags.** (1) The contrastive "learnable zone" is defined relative to an *arbitrary* strong/weak pair (DeepSeek-V4-Pro vs Flash); the paper does not study sensitivity to this choice, so it is unclear how the zone moves or whether gains transfer when the deployed model differs from both solvers. (2) There is **no explicit Limitations section** and no RL experiment — only SFT — so the claim that calibration helps "agent training data" is shown only for one regime. (3) The failure analysis appendix reveals that the trained 30B model fails on basic task-completion behaviors (e.g., *regex-log*: it constructs the regex but never writes the required `/app/regex.txt` file before timing out), suggesting some gains are benchmark-specific and the models still lack simple artifact-commitment discipline.

## Strengths

- A clean, well-motivated conceptual contribution: solver behavior as construction-time feedback, with two concrete, formal retention criteria (`0 < Σ y_i < K` and `y_s=1 ∧ y_w=0`).
- Strong, controlled experimental design: identical teacher-distillation and SFT recipe across all data sources, matched task-count ablation, and the trajectory-volume control that rules out the obvious confound.
- Convincing "revision vs. filter" evidence (19% → 96% under contrastive calibration) that the loop genuinely reshapes tasks.
- Broader, less skewed domain coverage than competing datasets (25.5% largest category vs 49.9–74.6% for prior sets).
- Honest per-category and per-trajectory analysis showing gains are broad (every TB2 category improves or matches) and that CalibForge elicits more reasoning (5.3k thinking tokens) with fewer steps (21) than CLI-Gym.
- The failure analysis appendix is a sign of intellectual honesty — it publishes concrete, mechanism-level failure cases rather than only aggregate scores.

## Limitations

- **No explicit Limitations section** in the paper; limitations must be inferred.
- **High construction cost:** up to `Rmax=50` calibration rounds, each round dispatching multiple solver subagents (each capped at 100 interaction steps and 30 minutes) — this is far more expensive than one-shot synthesis, and the 4% discard rate represents wasted effort. The paper does not report wall-clock or dollar cost per task.
- **Dependence on multiple frontier models** (DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Flash, GLM-5, Kimi K2.5) that may not be openly available, raising reproducibility and access concerns.
- **The contrastive "learnable zone" is defined relative to an arbitrary strong/weak pair;** no sensitivity analysis is provided for how the zone — and downstream gains — change when that pair is redefined or when the deployed model differs from both solvers.
- **Diminishing returns for stronger backbones:** 35B OOD gains over the best baseline are marginal (+0.41pp SWE-Pro, +1.57pp Doc2Repo), and SWE-Pro is evaluated only once with no variance, so the transfer claim is much weaker at 35B than the headline 30B numbers suggest.
- **Only SFT is studied;** no RL experiment, so the value of calibration for RL-trained agents is unaddressed.
- **No ablation on `Rmax` or solver pool composition**, leaving open how the calibration horizon trades off against cost and quality.

## Relevance

与用户对终端/命令行智能体训练数据合成的兴趣高度相关：CalibForge 直接解决"如何让合成任务既可解又有适当难度"这一核心问题，其对抗式求解器校准思路和"可学习区间"概念对任何做 agent 训练数据构造/课程设计的工作都有方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How sensitive are the gains to the choice of the strong/weak solver pair in contrastive calibration? Does the "learnable zone" generalize when the deployed model is outside the interval (e.g., stronger than the "stronger" solver)?
- What is the wall-clock and compute cost per retained task, and how does performance degrade if `Rmax` is cut from 50 to, say, 10 or 20? The funnel shows 93% are retained within 20 probes — is the long tail worth the cost?
- Does adversarial solver calibration also improve RL (e.g., GRPO/PPO) beyond SFT, or does the gain come mainly from higher-quality teacher trajectories?
- Can the calibration signal be made online/self-improving — i.e., let the trained student model itself join the solver pool, so the learnable zone tracks the student's improving capability?
- The failure analysis shows the 30B model constructs solutions but fails to commit required artifacts (e.g., `/app/regex.txt`); could adding "artifact-commitment" test patterns to the calibration criteria close this specific gap?
- How much of the OOD transfer to SWE-bench Pro / Doc2Repo comes from the broader domain mix (25.5% software engineering vs skewed prior sets) versus the calibration criterion itself? A domain-matched comparison would separate these.
