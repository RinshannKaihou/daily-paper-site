SEAM: Global consistency beyond local accuracy.

Locally-correct scientific explanations can still fail to glue into one globally admissible explanation — SEAM makes that failure computable, and detects it even when every local model scores R² > 0.999.

Why This Matters
A generator-agnostic global-consistency audit that wraps PINNs, neural operators, and regressors — surfacing invisible cross-region incompatibilities and channel-level attribution where local scores see nothing wrong.
Critical Edge
Only ensemble-variance is benchmarked; established OOD detectors (ODIN, energy, Mahalanobis, two-sample tests) are absent — so the practical advantage over real shift-detection baselines remains unestablished.
01 / METHODOLOGY

SEAM-Ω: obstruction as a 1-cochain

A finite cellular sheaf over the 1-skeleton of a cover's nerve. Each region emits a structured explanation; neighbors are compared on overlaps; disagreement is assembled into a cochain, decomposed by channel, and resolved by budgeted, refutable hypothesis tests.

Generate
Local structured explanation
Each region U_i emits e_i = (state, closure, observation, contract) blocks — stalk is F(U_i) = ℝ^d partitioned into channels.
Restrict
Block-diagonal overlap maps
Linear ρ_{i,ij} act per channel (interpolation for state, identity for closure, sensor selection for observation).
d⁰s = ρ_j·s_j − ρ_i·s_i
Obstruct
Admissibility defect cochain
Raw defect ω = Ds assembled from the coboundary matrix; a global section lives in ker D.
ω = Ds
Diagnose
Channel & overlap decomposition
ω split into state / closure / observation / contract fractions; dominant channel and overlap pinpoint failure locus.
ω = ω_state ⊕ ω_closure ⊕ ω_obs
Intervene
Budgeted, refutable repair
Each hypothesis is a projector P; hard feasibility is the linear test ω ∈ im(DP); closed-form min-cost solution (Thm 1).
Identify · Monitor
Blind directions & streaming ω(t)
𝒩 = ker D ∩ ker O captures admissibility-preserving but observation-invisible directions; streaming ω(t) costs one cached matvec.

03 / APPRAISAL

Strengths vs. Limitations

+ Strengths

  • Conceptually original: explanation-admissibility as a sheaf-theoretic gluing problem, distinct from local accuracy or residual satisfaction.
  • Clean, fully proven theory (Thms 1–2, Prop 7.2, Cor 7.3) with explicit assumptions A0–A4 and an operational mapping table.
  • Generator-agnostic: PINNs, FNOs, DeepONets, finite-volume solvers, and OLS/XGB regressors share one backend interface.
  • Honest reporting: separate hard vs soft verdicts, residuals retained, "unresolved" reported when all proper budgets fail — no forced causal attribution.
  • Identifiability analysis separates detected inconsistency (ω) from genuinely unresolved directions (𝒩 = ker D ∩ ker O).
  • Strong FNO OOD signal (r=0.995) at ~5× lower compute than the ensemble baseline.

— Limitations

  • All experiments use C = I. The generalized cost-metric theory — including the restricted-inverse subtlety flagged as load-bearing — is never exercised.
  • No public code or data release. Availability is "on reasonable request," undermining otherwise careful reproducibility documentation.
  • Weak OOD baselines. Only ensemble variance; no ODIN, energy, Mahalanobis, or two-sample tests.
  • Predominantly synthetic & small-scale. Real-world experiments are tabular regressions on 3-region covers; no coupled multi-physics demos where channel decomposition would matter most.
  • Exact-feasibility brittle under A0. Single-channel hard budgets fail when obstruction spans multiple channels, pushing attribution onto the soft Tikhonov branch.
  • Hand-crafted linear restrictions. The maps that materially shape ω are modeling choices, assumed rather than audited.
  • Pairwise overlaps only. SEAM-Ω uses the 1-skeleton; higher-order (triple+) consistency is out of scope.
04 / OPEN

Questions for further exploration

Q1
How does ω(t) compare as an OOD monitor against ODIN, energy scores, Mahalanobis distance, or two-sample dataset-shift tests on the same FNO/Burgers benchmark?
Q2
Does the diagnostic hold when C ≻ 0 is non-identity — e.g., a physics-informed or sensor-noise-calibrated cost metric — and does it change the surviving budget or dominant channel?
Q3
Can learned, interpretable restriction maps replace hand-crafted linear ones without breaking the auditability of D, and how sensitive is ω to restriction misspecification?
Q4
How does SEAM scale to higher-order overlap consistency (full nerve vs 1-skeleton), and does exact-feasibility become less brittle with multi-overlap constraints?
Q5
On genuinely coupled multi-physics or multi-sensor systems, does the channel decomposition yield qualitatively different attributions than a scalar consistency radius?
Q6
Can streaming ω(t) be turned into a calibrated sequential change-detection statistic (e.g., CUSUM) with controlled false-alarm rate, beyond a correlation with L² error?
