01 Methodology

Nonasymptotic analysis on a single calibration event — not an asymptotic average.

Setting is split conformal with calibration sample D_n^cal of size n. RLCP (Hore & Barber 2025) draws an auxiliary centre X̃_{n+1} from the kernel H_h, then performs weighted conformal prediction with weights w_{x̃,h}(X_i). The machinery:

  • Reverse-law identity. P{X_{n+1} ∈ A | X̃ = x̃} = Λ_{x̃,h}(A) — conditional on the realized centre, the test covariate follows the localization-weighted law. This delivers marginal validity while allowing a conditional-on-x̃ analysis that retains the randomness determining the RLCP set.
  • One calibration event. A single event Ω^cal_{n,x̃,h,δ} of probability ≥ 1−δ on which the kernel-weighted empirical CDF concentrates uniformly around the localized population CDF at rate ε_{n,h}(δ) (Eq. 37) plus a "+∞ mass" correction ρ_{n,h}.
  • Quantile bracketing (Lemma 28). CDF-band proximity becomes a quantile bound via the localized score-density floor κ_{α,x̃,h}^{(S)}; β-Hölder continuity of the conditional score CDF turns the localized average into the h^β bias.
  • Learned scores (Thms 6–7). Conditioning on an independent training fold lets the fixed-score bound apply to Ŝ; "oracle pivotality" (A4) removes the h^β bias and the sup-norm training error ε_∞^Δ(m,δ) enters linearly.

03 Strengths ↔ Limitations

▲ Strengths

  • Provides genuinely new finite-sample, realized-set, uniform-over-neighbourhood guarantees (Min et al. 2023 only averaged over calibration and localization randomness) plus a new oracle-relative length bound.
  • Clean bias–variance decomposition A^cal_{n,h}(δ) + h^β makes the bandwidth trade-off quantitative rather than folklore; balanced bandwidth recovers n^{−β/(2β+d)}.
  • The pivotality insight — for CQR/PIT targets localization adds no bias, bandwidth only governs effective calibration size — is a sharp, reusable structural observation about when local calibration is worth its cost.
  • Assumptions are instantiated, not assumed: three full propositions (residual, CQR, PIT) derive the constants (σ_up, L_S, κ, L_len) from standard conditional-density regularity.
  • High intellectual honesty throughout: explicit hybrid-vs-formal-RLCP gaps, fallback frequencies, descriptive (not confirmatory) status of real-data inference.

▼ Limitations

  • Length bound is informative only when κ_{α,x̃,h}^{(S)} > 0; it goes vacuous (1/κ → ∞) at thin-tailed or multi-modal conditional score distributions, even when the coverage bound still holds.
  • Common-support / uniform-density-floor assumption P2′ is strong — excludes location-scale families with unbounded support under uniform constants (appendix relaxation helps but only near the two target quantiles).
  • Real-data evaluation is small-scale (≤12 covariates, histogram gradient boosting with 200 trees), uses a hand-tuned fixed-constant bandwidth; no image/text/high-dimensional regression.
  • No comparison against other localized conformal rules (Guan 2023 localized CP, Mondrian/conditional CP, W-CP under covariate shift) — only global Split-CP and Split-CQR. The "is RLCP the best local calibration rule?" question is unaddressed.
  • All results are conditional on realized x̃ ∈ X; the paper itself notes "membership of the auxiliary centre in X is not automatic near the boundary" — boundary behaviour is not fully controlled.

04 Questions for further exploration

  1. Can the pivotality-based bias removal extend to approximately pivotal learned scores (where ∆_{S⋆} is small but not zero), giving an adaptive h^β-then-ε_∞^Δ transition rather than the current binary fixed-vs-pivotal split?
  2. How does RLCP compare empirically against other localized conformal rules (Guan 2023 with level correction, Mondrian/conditional CP, W-CP under covariate shift) on the same UCI benchmarks, rather than only against global Split-CP / Split-CQR?
  3. Does the hybrid fallback (global-CQR for empty windows, y-range cap otherwise) preserve any finite-sample validity guarantee, or can a principled fallback be designed that stays distribution-free while avoiding the 2×+ length premium on datasets like concrete?
  4. Can the sup-norm training-rate assumption A5 (ε_∞^Δ → 0) be replaced by an L_1(Λ_{x̃,h}) rate — which is all the threshold comparison actually uses — to admit estimators (neural quantile networks) that plateau in sup-norm?
  5. What is the behaviour of the realized-set bounds near the boundary of X, where the auxiliary centre X̃_{n+1} may fall outside the support and the reverse-law identity requires Z_{x̃,h} > 0 — can a boundary-corrected kernel recover the same n^{−β/(2β+d)} rate there?
