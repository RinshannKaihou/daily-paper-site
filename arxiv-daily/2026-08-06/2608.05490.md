> **一句话总结：** 本文为 Yeh et al. (2026) 提出的"仅用成功轨迹训练、对失败轨迹逐步打分"的智能体失败归因方法建立了完整的统计理论——证明了一步创新残差可实现精确局部化（一次错误只产生一个标记），给出了轨迹级 FDR 控制与选择敏感性分析，并用可识别性定理刻画了一个本质极限：在 d≈256 的表示维度下，可归因误差下界随校准数据百倍增加只下降不到 2%，即表示维度而非数据量才是瓶颈。

> **TL;DR:** This paper builds the statistical theory for the unsupervised (success-only) step-level failure-attribution method of Yeh et al. (2026) for autonomous analysis agents. It proves that a *one-step innovation residual* gives exact localization (one mistake → one flag) under predictable conditioning, supplies Benjamini–Yekutieli / e-BH procedures controlling per-trajectory FDR, and gives a sensitivity analysis for content-dependent audit selection. Its central negative result is a confusability floor on the smallest attributable error: at the embedding dimension actually in use (d=256) a 100× increase in calibration trajectories lowers the floor by under 2%, so dimension — not data volume — is the binding constraint. There are no empirical experiments; all results are theorems plus exact finite-dimensional tables.

## 核心贡献

- **精确局部化定理（Theorem 1）**：在可预测条件化与一步评分下，错误操作之后正确执行的操作其广义残差条件分布为 null（均匀），故"继承性污染"不产生信号——一次机制性错误只标记一处。这从理论上消除了"症状掩盖源头"的难题，且对随机停止时间 T 与随机可预测错误集 I 均成立。
- **多视界残差与累积权衡（§3.2, Theorem 10）**：对逐步累积的漂移型错误，一步评分在非中心度上损失因子 k；作者证明 dyadic 视界阶梯 {1,2,4,…,2^J} 相对匹配视界是 minimax 至 √2 因子以内的（Theorem 10），而一步评分会损失因子 k。
- **轨迹级 FDR 控制程序（§5）**：在轨迹内任意相依性与随机停止时间下，给出 Benjamini–Yekutieli（Theorem 3）与 e-BH（Theorem 4）两种程序，仅需校准轨迹可交换性而非模型正确设定；并给出对模型误设定的 TV 鲁棒界（Theorem 5）与选择机制敏感性 Γ*（Theorem 6）。
- **可识别性与检测下限（§11）**：证明不带最小效应阈值的精确错误集是不可检验的（Theorem 8），并给出"混淆下界" ρ_floor ∝ n^{-1/(d+2)}（Theorem 9）——在高维下几乎不随校准数据下降，这是任何此类审计程序都无法绕过的硬性极限。
- **桥接实例化与终端多模态（Part II, §8）**：给出 Gaussian bridge 实现，分析预算归一化、代价作为第二审计通道、锚点投影的污染效应（Proposition 8），并证明点值锚点在多模态终端下要么抬高终端假阳率要么降低终端灵敏度（Proposition 10），准确路由可将盲区从 ρ²/√d 降到 σ_c·√d（Proposition 12）。

## Key Contributions

- **Exact localization theorem (Theorem 1).** Under predictable conditioning and a one-step score, a benign operation executed *after* an earlier corruption has a null generalized residual (i.i.d. uniform) conditional on its realized input state, so inherited corruption produces no signal — one mechanism violation generates exactly one flag. This formally dissolves the "symptoms mask the source" problem and accommodates a random stopping time and a random predictable corruption set.
- **Multi-horizon residuals and the accumulation trade-off (§3.2, Theorem 10).** Reconstruction/rollout scores spread one mistake across every downstream operation (Proposition 1); for gradual perturbations spread over k steps the one-step channel loses a factor k in noncentrality (Proposition 2). A dyadic horizon ladder {1,2,4,…,2^J} is minimax within a factor √2 of the matched horizon (Theorem 10), giving O(log T) tests that recover bounded gradual corruptions.
- **Distribution-free error control for the reported set (§5).** Benjamini–Yekutieli on the padded family controls trajectory FDR at q·m₀/T_max under arbitrary within-trajectory dependence (Theorem 3); e-BH avoids the harmonic factor H_Tmax (Theorem 4). Validity needs only calibration exchangeability (A1), not correct model specification (A1*). A TV-robustness bound (Theorem 5) and a selection-sensitivity value Γ* per flag (Theorem 6) quantify degradation under misspecification and content-dependent submission.
- **Identifiability limits (§11).** The exact corrupted set is not testable at any sample size without a minimum-effect threshold (Theorem 8, Corollary 6). With such a threshold the smallest attributable corruption has a confusability floor ρ_floor ∝ σ²(LΔ)^d/(c·nT), decaying as n^{-1/(d+2)} (Theorem 9, Corollary 7) — a hard, asymptotically derived ceiling on what any auditor in this formulation can report.
- **Bridge instantiation and multimodal endpoints (Part II, §8).** A Gaussian bridge with budget-normalized time, terminal anchoring, and cost as a second audit channel is worked out in closed form. A point-valued terminal anchor provably cannot separate well-separated valid endpoints without either inflating terminal false positives or inflating terminal variance and blinding late-operation corruption (Proposition 10); accurate routing reduces the blind region from ρ²/√d to σ_c·√d (Proposition 12).

## Methodology

This is a **pure theory paper**; there is no experimental pipeline, no learned model is trained, and no benchmark is run. The methodology is axiomatic-probabilistic:

1. **Trajectory and filtrations (§2.1).** A trajectory is a sequence of operations o₁,…,o_T with embeddings x_t = ϕ(o_t) ∈ ℝ^d and execution costs c_t > 0, run under a budget C_max. Two filtrations are defined, F_t and G_t = F_{t-1} ∨ σ(c_t), the latter being what is known just before the embedding of step t is realized; all conditioning uses G_t because normalized time is G_t-measurable but not F_{t-1}-measurable.
2. **Generalized residuals (§2.2).** The model M is a predictable family of conditional laws Q_t on x_t given G_t (plus P_t for c_t). The Rosenblatt transform R_t gives uniform residuals u_t = R_t(x_t) ∈ [0,1]^d; the audit statistic is a_t = S(u_t). The Gaussian bridge instance uses Q_t = N(μ_t, Σ_t), recovering a_t = ‖ε_t‖² with ε_t the whitened prediction error.
3. **Corruption model.** For corrupted steps t ∈ I the true conditional law is Q̃_t ≠ Q_t with predictable parameters; a Gaussian mean shift Q̃_t = N(μ_t + b_t, Σ_t) is the canonical case. The corruption set I is required to be predictable ({t ∈ I} ∈ G_t).
4. **Proof techniques.** Peeling/sequential maximal coupling (Theorems 1, 5), Grönwall-type two-sided comparison for the propagation envelope (Theorem 2), DKW with Massart's constant for the simultaneous FDP bound (Proposition 6), Pinsker/chain-rule arguments for the confusability floor (Theorem 9), and a bias-variance decomposition of the noncentral χ² for the detection boundary (Proposition 3). All proofs are in the appendix.
5. **The concrete instantiation (§6).** A neural-bridge drift µ_t(x) = x + ((x̂_T^(t) − x)/(1−s_{t-1}) + u_θ(x,s_{t-1},x̂_T^(t)))·Δ_t with heteroscedastic covariance floored at σ_min·I, Euler-discretized — but only as the object the theory is built for; nothing is fitted here.

## Results & Highlights

The paper reports **no empirical experiments** (no datasets, no trained baselines, no measured accuracy). All quantitative claims are derived analytically; the ones below are stated verbatim from the paper's finite-dimensional tables and theorem corollaries.

- **Confusability floor barely moves with data at deployed dimensions (§11.2 table, Corollary 7).** At representation dimension d=256, a **100-fold** increase in the number of valid calibration trajectories reduces the minimum-attributable-corruption floor ρ_floor by **under 2%** (0.982 → 0.965); a **10⁴-fold** increase reduces it by under 4%. Halving the floor requires **2^{d+2}** times more calibration data — at d=256 that is ≈10⁷⁸ more trajectories.
- **Dimension, not data, is the binding constraint (Corollary 8).** Reducing the modeled dimension from d=256 to d=8 turns a floor that is effectively fixed in n into one decaying as n^{-1/5}; this is presented as the *stronger* motivation for dimension reduction than the finite-dimensional power comparison.
- **One-step vs. k-step power for gradual perturbations (Proposition 2).** A corruption of total displacement b spread evenly over k consecutive steps has one-step noncentrality ‖b‖²/(k²σ²) but k-step noncentrality ‖b‖²/(kσ²) — a factor-k gap that the dyadic horizon ladder (Theorem 10) closes to within √2.
- **Sparse-alternative power table at α=0.01, 80% power (§4.2).** At d=256 the dense χ² channel requires noncentrality λ*=80.0, whereas the max-coordinate (Bonferroni) channel requires λ*_{k=1}=24.5 for a 1-sparse perturbation (a **3.3× reduction**) and λ*_{k=3}=45.5 for a 3-sparse perturbation (1.8× reduction); the max channel wins only for sparsity k* ≤ 7 coordinates, i.e. <3% of dimensions.
- **Cost of multiplicity control (§5.4).** At T_max=40 the harmonic number H_{Tmax} ≈ 4.3, so the Benjamini–Yekutieli threshold q/(T_max·H_{Tmax}) is substantially lower than the nominal BH level; the e-BH procedure removes this harmonic factor.
- **Per-report FDP bound is weak at realistic trajectory lengths (Proposition 6).** The simultaneous additive slack is **3.9 flags at T=10, 5.5 at T=20, and 8.7 at T=50** (at δ=0.1) — i.e. the high-probability per-report bound is uninformative unless the report contains many operations, so the authors deliver only a long-run FDR guarantee, not a per-report probability statement.
- **Adversarial late shifts are up to 9× harder to catch (Proposition 15).** At s̄=0.9, a shift injected at normalized time s₀=0.75 has a damage-to-detectability ratio 4× that of the same shift at the start of the trajectory, and at s₀=0.89 it is 9× — because the bridge anchor contracts early perturbations by (1−s̄)/(1−s₀) while innovation detectability is position-independent.
- **Recurrence gain vs. identifiability (Proposition 13 vs. Corollary 5).** Pooling m_c recurrent occurrences of an error type lowers the noise-limited detection boundary by a factor √m_c, **but** this does not reduce the confusability floor, which is governed by the number n of valid calibration trajectories and never by m_c.

## Authors & Credibility

- **Ahmed Hassoon and Mark Dredze, Johns Hopkins University** (dated August 7, 2026; arXiv:2608.05490v1, 6 Aug 2026). Mark Dredze is an established researcher in NLP/ML; the paper is positioned as the theoretical companion to Yeh et al. (2026, arXiv:2607.12747, "Tracing agentic failure from the flow of success"), whose empirical formulation it adopts verbatim (one-class training, continuous latent path, pooled conformal threshold).
- The mathematical machinery is drawn from mature, correctly-cited literatures: Kalman innovations and model-based fault detection (Kailath 1968; Willsky 1976; Basseville & Nikiforov 1993), conformal prediction (Vovk et al. 2005; Tibshirani et al. 2019; Bates et al. 2023), e-values and e-BH (Vovk & Wang 2021; Wang & Ramdas 2022), FDR control (Benjamini–Hochberg 1995; Benjamini–Yekutieli 2001; Katsevich & Ramdas 2020), and sparse Gaussian-sequence detection (Ingster & Suslina 2003; Donoho & Jin 2004). The citations are accurate and the proof techniques (sequential maximal coupling, Grönwall comparison, DKW) are standard for the claims made.
- Proofs are provided in full for all 15 propositions and 10 theorems; the level of rigor is high and self-consistent. The authors are unusually explicit about the limits of their own results (an entire §12 on structural limitations, plus the negative §11).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The exact-localization result (Theorem 1) is the cleanest contribution and is genuinely new in the agent-attribution setting: the conditional-on-realized-input-state nullity of inherited residuals is a sharper statement than the reconstruction-score heuristics in prior work. The confusability floor (Theorem 9) and the non-testability of the exact estimand (Theorem 8) are the right negative results to state. The bridge-specific material (§6–§8) is more derivative — it is largely a re-derivation, with explicit filtrations, of properties already implicit in Yeh et al.'s construction, and the authors concede (§1.1) that Yeh et al.'s control path "resolves the multimodal endpoint problem … more directly than the construction developed here."
- **Rigor.** High. Assumptions (A0)–(A5) are each invoked only where stated; every theorem is paired with a proof; the TV-robustness bound (Theorem 5) and the sharpened calibration term (Proposition 7, improving nε to ε + O(n^{-1/2})) are carefully stated. One genuine technical caveat: Theorem 1's exact localization rests on **(A0) cost non-degeneracy** (the conditional law L(x_t|G_t) must be atomless, which fails if cost determines the embedding) and **(A2a) on-support correctness** — both are exactly the conditions most likely to fail with a real lossy embedding ϕ and a fitted heteroscedastic Gaussian, and the paper does not verify them for any actual deployed model.
- **Reproducibility.** **Weak, by construction.** There is no code, no data, no trained model, and no experiments — the §9 "diagnostics" (QQ/KS checks, four simulation studies including a bimodal-endpoint simulation) are described as things a deployer *should* run but are not actually run here. The numerical tables in §4.2 and §11.2 are exact analytical evaluations and are reproducible from the stated formulas, but nothing about the bridge model itself is fit or tested.
- **Significance.** The practical significance is double-edged. On the positive side, the paper gives deployers of success-only attribution a calibrated FDR procedure and a sensitivity value Γ* per flag, which are directly actionable. On the negative side, Theorem 9 plus the §11.2 table effectively say that **at d=256 the entire program has a near-fixed floor that more calibration data will not move** — the authors frame dimension reduction as the remedy, but they provide no method to find the "intrinsic dimension" of a real agent's trajectory manifold, and Corollary 9 notes that as d grows the floor approaches LΔ (one operation's worth of drift), i.e. attribution becomes impossible for any sub-operation-magnitude error.
- **Red flags / overclaiming.** The authors are commendably restrained — they repeatedly flag that report-specific FDP guarantees are weak (§5.5), that competence preservation is required for step-level attribution to be well posed (Proposition 14), and that the harmonic factor in BY is a real power loss. The main overclaim risk is implicit: the theory is built for Yeh et al.'s *specific* score (squared CDE-reconstruction error), but the paper does not establish that Yeh et al.'s fitted neural CDE satisfies the on-support correctness (A2a) or the off-support Lipschitz/variance-inflation conditions (Theorem 7) that the theorems require — so the gap between "the theory holds" and "the deployed auditor enjoys the guarantees" is exactly the unverified modeling assumptions, which the paper acknowledges (§2.3, §9) but does not close.

## Strengths

- Solves the masking problem *by construction* under one-step innovation scoring: Theorem 1's conditional nullity of inherited residuals is the precise statement that prior reconstruction-based auditors only approximated.
- Provides a complete error-control toolkit (BY, e-BH, simultaneous FDP, TV-robustness, selection sensitivity Γ*) with explicit dependence assumptions for each, so a deployer can pick the procedure whose assumptions actually hold.
- The negative identifiability results (Theorems 8–9, Corollaries 6–9) are the most valuable part of the paper: they tell the field where success-only auditing *cannot* go, independent of any particular model. The §11.2 table (100× data → <2% floor reduction at d=256) is a striking, citation-worthy negative result.
- Honest about its own scope: §12 enumerates three structural limitations (no causal interpretation; (A2a) fails under post-error regime change; per-report FDP is weak at T in the tens) and explicitly recommends E-detectors / conformal test martingales for the regime-change setting the framework cannot handle.

## Limitations

- **Zero empirical validation.** No model is fit, no real agent trajectories are audited, and the four simulation diagnostics in §9 are prescribed but not executed. The gap to Yeh et al. (2026)'s empirical setup — which the theory is meant to underpin — is never closed; we do not learn whether any real neural-CDE auditor satisfies (A0), (A2a), or the off-support conditions of Theorem 7.
- **Strong assumptions for the headline result.** Exact localization needs predictable conditioning, which the paper shows (§6.1) forces an explicit joint factorization of cost and embedding — a non-trivial modeling requirement that deployers may not meet. The benign-step null also requires competence preservation (Proposition 14); if the agent's mechanism changes after an error and stays changed, step-level attribution is declared inadmissible and the framework does not apply.
- **Per-report guarantees are weak at realistic T.** Proposition 6's additive slack (3.9 flags at T=10) means an individual audit report gets only a long-run FDR guarantee plus a point estimate, not a high-probability bound — a practical caveat for anyone acting on a single audit.
- **The floor result cuts both ways.** Theorem 9 is honest but also somewhat deflating for the research program: at the dimensions used in practice, more calibration data is nearly useless, and the proposed remedy (model in intrinsic dimension) is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **Anchor and terminal-multimodality analysis (§6.3, §8) is intricate but untested.** Proposition 8's contamination bound grows as √λ_t (unbounded in corruption magnitude) and the bounded-influence fix forfeits the exact χ²_d null — a real but unquantified trade-off.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does any *actually deployed* neural-CDE or bridge auditor (e.g., a re-implementation of Yeh et al. 2026 on a real data-analysis agent) satisfy (A0) cost non-degeneracy and (A2a) on-support correctness — and what does the §9 QQ/KS diagnostic show on held-out valid trajectories stratified by s_{t-1}?
- How large is the *intrinsic* dimension of real agent-trajectory embeddings? Corollary 8 says reducing modeled dimension from 256 to 8 changes the floor's rate from effectively flat to n^{-1/5}, but no method is given to estimate this intrinsic dimension or to fit the dynamics in a representation that exposes it.
- Can the selection-sensitivity value Γ* (Theorem 6) be estimated from reviewer-score data in a real deployment, and how often do flags survive at, say, Γ=2 in practice?
- Empirically, for gradual cohort-narrowing or covariate-drift attacks (the paper's own §11.3 motivating examples), how much power does the dyadic horizon ladder {1,2,4,16} recover over one-step scoring on a real bridge model, and does the matched-horizon gain (Theorem 10) survive the O(log T) multiplicity cost?
- The framework declares step-level attribution inadmissible under post-error regime change (Proposition 14) and defers to E-detectors / conformal test martingales — can the two be unified so that the FDR-controlled step set and the changepoint estimate are reported jointly?
