> **一句话总结：** 本综述将 LLM 与智能体时代的持续学习重构为沿 When（预训练/后训练/推理期）、Where（参数 vs. 记忆/技能/协议等 harness 组件）、How（off-policy / on-policy / 超越梯度）三轴的"能力持续演化"，把经典 CL 定位为该三维空间中的一个点，并据此统一梳理了从持续预训练、测试时训练到技能库/记忆系统/提示演化等原本各自为政的方法谱系，指出了稀疏与空缺区域作为未来方向。

> **TL;DR:** A position-and-survey paper that reframes continual learning (CL) for the LLM/Agentic-AI era as **continual capability evolution** along three orthogonal axes — *When* (pre-training / post-training / inference-time), *Where* (parameters vs. external **harness**: memory, skills, protocols), and *How* (off-policy gradient / on-policy / beyond-gradient) — situating classical CL (post-training + parameters + off-policy gradient) as a single point in this space. It surveys ~150+ representative methods (continual pre-training, TTT-LM/TiTans, Voyager, MemoryBank, Promptbreeder, model merging, zeroth-order ZeroFlow, RLHF/RLVR on-policy alignment) that it locates in the joint (When, Where, How) space, identifies dense vs. sparse regions, and argues that hand-tuned harness engineering "functionally substitutes for but does not mechanistically constitute" continual learning. It reports **no original experiments**; the closest thing to quantitative evidence is its synthesis of cited findings — e.g., that on-policy RL's forgetting scales with KL from the reference policy (Shenfeld et al., 2025) whereas off-policy SFT can drift arbitrarily far, that ZeroFlow's zeroth-order updates match or beat first-order fine-tuning on forgetting benchmarks, and that continual fine-tuning degrades domain/reasoning/reading ability with the effect intensifying across the 1B–7B scale (Luo et al.).

## 核心贡献

- **提出三轴统一框架（When / Where / How）**：把持续学习从"防止灾难性遗忘的单一算法问题"重新刻画为"能力在模型全生命周期如何组织"的系统级问题。经典 CL 被精确定位为该三维坐标系中的一个点——*post-training + parameters + off-policy gradient*。
- **把 agent harness 纳入同一分析框架**：与以往主要沿经典参数中心分类法延伸的 LLM-CL 综述不同，本文首次把外部 **harness 组件**（memory / skills / protocols）、**推理期更新**（TTT-LM、Titans、TTRL）与**超越梯度机制**（模型合并、零阶优化、提示演化）放进与参数级学习同一坐标系，揭示它们之间隐含的关系。
- **系统梳理三轴各自的方法谱系**：When 轴覆盖 continual pre-training（SimpleCPT 的 LR rewarming + replay）、post-training（SFT→RLHF/RLVR 多阶段对齐及其遗忘）和 inference-time（TTT-LM/TTT-E2E、TTRL 的奖励驱动测试期学习）；Where 轴从 LoRA 族（O-LoRA、LoRAMoE、SLIM）扩展到 memory（MemoryLLM、MemoryBank 的 Ebbinghaus 遗忘曲线、A-MEM 的原子化记忆、MemRL 的奖励驱动更新）、skills（Voyager 的自生成代码技能库、SkillRL/SAGE 的 RL 联合优化、SKILL0 的 harness→参数内化轨迹）和 protocols（Promptbreeder 的自指演化、规则文件、消息格式）；How 轴区分 off-policy（SDFT 自蒸馏拉近分布）、on-policy（RLHF/RLVR，Shenfeld 等证明其遗忘正比于 KL，结构性天然抗遗忘）与 beyond-gradient（Task Arithmetic/TIES 合并、MeZO/ZeroFlow 零阶、heuristic learning）。
- **指出稀疏/空缺坐标作为未来方向**：通过对比稠密区（参数级 + post-training + off-policy/on-policy）与稀疏区（harness 与参数的协同演化、推理期 on-policy、长期遗忘的多层面度量），把空缺坐标转译为具体研究机会。
- **给出对 AGI 路径的明确判断**：主张 *frontier model 的持续学习* 是第一优先级，领域专用 CL 不是；并诚实估计"记忆+harness 路线在工程层面已快速逼近通用助理的能力天花板，但真正能自主扩展能力、跨域迁移、长程决策的 AGI 仍有相当距离，且单纯更长的上下文/更大的记忆/更多技能无法填补这一距离"。

## Key Contributions

- **A tri-axial (When / Where / How) unifying framework.** Recasts continual learning as *continual capability evolution* along three complementary questions: *when* in the model lifecycle learning happens (pre-training / post-training / inference-time), *where* capability is carried (parameters vs. the harness: memory, skills, protocols), and *how* updates are driven (off-policy gradient / on-policy / beyond-gradient). Classical CL collapses to a single canonical point: post-training + parameters + off-policy gradient.
- **Brings the agent harness into the same frame as parametric learning.** Unlike prior LLM-CL surveys that extend the classical parameter-centric taxonomy, this work jointly situates external memory, skill libraries, interaction protocols, test-time training, and gradient-free mechanisms (model merging, zeroth-order, prompt evolution) in one coordinate space, making otherwise implicit relationships explicit.
- **A systematic per-axis methodological lineage.** Each axis is traced through its representative methods: continual pre-training (SimpleCPT's LR rewarming + small replay buffer keeping degradation near-negligible for 405M/10B models), the multi-stage post-training pipeline as a CL problem in its own right (with TRACE benchmark and "spurious forgetting" diagnoses), inference-time persistence (TTT-LM, TTT-E2E, reward-driven TTRL); the LoRA family (O-LoRA orthogonality, LoRAMoE routing, SLIM identity path), memory carriers (MemoryLLM self-writable pool, MemoryBank's Ebbinghaus forgetting, A-MEM atomic propositions, MemRL reward-driven evolution), skill carriers (Voyager's growing code library, SkillRL/SAGE RL joint optimization, SKILL0's harness→parameter internalization), protocol carriers (Promptbreeder self-referential mutation); and the How axis distinction between on-policy learning's structural small-KL anti-forgetting property (Shenfeld et al.) and beyond-gradient mechanisms (Task Arithmetic/TIES merging, MeZO/ZeroFlow forward-only optimization matching first-order on forgetting benchmarks).
- **Identification of sparse and empty coordinates as future directions.** The contrast between densely populated regions (parametric + post-training + off-policy/on-policy) and sparse ones (coordinated harness-parameter evolution, inference-time on-policy, multi-faceted long-horizon forgetting) is translated into concrete research opportunities.
- **An explicit editorial judgment on AGI priorities.** Argues that *continual learning for the frontier model* is the first priority while domain-specific CL is not, and gives an honest distance estimate: the memory-and-harness route is fast approaching the capability ceiling of a general assistant at the engineering level, but genuine AGI (autonomous capability expansion, cross-domain transfer, robust long-horizon decision-making) remains a considerable distance away and will not be closed by longer context, larger memory, or more skills alone.

## Methodology

This is a **survey / position paper**, so its methodology is taxonomic and synthetic rather than experimental. The construction proceeds in four steps:

1. **Exposing three implicit assumptions of classical CL.** Section 2 revisits the classical formulation (a sequence of tasks T1…TK with distributions Dk and losses Lk, evaluated on the stability–plasticity trade-off and inter-task generalization) and the four classical method families (replay, gradient-based, architecture-based, regularization-based). It argues all four silently fix three answers: (i) learning happens during a dedicated training stage, (ii) capability is carried by parameters, (iii) updates are gradient-based on off-policy data.
2. **Converting assumptions into questions.** Each fixed assumption is relaxed into a variable, yielding the When, Where, How questions. A method is then characterized not by one immutable grid point but by a **When–Where–How profile** that may change over time (e.g., SKILL0 migrates from harness-skills to parameters).
3. **Per-axis literature survey.** Sections 3.2–3.4 trace each axis through representative methods, drawn from continual pre-training, post-training alignment, test-time training, the LoRA/PEFT family, agent memory systems, skill libraries, prompt/protocol evolution, on-policy RL alignment, and gradient-free learning. Methods are placed in Figure 2/3 by their *most salient* carrier or mechanism.
4. **Cross-dimensional joint-space analysis.** Section 3.5 (and Appendix Table C.1) records combined profiles, explicitly noting that Figure 2 is illustrative rather than the output of an exhaustive enumeration protocol, so region density should not be read as a statistical estimate.

The synthesis is anchored on a small set of theoretical/empirical landmarks used repeatedly: the Bayesian view of CL as sequential posterior updating; Shenfeld et al.'s result that forgetting scales with KL divergence between fine-tuned and reference policy and that on-policy RL implicitly minimizes it; the distinction between genuine and "spurious" forgetting (Zheng et al.); and the observation that the post-training *pipeline* (not any single step) is the unit at which forgetting must be measured.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported.

As a survey the paper offers no original experiments; its "results" are organizing claims and syntheses of cited findings. The most quantitatively flavored evidence it assembles:

- **On-policy learning is structurally aligned with CL's anti-forgetting objective.** Citing Shenfeld et al. (2025, "RL's Razor"): the degree of forgetting depends on the KL divergence between the fine-tuned and reference policy on the new task; on-policy RL implicitly prefers the solution with smallest KL among the many that satisfy a new task, so its drift stays small, whereas *off-policy supervised fine-tuning can converge to distributions arbitrarily far from the reference model* — a qualitative but decisive delta between the two regimes on the same alignment benchmarks.
- **Zeroth-order optimization matches first-order on forgetting.** Citing ZeroFlow: across forgetting benchmarks, the implicit smoothing of zeroth-order (forward-only) updates *matches or exceeds* first-order fine-tuning at mitigating catastrophic forgetting — an intrinsic stability property the authors explicitly parallel to on-policy RL's small-KL behavior.
- **Forgetting intensifies with scale in the 1B–7B range.** Citing Luo et al.: during continual fine-tuning of LLMs, domain knowledge, reasoning, and reading comprehension all degrade as instruction tuning proceeds, with the effect *intensifying with model scale* — a scale-dependent hazard for the post-training pipeline. The TRACE benchmark (cited) consolidates this, showing sequential training erodes not only general ability but also instruction following and safety alignment.
- **Continual pre-training can be near-lossless.** Citing Ibrahim et al. (SimpleCPT): combining LR rewarming with a small replay buffer of base-distribution tokens lets **405M and 10B** models absorb new corpora while keeping degradation on the original distribution *close to negligible*.
- **Spurious vs. genuine forgetting is partly recoverable.** Citing Zheng et al.: freezing bottom layers largely prevents the recoverable (task-alignment) component of measured degradation, refining what had been over-counted as forgetting.
- **Taxonomic density findings (qualitative).** The dense region of the joint (When, Where, How) space is *parametric + post-training + off-policy/on-policy gradient*; the sparse/empty regions — flagged as opportunities — are *coordinated harness-parameter evolution*, *inference-time on-policy* (only TTRL is highlighted), and *unified multi-faceted forgetting metrics* across the four forgetting faces (parameter overwrite, memory retrieval decay, context window overflow/attention dilution, skill/protocol context drift).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (taxonomy).** The When/Where/How framing is genuinely useful and more inclusive than the standard replay/regularization/architecture/gradient taxonomy reproduced in most LLM-CL surveys (e.g., Wu et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2024 — both cited). Treating the agent *harness* (memory/skills/protocols, following Zhou et al. 2026 [33]) as a first-class capability carrier inside a CL taxonomy is the paper's strongest conceptual move. However, the individual axes are not themselves new — on-policy-vs-off-policy forgetting, test-time training, and harness externalization each have their own prior surveys — so the contribution is the *joint coordinate system*, not any single axis.
- **Rigor.** The paper is admirably explicit about the limits of its own figures: Section 3.5 states Figure 2 "do[es] not result from an exhaustive literature-enumeration protocol" and that empty regions should not be read as evidence no work exists. This honesty is commendable but also means the dense/sparse claims are illustrative, not bibliometric — a reader cannot cite "X% of methods are parametric" from this paper.
- **Reproducibility.** N/A in the experimental sense (no code, no experiments). The taxonomy is operational enough to apply: a reader can assign a When–Where–How profile to a new method. But the placement rules ("most salient carrier or mechanism") are subjective, and the paper concedes "alternative classifications are equally reasonable" for cross-axis methods.
- **Coverage and balance.** The Where axis is unevenly developed: memory and skills get rich, well-populated lineages; **protocols** is acknowledged as "at an early stage" with essentially Promptbreeder + rule files + message-format schemas — thinner than the other two carriers, and a reader may feel the tri-partite harness split is asserted more than demonstrated for protocols. The How axis over-weights on-policy RL given the authors' own affiliations with RL-alignment work; the "frontier model is the first priority" judgment in 4.5, while clearly flagged as a judgment, reads as an editorial stance that domain-specific CL practitioners would contest.
- **Missing quantitative meta-analysis.** For a survey of a field with many benchmarks (TRACE, MMLU-drift variants, forgetting benchmarks, long-horizon agent benchmarks), there are **no comparative tables of method vs. method**, no pooled numbers, no leaderboard snapshot. The paper relies on verbal characterization ("matches or exceeds," "close to negligible," "intensifying with scale"). A reader wanting to know *which* continual-FT method actually wins on TRACE is not answered here.
- **Missing ablations in the conceptual sense.** The framework claims three axes are "complementary perspectives rather than mutually exclusive categories," but does not test whether a 2-axis (or 4-axis) framing would do better — e.g., a *Who* axis (which actor updates: model / human engineer / RL loop) is arguably doing hidden work in Section 4.2's distinction between "externally editable" and "self-accumulated" capability.
- **Significance.** As a roadmap and teaching reference it is high-value; it will likely be cited as the canonical "LLM-era CL is system-level, not parameter-level" framing. As a source of actionable empirical guidance it is weaker.
- **Red flags.** None in the integrity sense. The main risk is over-reliance on a handful of landmark results (Shenfeld et al. on KL; Luo et al. on scale-dependent forgetting) that are themselves recent preprints and not yet independently consolidated; the survey's edifice would weaken if those landmarks revise.

## Strengths

- The tri-axial When/Where/How frame is the cleanest unified organization of LLM-era CL currently available; it makes the field's scatter legible.
- Excellent pedagogical narrative tying each axis to a *structural reason* it matters (KL-minimization for on-policy; retrieval decay for memory; context-window ceiling for inference), not just a category label.
- The SKILL0 "harness-to-parameters trajectory" and the When–Where–How *profile that changes over time* are important conceptual refinements — methods are not frozen grid points.
- The Section 4.2 argument that "harness engineering functionally substitutes for, but does not mechanistically constitute, continual learning" — with three concrete reasons (externally-edited vs. self-accumulated; immature closed-loop methods; context-coupled transfer) — is the paper's most provocative and useful analytical contribution.
- The four-faces-of-forgetting recasting (parameter overwrite / memory retrieval decay / context window overflow / skill-protocol context drift) is a genuinely novel synthesis that reframes forgetting as multi-layer active compression rather than a parameter catastrophe.
- Honest self-limitations section (boundary crossings; rapid iteration; evaluation in flux) and an honest AGI distance estimate.

## Limitations

- **No quantitative meta-analysis.** No comparative method-vs-method tables on standard benchmarks; all comparisons are verbal. A survey positioned as a roadmap would benefit from at least one pooled-evidence table.
- **Uneven carrier development.** The "protocols" carrier is markedly thinner than memory and skills; the tri-partite harness claim rests largely on Promptbreeder + engineering practice (Cline/Aider/Cursor rule files), which is more assertion than lineage.
- **Non-exhaustive, author-curated method set.** The paper explicitly disclaims exhaustive enumeration, so density claims are illustrative; readers cannot use Figure 2 as bibliometric evidence.
- **Subjective placement rules.** "Most salient carrier or mechanism" is judgment-dependent; the paper concedes alternative classifications are equally reasonable, which weakens the taxonomy's discriminative force.
- **Editorial stance on AGI priorities.** The "frontier model first, domain-specific CL not" judgment, while clearly labeled as a judgment, is contestable and partly reflects the authors' institutional positioning; it is not derived from the survey evidence itself.
- **Dependence on recent, unreplicated landmarks.** Several keystone claims rest on 2025–2026 preprints (Shenfeld et al., ZeroFlow, SKILL0, AgentEvolver) that have not yet been independently consolidated; the framework's narrative would shift if those revise.
- **Evaluation gap is acknowledged but not filled.** Section 4.4 correctly identifies long-horizon agents as the real testbed and the absence of trajectory-level metrics, but offers no concrete evaluation protocol — explicitly deferred to future work.

## Relevance

与本仓库兴趣（LLM 持续学习、智能体 self-evolving 系统、推理期适应、记忆/技能/harness 工程等）高度相关：它正是把"持续预训练、后训练对齐遗忘、测试时训练、agent 记忆/技能库/提示演化"这些被分散研究的主题放进统一坐标系的工作，可作为该方向后续选型与方法定位的顶层导航。尤其 Section 4.2（harness 工程是否已等同持续学习）与 4.3（参数与 harness 的协同演化）直接命中"自演化智能体"研究路线的核心争议。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a **single forgetting metric** be defined that covers all four faces (parameter overwrite, memory retrieval decay, context overflow, skill/protocol drift), or do they require fundamentally incompatible measurement protocols? The paper calls for unified criteria but proposes none.
- The paper claims on-policy RL is structurally anti-forgetting due to small KL (Shenfeld et al.). **Does this advantage survive when the reward model itself drifts across post-training stages**, or does drift simply migrate from the policy to the reward model?
- SKILL0 demonstrates harness→parameter internalization. **What is the inverse mechanism** — making latent parametric capability explicit as a retrievable memory/skill item — and can it be done without expensive distillation?
- Section 4.3 proposes that capability scheduling across carriers is analogous to a **cache/memory hierarchy**. Could existing OS cache-replacement theory (LRU-K, adaptive replacement) be imported to give a formal scheduling framework, or are the access patterns too task-dependent?
- The "frontier model first" AGI judgment assumes capability **pools across all copies** of an online-learning model (cited from [181]). How sensitive is that argument to the cost of synchronization and to negative-transfer between heterogeneous deployment contexts?
- Long-horizon error accumulation (Section 4.4) is identified as the core difficulty, yet most harness operations are **non-differentiable**, blocking gradient-based sensitivity analysis. Could zeroth-order or heuristic-learning estimators (the same beyond-gradient family surveyed in 3.4.2) provide a tractable error-accumulation model for agents?
- The protocols carrier is acknowledged as immature. **Is there a principled reason protocol evolution is harder than memory/skill evolution**, or is this an artifact of the field's recency — i.e., is there a Voyager-equivalent baseline still missing for protocols?
