> **一句话总结：** 本文为随机化局部保形预测（RLCP）给出了关于"已实现的"局部预测集的有限样本高概率界，证明条件覆盖误差与相对 oracle 的长度误差在已实现辅助中心附近一致受控（固定得分下为 h^β 局部偏差 + n·p_min·h^d 标定波动；学习得分下因 pivotality 消去偏差、按线性方式引入训练误差），并在合成与真实数据上印证了该速率。

> **TL;DR:** For randomly localized conformal prediction (RLCP), the paper proves finite-sample, high-probability bounds — uniform over the realized localization ball B(x̃, h) — on both the conditional-coverage gap and the oracle-relative length error, decomposing them into an O(h^β) localization bias plus an O((n·p_min·h^d)^{-1/2}) calibration term for fixed scores (rate n^{-β/(2β+d)} at the balanced bandwidth on the 1-D cusp model with fitted slope −0.285/−0.320/−0.325 vs. theory −0.250/−0.300/−0.333), and removing the bias via a pivotal target (CQR, PIT) so the learned-score bounds (Theorems 6–7) add the sup-norm score-estimation rate linearly; real-data UCI benchmarks show RLCP lifts the worst density-decile coverage vs. Split-CP (e.g., +0.163 on bike-synth, +0.045 on protein) at a length cost of 1.02–2.63×.

## 核心贡献

- 针对固定非一致性得分 S，给出了 RLCP 已实现预测集在辅助中心 x̃ 邻域 B(x̃, h)∩X 上一致成立的有限样本、高概率联合界，同时控制条件覆盖误差与相对条件得分 oracle 的长度误差。界 (1) 分解为局部化偏差 h^β 与标定项 (log(4/δ)/(n·p_min·h^d))^{1/2} + (log(4/δ)/(n·p_min·h^d))，显式揭示带宽的偏差–方差权衡。
- 推导出平衡带宽 h★ = (log(4n)/n·p_min)^{1/(2β+d)}，在该带宽下界的主阶为经典的非参数速率 n^{-β/(2β+d)}（仅带对数因子），并给出局部有效样本量 n·p_min·h★^d 的显式形式。
- 针对在独立训练样本上学得的得分 Ŝ，引入"oracle pivotality"条件（A4，要求存在一个共同的 x 无关阈值 τ★⋆ 使 F_{S⋆|X}(τ★⋆|x) = 1−α），证明 pivotality 消去了局部化偏差，使学习得分的界（Theorems 6–7）退化为标定项 + 线性进入的 sup-范数训练误差 ε_∞^Δ(m,δ)，将标定误差与得分估计误差干净地解耦。
- 在三类得分上验证全部抽象假设：残差得分 S_res（Proposition 4）、保形分位数回归得分 S_cqr（Proposition 8，pivotal τ★⋆ = 0）、概率积分变换分布得分（Proposition 9，pivotal τ★⋆ = (1−α)/2），并给出由局部多项式分位数估计器与 Nadaraya–Watson 条件 CDF 估计器推出的训练速率 ε_∞^Δ ≲ (log(cm/δ)/m)^{β/(2β+d)}。
- 合成实验用 1-D 尖点模型 Y = |X − x_0|^β + ε（ε ~ Unif[−B, B]）印证理论速率；真实数据实验在 5 个 UCI 回归数据集（concrete, bike, protein, airfoil, bike-synth）上比较 RLCP 与 Split-CP、Split-CQR，量化局部化校准在密度十分位上的覆盖–长度权衡。

## Key Contributions

- First finite-sample, high-probability bounds for the *realized* RLCP prediction set (not its population/local-population average), stated uniformly over every x ∈ B(x̃, h) ∩ X on a single calibration event of probability ≥ 1−δ, jointly controlling conditional coverage (Theorem 3) and oracle-relative length (Theorem 2).
- An explicit bias–variance decomposition A_{n,h}^{cal}(δ) + h^β, where the localization bias h^β reflects β-Hölder variation of the conditional score law (generalizing the Lipschitz assumption of Min et al. 2023) and the calibration term A_{n,h}^{cal}(δ) = √(log(4/δ)/(n·p_min·h^d)) + (log(4/δ)/(n·p_min·h^d))^{1/2} is governed by the local effective sample size n·p_min·h^d. The balanced bandwidth recovers the n^{-β/(2β+d)} pointwise nonparametric rate up to log factors.
- A learned-score theory (Theorems 6–7) that, under the new "oracle pivotality" condition, removes the h^β localization-bias term and propagates the sup-norm score-estimation error ε_∞^Δ(m, δ) *linearly* — improving the score estimator immediately sharpens the localized guarantee at the same rate. The threshold comparison uses only the localized L1(Λ_{x̃,h}) average of the score error; set comparison adds a pointwise term.
- Concrete verification of all structural assumptions for three canonical scores (residual, CQR, distributional/PIT), with explicit density-minorization constants (e.g., κ_{α,u,h}^{(S_cqr⋆)} ≥ μ_low, κ = 2 for the PIT score) and worked training-rate derivations from local-polynomial quantile and Nadaraya–Watson CDF estimators via uniform Bahadur expansions and VC/Rademacher + Bousquet bounds.
- Numerical diagnostics that (i) reproduce the predicted oracle rate on a 1-D cusp model with fitted log–log slopes, and (ii) quantify the coverage–length trade-off of a hybrid RLCP against Split-CP and Split-CQR on five UCI/semi-synthetic datasets, with paired-bootstrap CIs, Holm-adjusted randomization p-values, and bandwidth-sensitivity sweeps.

## Methodology

The setting is split conformal prediction with an i.i.d. calibration sample D_n^{cal} of size n and an independent test pair (X_{n+1}, Y_{n+1}). RLCP (Hore & Barber 2025) draws an auxiliary localization centre X̃_{n+1} from the convolution kernel H_h(X_{n+1}, ·) = h^{-d} K((x̃−x)/h) dx̃, then performs weighted conformal prediction with weights w_{x̃,h}(X_i). The *reverse-law identity* P{X_{n+1} ∈ A | X̃_{n+1} = x̃} = Λ_{x̃,h}(A) is the key probabilistic device: conditional on the realized centre, the test covariate follows the localization-weighted law, which yields marginal validity while enabling a *conditional-on-x̃* analysis that retains the randomness that determines the RLCP set.

The analysis conditions on x̃ and bounds two quantities uniformly over x ∈ B(x̃, h) ∩ X: (a) the conditional-coverage error |P{Y ∈ Ĉ_α^R(x; x̃) | X = x} − (1−α)|, and (b) the length error ||Ĉ_α^R(x; x̃)| − |C^{(S)}(x, q_{1−α}^{(S)}(x))|| relative to the infeasible conditional score-oracle region. Structural assumptions include: **P1** a lower-bounded design density p_X ≥ p_min > 0 with (c_reg, r_0)-regular support; **K1** a bounded, symmetric, compactly supported kernel with an interior lower bound; **A1(S)** a uniformly upper-bounded conditional score density; **A2(S)** β-Hölder continuity of the conditional score CDF; **A3(S)** Lipschitz continuity of the score-set length in the threshold.

The proof builds a single calibration event Ω_{n,x̃,h,δ}^{cal} (probability ≥ 1−δ) on which the kernel-weighted empirical CDF concentrates uniformly around the localized population CDF F_{x̃,h}^{(S)} with rate ε_{n,h}(δ) (Eq. 37) plus a "+∞ mass" correction ρ_{n,h}. A quantile-bracketing argument (Lemma 28) then converts CDF-band proximity into a quantile bound via the localized score-density floor κ_{α,x̃,h}^{(S)}. The Hölder condition turns the localized average (over B(x̃, h)) into the h^β bias. For learned scores, conditioning on an independent training fold lets the fixed-score bound apply to Ŝ, and Lemma 16 bounds the localized Kolmogorov perturbation between the learned and target score CDFs by σ_up · ||Δ_{S⋆}||_{L1(Λ_{x̃,h})} ≤ σ_up · ε_∞^Δ(m, δ/2).

## Results & Highlights

- **Fixed-score oracle rate (Figure 1, d=1, α=0.1, R=300):** at the balanced bandwidth h = n^{−1/(2β+d)} on the cusp model Y = |X−x_0|^β + Unif[−3,3], least-squares log–log fits give slopes −0.285 (±0.009), −0.320 (±0.010), −0.325 (±0.010) for the conditional-coverage gap at β ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1}, against the theoretical leading exponents −β/(2β+d) = −0.250, −0.300, −0.333; the absolute set-length error shows the *same* fitted slopes (−0.286/−0.320/−0.325) because in this model it is a deterministic rescaling of the threshold error. The +∞-threshold frequency is 0 across the grid.
- **Bandwidth bias–variance balance (Figure 2, R=1000):** the empirical minimizer h★(n) follows the predicted balance −1/(2β+1), with fitted log h★ vs. log n slopes −0.50 [−0.53,−0.49], −0.40 [−0.41,−0.38], −0.37 [−0.39,−0.34] (90% bootstrap CIs) against theory −0.500, −0.400, −0.333 — the first two lie inside their CIs.
- **Uniform score-estimation rate (Table 1):** for the local-linear CQR pair the fitted sup-norm error slope vs. m/log m is −0.293±0.008 (β=0.5) and −0.403±0.007 (β=1) against theory −0.250 and −0.333; for the Nadaraya–Watson CDF estimator −0.261±0.005 and −0.370±0.005 against the same theory. Grid refinement to 900/1800 nodes leaves slopes unchanged to 5×10^{-4}.
- **Real-data conditional coverage (Table 2, target 0.90, 20 random 50/25/25 splits):** hybrid RLCP attains marginal coverage 0.918/0.908/0.907/0.919/0.910 on concrete/bike/protein/airfoil/bike-synth. It improves the worst density-decile coverage vs. Split-CP by +0.054 (concrete), +0.039 (bike), +0.045 (protein), +0.041 (airfoil), and +0.163 (bike-synth); Holm-adjusted paired randomization p < 0.05 on bike, protein, airfoil, and bike-synth.
- **Length cost of localization (Table 2):** mean-length ratio vs. Split-CP is 2.63 (concrete), 2.07 (bike), 1.02 (protein, essentially parity), 2.15 (airfoil), and 1.51 (bike-synth). On protein Split-CQR is actually shorter than Split-CP (ratio 0.91) with a negligible worst-slice gain (−0.004), identifying it as the strongest global baseline there.
- **Fallback frequency:** the complete formal-RLCP +∞ event (W_cal < 9·W_test) occurs for 16.26% (concrete), 5.78% (bike), 3.91% (protein), 8.95% (airfoil), 6.25% (bike-synth) of test points, of which the empty-window sub-event is <0.6% in every case — the hybrid (global-CQR fallback / y-range cap) materially departs from formal RLCP, so the real-data results are diagnostics, not verifications of the theorem.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The genuine advance over Min et al. (2023) is the *mode of control*: a single calibration event of probability ≥ 1−δ on which bounds hold uniformly over the *realized* neighbourhood B(x̃, h) ∩ X, plus the addition of an oracle-relative *length* bound — Min et al. give only conditional-miscoverage averaged over calibration and localization randomness. The paper is honest that this is "not an unconditional rate improvement" and that the two analyses are complementary; the learned-score pivotality decomposition (separating calibration from linear training error) is the more conceptually novel contribution.
- **Rigor.** Proofs are fully nonasymptotic with explicit sample-size thresholds n_1(h, δ, α), n_2(h, δ, α), n_1^⋆, m_Δ (Eqs. 45–46, 59–60). Assumptions are verified, not assumed, for all three score families (Propositions 4, 8, 9) with explicit constants. A notable gap is the common-support assumption P2′ (bounded-above-and-below conditional density on a fixed [−M, M]) needed to make the length bound informative; the appendix relaxation to local ρ_0-neighbourhoods of the two quantiles helps but excludes pure thin-tailed Gaussian Y on the whole real line with uniform constants.
- **Reproducibility.** Strong: a single global seed (20260710), recorded package versions (Python 3.13.2, NumPy 2.2.3, SciPy 1.15.2), an implementation cross-check (RLCP threshold computed two algebraically-equivalent ways, agreeing to machine precision), a supplementary computational archive with PARAMS.md and requirements-lock.txt, and bootstrap CIs / Holm-adjusted p-values for the real-data study.
- **Significance.** The results clarify *when RLCP tracks the oracle* (pivotal score, large m, bandwidth as large as density minorization permits — at which point it approaches marginal split-conformal). This is an actionable design insight for practitioners, but the real-data experiments show the practical regime is narrow: the length premium is 2×+ on 3 of 5 datasets, and the worst-slice gains, while statistically significant, are modest (≤0.054) on the real datasets.
- **Red flags / weak points.** (i) The real-data RLCP is a *hybrid* with two fallback rules (global-CQR for empty windows, y-range cap for insufficient-weight windows) that the theorems do not cover, and the fallback frequency reaches 16.3% on concrete — so the headline coverage/length numbers are empirical properties of the hybrid, not of formal RLCP. (ii) The bandwidth h = 2·median pairwise distance · n^{−1/(2+d)} is a fixed heuristic with a hand-set constant 2; the bandwidth-sensitivity sweep (Figure 7) shows worst-slice coverage is sensitive to this choice (peaks near c=1, the reported study uses c=2). (iii) No comparison against *other* localized/conditional conformal methods (Guan 2023 localized CP, MX/W-CP) — only against global Split-CP and Split-CQR — so the "is RLCP the best local calibration rule?" question is unaddressed. (iv) The experiments are restricted to d=1 synthetic and ≤12-dimensional tabular real data; no high-dimensional or modern-network evaluation (the paper explicitly notes a ReLU quantile network "plateaus in sup-norm and therefore cannot speak to (146), so it is not reported").

## Strengths

- Provides genuinely new finite-sample, *realized-set*, uniform-over-neighbourhood guarantees that prior work (Min et al. 2023, Hore & Barber 2025) did not supply, with a clean bias–variance decomposition that makes the bandwidth trade-off quantitative rather than folklore.
- The pivotality insight — that for CQR/PIT targets localization adds no bias and the bandwidth only governs effective calibration size — is a sharp, reusable structural observation that explains when local calibration is worth its cost.
- Assumptions are instantiated, not left abstract: three full propositions derive the constants (σ_up, L_S, κ_{α,u,h}, L_len) from standard conditional-density regularity, making the theory applicable rather than ornamental.
- Intellectual honesty is high throughout: explicit disclosure of hybrid-vs-formal-RLCP gaps, fallback frequencies, descriptive (not confirmatory) status of real-data inference, and a clear statement that the experiments "are not comparative benchmarks."

## Limitations

- The bounds are informative only when the localized score-density floor κ_{α,x̃,h}^{(S)} > 0; though Lemma 1 gives a sufficient condition, in practice this can fail at thin-tailed or multi-modal conditional score distributions, making the length bound vacuous (1/κ → ∞) even when the coverage bound remains useful.
- The common-support / uniform-density-floor assumption P2′ is strong: it excludes location-scale families with unbounded support under uniform constants (the appendix relaxation requires the floor only near the two target quantiles, but the global bound still needs it).
- The real-data evaluation is small-scale (≤12 covariates, histogram gradient boosting with 200 trees) and uses a hand-tuned, fixed-constant bandwidth; no image/text/high-dimensional regression, and no comparison with competing localized conformal rules beyond the two global baselines.
- All theoretical results are conditional on the realized auxiliary centre x̃ ∈ X; the paper notes that "membership of the auxiliary centre in X is not automatic near the boundary," so boundary behaviour of the auxiliary draw is not fully controlled by the fixed-centre theorems.
- The "+∞ mass" correction ρ_{n,h} is essential to the calibration event but produces conservative full-envelope sets in sparse regions; the real-data hybrid replaces this with two ad-hoc fallbacks whose coverage properties are only empirical.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the pivotality-based bias removal be extended to *approximately pivotal* learned scores (where ∆_{S⋆} is small but not zero), giving an adaptive h^β-then-ε_∞^Δ transition rather than the current binary fixed-vs-pivotal split?
- How does RLCP compare empirically against other localized conformal rules (Guan 2023 localized CP with level correction, Mondrian/conditional CP, W-CP under covariate shift) on the same UCI benchmarks, rather than only against global Split-CP/Split-CQR?
- Does the hybrid fallback (global-CQR for empty windows, y-range cap otherwise) preserve any finite-sample validity guarantee, or can a principled fallback be designed that remains distribution-free while avoiding the 2×+ length premium on datasets like concrete?
- Can the sup-norm training-rate assumption A5 (ε_∞^Δ → 0) be replaced by an L_2(Λ_{x̃,h}) or L_1(Λ_{x̃,h}) rate, which is all the threshold comparison actually uses, to admit estimators (neural quantile networks) that plateau in sup-norm?
- What is the behaviour of the realized-set bounds near the boundary of X, where the auxiliary centre X̃_{n+1} may fall outside the support and the reverse-law identity requires Z_{x̃,h} > 0 — can a boundary-corrected kernel recover the same n^{-β/(2β+d)} rate there?
