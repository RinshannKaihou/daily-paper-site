> **一句话总结：** 本文构造性地给出了第一个在任意噪声水平 L* 下都达到统计最优超额风险的二分类 PAC 学习器，其界 L(ĥ) ≤ L* + C(√(L*(d+log(1/δ))/n) + (d+log(1/δ))/n) 在通用常数意义下匹配 Devroye–Györfi–Lugosi (1996) 的下界，从而彻底解决了不可知 PAC 学习的样本复杂度问题。

> **TL;DR:** For any binary hypothesis class of VC dimension d, the authors give a deterministic improper learner that, from n i.i.d. samples, achieves excess risk L(ĥ) − L* ≤ 2·10⁸·(√(L*(d+log(48/δ))/n) + (d+log(48/δ))/n) with probability ≥ 1−δ — matching the Devroye–Györfi–Lugosi (1996) minimax lower bound (eq. 1) up to universal constants at every fixed L*, and strictly improving the prior best (Hanneke–Larsen–Zhivotovskiy, FOCS 2024), which carried an extra polylog(n/d) factor of log⁵(n/d) in the fast-rate term. The key new ingredient is a class-dependent edge-isoperimetric inequality on the Boolean cube (Lemma 2.1) that, combined with Long's agnostic one-inclusion-graph orientation and a suffix-averaging martingale argument, yields a coefficient-one leave-one-out bound on the empirical optimum.

## 核心贡献

- **彻底解决不可知 PAC 学习的最优样本复杂度问题**：在任意噪声水平 L* 下，构造出第一个达到统计最优超额风险的高概率 PAC 学习器，其风险界在通用常数意义下匹配 Devroye–Györfi–Lugosi (1996) 的极小极大下界，填补了 realizable（L*=0）与完全 agnostic（L* 为常数）两类已知最优结果之间的全部中间速率。
- **关键新工具——类相关的布尔立方体边等周不等式（Lemma 2.1）**：给出一组同时满足互补条件 w_{v,p}+w_{v⊕p,p}=1 与对所有坐标子集 D 成立的局部化界 Σ w_{v,p} ≤ ρ_F(v)+R_D(F) 的权重，将诱导边数同时通过 Hamming 距离 ρ_F 与投影 Rademacher 宽度 R_D 控制——这是把 VC 维引入证明的唯一位置。
- **系数为一的 leave-one-out 界（Theorem 2）**：得到 L̂^LOO ≤ L̂* + 120√(dL̂*/n) + 7202√(d/n)，经验最优项上的系数精确为 1，突破了 Long (1999) 原始立方体定向中系数为 15、从而无法得到系数为一的 leave-one-out 界的障碍。
- **无对数因子的去随机化**：通过对阈值化得分类做经验风险最小化，结合局部熵界（Lemma 3.3），在不需要额外 log(n/d) 因子的前提下把随机预测器转化为确定性二分类器；此前 HLZ (2024) 的结果恰好受困于这个 polylog 因子。
- **诚实披露 AI 辅助**：附录 A 详细记录了 Lemma 2.1 的发现过程中使用 OpenAI GPT-5.5 Pro / GPT-5.6 Sol 的情况（8 月 6 日 16 次 GPT-5.6 Sol Pro 试验中 11 次产出了基本正确的证明骨架，平均每次 123 分钟）。

## Key Contributions

- **Settles the sample complexity of agnostic PAC learning up to universal constants at every fixed L*.** The main result (Theorem 1) gives a deterministic improper learner achieving L(ĥ) ≤ L* + 2·10⁸·(√(L*(d+log(48/δ))/n) + (d+log(48/δ))/n) with probability ≥ 1−δ, matching the Devroye–Györfi–Lugosi (1996) minimax lower bound (eq. 1) that had been open for thirty years in the intermediate-noise regime.
- **A new class-dependent edge-isoperimetric inequality on the Boolean cube (Lemma 2.1).** A single weight assignment w_{v,p}∈[0,1] simultaneously satisfies the complementary condition w_{v,p}+w_{v⊕p,p}=1 for every vertex/coordinate and the localized inequality Σ_{p∈D} w_{v,p} ≤ ρ_F(v) + R_D(F) for every coordinate subset D, where ρ_F is Hamming distance to the trace and R_D is the projected Rademacher width. This is the single point where VC dimension enters the proof (via R_D(F) ≤ 60√(d|D|)).
- **A coefficient-one agnostic leave-one-out bound (Theorem 2, eq. 12).** L̂^LOO_S ≤ L̂*_S + 120√(dL̂*_S/n) + 7202√(d/n) — the leading coefficient on the empirical optimum L̂*_S is exactly 1, removing the coefficient-15 obstruction in Long's original cube orientation (1999).
- **Log-factor-free derandomization.** A reverse+forward martingale argument (suffix averaging, adapted from Aden-Ali–Cherapanamjerid–Shetty–Zhivotovskiy, FOCS 2023) converts the leave-one-out rule into a high-probability PAC bound for a randomized predictor (Theorem 3, constant 17000); a final ERM-over-thresholds step with a local entropy bound (Lemma 3.3, constant 223000) produces a deterministic binary classifier without the extra log(n/d) factor that plagued the prior best HLZ (FOCS 2024) bound.
- **Transparent AI-assistance disclosure (Appendix A).** The authors document that GPT-5.5 Pro / GPT-5.6 Sol were used to probe Lemma 2.1 on threshold/interval/rectangle VC classes; on Aug 6 2026, 11 of 16 GPT-5.6 Sol Pro runs produced essentially correct proof outlines for Lemma 2.1 plus the remaining steps, averaging 123 minutes per run.

## Methodology

The proof is a four-stage pipeline that converts a combinatorial cube-orientation result into a distribution-free PAC bound.

**Stage 1 — Class-dependent edge isoperimetry (Lemma 2.1).** Fix a finite class F⊆{−1,+1}^P with VC(F)≤d. Define ρ_F(v)=min_{f∈F}|DIS(v,f)| (Hamming distance to F) and the projected Rademacher width R_D(F)=E_ε max_{f∈F} Σ_{p∈D} ε_p f(p). A random-restriction argument draws a random coordinate subset T (each coordinate kept w.p. 1/2), sets π_T(v) to be the closest fit to v on T, and defines weights w_{v,p}=P_T(p∈DIS(v,π_T(v)) | p∉T). The complementary identity w_{v,p}+w_{v⊕p,p}=1 follows because v and v⊕p agree off p; the localized bound Σ_{p∈D} w_{v,p} ≤ ρ_F(v)+R_D(F) follows by rewriting the conditional expectation as 2E_T|DIS(v,π_T(v))∩D∩T^c|, applying the identity |B∩D∩T^c|=|B∩D∩T|+Σ ε_p 1{p∈B}, and recognizing the Rademacher average. Plugging in the chaining/covering bound R_D(F) ≤ 60√(d|D|) (the only use of VC dimension) gives the vertex-localized edge-count bound |E_U| ≤ Σ_{v∈U}(ρ_F(v)+R_{D_U(v)}(F)).

**Stage 2 — Hall orientation and leave-one-out (Theorem 2).** Using the standard Hall-capacity criterion (|E_G(W)| ≤ Σ_{x∈W} c_x ⇒ orient with outdegree ≤ c_x) with capacity ⌈ρ_F(v)+120√(dρ_F(v))+7202d⌉, the fractional weights induce a deterministic orientation of the full cube with out(v;σ) ≤ ρ_F(v)+120√(dρ_F(v))+7202d. Applied to the trace F={(h(x_1),…,h(x_n)):h∈H} at a fixed unlabeled sequence, the agnostic leave-one-out rule (Algorithm 1) predicts at the head of the held-out edge and yields n·L̂^LOO_S = out(v_y;σ) ≤ nL̂*_S+120√(dnL̂*_S)+7202d√n, hence eq. (12). The coefficient one on L̂*_S is the crux and is what prior Long-style orientations could not achieve.

**Stage 3 — Symmetrization + suffix averaging + martingales (Theorem 3).** The orientation is not permutation-invariant, so predictions are symmetrized by uniformly permuting (sample, query) and averaging. Suffix averaging over training prefixes S_{≤t}, t=k,…,2k−1, produces a randomized predictor p̂ (eq. 14). A reverse martingale (Lemma 3.1, exploiting permutation symmetry + Freedman's inequality) controls the realized held-out excess loss on the suffix by √(L(h)(d+log(1/δ))/k); a forward martingale (Lemma 3.2, Bernstein) lifts the sequential loss to population risk with comparator-dependent variance. Setting h=h* (the best-in-class) and union-bounding gives L(p̂) ≤ L* + 17000·(√(L*(d+log(3/δ))/k) + (d+log(3/δ))/k).

**Stage 4 — Determinization via thresholded-ERM (Theorem 1).** Split n into 2k−1 (predictor) + k (validation), k=⌊n/3⌋. Threshold p̂ at its distinct validation-block values to form a nested threshold class T_{p̂}, then ERM over T_{p̂}. Lemma 3.3 uses the nested/disjoint disagreement structure of the threshold chain to get a local Rademacher-style bound with constant 223000 and no log(n) factor. Combining with √(a+b)≤√a+√b and k≥n/6 yields the final constant 2·10⁸ in eq. (2); the abstract's 7·10⁸ absorbs the conversion d+log(48/δ) ≤ 27(d+log(1/δ)).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main theorem (Theorem 1, eq. 2):** For every class H with VC(H)=d≥1, every n≥1, every 0<δ<1, the deterministic improper learner achieves
  L(ĥ) ≤ L* + 2·10⁸·(√(L*(d+log(48/δ))/n) + (d+log(48/δ))/n)
  with probability ≥ 1−δ, without using L* or δ at training time. The abstract rounds the constant to 7·10⁸ to state the cleaner d+log(1/δ) form.
- **Matches the DGL (1996) minimax lower bound (eq. 1) up to universal constants at every fixed L*.** The lower bound states that for every learner (including randomized/improper) there is a distribution with minh∈H L(h)=L* for which L(ĥ) ≥ L* + c·(√(L*(d+log(1/δ))/n) + (d+log(1/δ))/n) with probability ≥ δ. Together the two results determine the distribution-free high-probability minimax excess risk for binary classification at every fixed L*.
- **Coefficient-one leave-one-out bound (Theorem 2, eq. 12):** L̂^LOO_S ≤ L̂*_S + 120√(d·L̂*_S/n) + 7202·√(d/n). The coefficient on L̂*_S is exactly 1 — compare Long (1999, Lemmas 15–16), whose distance-shell orientation gave a coefficient of 15 on ρ_F(v) and thus a coefficient >1 on the empirical optimum, blocking the optimal PAC conversion.
- **Randomized predictor bound (Theorem 3, eq. 18):** L(p̂) ≤ L* + 17000·(√(L*(d+log(3/δ))/k) + (d+log(3/δ))/k), the intermediate randomized guarantee feeding Theorem 1.
- **Strict improvement over the prior best upper bound (Hanneke–Larsen–Zhivotovskiy, FOCS 2024):** HLZ achieved L(ĥ) ≤ L* + C·(√(L*(d+log(1/δ))/n) + log⁵(n/d)·(d+log(1/δ))/n) — suboptimal by a polylogarithmic factor log⁵(n/d) in the fast-rate term; this paper removes it entirely. ERM itself is even worse, incurring a multiplicative log(1/L*) factor in the √-term and log(n/d) in the fast-rate term.
- **Orientation outdegree (Theorem 2, eq. 11):** out(v;σ) ≤ ρ_F(v) + 120√(d·ρ_F(v)) + 7202d, the deterministic cube orientation induced by Lemma 2.1.
- **Rademacher width bound (eq. 4):** R_D(F) ≤ 60√(d|D|), the single point at which VC dimension enters the entire proof.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — high, genuinely settles an open problem.** The intermediate-L* regime between realizable (L*=0, optimal PAC rate known since Hanneke 2016 / Simon 2015) and fully agnostic (L* bounded away from 0, optimal d/n rate classical) had no matching upper bound. The DGL (1996) lower bound (eq. 1) gives the correct target everywhere; whether any learner could attain it was explicitly open. The new ingredient (Lemma 2.1) is a real conceptual advance — a single weight assignment that is simultaneously a fractional orientation (eq. 5) and a localized isoperimetric inequality (eq. 6) for *every* coordinate subset D, which is exactly what is needed to feed the vertex-wise choice D=D_U(v) in the Hall argument. Remarks 2.2–2.3 carefully position it against four partial precedents (Haussler–Littlestone–Warmuth, Long, Asilis et al., Dughmi–Kalayci–York), and the differentiation is substantive, not cosmetic.
- **Rigor — very high.** Every numerical constant is traced through (60, 120, 3601, 7202, 17000, 223000, 2·10⁸, 7·10⁸); the four-stage proof is modular and each stage's hypotheses are explicit. The Hall-capacity step, the reverse/forward martingale steps (Freedman, Bernstein), and the local-Rademacher thresholding step all cite their sources. The supplementary Appendix A's full GPT prompt is a laudable reproducibility gesture.
- **Reproducibility — mixed but appropriate for theory.** The learner is fully specified (Algorithm 1, the suffix-averaging predictor in eq. 14, the thresholded-ERM final step), but it is **not computationally tractable**: orienting the full Boolean cube over the trace is exponential in n, and ERM over an exponentially large class is implicit. The paper is about *statistical* (information-theoretic) optimality, not computational complexity, and it is honest about this — but readers should not expect an implementable classifier. No code is provided, which is standard for pure-learning-theory papers.
- **Significance — high within learning theory; modest outside it.** This is a keystone result for the PAC/VC sample-complexity program, but the practical gap over the previous best (HLZ 2024) is "only" a polylog(n/d) factor, and all constants (2·10⁸) are far from the lower bound's universal constant c. The practical impact on how people train or evaluate real classifiers is essentially nil; the value is in closing the theoretical gap.
- **Red flags / things to probe.** (1) The final constants are enormous (2·10⁸ in Theorem 1, 223000 in Lemma 3.3) and the analysis makes no attempt to tighten them — the 1/3 sample split for validation alone inflates constants by ≥3. A reader interested in tight constants will find little optimization here. (2) The improvement over HLZ (2024) is the removal of log⁵(n/d); whether a sharper analysis of HLZ's own construction could already have removed some of those logs is not discussed. (3) The comparison to "ERM incurs log(1/L*)" is made only in passing (Section 1) without a formal statement, so the precise ERM lower bound being improved upon is not pinned down. (4) The derandomization step (Stage 4) is the most technically heavy and the constants blow up most there (17000 → 2·10⁸); a reader skeptical of the final number should focus verification effort on Lemma 3.3 and the √(a+b)≤√a+√b combination in the Proof of Theorem 1. (5) The paper is entirely binary-classification; the multiclass extension (referenced via Asilis et al. 2024) is not addressed.

## Strengths

- Resolves a clean, well-known, thirty-year-old open problem with a single, reusable new tool (Lemma 2.1) rather than an ad hoc construction.
- The proof is modular: the cube-isoperimetry lemma, the leave-one-out bound, the martingale PAC conversion, and the derandomization are cleanly separated and each is independently useful.
- Honest, constant-by-constant accounting; the role of every numerical constant is traceable, and no step hides a "≤C" that secretly depends on d, n, or L*.
- Transparent, detailed AI-assistance disclosure (Appendix A, including the full prompt and the 11/16 success rate) — exemplary for the current moment.
- The learner is improper and distribution-free, uses neither L* nor δ, and works for every n≥1 (with a trivial fallback for n<6).

## Limitations

- **Constants are huge and unoptimized** (2·10⁸ in the main theorem; 223000 in the thresholding lemma). The 1/3 validation split and loose √(a+b)≤√a+√b steps each cost factors; the paper does not try to minimize them.
- **The learner is computationally intractable**: orienting the trace's Boolean cube is exponential in n, so this is an information-theoretic existence result, not a practical algorithm.
- **Binary classification only.** Multiclass and regression analogues are not treated; the multiclass one-inclusion formalism (Asilis et al. 2024) is cited but not extended.
- **No experiments**, which is expected and acceptable for a pure sample-complexity paper but means the large constants are never sanity-checked empirically.
- **The gain over the prior best (HLZ 2024) is a polylog factor.** Practically this is small; its importance is entirely theoretical (closing the gap to the DGL lower bound).
- **The ERM lower-bound comparison** (Section 1) is informal — no precise theorem is stated for the claimed log(1/L*) ERM overhead, so a reader wanting that as a baseline must reconstruct it from the cited Boucheron–Bousquet–Lugosi (2005) survey.

## Relevance

该论文属于"ML Reliability & Mathematical Foundations"方向——为 PAC/VC 学习模型给出了一个尖锐的、分布无关的形式化统计保证，并彻底匹配了 1996 年的下界，是统计学习理论中最核心的样本复杂度问题之一。它不直接涉及 LLM 训练稳定性、推理评测或隐状态可解释性，但其"在所有噪声水平下达到最优超额风险"的结论，为理解分类器在任意标签噪声下的最坏情况可靠性提供了理论基础，对关注 ML 系统形式化边界与失效模式的分析具有背景价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the constant 2·10⁸ in Theorem 1 be substantially reduced by avoiding the 1/3 validation split — e.g., via a self-bound or a single-sample derandomization that reuses the same data for the score and the threshold?
- Is there a computationally efficient learner achieving the same optimal rate, or can one prove a computational–statistical gap showing that exponential-time orientation is necessary for coefficient-one leave-one-out?
- Does Lemma 2.1 extend to multiclass (k-ary) one-inclusion graphs to give the optimal agnostic multiclass PAC rate, and does the projected-Rademacher-width term generalize cleanly to the Natarajan/graph dimensions?
- The paper notes that even in the realizable case some optimal-in-expectation one-inclusion rules have tails no better than Markov's (Aden-Ali et al. 2023). Does Lemma 2.1's localized isoperimetry explain *which* orientations have bad tails, and can it diagnose the failure mode directly?
- Given the disclosed role of GPT-5.6 Sol in conjecturing Lemma 2.1 (11/16 success), is the random-restriction weight construction in eq. (9) recoverable by a human from the four cited partial precedents alone, or is it genuinely an LLM-discovered combinatorial object worth adding to the standard learning-theory toolbox?
- Can the lower-bound constant c in eq. (1) and the upper-bound constant 2·10⁸ be brought within a small multiplicative factor, or is there a fundamental reason the optimal learner must pay a large constant?
