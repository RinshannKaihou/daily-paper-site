Methodology — a four-stage proof pipeline cube isoperimetry → PAC bound

STAGE 01
Class-dependent edge isoperimetry

Random-restriction weights on the Boolean cube satisfy a complementary identity and a localized inequality for every coordinate subset. The single place VC dimension enters.

Lemma 2.1 R_D(F) ≤ 60√(d|D|)
STAGE 02
Hall orientation + leave-one-out

Fractional weights induce a deterministic cube orientation; the agnostic LOO rule yields a bound whose leading coefficient on the empirical optimum is exactly one.

Theorem 2 · coef. = 1 out(v;σ) ≤ ρ + 120√(dρ) + 7202d
STAGE 03
Symmetrize · suffix-average · martingales

Permutation symmetrization + suffix averaging give a randomized predictor. A reverse (Freedman) then forward (Bernstein) martingale lift it to a PAC bound.

Theorem 3 · const. 17000 randomized predictor p̂
STAGE 04
Derandomize via thresholded-ERM

Threshold p̂ at its validation values, then ERM over the nested threshold class. A local-entropy bound removes the extra log(n/d) factor that plagued HLZ.

Lemma 3.3 · 223000 → final const. 2·10⁸

Strengths +

  • Resolves a clean, well-known 30-year-old open problem with one reusable new tool (Lemma 2.1), not an ad hoc construction.
  • Modular proof — isoperimetry, LOO bound, martingale conversion, and derandomization are each independently useful.
  • Honest, constant-by-constant accounting; no hidden “≤C” that secretly depends on d, n, or L*.
  • Exemplary AI-assistance disclosure (Appendix A): full prompt, 11/16 success rate, 123 min/run.
  • Improper, distribution-free; works for every n ≥ 1 (trivial fallback for n < 6).

Limitations –

  • Constants are huge and unoptimized — 2·10⁸ in the theorem, 223000 in the thresholding lemma; the 1/3 validation split alone inflates by ≥3.
  • Computationally intractable: orienting the trace's cube is exponential in n. Pure information-theoretic existence.
  • Binary classification only — multiclass (Natarajan/graph dimensions) and regression not treated.
  • No experiments; large constants never sanity-checked empirically (standard for theory, but notable).
  • Gain over HLZ (2024) is “only” a polylog factor; the ERM-lower-bound comparison is informal.

Open questions worth chasing ?

  1. Can 2·10⁸ be cut by avoiding the 1/3 validation split — e.g. a self-bound or single-sample derandomization reusing the same data?
  2. Is there a computationally efficient optimal learner, or can one prove a computational–statistical gap forcing exponential-time orientation?
  3. Does Lemma 2.1 extend to multiclass one-inclusion graphs — and does projected Rademacher width generalize to the Natarajan/graph dimensions?
  4. Does the localized isoperimetry explain which one-inclusion orientations have bad tails (only-Markov, per Aden-Ali et al. 2023)?
  5. Can the lower-bound constant c and 2·10⁸ be brought within a small factor — or must the optimal learner pay a large constant?
  6. Is the random-restriction weight construction human-recoverable from the four precedents — or genuinely an LLM-discovered combinatorial object for the toolbox?
