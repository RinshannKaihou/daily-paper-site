Predicting Task Difficulty Without Rollouts

Stefan Krsteski  ·  Charlotte Meyer
item-response theory token entropy agentic benchmarks difficulty prediction benchmark contamination AUC critique
Key finding

Token-level entropy from a small open-weight scorer predicts agentic-task difficulty without rollouts — but the field's favourite metric, AUC, hides how weak those estimates truly are.

Results & Highlights

结果与亮点
AUC inflation · demonstrated
0.806 AUC

A benchmark-average predictor reaches AUC 0.806 / raw ρ_β 0.519, yet its within-benchmark pairwise accuracy is pAcc = 0.000 — it cannot rank tasks inside any single benchmark.

Best rank recovery · full model
ρ=0.399

Combined feature set under K-fold (p<0.001 vs. baseline). Drops to ρ=0.225 under leave-one-benchmark-out. Length baseline only reaches 0.086.

Entropy beats length & embeddings
ρ=0.193

Token-level Shannon entropy from Qwen3-8B-Base is the strongest single feature family (vs. embeddings 0.107, length 0.086). Trajectory spread & shape add lift in-distribution.

Estimator ablation · no free lunch
ρ=0.475 / 0.295

Random forest gives best K-fold ρ=0.475 (pAcc 0.683); linear regression gives best LOBO ρ=0.295. No estimator closes the held-out-benchmark gap.

Strengths & Limitations

优势与局限 · 并列对照

Strengths 优势

  • Crisp AUC critique. Equation-level proof that AUC conflates agent-ability spread with task-difficulty spread; the benchmark-average estimator reaching ρ_β=0.519 / AUC 0.806 with pAcc=0.000 is a sharp illustration.
  • Broad coverage. 17 benchmarks across 8 families; a genuinely reusable artifact with IRT difficulties for 7,266 tasks.
  • Honest baselines. Publishes the constant / benchmark-average / shuffled rows a less careful paper would bury; explicit that LOBO is the open problem.
  • Concrete cost argument. ~25× fewer tokens than empirical estimation, assumptions stated.
  • Variational IRT with posterior uncertainty is better-founded than point MLE under this dataset's imbalance.

Limitations 局限

  • Modest absolute numbers. Best KF ρ=0.399, LOBO ρ=0.225; pAcc≈0.68 is unlikely to rank candidate tasks confidently in a new benchmark.
  • Diagnostics need rollouts. The r_t residual uses empirical β_t from full rollouts — §5.3 is detection, not pre-rollout prediction.
  • Tiny case study. Two SWE-bench examples is anecdotal; no retrieval evaluation (precision/recall) vs. the audit.
  • Scorer ceiling. Entropy from 7–8B open-weight models + a Claude trace that itself consumes tokens — a partial, cheaper rollout.
  • Missing ablations. No per-benchmark ρ breakdown, no reasoning-trace-length ablation, no frontier-scorer test.

Questions for Further Exploration

待深究的问题
01

Would a per-benchmark breakdown of ρ reveal which families (math vs. web navigation) drive the 0.399 aggregate and which drag the 0.225 LOBO number — and does entropy signal come from a subset of families?

02

If residuals were evaluated quantitatively against the full OpenAI audit set, what precision/recall would they hit at a given |r_t| threshold, and how does that compare to a perplexity-only contamination detector?

03

Does the Claude-Sonnet-4.6 reasoning trace leak difficulty that a strictly no-generation feature set would lack — i.e., how much signal survives using only C_t without R_t?

04

Would modeling the entropy profile as a time series (frequency-domain, 1D-CNN/RNN) materially close the KF→LOBO gap, or is the OOD ceiling set by benchmark-specific phrasing no entropy engineering can remove?

05

Could a hierarchical/multilevel IRT (benchmark-level mean + task-level deviation) replace within-benchmark standardization, and would that change transfer behavior?
