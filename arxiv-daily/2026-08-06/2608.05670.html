01

The measurement instrument

render-equivalence sets · exact answer key · togglable style factors

The core construct is a render-equivalence set: the same underlying data D is rendered under K=8 styles that do not change the encoded information — so every rendering shares one exact, programmatically known answer a*(D,q). Any answer change across the set is attributable to the model, not to a meaning change.

Two label-free quantities are defined on each set:

  • REA (render-equivalence agreement) — the modal answer's share among the K parsed answers. Upward-biased at finite K; the pairwise variant REApair is unbiased for the collision probability A = Σₐ P(a)².
  • RM (render marginalization) — mode (categorical) or median (continuous) aggregation over the K renderings.
  • Because the answer program exposes intermediate reads, every instance is partitioned into perception failures (≥1 read wrong) vs reasoning failures (all reads correct, answer wrong).

The formal analysis (Sec. 4) derives four pre-registered predictions (Table 2): aggregation helps wherever dispersion exists; the perturbation with the largest single-factor disagreement Dⱼ wins at matched budget; agreement need not beat evidence-carrying signals (Corollary 6); and an objective that raises concentration π while lowering diffuseness L destroys its own certificate (Prop. 5).

  • Reliability — re-rendering beats resampling (all 3 models). REA beats fixed-image self-consistency B3 by Δ = +0.102 AUROC (95% CI [+0.083, +0.121], p < 10⁻⁴); per-model lifts +0.061 to +0.157.
  • Accuracy — RM beats SC on every model. Qwen2.5-VL-7B +0.057 (0.701 vs 0.644), 3B +0.063 (0.626 vs 0.563), InternVL2-8B +0.048 (0.624 vs 0.576).
  • REA vs token-logprob (B1). 7B: 0.864 vs 0.771 (Δ +0.094); 3B: 0.858 vs 0.867 (statistical tie); InternVL2-8B: 0.907 vs 0.875 (Δ +0.033).
  • Dispersion concentrates in one factor. The plotting library dominates with lift 0.102 (CI [+0.084, +0.120]) — more than double bar_labels (0.043) and ~10× the noise floor. Every other factor's CI includes zero.
  • The inversion (Sec. 6.4). LoRA (r=8, 3 epochs) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with the model's own K=8 majority as the label drops RM accuracy in all 5 runs — by 3.6–21.2 pts on training families and 10.4–27.2 on held-out families. REA's AUROC rose on held-out families while falling on training ones: the model grew more confidently self-consistent on families it now answers worse.
  • Diagnostics. Render-flip rate 0.59–0.73, strongly negatively correlated with per-family accuracy (r = −0.69). ~4/5 of instances have ≥1 bad intermediate read; pure reasoning failures are rare. Median diffuseness L̂ = 1.33–1.50.
03

Strengths & limitations

what the instrument earns · what it does not yet establish

Strengths

  • A genuinely well-motivated measurement instrument: render-equivalence sidesteps the two obstacles (assumed meaning preservation, no exact answer key) that blocked directly measuring the agreement–accuracy coupling in natural-image work.
  • Pre-registered predictions tested against outcomes, with honest accounting — Table 2 marks which held and at what replication level.
  • Per-factor causal attribution (individually togglable styles) is more informative than "apply many augmentations and average."
  • A mechanism-explained negative result on consensus self-training that contradicts a published positive finding — a valuable boundary condition for test-time-scaling.
  • Full code & data release: pinned versions and seeds, MIT-licensed, CC-BY-4.0 data.

Limitations

  • Single in-style synthetic split — the out-of-style split and real-data anchor were designed but not run, so generalization to real charts is untested.
  • The dominant dispersion factor (plotting library) is the least "cosmetic" axis, and the library-held-fixed ablation that would isolate it is missing.
  • No approximate-perturbation arm — the central claim (exact equivalence beats ordinary pixel augmentation) is reasoned, not measured.
  • Standard label-free baselines omitted: no semantic entropy, no P(True), no direct run of Zhang et al. [1]; Jiang & Luo discussed but not benchmarked vs RM.
  • Narrow model coverage (3 models, 2024/early-2025, two from one family); the negative fine-tuning result is one model, one LoRA config — direction stable, magnitude unstable.
  • τ = 5% tolerance never varied; ~18 paired comparisons without multiple-testing correction; intermediate-read reliability unvalidated.
