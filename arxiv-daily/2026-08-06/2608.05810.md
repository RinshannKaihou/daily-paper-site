> **一句话总结：** 本文揭示自进化智能体的"能力-污染相变"现象——技能池超过临界规模后新技能反而降低性能，且污染因衍生技能的血缘传递而结构性不可逆（事后删除源头仅能挽回 17% 损失）；作者据此提出预提交门控机制 VaG（Cold→Warm→Hot 三级信任层级 + 三类异构评论家 + 边际增益子集选择），在 Terminal-Bench 2 上实现单调提升至 72% pass@1（比无门控峰值再高 10pp，技能池缩小约 5 倍），且冻结技能池可跨 5 个骨干模型与第二个基准正向迁移。

> **TL;DR:** The paper shows self-evolving LLM agents hit a "capability–contamination tipping point" on Terminal-Bench 2: ungated skill accumulation peaks at 62% pass@1 (R3, 105 skills) then collapses to 50% (R5, 179 skills), and post-hoc removal of harmful source skills recovers only 1.7pp of the 12.3pp drop because descendants inherited the flawed reasoning. Their Verifier-as-Gatekeeper (VaG) — three heterogeneous critics (schema / A-B replay / LLM semantic) at the Cold→Warm gate plus a marginal-gain greedy subset selection at Warm→Hot — improves monotonically to 72% pass@1 (+10pp over the ungated peak, +22pp over ungated R5) with a ~5× smaller pool, and the frozen pool transfers positively (+8 to +16pp) to four other backbones and to InterCode NL2Bash (69.0% vs ungated 65.5%).

## 核心贡献

- **现象的形式化：** 首次识别并形式化自进化智能体中的"能力-污染相变（capability–contamination tipping point）"。在 Terminal-Bench 2 上，无门控的技能累积呈现倒 U 形曲线——pass@1 从 R1 的 48% 上升到 R3 的 62%（临界规模 k\*≈105 技能），随后跌回 R5 的 50%，仅比自身 R1 高 2pp，尽管技能池已从 35 增长到 179。
- **三级污染分类法：** 将污染细分为个体污染（单个技能独立降低成功率）、组合污染（各自无害的技能 jointly 注入后冲突）、系统性污染（前两者在宏观上产出的非单调能力曲线）。
- **结构不可逆性证明：** 论证事后回滚无法挽回全部损失——后续技能是在含缺陷源技能的池子上蒸馏出来的，源技能的有缺陷推理通过血缘（descendant lineage）存活于从未点名它的后代中。源技能删除仅恢复 17% 的退化；即便 Oracle 全血缘清理也仍有约 5.6pp/12.3pp 不可恢复。
- **预提交门控方法 VaG：** 将技能准入视为显式"晋升决策"而非"写入"。三级信任层级 Cold ≺ Warm ≺ Hot；Gate 1 用三类异构评论家（SchemaCritic 确定性格式校验、ExecCritic 单技能 A-B 行为重放、AgentCritic 一次 LLM 语义审查）做合取过滤；Gate 2 用边际增益贪心子集选择消除组合污染。
- **实证：** VaG 单调提升至 72% pass@1，技能池仅 37（约 5 倍小），冻结的 Hot 池在 5 个骨干（Hy3、DeepSeek-V4-Pro、GPT-5.4、Claude Sonnet 4.5、Qwen3.6-35B-A3B）上正向迁移 +8 至 +16pp，并在 InterCode NL2Bash 上达 69.0%（优于无门控 65.5%）。

## Key Contributions

- **Phenomenon formalization.** Identifies and formalizes the capability–contamination tipping point in self-evolving agents. On Terminal-Bench 2, ungated pass@1 follows an inverted-U: 48% (R1) → 62% (R3, ~105 skills) → 50% (R5, 179 skills), ending only 2pp above its own R1 despite a 5× larger pool — empirically instantiating the critical size k\* of Equation (4).
- **Three-level contamination taxonomy.** Distinguishes individual contamination (a single skill lowers success on its own, g({s}, τ) < 0), combinatorial contamination (skills each harmless in isolation conflict once jointly injected, g({sa, sb}, τ) < 0), and the systemic non-monotonicity they jointly produce.
- **Irreversibility argument.** Proves post-hoc rollback is structurally limited: because skills distilled in round r are conditioned on M_{r-1}, removing only a source skill leaves its descendants intact, so R(M_r \ {s}) < R(M_r \ ({s} ∪ desc(s))). Source-only rollback recovers only 17% of the degradation; even an oracle with full provenance leaves ~5.6pp/12.3pp irrecoverable.
- **VaG method.** A pre-commit gating mechanism with a progressive trust hierarchy L = {Cold, Warm, Hot}. Gate 1 (Cold→Warm) conjoins three deliberately heterogeneous critics — a deterministic SchemaCritic, a behavioral ExecCritic (single-skill A-B replay on held-out tasks), and a single-call AgentCritic (LLM semantic review for fabricated/self-contradictory/unsafe advice). Gate 2 (Warm→Hot) is a marginal-gain greedy subset selection that admits a Warm candidate only if it strictly improves measured joint held-out performance — the only check that can detect combinatorial contamination.
- **Evidence.** VaG improves monotonically across all five rounds to 72% pass@1 (+10pp over the ungated peak at R3, +22pp over ungated R5), with a Hot pool of 37 skills (~5× smaller). A frozen pool transfers positively (+8 to +16pp) to four other backbones on Test-25 and to InterCode NL2Bash (69.0% vs ungated 65.5%).

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in three movements.

**1. Formalization and contamination taxonomy.** A self-evolving agent at round r solves tasks using pool M_{r-1} as decision context, then distills new skills S_r ~ π(· | M_{r-1}). Existing methods (Voyager, AHE, ACE, MetaClaw) all share an implicit lifecycle: every distilled skill is admitted via admission operator G, so M_r = M_{r-1} ∪ G(S_r), with ungated evolution being G = identity. Using a held-out task set D_holdout and an injection gain g(A, τ) = R(M ∪ A, τ) − R(M, τ), the authors define the three-level contamination taxonomy. The systemic effect yields a critical pool size k\* = argmax_k P(k) beyond which P(k) declines — the phase transition.

**2. Irreversibility argument.** Because S_r is generated conditioned on M_{r-1}, a defective skill admitted in round r belongs to the reference context of everything distilled afterwards. Defining desc(s) as the set of skills whose distillation context contained s, the recovery from source-only removal R(M_r \ {s}) is strictly dominated by full-lineage cleanup R(M_r \ ({s} ∪ desc(s))). Lineage cleanup is out of reach in practice because real skill libraries do not record which skills were in context when a given skill was written. The design implication: contamination must be intercepted *before* skills enter the runtime context — pre-commit gating is a structural necessity, not one option among several.

**3. VaG — pre-commit gating with a progressive trust hierarchy.** Every distilled skill starts Cold (recorded but invisible to the agent). Promotion is governed by two gates of increasing stringency:
- **Gate 1 (Cold→Warm): individual harmlessness.** Conjunction of three heterogeneous critics: SchemaCritic (deterministic frontmatter schema check, ν_struct ∈ {0,1}); ExecCritic (single-skill A-B replay on D_holdout, passing when R(M ∪ {s}) ≥ R(M)); AgentCritic (one LLM call flagging fabricated facts, logical contradictions, or unsafe operations). A skill must clear all three — chosen because they inspect disjoint failure surfaces (format, behavior, meaning), so a harmful skill must fool all three simultaneously. The two cheap filters (schema + replay) eliminate most candidates before any LLM is called.
- **Gate 2 (Warm→Hot): combinatorial safety via marginal-gain selection.** Because f(H) = E_τ[R(agent ⊕ H, τ)] is not globally monotone (adding a skill can lower it), the authors use a greedy that adds the Warm candidate with the largest estimated joint gain only when it strictly improves measured held-out performance, stopping when no remaining candidate helps. Each f(H) is the mean of k=3 held-out replays. With |W| ≤ 15 Warm candidates per round, a single greedy pass costs at most |W|−1 ≤ 14 joint replays.

At runtime only Hot skills are injected into the agent's system prompt; rejected skills never cross the pre-commit boundary and therefore never re-enter the next round's distillation context.

**Setup.** Primary benchmark is Terminal-Bench 2 (TB2), a verifier-checked suite of hard terminal tasks run inside sandboxed Docker containers and graded by a deterministic checker. Tasks are split by TB2 difficulty into Event (50, drives distillation), Holdout (14, serves A-B replays and marginal-gain estimates inside the gates), and Test (25, reserved for final evaluation). Agent is a hand-rolled ReAct loop; primary backbone is Hy3. Cross-model backbones are DeepSeek-V4-Pro, GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. Cross-benchmark transfer uses the 200 InterCode NL2Bash tasks.

## Results & Highlights

- **Phase transition is real and sharp (Terminal-Bench 2, Event-50).** Ungated pass@1 rises 48% (R1, 35 skills) → 60% (R2, 68 skills) → peaks at 62% (R3, 105 skills), then erodes to 52% (R4, 141 skills) and 50% (R5, 179 skills) — ending only +2pp over its own R1 despite a 5× larger pool. Seed baseline holds at 46%.
- **VaG converts the inverted-U into monotone improvement.** VaG rises every round: 52% (R1) → 58% (R2) → 62% (R3) → 68% (R4) → 72% (R5), with a Hot pool of 37 skills. At R5 VaG exceeds Ungated by 22pp and Ungated's best round (R3, 62%) by 10pp — gating beats both unchecked accumulation and oracle early-stopping.
- **Contamination bites hardest on Hard-tier tasks.** Ungated Hard-tier pass@1 peaks at 53% (R2) and erodes to 35% (R5); VaG lifts Hard to 59% at R5 — a 24pp margin over Ungated on the multi-step tasks where contamination chains are longest. Medium falls 65%→55% under Ungated but rises to 77% under VaG.
- **Post-hoc rollback cannot undo the damage.** Of the 12.3pp peak-to-R5 drop (62.3% → 50.0%), source-only removal recovers only 1.7pp (individual contamination), full-lineage oracle cleanup a further 5.0pp (combinatorial via descendants), and 5.6pp remains irrecoverable even under Oracle. VaG (72.0%) exceeds Oracle cleanup by 15.3pp. Source-only rollback recovers 17% of the degradation — the empirical signature of irreversibility.
- **Component ablation (Event-50, R5).** Removing the holdout replay costs −10pp (the only behavior-grounded check; without it a skill that reads well but silently lowers success passes Gate 1); removing the marginal-gain gate costs −8pp and balloons the Hot pool from 37 to 58 skills (direct evidence of combinatorial contamination); removing the semantic check costs −4pp; removing schema validation costs −2pp. The three critics intercept largely disjoint classes of harmful skills — none substitutes for another.
- **Cross-model transfer (Test-25, frozen Hy3-evolved R5 Hot pool, no re-evolution).** Positive lift on all five backbones: Hy3 +12pp (32%→44%), DeepSeek-V4-Pro +8pp (32%→40%), GPT-5.4 +12pp (44%→56%), Claude Sonnet 4.5 +8pp (48%→56%), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B +16pp (36%→52%). GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 reach 56%, above Hy3's own 44% — the gate-filtered skills encode model-agnostic engineering knowledge from which stronger backbones extract more.
- **Cross-benchmark transfer (InterCode NL2Bash, 200 tasks, frozen R5 pools).** VaG 37 skills → 69.0% (+11.5pp over Seed 57.5%); Ungated 179 skills → 65.5% (+8.0pp). Ungated stays net-positive on these short single-step tasks because contamination chains stay shallow — consistent with the Hard-tier finding.
- **Efficiency.** VaG per-trial token cost stays flat at 0.77–0.94M while Ungated's grows with its bloated pool up to 1.30M for worse accuracy — the gate buys accuracy and efficiency together. Gate 1's first two checks use no LLM calls; only the semantic critic consumes one inference per candidate, and Gate 2 reuses the Gate-1 replays.

## Authors & Credibility

All authors are from Tencent (industry research lab), with corresponding author Lan Xu and equal-contribution first authors Linfang Shang and (implied) co-first authors. The work positions itself explicitly against recent self-evolution systems (Voyaner, AHE/Agentic Harness Engineering (Lin et al. 2026), ACE/Agentic Context Engineering (Zhang et al. 2025), MetaClaw (Xia et al. 2026)) and engages seriously with the submodular-optimization literature (Nemhauser-Wolsey-Fisher 1978; Feige-Mirrokni-Vondrák 2011; Das-Kempe 2011; Mirzasoleiman et al. 2014). The framing — formalize a phenomenon, prove a structural necessity, then derive a method from the necessity — is methodologically clean. The honesty about statistical power (Wilson CIs ≈30pp wide, n=50, k=3; per-round point tests underpowered; claim rests on trajectory direction divergence) is unusually candid for the area.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine. The irreversibility argument (descendant skills conditioned on a contaminated pool inherit flawed reasoning that survives source removal) is a sharp structural insight and reframes skill admission from bookkeeping to a pre-commit necessity. The three-critic conjunction with disjoint failure surfaces, plus the marginal-gain gate for combinatorial safety, is a clean synthesis of submodular selection into agent pipelines that prior work (Voyager's heuristic dedup, AHE's deferred harness-level scoring, MetaClaw's weight-level meta-learning) does not offer.
- **Rigor.** Mixed. The formalization (Equations 1–5) is clean, but the central algorithmic claim — that f(H) exhibits "bounded submodularity" — is asserted, not proven; the authors themselves concede the greedy is "a heuristic rather than an algorithm with a worst-case optimality guarantee" because the utility surface is "neither known to be submodular nor monotone." This is honest but means the theoretical scaffold does not actually deliver approximation guarantees; the work rests on its empirical results.
- **Statistical power is the central weakness.** With n=50 distillation tasks, k=3 rollouts, and 95% Wilson CIs ≈30pp wide, per-round point comparisons are underpowered — the authors acknowledge this and rest the claim on trajectory-shape divergence (Ungated single-peaked about R3 vs VaG monotone non-decreasing across all five rounds). This is a defensible but weaker evidentiary standard. The Test-25 split (the honest held-out estimate) is only 25 tasks, so the headline cross-model and cross-benchmark deltas (+8 to +16pp) carry wide CIs that are not reported for those tables.
- **Scale and representativeness.** Deliberately small (50 distillation tasks) and single-domain (all TB2/NL2Bash tasks are shell/terminal). The authors explicitly position this as isolating the contamination phenomenon rather than chasing SOTA, and note ACE (68.9) and AHE (77.0) reach higher absolute numbers on TB2-89 with stronger backbones and ten rounds — "These are not head-to-head baselines: backbone, task suite, and round budget all differ." Fair, but it leaves open whether the tipping point and the 5× pool compression replicate at scale, on non-shell tasks, or with frontier distillation backbones. The distillation backbone "Hy3" is not a widely benchmarked frontier model, which limits comparability.
- **Reproducibility.** Concerns. No mention of code or skill-pool release. The Harbor TB2 runtime and the hand-rolled ReAct loop are described but not specified enough to reconstruct (seed skills, exact prompts, distillation prompt, AgentCritic rubric are relegated to a Supplementary Material that is not in the body). k=3 rollouts and a 14-task Holdout split make the marginal-gain estimates noisy, and the paper does not report variance for the cross-model/cross-benchmark tables.
- **Significance.** High if it replicates. The "admission is a pre-commit necessity, not a post-hoc fix" reframing is load-bearing for the entire self-evolving-agent research program and directly contradicts the unconditional-admission assumption shared by Voyager, ACE, and AHE. The 17% rollback-recovery number is a memorable, citable result.
- **Red flags.** (1) The headline 72% pass@1 is on Event-50 — the distillation split, which the authors themselves call "an optimistic upper bound"; the honest Test-25 number for VaG is not reported in the main text (only the cross-backbone transfers are, where Hy3 scores 44%). (2) The "5× smaller pool" comparison is at R5, the collapsed Ungated state, rather than against Ungated's peak at R3 (105 skills) — against the peak the compression is ~3×, still meaningful but less dramatic. (3) No failure-mode analysis of skills the gate wrongly rejects (false-positive rate of benign skills) is given, so the cost of gating beyond "a little while the pool is small" is under-characterized.

## Strengths

- The irreversibility argument (Eq. 5 and the descendant-lineage propagation) is a crisp structural result that reframes the problem and directly motivates the method — a tight theory-to-design loop rare in agent papers.
- The three-critic design is principled: each critic targets a disjoint failure surface (format / behavior / meaning), so the conjunction is empirically far stricter than any single dimension, and the ablation confirms mutual non-substitutability (removing replay −10pp, marginal-gain gate −8pp, semantic −4pp, schema −2pp).
- Honest about statistical limitations — explicitly states that per-round point tests are underpowered with n=50 and that the claim rests on trajectory-shape divergence, rather than burying the CI width.
- Computationally economical design: Gate 1's two cheap deterministic/behavioral filters reject most candidates before any LLM call, and Gate 2 reuses Gate-1 replays; per-trial token cost stays flat (0.77–0.94M) while Ungated's bloats to 1.30M.
- Cross-model and cross-benchmark transfer are the right generalization tests, and the positive lifts (especially stronger backbones extracting more from the frozen pool) support the claim that gated evolution distills model-agnostic engineering knowledge rather than backbone-specific artifacts.

## Limitations

- Small scale and single domain. Only 50 distillation tasks, all shell/terminal; whether the tipping point and the 5× compression replicate on coding, web, or multi-modal agent tasks, or at the 68.9–77.0 scale of ACE/AHE, is unaddressed.
- Statistical power. n=50, k=3, Wilson CIs ≈30pp; per-round point tests underpowered. The cross-model (+8 to +16pp) and cross-benchmark tables do not report CIs at all, and Test-25 has only 25 tasks.
- Bounded submodularity of f(H) is asserted, not proven, so the greedy has no worst-case guarantee; the authors acknowledge this honestly but it weakens the theoretical contribution.
- Headline 72% pass@1 is on Event-50 (the distillation split, explicitly an "optimistic upper bound"); the honest Test-25 number for VaG-in-isolation is not reported.
- No failure analysis of benign skills wrongly rejected by the gate (false-positive cost), so the "gating gives up a little while the pool is small" claim is under-characterized.
- Distillation backbone "Hy3" is not a widely benchmarked frontier model, limiting comparability and raising the question of whether stronger distillers produce fewer contaminated skills and thus a softer tipping point.
- No code or skill-pool release mentioned; key implementation details (distillation prompt, AgentCritic rubric, seed skills) are in a Supplementary Material not present in the main body.

## Relevance

这项工作直接落在自进化智能体、技能库与智能体记忆这一核心研究方向上——它对 Voyager / ExpeL / ACE / AHE 等无条件准入的技能蒸馏流水线提出结构性批评并给出预提交门控方案，对任何构建长期记忆、经验蒸馏或技能库的智能体系统都是必须正视的警示与方法参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the capability–contamination tipping point replicate at the scale of ACE (68.9) and AHE (77.0) on TB2-89, or does a stronger distillation backbone (e.g., GPT-5.4 instead of Hy3) push k\* further out or eliminate the inversion entirely?
- How does the false-positive rate of VaG (benign skills wrongly rejected) behave as the pool grows — does the marginal-gain gate become increasingly conservative and starve the pool of useful diversity?
- Can the lineage-tracking that VaG's irreversibility argument assumes is "out of reach in practice" actually be added cheaply (e.g., logging the in-context skill IDs at distillation time), and would full-lineage cleanup then close the 5.6pp irrecoverable gap?
- Does the three-critic conjunction generalize beyond shell tasks — e.g., to coding agents where "behavioral harmlessness" is harder to define, or to web agents where A-B replay is expensive?
- What is the interaction between pre-commit gating and weight-level self-evolution (e.g., MetaClaw's dual-timescale RL fine-tuning) — can the two be composed, or does gating at the skill level interfere with gradient-based skill internalization?
- The marginal-gain greedy assumes f(H) is approximately submodular; on what task families does this break (e.g., where skills are strongly synergistic rather than redundant), and what selection algorithm replaces it?
