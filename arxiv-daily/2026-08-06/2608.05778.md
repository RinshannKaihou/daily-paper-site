> **一句话总结：** 本文系统性地研究了"冻结的 prompt-side playbook（从源环境蒸馏出的提示侧操作手册）能否跨模型/领域/运行时迁移"，发现在 ALFWorld 上贪心解码下迁移有效（最强提升 +23.6 pp，且缩短轨迹），但在 TAU2-Bench 服务智能体场景经 Holm 多重校正后仅 1/135 条路线显著，在 XBench-DeepSearch 上 32K→128K 运行时偏移会导致重复查询、延迟停止与成本膨胀——结论是冻结迁移只是一个有条件的冷启动选项，而非可默认复用的策略。

> **TL;DR:** An empirical study of *frozen* prompt-side playbook transfer (distilled by Claude Opus 4.6 / GPT-5.4 from source runs, then deployed without target-side retuning) across three benchmarks. On ALFWorld under greedy decoding, transfer helps eight targets, reaching +23.6 pp success-rate lift on Llama3.1-70B, and a near-budget-matched distilled playbook beats a 5-shot prompt by +10.8 pp (72.3% vs 61.5%) on Qwen3-32B. On TAU2-Bench (135 route hypotheses), global Holm correction retains only 1 positive route; a prespecified matched-vs-mismatched contrast shows a modest +1.72 pp average domain-alignment advantage. On XBench-DeepSearch, a 32K→128K context-runtime shift destabilizes behavior: under Qwen3-32B 128K, search calls rise from 5.9 to 15.7 and the >30-tool-call rate from 8% to 25%. Verdict: frozen transfer is a conditional cold-start option requiring target-side validation, not a reuse-by-default strategy.

## 核心贡献

- 定义了一套面向部署的 **distill–validate–transfer**（蒸馏-验证-迁移）协议，用于评估"冻结的 prompt-side playbook"在跨目标模型/领域/家族/运行时下的可迁移性，而非提出新的学习算法。
- 在 ALFWorld 上给出受控存在性证明：贪心解码（t=0）下迁移在场景-OOD、跨规模、跨家族上均有效，且增益随目标模型能力提升而增强（最强 +23.6 pp），并伴随轨迹变短而非成本膨胀。
- 在 TAU2-Bench 上做了严格的服务智能体评估：对 135 条路线做 **global Holm 多重校正**，仅保留 1 条正面路线；同时通过预设的 matched-vs-mismatched 对比给出"匹配领域平均 +1.72 pp"的温和结论，避免了"playbook 普遍有效"的过度解读。
- 通过近预算匹配对比证明：在 Qwen3-32B 上，蒸馏出的 playbook（X7D，4191 字节）比同样源数据的 5-shot 固定示范（FS-5，4001 字节）高 +10.8 pp（72.3% vs 61.5%，McNemar p=.0139）。
- 揭示了运行时偏移下的**操作行为失稳**：XBench-DeepSearch 在 32K→128K 上下文切换后，Pass@1 混合但 Pass@3 下降 6-16 pp，重复查询率从 17.3% 升到 39.3%，并强调**目标侧重蒸馏并不自动更安全**（工具调用 +247%）。
- 给出部署检查清单：冻结目标切片与风险标准，验证准确率/终止/协议兼容性/成本，保留回滚路径——把"playbook 复用"重新定义为**验证问题**而非默认复用。

## Key Contributions

- Defines a deployment-oriented **distill–validate–transfer** protocol for evaluating *frozen* prompt-side playbooks (compact operational procedures distilled by a stronger teacher — Claude Opus 4.6 via Claude Code or GPT-5.4 via Codex — then deployed without target-side retuning), rather than proposing a new learning algorithm.
- Provides a controlled existence proof on ALFWorld: under greedy decoding (t=0), transfer succeeds across scene-OOD, scale, and family axes, with gains that grow with target capacity (best lift +23.6 pp) and that shorten trajectories rather than inflate cost.
- Conducts a rigorous, multiplicity-corrected service-agent study on TAU2-Bench: across 135 route hypotheses, global Holm correction retains only 1 positive route, while a separately defined matched-vs-mismatched contrast supports a modest +1.72 pp average matched-domain advantage (CI95 [+0.25, +2.97], p=.035).
- Shows distilled guidance beats near-budget-matched fixed demonstrations: on Qwen3-32B valid_unseen, X7D (4,191 bytes) scores 72.3% vs FS-5's 61.5% (4001 bytes) — +10.8 pp, McNemar p=.0139.
- Demonstrates runtime-shift destabilization on XBench-DeepSearch: a 32K→128K context change preserves some heuristics but causes re-querying, delayed stopping, and cost inflation (search calls 5.9→15.7; >30T rate 8%→25%), and shows target-side redistillation is *not* automatically safer (total tool calls +247%).
- Reframes playbook reuse as a **validation problem** rather than a reuse-by-default strategy, issuing a concrete deployment checklist (validate accuracy, termination, protocol compatibility, cost; keep a rollback path).

## Methodology

The paper studies **frozen prompt-side playbook transfer**. A "playbook" is a compact, prompt-side operating procedure (not a retrained policy or memory system) inserted into the agent prompt at deployment.

The shared **distill–validate–transfer** protocol has five steps:
1. Run the source agent K=8 times to collect trajectories.
2. Mine all-fail and mixed-outcome bundles (failure mining).
3. A stronger closed-source distiller (Claude Opus 4.6 via Claude Code, or GPT-5.4 via Codex) abstracts recurrent rules, search priorities, and recovery heuristics into a compact playbook.
4. Validate and refine the playbook on the source split.
5. Freeze it for transfer; audit under OOD / cross-domain / cross-family / runtime-shift conditions.

Three benchmarks play complementary roles:
- **ALFWorld** (controlled existence test): scene-OOD, target scale, cross-family transfer under matched decoding. Four artifacts studied: C7S/C32S (Claude-distilled), X7D/X7C (Codex-distilled detailed/compact). Metric: normalized success rate (environment-broken games removed); 400-run audit including t=0 vs t=0.3.
- **TAU2-Bench** (main service-agent benchmark): airline/retail/telecom, in-family and cross-family transfer. 81 in-family source–target–domain configs; 135 route hypotheses. Metric: corrected official-style Pass_k, with Pass1 as the sole route-level headline, **global Holm correction** applied; a separately defined matched-vs-mismatched aggregate contrast.
- **XBench-DeepSearch** (runtime-shift stress test): one Claude-distilled playbook from Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B 32K source trajectories, transferred across Tongyi-DR/Qwen3-32B × 32K/128K conditions. Metrics: Pass@1, question-keyed True Pass@3, deployment-side cost (search calls, tool calls, >30T rate).

Controls include no-playbook baselines, same-length generic placebos (structure-matched rewrites removing domain-specific content), and a near-budget-matched 5-shot comparator (FS-5). The authors explicitly track **operational cost** (message/turn/token counts, trajectory length, hit-50 rate) alongside task success.

## Results & Highlights

- **ALFWorld, greedy decoding (t=0):** Transferred playbooks help across scene-OOD, scale, and family. Best lift **+23.6 pp** success rate on Qwen3-32B unseen (X7D); gains grow with target capacity (modest on Qwen2.5-7B, uniformly positive on Qwen2.5-32B, strongest on Qwen3-32B and Llama3.1-70B). Gains are accompanied by trajectory shortening (e.g., up to −36% avg steps on Qwen3-32B seen) rather than cost inflation.
- **ALFWorld, distilled vs fixed few-shot:** On Qwen3-32B valid_unseen with near-matched budgets (4,191 vs 4,001 bytes), X7D succeeds on **107/148 (72.3%) vs 91/148 (61.5%)** for FS-5 — **+10.8 pp**, CI95 [+2.7, +18.9], exact McNemar p=.0139.
- **ALFWorld, decoding robustness (t=0 vs t=0.3):** Effect is decoding-dependent and capacity-dependent. On Qwen2.5-7B, X7D flips from positive at t=0 to harmful at t=0.3, with hit-50 rising from **58.1% to 79.8%** and mean trajectory length jumping **32.8 to 42.4 steps**. On Qwen3-32B and Llama3.1-70B, sampling instead improves the baseline and keeps all four playbooks positive.
- **TAU2-Bench, route-level:** Global Holm correction over **135 Pass1 routes retains only 1 positive route** (P14T→Q2.5-7B/T, +9.69 pp); family-specific Holm additionally retains 2 negative cross-family routes; global Benjamini–Hochberg retains all three only as an FDR sensitivity analysis.
- **TAU2-Bench, matched-vs-mismatched aggregate:** Matched-domain transfer is stronger by **+1.72 pp on average** (CI95 [+0.25, +2.97], exact blocked-permutation p=.035) — supports a modest average domain-alignment advantage, not improvement on most routes.
- **TAU2-Bench, same-length placebo:** On three selected routes, the structure-matched generic placebo stays near baseline (e.g., 55.2→57.1) while the transferred playbook reproduces the corresponding direction — weakening a pure prompt-length explanation.
- **XBench-DeepSearch, runtime shift:** Under same-context transfer, Pass@1 rises +4 to +8 pp and Pass@3 is flat or slightly lower. Under **cross-context (32K→128K)** transfer, Pass@1 is mixed (0 to +6 pp) but Pass@3 **falls 6 to 16 pp**. Under Qwen3-32B 128K, search calls rise **5.9→15.7**, total tool calls **10.4→24.7**, and overlong-trajectory (>30T) rate **8%→25%**.
- **XBench behavioral audit (150 pairs):** Trajectories with an identical repeated query rise **17.3%→39.3%**, those with ≥3 identical searches rise **8%→24%**, and submissions within 10 tool calls fall **78%→30%** — identifying re-querying and delayed stopping as the proximal pathway.
- **XBench, redistillation not automatically safer:** Target-side redistillation leaves Qwen3-32B 128K accuracy statistically unresolved while raising average total tool calls **10.4→36.1 (+247%)**, with ~24% of trajectories hitting budget.

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors (Weihong Lin, Lin Sun, Xiangzheng Zhang) are from Beijing Qiyuan Technology Co., Ltd. (a 360.cn-affiliated entity), positioning this as an **Industry Track deployment study** rather than a novel algorithm paper — a framing they are unusually explicit and honest about. The statistical hygiene is notably strong for an applied agent paper: they predefine the matched-vs-mismatched contrast (so it is not selected from the 135 route hypotheses), apply **global Holm correction** across all 135 routes, report CI95 intervals and exact McNemar / blocked-permutation p-values, and run a Benjamini–Hochberg FDR sensitivity analysis. They explicitly state that "failure to survive correction is not interpreted as evidence of zero effect," which is the correct frequentist framing. The distillers named are concrete (Claude Opus 4.6 via Claude Code; GPT-5.4 via Codex), and they include same-length placebo controls and near-budget-matched few-shot comparisons — methodological care that lends credibility to the modest, conditional claims.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The contribution is diagnostic, not algorithmic — the authors explicitly disclaim proposing a new learning algorithm. The novelty lies in the deployment-oriented framing (when does a *frozen* artifact transfer?) and in systematically coupling accuracy with operational cost. This is genuinely useful but incremental; the conceptual "operational-compatibility view" is intuitive rather than theoretically novel.
- **Rigor:** Statistically the strongest aspect. The global Holm correction over 135 routes is the right call and is rare in agent-transfer papers, and the separately-defined aggregate contrast avoids the garden-of-forking-paths problem. The same-length placebo (3 routes) and the near-budget few-shot comparison (X7D vs FS-5) are thoughtful controls.
- **Reproducibility concerns:** The reliance on **closed proprietary distillers** (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4) is a real reproducibility hazard — these are moving targets, and a playbook distilled today may differ from one distilled next quarter. The playbooks themselves are said to be included (Appendix B.3 gives P14R and an X7D excerpt), but the full artifact–trajectory provenance chain is not fully open. ALFWorld's two-implementation (Claude Code + Codex) design partially mitigates single-stack dependence.
- **Statistical power:** TAU2-Bench has **limited per-cell power** (acknowledged), and the headline "+1.72 pp average" rests on a single aggregate contrast. The XBench result is explicitly a **single artifact–runtime pairing** on 100 problems — the authors are admirably upfront that this is "not evidence for a general law," but it does limit how much one can generalize the runtime-shift claim.
- **Missing comparisons:** No comparison against **human-authored playbooks or deployment-time dynamic retrieval systems** (acknowledged limitation). The few-shot win is one artifact in one compatible setting — it does not establish cross-paradigm superiority. The cost-screening pilot (Gemini-2.5-Pro) is inconclusive: held-out inflation of +4.86 narrowly agrees on "no alert" but "demonstrates neither calibration nor a validated selector."
- **Red flags:** None severe. The paper is if anything *over-hedged* — the repeated "descriptive, not confirmatory" caveats are correct but make the practical guidance thin. The strongest deployment claim (the ALFWorld existence proof) is exactly the kind of clean, low-stakes benchmark where positive transfer is most expected, so readers should not over-extrapolate the +23.6 pp number to production service agents.

## Strengths

- Exceptional statistical honesty: global Holm correction, predefined contrasts, CI95 and exact p-values throughout, and an explicit refusal to treat non-retained routes as null effects.
- Tracks **operational cost** (tokens, turns, trajectory length, hit-50, >30T) alongside accuracy — a genuinely deployment-relevant axis that most transfer papers ignore, and the source of the most interesting finding (runtime-shift destabilizes stopping even when accuracy looks okay).
- The same-length placebo control is a clean way to separate content-driven transfer from format/prompt-length effects.
- Clear, actionable deployment checklist rather than vague "playbooks work" advice.
- The negative result on target-side redistillation (+247% tool calls) is a valuable and counterintuitive finding.

## Limitations

- TAU2-Bench per-cell power is low; the headline aggregate advantage (+1.72 pp) is modest and rests on one predefined contrast.
- XBench-DeepSearch is a **single artifact–runtime pairing** on 100 problems — the runtime-shift finding is a stress test, not a generalizable law.
- Distillation depends on **proprietary, version-sensitive models** (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4), threatening long-run reproducibility.
- No comparison with human-authored playbooks or dynamic retrieval systems; the few-shot win is a single compatible-setting comparison.
- Domains tested are embodied (ALFWorld), service (TAU2), and search (XBench) agents — software engineering, code generation, and data analysis are untested.
- The cost-screening pilot (Gemini-2.5-Pro) is inconclusive; no validated compatibility selector is provided.
- The paper is densely hedged, which is statistically virtuous but leaves the practitioner without a concrete decision threshold for when to freeze vs. redistill.

## Relevance

这项研究与"LLM 智能体的经验外化、提示工程、跨设置迁移与部署成本评估"高度相关：它直接回答了"从源环境蒸馏出的提示侧操作手册能否跨模型/领域/运行时复用"这一部署关键问题，并为评估智能体经验迁移提供了可复用的统计严谨范式（多重校正 + 成本审计 + 同长度安慰剂对照），对智能体系统的工程化落地与经验复用研究有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a **pre-deployment compatibility selector** (e.g., a cheap probe measuring behavioral boundedness on a small target slice) predict runtime-shift destabilization before committing to full rollout? The Gemini-2.5-Pro pilot suggests this is open.
- Does the ALFWorld capacity-scaling pattern (gains grow with target capability) hold on harder, longer-horizon benchmarks, or is it an artifact of ALFWorld's 50-step ceiling?
- How much of the TAU2 compatibility-sensitivity is driven by **tool-schema / API-protocol mismatch** versus workflow mismatch? A decomposition would make the deployment checklist actionable.
- Would **artifact ensembling or routing** (pick the playbook by target profile) recover the route-level gains lost to multiplicity correction?
- Is the XBench re-querying/delayed-stopping pathway a **context-length-OOD attention effect**, and does it disappear with position-encoding fixes or sliding-window attention?
- How do results change when the distiller is an open model (e.g., a strong open-weight LLM) rather than Claude Opus 4.6 / GPT-5.4, given version drift?
