> **一句话总结：** CircuitSteer 通过在 SAE 特征流电路上同时要求“特征共激活”与“解码器方向几何对齐”，构造跨多层、行为特异的稠密干预向量，从而在 Gemma-2-2B 与 Llama-3.1-8B 上成为唯一在 8 个“模型×数据集”组合中都能保持流畅度并仍有效转向的方法，并将 AdvBench 上的拒答率从 89% 降到 0%。

> **TL;DR:** CircuitSteer is a training-free, multi-layer steering method that builds a behavior-specific Sparse-Autoencoder "feature flow circuit" using joint feature co-activation and decoder-direction cosine-alignment, then injects dense per-layer vectors at every circuit layer. On Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct across RealToxicityPrompts, Jigsaw, Emotion, and Sycophancy, it is the only method among 8 baselines with a fluency-valid operating point on all 8 model-dataset cells (e.g., Llama RTP Δ=0.128 at PPL=0.51 vs. SAE-SSV Δ=0.125 at PPL=0.34, and on Gemma-Sycophancy Δ=0.057 where every baseline collapses), and it drops AdvBench refusal on Llama from 89% to 0% at λ=−3 where single-layer CAA is inert.

## 核心贡献

- 提出训练免（training-free）的 **CircuitSteer** 多层转向框架：以 SAE 为基础，先用“特征共激活 + 解码器方向几何对齐（cosine > τ_sim）”作为有向边条件构造跨层特征流电路 C = (V, E)，再用对比 specificity 评分（D+ 与 D− 上边频之差）抽取行为特异子电路。
- 证明 **几何对齐是稳定多层转向的必要条件**：消融显示去掉对齐（NoGeo）、反向对齐（NegativeAlign）、随机 SAE（RandomSAE）或限制为单层都会使流畅度坍塌或行为减少消失，尤其在 Gemma-Sycophancy 上只有完整方法有有效工作点。
- 将稀疏电路“重投影”为每层稠密转向向量 v_l = mean(d_{l,i})（按层平均解码器方向），用单一标量 λ 在所有电路层同时干预；这使得每 token 计算开销与 CAA 同阶，仅多了一次性的离线电路发现（Gemma-2-2B 上约 38 秒，10–12 GB 显存）。
- 在 2 个模型族、4 个行为基准、8 个基线上系统评测，**CircuitSteer 是唯一在全部 8 个“模型×数据集”单元都有有效工作点的方法**；并将方法扩展到拒答转向（AdvBench，Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct）：拒答率从 89% 降到 0%。
- 额外扩展到更大、不同 SAE 家族的 Qwen3.5-27B + TopK Qwen-Scope，所有电路发现超参沿用不变，在 RTP 上四个系数中三个领先且 PPL ≈ 1.0。

## Key Contributions

- A **training-free multi-layer steering framework (CircuitSteer)** that discovers behavior-specific SAE circuits via two joint edge criteria: feature co-activation (c_{l,i}, c_{l+1,j} > τ_act) and geometric alignment of decoder directions (cosine > τ_sim), combined with a contrastive specificity score S = P(edge | D+) − P(edge | D−) > τ_diff to isolate the target-behavior subcircuit.
- Empirical evidence that **geometric alignment is necessary, not merely beneficial**, for stable multi-layer steering: the NoGeo / NegativeAlign / RandomSAE / SingleLayer ablations collapse fluency or behavioral reduction, with Gemma-Sycophancy being valid only for the full method.
- A **dense-vector synthesis rule** that decouples magnitude from circuit size — each layer's steering vector is the mean of its participating decoder directions, applied at every circuit layer with one shared scalar λ — yielding per-token cost identical in form and order to single-layer CAA and an offline discovery cost of ~38 s on a single A100-40GB for Gemma-2-2B (10–12 GB peak).
- A **systematic 8-baseline, 4-dataset, 2-model-family benchmark** showing CircuitSteer is the only method with a fluency-valid operating point (0.01 ≤ PPL ≤ 1.5, Δ ≥ 0.02) on all 8 model-dataset cells, plus a refusal-steering extension on AdvBench where decision-point circuit discovery drops Llama's refusal rate from 89% to 0% at λ = −3.
- A **generalization probe** on Qwen3.5-27B (3.4× larger, different architecture, TopK Qwen-Scope SAEs) with circuit-discovery hyperparameters carried over unchanged, leading at 3 of 4 coefficients on RTP while holding PPL near 1.0.

## Methodology

CircuitSteer is a training-free, inference-time steering method built on pretrained residual-stream SAEs (Gemma-Scope canonical width 16k at layers {6,12,18,24}; Llama-Scope lxr 8x width 32k at layers {3,8,16,24,29}, all via SAELens).

The pipeline has three stages:

1. **SAE decomposition & graph construction.** For each input, the SAE encoder produces sparse feature activations c_l = JumpReLU(W_enc h_l + b_enc). A directed cross-layer edge (l,i) → (l+1,j) is added iff (i) both features are co-active above τ_act = 1.5, and (ii) their decoder directions d_{l,i}, d_{l+1,j} have cosine similarity > τ_sim = 0.10 (Eq. 2–3). This joint criterion approximates functional causality rather than gradient-based attribution (contrast with Marks et al. 2024).

2. **Contrastive circuit discovery.** Over target prompts D+ and contrastive prompts D−, compute the specificity score S(edge) = P(edge | D+) − P(edge | D−) (Eq. 4); retain edges with S > τ_diff = 0.05, keeping top K = 30 edges. This isolates the multi-layer semantic trajectory specific to the target behavior.

3. **Dense vector synthesis & multi-point intervention.** For each layer l in the subcircuit, collect features F_l participating in any retained edge (Eq. 5) and synthesize a dense steering vector v_l = (1/|F_l|) Σ d_{l,i} (Eq. 6). At inference, modify the residual stream h'_l = h_l + λ v_l at every circuit layer with a single shared scalar λ (swept over {−4,…,2}, reported at argmax Δ within 0.01 ≤ PPL ≤ 1.5). Averaging decoder directions (not activations) makes λ's effect consistent regardless of circuit size; applying aligned vectors at successive layers prevents the residual stream from "recovering" toward the unsteered trajectory between interventions.

A separate **decision-point circuit discovery** variant is introduced for refusal: SAE features are extracted at the generation step where the model commits to refusal rather than at the input-encoding step, which is needed because refusal is a discrete behavioral decision rather than a continuously distributed representation.

Datasets: RealToxicityPrompts (RTP, toxic > 0.85 / benign < 0.10), Jigsaw/Civil Comments (≥0.5 toxic / =0.0 benign), Emotion (anger suppressed against joy contrast class), Sycophancy (Anthropic multiple-choice survey, suppress agreement with user preference). 80% of examples for vector construction, 100 held-out prompts (50 target / 50 benign) for evaluation. Temperature 1.0, single NVIDIA A100 40GB. Toxicity scored via Detoxify; anger and sycophancy scored as the target-class probability.

## Results & Highlights

- **Coverage (the central claim).** Under the validity window 0.01 ≤ normalized PPL ≤ 1.5 with Δ ≥ 0.02, CircuitSteer is the **only** method with a valid operating point on all 8 (model × dataset) cells; every one of the 8 baselines (Prompt, CAA single/multi-layer, RepE, ITI, LoReFT, SpARE, SAE-SSV) shows at least one dash (no valid point) (Table 1).
- **Llama-3.1-8B RTP:** CircuitSteer achieves Δ = 0.128 at PPL = 0.51, the best behavioral reduction in that column; the next-best valid points are SAE-SSV (Δ = 0.125, PPL = 0.34) and CAA-multi-layer (Δ = 0.114, PPL = 0.83).
- **Gemma-2-2B Sycophancy:** CircuitSteer achieves Δ = 0.057 at PPL = 1.04, and is the **only** method in the entire Table 1 with any valid operating point for this cell — ITI, CAA, RepE, SpARE, SAE-SSV all dash.
- **Gemma-2-2B Jigsaw:** CircuitSteer leads at Δ = 0.072, PPL = 0.93; no other method produces a valid point in this column.
- **Refusal steering (AdvBench, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct):** decision-point circuit discovery drops the refusal rate from **89% (base) to 0%** at λ = −3 (Δ_refusal = 0.89, median PPL = 35.7), while single-layer CAA at the identical coefficient leaves refusal virtually intact (Appendix E, Table 10).
- **Statistical significance.** One-sided 95% bootstrap lower bounds confirm CircuitSteer's reductions are significant on **7 of 8** configurations; only Llama–Jigsaw is not separable from zero (Appendix C, Table 7).
- **General capability preserved.** Under strong steering (λ = −3), MMLU accuracy moves only −0.03 on both Gemma-2-2B (0.46 → 0.43) and Llama-3.1-8B (0.672 → 0.641), and GSM8K reasoning is retained (0.236 → 0.220 Gemma, 0.824 → 0.800 Llama) (Appendix K).
- **Benign-prompt collateral damage.** On benign inputs, CircuitSteer inflates median perplexity far less than CAA: 30.4 (CircuitSteer) vs. 41.8 (CAA-Single, λ=10) and 46.0 (CAA-Single, λ=15) against a base of 27.5 (Table 4).
- **Circuit compactness.** Behavioral reduction saturates at a few dozen edges: Δ_tox goes 0.169 (k=10) → 0.229 (k=30) → 0.242 (k=100) while median PPL rises from 31.6 → 38.4 → 45.3, so additional edges add fluency cost without proportional gain (Table 3).
- **Offline cost.** Full circuit discovery on a 500-example contrastive set takes **~38 s** for Gemma-2-2B on a single A100-40GB at 10–12 GB peak memory, on par with extracting CAA vectors over the same data; inference-time per-token cost matches CAA (Appendix A).
- **Generalization to Qwen3.5-27B.** With circuit-discovery hyperparameters carried over unchanged, CircuitSteer leads at 3 of 4 coefficients on RTP while keeping PPL near 1.0 (Appendix F, Table 11).

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from the University of Southern California (USC). The paper is a Preprint "Under review" (arXiv:2608.05732v1, 6 Aug 2026). Code is publicly released at the GitHub URL given in the abstract. The work builds directly on the established Gemma-Scope / Llama-Scope / SAELens infrastructure, which makes the experimental setup reproducible by anyone with an A100-class GPU. The framing is appropriately hedged: the authors explicitly note that co-activation and geometric alignment are "candidate indicators that approximate cross-layer causal influence rather than establishing it," and that causal evidence comes from the component ablations rather than from any per-edge guarantee — a more cautious stance than is typical in this subfield. Statistical significance is reported via one-sided 95% bootstrap lower bounds rather than bare point estimates. One concern is the heavy concentration of USC-affiliated self-citations (hardware timing prediction, financial time-series, defogging, MLLM aesthetics, etc.) that are unrelated to LLM steering; these inflate the reference list without strengthening the technical argument.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core idea — using decoder-direction cosine similarity to define cross-layer SAE edges — is concurrent with Laptev et al. (ICML 2025, "Analyze feature flow"), which the authors candidly cite. The delta over Laptev et al. is the addition of co-activation as a second edge criterion, the contrastive specificity score (Eq. 4), and steering via a single dense vector per layer with one λ rather than clamping individual features. These are reasonable but incremental deltas; the geometric-alignment insight itself is not original.
- **Rigor.** Strong points: a well-justified validity window (0.01 ≤ PPL ≤ 1.5, Δ ≥ 0.02) selected on disjoint data with both an LLM judge and human inspection, one-sided bootstrap significance on 7/8 cells, and component ablations (NoGeo / NegativeAlign / RandomSAE / SingleLayer) that genuinely isolate the load-bearing ingredients. The refusal result is reported honestly with the median PPL = 35.7 disclosed, not hidden.
- **Weak / missing baselines.** There is no comparison to activation-patching or path-patching circuit-discovery methods (e.g., Conmy et al. ACDC, Syed et al. EAP-IG), and no comparison to the concurrent Laptev et al. feature-flow steering despite citing it — a notable omission given that the central methodological claim is layered on top of that work. The "Prompt" baseline uses a single hand-written polite-tone instruction; a stronger instruction-tuned / system-prompt baseline (or a best-of-many-prompts baseline) would better calibrate the prompting floor.
- **Cherry-picking / scope concerns.** The headline "only method with valid operating points on all 8 cells" is true but partly an artifact of the chosen validity window and the Δ ≥ 0.02 floor; on the cells where CircuitSteer is not best (Gemma-RTP Δ=0.113 vs ITI 0.119; Gemma-Emotion 0.135 vs ITI 0.230; Llama-Emotion 0.091 vs RepE 0.147; Llama-Sycophancy 0.156 vs ITI 0.356), competitors achieve 1.3–2.3× larger Δ but dash on other cells, so the framing emphasizes coverage over peak performance. The Llama–Jigsaw result (Δ = 0.034) is the one cell not statistically separable from zero, which slightly weakens the "all 8 cells" claim in practice.
- **Data quirk.** The refusal base rate of 89% on AdvBench is computed via keyword-based refusal detection following Arditi et al.; the paper itself acknowledges this only captures a structural shallow refusal direction, and the Median PPL of 35.7 post-steering (vs 13.4 base) suggests the "compliant" outputs are measurably less fluent than base — a cost the headline "89% → 0%" underplays.
- **Reproducibility.** Good: code released, SAEs and models are public, hyperparameters (τ_act=1.5, τ_sim=0.10, τ_diff=0.05, K=30, layers enumerated) are all stated. The cross-seed robustness appendix (Appendix J) reports unchanged vectors and behavioral reduction across resplits.
- **Significance.** Real but moderate. The empirical demonstration that geometric alignment is necessary for multi-layer steering is a useful negative result for the field; the coverage advantage is genuine for practitioners who need a single method that does not dash. The method is unlikely to displace fine-tuning or RLHF for alignment at scale, but it is a credible inference-time tool.

## Strengths

- Honest framing of "training-free" (no gradient updates, off-the-shelf SAEs as prerequisites, not trained in-method) and of the causal-status caveat for co-activation/alignment edges.
- The ablation suite (NoGeo, NegativeAlign, RandomSAE, SingleLayer, plus matched-layer CAA in Appendix L) genuinely localizes which component carries the gain, and the RandomSAE collapse is a clean control showing it is the learned features, not the pipeline scaffolding.
- Strong collateral-damage analysis: MMLU and GSM8K are essentially unchanged under λ = −3, and benign-prompt perplexity is several times lower than CAA (Table 4) — directly addressing the "does steering break the model?" worry.
- Compactness and stability evidence (Table 3 saturation at ~30 edges; Appendix H threshold non-criticality; Appendix J seed robustness) makes the method look practically tunable, not brittle.
- Refusal result on AdvBench, while disclosely less fluent, is a striking demonstration that RLHF refusal is structurally shallow and corroborates Arditi et al.'s single-direction finding via a different mechanism.

## Limitations

- The geometric-alignment core idea is concurrent with Laptev et al. (ICML 2025); the delta is the co-activation criterion, contrastive specificity score, and dense-vector synthesis rather than the alignment principle itself.
- Headline coverage claim depends on the specific validity window (0.01 ≤ PPL ≤ 1.5, Δ ≥ 0.02) chosen on a held-out split; a different window could change which methods are "valid."
- On four of eight cells, CircuitSteer is not the best method by Δ (ITI or RepE lead by 1.3–2.3×) — coverage is purchased at the cost of peak behavioral reduction on individual tasks.
- The Llama–Jigsaw reduction (Δ = 0.034) is not statistically significant at the 95% level, so one of the eight "valid" cells is marginal.
- Refusal-steering median PPL (35.7) is meaningfully worse than base (13.4); the "89% → 0%" framing understates this fluency cost.
- Evaluation uses Detoxify for toxicity scoring (known to be miscalibrated) and keyword-based refusal detection; both are standard but coarse. The LLM-as-judge ablation uses gpt-5.4-mini and is only run on a subset, not as the primary metric.
- All experiments use pretrained SAEs of one specific width and layer set per model; sensitivity to SAE width, training corpus, or JumpReLU-vs-TopK choice is only partially probed (the Qwen3.5-27B TopK extension is one data point, not a systematic sweep).
- Heavy use of USC-affiliated self-citations unrelated to LLM steering (hardware timing, financial time-series, defogging, MLLM aesthetics) inflates the bibliography.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to mechanistic-interpretability-driven LLM alignment and to inference-time control of model behavior — both core interests. The SAE-circuit view of multi-layer steering, the geometric-alignment necessity result, and the refusal-direction fragility demonstration are all useful signals for anyone working on representation-level safety interventions or sparse-feature interpretability.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does CircuitSteer behave when the target concept is genuinely distributed across many layers (e.g., long-horizon planning, in-context-learning task inference) rather than relatively shallow behaviors like toxicity and sycophancy?
- Can the dense-vector synthesis (Eq. 6, a simple mean of decoder directions) be replaced by a weighted combination (e.g., by activation magnitude or causal influence à la Arad et al. 2025) without reintroducing destructive interference?
- How sensitive are the results to SAE width and to the choice of JumpReLU vs TopK vs ReLU SAEs — is the geometric-alignment criterion robust across SAE families, or does it depend on Gemma-Scope / Llama-Scope specifically?
- Does the contrastive specificity score (Eq. 4) generalize when D− is not a clean "benign" class but a confusable related behavior (e.g., steering away from sycophancy without suppressing legitimate agreement)?
- The matched-layer CAA ablation (Appendix L) shows layer count alone does not explain the gain — but does combining CircuitSteer's alignment criterion with attribution-based (gradient) circuit discovery (Marks et al. 2024) outperform either alone?
- The refusal result (89% → 0% on AdvBench) corroborates that RLHF refusal is shallow; does decision-point circuit discovery transfer to red-teaming settings beyond AdvBench, or is it specific to keyword-detectable refusal?
