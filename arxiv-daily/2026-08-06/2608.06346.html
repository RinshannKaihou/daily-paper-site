Macro-average over 7 datasets on the new TrajErrBench (486 human-annotated failed trajectories; κ=0.91 on τ²-Bench). Beats every frontier direct-prompting and multi-agent baseline.

Why this matters

The error-lifecycle framing and the 486-trajectory TrajErrBench are directly reusable for self-improving agents and process-supervision evaluation.

⚠ sharpest critique

Critical edge

Strip the state classifier and SWE-Bench Pro accuracy drops to 16.28 — below the 17.44 direct-prompting baseline — so the candidate detector alone is net-negative on long coding trajectories.

03Method · the error-lifecycle pipeline

A failed trajectory τ enters; a single critical (earliest decisive) error step exits — via three auditable stages instead of one brittle end-to-end judgment.

Stage 2 taxonomy · resolved × terminal-footprint → which errors survive into F(τ)?

discarded

Clean Resolution

Resolved cleanly, no terminal footprint. Not a candidate.

→ survives into F(τ)

Costly Resolution

Resolved only after burning >50% of the trajectory budget.

→ survives into F(τ)

Manifest Active

Unresolved, semantic footprint visible in the terminal state.

discarded

Latent Active

Unresolved but no irreversible / semantic terminal footprint.

Anti-hallucination guard

  • Every trigger carries qw (an erroneous commitment in the current step) and qr (a verbatim-citable violated reference).
  • If either cannot be quoted explicitly, the trigger is discarded — the ablation ties +4.99 AVG to this requirement.
  • Reference category c ∈ {Task Conflict, History Conflict, Intra-Step Conflict, Environment Anomaly}.

Notable design choices

  • Object-anchored clustering: triggers sharing a violated reference object are merged, avoiding double-count of one constraint surfacing in planning/action/verification.
  • Attribution ≠ earliest: an earlier budget-debt instance is not auto-chosen over a later semantic one unless the wasted budget plausibly caused the miss.
  • Backbone: Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking, temperature 0, shared across all baselines.

04Results & highlights

Load-bearing numbers, pulled out of the prose. Absolute accuracy stays modest — the story is the relative gap and length-robustness.

  • +8.42abs. pts
    Overall macro-average · 34.11% vs. 25.69% direct prompting (same Qwen3-235B backbone). Beats Gemini-3.1-Pro (31.29%), AgentDebugger (23.72%), CHIEF (18.77%), AgentRX (23.10%).
  • +13.00ALFWorld
    Longest-horizon wins · 26.00 vs. 13.00 on ALFWorld (avg 60 steps). On SWE-Bench Pro (avg 119.7 steps): 24.41 vs. 17.44.
  • −21.02ablation
    Compression is the single biggest lever · removing multi-granularity views drops AVG 38.58 → 17.56. Removing state classification → −7.31 overall, and on SWE-Bench Pro → 16.28 (below baseline).
  • +10.80oracle repair
    Downstream agent gain · GLM-5.1 average success 78.07 → 88.87 with TRAJDEBUG feedback; largest single-task lift Retail +11.40. Beats Vanilla Debug & Self-Reflection (both 85.57).
  • +5.70memory transfer
    The harder, realistic test · aggregating diagnoses into “failure memory” for held-out tasks adds +6.06 / +6.57 / +4.48 across Airline/Retail/SWE — where Vanilla Debug hurts Airline (−3.03).
  • 60.7%failure phase
    Reasoning dominates failures · 60.7% of critical errors (τ²-Bench) / 57.1% (SWE-Bench Pro). On SWE-Bench Pro, reason.WrongChoice alone = 40.7%.

05Strengths & limitations · side by side

No cards-inside-cards — two parallel panels. The same author team publishes AgentTIF / WhoAndWhen-adjacent work; MIT release promised.

● Strengths

  • Genuine reframing. The four-state taxonomy (resolved × terminal-footprint) is the novel core — prior work stops at candidate detection or uses graph connectedness as a proxy for terminal responsibility.
  • Compression is transferable. Multi-granularity views are the single largest contributor (+21.02 AVG) and a reusable technique beyond this paper.
  • Principled anti-hallucination. Hard verbatim-citation requirement, contribution quantified at +4.99 AVG.
  • Honest downstream eval. Both an idealized (per-trajectory oracle) and a realistic held-out failure-memory transfer test — rare in this literature.
  • Controlled comparison. All multi-agent baselines on the same Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking backbone at temperature 0.

▲ Limitations

  • Absolute numbers are low. Best system finds the exact critical step only 34% overall, 24% on SWE-Bench Pro — downstream gains hinge on “best detector vs. mediocre detector” being a small gap.
  • Annotation anchoring. Annotators got model pre-annotations plus full benchmark failure signals; model-vs-human exact-match 48.8–76.8% suggests real but uncontrolled anchoring. No anchoring-free control.
  • SWE-Bench Pro shrank 110 → 86 by dropping no-agreement trajectories — defensible but may inflate κ (0.67).
  • Cascading self-evaluation. “Resolved vs. active” is itself an LLM judgment; false negatives propagate into F(τ). No quantitative bound on the out-of-set path.
  • Small downstream samples (two τ²-Bench subsets, 100 SWE-Bench Verified), single actor (GLM-5.1), no confidence intervals or significance tests.
  • Domain coverage. Only customer-service tool use + Python repo repair; no scientific, math, or multi-modal failures.

06Open questions for further exploration

The sharpest threads a follow-up could pull — drawn from the paper’s own Critical Assessment and Questions sections.

i.
How much of TRAJDEBUG’s gain is simply multi-granularity compression (the +21.02 ablation)? Would handing baselines the same compressed trajectory close the gap further?
ii.
Can the four-state classifier be distilled into a smaller model so the pipeline runs inside a single rollout budget rather than as post-hoc analysis?
iii.
Does failure-memory transfer (+5.70% avg) hold when the actor is a frontier model (GPT-5.4, Claude Opus 4.6) where headroom is smaller?
iv.
Reasoning drives 57–61% of critical errors — can reason.WrongChoice / MissingAssumptionCheck subtypes become online guardrails that fire at generation time?
v.
Is the “not-just-earliest” attribution rule robust to adversarial trajectories where the earliest local error really is the principal cause but also a budget-debt instance?
vi.
Would from-scratch human annotation (no model pre-annotation) produce harder or easier TrajErrBench labels?
