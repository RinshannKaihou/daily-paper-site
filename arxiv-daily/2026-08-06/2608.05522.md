> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一种无方程的、周期感知的预测误差收缩方法，用 k 近邻预测器在相位一致的多步长上拟合几何平均预测误差的对数斜率，从而从短轨迹集合中估计主导负最大 Lyapunov 指数；在逻辑斯蒂映射上覆盖 112 个负值中的 92 个（MAE 0.0253，R²=0.886），在无不动点二维映射上三种标量观测给出 MAE 0.00879–0.01145、R² 0.983–0.986。

> **TL;DR:** The authors extend their earlier positive-Lyapunov-exponent forecast-error estimator to stable (contracting) regimes by adding two adaptations: forecast horizons synchronized to the detected orbit period, and a transient-length consensus rule. On the logistic map the method recovers 92/112 negative-exponent values (MAE 0.0253, R²=0.886), and on a 2D no-fixed-point map three independent scalar observables (xₙ, yₙ, xₙ²+yₙ²) achieve MAE 0.00879–0.01145 and R² 0.983–0.986, versus Jacobian/QR-based reference exponents. The method is equation-free (no Jacobian reconstruction or learned surrogate) and targets only the dominant observable contraction rate, leaving direct experimental validation for future work.

## 核心贡献

- 将作者前期用于正最大 Lyapunov 指数（LLE）的"预测误差增长"框架推广到稳定（收缩）动力学，首次用同一个观测量 `log E(h)` 在不同剖面选择规则下提取相反符号的斜率。
- 提出两个对负 LLE 估计不可或缺的机制性改进：(1) 预测步长与检测到的轨道周期 `p` 同步（`s ∈ {1, p}`），把振荡/多分支误差剖面压缩为单一收缩包络；(2) 跨多个瞬态长度 `Tⱼ` 的稳定斜率共识规则（≥3 个一致才判为 reliable，成对为 acceptable，孤立斜率被拒绝），用来规避"高 R² 但斜率错误"的早期瞬态陷阱。
- 采用 k 近邻（KNN）预测器加几何平均绝对误差的纯标量管线，不重建局部 Jacobian、不微分学习型替代模型，仅用样本外预测误差衰减直接读出收缩速率，并输出显式的 reliable/acceptable/unreliable 分级。
- 用一个无不动点的二维映射（Huynh 等 2019）作为更严苛的验证：对 `xₙ`、`yₙ`、`zₙ = xₙ²+yₙ²` 三个标量观测各自独立运行完整管线，三者结果的一致性直接检验了对观测函数的不敏感性。
- 给出了面向实验复现松弛场景的使用协议（重复扰动后的短响应记录），把方法定位为结构自由衰减/桥梁振铃等领域的方程无关收缩率估计器，而非模态阻尼替代品。

## Key Contributions

- Extends the authors' prior positive-LLE forecast-error-growth estimator to contracting dynamics, reusing the same observable (`log E(h)`) but fitting the opposite slope under different profile-selection rules, so one short-trajectory protocol supports both growth and contraction analysis.
- Identifies two adaptations that are essential (not merely helpful) for negative-LLE estimation: (1) period-aware horizons, where the forecast step `s` is set to the detected orbit period `p` so the same orbit phase is compared, collapsing an alternating or multi-branch error profile into a single contraction envelope; (2) a stable-transient consensus rule that groups slopes across several transient lengths `Tⱼ` and accepts the median only when at least three agree, explicitly rejecting isolated high-R² fits that have the wrong asymptotic slope.
- Delivers an equation-free, Jacobian-free, surrogate-free scalar estimator: a k-nearest-neighbor (KNN) predictor generates multi-horizon out-of-sample forecasts, the geometric-mean absolute error `E(h)` is fit in log-coordinates, and the output includes an explicit reliable/acceptable/unreliable reliability class rather than only a point estimate.
- Validates on a 2D map with no real fixed points (Huynh et al. 2019, period-two onset) by running the complete pipeline independently on three scalar observables `xₙ`, `yₙ`, and `xₙ²+yₙ²`; their agreement is a direct test of insensitivity to the observation function since no coordinates are combined during period detection or exponent estimation.
- Positions the method for experimental repeated-relaxation settings (structural ring-down, electromechanical recovery) via an explicit use protocol, while carefully framing it as an estimator of the "dominant observable contraction rate" rather than a claim that an LLE equals a damping ratio.

## Methodology

The pipeline operates on an ensemble of `N` short post-transient scalar trajectories `uᵢ(n)`, split into training and test subsets (70/30). For each candidate transient length `T`, a trajectory is represented by a history vector `vᵢ(T) = (uᵢ(T), uᵢ(T+τ), …, uᵢ(T+(Y-1)τ))` of length `Y` with history step `τ`. A KNN predictor (default `K=3`, `Y=1` for the maps) identifies the `K` training histories nearest to each test history and averages their future values. The per-trajectory forecast error at horizon `h` is `eᵢ(h) = |uᵢ(T+(Y-1)τ+h) − ûᵢ(h)|`, aggregated across the test ensemble by the geometric mean `E(h) = exp( (1/N_test) Σ log max[eᵢ(h), ε] )`. In a locally contracting regime, `log E(h) ≈ C + λ₁h` with `λ₁ < 0`, so the fitted slope estimates the dominant negative exponent.

Two adaptations distinguish the method from a naive sign-reversal. **Period-aware horizons** use a recurrence statistic `Q_p(T) = median|uᵢ(n+p) − uᵢ(n)|` over a short window for `p = 1, …, p_max`; the smallest period near the minimum of `Q_p` is selected and confirmed in a shifted window. Candidate profiles are then evaluated at `h_m = m·s` with `s ∈ {1, p}`, preferring `s = p` so the same orbit phase is compared. **Stable-transient consensus** evaluates several transient lengths `Tⱼ`; at each `Tⱼ` the profile must have negative slope, sufficient linearity, predominantly decreasing log-error, and limited contamination by exact-zero/numerical-floor errors. Slopes consistent across ≥3 transients yield a "reliable" median, a consistent pair is "acceptable," and an isolated slope is rejected — a rule the authors call essential in narrow periodic windows where a single visually convincing profile can be strongly biased.

The method requires neither the governing equations nor an analytical Jacobian; the only inputs are complete short realizations. Benchmarks are the logistic map `x_{n+1} = r·x_n·(1−x_n)` over `r ∈ [3.5, 4.0]` (reference exponents from the analytical formula) and the 2D no-fixed-point map `x_{n+1}=x_n+y_n`, `y_{n+1}=y_n−a|y_n|−x_n·y_n+b·x_n²−c·y_n²+d` with `a=0.01`, `b=d=0.1`, `c∈[1.7,2.0]` (reference exponents from QR accumulation of the Jacobian). Each scalar observable of the 2D map is processed by the complete pipeline independently.

## Results & Highlights

- **Logistic map, negative branch:** the method recovers 92 of 112 negative-exponent parameter values (82.14% coverage), with **MAE 0.0253, RMSE 0.0586, median absolute error 0.00488, and R²=0.886** against analytical reference exponents; six isolated single-transient fits were rejected for lacking genuine consensus, and the RMSE is dominated by a few deep/narrow stable windows and the strongly contracting interval start rather than typical error.
- **Transparent fixed-point check (logistic map, r=2.7):** with theoretical `λ₁ = log|2−r| = −0.35667494`, the estimator returned `λ̂₁ = −0.35667476`, an **absolute deviation of 1.8×10⁻⁷** with R² indistinguishable from unity — confirming the contraction mechanism on an intentionally simple case.
- **2D no-fixed-point map, three independent scalar observables:** MAE is **0.00909 for xₙ, 0.01145 for yₙ, and 0.00879 for xₙ²+yₙ²**, with corresponding **R² values of 0.9858, 0.9830, and 0.9852**, and coverage ranging from 83.23% to 90.40%; the close agreement across independently processed observables demonstrates near-invariance to the observation function.
- **Positive-branch context (ensemble reformulation):** applying the ensemble-based forecast-error-growth formulation to the logistic map's chaotic regime with `T=1000, Y=1, K=3` yields **MAE 0.00709, RMSE 0.00900, R²=0.9964** — within rounding of the R²=0.998 reported in the authors' prior single-long-trajectory work (Ref. [9]), showing the short-trajectory protocol preserves prior positive-LLE performance.
- **Identified failure mode (residual bias):** the main residual bias is positive (estimated exponents tend to be slightly less negative than references), and strongly stable/superstable neighborhoods are underestimable or missing because the ensemble collapses to a numerical floor before a long asymptotic profile can be measured — an identifiability limit at finite precision, not a statistical outlier.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** is moderate and honestly scoped: the paper does not claim to be the first to estimate negative Lyapunov exponents from data (it cites Yang–Wu nonlinear-mapping, Sun–Wu RBF-surrogate, and Koopman/phase-isostable precedents). The genuine contribution is the specific combination of (a) short-trajectory ensembles, (b) period-synchronized forecast horizons, and (c) the transient-slope consensus rule applied directly to forecast-error decay — explicitly avoiding Jacobian reconstruction or surrogate differentiation. The novelty is a design recipe, not a new theory of contraction.
- **Rigor** is uneven across the two benchmarks in a physically informative way. The 2D no-fixed-point map is the stronger result: three independently processed scalar observables agree to MAE 0.00879–0.01145 with R² 0.983–0.986, and the self-consistency across observables is a non-trivial internal check. The logistic-map negative branch is notably weaker (R²=0.886, only 82% coverage), with the RMSE dominated by deep/narrow stable windows where the method visibly underestimates the most negative exponents — the authors concede this is an "identifiability limit at finite precision," but it means the method's accuracy is worst exactly in the strongly contracting regime it is most interested in.
- **Missing baselines / ablations** are the most important weakness. The authors explicitly defer a "like-for-like numerical comparison with nonlinear local-mapping, surrogate-Jacobian, empirical Floquet, and Koopman estimators" to future work because those methods "require different data structures." This means the reader cannot judge whether the forecast-error approach beats or even matches Yang–Wu, Sun–Wu, or DMD-based decay-eigenvalue estimation on the same data. There is also **no quantitative noise study**: the authors transfer the earlier positive-exponent noise robustness claim only verbally, noting that negative exponents drive errors to the observational noise floor earlier and that a dedicated noise study is needed — but it is absent. No ablation isolates the marginal contribution of the period-aware step `s=p` versus `s=1`, nor of the consensus rule's `≥3` threshold versus `≥2`.
- **Reproducibility** is reasonable for a methods paper: hyperparameters (N=5000 trajectories, 70/30 split, K=3, Y=1, candidate periods to 16, profiles H=5…10, transient lengths) and reference-exponent computation (analytical for logistic; QR accumulation, 5000 transient + 10000 post-transient iterations for the 2D map) are specified, and the benchmarks are public maps. However, no code or data release is mentioned, and the full transient-sweep and consensus-acceptance logic is described in prose rather than pseudocode, which would hinder exact replication.
- **Significance** is real but narrow. The estimator targets only the dominant observable contraction rate, explicitly not the full Lyapunov spectrum, and is validated only on deterministic discrete maps. The repeated-relaxation experimental motivation (bridge ring-down, electromechanical recovery) is repeatedly invoked but **not demonstrated on any experimental data** — the authors are commendably explicit that "experimental validation remains a subject of future work." Until that validation exists, the practical value is a promising recipe rather than a proven tool.
- **Red flags:** none severe. The framing is unusually careful: the authors repeatedly warn against equating the estimated rate with a damping ratio or with the asymptotic LLE for continuous-time limit cycles (where the largest exponent is zero by phase neutrality), and the reliability class is part of the estimator rather than a post-hoc label. The positive-branch "context" computation is clearly labeled as not the contribution of the paper.

## Strengths

- Elegant conceptual unification: one observable quantity `log E(h)` and one short-trajectory pipeline serve both the chaotic (growth) and stable (contraction) regimes, differing only in horizon step and profile selection.
- The transient-consensus rule directly addresses a concrete, well-explained failure mode — high-R² early-transient fits with wrong asymptotic slope — and embeds the accept/reject decision into the estimator itself, which is more disciplined than reporting a single point estimate.
- The 2D no-fixed-point validation running three independent scalar pipelines is a clever internal-consistency test that does not require combining coordinates, and the three observables agree tightly.
- Intellectual honesty throughout: the authors are explicit about what the method is (a dominant observable contraction rate, not a full spectrum; not interchangeable with modal damping), what it cannot do (continuous-time autonomous limit cycles, where the largest exponent is zero), and what remains unvalidated (experimental data, noise robustness, head-to-head baselines).

## Limitations

- No experimental validation — only deterministic discrete-time maps with known reference exponents. The structural-vibration and electromechanical-recovery motivation is stated but not demonstrated.
- No quantitative noise study; the authors note that negative exponents drive forecast errors to the observational noise floor earlier than the positive-exponent case, so the recoverable exponent magnitude under noise is unknown.
- No head-to-head comparison with the most obvious competitors (Yang–Wu nonlinear mapping, Sun–Wu RBF surrogate, Koopman/EDMD decay eigenvalues, empirical Floquet multipliers), explicitly deferred to future work.
- Logistic-map coverage is only 82.14% with R²=0.886, and accuracy degrades exactly where the exponent is most negative (strongly stable / superstable windows), where the ensemble collapses to a numerical floor.
- Only the dominant exponent is targeted; no contracting subdominant directions, no full spectrum.
- No code/data release mentioned; the consensus-acceptance logic is described in prose rather than pseudocode, slightly hampering exact reproduction.

## Relevance

该工作属于非线性时间序列分析与动力系统方程无关（equation-free）推断，与机器学习（KNN 预测器）结合用于 Lyapunov 指数估计，对结构健康监测、振铃/自由衰减响应等"重复短轨迹"场景有直接的方法学启发，可视为数据驱动动力学推断方向的一个具体案例，但其当前验证仅限于已知解析参考的离散映射，尚未触及真实实验或高维混沌系统。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the method perform under additive measurement noise of controlled amplitude, and at what noise floor does the recoverable negative-exponent magnitude saturate? The authors flag this as the most pressing open question.
- What is the marginal contribution of period-aware horizons (`s=p`) versus the consensus rule when ablated independently on the logistic-map narrow periodic windows and the 2D map?
- How does the forecast-error estimator compare head-to-head (MAE, coverage, reliability-class calibration) with Yang–Wu nonlinear mapping, Sun–Wu RBF surrogate, and Koopman/EDMD decay-eigenvalue methods on the identical short-trajectory ensembles?
- Does the method extend to continuous-time experimental ring-down data (bridges, electromechanical recovery), where the largest exponent of an autonomous limit cycle is zero and the estimator must instead target a transverse/Floquet decay rate?
- What is the minimum ensemble size `N` and perturbation amplitude needed to keep the contraction interval measurable before the numerical/noise floor truncates it, especially for strongly negative exponents?
- Could the consensus rule be tightened or replaced by an information-theoretic model-selection criterion (e.g., comparing one- versus two-line fits to the log-error profile) to reduce the residual positive bias without lowering coverage?
