◆ Key metric

ρ < 0.5

Most methods' epistemic Spearman correlation with the oracle stays well below 0.5; method rankings only moderately preserved (Kendall W ≈ 0.46).

◆ Why this matters

Unifies uncertainty under a sample-conditional posterior risk with exact, computable GP oracle targets — directly actionable for auditing Bayesian deep learning and probabilistic regression beyond OOD proxies.

▲ Critical edge

Oracle targets come only from GP ground truths on small tabular tasks under MSE — the method ranking may not survive classification, image data, or other scoring rules.

01Methodology

Uncertainty is redefined as the expected loss of the deployed predictor under the posterior over plausible ground-truth functions — collapsing Bayesian (variance) and frequentist (excess-risk) views into one frame under explicit assumptions.

Bregman decomposition · MSE + Gaussian noise posterior risk(x) = σ²ε(x) + σ²f(x) + (f̂(x) − m_S(x))²
                  aleatoric  +  gen. variance  +  gen. bias (estimator-dep.)
  • Unifying theory. Posterior-variance EU and excess-risk EU both emerge as special cases once you assume f̂ = posterior mean, or the function-posterior collapses to a Dirac.
  • Semi-synthetic oracle benchmark. 14 UCI/OpenML tabular presets — real covariates, GP-sampled targets — yield analytically correct aleatoric & epistemic oracles at every evaluation point.
  • Controlled covariate shift via Mahalanobis-distance bin sampling: balanced / OOD-interior / bimodal profiles.
  • Two-stage evaluation: 7 dev datasets fix architecture & training via ablation; 7 held-out datasets produce the final ranking. Spearman ρ is primary (scale- and calibration-invariant).

02Results & highlights

  • Far from oracle. Most epistemic ρ < 0.5; rankings preserved at Kendall W ≈ 0.46.
  • Central finding. Predictive performance is near-identical across models (only BLR slightly behind), yet uncertainty rankings vary widely.
  • Ablations are tiny. ρ̂ flat across depth (0.84±0.01) and width; 91% of width pairs show negative AU slope — Deep Ensembles degrade fastest.
  • Tuning objective ≈ negligible. Validation NLL vs. RMSE differences are "well within variability."
  • Spread, not bias. Most epistemic estimators track posterior variance, missing the estimator-bias term from misspecification.
  • Practical winners: Deep Ensembles · FSP-Laplace · CatBoost-KGB — but "no method should be used uncritically in high-stakes settings."

03Strengths

  • Clean unifying theory — Bayesian, excess-risk, and resampling-frequentist views recovered under stated assumptions.
  • Exact, computable oracles (not OOD proxies); posterior correct by construction; controlled shift profiles.
  • Disciplined dev/held-out split; per-preset hyperparameters fully disclosed; <100 h compute makes replication realistic.
  • Honest reporting — repeatedly stresses no method is uniformly reliable and many differences sit within noise.
  • Practical guidance distilled from ablations (default tanh, width ∈ {16,32,64}, Adam; treat SOAP cautiously for Laplace family).

04Limitations

  • Narrow scope. Oracle = GP ground truth, MSE only — no evidence beyond tabular regression; generalization risk under-acknowledged.
  • Underpowered comparisons. Held-out suite only 7 datasets; large error bars make many pairwise claims suggestive, not definitive.
  • Squared-bias confound. The oracle treats deviation from the GP posterior mean m_S as epistemic — can conflate genuine estimator bias with function-class mismatch.
  • Missing pieces. No head-to-head with QUAM; no calibration-weighted ranking; DEUP has many missing-dataset cells.
  • Soft-OOD only — the benchmark cannot speak to strict distribution-membership detection.

05Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the ranking survive when the oracle risk is computed under CRPS or log-score rather than MSE?
  2. How much of the "squared-bias" signal is a GP-as-truth artifact versus genuine estimator misspecification?
  3. Can the posterior-excess-risk oracle extend to classification (Dirichlet / GP-softmax) to challenge the image-classification UD literature?
  4. Is NLL ≈ RMSE for UD a real finding, or an artifact of Spearman ρ's scale-insensitivity? Would a calibration-weighted metric differ?
