When Do Prompt-Side Agent Playbooks Transfer? Accuracy, Cost, and Runtime Shift in Agent Deployment

Weihong Lin · Lin Sun · Xiangzheng Zhang — Beijing Qiyuan Technology Co., Ltd. (360.cn-affiliated)

2 Why this matters

Answers a deployment-critical question — can distilled prompt playbooks transfer across models, domains, and runtimes? — and ships a reusable, statistically rigorous evaluation protocol: global multiplicity correction, cost audit, and a same-length placebo control.

3 Critical edge

The +23.6 pp comes from ALFWorld — exactly the clean, low-stakes benchmark where positive transfer is most expected; do not extrapolate it to production service agents.

Existence test

Strengths & Limitations

side by side

◆ Strengths

  • Exceptional statistical honesty — global Holm over 135 routes, predefined contrasts, CI95 + exact p-values throughout; refuses to treat non-retained routes as null.
  • Tracks operational cost (tokens, turns, trajectory length, hit-50, >30T) alongside accuracy — the source of the most interesting finding: runtime shift destabilizes stopping even when accuracy looks fine.
  • Same-length placebo cleanly separates content-driven transfer from format / prompt-length effects.
  • Actionable deployment checklist, not vague "playbooks work" advice.
  • The counterintuitive redistillation result (+247% tool calls) is genuinely valuable.

◇ Limitations

  • TAU2 per-cell power is low; the headline +1.72 pp rests on a single predefined contrast.
  • XBench is a single artifact–runtime pairing on 100 problems — a stress test, not a generalizable law.
  • Distillation depends on proprietary, version-sensitive models (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4) — long-run reproducibility hazard.
  • No comparison with human-authored playbooks or dynamic retrieval; few-shot win is one compatible setting only.
  • Software engineering, code generation & data analysis agents are untested.
  • Densely hedged — virtuous, but leaves no concrete freeze-vs-redistill threshold for practitioners.

Open Questions for Further Exploration

where this points next
Can a pre-deployment compatibility selector (a cheap behavioral-boundedness probe) predict runtime-shift destabilization before full rollout?
Does the ALFWorld capacity-scaling pattern hold on harder, longer-horizon benchmarks, or is it an artifact of the 50-step ceiling?
How much of TAU2's compatibility-sensitivity is tool-schema / API-protocol mismatch versus workflow mismatch?
Would artifact ensembling or routing (pick the playbook by target profile) recover route-level gains lost to multiplicity correction?
Is the XBench re-querying / delayed-stopping pathway a context-length-OOD attention effect — does it vanish with position-encoding fixes?
How do results change when the distiller is an open-weight model rather than Claude Opus 4.6 / GPT-5.4, given version drift?
