02 / METHOD

One unit, four stagesReplaces the package / text / post-retrieval-graph unit mismatch with a single contract-bearing section node.

Sec2Graph

Construct

Open each skill package into 9-role section nodes (Intent → Output) with typed dependency, resource & verifier edges.

MotifZip

Compress

Mine recurring motifs only when BoundaryClear ∧ SignatureStable ∧ DependencyClosed ∧ VerifierReachable → reversible ported macros.

PathHydrate

Retrieve

Dual-seed fusion + constrained subgraph search; render macros at the lowest sufficient level under a token budget.

ReZip

Maintain

Execution traces score macro risk ρ; demote, split, or retire高风险 macros — always preserving source expansion.

The diagnosis is a unit mismatch: today's pipelines retrieve whole packages, compress raw text, and build graphs only after retrieval — three different units, three heuristics. SkillZip unifies them on one representation: a typed, source-grounded section node carrying a procedural contract across interface execution verification aspects.

Compression is governed by a typed attributed graph grammar. A motif becomes a production rule M_g[I_g,O_g] ⇒ (V_g,E_g,π_g,χ_g) scored by an MDL-style gain Δ(g). Proposition 1 proves compositional structural lifting, so ξ(κ(P)) ≅ P — expansion is the exact inverse of compression. PathHydrate's filling is sufficiency-gated: it stops once anchors are covered, dependencies closed, and the verifier is reachable; the budget is a ceiling, not a quota.

04 / ASSESSMENT

Strengths vs. LimitationsWhat the evaluation establishes — and where its own honesty tempers the claims.

+ Strengths

  • Precise structural diagnosis. Solves the retrieve/compress/execute unit mismatch with one representation instead of three stacked heuristics.
  • Falsification-ready metrics. DPR / VR / recovery / downstream-inflation are designed to catch compression that just shoves missing info downstream.
  • Reversible duality. Four hydration levels (name → contract → outline → full source) backed by Proposition 1; PathHydrate is sufficiency-gated, not fill-to-budget.
  • Closed-loop maintenance. ReZip uses execution evidence (risk-driven demotion/split/retire) — the problem most one-shot compressors ignore.
  • Backbone-agnostic. Plug-and-play across six LLMs; the largest gains accrue to weaker executors — a deployable property.

− Limitations

  • Narrow benchmarks. SkillsBench + ALFWorld only; no SWE-Bench, web-agent, or long-horizon reasoning coverage.
  • Fragile foundation. LLM contract extraction tops out at 88.7 F1 on preconditions/guards; 40% corruption collapses reward by 9.2 pts. No self-correcting loop.
  • Best-case headline. +12.2 pts is the easiest setting; on the strongest backbone the gain is +2.8 with a CI that barely excludes zero.
  • Scaling caveat. The 100K-skill run adds distractor-only packages — it tests retrieval ambiguity, not true large-library task success.
  • Unbounded greedy step. MotifZip disclaims global optimality; the four weights (α, λ, μ, η) have no reported sensitivity analysis.
05 / OPEN

Questions worth chasingWhere this work points next.

  1. Q1Can contract extraction be made self-correcting — with the executor flagging verifier failures back to Sec2Graph to re-extract the missing guard at field level?
  2. Q2How does SkillZip behave on code-centric benchmarks (SWE-Bench, RepoBench) where sections are functions/tests and verifiers are executable suites?
  3. Q3Is there a principled (Bayesian or bandit) way to set MotifZip's four weights per-library, and how sensitive is selection to them?
  4. Q4Could a learned policy predict the right hydration level from task features, instead of expanding reactively on contract failure?
  5. Q5How much of the 47% prompt reduction comes from fewer agent turns vs. smaller per-turn context — and does that split hold for multi-skill tasks?
