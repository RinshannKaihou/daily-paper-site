> **一句话总结：** GROM 把 LLM 机器遗忘建模为岭回归最小二乘问题，给出闭式解析解 W+P，仅需一次梯度-free 前向传播即可在数秒内完成对一层权重的精确编辑，在 TOFU/MUSE/WMDP 五个基准上取得 Pareto 最优，且对 4-bit 量化攻击鲁棒（而 SimNPO 被量化后遗忘内容基本恢复）。

> **TL;DR:** GROM replaces iterative fine-tuning with a single closed-form ridge-regularized least-squares weight edit `W+P` computed from gradient-free forward passes over forget/retain data; on TOFU-5% it reaches Final Score 0.79 vs SimNPO's 0.73 while keeping model utility at 0.62, on WMDP-Bio it is ~180× faster than SimNPO (0.2 min vs 21.6 min) with higher MMLU (0.55 vs 0.44), and unlike gradient baselines it survives the 4-bit quantization attack (MUSE-Books VerbMem stays 2.9 at 4-bit vs SimNPO's 72.5).

## 核心贡献

- **闭式遗忘公式：** 首次将 LLM 机器遗忘形式化为一个有唯一解析解的岭回归最小二乘问题 `min_P (w_r/r)||PX_r||² + (w_f/s)||PX_f - D||² + μ||P||²`，完全绕开迭代梯度优化与收敛不稳定问题。
- **梯度-free 高效性：** 每个被编辑层的最优加性更新 `P* = (w_f/s) D X_f^T A^{-1}` 仅由 forget/retain 数据的前向传播收集的 keys 计算，无反向传播、无迭代，比梯度方法快最多约 180 倍。
- **Pareto 最优的遗忘-效用权衡：** 在 TOFU-5%、TOFU-10%、MUSE-News、MUSE-Books、WMDP-Bio 五个基准上同时取得最高 Final Score 与最高模型效用。
- **量化鲁棒性：** 由于更新从权重中真正删除而非掩盖目标内容，GROM 在 4-bit 量化下遗忘效果与金标准 Retrain 模型一致，而 SimNPO 等梯度方法被量化后几乎完全恢复被遗忘内容。
- **精确删除可审计性：** 利用 Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury 恒等式给出单个遗忘样本对编辑贡献的闭式 downdate（Theorem 2），可在秒级精确审计每个删除请求，这是梯度方法（需影响函数近似）无法做到的。

## Key Contributions

- **Closed-Form Unlearning Formulation:** Casts LLM machine unlearning as a ridge-regularized least-squares problem with a unique analytical solution (Theorem 1), eliminating the optimization instability and computational cost of iterative fine-tuning while also giving the first explicit analytical formulation that gradient methods lack.
- **Gradient-Free One-Shot Edit:** Each layer's optimal additive update `P* = (w_f/s) D X_f^T A^{-1}` is computed from a single forward pass over forget data and one over retain data (collecting per-token keys `X_f`, `X_r`), with no backpropagation and no convergence loop — up to ~180× faster than SimNPO.
- **State-of-the-Art Trade-offs:** Pareto-best forgetting-utility trade-offs across five benchmarks (TOFU-5%, TOFU-10%, MUSE-News, MUSE-Books, WMDP-Bio), achieving both the strongest forgetting and the highest model utility.
- **Quantization Robustness:** Because the edit removes targeted content from the weights instead of masking it in low-magnitude adjustments, GROM withstands the low-bit quantization attack of Zhang et al. (2025) that undoes most gradient-based unlearning; at 4-bit it stays as forgetful as the gold Retrain model.
- **Exact Deletion Auditing:** Derives (Theorem 2) a closed-form Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury downdate giving the exact per-example deletion influence `ΔP_i = L_i R_i^T` (rank ≤ c_i) from a single cached `A^{-1}`, enabling per-request accountability in seconds — something gradient methods can only approximate via influence functions.

## Methodology

GROM edits one or a small contiguous band of late MLP **down-projection** matrices `W ∈ R^{m×n}` (chosen because feed-forward blocks store facts and write them directly to the residual stream; up-/gate-projections are excluded since their outputs pass through a nonlinearity, breaking the additive-shift control).

**Setup.** Two gradient-free forward passes collect per-token input vectors ("keys") for the forget set (`X_f ∈ R^{n×s}`) and retain set (`X_r ∈ R^{n×r}`); keys are subsampled to a fixed budget so cost is independent of forget-set size. A target matrix `D ∈ R^{m×s}` encodes the desired output-space displacement for each forget position.

**Objective (Theorem 1).** The optimal additive update is the unique minimizer of

`min_P  (w_r/r)||P X_r||²_F + (w_f/s)||P X_f − D||²_F + μ||P||²_F`

with closed-form solution `P* = (w_f/s) D X_f^T A^{-1}`, where `A = (w_r/r)X_r X_r^T + (w_f/s)X_f X_f^T + μI_n` is symmetric positive definite (hence invertible). The proof (Appendix A) differentiates the three Frobenius-norm terms, sets the gradient to zero, and solves for `P`.

**Two unlearning targets** (chosen per how the benchmark probes forgetting):
- *Token suppression* (TOFU, MUSE): for each forget position with gold next token `g_j`, steer the logit away from `g_j` via `d_j = -β α_j u_j`, where `u_j` is the LM-head row direction (or its normalized form for hidden-layer edits), β is edit strength, and a *specificity weight* `α_j ∈ [0,1]` suppresses only tokens distinctive to the forget set (α≈0 for tokens common to both sets).
- *Representation corruption* (WMDP, multiple-choice): since the answer is a choice label not memorized text, a single fixed random unit vector `u` is used and `d_j = c·u` for all forget positions, driving the layer's output on forget-like inputs toward a meaningless direction.

**Layer selection.** A logit-lens attribution score `S_ℓ = F_ℓ − R_ℓ` (direct contribution of layer ℓ to forget-gold-token logits minus retain-gold-token logits) picks the contiguous window of `k` late MLP layers with the largest average score, rather than relying on grid search.

**Per-example deletion audit (Theorem 2).** Partitioning the forget keys/target by example and deleting example `i` perturbs `A` by a symmetric rank-`c_i` downdate. The Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury identity resolves `ΔP_i` analytically as `L_i R_i^T` from a single `c_i × c_i` solve against the cached `A^{-1}`, with no `n×n` reinversion — enabling exact per-request auditing in seconds. Only three true hyperparameters remain (edit strength β, retain weight `w_r`, ridge scale ρ); the ridge is a numerical safeguard, not a tuning knob.

## Results & Highlights

- **TOFU-5% (LLaMA2-7B-Chat):** GROM attains Final Score **0.79** with Model Utility **0.62** (matching the unedited Original's 0.62), vs SimNPO's Final Score 0.73 / MU 0.58 and GradDiff's 0.77 / MU 0.56; GROM also reaches near-saturated forgetting (1-Rouge-L 0.95, 1-Prob. 1.00, 1-Extr. 0.97). GROM's 1.8 min runtime is within the same order as gradient baselines (SimNPO 2.6 min) on this small forget set.
- **TOFU-10% (LLaMA3.2-1B-Instruct):** GROM Final Score **0.75** / MU **0.60** in **0.5 min**, beating SimNPO (0.70 / 0.56 / 2.5 min) and IDKDPO (0.73 but MU collapsed to 0.52 due to over-forgetting, 6.9 min); GROM avoids both failure modes (over-forgetting vs under-forgetting) with a single closed-form edit.
- **WMDP-Bio (Llama-3-8B-Instruct):** GROM reaches Final Score **0.63** with MMLU **0.55** in **0.2 min**, vs SimNPO 0.60 / MMLU 0.44 / 21.6 min — roughly **~108× faster** while preserving far higher general-knowledge utility; GradDiff suppresses bio knowledge (0.73) but collapses MMLU to 0.26. GROM's suppression (1-AccBio 0.71) is competitive with the strongest suppressors.
- **MUSE-News (LLaMA2-7B):** GROM Final Score **55.93** in **0.3 min** vs SimNPO 49.81 / 36.3 min (a **~121× speedup**), driving PrivLeak to −3.80 (near the Retain target of 0.00) while GA/GradDiff/NPO collapse KnowMem Dr to 0.00 (destroy retain utility).
- **MUSE-Books (ICLM-7B):** GROM Final Score **76.20** in **0.3 min** vs SimNPO 70.83 / 53.8 min (a **~179× speedup**), achieving the highest retain utility (KnowMem Dr 65.56) while nearly eliminating verbatim memorization (VerbMem Df 2.40) and driving privacy leakage to −0.22.
- **Quantization robustness (MUSE-Books, 4-bit):** SimNPO's VerbMem Df jumps from **0.00 (full-precision) to 72.5 (4-bit)** — i.e., the supposedly forgotten Harry Potter text is recovered — while GROM stays at **2.40 → 2.9**, matching the gold Retrain model (14.1 → 14.3); GROM is thus the only method that truly removes rather than masks the content.
- **Per-example deletion audit (TOFU-10%, layer 15, 400 examples):** the largest single-example influence `γ = 4.23%` of `||P*||_F` with median 1.83%, a tight (not heavy-tailed) distribution, confirming O(1/s) decay as the forget set grows (γ/||P*|| falls from 0.173 at N=40 to 0.042 at N=400); this matches brute-force re-solve exactly.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity (Appendix B.2):** edit strength β buys forgetting "almost for free" (forget efficacy 0.199→0.866 while MU stays 0.601→0.600 up to β=45); retain weight `w_r` is the only axis that fails at both ends (MU→0.000 at w_r=0; forget collapses to 0.367 at w_r=1600); ridge ρ varies Final Score by only 0.005 over two orders of magnitude.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine):** The framing of unlearning as an exact ridge-regularized least-squares solve with a closed-form solution is a real and welcome departure from the dominant iterative-fine-tuning paradigm, and the exact per-example deletion audit (Theorem 2) is, to my knowledge, genuinely new — no gradient method can give exact, non-approximate deletion attribution.
- **Rigor (strong with caveats):** Proofs are complete and the empirical coverage is broad (five benchmarks, multiple model sizes). However, **GROM's claim of being "Pareto-best" is benchmark-dependent in a subtle way**: on TOFU-10% GROM's 1-Rouge-L (0.78) is actually *lower* than IDKNLL (0.98), ROME (0.98), MEMIT (0.88), AlphaEdit (0.98), ZeroUnlearn (0.92) — GROM wins on the *aggregate* Final Score largely because it preserves utility (MU 0.60) while the knowledge editors and IDKDPO over-forget. A reader who cares only about verbatim forgetting strength, not utility, would find several baselines stronger on that single axis.
- **Reproducibility:** The three true hyperparameters (β, `w_r`, ρ) plus edit-width `k` are tuned per benchmark; the sensitivity table (B.2) shows `w_r` must be tuned ("the only axis whose two ends both fail outright"), so reproducing peak numbers requires per-dataset hyperparameter search — not fully zero-knob. Code release mitigates this.
- **Missing ablations / comparisons:** The locate-then-edit baselines (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit, ZeroUnlearn) are *only* compared on TOFU and ZsRE because they require annotated (subject, relation, object) triples; GROM is not benchmarked against them on MUSE/WMDP, which is reasonable but leaves the "vs knowledge editors" story incomplete on the harder corpora. There is no evaluation against stronger recent attacks beyond 4-bit quantization (e.g., relearning/jogging attacks of Hu et al. 2024, or adversarial prompting), so "removes rather than hides" is demonstrated only for the quantization vector.
- **Significance:** If the closed-form update scales as claimed (cost set by model size and number of edited layers, independent of forget-set size), this is a meaningful step toward unlearning very large models where gradient methods are infeasible — but the paper does not yet demonstrate this beyond 8B parameters.
- **Red flags:** None severe. The "to our knowledge the first one-shot unlearning method that achieves and surpasses iterative methods" claim is scoped to the experimental benchmarks and is defensible. The 180× speedup figure is the MUSE-Books/SimNPO ratio (53.8 min vs 0.3 min); on the smaller TOFU-5% the gap is narrow (SimNPO 2.6 min vs GROM 1.8 min), so the headline speedup should not be over-generalized to small forget sets.

## Strengths

- Mathematically clean: a single closed-form solve per edited layer, with complete proofs and a clean `P* = (w_f/s) D X_f^T A^{-1}` expression that is easy to implement and audit.
- Genuinely gradient-free: only forward passes are needed, so the method is applicable where backprop is prohibitive and is naturally compatible with frozen/quantized inference engines.
- Quantization robustness is a strong, timely property given that Zhang et al. (2025) showed most current unlearning is reversible by 4-bit rounding; GROM is the rare method that survives.
- The specificity weight `α_j` (which confines suppression to forget-distinctive tokens) is well-motivated and the ablation (Table 7) confirms it — disabling α drops every score, with the largest drops on benchmarks where forget/retain vocabularies overlap most (MUSE-News, TOFU-5%).
- Practical auditability: per-example deletion influence in closed form is directly useful for GDPR-style right-to-be-forgotten compliance reporting.

## Limitations

- **Per-benchmark hyperparameter tuning is required** for the retain weight `w_r` (and to a lesser extent β); the method is not zero-knob despite having an analytical solution.
- **Layer choice is heuristic-then-fixed:** the logit-lens attribution band plus a fixed edit-width `k` works empirically (Table B.1), but there is no principled, per-model automatic layer selector, and early/wide variants occasionally come close (e.g., Wide on TOFU-10%: 0.740 vs Selected 0.747).
- **Evaluation ceiling at ~8B parameters:** no experiments on 30B/70B-scale models, so the "paves the way to efficiently unlearning even larger models" conclusion is aspirational rather than demonstrated.
- **Single attack vector tested:** robustness is shown against 4-bit quantization but not against relearning/jogging (Hu et al. 2024), adversarial prompts, or membership-inference attacks, all of which are documented failure modes of current unlearning.
- **Forgetting strength on verbatim metrics can trail over-forgetting baselines:** on TOFU-10%, several methods (IDKNLL, ROME, AlphaEdit) achieve higher raw 1-Rouge-L; GROM wins on aggregate Final Score via utility preservation, which is the right trade-off but should be stated explicitly.
- **MLP down-projection only:** although justified (residual writer, fact storage), editing only FFN blocks may miss knowledge stored in attention or embeddings; Table 7 shows attention `o_proj` edges out down_proj on TOFU-5%, hinting the restriction is not strictly optimal.

## Relevance

与本用户关注的 LLM 高效训练/推理与模型编辑方向高度相关：GROM 提供了一种无需梯度迭代、可在数秒内完成、且对量化鲁棒的权重级遗忘方法，对大模型的安全合规删除（GDPR/版权）和可审计的模型编辑都有直接价值，其闭式最小二乘框架也为"知识定位-编辑"类研究提供了新的解析工具。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the closed-form update scale to 30B/70B-class models where gradient-based unlearning is infeasible, and does the `n×n` Gram matrix `A` (with `n` = intermediate/hidden size) become a memory bottleneck requiring approximation?
- How does GROM behave under relearning/jogging attacks (Hu et al. 2024) and adversarial-prompt extraction, not just quantization — i.e., is the content truly deleted or only removed from the edited MLP layers' write paths?
- Can the exact per-example deletion influence (Theorem 2) be used not just for auditing but for *curating* the forget set (e.g., flagging examples whose removal would damage retain utility), turning an audit tool into a data-selection tool?
- What happens when the forget and retain sets are highly entangled beyond MUSE-Books (e.g., unlearning a specific capability whose vocabulary and representations overlap heavily with retained tasks) — does the specificity weight `α_j` still suffice?
- Could the ridge-regularized least-squares formulation be extended to attention output projections and embeddings simultaneously (multi-matrix joint solve) to capture knowledge that FFN-only edits miss?
