A vision-language model's reading error is invisible to a data edit exactly when it commutes with that edit's answer-transform.

Methodology — detectability as commutation

Theory-first. A figure is rendered from data D by a program R(D;θ); an edit g acts on the data and is paired with a known answer-transform φ_g. A systematic error is undetectable exactly when it lands in the joint centralizer of the suite.

  • Centralizer (Theorem 2). The undetectable set is U_τ(G) = ∩ C_τ(φ_g) — antitone in G (more edits only shrink it), monotone in τ.
  • Invariance is free (Cor. 3). C_τ(id) = E, so an identity/invariance edit contributes nothing structurally — the blind spot is a property of the relation, not the implementation.
  • Affine completeness (Thm 4). The pair {×c, +δ} (c≠1, δ≠0) detects every non-identity affine error; pointwise, independent of a.
  • Permutation incompleteness (Prop. 5, Cor. 6). A swap leaves 2(m−2)! errors hidden; an order-m cycle leaves only m. Cycle beats swap for m≥5.
  • ECS. ECS = (1/|G|) Σ 1[ρ_g ≤ τ_g] — references the model's own base answer, so it is label-free. Flag when ECS < t.

Results & highlights

Numbers are reproduced from real model inference (generator, edit suite and harness rebuilt end-to-end). Primary: Qwen2.5-VL-7B, N=900 over 3 seeds. Total compute < 3 GPU-hours on A100. The headline test is a prediction fixed before measurement — including its own predicted failures.

  • Edit orderingscale › offset › del-max › swap ≈ id
    Flagged rates on IBEM follow the predicted centralizer ordering: scale 57/64 > offset 48/64 > delete-max 4/24 > swap 2/31 ≈ identity 13/97. Numeric-vs-non-numeric gap significant after Bonferroni on all three models (p<0.0002).
  • ECS reaches IBEM53.6% vs 0.0%
    At a matched 14% false-positive rate, ECS flags 53.6% of invariance-blind errors vs REA's 0.0% (zero by construction). A second independent invariance-family method (perturbation uncertainty) also flags 0.0% — confirming the blind spot belongs to the relation family, not to re-rendering.
  • REA ∧ ECSAUROC 0.788 → 0.906
    Combined correctness AUROC gain of +0.118 [0.089, 0.149], with combined gains of +0.118 / +0.100 / +0.075 across the three models. High-confidence calibration error drops from 0.140 to 0.055 (non-overlapping CIs).
  • Cyclic relabeling5.3% → 44.0%
    On 150 matched m=6 instances, cyclic relabeling flags 66/150 (44.0%) vs swap's 8/150 (5.3%) — matching the closed-form prediction 2(m−2)!/m = 48/6.
  • REA-independent check92.1% / 29.8%
    200 hand-labeled wrong answers (75.5% affine, 24.5% permutation): detection rates 92.1% for affine-labeled errors and 29.8% for permutation-labeled — the gap sits exactly where Proposition 5 predicts.
  • Real figures94.8% extraction
    ChartQA pilot (N=300): a ×2 scale edit after digitization-assisted re-plotting yields ECS-inconsistency of 47.5% (GPT-4o) and 22.0% (Claude Sonnet 4).

Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • Turns an empirical question ("which edits?") into a decidable, closed-form one — sidestepping NP-hard MR-selection.
  • Theory produces falsifiable, pre-registered predictions, including predicted failures (identity, swap) — and these are the primary evidence.
  • Unusually honest: discloses the IBEM/REA circularity, the two operators that fail difficulty control, and the 24.5% non-affine share.
  • Second independent invariance-family baseline confirms the blind spot is a property of the relation family, de-risking the central claim.
  • The Xie et al. classifier-metatest inversion is a non-trivial unification the theory was not fitted to.

Limitations

  • Completeness (Thm 4) is affine-only; ~24.5% of real errors are permutation-type and provably uncoverable by any swap suite.
  • ECS needs the figure's underlying data — natively only programmatically renderable figures; real figures need digitization (94.8% success), scanned figures not addressed.
  • ECS detects but does not repair; costs |G|≤3 extra forward passes; false negatives by construction inside the joint centralizer.
  • Difficulty control partially fails: delete-max and offset are measurably easier than base on all three models.
  • Headline IBEM win is concentrated on a narrow ~10.8% subpopulation (pie charts + arithmetic). More a theoretical-clarification paper than a SOTA-beating detector.
04

Open questions

  • Can commutation extend to stochastic, input-dependent error maps — bounding detection probability under a learned error model rather than an opaque P?
  • The 75.5 / 24.5 affine-permutation split with 0% "other" — artifact of two-category forcing, or do real errors genuinely decompose this cleanly?
  • Corollary 6 recommends cycles, yet released REND-EQUIV still uses swaps — what is the downstream effect once cycles roll out across the full benchmark?
  • Does equivariance still beat invariance baselines on real digitized figures, where extraction noise may dominate? (Pilot reports inconsistency rates, not AUROC.)
  • Can {×c, +δ} be augmented with 1–2 non-affine edits to provably cover piecewise-affine or log-axis errors? What is that centralizer?
  • How robust are detection rates when the error is not style-invariant (e depends on θ), e.g. OCR-sensitive failures?
