02 / Methodology

An SES system is modeled as Σ = (T, F, E, S): trajectory pool, quality filter, LLM evolver, versioned skill bank, updated via S' = S ⊕ E(F(T), S).

The attacker is a skill-visible black-box contributor: it may inspect public skills and contribute a bounded fraction of trajectories, but cannot see private pools, filters, evolver prompts, or edit the skill bank.

The attack inserts an outcome-conditioned causal bridge — constraint → b → success (or missing b → failure) — at a legitimate workflow locus, so the behavior recurs invariantly across k transformed records.

04 / Strengths

  • Sharp threat model: skill-visible black-box attacker captures realistic multi-user / Sybil / upstream contributors without implausible privileges.
  • The C1–C3 formalization turns "poison the data" into a falsifiable mechanism with a named, attackable bottleneck.
  • Decisive ablations: the k=1→k=2 recurrence jump and the Causal-vs-Local contrast cleanly isolate the mechanism.
  • Honest scoping — inert canaries, explicit non-goals, artifact-vs-runtime distinction stated repeatedly.
  • Cross-architecture transfer with an explanation of why family sensitivity inverts between pipelines.

05 / Limitations

  • Artifact-level only — no ASR / runtime-trigger measurement on realistic downstream tasks.
  • Single-evolver, single-split benign-utility check; stealth across the full matrix is not established.
  • Provenance-diversity gate is a pilot: only F1 single-cluster tested; Sybil resistance unproven.
  • Multi-cycle / longitudinal evolution — flagged as compounding risk — is not evaluated.
  • Six frontier evolvers are a convenience sample; model-sensitivity claims conflate provider, policy, scale.

06 / Questions for Further Exploration

  1. What is the realistic attack success rate (ASR) — once a poisoned skill installs, how often does it actually trigger and execute per family on a natural task distribution?
  2. How does poisoning evolve across multiple SES cycles? Does a self-reinforcing feedback loop form, and can versioned lineage / counterfactual evaluation break it?
  3. Can a stronger provenance-diversity gate defend F2/F3/F4 and resist coordinated Sybil clusters that mimic diversity — not just the single-cluster F1 case?
  4. Does the attack transfer to out-of-scope classes — dialogue-memory, verifier-coupled, training-coupled systems where the update signal is not a trajectory pool?
  5. Is there a detectable signature at evolution time (attribution-confidence anomalies, lineage-graph structure) that flags promotion of attacker-correlated evidence before it becomes durable?
