> **一句话总结：** 提出 RelayEvolve，一种无需训练的种群交接框架：用廉价模型在 Grow-Deepen bandit 调度下探索多条轨迹，以"Relay Gain"（紧凑质量-多样性候选库的边际改进）为奖励决定何时把整理后的种子种群交接给强模型精修，在 4 个程序进化基准 × 3 个预算下 12 个设定中取得 11 项最高均分。

> **TL;DR:** RelayEvolve reframes cost-efficient LLM-driven evolution as a *population handoff* problem rather than per-call model routing: a cheap model (Qwen-3.5-Flash) explores multiple trajectories under a Grow-Deepen bandit guided by "Relay Gain," then a greedy submodular selection hands off a compact quality-diverse seed bank to a strong model (Qwen-3.5-Plus) for refinement. Across Circle Packing, TXN Scheduling, and Prism under 3 budgets, RelayEvolve achieves the highest mean score in 11 of 12 settings, e.g. on Circle Packing (Square) at the 100% budget it reaches 2.5364 vs 2.4453 for All-strong and 2.4558 for Random routing.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统刻画 LLM 驱动进化轨迹的三条经验规律**：(1) 进度强烈前载——前 20 代贡献廉价模型 76.3%、强模型 79.1% 的总改进；(2) 早期轨迹质量信号有用但噪声大（Spearman ρ 在 K=10 仅 0.28，K=50 才到 0.62）；(3) 廉价模型在前 20 代以更低成本捕获强模型 0.82×–1.01× 的早期增益。
- **提出 RelayEvolve，一个无需训练的种群交接框架**：把预算分配从"单次调用选模型"提升到"随种群演化的交接时机"层面。核心是 Relay Gain——一个紧凑质量-多样性候选库的边际改进，同时驱动轨迹分配、停止时机与种子选择。
- **设计 Grow-Deepen bandit 调度器 + 子模贪心种子整理**：bandit 在"开新轨迹(Grow)"与"加深现有轨迹(Deepen)"间分配搜索努力；交接时用 greedy submodular selection + 局部搜索从完整终端池中选出互补种子，享有 (1-1/e) 近似保证。
- **在 4 个程序进化基准 × 3 个预算（共 12 个设定）中取得 11 项最高均分**，并在所有基准上取得或并列最佳单次成绩，验证"围绕种群而非单次调用组织预算"这一核心论点。

## Key Contributions

- **Empirical characterization of LLM-driven evolutionary trajectories.** Three findings: progress is strongly front-loaded (first 20 generations yield 76.3% / 79.1% of total improvement for cheap / strong models); early trajectory rankings are informative but noisy (Spearman ρ = 0.28 at K=10, rising to 0.62 only at K=50); cheap models capture 0.82×–1.01× of strong-model early progress at lower cost.
- **RelayEvolve, a training-free population-handoff framework.** It lifts budget allocation from per-call model selection to evolving-population handoff, using a single shared marginal-reward signal (Relay Gain) to coordinate trajectory allocation, adaptive stopping, and seed selection.
- **Grow-Deepen bandit scheduler + submodular seed curation.** A block-level bandit chooses between starting (Grow) and extending (Deepen) trajectories; at handoff a greedy submodular maximization with local search produces a quality-diverse seed bank with the classical (1-1/e) guarantee.
- **State-of-the-art cost-performance trade-off.** Highest mean score in 11 of 12 benchmark-budget settings, with the only miss on Prism at the lowest budget where the task is already near saturation.

## Methodology

RelayEvolve operates under a fixed inference budget `B` and call cap `N`, with a cheap model `mc` (Qwen-3.5-Flash) and a strong model `ms` (Qwen-3.5-Plus). Its core is a single population-level objective that ties together three decisions.

**Relay objective.** For a candidate pool `C` and bank `S ⊆ C`, the relay value is `F_C(S) = λ·Q_r(S) + (1-λ)·D_C^q(S)`, combining (i) top-r quality `Q_r` (mean of the r best normalized fitnesses in S) and (ii) quality-weighted diversity coverage `D_C^q`, a facility-location term that rewards covering high-quality mass of the reference pool while weighting by `q(v)` to avoid spending capacity on low-quality outliers. Both terms are monotone submodular, so `F_C` is too.

**Online bank update and Relay Gain.** After each block, candidates stream into a deduplicated pool `C_{t+1}` and the online bank `S_{t+1}` is greedily updated (direct add if not full, else replace the element whose removal most increases the objective). Relay Gain `g_t = F_{C_{t+1}}(S_{t+1}) - F_{C_{t+1}}(S_t) ≥ 0` is computed against the *same updated reference pool*, so it measures handoff-population improvement, not pool drift. A normalized relative gain `ρ_t = clip(g_t / max(F_{C_{t+1}}(S_t), ε_F), 0, 1)` feeds the scheduler.

**Grow-Deepen bandit.** Cheap search runs in blocks of `h` generations. Two action types: Grow (start a new trajectory from the common initial program) and Deepen(i) (extend trajectory i by one block). A shared meta-arm holds Grow statistics; each active trajectory has its own Deepen arm. Selection uses a recent-window UCB-style score (Besbes-Gur-Zeevi 2014) with mean of the last `w` rewards, encouraging exploration while adapting to non-stationary trajectory utility. Active-trajectory and per-trajectory-horizon limits prevent any one region from monopolizing the cheap budget.

**Adaptive handoff.** Cheap exploration stops when every block in a patience window `p` has relative gain below a saturation threshold `ε_rel`, or when the cheap-stage budget `B_c` is exhausted.

**Population handoff and strong refinement.** Because the online bank depends on arrival order, at handoff RelayEvolve re-optimizes `F_C` over the *complete fixed terminal pool* `C_τ`: greedy submodular selection followed by objective-improving single-element swaps yields `S_g`; the terminal online bank is refined the same way into `S_o`; the final handoff population `S* = argmax_{S∈{S_g,S_o}} F_{C_τ}(S)`. These seeds initialize a *single shared* strong-model population (not separate budgets) so that strong-model refinement can recombine discoveries from different cheap trajectories. The strong model consumes the remaining budget, and the best valid program across both phases is returned.

## Results & Highlights

- **Highest mean score in 11 of 12 settings** across 4 benchmarks (Circle Packing Square/Rect, TXN Scheduling, Prism) × 3 budgets (β ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1.0} of an all-strong run's cost), with 3 runs each.
- **Circle Packing (Square), 100% budget:** RelayEvolve reaches **2.5364 ± 0.0218**, beating All-strong **2.4453 ± 0.0290** (+0.091) and Random routing **2.4558 ± 0.0400** (+0.081).
- **Circle Packing (Rectangle), 100% budget:** RelayEvolve **2.3616 ± 0.0040** vs All-strong **2.3471 ± 0.0120** and LEVI **2.2573 ± 0.0380** (a +0.104 margin over LEVI).
- **TXN Scheduling, 100% budget:** RelayEvolve **3942.61 ± 50.29** beats All-strong **3721.72 ± 152.27** (+220.9) and even surpasses All-cheap **3873.89 ± 129.43** (+68.7) — here the cheap model is the stronger single-model baseline, yet the cascade still wins.
- **Front-loading evidence (motivation):** first 20 generations contribute **76.3%** (cheap) and **79.1%** (strong) of total best-so-far improvement; cheap/strong gain ratio over those 20 generations is **0.82×, 1.01×, 0.83×** on Circle, TXN, Prism respectively.
- **Early-signal noisiness:** Spearman ρ between cheap-model trajectory rank at K and final rank is only **0.28** at K=10 and **0.32** at K=20, climbing to **0.62** at K=50 — empirically justifying maintaining multiple trajectories rather than committing to an early leader.
- **Ablation (Circle Packing Square, 100% budget):** removing relay-gain-based allocation/stopping, replacing Grow/Deepen with random, quality-only curation, diversity-only curation, random seeds, and reversing the cascade (strong→cheap) all reduce performance; the default strong-reserve split of **0.85** is the budget-split optimum.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuinely above incremental.** The reframing from per-call model routing to *population-level handoff* with a single shared marginal-reward signal is a clean conceptual contribution and is not obvious from prior work like LEVI (role-based per-call routing) or ShinkaEvolve (bandit over an LLM ensemble selector). The submodular curation at handoff is a well-motivated use of classical results (the (1-1/e) guarantee is correctly invoked).
- **Rigor: solid but narrow.** Three independent runs with mean±std is the field's bare minimum; given the noisy early-signal finding (Spearman ρ as low as 0.28), more runs (5–10) would materially tighten the error bars, especially on TXN Scheduling where RelayEvolve's std (±50–153) is comparable to its margin over All-cheap. The single non-winning setting (Prism, 50% budget: 26.2408 vs All-cheap 26.2440) is honestly disclosed but also shows the method adds nothing when a task is near saturation.
- **Reproducibility: moderate concern.** No mention of code or seed release; all experiments route through the OpenRouter API for Qwen-3.5-Flash/Plus, whose realized token costs and sampling nondeterminism are not pinned down; the ShinkaEvolve backend is cited but its exact configuration (mutation template, population size, context budget) is only summarized as "the same." The ε_rel saturation threshold, patience window p, block length h, trajectory/horizon limits, and λ are described qualitatively but their exact values are not tabulated.
- **Significance: real within a niche.** The result is confined to four program-evolution / algorithm-discovery tasks; whether population handoff generalizes to, e.g., prompt evolution, agentic-tool search, or symbolic math is untested. The claim "budget allocation should be organized around the population" is well-supported but only over this task family.
- **Red flags / weak points.** (1) Only a *single* cheap-strong pair is studied — the paper never tests a 3-tier cascade (cheap→mid→strong) or an alternative cheap model, leaving the central "which cheap model" question open. (2) AdaptEvolve (Ray et al. 2026) is excluded solely because it needs token-level logits unavailable via the API — a reasonable practical choice but it removes arguably the most direct competitor. (3) The "best of {S_g, S_o}" handoff selection and the 0.85 budget split are tuned on Circle Packing (Square) and then reused across tasks, a mild cherry-picking risk the ablation section does not fully discharge. (4) All-cheap *already* matches or beats All-strong on TXN and Prism, so on those tasks RelayEvolve's gain over All-cheap (e.g., +68.7 on TXN at 100% budget) is the honest bar, not the gain over All-strong.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated central idea (population handoff vs call-level routing) with a single shared objective coordinating allocation, stopping, and seed selection.
- Strong empirical motivation section with three concrete, quantified findings that directly justify each design choice (short blocks, multi-trajectory, cheap-first cascade, marginal set-level reward).
- Principled curation via submodular maximization with the (1-1/e) guarantee, and a thoughtful offline + online bank comparison to remove arrival-order dependence.
- Honest reporting: the one non-best setting (Prism at 50% budget) is acknowledged, and complementary single-model strengths (All-cheap winning on TXN/Prism, All-strong on Circle Packing) are surfaced rather than hidden.
- Training-free and API-only, so it is immediately deployable on any frontier cheap/strong pair without fine-tuning.

## Limitations

- **Single cheap-strong model pair only.** No multi-tier cascade, no alternative cheap model (e.g., an even smaller open model), so the sensitivity of Relay Gain and the handoff threshold to the capability gap is unmeasured.
- **Task family is narrow.** Four tasks, all program/algorithm evolution; no prompt search, agentic search, combinatorial optimization, or symbolic math benchmarks.
- **Few runs (3) for a high-variance setting.** The paper's own Finding 2 reports early-trajectory rank noise; more seeds would strengthen the close margins (e.g., Prism 50%: 26.2408 vs 26.2440).
- **Hyperparameter opacity.** ε_rel, patience window p, block length h, λ, η, k, r, trajectory/horizon limits, and the 0.85 strong-reserve default are described but not all tabulated, and several are tuned on one task.
- **API nondeterminism.** OpenRouter routing of Qwen models introduces uncontrolled sampling and cost variance that is not separated from method variance.
- **No public code/data artifacts mentioned**, limiting independent replication.
- **AdaptEvolve exclusion** removes the closest adaptive-model-selection competitor (justified by API constraints, but it weakens the comparative claim).

## Relevance

与用户对 LLM 驱动进化搜索与成本感知推理调度的研究兴趣高度相关：它直接承接 FunSearch/AlphaEvolve/ShinkaEvolve 这条算法发现主线，并提出一种无需训练、可即插即用的廉价-强模型预算分配层，适用于任何固定预算下的程序进化、提示进化或智能体搜索场景。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does population handoff generalize beyond the single cheap-strong Qwen pair — e.g., to a 3-tier (small→mid→frontier) cascade, or to a cheap model from a different family (Llama, Gemma)?
- How does Relay Gain behave when the cheap and strong models have a *smaller* capability gap (e.g., Qwen-3.5-Flash vs Qwen-3.5-Turbo) — does the handoff still fire, or does it collapse into all-cheap?
- Can Relay Gain be extended to continuous-control or symbolic-math evolution where "fitness" is noisier and multi-objective, rather than the relatively clean fitness in Circle Packing / TXN / Prism?
- What is the sensitivity of the 0.85 strong-reserve split and ε_rel threshold when tuned per-task vs globally — is the global default a robust compromise or a Circle-Packing-biased artifact?
- Could the Grow-Deepen bandit be replaced by a learned scheduler (e.g., a small transformer over trajectory embeddings) without losing the training-free appeal, and would it pay off at longer horizons (T ≫ 100)?
- Does the offline + online bank comparison (`S_g` vs `S_o`) actually differ in practice, or is one consistently dominant — and if so, can the redundant curation be dropped for efficiency?
