> **一句话总结：** 本文提出通过让 LLM 进行"随机数生成"来测量其内在的潜在数字偏好（latent number bias），并在评估阶段对数字 token 的生成概率做 logit 级修正；该方法在摘要、STS、STR 三类打分任务上超越 Vanilla、Contextual Calibration 和 Distribution Calibration，其中在 STS-B 上的平均 SDD 从 6.44（Vanilla）降到 1.80（Deb-R）。

> **TL;DR:** The authors mitigate LLM-as-a-Judge scoring bias by prompting the evaluator to generate random numbers, measuring how far the resulting distribution deviates from uniform (the "latent number bias"), and adding a logit-space correction term at scoring time; on SummEval the method halves average MSE (2.15 → 1.02 with Deb-T, beating CC's 1.40 and DC's 1.94), and on STS-B the task-conditioned variant (Deb-T) lifts average Spearman to 71.5 vs vanilla 61.9, CC 68.1, and DC 61.0, while the SDD metric drops 3-4x below all baselines.

## 核心贡献

- **提出一种基于"随机数生成"的去偏框架**：通过让 LLM 做随机数生成任务，直接量化其偏离均匀分布的程度作为"潜在数字偏好"（latent number bias），再据此构造去偏项 B(y) = log U(y) − log P_random(y)。
- **提出任务相关的去偏变体 Deb-T**：在随机数生成提示前拼接下游评估任务的指令，显式建模"不同任务下 LLM 的数字偏好不同"这一现象，实验证明 Deb-T 在大多数设置下优于任务无关的 Deb-R。
- **提出新评估指标 Score Distribution Divergence (SDD)**：基于 KL 散度衡量预测分布与真值分布之间的差距，弥补 Spearman/Pearson 只看排序、MSE 只看点估值的不足。
- **大规模实证**：覆盖 4 个任务（LLM-Align/HelpSteer2、SummEval、STS-B、SemRel2024）、5 个开源指令微调 LLM（Llama-3.1-8B、Qwen2.5-14B、Gemma-2-9B、Prometheus-7B、Mistral-7B），并与 Contextual Calibration (CC)、Distribution Calibration (DC) 两条代表性基线对比。

## Key Contributions

- A debiasing framework that estimates an LLM's inherent numerical preference via random number generation and converts the deviation from the uniform distribution into a per-token correction term B(y) = log U(y) − log P_random(y) applied to the scoring logits.
- A task-conditioned variant (Deb-T) that prepends the downstream evaluation instruction to the random-generation prompt, explicitly modelling the observation that latent number bias shifts with the task and the score range.
- A new metric, Score Distribution Divergence (SDD), that measures the average KL divergence between the predicted score distribution and the ground-truth distribution, complementing rank/point metrics.
- A comprehensive empirical study spanning four LLM-as-a-Judge tasks (HelpSteer2 helpfulness, SummEval coherence, STS-B, SemRel2024), five open-source instruction-tuned LLMs, and three baselines (Vanilla, CC, DC).

## Methodology

The method has three stages. **(1) Latent number bias measurement.** One hundred paraphrases of "generate a random integer between {{min_score}} and {{max_score}}" are produced by ChatGPT-o3; the top T_p prompts by lowest perplexity (T_p = 10 by default) are retained. For each kept prompt x_i the evaluator's per-token probability p(y|x_i) is averaged into P_random(y). Two variants are defined: **Deb-R**, using the bare random-generation prompt, and **Deb-T**, prepending the downstream task instruction to capture task-specific bias. A floor p_min = 1e−12 prevents zero probabilities.

**(2) Debiasing term.** The deviation of P_random from uniform U(y) = 1/(N+1) is encoded as a log-space term B(y) = log U(y) − log P_random(y), which is negative for numbers the LLM over-generates and positive for under-generated ones.

**(3) Inference-time correction.** During scoring, the token logits are revised as P̃(y) = softmax(l(y) + λ·B(y)), where λ (tuned on the dev set, default search 0.1–1.0) controls correction strength. Following G-Eval, the final predicted score is the probability-weighted sum s_llm = Σ P̃(y)·y rather than an argmax. The pipeline is a zero-shot, training-free, plug-in on top of any decoder LLM; temperature is fixed at 0.2.

## Results & Highlights

- **On SummEval (coherence, 5-fold CV), average MSE drops from 2.15 (Vanilla) to 1.02 with Deb-T**, a 52.6% reduction, versus 1.40 for CC and 1.94 for DC — Deb-T more than halves the error and beats the strongest baseline (CC) by 0.38 MSE.
- **On STS-B, average Spearman reaches 71.5 with Deb-T**, up from Vanilla 61.9 (+9.6 pts), CC 68.1 (+3.4 pts), and DC 61.0 (+10.5 pts); for the weakest evaluator (Llama-3.1-8B), Deb-T lifts STS-B Spearman from 7.45 (Vanilla) / 2.43 (DC) to 47.0, a +44.6 pt gain over DC and +15.5 pt over CC.
- **On the new SDD metric, Deb-R sets the state of the art on the two sentence-pair tasks**: average STS-B SDD falls to 1.80 (Deb-R) / 1.85 (Deb-T) versus Vanilla 6.44, CC 5.59, and DC 4.22 — roughly a 2.3x improvement over the best baseline (DC), and average STR SDD falls to 4.30 (Deb-T) versus Vanilla 5.39 and CC 3.73.
- **On SummEval average SDD, Deb-T achieves 2.93** versus Vanilla 3.85, CC 3.16, and DC 3.37, confirming the correction tightens the predicted distribution, not just the point estimate.
- **Deb-T generally beats Deb-R**, supporting the central hypothesis that latent number bias is task-dependent; the divergence between the two is largest exactly where Llama is weakest (STS-B: 47.0 vs 14.2 Spearman).
- **Latent bias is model-specific and score-range-specific**: Llama and Prometheus over-favour the highest score, Gemma spikes at both ends, while Qwen and Mistral cluster on "1"; Qwen/Mistral change their bias when the range is [1,5] (Summ) versus [0,5] (STS/STR).
- **The LLM-Alignment (HelpSteer2) task is an exception**: Deb-R/Deb-T only marginally improve MSE and do not help Spearman/Pearson. The authors attribute this to the vanilla LLMs (except Prometheus) already collapsing on score "3", which has low latent bias, so debiasing further inflates "3" rather than correcting it.

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors (Yuma Asato, Kiyoaki Shirai, Natthawut Kertkeidkachorn) are all at the Japan Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (JAIST). The work engages directly with the most relevant prior art on LLM-as-a-Judge bias: Sato et al. (numerical/Distribution Calibration), Zhao et al. (Contextual Calibration), Fujinuma (score-range bias), and the "LLMs are bad at randomness" line (Zhao & Du, West & Potts, Coronado-Blázquez, Hopkins et al.). The experimental design is solid: four tasks with distinct score ranges (0–4, 1–5, 0–5), five open-weight models covering four model families, four metrics (Spearman, Pearson, MSE, and the newly proposed SDD), three named baselines, proper dev/test splits (5-fold CV where no test set exists), and the G-Eval probability-weighting convention. The paper is candid about the one task where the method fails and provides a mechanistic explanation backed by Figure 4.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: High and genuinely under-explored. Prior work either used context-free prompts (CC) or Monte-Carlo/Beta-fit calibration against ground-truth labels (DC); this paper's insight — repurpose the *documented inability of LLMs to sample uniformly* as a probe of scoring bias — is a fresh and elegant reframe that needs no labels at bias-measurement time (only the score range).
- **Rigor**: The four-metric, five-model, four-task matrix with statistical correlation + MSE + a distribution-level KL metric is thorough, and the per-model latent-bias plots (Figure 3) directly support the mechanism story. However, **no significance testing (bootstrap CIs, paired t-tests) is reported** despite many "wins" being within plausible noise (e.g., STS-B Spearman Deb-T 71.5 vs CC 68.1, average over 5 models).
- **The hyperparameter λ is a quiet weakness**: it is tuned per-LLM-per-task on the dev set, and Section 4.5 admits that widening the search from λ≤1 to λ≤10 *does not* consistently help because "the best λ optimized on the development set does not always yield the best performance on the test set." This is a generalisation-gap that the paper flags but does not resolve, and it inflates the method's apparent flexibility (more knobs than baselines).
- **The LLM-Alignment failure mode is a real dent**: the method can *amplify* the wrong bias when the model's mode already lands on a low-latent-bias number ("3"). This is a structural limitation of any uniform-deviation correction, not just an unlucky dataset, and the paper does not propose a guard against it (e.g., gating λ per-sample, which they relegate to future work).
- **Baseline fairness**: DC requires fitting a Beta distribution to *ground-truth* dev scores, so it has strictly more information than Deb-R/Deb-T at calibration time; the comparison is therefore generous to the proposed method, which is fair but should be stated explicitly. CC, by contrast, is context-free and label-free like the proposed method.
- **Reproducibility**: "Codes will be available upon acceptance" — not yet public at submission. The 100 RNG prompts are generated with ChatGPT-o3, an undocumented stochastic step; the meta-prompt is given but the actual 100 prompts and the top-10 selection are not in the appendix, so exact replication requires regenerating them. T_p=10 is "intuitively set," and the floor p_min=1e−12 is an unstudied hyperparameter.
- **Significance**: Practically useful for anyone deploying open-weight LLM judges on ranking/similarity tasks, especially with weak base models; the gain is largest exactly where it is most needed (Llama-3.1-8B on STS-B). Less impactful for strong evaluators that are already well-calibrated (Qwen, Mistral on STS-B barely move).
- **Red flags**: None egregious. The +44.6 pt DC-comparison headline on STS-B/Llama is partly because DC *collapses* Llama's STS-B Spearman to 2.43 — a baseline pathology that inflates the delta; the Vanilla-to-Deb-T gain (+39.6 pts) is the more honest framing.

## Strengths

- Training-free, label-free at measurement time (only needs the score range), and a clean one-line logit-space edit — easy to bolt onto any decoder LLM judge.
- The task-conditioned variant (Deb-T) is well-motivated and the Figure 3 visualisation of per-model, per-task latent bias is genuinely informative (e.g., Qwen/Mistral's "1"-fixation, the score-range effect).
- The new SDD metric catches distribution-level improvements that Spearman/Pearson miss and is the metric on which the method wins most decisively.
- Honest reporting of the LLM-Alignment failure with a mechanistic explanation rather than a hand-wave.

## Limitations

- No significance testing; several headline margins are small and over only five models.
- The dev/test generalisation gap for λ is acknowledged but unsolved (Section 4.5).
- Only zero-shot prompts; few-shot/CoT prompts (which often change calibration behaviour) are deferred to future work.
- Smallest tested model is 7B, largest 14B — no read on whether debiasing helps or hurts with frontier-scale judges that may already be well-calibrated.
- The method can backfire when the model's mode sits on a low-latent-bias score (LLM-Alignment case); no per-sample gating is implemented.
- RNG prompt set is LLM-generated (ChatGPT-o3) and only the meta-prompt is disclosed; the prompt-selection step (T_p=10) is shown to matter little, but is "intuitively set."

## Relevance

这篇论文直接对应用户关心的"LLM-as-a-Judge / LLM 自动评估"主题，提供了一个无需标签、即插即用的去偏方法，可与现有的 G-Eval、Prometheus 等评估框架叠加使用，对提升自动评估与人类判断的相关性、以及构建更可信的 paper 排行榜评估有实际价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would per-sample adaptive λ (gated to 0 when the vanilla model is already confident/accurate) rescue the LLM-Alignment failure mode, and can it be estimated without ground truth?
- Does the latent-number-bias signal transfer — i.e., can bias measured on a cheap proxy task correct scoring on an expensive target task, or must Deb-T be re-measured per task?
- How does the method interact with few-shot and chain-of-thought judging prompts, which are known to shift both accuracy and calibration?
- Do frontier closed judges (GPT-4-class, Claude-class) exhibit the same measurable deviation from uniform in random generation, and if so does the correction still help once baseline Spearman is already >80?
- Can the correction term B(y) be learned jointly with a small reward model rather than estimated once via Monte-Carlo RNG, to smooth the dev/test λ gap?
- Is the SDD metric (KL between predicted and ground-truth score distributions) a better proxy for downstream ranking usefulness than Spearman, and does it correlate with human agreement at the per-sample level?
