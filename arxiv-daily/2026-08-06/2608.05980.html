How Far Do Simple Transformations Translate Across Text Embedding Models?

01 Methodology

Four “simple translators” are stress-tested across nine deliberately heterogeneous embedding models — spanning BERT, MPNet, RoBERTa, T5-encoder, XLM-RoBERTa, NomicBert, and a Qwen3 causal decoder, with dimensions 768–2560 and mean / CLS / last-token pooling.

Linear (pinv)

Closed-form least squares T* = YX⁺ over a small anchor set.

Relative Repr. (RR)

Re-express each embedding as cosine similarities to m anchors — isometry-invariant coordinates.

Inverse Relative Proj.

Maps the relative space back to a usable target embedding, with anchor pruning.

Linear (SGD)

Same ∥Y−TX∥² objective over a larger Wikitext-103 corpus — an empirical linear upper bound.

Section 4 re-derives all three anchor methods as variations of one template, inheriting the same fragile assumption: local linear geometry around shared anchors is preserved across models. The rest of the paper stress-tests that assumption via four complementary diagnostics: CKA (geometry), SVM downstream transfer on SST-2 / AG News / emotion / CoLA / DBpedia / IMDB / MRPC / QQP, fidelity (cosine + normalized error), and k-NN retrieval with a hubness analysis.

02 Results

Linear (SGD) dominates as the linear ceiling; IRP is the strongest anchor-based method; transfer is sharply non-uniform and often far below same-model performance.

  • IRP beats pinv & RR among anchor methods — attributed to its anchor-pruning step.
  • T5-family pair gtr-t5-large ↔ instructor-xl reaches 0.99–1.00 top-1 k-NN agreement under RR — shared T5 encoder + mean pooling + contrastive fine-tuning.
  • Training objective overrides architecture: Qwen3-Embedding-4B (causal decoder, 2560-dim) clusters closer to the encoder group than e5-large-v2 does.
  • Retrieval ≠ transfer: k-NN measures neighborhood geometry while classification depends on decision boundaries; hubness confounds k-NN evaluation.

03 The e5-large-v2 counterexample

0.25CKA · vs all others

e5-large-v2 shares BERT + mean-pooling with several high-compatibility models, yet sits at CKA 0.25–0.27 with every other model — versus 0.81 between gtr-t5-large ↔ instructor-xl and 0.66 between mpnet ↔ roberta. Its cosine distribution is compressed to [0.7, 1] rather than [−1, 1], breaking angle-sensitive metrics. Any future “latent universality” claim must explain this single, well-chosen outlier.

04 Strengths & Limitations

▲ Strengths

  • Unusually candid: Linear (SGD) is framed honestly as an upper-bound probe, not a baseline to beat.
  • Section 4’s geometric unification — three named methods reduce to one operation — is a real conceptual contribution.
  • The compatibility dendrogram + per-pair matrices give an immediately usable “stitching map.”
  • The e5-large-v2 case is sharp and well-chosen; the agent-to-agent latent-communication motivation is forward-looking.

▼ Limitations

  • Only linear/affine translators evaluated — “more complex translators are needed” is a recommendation, not a result.
  • Fixed-length embeddings only; sequence-level translation (lA·dA → lB·dB) with differing tokenizers is out of scope.
  • Anchor selection deliberately minimal — fair for a stress-test, but under-tests RR/IRP at their best.
  • No uncertainty estimates, no significance tests, no compute accounting; classification tasks only.

05 Open questions

  1. Would a shallow nonlinear translator (2-layer MLP, or learned orthogonal+scale) close the gap on the hard incompatible pairs — or is the incompatibility fundamentally non-smooth?
  2. Which property of e5-large-v2’s weakly-supervised contrastive pretraining drives its [0.7, 1] cosine distribution — data scale, the weak signal, or the lack of hard negatives?
  3. How do compatibility clusters shift when anchors are selected jointly across all nine models, and what is the minimum anchor count to plateau?
  4. Can sequence-level translation be attacked by a position-wise translator + learned token-alignment, or does it require full cross-attention?
  5. Does the “training objective overrides architecture” finding hold for other causal-decoder embeddings trained with different objectives?
