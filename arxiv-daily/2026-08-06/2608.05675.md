> **一句话总结：** 本文给出 VLM 读图可靠性检测的可计算盲点——错误不可见当且仅当它与编辑的答案变换可交换（即落在联合中心化子中）；据此提出无需标注、无需训练的 Equivariance-Consistency Score（ECS）与 REND-EQUIV 基准，用两个互补编辑（缩放 ×c、平移 +δ）对仿射误差完全可测、循环重标注将标签误差检测率从 5.3% 提升到 44.0%。

> **TL;DR:** This paper proves that a systematic VLM figure-reading error is invisible to a data edit exactly when it commutes with the edit's answer-transform, so the undetectable errors form the joint centralizer of the suite—a closed form, not a guess. It builds the label-free Equivariance-Consistency Score (ECS) and the REND-EQUIV benchmark: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (N=900) ECS flags 53.6% of invariance-blind errors (IBEM) vs re-rendering-agreement (REA)'s 0.0%, and REA∧ECS lifts correctness-detection AUROC from 0.788 to 0.906 (+0.118); cyclic relabeling raises label-error detection 8× (5.3%→44.0%) on a matched m=6 sample.

## 核心贡献

- **可检测性的代数刻画（Theorem 2）。** 把"一个系统性读图错误是否会被某编辑套件发现"从经验问题变成可写下来的闭式结论：错误不可见当且仅当它与套件中每个编辑的答案变换 φ_g 都可交换，即落在它们的联合中心化子 C_τ(G)=∩_g C_τ(φ_g) 中；该集合随编辑增加单调收缩、随容差增大单调扩张。不变性关系对应 φ_g=id，一切皆与之可交换，因此盲点是关系本身的属性而非具体实现的缺陷——适用于重渲染、prompt 扰动、元关系鲁棒性套件等所有不变性家族方法。
- **匹配的"完全 vs 不完全"对（Theorem 4 / Proposition 5 / Corollary 6）。** 对仿射误差类 E_aff={a↦αa+β}，两元素套件 {×c, +δ}（c≠1, δ≠0）可完全检测每一个非恒等仿射误差，且单独任一编辑都不够（各自只覆盖 αa 或 a+β）。对标签排列类 S_m，任何 swap 套件都不完全（swap 落在自己的中心化子里），一个 swap 留下 2(m−2)! 个不可测误差；用 m 阶循环重标注替换后降为 m，m≥5 时严格更优（m=6 时从 48 降到 6）。
- **ECS 与 REND-EQUIV 基准。** 把上述理论实例化为无标注、免训练的检测器 ECS = (1/|G|) Σ 1[ρ_g≤τ_g]，并发布 REND-EQUIV：每个实例在同一数据上同时配对不变性集（K=8 次风格重渲染）与等变性集（≤3 个受控编辑）。
- **理论预测的顺序在真实模型与真实误差上得到确认。** 检测率随中心化子大小排序（缩放 57/64 > 平移 48/64 > 删最大值 4/24 > swap 2/31 ≈ identity 13/97），数值型 vs 非数值型在三个模型上经 Bonferroni 校正后显著（p<0.0002）。手工标注的 200 例 REA-独立样本复现同一排序，且第二个不变性家族方法（扰动渲染风格+问题措辞的 VL-Uncertainty 风格）在 IBEM 上检出率为 0.0%，证实盲点属于关系而非实现。
- **解释了元测试文献中的"反转"。** 对分类器的经典元测试中，标签排列最敏感、仿射最不敏感，与本文读图场景顺序相反；Theorem 2 把两者统一为"可检测性是关系与错误类的联合属性"，而非关系的固有属性——这是本文最有力的证据，因为理论并未为任一结论专门拟合。

## Methodology

The paper is theory-first and the experiments are designed to test predictions rather than maximize a leaderboard number.

**Setup.** A figure is rendered from data D by program R(D;θ) in cosmetic style θ; a quantitative question q has an exact program-computed answer a*(D,q). An *edit operator* g acts on the data and is paired with a *known answer-transform* φ_g satisfying a*(g(D),q)=φ_g(a*(D,q)) (Eq. 1). Edits are realized by re-rendering g(D) in the *same* style as D, isolating the data change from any style change. A *systematic* (style-invariant) error e maps a*→e(a*) identically across base and edited renderings.

**Core algebraic object — the centralizer.** For an edit g and tolerance τ, define C_τ(φ_g) = { e ∈ E : d(e(φ_g(a*)), φ_g(e(a*))) ≤ τ } — the errors φ_g cannot separate. C_0(φ_g) is the group-theoretic centralizer. Lemma 1 shows the equivariance residual ρ_g vanishes iff e commutes with φ_g at a*. Theorem 2 then says the suite's undetectable set is U_τ(G)=∩_{g∈G} C_τ(φ_g) — the joint centralizer — which is antitone in G (adding edits can only shrink it) and monotone in τ. Corollary 3: because C_τ(id)=E, adding an invariance/identity edit contributes *nothing* structurally.

**Equivariance-Consistency Score (ECS).** ρ_g = d(ŷ_g, φ_g(ŷ)) with relative error for continuous answers and 0/1 disagreement for categorical/ordinal ones; ECS(D,q) = (1/|G|) Σ 1[ρ_g ≤ τ_g] ∈ [0,1]. τ_g is 0 for exact categorical/ordinal transforms and a fixed 5% relative tolerance for continuous answers. ECS references the model's *own* base answer ŷ, not a*, so it is label-free. An answer is flagged unreliable when ECS < t. The paper also studies min(REA, ECS), which by Theorem 2 must flag a superset of what invariance alone flags.

**Completeness results (the design rules).** Theorem 4 (affine): {×c, +δ} detects every non-identity affine error — e(φ_c(a))−φ_c(e(a))=β(1−c) forces β=0, and e(ψ_δ(a))−ψ_δ(e(a))=δ(α−1) forces α=1, giving α=1,β=0 jointly; pointwise and independent of a. Proposition 5 (permutations): for S_m acting on labels, a swap (A B) leaves 2(m−2)! of m! errors undetectable (it centralizes itself), while an order-m cycle leaves only m. Corollary 6: a cycle strictly beats a swap for m≥5 (m=6: 48→6).

**Five operators in REND-EQUIV.** Scale (×c, multiplicative), offset (+Δ, additive), delete-max (ordinal), swap-labels (permutation), axis-range/zoom (identity, invariance control). The first two instantiate the complete affine suite; swap instantiates the incomplete one; zoom is the control whose centralizer is everything.

## Results & Highlights

All numbers are reproduced from real model inference; the generator, edit suite, and harness were rebuilt end-to-end. Models: Qwen2.5-VL-7B (primary, N=900 over 3 seeds), Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL2-8B (N=1500 each, 5 seeds). Total compute < 3 GPU-hours on A100.

- **Predicted edit-type ordering holds (Table 1, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, N=900).** Flagged rates on IBEM by edit type: scale 57/64, offset 48/64, delete-max 4/24, swap-labels 2/31, identity 13/97. Numeric-vs-non-numeric gap significant after Bonferroni on all three models (p<0.0002); scale vs offset does not differ significantly (Fisher exact p=0.064, Bonferroni p=0.192), and no ordering is claimed or predicted between them.
- **ECS reaches the invariance-blind population (IBEM) that invariance provably cannot (Table 2, Qwen2.5-VL-7B).** At a matched 14% false-positive rate on correct instances, ECS flags 53.6% of IBEM vs REA's 0.0% (zero by construction), token logprob 25.8%, self-consistency 23.1%, semantic entropy 25.6%. Perturbation uncertainty (a second, independent invariance-family method) flags 0.0% of its IBEM sample — matching REA and confirming Corollary 3.
- **Combining REA with ECS lifts correctness AUROC from 0.788 to 0.906** (gain +0.118, 95% CI [0.089, 0.149], template-clustered, excluding zero) and improves RC-AUC from 0.077 to 0.071; the combined gain over REA alone is +0.118, +0.100, +0.075 across the three models, all excluding zero. High-confidence calibration improves from 0.140 [0.111,0.169] to 0.055 [0.041,0.080] (non-overlapping intervals).
- **Cyclic relabeling delivers its predicted ~8× gain on a matched real sample (Corollary 6 tested directly).** On 150 matched m=6 instances, cyclic relabeling flags 66/150 (44.0%) vs swap's 8/150 (5.3%); of 62 disagreements, the cycle uniquely catches 60 and swap uniquely catches 2 — matching the closed-form prediction 2(m−2)!/m = 48/6.
- **REA-independent hand-labeled check removes the one circularity.** 200 wrong-answer instances hand-labeled by algebraic type: 151/200 (75.5%) affine, 49/200 (24.5%) permutation, zero needing a third category. Detection rates on this REA-independent population are 92.1% for affine-labeled errors and 29.8% for permutation-labeled errors — the coverage gap concentrates exactly where Proposition 5 predicts.
- **Pilot on real (non-synthetic) ChartQA figures (N=300, 94.8% extraction success).** A ×2 scale edit applied after digitization-assisted re-plotting yields ECS-inconsistency rates of 47.5% for GPT-4o and 22.0% for Claude Sonnet 4.

## Authors & Credibility

Two authors (Rasul Khanbayov, Hasan Kurban) from the College of Science and Engineering, Hamad Bin Khalifa University, Doha, Qatar, affiliated with Kurban Intelligence Lab. Reference [4] is the authors' own prior work (the REA re-rendering-agreement line this paper extends), so the equivariance contribution is a natural and clearly-motivated next step rather than a disconnected result. Proofs (Lemma 1, Theorems 2/4, Proposition 5, Theorem 7) are given in full in Appendix A, including the non-invertible (constant-map) case for E_aff. Code, edit operators, the ECS implementation, the matched REND-EQUIV sets, and evaluation scripts are released at the project GitHub with pinned versions and seeds; raw per-instance outputs and exact commands are part of the artifact. The paper is unusually disciplined about negative evidence: it states the one circularity (IBEM is defined by REA≥0.75, itself re-rendering agreement) rather than burying it, then removes it with the hand-labeled check, and reports that two of five operators are measurably easier than base rather than assuming the difficulty-control holds.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The central move — identifying detectability with commutation and the joint centralizer — is genuinely new for the VLM-reliability setting and buys a *closed form* where the metamorphic-testing literature (Li et al. [8]) has only a Set-Cover-hard combinatorial problem. The framing of invariance's blind spot as "everything commutes with id" (Corollary 3) is crisp and falsifiable, and the explanation of the Xie et al. [32] classifier-metamorphic-testing inversion (affine vs permutation swap roles by fault class) is a real unification, not a post-hoc rationalization.

**Rigor.** The headline test is a *prediction fixed before measurement* (Table 1) with its own negative cases (identity, swap), which is far stronger than a post-hoc AUROC gain. Bonferroni correction is applied; per-seed breakdowns and template-clustered intervals are reported instead of a naive row-level bootstrap. However: (a) the affine-completeness guarantee (Theorem 4) is a statement about the *class* E_aff, and the authors themselves hand-label only 75.5% of real errors as affine and 24.5% as permutation — so the completeness theorem covers at most ~3/4 of observed failures, and the remaining permutation channel is *provably* incomplete (Proposition 5); the paper is honest about this but readers should not read "two edits suffice" as a blanket guarantee. (b) The difficulty-control assumption is *not* fully validated: delete-max and offset are consistently 6.9–12.5 and 3.8–8.4 points easier than base across all three models, so some residuals on those two operators could reflect easier edited figures rather than caught errors — the paper concedes this but the theory-ordering defense, while reasonable, is not a direct control. (c) The identity/zoom control is measured on a subpopulation *selected* for stability under cosmetic re-rendering, which is partly circular as a test of Theorem 2; the REA-independent hand-labeled check mitigates this but does not fully replace it for the identity row specifically.

**Reproducibility.** Strong: standard-library-style claims, all numbers reproducible from released code/seeds, <3 GPU-hours total, public models. The 200-instance hand-labeling was performed by the authors (not external annotators), which is a limitation on label objectivity but disclosed.

**Significance.** The design rule (Corollary 6: prefer cyclic relabeling over swaps for m≥5) is immediately actionable for anyone building a chart-VQA robustness suite, and the commutation framing generalizes beyond charts to any setting with a computable answer-transform. The aggregate practical gain is *modest* — ECS loses to REA on general-purpose AUROC (0.881 vs 0.788 is the combined win, not ECS alone) and the headline IBEM win is on a narrow 10.8% subpopulation — so this is more a theoretical-clarification paper than a SOTA-beating detector.

**Red flags.** None severe. The main overclaiming risk is linguistic: phrases like "provably complete" and "blind spot" are precisely scoped in the math but could be misread by a skimming reader as covering all reading errors; the paper's own Limitations section is appropriately narrow. A high ECS is explicitly and correctly flagged as *not* a correctness certificate (errors in the joint centralizer pass silently).

## Strengths

- Converts an empirical/incremental question ("which edits should I include?") into a decidable one with a closed-form answer (the joint centralizer), sidestepping the NP-hardness that plagues combinatorial MR-selection.
- Theory generates falsifiable, pre-registered predictions — including *predicted failures* (identity, swap) — and these are the primary evidence, which is rare and credible.
- Honest about every soft spot: the IBEM/REA circularity, the two operators that fail the difficulty control, the 24.5% of errors outside E_aff, and the modest aggregate gain. The repair of three defects in an earlier pilot (Appendix D) is disclosed rather than hidden.
- Second independent invariance-family baseline (perturbation uncertainty) confirms the blind spot is a property of the relation family, not of re-rendering specifically — this substantially de-risks the central claim.
- The Xie et al. [32] inversion explanation is a non-trivial unification that the theory was not fitted to.

## Limitations

- The completeness guarantee (Theorem 4) is only for the affine class E_aff; ~24.5% of real errors are permutation-type and are *provably* not completely coverable by any swap suite. Cyclic relabeling shrinks but does not eliminate this gap, and the main REND-EQUIV release still uses swap edits throughout, so the cycle recommendation is forward-looking, not a property of the released dataset.
- ECS requires the figure's underlying data, so it applies natively only to programmatically renderable figures; real figures need digitization (94.8% success in the ChartQA pilot), and scanned figures are not addressed at all.
- ECS detects but does not repair; it costs |G|≤3 extra forward passes; and it has false negatives by construction for any error in the joint centralizer.
- Difficulty control partially fails: delete-max and offset edits are measurably easier than base on all three models, leaving a residual confound for those two operators specifically.
- The 200-instance hand-labeling is author-performed (no inter-annotator agreement reported), and the headline IBEM win is concentrated on a narrow subpopulation (pie charts + arithmetic questions, ~10.8% of instances) where errors happen to be affine.
- The real-figure ChartQA pilot uses only a single edit type (×2 scale) and two API models, so the suite-composition conclusions do not transfer to real figures yet.

## Relevance

直接相关：本文为 VLM 读图（chart/question answering）提供了一种无需标注、无需训练的可靠性/幻觉检测信号，与我对视觉-语言模型可靠性评估、不确定性量化和 chart understanding 的研究兴趣高度契合；其"可检测性=可交换性"的代数刻画也为设计元测试/对抗编辑套件提供了可迁移的方法论工具。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the commutation/centralizer characterization be extended to *stochastic* (input-dependent) error maps beyond the distributional Theorem 7 framing — e.g., to bound detection probability under a learned error model rather than an opaque P?
- The hand-labeling shows a 75.5/24.5 affine/permutation split with zero "other" — is this an artifact of the two-category forcing, or do real chart-reading errors genuinely decompose this cleanly? What does the residual 0% "third category" look like if a richer taxonomy is allowed?
- Corollary 6 recommends cyclic relabeling for m≥5, but the released REND-EQUIV still uses swaps; what is the effect on downstream reliability metrics once the cycle replacement is rolled out across the full benchmark?
- The ChartQA pilot reports ECS-inconsistency rates (47.5% GPT-4o, 22.0% Claude Sonnet 4) but not detection AUROC or IBEM-style correctness gains — does the equivariance signal still beat invariance-family baselines on real (digitized) figures, where extraction noise may dominate?
- Could the affine-completeness pair {×c, +δ} be augmented with one or two non-affine edits to provably cover a larger error class (e.g., piecewise-affine or log-axis errors), and what would the corresponding centralizer calculation look like?
- The theory assumes the same error map e is applied to base and edited renderings; how robust are the detection rates when the model's error is *not* style-invariant (i.e., e depends on θ), as might happen with OCR-sensitive failures?
