A paired longitudinal benchmark proves that self-evolution — not backbone strength — drives +9 to +19 point gains across four agent scaffolds on real financial workflows.

FinEvo-Bench: A Longitudinal Benchmark for Self-Evolving Agents in Professional Financial Workflows

Bo Deng · Kang Zhou · Lifan Guo · Chongyang Tao · Xuanren Chen · Chenggang Xie · Renzhao Liang · Feng Chen · Chi Zhang — Beihang Univ. & Alibaba Qwen DianJin

self-evolving agents longitudinal evaluation financial workflows paired state-reset control rubric judge · ICC 0.95 120 tasks / 20 scenes
⬢ Key Metric
+19.37pts

Largest self-evolution gain (Codex) over state-reset control. Range +9.33 → +19.37 across all four scaffolds; compliance issues drop 0.12–0.44/task.

⬢ Why This Matters

Hits two live research lines — self-evolving agent evaluation & finance-domain LLM agents. Its paired state-reset protocol is a reusable methodology template for any longitudinal agent benchmark.

⬢ Critical Edge

Carrier comparison is limited to Claude Code alone — the headline "skill-only > memory-only > combined" finding may be a retrieval-engineering artifact, not a general property of agent memory.

01 · Methodology

  • 120 tasks, 20 scenes, 6 domains — institution-provided procedures & eligible cases, PII removed, 775 input files (avg 6.46/task).
  • Paired longitudinal protocol — three independently shuffled globally-interleaved streams; each evolving run paired with a per-task state-reset control on the same permutation.
  • Shared backbone — Qwen3.7-Max (1M ctx, greedy, temp 0) across all four scaffolds isolates scaffold effects.
  • Independent judge — Claude Opus 4.6 rubric agent, separate from evaluated scaffolds; ICC(A,1) = 0.95 vs. human expert.
  • Feedback discipline — scaffold sees only rubric-based problem summaries, never the full rubric or item-level scores, forcing generalization.

03 · Results & Highlights

  • All four scaffolds beat state-reset controls on all three shuffled streams — scores +9.33 → +19.37, compliance issues −0.12 → −0.44/task.
  • Activated experience ⇒ higher scores — selective-retrieval scaffolds score +8.31 → +9.06 pts higher on tasks where experience fires (Codex 102/120 activated).
  • Rubric feedback > reference-answer feedback across all scaffolds — +3.95 → +7.93 pts, fewer compliance issues, 4–9 more activations per run.
  • All 5 capability dimensions improve; report quality is the biggest gainer (+6.85 → +18.17 pp).
  • Scene isolation (5 scenes/30 tasks): score deltas small & mixed, but isolation triggers +0.01–0.05 more compliance issues/task — evidence of cross-scene transfer of cautious practices.
  • Judge reliability: ICC(A,1) = 0.95, 95% CI [0.93, 0.97], mean abs. diff 1.6 / max 5 pts on 0–100 scale.

04 · Strengths

  • Methodological rigor above field average — paired controls, 3 shuffles, shared backbone, ICC-validated judge make deltas credible.
  • Real professional content — institution-provided procedures & eligible cases give ecological validity synthetic benchmarks lack.
  • Honest reporting — separates evolved score from self-evolution gain, avoiding the "strongest agent = best learner" conflation.
  • Actionable design insights — skill-over-memory carrier, rubric-over-reference feedback, late-rank-gains-more trend.
  • Compliance as a first-class metric — rare in agent benchmarks, essential for finance.

05 · Limitations

  • Single backbone (Qwen3.7-Max) — do gains survive on weaker / non-Qwen models? Parametric/RL evolution out of scope.
  • Carrier ablation run on Claude Code only — combined memory–skill null result mechanistically under-explained.
  • Cross-scene diagnostic underpowered — 5 scenes / 30 tasks / one shuffle for compliance-transfer claims.
  • Cost reported as tokens only — no latency, dollar cost, or model-call count.
  • Judge validated on one run — cross-run/cross-stream consistency not reported; residual same-vendor exposure.
  • "Real-case-grounded" not fully auditable — 775 input files & rubrics may not be releasable, limiting replication.

06 · Questions for Further Exploration

  • Do the +9–19 pt gains and skill-over-memory ranking hold on backbones other than Qwen3.7-Max, or on parametric/RL evolution (RAGEN, AFlow)?
  • Why does combined memory–skill underperform skill-only in Claude Code — retrieval bottleneck, context dilution, or reflection quality?
  • How robust is the rubric judge across the three shuffled streams, and does Claude-judging-Claude-family bias the cross-scaffold ranking?
  • Would scaling the cross-scene diagnostic beyond 30 tasks change the mixed-score / consistent-compliance conclusion?
  • Can the paired interleaved-stream protocol port to legal, medical, engineering domains where shared procedures recur across distinct cases?
  • Can a semantic index lift GenericAgent's 71/120 activation toward Codex's 102/120 without sacrificing its token-efficiency edge?
