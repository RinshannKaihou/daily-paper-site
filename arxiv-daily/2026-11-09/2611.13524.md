> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"RSI Tax"（自我改进过程的 token 开销）并将其计入端到端成本，在 SWE-Chain-Evo 与 Terminal-Bench 2.1 两条任务流、两个模型上系统评估六种递归自我改进方法，发现一个仅维护 2048 token 有界笔记的极简基线 SRN 以平均少用 65.6–71.0% token 的代价匹配或超越 Meta-Harness、EvoSkill、RRSI 的精度，且验证（verification）环节占这些昂贵方法 RSI Tax 的 74.8–93.6%，大量开销对应的产物后续从未被使用。

> **TL;DR:** On two task streams (SWE-Chain-Evo repo chains, 105 positions; Terminal-Bench 2.1, 89 tasks) with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GPT-6 Luna, the authors show that a trivial baseline — Simple Reflection with Notes (SRN), a single ≤2,048-token note revised from failed-task traces — matches or beats the observed accuracy of Meta-Harness, EvoSkill, and RRSI while saving 65.6–71.0% of total tokens on average (e.g., on DeepSeek/Terminal-Bench, SRN reaches 78.7% accuracy at 214.65M tokens vs Meta-Harness's 77.5% at 762.57M). Decomposing the "RSI Tax" (the token cost of self-improvement) reveals verification alone consumes 74.8–93.6% of these methods' update budgets — EvoSkill spends 497.45M tokens re-scoring its old frontier on DeepSeek/Terminal, more than the 182.26M spent actually serving the 89 tasks — and 23.7–63.6% of RSI tokens fund artifacts never used later. The paper is a rigorous cost-accounting wake-up call: expensive RSI pipelines as currently built are often not worth their tax.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 RSI Tax 概念与流式评估协议**：将自我改进过程本身消耗的 token（包括尝试后未采纳的更新）定义为"RSI Tax"，要求在任务流上先记录每个任务的成绩、再用其反馈更新智能体，从而把改进开销与任务执行开销合并为端到端成本进行公平比较。
- **一个反直觉的极简基线 SRN（Simple Reflection with Notes）**：仅维护一份上限 2048 token 的经验笔记，每完成 4 个任务后依据标量验证结果选取失败轨迹修订一次笔记，无候选搜索、无验证评估、无检索——却在四个模型×环境设置中的三个里取得最高观测精度（第四个与 EvoSkill 持平），同时平均比 Meta-Harness / EvoSkill / RRSI 少用 65.6% / 71.0% / 68.2% 的总 token。
- **RSI Tax 的三路分解与"后续使用"归因**：把改进开销拆为验证（Verify）、探索（Explore）、写作（Write）三类，并追踪每笔开销关联的状态/产物是否被后续任务实际使用；发现验证占三种昂贵方法 RSI Tax 的 74.8–93.6%，且 23.7–63.6% 的 RSI 开销对应从未被复用的产物。
- **揭示"省略税负会反转成本结论"**：只看任务执行 token 时 Meta-Harness 在 DeepSeek 终端流上比 SRN 更省（184.13M vs 214.37M），但其 578.45M 的 RSI Tax 使总成本反转为 762.57M vs 214.65M——说明仅报告执行成本的评估方式会系统性低估昂贵自我改进方法的真实代价。
- **为 RSI 研究提出新的设计目标**：改进策略应联合优化更新开销与未来任务执行的期望成本，而非只追求精度增益。

## Key Contributions

- **The RSI Tax framing and streaming protocol.** The token cost of the self-improvement process itself (charged even when an update is ultimately not adopted) is defined as the RSI Tax and added to task-execution tokens for end-to-end evaluation. Each task on the stream is scored *before* its feedback is used for updates, so accuracy is never inflated by re-running already-scored tasks.
- **SRN, a deliberately minimal adaptive baseline.** One bounded note (≤2,048 tokens), revised once per four completed tasks from scalar-outcome-selected failure traces, with no candidate search, no validation rollouts, no retrieval. Across the four model–environment settings it achieves the highest observed accuracy in three and ties EvoSkill in the fourth, at 65.6–71.0% mean total-token savings versus Meta-Harness, EvoSkill, and RRSI — a counterexample to the claim that costly update procedures are needed for the observed performance.
- **A three-way decomposition of the RSI Tax with use attribution.** Update costs are split into Verify (incumbent/candidate validation), Explore (extra rollouts, judgment, proposals, embeddings), and Write (persistent-state edits), and each cost item is tagged by whether its associated state/artifact is actually used in a later task. Verification dominates: 74.8% (Meta-Harness), 78.1% (EvoSkill), 93.6% (RRSI) of their aggregate RSI Taxes.
- **Evidence that omitted taxes invert cost rankings.** On the DeepSeek terminal stream, Meta-Harness uses fewer task-execution tokens than SRN (184.13M vs 214.37M), but its 578.45M RSI Tax pushes its total to 762.57M vs SRN's 214.65M at slightly lower accuracy (77.5% vs 78.7%).
- **An actionable research agenda:** jointly optimize update spend and expected downstream task-execution cost (when to update, how much to spend on validation, which artifacts to keep).

## Methodology

The paper evaluates **six methods** — Static, ReasoningBank (memory extraction + retrieval), EvoSkill (skill-folder search), Meta-Harness (controller-code search), RRSI (regularized harness editing), and the proposed SRN — on **two task streams**: (1) SWE-Chain-Evo repository tasks, a selected subset of 7 chains × 15 ordered positions = 105 tasks (state resets at chain boundaries; each task starts from its prescribed repo snapshot); (2) Terminal-Bench 2.1, all 89 heterogeneous terminal tasks in one fixed order with persistent learned state. Two backbones are used — **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash** and **GPT-6 Luna** — under a shared KIRA agent, high reasoning effort, 262,144-token context, 65,536-token output limit, ≤100 agent turns, yielding 6 × 2 × (105 + 89) = 2,328 scored method–task pairs.

**Streaming protocol.** For each task t, execution produces a trajectory τ_t and a binary verifier outcome y_t which is recorded *before* the task's feedback becomes available for updates. Updates may read public instructions, visible trajectories, and scalar outcomes — never future tasks, reference solutions, or hidden tests. SRN, Meta-Harness, and EvoSkill get an update opportunity every four completed tasks (support set = 2 most recent tasks on DeepSeek/SWE, 4 elsewhere); ReasoningBank keeps its per-task memory workflow.

**Cost accounting.** Every model call is classified as task-execution or self-improvement (disjoint; request IDs prevent double counting; cached input included). The RSI Tax rate is ρ = N_RSI/N_task (100% means updates cost as much as all task execution). SRN itself is nearly free: its note-revision operation totals just 1.21M tokens across all four settings.

**Decomposition and use attribution.** RSI calls are labeled Verify / Explore / Write / Other, and additionally tagged by whether the associated state or artifact (harness, skill, memory, note) is actually used by a later task before the next reset or stream end. The authors are explicit that this attribution records *subsequent use*, not a measured causal effect on accuracy.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline cost–accuracy result:** SRN saves on average **65.6%** of total tokens vs Meta-Harness, **71.0%** vs EvoSkill, and **68.2%** vs RRSI while matching or exceeding their observed accuracy (mean accuracy across the four settings: SRN **59.18%** vs Meta-Harness 57.10%, EvoSkill 55.35%, RRSI 54.94%, Static 54.46%, ReasoningBank 53.56%).
- **SRN vs Static:** +**4.7 percentage points** mean accuracy for only a **3.6%** mean increase in total tokens; SRN's update calls are **<0.4%** of its total tokens (RSI Tax rate 0.04–0.36% across settings).
- **Per-setting extremes (Table 2):** DeepSeek/SWE — SRN 48.6% accuracy at 492.56M tokens vs EvoSkill 41.0% at 1,166.51M; GPT-6/SWE — SRN 41.0% at 157.07M vs Static 38.1% at 179.69M (12.6% cheaper *and* 2.9 points better); GPT-6/Terminal — SRN 68.5% at 87.08M vs RRSI 61.8% at 454.30M.
- **The RSI Tax can dwarf task execution (Table 3):** RSI Tax rates reach 314–393% for EvoSkill and 329% for Meta-Harness on Terminal-Bench streams (e.g., GPT-6/Luna: Meta-Harness 329.15%, EvoSkill 358.57%) — i.e., updates cost 3–4× the tokens of actually serving the benchmark.
- **Verification dominates:** Verify accounts for **74.8%** of Meta-Harness's, **78.1%** of EvoSkill's, and **93.6%** of RRSI's aggregate RSI Tax. On DeepSeek/Terminal, EvoSkill burns **497.45M tokens** re-scoring its existing frontier vs **182.26M** on the 89 arriving tasks; its rollouts/proposals cost 455.29M vs only 4.59M for actual skill materialization.
- **Much of the tax buys nothing used:** **52.78%** (Meta-Harness), **37.91%** (EvoSkill), **63.57%** (ReasoningBank), and **23.70%** (RRSI) of RSI tokens are associated with states/artifacts never used later; Meta-Harness alone assigns 564.85M verification + 217.60M exploration + 69.22M writing tokens to candidates that were never deployed. SRN's update cost is 100% subsequently used.

The work is methodologically transparent to an unusual degree: full accounting conventions (Appendix B), per-method configuration disclosures (Appendix A.3, including exactly how EvoSkill/Meta-Harness were adapted to streaming), the verbatim SRN prompt (Appendix A.4), and an unusually candid limitations section. Benchmarks (SWE-Chain-Evo, Terminal-Bench 2.1) and compared systems (ReasoningBank, EvoSkill, Meta-Harness, RRSI) are current and relevant rather than strawmen from years past.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The framing is the contribution rather than a new method: nobody previously isolated the *token cost of the improvement process itself* as a first-class metric with a clean three-way decomposition plus use attribution. The SRN baseline is intentionally trivial — that is the point, but it means the paper offers no new capability, only an evaluation lens and a cautionary result.
- **Rigor:** Single run per method–setting (2,328 pairs but no seeds/repeats), so the paper carefully says "observed accuracy" everywhere and never claims significance; differences like SRN 78.7% vs Meta-Harness 77.5% on DeepSeek/Terminal are within plausible run-to-run noise, which the authors acknowledge ("repeated runs are needed to assess the stability"). The within-setting-ratio-then-average macro procedure is sensible and stated precisely.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong on accounting (request-ID dedup, cached-input inclusion, category definitions in Table 1) and the SRN prompt is published verbatim. But there is no code/data release mentioned, model identifiers are router names ("deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash", "openai/gpt-6-luna"), and the SWE-Chain-Evo subset (7 of 50 chains) selection criterion is only partially specified — reproducing the exact streams would require the authors' chain picks.
- **Fairness caveats the authors themselves flag:** the Meta-Harness configuration is *narrower* than its original population-based search (one iteration, strict-improvement selector), EvoSkill's rolling frontier re-scoring is the authors' streaming implementation whose full cost is charged, and the original EvoSkill proposer could inspect ground-truth answers while this evaluation's information boundary forbids it. So the expensive methods may underperform their published variants — the "RSI is not worth the cost" conclusion is really "these streaming adaptations are not worth the cost."
- **Use attribution is descriptive, not causal:** "not used later" does not prove those tokens are removable (early exploration can shape later updates), a confound the paper concedes but does not resolve with controlled ablations.
- **Token volume ≠ money:** cached input is counted at full weight and is ~98.1% of the RSI Tax for Meta-Harness/EvoSkill; since cached input is typically billed far lower, the *monetary* gap between methods is likely smaller than the token gap suggests.
- **Significance:** High. This is the kind of negative-result cost accounting the agent-learning field currently lacks, and it directly challenges the evaluation convention of reporting adaptation gains while omitting update costs.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-defined metric (RSI Tax) with an explicit, auditable accounting scheme that catches costs even for rejected updates.
- The SRN baseline is an elegant experimental control: it isolates "cheap cumulative note-keeping" from "expensive search + validation" and shows the latter buys nothing observable in these settings.
- The Verify/Explore/Write decomposition plus subsequent-use attribution turns a blanket "RSI is expensive" claim into actionable diagnostics (verification is the target; ~98% of it is input tokens from re-running support tasks).
- Honest hedging throughout: "observed accuracy," explicit macro-averaging formulas, a limitations section that pre-empts the main objections.
- Evaluation on current, deployment-relevant streams (SWE repo chains, terminal tasks) with two frontier-class backbones rather than toy domains.

## Limitations

- No repeated runs or variance estimates; several headline accuracy gaps (1–3 points) could be noise, so "matches or exceeds" should be read as point-estimate comparisons.
- Only 7 of SWE-Chain-Evo's 50 chains and a single fixed ordering of Terminal-Bench 2.1; ordering and chain-selection sensitivity are untested.
- Compared methods are streaming adaptations, not their original published procedures — arguably necessary for a common protocol, but it means the original Meta-Harness/EvoSkill papers' settings are not directly refuted.
- The paper compares fixed update procedures; it does not implement or test the adaptive update policy its own discussion section motivates.
- Token-based accounting ignores per-token pricing differences (input vs output vs cached) and wall-clock/latency costs of long validation loops.
- SRN's design is not ablated (note size, update cadence, failure-only selection are all fixed choices), so we don't know which ingredient drives its gains.

## Relevance

本文直击 LLM 智能体"自我改进/经验积累"方向的核心成本问题：在任务流部署中，学习过程的 token 开销（RSI Tax）可能数倍于任务执行本身，而一个几乎零成本的笔记基线即可达到同等精度。对任何关注 agent 持续学习、skill/memory 累积与成本高效评估的工作，这篇论文提供了新的度量框架与关键警示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would an adaptive policy that *predicts* expected future use before validating a candidate (e.g., cheap retrieval-likelihood screening before full verification rollouts) close most of the verification-dominated tax?
- Do the conclusions hold under the original, unrestricted Meta-Harness population search and EvoSkill v2 (which adopts each epoch's revision without validation) — i.e., is the tax an artifact of validation-heavy streaming adaptations?
- How much of the 23.7–63.6% "never-used" RSI spend is causally removable? A controlled intervention deleting those calls (freezing the affected update paths) would directly test the descriptive attribution.
- Since ~98.1% of the RSI Tax is input tokens, would frontier-refresh verification with smaller/cheaper verifier models or log-prob scoring retain selection quality at a fraction of the cost?
- Does SRN's advantage persist on longer streams (hundreds of tasks per chain) where a 2,048-token note may saturate while skill libraries keep growing?
