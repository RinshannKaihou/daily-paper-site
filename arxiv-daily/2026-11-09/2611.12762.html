+6.1 over the strongest baseline AI Scientist-v2 (47.9) — first on all 8 metrics, ELO 1322.5 (66.3% pairwise win rate), and faster: 29.9 min vs 39.3 min.

Why This Matters

A concrete blueprint for research-agent memory, search regulation, and method reuse — backed by a contamination-safe benchmark and five physically validated projects.

⚠ Critical Edge

No ablation isolates the metacognitive layer — the +6.1 gain may come from structured scaffolding and 10-way parallel generation, not the architecture itself.

Methodology

  • Object level tracks the scientific search as X = (Target Tree, Idea Graph, Evidence State) — question decomposition, proposal nodes linked by five transformations (Merge / Fuse / Diverge / Repair / Evolve), and source-linked judgments with applicability conditions.
  • Meta level holds a revisable Cognitive State (what is established, unresolved conflicts, dependencies shared across ideas) plus a reusable Cognitive Repertoire: 10 methodological heuristics and cognitive operators organized by Framing / Constructing / Evaluating / Learning.
  • The loop is Monitor → Select → Search (Algorithm 1), Nelson–Narens monitoring–control operationalized as data structures, not just citation.
  • A research harness turns control decisions into executable plans: parallel subagents explore idea branches, Taiyi-Optimizer (Bayesian optimization) searches condition/design spaces, and domain experts gate selection and validation.
  • Generic heuristics are instantiated per domain by analyzing classic papers, then refined by case-based feedback on task success/failure.
  • Evaluation: all systems on GPT-5.6-Sol (high reasoning), K = 10 ideas per task, against direct-generation, general-purpose, and scientific-discovery harness baselines.

Strengths

  • Theory mapped to executable structure: Nelson–Narens, Newell–Simon problem spaces, and metareasoning become concrete data structures and Algorithm 1.
  • Contamination-aware benchmark built only from papers first disclosed after the base model's knowledge cutoff — a clean, low-cost guard other ideation benchmarks could adopt.
  • Breadth of evidence: 265-task benchmark + two external benchmarks + ELO analysis + five projects with physical or simulation validation.
  • Higher quality in less time (29.9 min vs 39.3–44.8 min) thanks to parallel branch exploration.
  • Transparent uncertainty bookkeeping: rejected inferences struck through but retained; hypotheses kept distinct from external evidence.

Limitations

  • No component-level ablations (Cognitive State, repertoire, monitoring loop), so the source of the +6.1 gain cannot be pinned down.
  • Self-introduced benchmark with LLM-judge scoring; human-expert agreement claimed but not quantified in the main text.
  • Real-world wins are few-shot, expert-gated, and delivered by Bayesian optimization; proposed mechanistic experiments (isotope labeling, EPR/XAS) remain unexecuted.
  • Exact rediscovery (19.5) and Feasibility (52.4) show it still often fails to assemble complete, executable research routes.
  • Cost and frontier-model dependence unanalyzed; IG-Arena comparison mixes model versions and local vs published scores.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • How much of the +6.1 improvement survives if the Cognitive State, Repertoire, or monitoring-control loop is ablated while keeping 10-idea parallel generation fixed?
  • Can the contamination-control recipe (post-cutoff papers, progressive decision disclosure) become a maintained public benchmark with published verifier rubrics?
  • In the chemistry campaigns, how much yield gain is ideation quality versus Taiyi-Optimizer's sample efficiency — e.g., against random search?
  • Do the ten heuristics transfer to disciplines with weaker "classic paper" traditions for domain instantiation?
  • Does the metacognitive layer compensate more, or less, when the base model's single-shot ideation is weaker?
