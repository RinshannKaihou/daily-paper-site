Why this matters

Quantization effects must be reported relative to a measured noise floor — an immediately actionable protocol for selecting 8-bit vs 4-bit builds of deployed decision models.

— distilled from Relevance / 相关性

Critical edge

The floor itself — denominator of every effect-to-floor ratio — rests on a single restart pair per build, and its headline pattern already failed to replicate between the 9B and the 27B.

— from Critical Assessment & Limitations

Methodology

Thirteen configurations per model — a transformers BF16 reference, vLLM BF16/FP8/NVFP4, llama.cpp BF16/Q8_0/Q4_K_M, restart instances, a batch-8 rerun, and a forced-kernel run — all on one NVIDIA GB10 (DGX Spark, 128 GB unified).

The comparison unit is the top-1 decision flip on a fixed set of 2,400 paired public records (first 200 of each of 12 tasks; 31,275 questions total) from two Cloudflare decision models. Uncertainty: 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples over a shared resample matrix; zero-flip pairs get exact Clopper-Pearson bounds (0/2,400 → upper 0.15%).

The silent kernel hazard — transformers routes Gated DeltaNet layers by which packages merely import — was made observable by blocking imports and asserting kernel tags on every result line, measured at 10–14 flips.

Credibility: 11 hypotheses + 5 predictions pre-registered before any analysis; failed predictions reported as failed. Code, recipes, per-record outputs and weights public; numbers reconcile with independent community measurements. Much of the text/code was AI-generated under author direction (disclosed).

Strengths

  • Measures the exact confounds every real quantization comparison contains — engine, instance, batch, kernel — on the same records as the quantization effect, instead of assuming them away.
  • The failed pre-registered predictions are the most informative results: 8-bit quantization is larger than both the engine change and the restart floor, contrary to the authors' own expectations.
  • llama.cpp's exactly-zero restart floor and the format-dependent vLLM floor are genuinely useful operational facts.
  • Direction-split flips turn "accuracy barely moved" into "hundreds of individual decisions changed" — the quantity an operator actually cares about.
  • Full release: code, recipes, per-record outputs, pre-registration, quantized weights — reconciling with independent community measurements.

Limitations

  • Two models from one family on one device (GB10); neither the restart-floor ordering nor magnitudes can be extrapolated.
  • One restart pair per build — the floor, denominator of every ratio, is the least-certain measured quantity.
  • The 2,400-record unit (first 200/task, equal weights) differs systematically from full-set rates (0.8–1.6×).
  • The batch-size row is confounded with a rerun; its clean attribution was verified only on the 9B.
  • vLLM "restart" floors embed batch-composition variance (16 requests in flight) — deployment-realistic, not isolated instance drift.
  • The pre-registered equal-memory question (H8) could not be formed from the builds made; restart mechanism remains open.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the vLLM restart floor generalize beyond the GB10 (H100/B200, mature FP8/NVFP4 kernels), and does the format ordering stabilize with 5+ instances per build?
  2. What is the actual mechanism of vLLM restart drift once two-state selection and FlashInfer FP4 autotuning are excluded — cold graph compilation, request-stream composition, timing-dependent state?
  3. Would the 4-bit-vs-floor separation hold for generative workloads, where per-token compounding could shrink decision margins differently than one classification pass?
  4. Can flip-concentration-at-low-confidence (98–100% of 8-bit flips below 0.7 ref confidence) become a cheap predictor of quantization damage from the BF16 softmax alone?
  5. Does quantizing the layers kept in BF16 (GDN gates, norms, schema head) push 8-bit builds below the noise floor?
  6. Is the unexplained +1.26 pp ContractNLI gain under Clef-flash Q4_K_M real regularization or a split artifact?
