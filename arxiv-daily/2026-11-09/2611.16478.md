> **一句话总结：** 本文系统检验"同一权重的 8-bit 副本与低精度副本之间的分歧能否作为逐题错误信号"：在 Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B 的 8,398 道多选题（ARC/MMLU/HellaSwag 子集）上，精度分歧分数的 AUROC（0.684）与免费的答案熵（0.682）几乎持平（+0.001，p=0.760），与置信度特征组合仅带来 +0.009 的小增益，且匹配能量的高斯权重噪声控制表明该信号并非量化特有；一次选项移序的额外 8-bit 前向（+0.049 AUROC）反而是更强的信号，而免费的重校准才是本地部署的最佳性价比方案。

> **TL;DR:** A rigorous, mostly negative result paper from Intel: on 8,398 multiple-choice questions (ARC-Easy/Challenge, MMLU and HellaSwag subsets) with simulated round-to-nearest quantization of Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B, the best precision-disagreement score (1 − p_low[answer] against a 6-bit, group-128 copy, dev-selected) detects wrong answers at AUROC 0.684 vs 0.682 for the free entropy baseline on the held-out test half — a paired difference of just +0.001 [−0.009, +0.011], p=0.760 — and adding it to three confidence features gains only +0.009 [+0.002, +0.015]. A matched-energy Gaussian weight-noise control performs about as well as rounding, so the signal is generic perturbation, not quantization-specific. One extra 8-bit pass with cyclically shifted options is the stronger signal (AUROC 0.732, +0.049 [+0.031, +0.067] over entropy), and when routing 0.6B→1.7B at equal total weight traffic, free entropy routing beats both extra-pass scores. Worth reading for its unusually honest controls and deployment recipe, not for a new SOTA uncertainty method.

## 核心贡献

- **提出并严格检验了一个"免费午餐"式假设**：本地用户通常同时持有同一模型的多个精度副本；把 8-bit 部署副本的答案与一个低精度副本（3–6 bit，分组 32–256）对同一提示的输出之间的分歧，当作逐题不确定性/错误分数，用于弃答（abstention）、升级到大模型（escalation）和重问（re-asking）。额外成本仅一次低精度前向（以权重流量计约为 0.76 个 8-bit pass），无需采样、无需训练、无需额外模型。
- **给出了明确的（偏负面的）定量答案**：在 Qwen3-0.6B 上，开发集选出的最优精度分歧分数（w6g128 副本上的 1 − p_low[answer]）测试半区 AUROC 0.684，与免费的答案熵 0.682 统计上不可区分（+0.001 [−0.009, +0.011]，p=0.760）；与三个置信度特征做逻辑回归组合后仅 +0.009 [+0.002, +0.015]，且增益集中在 ARC 任务上。Qwen3-1.7B 上分歧分数与熵之差为 −0.001 [−0.009, +0.006]。
- **设计了决定性的对照实验，证明信号并非量化特有**：在相同误差能量（4-bit group-128）下，逐张量高斯权重噪声的平均 AUROC（0.597）与舍入量化（0.550）相当且种子间波动（0.531–0.649）与差距同量级；所有扰动副本的 AUROC 与"答案翻转比例"呈 rank 相关 −0.895 的单一趋势——起作用的是扰动幅度，不是量化本身。
- **识别出真正更强的同成本竞争者**：一次选项循环移序的额外 8-bit pass（成本 1.00 vs 0.76 单位）AUROC 达 0.732（+0.049 [+0.031, +0.067] 超过熵，p=0.003）；特征打乱对照（按任务与熵十分位分层打乱后增益归零）排除了"多列特征本身带来增益"的假象。
- **给出了面向本地部署的决策表（Table 10）与代价分析**：无空闲算力→用三个置信度特征做免费逻辑重校准（0.6B ECE 从原始 0.181 降至 0.014）；有低精度副本→作为附加特征小幅增益；能负担一次 8-bit pass→移序重问并平均两读（准确率 49.5%→52.3%）；有大模型→按免费熵排序升级 20–30% 最不确定的问题，等总流量下优于两种额外 pass 分数。

## Key Contributions

- **Asks and answers a practical question with unusual honesty.** Can per-question disagreement between an 8-bit answering copy and a lower-precision copy of the same weights serve as an error signal for selective prediction? The cost framing is local deployment: one extra forward pass over fewer bytes (0.76 units of one 8-bit pass), no sampling, no training, no second model.
- **Comprehensive signal zoo with a clean selection protocol.** Five disagreement scores (flip, 1 − p_low[answer] "pdrop", JSD, KL, margin drop) across 9 simulated RTN quantization configurations (3–6 bits, group 32–256) for Qwen3-0.6B (3 copies for 1.7B), evaluated against three free confidence baselines (entropy, 1 − max-prob, margin) on a held-out test half (n = 4,199) with paired bootstrap intervals; dev-half selection prevents the score from flattering itself.
- **Decisive controls rarely seen in uncertainty papers.** (1) Matched-noise control: Gaussian weight perturbation at the same error energy as the 4-bit quantizer is about as informative as rounding, with seed spread as large as the gap — no evidence the signal is quantization-specific. (2) Feature-shuffle control: shuffling disagreement features within task × entropy-decile cells removes the gain (−0.0005 avg vs observed +0.0082), proving the gain comes from question-level information. (3) Damage trend: AUROC correlates −0.895 with answer-flip rate across all 14 perturbed copies; 3-bit copies are anti-informative (JSD AUROC 0.410) because a near-random copy disagrees most with confident answers.
- **A cost-matched stronger alternative.** Option-order disagreement (one extra 8-bit pass with a cyclic option shift, cost 1.00 vs 0.76 units) reaches AUROC 0.732 and its rank correlation with entropy is only 0.472 (vs 0.832 for precision disagreement), explaining why it adds genuinely new information. The paper is explicit that the exact-cost variant (shifted options on a low-precision copy) was not run.
- **End-to-end deployment analysis.** Risk-coverage curves, ECE/Brier after logistic recalibration, escalation experiments to the 1.7B model, and a re-asking recipe, all reconciled into a budget-indexed decision table.

## Methodology

- **Models and data:** Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B (bf16 checkpoints, Apache-2.0), scored on 8,398 multiple-choice questions: ARC-Easy (2,376, full test split), ARC-Challenge (1,172), a fixed MMLU subset (2,850 = 50 per each of 57 subjects, seed 14), and a fixed HellaSwag subset (2,000 items). Zero-shot plain-text prompts ending in "Answer:"; the answer is the option letter with the largest next-token logit; option probabilities are the softmax over letter logits. No chat template, no sampling.
- **Simulated quantizer:** in-memory round-to-nearest with per-group asymmetric min-max scale/offset, applied to the q/k/v/o/gate/up/down projections of every decoder layer (440M weights for 0.6B, 1,409M for 1.7B); embeddings, LM head and norms stay bf16. Effective bits = b + 32/g (e.g., 4.25 for 4-bit g128). The 8-bit g128 copy is the "deployed" answering model; 9 low-precision copies were run for 0.6B and 3 for 1.7B; the 16-bit original provides a noise floor (the 8-bit copy flips 5.0% of answers vs bf16).
- **Signals:** free confidence baselines from p8 (entropy, 1 − max prob, margin); precision disagreement vs a low-precision copy (flip, pdrop = 1 − p_L[y], JSD, KL, margin drop); option-order disagreement from one extra 8-bit pass with options cyclically shifted by one position (logits mapped back before scoring); ensembles of four 4-bit copies and three noisy copies.
- **Statistics:** random dev/test split (seed 14); configuration + score selected on the dev half by AUROC; headline numbers on the test half; logistic combinations fitted on dev, scored on test; per-task/shift/routing analyses use five-fold cross-fitting; percentile bootstrap over questions (200–400 resamples), paired bootstrap for score differences, p-values floored at 1/(B+1), no multiplicity correction (acknowledged).
- **Metrics:** AUROC (wrong answer = positive), partial AUROC at false-alarm ≤ 0.2, AURC and accuracy at 50%/80% coverage, ECE (15 bins) and Brier after dev-fitted logistic recalibration. Cost is counted in weight bytes read per question, normalized to one 8-bit g128 pass (a b-bit g-group pass costs (b + 32/g)/8.25).
- **Matched-noise control:** for each tensor, Gaussian noise with the same RMS error as the 4-bit g128 quantizer added to bf16 weights, per-tensor (3 seeds) or per-group variance (2 seeds), 0.6B model only.

## Results & Highlights

- **Precision disagreement ≈ free entropy (Qwen3-0.6B, test half, n = 4,199):** entropy AUROC 0.682 [0.667, 0.698]; dev-selected precision-disagreement score (w6g128, pdrop, extra cost 0.76 units) 0.684 [0.666, 0.700]; paired difference +0.001 [−0.009, +0.011], p = 0.760. Logistic combination of confidence + precision features reaches 0.691, +0.009 [+0.002, +0.015] over entropy (p = 0.020); per-task the gain is +0.020 [+0.011, +0.027] on ARC-Easy and +0.024 [+0.012, +0.035] on ARC-Challenge, but inside the noise on MMLU (+0.007 [−0.002, +0.016]) and negative on HellaSwag (−0.006 [−0.015, +0.004]).
- **Same story at 1.7B:** entropy AUROC 0.767 vs 0.766 for the selected disagreement score (−0.001 [−0.009, +0.006]); combination +0.003 [−0.001, +0.006] on the test half; with all questions and five-fold cross-fitting the combination gains +0.004 [+0.001, +0.006], concentrated on HellaSwag (+0.018 [+0.007, +0.028]) — the task pattern reverses relative to 0.6B (ARC-Easy +0.000 [−0.003, +0.003]).
- **Option-order disagreement is the stronger extra-pass signal:** one 8-bit pass with shifted options gives 1 − p'[answer] AUROC 0.732 [0.716, 0.748], +0.049 [+0.031, +0.067] over entropy (p = 0.003) at 1.00 vs 0.76 cost units; combined with confidence it reaches 0.742 (0.6B) and 0.815 (1.7B, +0.048 over entropy). Averaging the two readings lifts 0.6B accuracy from 49.5% to 52.3%; averaging four option orders gives 55.4%.
- **Matched-noise control kills the "quantization-specific" story (0.6B, 4-bit energy):** AUROC of 1 − p_low[answer] is 0.550 for RTN rounding vs 0.597 (range 0.531–0.649, 3 seeds) for per-tensor Gaussian noise — noise is at least as good, and seed spread is as wide as the gap. Across all 14 perturbed copies the AUROC falls with flip rate (rank correlation −0.895); the signal reverses at 3 bits (JSD AUROC 0.410, and 1 − p_low AUROC 0.472).
- **Calibration: free recalibration beats any disagreement score.** On the 0.6B test half, raw max-probability ECE is 0.181; a logistic map of entropy gives 0.032; the three confidence features give 0.014; adding precision-disagreement features gives 0.020 (intervals overlap). On 1.7B the entropy-only map is insufficient (ECE 0.073) vs 0.021 for the three confidence features.
- **Escalation routing (0.6B → 1.7B on all 8,398 questions):** escalating the most uncertain 20% takes system accuracy from 49.5% to 53.9% (entropy), 54.7% (precision disagreement), 56.7% (option order), vs 53.0% random and 67.5% for 1.7B alone. But at equal total weight traffic (1.64 units), free-entropy routing escalates 51% of questions and reaches 61.3%, beating option-order routing's 56.7% at 20% share — the signal pass itself costs more than the escalation it steers.
- **Spending more passes does not scale:** two low-precision copies (1.39 units) give AUROC 0.690 vs 0.688 for one; four 4-bit copies voting (2.17 units) collapse to 0.574 because each member is individually damaged (ensemble mutual-information at chance, 0.500).

## Authors & Credibility

- Both authors (Mohammed Nayeem Khan and Allan Kevin T) are at Intel Corporation, with Intel email addresses; this is an industry research report in the style of a careful internal evaluation rather than a conference submission (v1, 10 November 2026, cs.AI cross-listed to cs.CL and cs.LG).
- The credibility of this paper rests on methodological hygiene rather than headline claims: dev/test split with the score configuration chosen out-of-sample, paired bootstrap over questions for every comparison, p-values reported alongside intervals, negative results (matched noise, 3-bit reversal, task-level sign flips between model sizes) reported with the same prominence as positive ones, and an explicit "When it does not help" section with worked examples of false alarms and misses (Table 11).
- Reproducibility is unusually strong: Appendix D states that every number, table and figure is recomputed from cached per-question option logits when the paper is built; the quantizer, scores, AUROC, risk-coverage, ECE, logistic fit and bootstrap are ~400 lines written from scratch (PyTorch 2.11, transformers 5.20), with fixed seeds for item lists (14), splits (14), folds (0) and bootstraps; weights are public (Apache-2.0) and the logits/code/builder are provided with the paper. Compute was a single shared RTX 4090 within a 6 GB memory allowance, which is why no 4B model and no wall-clock timings exist.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** the per-question use of two-precision disagreement as a selective-prediction signal appears genuinely unexplored (the authors state their literature search covered an index and abstracts only and a missed precedent is possible), but the finding is that this novelty buys almost nothing: the best disagreement score is statistically indistinguishable from free entropy (+0.001, p=0.760), and its rank correlation with entropy (0.832) shows it mostly repeats what confidence already said. The paper's real contribution is the negative result plus the controls, which is scientifically valuable but limits practical impact.
- **Rigor:** high. Pre-registered score orientation, dev-selected configurations evaluated on a held-out half, paired bootstraps, likelihood-ratio tests (chi-square 68 on 4 df, p<0.001 for the 0.6B feature gain), a two-tier feature-shuffle control that preserves task and entropy-cell structure, and an honest note that the cross-fitted analyses leak the dev-half configuration choice. However, no multiplicity correction is applied across the many tables (14 intervals in Fig. 8 alone), and the bootstrap resamples questions only — not noise seeds, prompt templates or checkpoints — so the reported intervals understate total uncertainty.
- **Reproducibility:** excellent on paper (cached logits, fixed seeds, from-scratch ~400-line implementation, builder that regenerates every number), though I cannot verify that the artifact is actually released. The in-memory simulated quantizer means no wall-clock or memory-saving claims are tested — cost is a bytes-read proxy that excludes the ~156M-weight embedding/LM head (counting it raises the w6g128 pass from 0.76 to 0.86 units, which the authors disclose).
- **Significance:** modest and honestly scoped. Two small models of one family (Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B), four-way MCQ with zero-shot letter-logit scoring, plain RTN quantization — real deployments use GPTQ/AWQ/K-quants, which damage less at equal bits and could shift the "informative vs destroyed" balance. The matched-noise control was run only on the 0.6B model at 4-bit energy with 2–3 seeds, and rounding itself is a single deterministic draw, so its seed spread is unknown.
- **Red flags / specific weaknesses:** (1) The dev-half selection always picked the highest-precision copy tried (w6g128), and no copy between 6.25 and 8.25 effective bits was run, so the optimum may sit in the unexplored gap — the useful-band conclusion is one-sided. (2) The 1.7B model got only 3 low-precision copies vs 9 for 0.6B, an asymmetry that weakens cross-scale claims. (3) Self-consistency, the most obvious competing extra-pass method, "was not completed within the compute budget" and is excluded from the cost table, leaving the strongest sampling-based baseline untested. (4) Small-model accuracies are low (49.5% overall for 0.6B, 41.8% on MMLU), where much of the error mass is uninformative guessing — the authors themselves show the task-level gains flip sign between model sizes, so the ARC-concentration story is model-specific, not a law. (5) The escalation analysis uses the larger model's cached answers rather than a live routing system, ignoring latency and batching.

## Strengths

- Exceptional experimental hygiene: out-of-sample score selection, paired statistics, and controls (matched-noise, feature-shuffle, bit/group sweeps, ensembles, domain-shift transfer) that are designed to destroy the paper's own hypothesis — and largely do, which the authors report plainly.
- A cost model (weight bytes per question, normalized to one 8-bit pass) that makes the comparison between a cheap low-precision pass and a full extra 8-bit pass concrete; the finding that entropy routing wins at equal total traffic is the kind of result only a cost-aware analysis can produce.
- The "Key findings" box recomputes every headline number with intervals up front; the deployment recipe (Table 10) and "When it does not help" section turn the results into actionable guidance for local LLM users.
- Clear mechanistic explanations: why graded pdrop beats flip/JSD (it keeps the confidence grading), why 3-bit disagreement reverses (near-random copies disagree most with confident answers), and why option order adds more than precision (correlation 0.472 vs 0.832 with entropy).
- Full reproducibility pipeline: cached logits + a builder that regenerates every table and figure from scratch.

## Limitations

- Scope: two small models of one family (0.6B, 1.7B); no 4B confirmation (6 GB GPU limit), no claims about 7B+ models where quantization damage is far smaller; plain RTN quantization only — GPTQ/AWQ/imatrix could change the balance; four-way MCQ with letter-logit scoring, no generative or open-ended answers.
- The useful precision band (5–6 effective bits for 0.6B) was only observed from one side; no copy between 6.25 and 8.25 effective bits was tried, and no 7-bit copy was run.
- Statistical caveats: no multiplicity correction across many comparisons; intervals resample questions only (not seeds, templates or checkpoints); matched-noise control limited to one model, one energy, few seeds; rounding is a single deterministic copy.
- Cost is a byte-read proxy excluding the embedding/LM head; no wall-clock or memory measurements (quantization simulated in memory); self-consistency omitted for compute reasons; escalation evaluated on cached answers rather than a live router.
- The AUROC ceiling is bounded by benchmark label noise — ambiguous or mislabelled questions look uncertain to every score and count as errors for all.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a 7-bit or 6.75-effective-bit copy (the untried 6.25–8.25 band) dominate the current best, and does the optimum track the copy that flips ~5–10% of answers regardless of model size?
- Do modern learned quantizers (GPTQ, AWQ, imatrix, K-quants) — which perturb weights less at the same bits — produce a stronger or weaker disagreement signal than RTN, given the −0.895 damage trend found here?
- Does the conclusion transfer to larger models (4B–70B), where quantization damage is much smaller and entropy AUROC is already higher (0.767 at 1.7B vs 0.682 at 0.6B)?
- Can option-order disagreement on a low-precision copy (the exact-cost variant that was not run) match the 8-bit shifted-option pass at 0.76 units?
- How do these signals behave on free-form/generative QA, where letter logits are unavailable — does pdrop-style disagreement survive when the "answer" is a decoded span?
- Since self-consistency was cut for compute budget, a proper cost-matched comparison against k-sample voting (which needs hundreds of decoded tokens per question) remains open.
- Could multiple noise seeds at low energy replace holding a quantized copy, given that matched Gaussian noise performs about as well as rounding?
