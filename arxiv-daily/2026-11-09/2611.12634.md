> **一句话总结：** 作者通过预注册（pre-registered）的 GPU 实验系统检验了用 Fisher 信息二阶展开 ½ΔᵀFΔ 预测 LLM 量化后 KL 散度损失的可靠性：相对排序在 16 倍扰动范围内稳健成立，但绝对预测呈重尾（Qwen3-32B 的 int4 被对角估计低估 5.18 倍，根因是单个张量内两个输入通道的舍入误差与梯度对齐）；据此构建的筛选器（fp32 Fisher 单次前向+裁剪均值+[0.75,1.50] 置信带+对齐张量标记）在未见过的 Mistral-7B-v0.3 上 6 项预测全部落带、3 个 KL 预算全部达标、vLLM 吞吐预测误差仅 0.4%。

> **TL;DR:** A pre-registered measurement study on five LLMs (Qwen3-8B/4B/32B, OLMo-2-7B, Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE) stress-tests the standard second-order screen KL ≈ ½ΔᵀFΔ used by Hessian-aware quantizers. Relative predictions hold across a 16-fold perturbation range (R within 0.99–1.05) and a 5%-trimmed average lands within ±15% of measured KL in 10 of 12 cells on Qwen3-4B/OLMo-2-7B — but the elementwise-diagonal baseline under-predicts Qwen3-32B int4 by 5.18× (fixed to 0.93 by a tensor-block form), the MoE model responds non-quadratically (int8 KL ∝ α^0.63, 27× off at α=1/4), and bf16 gradients can inflate predictions 3–4×. The resulting screener, validated on the unseen Mistral-7B-v0.3 family, hits all 6 band criteria, all 3 KL budgets, and predicts vLLM throughput within 0.4% — for 0.77 GPU-hours per screening decision.

## 核心贡献

- **对二阶量化筛选器做了罕见的"预注册"式可靠性审计**：所有预测、评分规则和文件校验和都在 GPU 运行前冻结（附录含完整台账），排除事后挑选（post-hoc cherry-picking），这是量化文献中极少见的实验规范。
- **系统刻画了绝对预测的重尾性**：每序列二次型 q 的 Hill 尾指数 1.7–2.4（处于无穷方差边缘），前 5% 序列可占预测值的 24%；证明 5% 裁剪均值能将 Qwen3-4B 与 OLMo-2 的 12 个格中 10 个拉到实测 KL 的 ±15% 以内。
- **定位了两种明确的失效模式**：(1) OLMo-2 int4 的偏差是四阶项（α→0 时消失），(2) Qwen3-32B int4 的 5.18 倍低估源于最后一层 down-projection 单个张量——其块形式是对角形式的 32 倍，两个（共 25,600 个）输入通道贡献了 96% 的列块项，且该对齐无法被任何权重侧统计量检出。
- **发现并修复了 bf16 梯度的数值陷阱**：个别序列（1,280 条中仅 1 条超 1000 倍，最大 1581.8×）在反向传播下半部分（第 15 层以下）炸掉；下半段改用 fp32 即可将误差降至全 bf16 的 0.017。
- **量化了确定性引擎的代价与噪声底**：vLLM batch-invariant 内核吞吐仅为默认模式的 0.55 倍，而归约顺序变化引入 0.17–0.22 nats/序列的 KL 噪声底——低于此量级的 KL 差异不可分辨。
- **构建并盲测了端到端筛选器**：单次流式 fp32 Fisher 前向（Mistral-7B-v0.3 上 256 序列仅 8.0 分钟）+ 张量块形式 + 置信带 [0.75, 1.50] + 对齐张量标记 + 逐层格式分配器，在未见模型族上预注册测试全部通过。

## Key Contributions

- A **pre-registered reliability audit** of the second-order screen KL ≈ ½ΔᵀFΔ that underlies Hessian-aware bit allocation and second-order PTQ (AdaRound, BRECQ, GPTQ, OBC): every decision rule, prediction, and file checksum was frozen before its GPU run, with a full ledger in the supplementary material. Models added after Qwen3-8B played no part in developing the analysis.
- **Characterization of the heavy tail in absolute predictions**: per-sequence quadratic form q has Hill tail index 1.7–2.4 (edge of infinite variance), with the top 5% of sequences carrying up to 24% of the prediction; a 5%-trimmed average brings 10 of 12 cells on Qwen3-4B and OLMo-2-7B within ±15% of measured KL, and the trim-vs-mean sign follows the two tail indices in 30 of 32 cells.
- **Two distinct failure modes isolated**: OLMo-2's int4 miss (R = 1.31, stable in α estimator-wise) is a quartic term (22% of KL at α = 1) that vanishes as α → 0; Qwen3-32B's int4 miss (R = 5.18 with fp32 diagonal) is coherent rounding error in one tensor (the last layer's down projection, block form 32× its diagonal, 99% of the gap), where two of 25,600 input columns carry 96% of the column-block term — invisible to every weight statistic computed.
- **Diagnosis and fix of bf16 gradient blow-ups**: a few sequences (0.6–0.8% on the affected sample set; max 1581.8×) have early-layer gradients blow up element-wise in bf16 while the linear term is unaffected; the error is *made* in the lower half of the backward (keeping layers 0–15 in fp32 drops the error to 0.017 of the all-bf16 value, vs 0.96 for the upper half) and jumps 4.4–11.8× through layer 15.
- **Pricing of determinism**: vLLM's batch-invariant mode runs at 0.55× default throughput; any change of reduction order (kernel selection, tensor parallelism) introduces a KL floor of 0.17–0.22 nats per sequence, so smaller inter-configuration differences are unresolvable.
- **An end-to-end screener validated on an unseen family**: one streamed fp32 Fisher pass (1.88 s/sequence on GH200; 8.0 min for 256 sequences × 3 formats × 224 tensors on Mistral-7B-v0.3), trimmed tensor-block predictions with a frozen band [0.75, 1.50], an alignment flag (own ratio ≥ 10 and ≥ 5% of predicted KL — fires exactly once over 19 archived cells), and a greedy per-layer int4 < int8 < bf16 allocator that writes vLLM (compressed-tensors) checkpoints.

## Methodology

The paper tests the linear-response identity KL(model ‖ quantized model) ≈ ½ΔᵀFΔ, where Δ = α(Ŵ − W) is the weight perturbation from a candidate format and F is the Fisher information estimated from *sampled* continuations (the true Fisher, not the empirical Fisher). The per-sequence prediction is the one-sample diagonal estimate q = ½Σᵢ gᵢ²Δᵢ² (g = gradient of the sequence log-likelihood); the same streamed pass records per-tensor projections d_ℓ = g_ℓ·Δ_ℓ, giving the tensor-block form ½Σ_ℓ d_ℓ² and the full form ½(Σ_ℓ d_ℓ)² for free. Measured mismatch is the token-level KL between base and perturbed model on 256-token sampled continuations, averaged per sequence then over sequences; the headline statistic is R = measured KL / prediction.

**Setup.** Primary model Qwen3-8B with channel-wise int8, per-tensor fp8, and group-wise int4 (group 128, plus 32/64/256; nearest and stochastic rounding), perturbation scale α ∈ {1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1}. Three sample sets: A (256 prompts, HF fp32 sampler), B (512 prompts, vLLM sampler), C (same 512 prompts, experimental-pipeline sampler). Pre-registered extensions: Qwen3-4B and OLMo-2-1124-7B (three schemes at α = 1), Qwen3-32B (int4 α scan), and the MoE model Qwen3-30B-A3B. Gradients are computed in both fp32 and bf16. All GPU batches are logged in a pre-registration ledger with frozen predictions and outcomes (Appendix A, Table 3).

**Analysis of failures.** Heavy tails are quantified with Hill estimators and an exact identity R_trim/R_mean = (1 − s_K)/(1 − s_q); the OLMo-2 int4 miss is attributed via a pre-registered α scan fitting KL(α) = aα² + bα³ + cα⁴; the 32B int4 miss is localized post hoc via per-tensor block-vs-diagonal comparisons, a Chernoff bound for Rademacher quadratic forms (own ratio 32 with sign-independent error has probability < 1.1×10⁻⁶), and per-row/per-column projections; the bf16 blow-ups are localized by back-propagating four blown-up sequences and two controls in both precisions, comparing hidden-state gradients layer by layer, then confirmed by a frozen lower-half-vs-upper-half fp32 experiment.

**The screener** records, in one forward+backward pass per calibration sequence (gradients consumed by post-accumulate hooks and freed), d₁ and d₂ for every (tensor, format) pair; every format assignment is then a sum. It reports the trimmed tensor-block prediction with band [0.75, 1.50] (the envelope of 17 archived dense cells at α = 1, frozen before the test), flags aligned (tensor, format) pairs which are never assigned, and greedily upgrades decoder layers int4 → int8 → bf16 by predicted-KL-reduction per added byte until 1.50·q̄ fits the budget. Throughput is predicted by harmonic interpolation 1/tps(c) = Σ_f s_f(c)/tps_f over three measured uniform configurations.

## Results & Highlights

- **Relative predictions are robust on Qwen3-8B**: R changed by a factor of only 0.99–1.05 across the 16-fold perturbation range α = 1/8 → 1; int4 group sizes 32/64/256 predicted from group-128 calibration within 0.96–1.01 of measured KL; stochastic rounding raised measured KL 2.25× against a predicted 2.28×.
- **Trimmed absolute predictions hold on new dense models**: the pre-registered criterion R_trim ∈ [0.85, 1.15] passed 10 of 12 cells on Qwen3-4B and OLMo-2-7B; both misses are OLMo-2 int4 (R_trim = 1.35), attributed by α scan to a quartic term contributing 22% of the KL at α = 1 (second-order coefficient 1.04× the Fisher prediction).
- **The diagonal form fails at 32B int4**: on Qwen3-32B, R = 5.18 (fp32 gradients; 5.17 bf16), and the dense control failed too (int4 KL slope 2.18 in α vs. frozen band [1.9, 2.1]). The tensor-block form (3.63× the diagonal) closes the gap: KL/(α²q_block) = 0.93 (mean) at α = 1/4. Quantized alone at α = 1, the responsible tensor gives KL 195.8 nats/sequence — more than the whole int4 model (193.2) — vs 27.4 for all other tensors combined; stochastic rounding removes the coherence (own ratio 0.87/0.91) and cuts that tensor's KL by 15× at α = 1/4.
- **bf16 gradients cause up to 4× prediction errors**: on sample set C the bf16 R for int8 was off by 3–4× until 0.6–0.8% of blown-up sequences (one at 1581.8× its fp32 quadratic form) were dropped; with the lower half of the backward in fp32 the bottom-layer error fell to 0.017 of the all-bf16 value (vs 0.96 with the upper half in fp32).
- **The MoE model is non-analytic at small perturbations**: on Qwen3-30B-A3B, the share of flipped (layer, token) expert sets grows as α^0.41 (6.6% at α = 1/4, 11.7% at α = 1) and int8 KL scales as α^0.63; trimmed, int8 is 4.1× the prediction at full scale and 27× at a quarter, while int4 stays close (1.09, KL ∝ α^1.65). fp32 and bf16 references disagree on 12% of routing decisions. The pre-registered MoE test is inconclusive because its dense control failed.
- **Determinism has a price**: vLLM batch-invariant kernels run at 0.55× default throughput (0.81× teacher-forced); every reduction-order change (kernel selection, 2/4-way tensor parallelism) introduces a 0.17–0.22 nats/sequence KL floor, while reruns with the same kernel selection are bitwise identical.
- **Pre-registered screener test on unseen Mistral-7B-v0.3**: all 6 cells (3 uniform formats + 3 budgets) land in the frozen band [0.75, 1.50] with ratios 1.03–1.12; all 3 KL budgets met (e.g. B1: 6.55 bits, predicted q̄ = 3.31 vs measured 3.58 nats); vLLM throughput predicted within 0.4% (B1 predicted 4343 tok/s, measured 4343; B2 4244 vs 4226; B3 4196 vs 4180). Cost: 8.0 GPU-minutes for the Fisher pass, 0.77 GPU-hours for the whole pre-registered test.
- **Against a diagonal (Hessian-trace-style) allocator**: the two allocators agree on 81–94% of tensors on Mistral-7B-v0.3; on Qwen3-32B at a loose budget, the diagonal allocator accepts uniform int4 (measured 5.18× its own prediction, ≈3.5× the budget) whereas the screener moves one tensor to int8 for 0.02 extra bits/param (4.14 vs 4.12) and achieves a measured 27.4 nats.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The expansion itself is textbook (Optimal Brain Damage through GPTQ/BRECQ); the novelty is in *auditing it as a predictor* on modern LLMs at deployment scale, with pre-registration — a methodological upgrade over virtually all PTQ evaluation papers — plus the discovery of gradient-aligned rounding coherence in a single tensor, which is a genuinely new failure mode related to but distinct from the outlier/super-weight literature.
- **Rigor**: Exceptional. Pre-registered criteria with pass/fail ledgers, exact identities derived for the trimmed/mean ratio, a Chernoff bound to rule out chance for the own-ratio-32 tensor, bit-for-bit reproduction of bf16 blow-ups, and two independent checkpoint readers compared bit-for-bit at build time. The distinction between pre-registered and post-hoc findings is maintained throughout.
- **Reproducibility**: High in principle (frozen checksums, ledger, named hardware: GH200), though no code release is mentioned in the main text. The finding that the entire screener test cost 0.77 GPU-hours makes independent replication cheap.
- **Significance caveats**: The single most load-bearing finding — the alignment flag and tensor-block form — rests on **one archived cell (Qwen3-32B int4)**; the authors concede the Mistral-7B-v0.3 test flagged no tensor, so the flag's case history has n = 1. The two-column localization within that tensor is post hoc. The pre-registered throughput criterion (±10%) was **not discriminating** on Mistral: the three budgets gained only 4–8% throughput over bf16 (uniform int4: 17%), so "throughput predicted within 0.4%" validates the interpolation rule more than the allocator's practical value. The MoE result — arguably the most important negative finding for modern serving — is officially *inconclusive* because its dense control failed, and is reported as an observation. The bf16-error localization used one model and four sequences, and the trimming fraction (5%) was chosen on Qwen3-8B and only afterwards tested on three models.
- **Red flags**: None serious, but note the AI-usage disclosure covers a broad share of the research process (experiment execution, analysis, and writing), the scope is one GPU type (GH200), two model families for the dense analysis plus one unseen test family, three schemes, and 256–512-sequence sample sets — so the band [0.75, 1.50] may not transfer to other architectures or hardware.

## Strengths

- Pre-registration with a published ledger of frozen predictions, including failures — a model of experimental hygiene rare in systems/ML evaluation papers.
- Separates *relative* validity (ranking formats — holds well) from *absolute* validity (KL budgeting — heavy-tailed, needs trimming), which directly changes how practitioners should use Hessian-aware screens.
- Each failure is not just observed but mechanistically localized: the 32B int4 miss to one tensor and two input channels; the bf16 blow-ups to the lower half of the backward pass through layer 15; the OLMo-2 miss to a quartic term.
- Actionable output: a concrete checklist (Section 10) and a working screener that writes deployable vLLM checkpoints, validated end-to-end (KL band, budgets, throughput) on an unseen model family for under 1 GPU-hour.

## Limitations

- The tensor-alignment flag and block form are validated by a single cell (Qwen3-32B int4); no tensor was flagged on the unseen Mistral test, so the flag's real-world firing rate is unknown.
- The MoE investigation (Qwen3-30B-A3B) is officially inconclusive: the pre-registered dense control failed (int4 slope 2.18 outside [1.9, 2.1]) and the run stopped in its final phase; the α^0.63 non-analytic scaling is an observation, not a confirmed result.
- Weight-only quantization only (no activation quantization), three schemes, one GPU type, 256–512 calibration sequences, and dense models from two families plus one unseen test family; the frozen band's transferability beyond this scope is untested.
- Throughput gains from mixed formats were 4–8% at the tested operating point (512-token prompts, 32 concurrent requests) — below the ±10% criterion's resolving power, so the allocator's serving benefit is undemonstrated at this scale.
- Several key localizations (the two input columns, the layer-15 jump) are post hoc; the trimming fraction and threshold rules were fixed on the primary model before only one round of out-of-sample testing.

## Relevance

对任何需要为 LLM 服务系统选择权重格式（int8/fp8/int4、混合精度逐层分配）的工作都直接相关：本文给出了二阶 Fisher 筛选器何时可信、何时失效（重尾、单张量梯度对齐、MoE 路由翻转、bf16 梯度炸裂）的定量边界，以及一个不到 1 GPU 小时即可复现的端到端筛选流程，可直接用于部署前的量化质量预算与吞吐预测。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the gradient-aligned rounding coherence found in Qwen3-32B's last down projection appear at 70B+ scale or in other families, and does its input-channel concentration (2 of 25,600 columns) connect to known massive-activation/super-weight channels?
- What is the true prevalence of the MoE non-analytic response — does KL ∝ α^β (β ≈ 0.63) hold across other sparse models, and can a routing-flip-aware correction term extend the screen to them?
- Can the alignment flag fire *before* quantization errors are known, e.g., by cheaply estimating the top eigen-directions of each tensor's Fisher block and correlating them with candidate rounding patterns, or does it fundamentally require the post-hoc projection d_ℓ?
- Would the band [0.75, 1.50] and the 5% trimming fraction survive larger calibration sets (tail index near 2 suggests slow convergence), other GPUs, and activation-aware quantization schemes?
- Since stochastic rounding removed the coherent tensor's excess (own ratio ≤ 1, KL cut 15×), should stochastic rounding be the default for int4 last-layer projections, and what does that imply for block-scaled (MX) formats?
