> **一句话总结：** 在 OLMo 3 的九句扰动定位任务（Hahami et al. 范式）上，作者把"模型自己报告哪句被注入"与"外部线性探针从激活中解码位置"沿预训练轨迹对照：注入强度为残差范数 4 倍、探针读第 24 层时，探针在仅 8.4B token 后即达 98.3% 而模型零样本回答仍在随机水平（11.1%），发布版基座上两者分别为 99.1% 与 89.6%；且位置信息在随机初始化（未经预训练）网络中即可被线性解码（step-0 达 37.2% vs 打乱标签 11.3%），说明"定位成功"可能只依赖一个浅层可解码的痕迹，并不必然证明模型具备对自身计算的广义内省能力。

> **TL;DR:** A Georgia Tech team re-examines activation-perturbation localization (the Hahami et al. nine-sentence task) on OLMo 3 7B/32B public pretraining checkpoints, comparing the model's own zero-shot answer against a separately trained linear probe on the same trials. At the fixed setting (injection at layer 0, strength α=4, probe readout at layer 24), the probe hits 98.3% after just 8.4B training tokens while the 32B model answers at chance (11.1%, 1-in-9); at the released base checkpoint the gap nearly closes (probe 99.1% vs model 89.6%). Crucially, location is already linearly decodable at step 0 (37.2% vs 11.3% shuffled-label control) and in never-trained random networks (31.5% at α=8 vs 11.5% uninjected control), so successful localization need not imply introspective access — and reporting accuracy swings from 37.7% to 89.6% in the same base model depending on injection layer, warning that single-configuration introspection claims are fragile.

## 核心贡献

- **探针-回答对照范式**：在同一定位试验上同时测量两个量——外部九类逻辑回归探针从最后一个输入 token 的残差流中解码被扰动句子的能力，与模型自身零样本回答（logits 中 1–9 标签的最高分）——从而把"信息可解码性"与"行为报告"分离开，检验定位成功到底要求多少内省能力。
- **沿预训练轨迹的纵向对照**：利用 OLMo 3 全公开的检查点阶梯（step0 → 8.4B → 50B → 500B → 2T → 4T → stage-1 结束 → 发布基座），发现位置信息在预训练极早期（8.4B token）即近饱和可解码（探针 98.3%），而可靠报告直到后期才出现（发布基座 89.6%），且无需后训练。
- **随机网络中的可解码痕迹**：在三个独立初始化、完全未训练的高斯随机网络中，注入随机方向后，探针在无任何提示、无定位问题的条件下仍能从后续未注入 token 中解码位置（强度 8 时 gap-0 为 31.5%、256 个中性 token 后仍 21.5%，对照组 11.5%/10.9%，经 Holm 校正的置换检验通过）——架构本身即可传播可恢复的位置信息。
- **对内省实验设计的警示**：同一发布版 32B 基座上，注入层从 0 换到 6，原始报告准确率从 89.6% 掉到 37.7%（均为 α=4）——单一注入配置下的弱报告不能证明模型缺乏定位能力，内省结论高度依赖实验配置。
- **与先前工作的强度校准对比**：将 Hahami et al. 在 Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct 第 2 层、系数 5 的注入换算为相对强度 α≈2.03（中位残差范数 2.464），说明"88% 定位成功率"这类结果处于位置信息本就线性可解码的强度区间。

## Key Contributions

- **Probe-vs-report paradigm**: On identical trials, the authors measure both (a) a nine-way logistic-regression probe decoding the injected sentence from the residual stream at the final prompt token, and (b) the model's own zero-shot answer (highest-scoring label 1–9 from the logits). This separates *decodable information* from *behavioral reporting*, asking how demanding localization success really is as evidence of introspection.
- **Longitudinal pretraining comparison**: Using OLMo 3's fully public checkpoint ladder (step 0 through ~4T+ tokens and the released base), they show location decodability saturates extremely early (probe 98.3% at 8.4B tokens) while reliable reporting emerges only later (89.6% at the released base), and reporting develops *without* any post-training.
- **Decodable traces without pretraining**: Location is linearly decodable at step 0 (37.2% vs 11.3% shuffled-label control at the fixed setting; 30.5% vs 10.1% at α=2) and even in three freshly Gaussian-initialized untrained networks under a no-prompt protocol (31.5% at strength 8 immediately after the list, 21.5% after 256 intervening neutral tokens, vs 11.5%/10.9% uninjected controls; passes 199-label permutation tests with Holm correction). The architecture and random weights alone can propagate a recoverable location signal.
- **Configuration fragility of introspection claims**: In the same released 32B base model at α=4, raw reporting accuracy is 89.6% with layer-0 injection but only 37.7% with layer-6 injection — so a weak score at one injection site does not establish a general inability to localize, and cross-study comparisons are confounded by injection configuration.
- **Calibrated strength comparison to prior work**: Hahami et al.'s 88% localization on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (coefficient 5 at layer 2) corresponds to a relative injection size of ≈2.03× residual norm — near this paper's α=2 regime where a linear probe already achieves strong decoding.

## Methodology

Two experiments on OLMo 3 (mostly the 32B model: 64 blocks, d=5120, bf16, PyTorch forward hooks in HuggingFace Transformers), using the nine-sentence localization test of Hahami et al.: a fixed setup text, nine numbered neutral sentences sampled from a 100-sentence pool ("The dog barks loudly."), a question, and the answer prefix "SENTENCE␣". A forward hook adds the same vector α·r_L·v̂ to every word token of one target sentence at layer L, where r_L is the median residual norm at that layer (so α=1 injects a vector as long as a typical token's residual) and v̂ is either a concept vector (mean hidden state over a concept word minus the mean over 99 baseline words; 19 concepts like Volcanoes, Chess, Kites) or a random unit Gaussian direction; prompted results pool both equally.

**Measurements**: (1) *Linear probe* — nine-way logistic regression (L2, C=0.5) on the residual h_T at the last prompt token, read at a layer after injection; trained on 288 labeled trials per fold with passage-grouped 5-fold cross-validation in the sweep (seeds 21/101/102, 40 passages/seed × 9 target positions = 360 trials/condition); the LM's weights stay frozen. (2) *Model's raw answer* — the highest-scoring label among 1–9 at the final input position, no labeled examples in context.

**Pretraining sweep**: seven stage-1 checkpoints plus the released base (which includes mid-training and long-context training), at a fixed setting chosen after a wider layer/strength sweep (injection layers {0,6,16,32,48} in 32B; strengths α ∈ {0.25,0.5,1,2,4}): injection layer 0, α=4, probe readout layer 24 — the setting maximizing the released base's raw-answer accuracy across all three held-out seed folds.

**No-prompt experiment**: fresh random networks (linear/embedding weights ~ N(0, 0.02²), biases zeroed, normalization weights one) receive random-direction injections at layer 6 into a numbered sentence list *without* setup text or question; probes read layer 24 at uninjected newline-marker tokens placed 0/16/64/256 tokens after the list, with disjoint training/test sentence pools (360 train / 180 balanced test trials per seed). Controls include strength-0 uninjected trials, shuffled-label probes, norm-only baselines, and an attention-blocking check (masking all outward attention from the injected span eliminates the perturbation at later readouts exactly).

## Results & Highlights

- **Probe–answer gap across pretraining (OLMo 3 32B, fixed setting: injection layer 0, α=4, readout layer 24)**: the probe reaches 98.3% accuracy at the 8.4B-token checkpoint while the model's zero-shot answer is exactly at chance, 11.1% (1-in-9); at the released base checkpoint the two converge to 99.1% (probe) vs 89.6% (model answer) — i.e., a +87.2-point decodability-vs-reporting gap early in training that closes only late in pretraining.
- **Decodability before any training**: at step 0 the same probe setting achieves 37.2% vs 11.3% for the shuffled-label control (+25.9 points); at α=2, step-0 decoding is 30.5% in 32B (28.6–32.2%) and 27.6% in 7B vs 10.1% shuffled control; at α=1, 32B probe accuracy rises from 18.3% (step 0) to 76.9% after 8.4B tokens.
- **Random untrained networks, no prompt**: at injection strength 8, probe accuracy is 31.5% at gap 0 and 21.5% after 256 neutral suffix tokens, vs uninjected controls of 11.5% and 10.9% (chance 11.1%); for targets in sentences 1–8 the figures are 30.6% (gap 0) and 21.9% (gap 256); all prespecified strength-4/8, gap-0/256 tests at readout layers 24 and 58 pass 199-permutation tests with Holm correction.
- **Injection-layer sensitivity (released 32B base, α=4)**: raw reporting accuracy is 37.7% (35.8–41.1% across seeds) with layer-6 injection but 89.6% with layer-0 injection — a 51.9-point swing from the configuration alone; at the fixed layer-6 setting the probe still reaches 83.1% (80.8–85.6%) vs the shuffled-label probe's 12.0%, and concept injections are easier than random ones (probe 86.1% vs 80.2%; answers 42.8% vs 32.6%).
- **Frozen-probe transfer breaks under format change**: probes transfer to fresh numbered text at only 20.6–28.9% (random networks, α=8, gap 0) and collapse to 11.1–12.2% (chance) when sentence numbering is removed — though label removal also shortens the input, confounding the attribution.
- **Unexplained pretrained anomaly**: in the pretrained no-prompt condition the *uninjected* strength-0 control is elevated (14.3% at gap 0, 77/540 correct; post-hoc permutation p=0.014/0.018), so the authors refuse to draw conclusions about how pretraining changes propagation — an honest negative that weakens the pretrained arm of the no-prompt experiment.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is from a six-author team (Dustin Rubin, Vincenzo Timmel, Xufeng He, Aritra Saha, JooMan Kim, Vijay Madisetti) at Georgia Institute of Technology, dated November 10, 2026 (arXiv v2, 14 Nov 2026; cs.CL). The work is methodologically credible: it leverages OLMo 3's unique fully public checkpoint ladder for a longitudinal analysis few labs can run, pre-registers the no-prompt experiment, applies permutation tests with Holm correction, includes shuffled-label and uninjected controls, an attention-blocking propagation check, and a numerical-replay audit, and it openly reports failures (the elevated pretrained clean baseline, non-reproducible replays across attention implementations, post-hoc setting selection). The framing is careful and adversarial in the best sense — it stress-tests an interpretation (localization ⇒ introspection) rather than advocating for or against it. The paper engages directly and fairly with the relevant 2025–2026 literature (Lindsey; Hahami et al.; Macar et al.; Lederman & Mahowald; Ferrara; Singh et al.; Tang et al.; Fornasiere et al.).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The probe-vs-answer contrast itself is not new (Ferrara; probing-vs-reporting is established), but the longitudinal pretraining trajectory and especially the random-init/no-prompt propagation result are genuinely novel and the most damaging evidence yet that localization success can ride on an architectural artifact rather than learned introspection.
- **Rigor (strong)**: passage-grouped cross-validation, disjoint sentence pools in the no-prompt experiment, permutation tests with Holm correction, shuffled-label and uninjected controls, attention-blocking verification, and honest disclosure of anomalies. **Rigor (weak spots)**: the headline fixed setting (layer 0, α=4, readout 24) was chosen *after inspecting the sweep* and is precisely the setting that maximizes the base model's answer accuracy — the authors flag this, but it means the 89.6% base-reporting figure is a best-case post-hoc selection, and the selected-setting curves reuse seeds for selection so their error bars are not independent. Also, the sweep's cross-validation groups by passage but lets sentences and the 19 concepts recur across folds, so 98–99% probe accuracies likely overstate generalization to genuinely new text — the transfer test (dropping to 20.6–28.9% on fresh numbered text, chance without numbering) confirms the within-distribution numbers are inflated.
- **Reproducibility**: Good in principle (OLMo 3 checkpoints public, templates, concepts, seeds and hyperparameters all specified), but the authors themselves report that numerical replay of saved activations failed under the default attention implementation (exact only with explicit math attention, and even then differing from stored activations), and results depended on the attention kernel — a real portability hazard.
- **Significance**: The step-0 and random-network results substantially raise the burden of proof for introspection claims built on activation-perturbation localization. However, the effect is strength-dependent: at α=1 step-0 decoding is a modest 18.3%, while headline claims lean on α=4 injections four times the typical residual norm — a very large perturbation whose traces may be trivially salient in ways natural computation is not.
- **Red flags**: (a) the unexplained elevated pretrained uninjected control (14.3% vs 11.1% chance) sits inside the very experiment meant to compare pretrained vs random propagation; (b) the random-init no-prompt networks use a custom Gaussian init (std 0.02), *not* OLMo's actual step-0 checkpoint, slightly weakening the "architecture, not pretraining" claim; (c) single model family (OLMo 3), short synthetic sentence lists, and a 9-way forced choice rather than free generation; (d) no causal test that the model's answer uses the probe-decoded features (the authors acknowledge this).

## Strengths

- Separates *information availability* from *behavioral reporting* on identical trials — exactly the right decomposition for evaluating introspection evidence.
- The pretraining-trajectory analysis (7 checkpoints + base, two scales) is a rare longitudinal resource made possible by OLMo 3's open checkpoints.
- The random-network no-prompt experiment is a decisive control: recoverable location traces exist without pretraining, instructions, or a question, and survive 256 intervening tokens.
- The layer-0 vs layer-6 contrast (89.6% vs 37.7% in the same base model) is a concrete, citable warning that introspection benchmarks are extremely configuration-sensitive.
- Injection strength calibrated to each layer's median residual norm enables principled (if approximate) cross-model and cross-paper comparisons, including a quantitative bridge to Hahami et al.'s Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct result.
- Exceptionally honest limitations section: anomalies, replay failures, post-hoc selections, and confounds (e.g., label removal shortening the input) are all disclosed rather than buried.

## Limitations

- Decoding by a probe does not establish that the model's own answer causally uses the decoded features; no intervention tests this link.
- The main fixed setting was selected post hoc to maximize base-model reporting, and selected-setting curves reuse seeds, so the headline base-model number is optimistic.
- Extreme injection strengths (α=4, four times typical residual norm) dominate the headline results; at α=1 the pretraining-decodability story is far weaker, and norm-matched strength does not guarantee matched functional disruption across checkpoints.
- The stimulus universe is tiny and synthetic (100 neutral sentences, 19 concepts), and the sweep's CV lets sentences/concepts recur across folds; frozen probes largely fail to transfer to new text or unnumbered formats.
- The pretrained arm of the no-prompt experiment is compromised by an unexplained elevated clean baseline (14.3% vs 11.1% chance), so pretraining's effect on propagation remains open.
- Scope: one architecture family (OLMo 3, 7B/32B), one Gaussian init scheme for random networks, artificial single-sentence injections unlike natural computation, 9-way forced-choice scoring rather than free generation, and aggregate-only exports preventing bootstrap confidence intervals.

## Relevance

直接关乎"LLM 能否忠实报告自身内部计算"这一可解释性核心问题：论文给出强证据表明扰动定位成功可以由架构自带的线性可解码痕迹支撑（甚至无需预训练），为评估各类内省/自我报告基准提供了一个低成本的探针-回答对照检查协议。对任何关注机制可解释性、模型自监控与可靠性评估的工作，这都是设计实验与解读结论时必须引用的警示性对照。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the model's answer causally depend on the linearly decodable location signal? Attention-knockout or causal-tracing interventions on the propagated trace could directly test the probe–answer link the paper leaves open.
- How do the results change under *functionally* matched (e.g., behavioral-output-change-matched) rather than norm-matched injection strengths, and at sub-behavioral-threshold perturbations?
- Does post-training (instruction tuning, preference optimization — e.g., the yes/no detection emergence in Macar et al.) shift the probe–answer gap differently from continued pretraining?
- Do the step-0/random-init decodable traces replicate across architectures (Llama, Qwen, Mistral) and with OLMo's actual step-0 checkpoint rather than a custom Gaussian init?
- What explains the elevated pretrained uninjected-control accuracy (14.3% vs 11.1% chance) — probe artifacts, tokenization quirks, or a genuine positional prior?
- Can probes trained with genuinely disjoint sentence/concept pools still reach ~99% decoding, or is the within-distribution ceiling partly memorization of stimulus-specific features?
