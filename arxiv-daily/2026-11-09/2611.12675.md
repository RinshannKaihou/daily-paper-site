> **一句话总结：** 本文证明在单个模型上发现的线性可解释性工具（拒答方向、正确性方向、情感探针）可以通过仅用无标注提示拟合的残差流之间的岭回归线性映射迁移到其他模型：在 110 对模型（5 个家族、1B–72B 参数）上，仅用普通问题拟合的映射恢复 54% 的原生拒答转向效果，再补充每类仅 16 条来自工具作用区域的无标注提示后恢复率提升至 102%（控制方向最多 1%），并给出 Proposition 1 与一个无需标签的"覆盖度分数"（Spearman ρ≈0.45–0.71）来解释和预测何时迁移成功。

> **TL;DR:** University of Houston authors show that interpretability tools built in one LLM (a refusal steering direction, an ARC/TruthfulQA correctness-scoring direction, an SST-2 sentiment probe) can be carried to other models through a ridge-regression map between residual streams fit on unlabeled prompts only, tested over all 110 ordered pairs of 11 instruction-tuned models (Llama/Qwen/Gemma/Phi-3/Mistral, 1B–72B). A map fit on 5,400 general questions recovers 54% of the target's own refusal steering effect and 16% (ARC) / 77% (TruthfulQA) of its correctness gain — the ARC delta is a 72-point jump to 88% once unlabeled answer-candidate prompts from the tool's region are added (refusal rises to 102%, sentiment matches native within 0.2 points), while random/orthogonal/mapped-random control directions recover ≤1% of refusal. A proposition plus a label-free coverage score (Spearman ρ = 0.67–0.71 with recovery; runs with c ≥ 0.95 average 99% recovery, c < 0.8 only 4%) explains both when transfer works and why prior cross-domain mapping attempts reported negative R².

## 核心贡献

- **跨模型工具迁移的系统实证**：在 110 对有序模型对（11 个指令微调模型、5 个家族、1B–72B 参数）以及 base↔instruct 模型之间，测试三类可解释性工具（拒答转向方向、正确性读出方向、情感探针）通过无标注岭回归映射的迁移，配备随机/正交/映射-随机三种控制方向、五个随机种子与模型级 bootstrap 置信区间。
- **"覆盖度决定迁移"的理论与度量**：Proposition 1 证明只要两模型在映射覆盖的数据上线性对应，被迁移工具与目标原生工具的偏差仅由数据未覆盖的部分界定（sin θ ≤ κ√(1−c²)）；由此导出的免标签覆盖度分数 c 在超过 16,000 次运行中追踪恢复率，呈阈值行为（c ≥ 0.95 → 平均恢复 99%；c < 0.8 → 仅 4%）。
- **解释了以往跨域映射失败的报告**：映射研究显示在某一个域上拟合的专门映射在域内 R² = 0.48、在其他八个域上 R² = −1.94（72 种组合中 90% 为负），失败本质是对训练数据之外的外推失败，而非模型间不可对应。
- **极少提示即可饱和**：拒答方向迁移只需在普通文本映射上补充每类 16 条无标注 harmful/harmless 提示即达 102% 恢复率（500 次运行中 97% 至少 80%）。
- **迁移 vs. 目标内重建的对比**：拒答方向在目标内同样可由这 16 条提示重建（97%），故其迁移证明的是机制跨模型对应；而正确性与情感读出工具的迁移优于目标无标注表征能产出的任何工具（如 ARC 上目标主成分 −76% vs 迁移 88%），相当于替代了约 25–200 条目标标注问题。
- **免标签的源模型排序**：通过将映射后的源表征 patch 进目标并测 next-token KL（map KL），可在目标内排序源模型（中心化后 ρ = −0.46），从最低 KL 源迁移把拒答恢复率从 54% 提升到 67%。

## Key Contributions

- **Systematic cross-model transfer study**: All 110 ordered pairs among 11 instruction-tuned models from five families (Llama-3.x, Qwen2.5, Gemma-2, Phi-3, Mistral; 1B–72B parameters) plus base↔instruct transfers, carrying three tools — a refusal steering direction, a correctness read-out direction, and a sentiment probe — through linear maps fit without any target labels, with three control directions and five seeds.
- **A coverage account of transfer**: Proposition 1 bounds the angle between the carried and native tools by κ√(1−c²), where the label-free coverage score c = ‖δ_S S_λ‖/‖δ_S‖ measures how strongly the map's training data vary along the tool; the score tracks recovery across 16,000+ runs (Spearman ρ = 0.67 refusal, 0.71 ARC, 0.68 TruthfulQA, 0.45 sentiment) with threshold behavior.
- **Reconciliation of prior negative results**: A domain-specialist map reaches R² = 0.48 on its own domain but −1.94 on the other eight (90% of 72 train/eval combinations negative), fully explaining earlier reports of cross-domain mapping failures as extrapolation failures.
- **Data efficiency**: Adding as few as 16 harmful + 16 harmless unlabeled prompts to a general map raises refusal recovery from 54% to 102%, with 97% of 500 runs recovering at least 80%.
- **Carrying vs. rebuilding in the target**: For refusal, the target can rebuild from the same 16 prompts per class (97%), so transfer demonstrates mechanism correspondence rather than label savings; for read-out tools (correctness, sentiment), carried tools beat anything derivable from the target's unlabeled representations — the carried ARC direction is worth about 25 labeled target questions, and a sentiment probe carried through 10 unlabeled reviews is 9 points more accurate than one built from 10 labeled reviews.
- **Label-free source ranking**: A "map KL" measure (KL divergence of the target's next-token distribution when patched with mapped source representations) ranks sources within a target (ρ = −0.46 centered, p < 0.001); choosing the lowest-KL source lifts mean refusal recovery from 54% to 67% (vs. 80% for the best source in hindsight).

## Methodology

The paper treats an interpretability tool as an estimate of a shared mechanism and asks when it survives translation into another model.

**Models and representations.** 11 instruction-tuned checkpoints (Llama-3.2-1B through Qwen2.5-72B; the two largest in 4-bit NF4). A prompt's representation is the residual stream at its last token after block ⌊dL⌉, L2-normalized: relative depth d = 0.5 for refusal (0.75 for Qwen2.5-72B), d = 0.75 for the read-out tools with templates "Question: {q} Answer: {a}" and "Review: {t} Sentiment:".

**Maps.** Ridge regression with intercept from source to target representations of the same prompts, penalty λ selected by exact leave-one-out over a log grid. Training-data variants: *General* (5,400 ordinary questions, 600 from each of nine domains: TruthfulQA, GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, CommonsenseQA, MBPP, MMLU-law, MedMCQA, XQuAD en/es); *General+task* (adds unlabeled prompts from the tool's operating region: 256 harmful + 256 harmless instructions, answer candidates of 200 questions per dataset, or 256 SST-2 reviews); *task only*; *narrow* (GSM8K only); *other datasets* (BeaverTails/Dolly; OpenBookQA/MMLU; Yelp); plus an RBF kernel map. Task prompts never carry labels.

**Tools.** (1) Refusal direction = difference of mean representations of 128 harmful and 128 harmless instructions (Arditi et al. style), tested causally by addition (α·m·r̂ at every position, scored as refusal rate on 64 held-out harmless prompts, at each target's working strength α*) and ablation (projection removed from every block, scored on 64 held-out harmful prompts); refusal detected by phrase matching and cross-checked with Llama Guard 3 (8B). (2) Correctness direction (Armstrong et al.) from 50 labeled questions, scoring candidates by projection on ARC-Challenge (500 test questions) and TruthfulQA MC1 (400 questions, four options). (3) Sentiment probe from 50 positive/50 negative SST-2 reviews, tested on 500 validation reviews. A tool is carried by mapping the source representations of its defining prompts and rebuilding it from the mapped means.

**Measurement.** Recovery R = (E_carried − E₀)/(E_native − E₀), against the target's own tool and three magnitude-matched controls (random, orthogonal-to-native random, mapped-random). Five seeds redraw tool prompts, task prompts, coverage subsets, and controls; intervals are 95% bootstrap resampling *models* (2–3× wider than pair-level). Targets must have a working native tool (e.g., Mistral-7B excluded as a refusal target for 9% baseline refusal; 9 ARC targets after two small models fail the native-beats-zero-shot criterion).

**Theory.** Under a linear correspondence y = xA + b + ε on training and tool prompts, E[δ_S W_λ] − E[δ_T] = −λδ_S(X_c⊤X_c+λI)⁻¹A and sin θ ≤ κ√(1−c²), κ = ‖A‖‖δ_S‖/‖δ_SA‖ ≥ 1. Covered directions (c→1) transfer exactly; directions the data never vary along are carried as zero (the GSM8K-map failure mode); adding prompts can only shrink the uncovered term. Geometry checks support linearity where the map has data: linear CKA 0.84 at mid depth (0.01 shuffled), kernel map adds only ~0.05 R², local linear maps no better than one global map, intrinsic dimension 18–24.

## Results & Highlights

- **Refusal steering (10 targets, 100 pairs, 5 seeds)**: A map fit on general questions alone recovers **54% [37, 70]** of the target's own refusal-addition effect; adding 16 unlabeled harmful + 16 harmless prompts raises recovery to **102% [99, 107]** (every target ≥97%), while random and mapped-random control directions recover **≤1%** [0, 1]. Under ablation (9 targets), the general map recovers 71% [53, 92] and task prompts 82% [62, 102], vs. ≤4% for controls.
- **Correctness scoring**: On ARC-Challenge the general map recovers only **16%** of the native accuracy gain over zero-shot (interval [−23, 46] includes zero) because the general data contain questions but not question+answer pairs; adding unlabeled answer candidates of 200 questions lifts recovery to **88%** [66, 101]. On TruthfulQA the corresponding numbers are **77% [69, 85] → 98%** [97, 100]. The mapped-random control sits at **−149%** on ARC — below the zero-shot baseline.
- **Sentiment (SST-2)**: The general map lands 7.8 points below the target's own probe (81.7% vs. 89.5% accuracy); with 256 unlabeled task reviews the gap closes to **+0.2 points** (89.6%). The random-direction control is at chance (52.2%).
- **Coverage score tracks recovery (16,000+ runs)**: Spearman ρ = 0.67 (refusal), 0.71 (ARC), 0.68 (TruthfulQA), 0.45 (sentiment); refusal runs with c ≥ 0.95 recover **99% on average** (94% of them ≥80%), while runs with c < 0.8 recover only **4%**. The GSM8K-only map (c = 0.46) transfers essentially nothing (2% refusal, −65% ARC).
- **Mapping study explaining cross-domain failures**: a domain-specialist map reaches **R² = 0.48** on held-out prompts of its own domain but **−1.94** on the other eight domains; 90% of the 72 train/eval domain combinations give negative R². A mixed map on all nine domains matches the specialists (0.47, trailing by ≤0.12).
- **Robustness across family, scale, and paradigm**: with task prompts, refusal recovery is 102% both within and across families and uncorrelated with the parameter-size gap — Llama-3.2-1B steers Llama-3.1-70B at 101% and the reverse at 96%. Between base and instruction-tuned models, TruthfulQA and sentiment tools recover 93–101% with task prompts (ARC 61–92%).
- **Carried vs. built-in-target (Table 3)**: without labels, the target's top principal component fails on ARC (**−76%** [−156, −6] at 10 questions) while the carried tool recovers 44% [8, 69]; at 200 questions carried reaches 88% vs. 109% with 200 labeled target questions — the carried ARC direction is worth ~25 labeled target questions. The sentiment probe carried through just 10 unlabeled reviews (−3.6 points) beats one built from 10 labeled reviews (−12.7 points) by ~9 points.
- **Per-block ablation (Appendix H)**: carried per-block directions recover **101% [89, 112]** of the native per-block effect, leaving 9% of harmful prompts refused vs. 8% native (and vs. 19% for the single native direction); ablating a carried direction makes Llama Guard 3 flag 33–54% of responses unsafe vs. 53% for the native direction and 6–8% at baseline.

## Authors & Credibility

Five authors from the Department of Computer Science at the University of Houston (four cougarnet student accounts plus Arjun Mukherjee, a faculty member working on NLP/interpretability). The paper is an arXiv preprint (cs.AI listing, v1, 9 Nov 2026), apparently not yet peer-reviewed, formatted in a NeurIPS/ICML-style template. It builds directly on the group's own prior work — Armstrong et al. 2026b (the correctness-direction tool, arXiv:2610.04512) and Armstrong et al. 2026a (the EMNLP 2026 cross-domain mapping-failure study) — so the tooling and evaluation machinery are inherited from a tested codebase. The empirical hygiene is unusually strong for a preprint: three distinct control directions, five seeds with model-level bootstrap intervals, pre-stated inclusion criteria for targets, an honest dose-response analysis over all steering strengths (95% integrated recovery with task prompts vs. 102% at the single working strength, bounded by a 96% normalization ceiling that explains >100% values), and appendix-level disclosure of every exclusion. Compute is modest (two RTX 6000 Ada 48 GB GPUs; the 70B/72B models in 4-bit), appropriate for a university lab.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core question (can interpretability tools be reused across models?) is timely, and the coverage theory is a genuine contribution — it converts "maps sometimes fail" (Syed 2026; Armstrong et al. 2026a) into a predictive, label-free criterion. However, the ingredients are standard: ridge-regression stitching, difference-of-means directions, and Arditi-style refusal steering; Oozeer et al. 2025 already transferred steering vectors between small (≤3B) models. The advance is breadth (110 pairs, up to 72B), controls, and the theory — not the mechanism.
- **Rigor**: Mostly high, with specific soft spots. The ablation coherence criterion that excludes Gemma-2-27B was **added after the main run** (disclosed, and results are reported both ways, but post-hoc exclusion criteria deserve scrutiny). Several conditions rest on **one seed** — ablation, kernel and task-only maps, per-block directions, and all base-model transfers — so the headline base↔instruct numbers (Table 18) carry no seed replication. Refusal is scored by **phrase matching** ("I'm sorry", "As an AI", …) at a single working strength per target; this is crude and English-centric, though Llama Guard 3 cross-checks the ablation side. The two largest models run in 4-bit NF4, and quantization noise in the residual stream is never analyzed.
- **Reproducibility**: Good on paper — standard HF checkpoints are named, prompt sources and counts are exact (e.g., 324 LLM-LAT + 97 HarmfulQA + 34 AdvBench + 13 HarmBench + 6 JailbreakBench harmful prompts), and the math is fully specified — but no code release is mentioned, and the many small design choices (working strengths α* grid {0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.2}, depth d = 0.5, 384-token truncation, 48/96-token generations) would take effort to replicate exactly.
- **Significance**: The read-out-tool result (carried ARC/sentiment tools beat anything derivable from the target's unlabeled representations) is the practically novel part; the refusal result is more of a mechanism-correspondence demonstration, as the authors themselves admit — anyone with white-box access to run the map can just rebuild the refusal direction from the same 16 prompts. The coverage score's within-pair ranking ability is limited (ρ = −0.05 across refusal pairs for a fixed map), and map KL correlates only loosely across targets (ρ = −0.35, interval [−0.72, 0.05] includes zero).
- **Red flags / overclaiming**: The title's scope ("Interpretability Is Not Model-Specific") outruns the evidence for anything beyond linear directions — SAE features and circuits are explicitly untested and may need different maps. ARC transfer is heterogeneous (depends strongly on source scale, ρ = 0.68 with the signed log size ratio; the two smallest sources recover −25% to 89%), so the "1B steers 72B" story holds for refusal but not uniformly for correctness. The dual-use dimension — a carried refusal direction whose ablation strips 53%-equivalent refusal (Llama Guard flags 33–54% unsafe) — is acknowledged but framed as requiring white-box access rather than analyzed as an attack surface.

## Strengths

- Exceptional experimental controls: three matched control directions (random, orthogonal, mapped-random — the last passes through the identical map and renormalization pipeline), five seeds, model-level bootstrap intervals, and target inclusion criteria that prevent dividing by near-zero native effects.
- A real theorem tied to practice: Proposition 1 is simple but exactly explains the observed phenomena — saturation of coverage curves, the GSM8K-map zero-transfer failure mode, monotone improvement from adding prompts, and the threshold behavior of recovery vs. c.
- Resolves a live contradiction in the literature: shows that the negative cross-domain R² results of Armstrong et al. 2026a and the null steering-transfer results of Syed 2026 are what a coverage-limited linear map must produce, and that both vanish once the map's data include the tool's region.
- Honest self-assessment throughout: the paper explicitly states where transfer saves nothing (refusal rebuildable from the same prompts), where it depends on source scale (ARC), which conditions are single-seed, and that the method requires white-box access.
- The label-free source-selection measure (map KL) turns the theory into an actionable recipe: coverage tells you whether the map's data suffice; map KL tells you which source to carry from.

## Limitations

- Only linear direction tools and one linear probe are tested; sparse-autoencoder features, circuits, multi-direction concepts (cf. Wang et al. 2026 on refusal dimensionality), and nonlinear tools are untested.
- Proposition 1 assumes a linear correspondence on the covered region, ignores renormalization of mapped representations, and gives no guidance on which model pairs correspond well (κ is pair-specific and unmeasured a priori).
- Single-seed conditions: ablation, kernel/task-only maps, per-block ablation, and all base↔instruct transfers.
- Coverage requires already knowing where a tool operates — the method presupposes task-region prompts; small draws can miss (the weakest 16-prompt refusal draw recovers only 44%, mostly on Llama-3.2-1B, whose small native effect makes the recovery ratio unstable).
- Refusal evaluation is phrase-matching based, at one strength per target, English-only refusal phrases; base models are untested for refusal (they do not refuse reliably); the read-out tools are never used to steer.
- The two largest models run in 4-bit precision, and only 11 open instruction-tuned models (plus 4 base models for read-out tools) are covered — no closed/API models, and the method inherently requires white-box activation access.
- Refusal recovery >100% at the working strength is an artifact of the normalized-vs-raw reference (96% ceiling), competently explained but worth remembering when reading the headline 102%.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是关于残差流表征几何、线性探针/转向向量与机制跨模型对应（representation correspondence）的核心工作，其覆盖度理论与免标签迁移方法对任何想在多个模型间复用内部方向/probe 的机制可解释性研究都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the coverage framework extend to sparse-autoencoder features and full circuits, where the "tool" is no longer a single linear direction and the map may need to be sparse or compositional?
- Can κ (the pair-specific conditioning factor in Proposition 1) be estimated label-free a priori, turning the coverage score into a true per-pair recovery predictor rather than a per-map one?
- How does transfer behave under heavier quantization (the 70B/72B models ran in 4-bit here) — does quantization noise in the residual stream shift the linear correspondence or merely rescale it?
- Would the coverage account predict transfer for emergent-misalignment directions (Syed 2026) or backdoor triggers, where the tool's operating region is harder to specify than "harmful vs. harmless prompts"?
- Can carried tools bootstrap a stronger native tool — e.g., use a carried correctness direction to pseudo-label target questions, then re-estimate the direction in the target's raw (unnormalized) space where ablation works better (the Phi-3-mini normalization failure)?
- Is the threshold at c ≈ 0.8–0.95 universal across depths, model scales, and concepts, or does it shift with the intrinsic dimension of the representations (18–24 here)?
