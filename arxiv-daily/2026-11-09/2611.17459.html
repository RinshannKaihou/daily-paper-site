Classification with Large-Step Gradient Descent: Unifying Self-Stabilization and Convergence Rates ↗

Salma Tarmoun, Lachlan E. MacDonald, Konstantinos Emmanouilidis, Ziqing Xu, et al.  ·  University of Pennsylvania

edge-of-stability gradient-descent convergence-theory implicit-bias training-dynamics

Key Metric
3× ηc

Step sizes up to 3× the classical stability threshold (η/η_c ∈ {1 … 3}) — a regime where descent-lemma theory guarantees nothing — still converge: support-vector components contract to ~10⁻¹³.

Why This Matters

Explains why large-scale pretraining tolerates learning rates beyond 2/λ_max without diverging — and warns such rates can leave persistent, geometry-dependent directional biases.

Critical Edge

All validation is d = 3 synthetic — no real datasets, no deep networks. Identifying the reference subspace in modern networks “remains an open problem.”

01 Methodology

Fixed-step GD θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η∇L(θ_t) is studied where corrected sharpness η(1+δ_r)λ₁ > 2 — beyond the classical stability threshold. Each iterate splits into a parallel part (along the max-margin direction v) and an orthogonal part, since no minimizer manifold exists for exponential-tail losses on separable data.

A second-order Taylor expansion of the gradient yields a coupled two-equation system: an orthogonal oscillation equation and a parallel drift equation. The third-order coupling term −(η²/2)θ⊥ᵀH′θ⊥ converts oscillation energy into forward motion along the max-margin direction — literally implicit gradient descent on the sharpness, the quantitative engine of self-stabilization.

Convergence rests on five structural assumptions — Valley Geometry (subspace alignment, curvature comparability, loss–curvature compatibility) and a Decay Profile — verified analytically, with explicit constants, for pair-symmetric logistic regression (Proposition 1) and regular-simplex multiclass cross-entropy (Appendix D). An admissibility window carves out a “moderately unstable” regime for (η, r, θ₀), proven non-empty.

03 Strengths

▲ What holds up

Why the paper earns its claim

  • True unification — one framework recovers the self-stabilization mechanism and delivers iterate-level rates for logistic and cross-entropy losses.
  • Self-stabilization gets a clean quantitative identity: implicit gradient descent on the sharpness.
  • Assumptions verified analytically with explicit constants — a real upgrade over numerically-checked conditions of prior work.
  • The spanning vs. non-spanning dichotomy turns a data-geometry property into a sharp, testable prediction — all three η-dependence regimes exhibited.
  • Honest positioning: Table 1 maps the prior landscape; Remark 4 concedes where specialized analyses are sharper.

04 Limitations

▼ Where it stops

Read before citing

  • Symmetric linear models only — pair-symmetric logistic, simplex multiclass; no neural networks; asymmetric extension is a numerical sketch outside the theorems.
  • All validation is d = 3 synthetic (≤ 24 pairs); no real datasets, no deep-network experiments.
  • The admissible step-size window's constants are impractical to estimate — cannot yet certify a learning-rate schedule for real training.
  • Uniform conditioning ρ_tail > 0 fails exactly where the new bias lives — the most interesting case gets the weakest guarantees.
  • Generalization/robustness implications of the transient bias are deferred entirely to future work.

05 Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does an analog of the curvature-defined reference subspace exist for deep networks — and can it be estimated online from Hessian-vector products as a training-monitor signal?
  2. Do stochastic gradients, momentum, and Adam wash out the persistent weak components of the transient implicit bias, or re-inject them?
  3. Can non-spanning amplification be detected on real data as learning-rate-dependent shortcut learning, and does the non-monotone η-dependence survive finite-sample, non-symmetric data?
  4. On non-separable data the loss regains finite minimizers — does this analysis degenerate gracefully into minimizer-manifold theory, or reveal an intermediate regime?
  5. At practical horizons (token-budget-limited LLM pretraining), is the polynomial tail slow enough that the transient bias measurably affects test-time margins?
