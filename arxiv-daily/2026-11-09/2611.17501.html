arXiv 2611.17501 · 12 Nov 2026 Meta × AMD · MI350X

Native MXFP6 GEMMs take production ads ranking below 8 bits — 1.73–1.99× faster than FP16, accuracy bar intact.

一句话总结：Meta 与 AMD 在 MI350X 上实现原生 MXFP8/MXFP6 GEMM 内核，首次在 0.01% NE 上线门槛内将生产级推荐模型量化推到 8 位以下。

Advancing Quantization for R&R Models with Microscaling Formats

Yavuz Yetim, Bor-Yiing Su, Zhe Wang, Arthur Liu et al. — 19 authors, Meta Platforms & AMD

Key metric · GEMM speedup vs FP16
1.73–1.99×

MXFP6 across all supported linear layers of both production models on MI350X — peaking at 2.90× on Model A's DCN down-projection. Production row-wise FP8 manages only 1.49–1.63×.

Why this matters

Carries OCP microscaling formats from LLMs into accuracy-critical industrial ranking — a full evidence chain from kernel engineering to end-to-end QPS/NE trade-offs.

▲ Critical edge

The central 0.01% NE compliance claim is asserted without underlying numbers, evaluation data, or the layer-selection rule — all offline replay, no online A/B.

Methodology

  • Four formats, one fight — FP16 baseline vs row-wise FP8 (today's production) vs MXFP8 (E4M3) vs MXFP6 (E2M3). OCP blocks of 32 elements share one E8M0 power-of-two scale; mantissa bits dominate error (~6 dB each), motivating the element picks, with Overflow-Aware Scaling bounding saturation.
  • Real kernels, real traffic — per-call-site QSNR against FP16 outputs, computed with the actual low-precision GEMM kernels over captured production batches (QuantSuite). No simulated quantization.
  • Two production DLRM-style rankers — Model A: 313 linear call sites / 47 GEMM shapes; Model B: 202 / 46, replayed from real serving traffic.
  • Kernel engineering — FlyDSL GEMMs crack 6-bit packing (0.75 B/element) via 192-element K-tiles split into three 64-element sub-tiles, 3-buffer deep pipelining, preshuffled weights staged in a register ring, XCD-aware L2 remapping, and split-K. PyTorch Inductor is extended to fuse activation quantization into producer kernels.
  • Benchmark discipline — L2-flushed median-of-50 GEMM timings; end-to-end paired alternating runs with 10,000-sample bootstrap CIs and output-agreement checks.

  • Accuracy holds: MXFP8 ≈ production FP8 (median per-call-site gap 0.1 dB); MXFP6 only ~1 dB below MXFP8 — far under the 6 dB-per-mantissa-bit rule, since E2M3 keeps all three mantissa bits.
  • End-to-end QPS beats production FP8: +5.3% / +1.5% (Model A) and +14.3% / +7.6% (Model B) at batches 1024 / 2048, with 6/6 and 3/3 prediction tasks inside the 0.01% NE bar (selective MXFP6 covers 80.8% / 72.9% of linear weights).
  • No universal winner: FP8 is fastest on the smallest request-level layers (M = 25–51, beating MXFP6 on 14–16 of 19 shapes); MXFP8 is slower than FP16 on 18/45 Model B shapes.
  • Fusion is existential: unfused activation preparation cost 1.7–5.2× FP16 in summed linear time — enough to erase the entire GEMM gain.

Strengths

  • First measured, quality-preserving native MXFP6 deployment — on real production rankers with real captured traffic, not synthetic proxies.
  • Disciplined measurement: real-kernel QSNR, median-of-50 with L2 flush, paired alternating E2E timings with bootstrap CIs, output-agreement checks.
  • Honest fine-grained reporting: per-shape results, disclosed MXFP8 losses on small shapes, explicit kernel-to-E2E gap accounting.
  • Actionable rule for serving teams: pick the format per GEMM shape — MXFP6 for large compute-bound layers, FP8-family for tiny request-level ones.

Limitations

  • Evidence base is exactly two proprietary Meta models on one GPU (MI350X); NVIDIA transfer untested — there MXFP6 peak equals FP8 peak.
  • NE compliance asserted at task level without published deltas, evaluation data, or the layer-selection rule; no online production A/B.
  • E2E gains (+1.5–14.3%) are modest vs 1.7–2.3× kernel gains — K-tile-major producer fusions are not yet in the production Inductor path.
  • Kernel tuning depends on the internal AlphaKernel agent; no public artifact, limiting independent verification.

Questions for further exploration

  • How close to the 0.01% NE bar do individual tasks actually land, and does the margin survive online A/B traffic?
  • Could QAT or GPTQ-style MX-block rounding push MXFP6 coverage beyond 80.8% / 72.9% — or enable MXFP4 on the least sensitive layers?
  • How much of the kernel-to-E2E gap closes once K-tile-major producer fusions land in Inductor, especially at large batches?
  • Do results transfer to NVIDIA GB300, where MXFP6 lacks the 4× headroom over FP8 that MI350X provides?
  • Can per-shape format selection be automated at compile time from shape and traffic statistics?
  • Why are small user-sequence layers so quantization-sensitive (22.3–28.5 dB QSNR) — and would Hadamard rotations fix it?
