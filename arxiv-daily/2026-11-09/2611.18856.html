Key Metric

+2.36 pts

Mean paired held-out pass@1 gain of SIM over memory-less continuation — Terminal-Bench 2.1 → 4, ten paired runs: 21.70 vs 19.33, p = 0.003, 9/10 pairs won, dz = 1.28.

Why This Matters

Answers how self-evolving agents carry hard-won experience across model and benchmark changes — improving continuously instead of restarting the search from zero.

回答自进化智能体的核心问题：换模型、换基准后如何携带经验继续进化，而非从零重搜。

Critical Edge

Outside coding, every condition is a single run — the headline +2.36 is the only statistically protected claim, and even the striking +11.33 cross-model gap rests on one run per arm.

— distilled from Critical Assessment & Limitations

Methodology

  • Proposer–selector loop (Meta-Harness): each of N = 10 rounds, the proposer generates k = 5 candidate harnesses from execution feedback (scores, traces, errors); the selector screens and picks by performance, complexity, domain-specificity, and regression risk. Only evolve-set feedback ever reaches the optimizer.
  • SIM, read whole — no retrieval, no truncation: the full memory sits in both proposer and selector prompts. Every edit is filed under a root-cause hypothesis, storing its rationale, task-fix predictions written before evaluation, measured outcomes after, and provenance (domain / benchmark / policy / round). Failed and regressing edits are archived, not dropped.
  • Four-condition CRSI protocol: continue from one shared evolved harness under Cold Start / Harness Transfer / Problem-Only Memory / Full SIM, with matched rounds, budgets, policy, and tasks — isolating what memory adds from what the starting harness adds.
  • Four domains + cross-model: coding (TB2.1→TB4), text classification (3→8 datasets), agentic workspace (Harvey LAB→JobBench+GDPval+APEX), engineering (EngDesign→Frontier-Eng); Opus 5.5 → Gemini 3.8 Flash transfers; plus ten independent paired coding runs.

  • Wins everywhere tested: Full SIM finishes highest in all six cross-dataset and all four cross-model settings, exceeding Harness Transfer by 2.43–4.35 points.
  • Diagnosis alone isn't the secret: Problem-Only Memory is mixed (−1.82 to +2.40 vs Harness Transfer); Full SIM beats it in all ten settings — the rationale/outcome/provenance evidence does the work.
  • Model-generation chain (TB2.1): carried harness+SIM beats fresh RSI in two of three generations (+3.5, +1.6, −0.8).
  • Less forgetting: during TB4 optimization, held-out TB2.1 falls 90.22 → 88.89 with SIM vs 87.56 without.
  • Honest negatives: Opus-evolved harnesses start below Gemini seeds (−0.60 to −5.48); memory-less transfer finishes below Cold Start in three of four cross-model domains.

Strengths

  • Genuinely controlled design: four memory conditions from one shared evolved harness with matched rounds, budgets, policy, and tasks — the right way to isolate what memory adds.
  • The paired ten-source-run coding study with t-test, CI, signed-rank, and effect size is rare rigor for agent-harness papers.
  • Transparent about failure: negative-transfer starts, one losing SIM pair, one losing generation, and Problem-Only's mixed results are all surfaced, not buried.
  • Case studies (P3/P5/P8, Table 8) explain how memory changes optimizer behavior — e.g., retaining completion verification while adding feasibility checks, versus misattributing an inherited gate and twice repairing deadlocks it caused.
  • Keeps failed predictions and replaced edits on file, giving later rounds the evidence behind a diagnosis, not just its name.

Limitations

  • Independent repetitions exist only in coding; the other three domains have one run per condition, so their margins are descriptive.
  • Inherited-memory vs. freshly-written-memory attribution is confounded — no write-without-inherit control arm.
  • Full-memory-in-prompt with no retrieval or truncation is untested beyond 10 rounds and a handful of entries; prompt-cost scaling unmeasured.
  • Only interface-compatible harness transitions are studied; planner and tool-interface adaptation stays fixed.
  • Short horizons: 10 rounds × 5 candidates per run; longest chain is four Gemini generations on one benchmark.
  • Workspace judging relies on a single LLM judge from the same family as the target policy.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How much of SIM's advantage comes from inherited source memory versus entries written fresh during target RSI? A write-but-don't-inherit control would cleanly separate the two.
  2. Does reading the full SIM in-prompt scale to 100+ entries or 100-round runs, or is retrieval or summarization eventually needed — and does entry ordering or pruning change the outcome?
  3. Does SIM help when benchmark and policy model change simultaneously, or when the harness interface itself is incompatible (tool-schema migration)?
  4. Most predicted task fixes fail (1/4, 0/2, 1/2 in the P3 trace) — can prediction calibration be improved so unresolved predictions better guide when to abandon a root-cause hypothesis?
  5. Does structured memory of evaluator-specific fixes increase or decrease the reward-hacking risk flagged in the ethics statement?
