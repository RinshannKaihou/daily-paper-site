Key Finding

Three mechanisms of increasing spatial reach — trap-assisted migration, wordline coupling, sense-amp disturb — unify RowHammer, RowPress, and ColumnDisturb.

结合 DRAM Bender 实测、器件测量与经约 70 万器件校准的 TCAD 仿真，以空间范围递增的三种机制（EM / CC / SAMD）统一解释 RowHammer、RowPress 与 ColumnDisturb 的器件级物理框架。

higher OFF-state leakage in unselected cells under the sense-amplifier-held rail bias vs. the retention-like precharged bitline — I_CD/I_RT ≈ 9 at V_G = −0.2 V.

Why This Matters

First physical framework spanning all three read-disturb modes — directly informing memory-reliability modeling and RowHammer attack-and-defense research.

Critical Edge

The tail-vs-bulk explanation rests on rare, favorably positioned traps that are simulated but never directly observed — no charge-pumping, TEM, or single-device tail statistics confirm them.

01Methodology — Three Evidence Layers

  • DIMM characterization — FPGA-based DRAM Bender at 85 °C, 4-s retention baseline, complementary data patterns; bitflips counted by location and polarity after stress.
  • Access-device measurements — OFF-state leakage vs. gate bias around VBBW = −0.2 V under retention-like (source 0.5 V) vs. ColumnDisturb-like (0 V) terminal conditions.
  • TCAD simulation — 6F² saddle-fin access device + distributed wordline RC network; Fermi–Dirac, SRH, BTBT models; calibrated against array-averaged leakage from ~700,000 devices; transients track the floating storage-node voltage VSN.
  • Analysis flow — measured spatial taxonomy → mechanism identification by spatial range → per-cycle ΔVSN decomposition (EM+CC+SAMD vs. CC+SAMD vs. SAMD-only) → polarity & geometry signatures.

  • Polarity crossover resolved — 0→1 flips appear at lower first-failure hammer counts, yet 1→0 outnumbers them at the full 8×10⁷ count: two regimes of one vulnerability distribution.
  • Trap position matters enormously — TCAD sweeps show per-cycle charge loss varying by orders of magnitude with trap site; trap presence alone doesn't set EM strength.
  • NWL→PWL reversal reproduced — NWL gives larger ΔVSN at 36-ns pulses; PWL wins at 70.2-μs holds, matching experiment.
  • RowPress as regime shift — a 7.8-μs hold accumulates far more storage-node shift than the initial ~35-ns transient: EM+CC+SAMD burst → sustained SAMD-dominated leakage.

03Strengths

  • Triangulates three independent evidence layers (DIMM, device, TCAD) — never simulation alone; TCAD anchored to ~700k-device measured leakage.
  • One framework, three spatially distinct mechanisms, cleanly explains an unusually broad signature set: local peaks, remote subarray-stepped plateaus, RowPress polarity, NWL/PWL reversal, polarity crossover.
  • Identifies a concrete overlooked boundary condition — the SA-held rail-level bitline bias — correcting a common simplification (constant VCORE/2) in prior device-level simulations.
  • Honest scoping: states explicitly what average-curve calibration does and does not establish; defers 4F²/3D DRAM and VRD to future work.

04Limitations

  • Tail-vs-bulk trap hypothesis lacks direct experimental verification of the rare high-effectiveness configurations it posits.
  • TCAD diverges from DIMM protocols (7.8-μs vs. 70.2-μs hold; representative geometry) — no quantitative ΔVSN-to-bitflip mapping.
  • One 6F² saddle-fin structure, one open-bitline architecture, one temperature (85 °C), one default data pattern; DIMM vendor undisclosed.
  • No compact analytical model delivered, no mitigation evaluated — system-level payoff remains prospective.
  • Variable read disturbance and fluctuating trap occupancy excluded, though they plausibly interact with the trap-effectiveness picture.

05Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How does the EM/CC/SAMD hierarchy change in 4F² and 3D-stacked cells, where access-transistor geometry and wordline coupling differ substantially?
  2. Can the posited rare, high-effectiveness traps be observed directly (charge pumping, single-device statistics, trap spectroscopy) rather than inferred from HCfirst distributions?
  3. Does SAMD open new attack surfaces — e.g., column-strided accesses inducing distant bitflips — and would SA-aware refresh or bitline-parking policies mitigate them?
  4. How does the framework extend to variable read disturbance and to temperature/voltage corners other than 85 °C?
  5. Can calibrated charge-transfer terms quantitatively predict failures for access patterns outside calibration (interleaved RowHammer+RowPress, realistic traces)?
