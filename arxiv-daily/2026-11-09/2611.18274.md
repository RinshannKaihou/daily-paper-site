> **一句话总结：** 本文把 SAE 激活干预重新表述为"固定 token 预算下的分配问题"——在何时花费干预配额才是唯一变量：提出无记忆的 CFP（对齐探针投影挑最不对齐的 token 干预）和轨迹感知的 CLABS（用带泄漏积分的 PI 反馈控制器按累计偏移动态设定干预概率），在 RealToxicityPrompts 去毒与 GoEmotions 情感诱导上，二者在相同实现预算下取得所有预算型门控中最大的行为偏移（最小预算下达不确定性门控/随机门控的 2–3 倍），并首个为稀疏干预门控给出闭环稳定性证明；在 AlpacaEval 上它们是仅有的在所有预算下都被双评委偏好优于不干预的门控，同时以约 10⁻⁶ 前向传播的开销保住流畅度。

> **TL;DR:** The paper reframes SAE-based activation steering as a per-token budgeted allocation problem (a binary gate decides *when* to inject a fixed direction, with all methods sharing direction, strength, and layer). It introduces CFP, a memoryless gate that steers the least-aligned tokens by their projection onto a contrastive SAE-feature probe, and CLABS, which sets a budget-calibrated steering probability from a PI controller with a leaky integral over accumulated misalignment. On Gemma 2 2B and Llama 3.1 8B across RealToxicityPrompts (toxicity reduction) and GoEmotions (fear/sadness elicitation), CFP and CLABS deliver the largest behavioral shift among budgeted gates — 2–3× the shift of the entropy-based SIA-PE/SIA-ER gates and random STS at the smallest budgets, with CLABS recovering 89–92% of the Full-steer shift on emotion targets at a moderate budget. Under toxicity steering on AlpacaEval, they are the only gates preferred over No-steer at every budget under both GPT-5-mini and Claude Sonnet 4.6 judges (e.g., CFP 53.0/56.4 vs. Prompting 42.4/46.3 and Full-Steer 43.2/44.9 win rate at p=0.2), while Full-Steer's perplexity balloons from 23.0 to 41.2 and theirs stays near 26 even at p=0.8. A Jury-test analysis gives the first closed-loop stability certificate for a sparse steering gate.

## 核心贡献

- **将激活干预表述为预算分配问题**：在固定"可干预 token 比例 p"的预算下，所有方法共享同一干预方向、强度、层与后处理修正，唯一自由变量是"在哪些 token 上花预算"——这使所有对比变成受控消融，把"何时干预"确立为与"干预多强"正交的关键控制轴。
- **CFP（Contrastive Feature Projection）**：无记忆门控，用目标语料与中性语料的 SAE 特征差分选出 top-10 行为选择性特征、构成可解释探针 u，对每个 token 只算一次内积，把预算花在探针投影最小（最不对齐）的 token 上——即特征触发式条件干预的预算校准版。
- **CLABS（Closed-Loop Adaptive Bernoulli Steering）**：轨迹感知门控，将分配视为离散反馈控制——对标准化对齐信号 ŝₜ 计算误差 eₜ 与泄漏积分（EWMA，β=0.2，约记忆最近 5 个 token），用 PI 律 pₜ = clip(p₀ + κₚeₜ + κᵢēₜ; 0, 1) 设定干预概率：持续性漂移比孤立波动吸走更多预算。单次解码内完成，无需额外 rollout、回溯或学习型打分器。
- **首个稀疏干预门控的稳定性证明**：在一阶线性干预响应模型下，用 Jury 判据导出保证闭环谱半径 ρ(A)<1 的显式增益区间（0 ≤ a=φ−gκₚ < 1 且 0 ≤ gκᵢ < (2−β)(1+a)/β），证明均值状态几何收敛、跟踪误差方差有界；并证明泄漏积分使稳态偏差随 κᵢ 严格递减但不会消除（区别于经典积分器的有界记忆换稳态偏差）。
- **系统的受控实证**：两个基础模型（Gemma 2 2B / Llama 3.1 8B）× 三个 span 级行为目标（RealToxicityPrompts 去毒、GoEmotions 恐惧/悲伤诱导），加上 AlpacaEval 指令遵循与流畅度评估、积分项消融、增益鲁棒性扫描与实现预算核查（全部方法实测干预率偏离名义预算 ≤0.04）。

## Key Contributions

- **Budgeted-allocation framing of steering.** All methods share the steering direction, per-token strength α, intervention layer (16th transformer layer residual stream), decoding settings, and post-intervention corrections (norm preservation, activation clamping, repetition penalty); the gate sequence m₁:T is the sole independent variable, turning every comparison into a controlled ablation of allocation.
- **CFP (Contrastive Feature Projection)** — a memoryless gate: build a task-agnostic probe u from the top-k (k=10) SAE decoder rows ranked by contrastive feature differential Δᵢ between a target corpus D⁺ and neutral corpus D⁻, then steer exactly the tokens whose projection ⟨hₜ, u⟩ falls below a budget-calibrated threshold τ. Cost: one dot product per token (~5–8 kFLOPs, ≈10⁻⁶ of a forward pass); no SAE pass at inference.
- **CLABS (Closed-Loop Adaptive Bernoulli Steering)** — a trajectory-aware gate: standardize the projection into ŝₜ, form tracking error eₜ = s⋆ − ŝₜ (setpoint s⋆=0.75) and a leaky integral ēₜ = (1−β)ēₜ₋₁ + βeₜ, then sample the gate from Bernoulli(pₜ) with pₜ = clip(p₀ + κₚeₜ + κᵢēₜ; 0, 1) at gains (κₚ, κᵢ, β) = (1.0, 0.2, 0.2). Sustained drift draws more budget than momentary dips (formalized in Appendix A.5); p₀ is recalibrated by bisection on a held-out split so the realized rate matches budget p.
- **First stability certificate for a sparse steering gate.** Under a linear steering-response model (Assumption 1: ŝₜ₊₁ = φŝₜ + (1−φ)β₀ + g·mₜ + ηₜ), Theorem 1 derives an explicit sufficient gain box for closed-loop stability, with geometric mean convergence, bounded asymptotic error variance c(A)σ̄², and O(1/T) transient decay of time-averaged squared error. Corollary 1 shows the leaky integral strictly decreases (but cannot remove) the steady-state offset, unlike a classical integrator.
- **Comprehensive controlled evaluation.** Two base models and three span-level targets, three seeds, two independent LLM judges with position randomization, a component ablation isolating the integral term, a κₚ×κᵢ gain-robustness sweep with every cell recalibrated to budget, and verification that all gates' realized intervention rates stay within 0.04 of nominal.

## Methodology

The paper separates steering into two axes: allocation (the binary gate mₜ) and magnitude (fixed strength α), and holds everything except allocation constant.

**Direction and probe from SAEs.** Using a pretrained residual-stream SAE at the intervention layer (Gemma Scope for Gemma 2 2B; EleutherAI SAE for Llama 3.1 8B), they compute per-feature differentials Δᵢ between a behavior-exhibiting corpus D⁺ and a neutral corpus D⁻ (200 examples per side), take the top-10 features by Δᵢ, and sum/normalize their decoder rows into a unit steering direction v. A task-agnostic probe u = sign(α)·v makes "larger ⟨hₜ, u⟩" always mean "more aligned", regardless of suppression (α<0) or elicitation (α>0) polarity. The probe is a fixed vector, so reading it needs no SAE pass at inference.

**CFP** thresholds the instantaneous projection sₜ = ⟨hₜˡ, u⟩ at τ calibrated to budget p, steering only the least-aligned positions. It is informed but memoryless — it cannot distinguish a momentary dip inside an on-target span from a sustained drift.

**CLABS** closes the loop. The projection is standardized with calibration statistics (ŝₜ = (⟨hₜ,u⟩−μ)/(σ+ε)), compared to a setpoint s⋆, and the error feeds a proportional term κₚeₜ (the same signal CFP uses) plus a leaky-integral term κᵢēₜ (an EWMA over roughly the last 1/β tokens) that accumulates persistent misalignment. The steering probability pₜ = clip(p₀ + κₚeₜ + κᵢēₜ; 0, 1) rises while the trajectory lags and relaxes when alignment is restored; the bias p₀ is calibrated by bisection under active steering so the expected rate matches p. All gates are causal and single-pass — the entropy-based baselines (SIA-PE/SIA-ER) necessarily read the previous step's entropy.

**Stability analysis.** Under Assumption 1 (first-order linear steering response with persistence φ, gain g, equilibrium β₀, zero-mean noise), the controller state xₜ = (eₜ, ēₜ)ᵀ follows a linear recursion with matrix A coupling model constants (φ, g) and controller gains (κₚ, κᵢ, β). Theorem 1 (via the Jury/Schur–Cohn test) gives a sufficient box for ρ(A)<1, plus geometric convergence of the mean state, a variance bound, and O(1/T) transient decay. Corollary 1 shows eₛₛ = N/(1−φ+g(κₚ+κᵢ)) is non-increasing in κᵢ (strictly decreasing when N≠0) but bounded strictly away from zero within the stability box — the leaky integral trades exact tracking for bounded memory. Appendix A.5 proves a persistent error receives weight (κₚ+κᵢ)e while an alternating error of the same magnitude receives only (κₚ+κᵢ·β/(2−β))e, formalizing why memory helps.

**Experimental controls.** Every method within a (model, task) pair shares direction, layer, α (selected as the largest magnitude preserving calibration-split fluency: e.g., −35/30/30 for Gemma toxicity/fear/sadness; −4/3/2.5 for Llama), corrections (norm preservation, clamping to [0,40], repetition penalty 1.1), and decoding (temperature 0.1, 128 new tokens for behavior; temperature 0.7, 256 tokens for AlpacaEval). Baselines: No-steer, Full-steer, iteratively-engineered Prompting, and four budgeted gates — SIA-PE (high predictive entropy), SIA-ER (entropy rise), SRE (low SAE reconstruction error, a new mechanistic counterpoint they introduce), and STS (independent Bernoulli(p) coin flips — the authors' own prior work, serving as the allocation-agnostic control). Behavioral shift is scored on continuations only (RoBERTa toxicity classifier; DistilRoBERTa Ekman emotion classifier); utility is AlpacaEval pairwise win rate vs. No-steer under gpt-5-mini and Claude Sonnet 4.6 with randomized positions, plus PPL, rep-3, distinct-2.

## Results & Highlights

- **Largest behavioral shift among budgeted gates at matched budgets (Figure 1, all six model–target panels).** On RealToxicityPrompts (toxicity suppression) and GoEmotions fear/sadness (elicitation), for both Gemma 2 2B and Llama 3.1 8B, CFP and CLABS achieve the largest behavioral shift among the budgeted gates at every matched realized budget, with the widest margin at the smallest budgets (p≈0.1), where they reach **2–3× the behavioral shift of the uncertainty gates SIA-PE/SIA-ER and the random STS control** while steering the same number of tokens; the ranking is unchanged when compared at realized rather than nominal rates.
- **Sparse allocation recovers most of dense steering.** Already at a moderate budget, CLABS recovers **89–92% of the Full-steer behavioral shift on the emotion targets** — steering about half the tokens attains most of the effect of steering all of them; both gates also surpass Prompting's (fixed) shift once the budget is no longer minimal.
- **Only gates preferred over No-steer at every budget (Table 1, Gemma 2 2B, AlpacaEval under toxicity steering).** CFP and CLABS are the only budgeted gates with win rate >50 vs. No-steer at every budget under both judges: at p=0.2, CFP 53.0±2.6 (GPT-5-mini) / 56.4±2.5 (Sonnet 4.6) and CLABS 52.5/56.4, vs. Prompting 42.4/46.3, Full-Steer 43.2/44.9, SIA-PE 58.6/56.7 (high at p=0.2 but collapses to 47.1/49.2 by p=0.8), and STS 51.0/53.2 collapsing to 43.5/48.5 by p=0.8, while CFP at p=0.8 still holds 52.2/53.8. The same pattern holds on Llama 3.1 8B (Table 5): CFP 57.0/56.0 at p=0.5 vs. Prompting 44.3/47.1.
- **Fluency preservation.** Full-Steer's AlpacaEval perplexity blows up from No-Steer's 23.0±2.4 to 41.2±6.8 with rep-3 0.09 and distinct-2 0.68, whereas CLABS at p=0.8 keeps PPL 26.5±3.0, rep-3 0.02, distinct-2 0.88 — quality within one standard deviation of No-steer; on the fear-steered setting (Table 4), CLABS keeps the lowest PPL and highest distinct-2 among all gates at every budget (e.g., 23.6 vs. Full-Steer 48.6 at p=1.0).
- **The history term matters (Table 3 ablation, Gemma 2 2B toxicity, p=0.5).** Toxicity reduction: open-loop constant gate 0.12±0.02, I-only 0.15±0.03 (largest seed variance), P-only 0.21±0.02, full CLABS (PI) 0.24±0.02 — the PI combination doubles the open-loop reduction while keeping PPL at 8.6±0.2 vs. No-Steer's 8.4±0.2 and open-loop's 9.8±0.7. The κₚ×κᵢ gain sweep (Figure 2) shows the result is robust across the grid with every cell recalibrated to the same budget.
- **Matched spending verified (Table 2).** Realized test-time intervention rates stay within 0.04 of nominal p for every method and budget (e.g., target 0.5 → 0.48–0.53 across all gates), and CFP/CLABS's realized ranges lie within STS's at every budget — they do not outperform by overspending.
- **Negligible overhead.** CFP costs one inner product per token (~5–8 kFLOPs vs. 5–16 GFLOPs for a forward pass, i.e., ≈10⁻⁶ relative cost); CLABS adds a few scalar ops and one Bernoulli draw; neither needs rollouts, backtracking, multi-sample decoding, or an extra forward pass.

## Authors & Credibility

The work is by Nima Eshraghi, Lovedeep Gondara, Sagarika Suresh, and Jithin Pradeep (The Vanguard Group, Inc.) with Xiaotong (Tone) Xu and Fanny Chevalier (University of Toronto) — an industry (financial-services) research group plus an academic visualization/ML lab. The authors are candid about provenance: STS (Bernoulli sparse steering), the allocation-agnostic baseline, is their own prior work (Eshraghi et al. 2026), and this paper is a natural follow-up asking *where* the budget should go. The experimental hygiene is unusually strong: matched realized budgets verified empirically, a shared direction/strength/layer/corrections protocol, three seeds with reported standard deviations, two independent LLM judges with position randomization, a component ablation, a gain sweep, exact prompts disclosed (Appendix D), full hyperparameters (Appendix B), and an explicit limitations appendix. Claims are scoped conservatively (the stability certificate is for the linearized interior regime, not the full nonlinear decoder; conclusions restricted to the 2B/8B base-model regime). No code release is mentioned, though the SAEs and classifiers are public HuggingFace artifacts.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel framing. Per-token gating itself exists (STS is the authors' own coin-flip gate; Breaking Bad Tokens thresholds SAE feature activations; SIA steers high-entropy junctions), but casting allocation-under-a-fixed-budget as *the* single decision variable — so that all comparisons become controlled ablations — plus a PI-feedback gate on accumulated alignment with a Jury-test stability certificate is a real conceptual and theoretical contribution; no prior sparse steering gate comes with a stability analysis.
- **Rigor:** High. Held-out budget calibration under active steering, realized-rate verification (within 0.04), shared corrections, three seeds, dual judges with randomized positions, ablation isolating exactly the claimed mechanism (the integral term), and a gain-robustness sweep. The theory honestly labels itself as a linearization around the interior operating point.
- **Weaknesses in the evaluation:**
  - **Classifier circularity.** The emotion direction corpora are sampled as texts whose DistilRoBERTa probability for the target Ekman category exceeds 0.9 — and behavioral shift is then scored by *the same classifier family* (j-hartmann DistilRoBERTa). Likewise, RealToxicityPrompts' built-in scores define the D⁺/D⁻ endpoints (toxicity ≥0.9 vs. ≤0.2) and evaluation uses a RoBERTa toxicity classifier. The measured "behavioral shift" is thus partially movement along the evaluation scorer's own axis; no human or independent-offfamily scorer is used.
  - **Strawman-adjacent baselines.** SIA-PE/SIA-ER are deliberately extracted single-pass signals from Hu et al.'s system rather than the published method (which uses a learned value model + Best-of-N search); the paper is transparent about why (no budget), but readers should note the full SIA and FASB (with backtracking) systems were never run. SRE is the authors' own new baseline, not an established one.
  - **Figure-only headline numbers.** The central efficacy result (behavioral shift vs. budget, the 2–3× claim) exists only as Figure 1 curves with shaded σ bands — no numeric table is given for the main efficacy comparison, so deltas must be read off plots; the quantitative anchor "89–92% of Full-steer shift" is a prose statement.
  - **Scale and target breadth.** Only two open base models (2B, 8B), no instruction-tuned chat models, one layer, one direction per target, English only, three targets chosen precisely because they are cleanly classifier-scorable — the authors acknowledge all of this, but it does bound significance.
  - **Judge dependence.** The "only gates preferred at every budget" claim rests on LLM judges (gpt-5-mini, Sonnet 4.6) that "differ in spread but agree on ranking"; no human evaluation was performed.
- **Reproducibility:** Good on paper — models, public SAEs (Gemma Scope; EleutherAI sae-llama-3.1-8b-32x), classifier IDs, α values, gains, setpoint, calibration-by-bisection, seeds, and judge prompts are all disclosed; but no code repository is cited, and some choices (e.g., α picked as the largest fluency-preserving magnitude per task on the calibration split) leave room for unstated discretion.
- **Red flags:** None serious. The corporate affiliation adds a disclaimer footer rather than affecting the science; the main risk of overclaiming is the phrase "first stability certificate for a sparse steering gate," which is qualified to the linear model — accurately, in my reading.

## Strengths

- The budgeted-allocation framing is clean and falsifiable: with direction, strength, layer, and corrections all shared, any behavioral difference between gates is attributable to *when* they intervene — a genuinely controlled experiment design rare in the steering literature.
- CLABS is a principled, nearly free mechanism (scalar PI control per token, ≈10⁻⁶ of forward-pass cost) that operationalizes an intuitive idea: spend scarce intervention budget where the generation is actually drifting, not on tokens already on target.
- The theory is connected to practice: the stability box tells you which (κₚ, κᵢ, β) are safe before deployment, and the persistent-vs-alternating-error analysis (Appendix A.5) explains *why* memory helps in exactly the regime where it empirically does (tight budgets).
- Honest, well-instrumented empirics: realized-budget auditing, an ablation whose outcome matches the mechanism claim, a gain sweep, dual judges, disclosed prompts and hyperparameters, and a limitations appendix that pre-empts the main objections.
- Qualitative examples (Tables 7–11) show the failure mode being fixed: Full-Steer lets the attribute hijack the response into anxious rumination, while the gated methods inject the attribute while staying on-task.

## Limitations

- Evaluation covers only two open base models (Gemma 2 2B, Llama 3.1 8B); no instruction-tuned or frontier chat models, and the authors explicitly restrict conclusions to this regime.
- Both gates depend on a pretrained residual-stream SAE of adequate quality at the intervention layer; sensitivity to SAE quality was not studied.
- Single mid-network layer and a single steering direction per target; multi-layer, multi-direction, and composed-behavior budget allocation are left open.
- Only three English targets with reliably classifier-scorable endpoints (toxicity, fear, sadness); behaviors without automatic scorers and non-English settings are unaddressed.
- The stability guarantee is for the first-order linearization in the interior regime (where the clip is inactive) — explicitly not a worst-case guarantee over nonlinear decoding.
- Efficacy and utility rest on automatic classifiers and two LLM judges; no human evaluation. The leaky integral provably reduces but cannot eliminate the steady-state tracking offset.
- Activation steering is dual-use; the paper makes suppression sparser and more controllable, but the same machinery applies to eliciting behaviors.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"兴趣方向：论文以 SAE 特征构建可解释干预方向与对齐探针，研究残差流内部表示的探测（probing）与表示空间干预，属于机制可解释性在推理时行为控制上的应用。其"预算化 token 分配 + 反馈控制"视角对内部状态监控与干预时机的定量分析也有方法学参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does trajectory-aware allocation transfer to instruction-tuned chat models and larger scales (e.g., 70B+), where alignment-tuned representations may respond differently to mid-layer injection?
- Can the measured circularity be broken — e.g., score behavioral shift with classifiers from a different family (or human raters) than those used to build D⁺/D⁻, and does the 2–3× margin at tight budgets survive?
- The gain box in Theorem 1 requires estimates of φ (persistence) and g (steering gain); how are these estimated in practice, and can the gate adapt them online from the observed response?
- How does a budgeted gate compose with a magnitude controller (PID steering à la Nguyen et al. 2026)? Does PI feedback over allocation plus PI feedback over magnitude interact stably, and does the linear stability analysis extend to that composed loop?
- How does allocation behave under multi-direction or composed-behavior steering — is there a principled way to split one budget across several directions, and does the Bradley-Terry-like ranking of gates persist when the probe must serve multiple behaviors?
- The setpoint s⋆=0.75 and gains (1.0, 0.2, 0.2) were fixed per task; is there a data-driven way to choose the setpoint from a desired behavioral target rather than standardized-probe units?
- Since realized behavioral shift is measured per continuation, what happens on long-form generation (thousands of tokens), where the leaky integral's ~5-token memory may be too short to track slow semantic drift?
