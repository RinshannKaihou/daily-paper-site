> **一句话总结：** 该方法用 AnchorNet 自适应选取地标点构造 Nyström 低秩核近似并加 FITC 对角修正，把 GP 超参数训练的目标函数与解析梯度全部化为只含 r×r 小矩阵、低秩因子和对角量的运算（单精度 fp32 实现，每次评估 O(nr(r+d))），在九个 UCI 大规模回归数据集（最大 184 万样本）上全部达到 Maddox 等人低精度 GP 基准的参考 RMSE，拟合时间原始加速 3.4×–1120×（如 Buzz 上 25,436 s → 22.7 s）；论文还系统刻画了 fp16/TF32 混合精度仅在条件数适中时可行且收益仅约 1.1×，病态问题（κ₂(M)≈10⁶–10⁷）下 Cholesky 分解大量失败。

> **TL;DR:** Exact GP training costs O(n³) via Cholesky on the dense kernel matrix; this paper ("ANGP") selects landmarks with AnchorNet (a kernel-free, O(mdn) geometry-adaptive scheme), builds a Nyström low-rank approximation plus an FITC diagonal correction, and derives the marginal-likelihood objective and analytic ARD gradients using only r×r dense factorizations — all in fp32 on an A40 GPU. On the nine UCI regression datasets of Maddox et al. (n = 13.5K to 1.84M), it matches the reference RMSE on all nine at some tested rank, with raw fit-time speedups from 3.4× (3DRoad, 373 s vs 1,260 s) to 1,120× (Buzz, 22.7 s vs 25,436 s); after dividing out the A40's ~2.4× fp32 throughput edge over the V100 baseline it is still faster everywhere, but only ~1.4× on 3DRoad. The required rank varies by two orders of magnitude (r = 50 on Buzz to r = 3000 on 3DRoad/Song) and is chosen retrospectively. Pushing selected GEMMs to fp16/TF32 yields only 1.04–1.10× and breaks down (7/8 and 9/11 factorization failures) on Buzz and HouseElectric where κ₂(M) ≈ 1.3e6–1.2e7; the fp32 full-kernel predictive CG solve itself stagnates on three datasets, forcing fp64 reference solves.

## 核心贡献

- **可扩展的低秩 GP 训练框架（ANGP）：** 用 AnchorNet 算法在数据几何上自适应地生成锚点并映射到训练点作为地标（landmark），构造 Nyström 近似 K ≈ K₂₁K₁₁⁻¹K₁₂；地标选取仅需 O(mdn) 且完全不接触核矩阵，在超参数优化全程保持固定。
- **FITC 修正下的完整解析梯度推导：** 低秩近似会低估训练点处的边缘方差，作者用 FITC 对角修正 D = Λ + σ²I 恢复边缘方差，并通过 Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury 与矩阵行列式引理把负对数边际似然及其对 ARD 长度尺度、信号尺度、噪声尺度的梯度全部化为低秩因子、对角量和 r×r 小矩阵运算——全程不形成任何 n×n 矩阵或其导数；单次目标+梯度评估代价 O(nr(r+d))。
- **全流程单精度（fp32）实现的系统评估：** 在九个 UCI 数据集上用统一的核、初始化、Adam（100 迭代、余弦退火）设置，只改变秩 r，全部达到 Maddox 等人的参考 RMSE，拟合时间原始加速 3.4×–1120×。
- **预测阶段与数值行为的深入分析：** 对比低秩预测均值与全核预测均值（稠密分解 / KeOps + 预条件 CG），揭示 fp32 全核求解在 3DRoad、Buzz、HouseElectric 上因小噪声方差与预条件子消减（cancellation, 放大因子约 16–400×σ⁻²）而精度不足，需 fp64 参考求解。
- **低于 fp32 的混合精度边界刻画：** 仅 4 个大 GEMM 可降精度；fp16 输入 + fp32 累积在 3DRoad（κ₂≈2.5×10⁴）可行但整体提速仅 1.04–1.10×；在 Buzz/HouseElectric（κ₂≈10⁶–10⁷）上大量 Cholesky 失败，相对梯度误差可达 5–650；TF32 行为定性相同——说明瓶颈是尾数精度而非动态范围。

## Key Contributions

- **A scalable low-rank GP training framework ("ANGP"):** landmarks are chosen by AnchorNet — low-discrepancy points (Halton / adaptive tensor grids) laid over a volume-weighted partition of the data's bounding boxes, then snapped to nearest training points — yielding a Nyström approximation K ≈ K₂₁K₁₁⁻¹K₁₂ at O(mdn) cost with no kernel evaluations, held fixed throughout optimization.
- **Analytic gradients of the FITC-corrected objective:** because the Nyström approximation underestimates marginal variances, an FITC diagonal correction D = Λ + σ²I (Λᵢᵢ = k(xᵢ,xᵢ) − [K̂]ᵢᵢ) is applied; the objective and its gradients w.r.t. all ARD hyperparameters are derived via SMW/matrix-determinant-lemma identities so that every dense factorization is r×r (through M = Iᵣ + WD⁻¹Wᵀ ⪰ Iᵣ), with the length-scale derivative assembled from n×3r / 3r×3r low-rank factors instead of n×n derivative matrices.
- **Complete single-precision (fp32) implementation and evaluation:** identical kernel, initialization, and Adam budget (100 iterations, cosine decay, softplus positivity with a 10⁻⁴ noise floor, gradient-norm clip of 10) across nine UCI datasets; only the rank r varies.
- **Numerical analysis of the predictive solve:** the fp32 full-kernel CG solve (preconditioned by ≤200 landmarks, KeOps mat-vecs) converges on Song, stagnates at ~10⁻² relative residual on 3DRoad, is split-dependent on Buzz, and fails on HouseElectric — traced to condition numbers growing like 1/σ² at small fitted noise (σ² ≈ 2.5×10⁻³ on HouseElectric) and to cancellation amplified by σ⁻² (≈400 on HouseElectric) inside the low-rank preconditioner.
- **A careful mixed-precision feasibility study:** profiling shows ~2/3 of fit time is GEMMs/TRSMs, but only 4 large GEMMs are reducible (triangular solves stay fp32); fp16-input/fp32-accumulate variants give only 1.04–1.10× end-to-end and are unreliable when u_fp16·κ₂(M) ≳ 1 — including a Weyl-inequality-based breakdown heuristic, a power-of-two scaling fix for fp16 overflow, an fp32-fallback on Cholesky failure, and an eigenvalue-clamp repair (raise eigenvalues of the computed M below 1 up to 1).

## Methodology

The paper targets hyperparameter training of a GP with an ARD squared-exponential kernel, where the exact negative log-marginal likelihood (3.1) requires an O(n³) Cholesky factorization of K̃ = K + σ²I. The proposed pipeline (Algorithm 5.1):

1. **AnchorNet landmark selection (Section 4):** compute the bounding box of the training inputs; scatter s = O(m) low-discrepancy points (Halton or adaptive tensor grids); assign each training point to its nearest (ℓ∞, fixed tie-breaking) point; within each nonempty group allocate mᵢ ∝ group bounding-box volume further low-discrepancy anchors; snap every anchor to the nearest training point. The resulting distinct landmark set S (r = #S ≤ m + Q) defines K₁₁ = k(S,S), K₂₁ = k(X,S). Cost is O(mdn), independent of kernel evaluations.
2. **Stabilized low-rank + FITC model (Section 3):** K₁₁ is jittered as K₁₁ + εσf²I (ε starts at 10⁻⁴, escalated ×10 up to 7 attempts) and Cholesky-factored; the Nyström approximation K̂ = K₂₁K₁₁⁻¹K₁₂ is augmented with the FITC diagonal D = Λ + σ²I so that K_FITC = K̂ + D is positive definite (Dᵢᵢ ≥ σ² > 0).
3. **Objective and gradients via r×r algebra:** with K_small = Iᵣ + K₁₁⁻¹K₂₁K₁₂D⁻¹ and the symmetrized M = Iᵣ + WD⁻¹Wᵀ (W = L⁻¹K₁₂), the inverse action and log-determinant of K_FITC follow from Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury and the matrix determinant lemma. Gradients w.r.t. each ARD length scale decompose the derivative of K̂ into n×3r · 3r×3r · 3r×n factors, so quadratic forms and traces never touch an n×n matrix; the σf and σ gradients exploit that σf² scales all blocks and that only D depends on σ. Hyperparameters are kept positive via softplus (noise floored at 10⁻⁴) and optimized with Adam (100 iterations, cosine-decayed learning rate, gradient norm clipped at 10), with no validation split or early stopping.
4. **Prediction:** either the low-rank predictive mean (reduced solves against landmarks only, ≤ 4.1 s in the experiments) or the full-kernel mean — dense factorization for n < 60,000, otherwise preconditioned CG with KeOps mat-vecs and a rank-min(r,200) preconditioner; fp64 reference solves establish ground truth where fp32 fails.
5. **Precision study (Section 7):** the four large GEMMs per objective/gradient evaluation are candidates for fp16 inputs with fp32 accumulation (two-GEMM and four-GEMM variants, power-of-two input scaling, fp32 fallback or eigenvalue repair on factorization failure); TF32 is also tested.

Experiments use the nine UCI datasets from Maddox et al. via the bayesian-benchmarks interface (standardized features/targets), five splits per configuration, all timings on an NVIDIA A40 (48 GB) versus the V100 (32 GB) timings reported by the reference.

## Results & Highlights

- **Reference accuracy reached on all 9 UCI datasets, with raw fit speedups of 3.4× to 1,120× over the Maddox et al. low-precision GP baseline:** e.g., on **Buzz** (n = 524.9K) test RMSE 0.2672 ± 0.0056 vs the reference 0.300 ± 0.010 with fit time 22.7 s vs 25,436 s (1,120.3×); on **HouseElectric** (n = 1.84M) RMSE 0.0518 ± 0.0003 vs 0.052 ± 0.002 with 431 s vs 42,751 s (99.1×); on **Song** (n = 463.8K) RMSE 0.7605 ± 0.0031 vs 0.761 ± 0.004 with 1,404 s vs 24,357 s (17.4×); on **3DRoad** RMSE 0.1915 ± 0.0040 vs 0.194 ± 0.010 with 373 s vs 1,260 s (3.4×, only ~1.4× after hardware normalization by the A40's ~2.4× fp32 throughput advantage).
- **The required approximation rank varies by ~two orders of magnitude and is not explained by n or d:** the reference RMSE is reached at r = 100 (PoleTele, Bike), r = 1600 (Kin40K, Protein), r = 3000 (3DRoad, Song), but only r = 50–800 on Buzz/HouseElectric; the paper attributes this to the decay of the fitted kernel spectrum and dataset geometry. At r = 6000 on 3DRoad, ANGP is already slower than the baseline (Table 4: 0.9× speedup, 1,419.6 s vs 1,260 s).
- **The low-rank predictive mean trades accuracy for cost:** across reported configurations it has a median relative RMSE increase of ~27% vs the full-kernel predictive mean (within ~4% on HouseElectric at r = 800, but ≥24% worse on Buzz); it needs at most 4.1 s versus 33 s–25 min for fp32 CG and up to ~24 h for the fp64 full-kernel solves on the three largest datasets.
- **The fp32 full-kernel CG solve is the accuracy bottleneck on ill-conditioned problems:** it converges on Song, stagnates at ~10⁻² relative residual on 3DRoad, varies across splits on Buzz (RMSE up to 0.3563 ± 0.0582 vs the fp64 0.2622 at r = 800), and fails outright on HouseElectric (RMSE 0.90–7.07 vs fp64 0.0467–0.0551); the low-rank mean on HouseElectric reaches RMSE 0.0536 at r = 1000 — within one standard deviation of the reference 0.052 ± 0.002 — with a 0.9 s prediction after a 544 s fit (78.5× faster than the reference).
- **Mixed precision below fp32 is a poor trade:** on 3DRoad (r = 3000, single fit) the two-GEMM fp16 variant gives test RMSE 0.3261 vs 0.3258 fp32 but only 1.04× speedup (449 s vs 465 s; 449→424 s = 1.10× for four GEMMs, RMSE 0.3277), with relative gradient errors of 1.65%/1.35%; on Buzz and HouseElectric, where κ₂(M) ≈ 1.3×10⁶–1.2×10⁷, the reduced-precision factorization fails for 7/8 and 9/11 tested ranks respectively, and the eigenvalue-repair fallback leaves relative gradient errors of 5–650.
- **A data quirk in the baseline was identified and corrected:** the Protein RMSE of 0.055 in Maddox et al. Table 1 is inconsistent with the same table's half-precision result (0.635) and their appendix (0.481–0.509), so it is treated as a misplaced decimal and 0.55 is used as the reference — which ANGP matches (0.5406 ± 0.0068, 20.5 s vs 71 s, 3.5×).

## Critical Assessment

- **Hardware confound in headline speedups (disclosed but still headline):** the 3.4×–1,120× speedups compare A40 timings against V100 timings copied from Maddox et al.; the authors honestly note the A40's ~2.4× nominal fp32 throughput edge and that hardware-normalized gains remain positive everywhere but shrink to ~1.4× on 3DRoad — yet the abstract-level message "substantially reduced training time" still leans on cross-hardware numbers, and on 3DRoad at r = 6000 the method is actually slower than the baseline.
- **Retrospective rank selection inflates the cost–accuracy story:** "the cheapest tested rank that reaches the reference RMSE" is chosen post hoc across the tested ranks (the authors admit this "should be interpreted as a retrospective cost–accuracy comparison rather than as a rank-selection procedure"), and no practical, validation-free rule for choosing r a priori is given — the rank varies from 50 to 3000 across the nine datasets with no dependence on n or d that a practitioner could exploit.
- **The baseline comparison is narrow:** only Maddox et al.'s low-precision GP numbers are compared; there is no comparison against standard scalable-GP baselines (e.g., GPyTorch SVGP, SKI/structured kernel interpolation, or recent inducing-point methods) on the same nine datasets, and the reference's Protein RMSE (0.055) had to be reinterpreted as a typo (0.55) for the "matches reference" claim to hold.
- **Fitting-time comparison excludes the predictive solve for both methods, but the solve matters:** for the full-kernel pipeline the fp64 solve can take up to ~24 h (HouseElectric) — dominating everything — while the cheap low-rank mean that avoids it carries a median ~27% RMSE penalty (≥24% even at the largest tested ranks on Buzz), so the end-to-end advantage depends heavily on which predictor one accepts.
- **The "single precision" claim needs an asterisk:** the fp32 full-kernel CG solve fails or stagnates on 3 of 9 datasets (small fitted noise σ² ≈ 2.5×10⁻³ and cancellation amplified ~400× by σ⁻² in the preconditioner), so reference-quality predictions there require fp64 solves — a genuine and honestly analyzed limitation, but one that undercuts the pure-fp32 narrative.
- **Missing ablations:** no systematic study of the FITC correction vs plain Nyström (Λ = 0) on accuracy, of the ε-stabilization choice, or of AnchorNet landmarks vs random/k-meains inducing points at matched r; the Adam budget is fixed at 100 iterations for all datasets with no sensitivity check.

## Strengths

- Clean, fully derived analytic gradients: the entire training objective and its ARD derivatives are reduced to r×r dense factorizations, low-rank factors, and diagonal operations — no n×n matrix or derivative tensor is ever formed, with a clean per-evaluation complexity of O(nr(r+d)).
- Kernel-free, geometry-adaptive landmark selection (AnchorNet) that is fixed before optimization and costs only O(mdn), avoiding the chicken-and-egg coupling between hyperparameters and inducing points.
- Exceptional experimental honesty: all tested ranks tabulated, five splits with standard deviations, explicit hardware normalization, an identified typo in the reference's Protein number, and disclosure that fp32 solves fail where they fail.
- The mixed-precision section is a genuinely useful negative/delimiting result: it quantifies exactly why fp16/TF32 GEMMs give only ~1.1× (only 4 GEMMs are reducible; TRSMs and kernel evaluation stay fp32) and when they break (κ₂(M) ≳ 10⁶), including trajectory-dependent conditioning along Adam iterations (M̃ becoming indefinite between iterations 18–40 on 3DRoad).
- Released code and reproducible protocol (same kernel, initialization, optimizer, budget across all datasets; only r varies).

## Limitations

- The rank r — the method's single crucial hyperparameter — has no a-priori selection criterion; it varies over two orders of magnitude across datasets and must currently be tuned by trial (reference accuracy on 3DRoad needs r = 3000, where the speedup over the baseline nearly vanishes).
- Speedups are cross-hardware (A40 vs V100) and exclude the predictive solve; with the solve included, pipelines needing full-kernel accuracy on ill-conditioned datasets pay fp32-CG failures or hours of fp64 time.
- The low-rank predictive mean is not uniformly accurate: median +27% RMSE vs the full-kernel mean, ≥24% worse on Buzz at all tested ranks.
- Below-fp32 precision is impractical here: ≤1.10× gain in the best case, unreliable for κ₂(M) ≳ 10⁶, and the eigenvalue-repair trick only helps when indefiniteness (not gradient corruption) is the failure mode.
- No comparison against modern scalable-GP software baselines (SVGP, SKI) and no FITC-vs-plain-Nyström ablation; only one kernel family (ARD squared-exponential) and one fixed 100-iteration Adam budget are studied.

## Relevance

作者将大规模 GP 训练中的核矩阵瓶颈通过“几何自适应低秩近似 + FITC 修正 + 全解析梯度”彻底消除，并系统刻画了 fp32/fp16/TF32 在病态核矩阵下的数值边界——这与可扩展高斯过程、低秩近似与混合精度计算的兴趣方向高度契合，是“数值线性代数为机器学习服务”的一个扎实范例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a cheap, a-posteriori indicator (e.g., numerical/effective rank of K₁₁, spectrum decay estimate, or a validation-free FITC objective gap) predict the required rank r before training, replacing the current retrospective search over r = 50…8000?
- How does ANGP compare at matched ranks against random and k-means inducing points, and against structured kernel interpolation (SKI) or GPyTorch SVGP on the same nine UCI datasets under the same budget?
- Would replacing the Cholesky-based factorization of M with an iterative or LDLᵀ-based solve (as the paper's own observation about Maddox et al. suggests) make fp16 training robust on Buzz/HouseElectric, where κ₂(M) ≈ 10⁶–10⁷?
- Can the ill-conditioned fp32 predictive solve be rescued in pure fp32 — e.g., via iterative refinement, mixed-precision preconditioning, or a factorization-based application of the low-rank preconditioner that avoids the σ⁻²-amplified cancellation?
- Does the FITC correction meaningfully change hyperparameter estimates or final RMSE relative to plain Nyström (Λ = 0), and how sensitive are the results to the ε-stabilization schedule and to the 100-iteration Adam budget?
