> **一句话总结：** 通过两个预注册（分析协议先冻结）的对照实验证明：在多轮任务中保留 LLM 自己的历史回复会把最终成功率拉低 0.21（GPT-4o-mini）到 0.29（Mistral-7B），在作者新建的 100 轮状态追踪基准 CDR-Stream 上，完整历史让准确率在约 20 轮内从约 0.6 崩塌到约 0.1，而求解器验证的外部状态摘要能维持 0.52–0.59 的优势；更关键的是，一致性、连贯性、稳定性等过程质量指标以及 CUSUM/EWMA/ADWIN 等校准漂移检测器全都无法提前预警这次崩塌——稳定性甚至随准确率下降而上升。

> **TL;DR:** Two pre-registered paired studies show that keeping an LLM's own replies in context is a silent reliability hazard. On 48 sharded GSM8K-derived tasks (the Lost in Conversation math subset), retaining history cut final-turn success from 0.896 to 0.688 for GPT-4o-mini (delta 0.208, Holm p=0.026) and 0.354 to 0.063 for Mistral-7B (delta 0.292, Holm p=0.004). On the new 100-turn CDR-Stream state-tracking benchmark, full history collapsed accuracy from ~0.6 to ~0.1 within 20 turns at matched difficulty for all three models, while a solver-verified state summary kept it 0.52–0.59 higher (Holm p<0.001) — and no reasoning-quality metric (consistency, coherence, stability) or calibrated drift detector (CUSUM, Page–Hinkley, EWMA, DDM, ADWIN) provided robust early warning; stability actually rose as accuracy fell.

## 核心贡献

- **研究 1（配对短期实验）**：在 48 个分片 GSM8K 数学任务上，用"对话历史 vs 新鲜前缀（只含用户信息）"的配对设计，前瞻冻结地量化了保留模型自身回复的净代价：GPT-4o-mini 最终成功率下降 0.208（Holm 校正 p=0.026），Mistral-7B 下降 0.292（p=0.004），Llama-4 Scout 同方向但不显著（0.104，p=0.125）。
- **研究 2（CDR-Stream 基准）**：构建了 100 轮、每轮有精确金标答案、按模型校准难度（12 级难度阶梯）的状态追踪对话基准，涵盖预算账本、仓储库存、实验规程、项目里程碑四个领域和五种难度调度场景；证明在难度固定的前提下，完整对话历史使三个模型（GPT-4o-mini、Llama-3 70B、Gemma 3 12B）的准确率在约 20 轮内从约 0.6 崩塌到约 0.1。
- **缓解方案对照**：一个由精确求解器渲染的"验证状态摘要"上下文（只含当前正确状态 + 本轮用户输入）能将准确率保持在完整历史之上 0.52–0.59（Llama-3 70B 差 0.545、Gemma 3 12B 差 0.588，Holm p<0.001），并把第 100 轮的提示从数万 token 缩到几百 token。
- **监测失效的系统证据**：六个 RQEval 质量维度、一个配对"就绪度"度量（CRL）、以及五个校准的单流漂移检测器（CUSUM、Page–Hinkley、EWMA、DDM、ADWIN）都无法给出稳健、跨模型的早期预警；一致性/连贯性下降远慢于准确率，稳定性反而上升（模型"锚定"在错误状态上，回复更相似但更错）。
- **功效边界的理论刻画**：给出闭式可检测下界（Proposition 1）与模拟，证明单条 100 轮流只能检测约 0.5 的巨大跌幅（实验中的实际漂移仅 0.1–0.3），而聚合 6–18 条对话可把可检测降幅压到 0.2–0.1——为"跨会话监测"给出定量依据。

## Key Contributions

- **Study 1 (paired, short-horizon):** A prospectively frozen paired estimate of the net cost of retaining assistant replies on 48 sharded GSM8K-derived math tasks (Lost in Conversation subset). Final-turn success fell by 0.208 for GPT-4o-mini (0.896 → 0.688, Holm-adjusted McNemar p=0.026) and 0.292 for Mistral-7B (0.354 → 0.063, Holm p=0.004); Llama-4 Scout showed the same direction without significance (0.104, p=0.125).
- **Study 2 (CDR-Stream benchmark):** A purpose-built long-horizon benchmark of 100-turn state-tracking dialogues across four domains (budget ledger, warehouse inventory, laboratory protocol, project milestones), with an exact solver-computed gold answer per turn, a 12-level difficulty ladder calibrated per model, and five difficulty schedules. At matched difficulty, dialogue-history accuracy collapsed from ~0.6 (turns 1–5) to 0.08–0.11 (turns 21–100) for all three models, even in stationary-easy dialogues where questions never got harder.
- **A mitigation upper-reference:** Replacing history with a solver-verified state summary (exact state + current user turn) preserved accuracy, with per-dialogue gaps of 0.545 (Llama-3 70B) and 0.588 (Gemma 3 12B) over turns 1–100 (Holm p<0.001), and 0.521 for GPT-4o-mini; it also shrinks the turn-100 prompt from tens of thousands of tokens to a few hundred.
- **A negative result on monitoring:** Six RQEval reasoning-quality dimensions, a paired Cumulative Resolution Lag (CRL) readiness measure, and five calibrated drift detectors were tested as early warnings; none gave robust, model-general warning at a controlled ≤5% false-alarm rate. Stability (BERTScore similarity of repeated samples) rose from 0.94 to 0.98 while accuracy fell from 0.61 to 0.08 — accumulated context makes responses more anchored, not more erratic.
- **A power analysis explaining why:** A closed-form bound (Proposition 1: detectable drop δ ≥ (z_{1-α}σ0 + z_β σ1)/√(mW)) and simulations show a single 100-turn stream can only detect drops of ~0.5 at 80% power, while the actual task drifts were 0.1–0.3; pooling 6–18 dialogues lowers the detectable drop to 0.2–0.1, grounding the recommendation to monitor across sessions rather than within one conversation.

## Methodology

The paper runs two controlled experiments, each with an analysis protocol frozen before outcomes were computed (evidence labelled confirmatory / prespecified secondary / exploratory / post hoc), and a shared monitoring framework.

**Context policies.** At turn *t*, the model sees one of three contexts: (1) *dialogue history* — the full transcript including its own earlier replies; (2) *fresh prefix* — all user turns only; (3) *verified state summary* — the exact solver-rendered task state after turn *t−1* plus the current user turn. Policies (1) vs (2) isolate the net effect of the model's own replies (Study 1); (3) tests a mitigation, not a mechanism, since it changes context length, reply presence, and state correctness simultaneously.

**Study 1.** From the 103-problem math subset of Lost in Conversation (GSM8K-derived, Laban et al. 2026), an input audit excluded 17 tasks and a hash-based draw (no model output involved) selected 48 tasks with 4–9 shards (median 6). GPT-4o-mini, Llama-4 Scout, and Mistral-7B answered every turn in both arms, with K=5 same-context samples and L=2 frozen rewordings per cell (temperature 0.7); replies had to end with FINAL_ANSWER or STATUS: NEED_MORE_INFORMATION. All 11,382 calls succeeded. The primary endpoint — paired final-turn success — was tested with exact McNemar tests, task-bootstrap CIs, and Holm correction. Pre-final candidate warnings (CS consistency, LS coherence, SS stability — the three label-free RQEval dimensions) were tested via task-clustered partial correlations adjusted for model, turn, revealed information, and log context length.

**Study 2 (CDR-Stream).** 100-turn dialogues in four state-tracking domains, each turn updating a shared state (deliveries, transfers, corrections, plus distractor sentences) and asking one question (sum, difference, count-above-threshold, max, what-if, share) with an exact solver gold answer. A 12-level difficulty ladder (query terms, events per turn, distractors, event complexity) was calibrated per model/domain via single-turn pilot runs targeting accuracy bands of 0.75–0.92 / 0.60–0.78 / 0.35–0.65, with a frozen rule excluding ceiling/floor domains. Five scenarios: two stationary nulls (S0-E easy, S0-H hard) and three scheduled changes (S1 staircase at turns 34/68; S2 sudden easy→hard at 51; S3 easy→hard→easy at 34/67). GPT-4o-mini, Llama-3 70B, and Gemma 3 12B each received 30 held-out dialogues (6 per scenario) in two arms: dialogue history vs verified state summary; frozen prompt, temperature 0.7, 2,048-token ceiling. The confirmatory hypothesis H-C1 (state summary beats dialogue history over turns 1–100 for Llama-3 70B and Gemma 3 12B) used one-sided sign-flip tests over 30 dialogues with Holm correction; GPT-4o-mini control data had been seen earlier and sits outside the family.

**Monitoring framework.** Each primary reply is scored into six RQEval dimensions in [0,1]: accuracy (CQ), consistency (CS, agreement among K samples), robustness (RS, agreement of reworded replies), logical coherence (LS, NLI-scored absence of contradictions between consecutive reasoning steps), efficiency (ES), and stability (SS, BERTScore similarity of the K traces). Only CS/LS/SS are label-free. Metric streams are smoothed by a window mean (W ∈ {10, 20, 30}, W=20 primary) and passed to five standard drift detectors (CUSUM, Page–Hinkley, EWMA, DDM, ADWIN) with thresholds calibrated on stationary nulls so the first-alarm rate is ≤ α = 0.05; an alarm within 33 turns of a scheduled change counts as a detection, and a true "early warning" must additionally alarm before the accuracy alarm in the same dialogue, keep its null calibration, and replicate across models. Proposition 1 derives the minimum detectable Bernoulli drop for a window-mean threshold, and simulations calibrate the actual detectors' power as a function of the number of pooled dialogues.

## Results & Highlights

- **Study 1 primary endpoint (48 paired sharded GSM8K tasks per model):** retaining the model's replies lowered final-turn success from 0.896 to 0.688 for GPT-4o-mini (difference 0.208, 95% CI [0.063, 0.354], Holm-adjusted p=0.026) and from 0.354 to 0.063 for Mistral-7B (difference 0.292, 95% CI [0.146, 0.438], Holm p=0.004); Llama-4 Scout fell 0.104 (CI [0.000, 0.208], p=0.125, not significant). Across models, only the fresh-prefix arm succeeded in 34 model–task pairs vs 5 for the history arm.
- **Study 2 collapse at matched difficulty (CDR-Stream, 30 dialogues per model):** dialogue-history accuracy fell from 0.61/0.60/0.61 (turns 1–5) to 0.09/0.11/0.08 (turns 21–100) for GPT-4o-mini / Llama-3 70B / Gemma 3 12B. In stationary-easy dialogues (S0-E) over turns 21–100, dialogue history scored 0.10–0.14 while the verified state summary scored 0.82–0.86. At the same ladder level, late turns (51–100) were less accurate than early turns (1–50) by −0.118 (prespecified secondary, p<0.001, n=90 dialogues; −0.185 in S0-E).
- **Confirmatory mitigation (H-C1):** per-dialogue accuracy gap (verified state summary minus dialogue history) over turns 1–100 was 0.545 for Llama-3 70B (95% CI [0.513, 0.576]) and 0.588 for Gemma 3 12B (95% CI [0.557, 0.619]), both Holm p<0.001; GPT-4o-mini showed 0.521 [0.476, 0.566]. Abstract-level summary: the state summary kept accuracy 0.52–0.59 above full history.
- **Quality metrics move the wrong way:** for GPT-4o-mini under dialogue history, accuracy fell from 0.61 (turns 1–5) to 0.08 (turns 81–100) while stability (SS) rose from 0.94 to 0.98 and coherence (LS) moved only 0.54 → 0.47; consistency (CS) fell 0.79 → 0.64 but slowly, long after most accuracy was lost. In a Study 1 replay, median Cliff's δ for stability at the fresh→history switch was +0.46 (GPT-4o-mini) and +0.42 (Llama-4 Scout) — i.e., responses became more uniform as they got worse.
- **Detectors fail three ways:** in the Study 1 replay the best detection rate of any calibrated configuration was 0.067; in Study 2's dialogue arm the stationary nulls collapsed themselves (no CQ threshold met the 5% false-alarm target) and other metrics detected at most 0.11 of drifts within 33 turns; on the verified-state arms, rules tuned on GPT-4o-mini failed frozen validation on the other models (CUSUM on CQ: 6/18 and 1/18 drift dialogues detected; LS threshold rule fired in 11/18 and 16/18 drifts but also falsely in 6/12 and 3/12 stationary dialogues).
- **Single streams are underpowered:** Proposition 1 gives a minimum detectable drop of ~0.24 for one W=20 dialogue (p0=0.8, α=0.05, β=0.8), rising to 0.38 if α is split over 67 looks; simulations show a single stream reaches 80% detection within 33 turns only for drops of ~0.5 (actual drifts: 0.1–0.3), while pooling 6 dialogues per turn lowers the detectable drop to ~0.2 and 18 dialogues to ~0.1. A CQ CUSUM on six-dialogue aggregates detected all three drift scenarios in both validation models, but with only 2 null aggregates per model the 5% false-alarm rate cannot be verified (95% upper bound 0.78 for n=2).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core finding that assistant history hurts is a replication (Laban et al. 2026; Huang et al. 2026), and the paper is admirably explicit that it does not claim priority. The genuinely new parts are (i) per-model difficulty calibration over a 100-turn horizon with exact per-turn gold answers, (ii) the direct negative result on label-free quality metrics as early warnings, and (iii) the power analysis (Proposition 1 + simulation) that converts "detection failed" into "single-stream detection of 0.1–0.3 drops is information-theoretically implausible at α=0.05". The negative monitoring result is the most valuable contribution and would have been easy to bury; it is instead the headline.
- **Rigor.** High by pre-registration standards: McNemar/sign-flip tests with Holm correction, task-clustered permutation tests, bootstrap CIs, thresholds calibrated on nulls disjoint from evaluation data, and honest evidence labelling. Weaknesses the author concedes: the Study 2 monitoring analyses beyond the frozen tests are exploratory or post hoc (including the headline Table 7 fixed-baseline CUSUM), and the repeat-based metrics CS/SS were complete only for GPT-4o-mini in Study 2, so the "no early warning from consistency/stability" claim leans on a single model there.
- **Reproducibility.** Strong: public code and frozen protocols, hashed inputs, regenerated figures, disclosed deviations and the ADWIN bug fix. The task generator, ladder, and prompts are all in the supplement. Remaining friction: all calls went through one academic gateway whose aliases matter for exact replication, and one investigator performed the task audit and calibration (an audit-bias risk the paper flags itself).
- **Significance.** The practical message — keep task state in a verified external store, monitor across sessions or via counterfactual re-queries, and do not trust a single conversation's coherence/stability dashboard — is directly actionable for agent and assistant system design. The Study 1 effect sizes (0.21–0.29) and the Study 2 gap (0.52–0.59) are large enough to matter for any multi-turn deployment.
- **Red flags / weaknesses to weigh.** (1) The verified state summary is an oracle: it changes context length, removes replies, AND injects a solver-computed correct state simultaneously — the paper admits this tests a mitigation, not a mechanism, and no user-only arm was run, so the share of the collapse attributable to the model's own replies (vs accumulating state load) is unestimated in Study 2. (2) Study 1's largest effect (Mistral-7B, 0.292) occurs on a model whose fresh-prefix baseline is already near floor (0.354), and the harm partly manifests as staying in "need more information" at the final turn — a behavioural failure mode entangled with the answer-format contract rather than pure reasoning loss. (3) Only 3 of 6 RQEval dimensions are label-free, so the deployable-monitoring space tested is narrower than the framing suggests; token entropy (ERGO's signal) and semantic entropy were not collected. (4) CDR-Stream's domains are synthetic, solver-generated arithmetic tracking; whether open-ended dialogue collapses the same way is untested. (5) Llama-4 Scout missing significance (p=0.125) in Study 1 and the reliance on GPT-4o-mini for the repeat-metric completeness in Study 2 leave the cross-model generality thinner than the abstract implies.

## Strengths

- Pre-registered, prospectively frozen analyses with per-result evidence labels — a methodological standard rarely met in LLM evaluation papers.
- CDR-Stream solves the confound that plagues long-horizon evaluation (harder questions later in the conversation look like context degradation) via per-model difficulty calibration and a same-ladder-level early-vs-late contrast.
- The negative monitoring result is established carefully: false-alarm calibration on stationary nulls, transfer of tuned rules across models, and a closed-form power bound plus simulation that explains the failure rather than just reporting it.
- A concrete, quantified mitigation upper-reference (verified state summary: +0.52–0.59 accuracy, prompt shrunk from tens of thousands of tokens to a few hundred) that also bounds what self-written summaries could achieve.
- Full transparency: public code and protocols, disclosed deviations, disclosed ADWIN implementation bug with a corrected re-run, and candid statements of what each study cannot show.

## Limitations

- Tasks are arithmetic and state-tracking with exact answers (GSM8K shards plus four synthetic ledger-style domains); open-ended dialogue, retrieval, and tool-use settings are untested, and the collapse dynamics may differ.
- The model sets differ between studies and none are frontier models; Gemma 4 31B had to be excluded because it exceeded the hard difficulty band, so extending the claim to stronger models requires a harder ladder, not just more calls.
- The verified state summary is solver-verified and not deployable as-is; model-written summaries are expected to do worse, and no intermediate (e.g., self-maintained state files) was tested.
- Study 2 lacks a user-only (fresh-prefix) arm, so it cannot decompose the collapse into history length, state-update count, and accumulated errors; that narrower attribution rests only on Study 1's ≤9-turn horizon.
- Repeat-based metrics (CS, SS) were complete only for GPT-4o-mini in Study 2; token probabilities (for ERGO-style entropy monitoring) were never collected; the pooling analysis rests on only two null aggregates per model.
- Monitoring conclusions beyond the frozen tests are exploratory or post hoc; one investigator performed the task audit and calibration.

## Relevance

直接命中"多轮/长程 LLM 系统可靠性"这一核心关切：论文定量证明对话历史是隐形可靠性风险，并给出可操作的工程结论——把任务状态放进外部验证存储而非裸转录、跨会话或用反事实重查询来监测，而不是信任单条对话的连贯性仪表盘。对任何构建多轮 agent、状态追踪助手或 LLM 监控/可观测性系统的工作都是必读的实验设计与证据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the Study 2 collapse is caused by the model's own earlier replies versus sheer state-update load or context length? A user-only (fresh-prefix) 100-turn arm, or a paraphrased-history arm, would decompose the mechanism.
- Would a model-maintained, periodically solver-verified state file (a deployable approximation of the oracle summary) recover most of the 0.52–0.59 gap, and at what verification cost?
- Do token-entropy (ERGO-style) or semantic-entropy signals — neither collected here — provide the early warning that consistency, coherence, and stability failed to give, especially under accumulated anchoring?
- Does the collapse replicate with frontier models on a harder difficulty ladder, and in open-ended domains (negotiation, codebase editing, multi-document tracking) without exact gold answers?
- Can counterfactual re-queries (comparing the deployed context against a history-free or state-grounded re-ask) be made cheap enough to serve as a production-grade drift monitor, given the paper's finding that pooled multi-session CUSUM detects 0.1–0.3 drops reliably?
