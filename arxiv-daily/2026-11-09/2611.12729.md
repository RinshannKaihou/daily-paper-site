> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 KiteNorm——一种由残差缩放（c=1/2L）、ReLU 方差正则与标量化双重归一化三部分组成的 Post-LN 训练方法；其理论依据是 LayerNorm Jacobian 的谱范数上界 ‖γ‖∞/(2σ(z))（方差越大梯度收缩越强），实证上则发现方差爆炸与 rank collapse 同步出现；在 SlimPajama-627B 上训练的最高 1.3B 参数 decoder-only 模型中，KiteNorm 在 48 层取得 19.08 的验证困惑度，比调优后的 GPT-2 Pre-LN 基线（20.24）低约 1.16，并在 GPT-2 Pre-LN 发散的学习率 5e-3 下保持稳定。

> **TL;DR:** Post-LN Transformers are hard to scale in depth, and prior fixes (DeepNet, KEEL) mainly improve initialisation and degrade at large learning rates. The authors first prove a spectral bound on the LayerNorm Jacobian (∥γ∥∞ / 2σ(z)) showing hidden-state variance growth directly contracts gradients, and empirically link early variance expansion to rank collapse. KiteNorm combines residual scaling 1/(2L), a ReLU variance regulariser, and scalarised double-normalisation. On SlimPajama-627B (Chinchilla 20 tok/param), it beats the tuned GPT-2 pre-norm baseline by ~1.16 validation ppl in a 48-layer model (19.080 vs 20.240) and by 0.57 ppl at 96 layers (16.189 vs 16.755); on FineWeb-22B with a Llama-3.2-style 1.3B model it reaches 14.929 vs 15.464 ppl while remaining stable at LR 5e-3 where the baseline diverges. Scaling laws favour KiteNorm across depth, width, batch size, and training budget, with ablations showing every component is needed.

## 核心贡献

- **理论：LayerNorm Jacobian 谱范数上界（Theorem 1）。** 对隐藏状态 z_l，Post-LN 中 LayerNorm 的 Jacobian 满足 ∥∂LN_l/∂z_l∥₂ ≤ ∥γ∥∞ / (2σ(z_l))。由于该 Jacobian 同时作用于 skip 分支与 residual 分支（式 4），隐藏状态方差增大会使梯度收缩，从而把"方差增长"确立为 Post-LN 梯度消失的一个具体来源。
- **实证：方差爆炸与 rank collapse 的耦合。** 用 token alignment（Noci et al., 2022 的 token 相关性度量）追踪 12 层 Post-LN 模型早期训练，发现方差膨胀与 rank collapse 同步发生；把残差分支缩放到 c = 1/(2L) 即可在模型发散前逆转 rank collapse，同时逆转方差膨胀。文中还形式化证明了 skip 分支缩放与 residual 分支缩放的等价性（前向只依赖比值 a/c）。
- **方法：KiteNorm = 残差缩放 + ReLU 方差正则 + 标量化双重归一化。** 子层形式为 X_{l+1} = LN_scalarised(X_l + (1/2L)·F_l(LN_scalarised(X_l)))，其中标量化 LayerNorm 用标量 γ′, β′ ∈ R 代替向量参数，为 skip 与 residual 分支提供可独立学习的输入尺度；正则项 mean_l ReLU(σ(z_l)² − 1)（λ=1）只在方差超过 1 时介入，可复用 LayerNorm 已算出的方差，开销极小。
- **规模化实证：** 在 SlimPajama-627B 上按 Chinchilla 比例训练 decoder-only 模型（12–96 层扫深、宽度/批大小/训练预算扫参、Pythia-1B 与 Llama-3.2 风格 1.3B 模型），KiteNorm 在所有轴上都优于 GPT-2 pre-norm、LayerNorm-Scaling、KEEL、peri-norm、mix-norm 与 vanilla post-norm 的调优结果，并在 GPT-2 pre-norm 发散的学习率下保持稳定。
- **消融：** 去掉内层 LayerNorm、去标量化、去方差正则均导致性能下降或后期训练发散，证明三个组件缺一不可；对 λ、残差缩放系数、RMSNorm 变体、warm-up 长度、Adam β 的敏感性分析进一步支撑结论稳健。

## Key Contributions

- **Theorem 1 (spectral norm bound):** the LayerNorm Jacobian in a post-norm sublayer satisfies ∥∂LN_l(z_l)/∂z_l∥₂ ≤ ∥γ∥∞ / (2σ(z_l)). Since this Jacobian multiplies both the skip (I) and residual (∂F/∂x) branches in the backward pass, large hidden-state variance contracts gradients — a concrete, variance-driven source of gradient vanishing in post-norm Transformers.
- **Variance–rank-collapse link:** using the token-alignment metric (average pairwise token correlation), the authors show that early-training variance expansion in vanilla post-norm coincides with rank collapse, and that residual scaling as small as 1/(2L) reverses both before divergence. They also prove skip-branch and residual-branch scaling are equivalent for the forward loss (only the ratio a/c matters), connecting to Joseph et al. (2024).
- **KiteNorm:** scalarised double-normalisation X_{l+1} = LN_scalarised(X_l + (1/2L)·F(LN_scalarised(X_l))) combined with the ReLU variance regulariser R = (1/2L) Σ_l E ReLU(σ(z_l)² − 1) added to the cross-entropy loss with λ = 1. The regulariser is inactive while variance stays below 1 and reuses LayerNorm's already-computed variance, so overhead is negligible.
- **Comprehensive scaling evidence:** decoder-only models up to 1.3B parameters on SlimPajama-627B (Chinchilla 20 tokens/param) and FineWeb, with scaling laws across depth (3→96 layers), width, batch size (128→1024), and training budget (0.25×→6× Chinchilla), plus zero-shot ARC-Easy/PIQA evaluation — KiteNorm beats all leading baselines at their tuned learning rates.
- **Ablations:** removing double-normalisation, scalarisation, or the variance regulariser each degrades stability or performance (e.g., the 96-layer model without regularisation diverges at LR 5e-3 late in training); sensitivity sweeps over λ, residual-scale rule (1/(2L) vs 1/L), RMSNorm variants, warm-up length, and Adam betas all preserve the advantage.

## Methodology

**Motivation and theory.** The paper starts from the observation that in the decoder-only setting, existing post-norm stabilisation methods (DeepNet's branch scaling, KEEL's double-normalisation) improve conditions at initialisation but lose robustness at large learning rates. Theorem 1 bounds the LayerNorm Jacobian's spectral norm by ∥γ∥∞/(2σ(z_l)); combined with the backward-pass structure ∂x_{l+1}/∂x_l = (∂LN/∂z_l)(I + ∂F/∂x), this makes the LayerNorm Jacobian a *shared bottleneck* whose contraction strength grows with hidden-state variance — so constraining variance throughout training (not just at init) becomes the design goal.

**Component 1 — residual scaling (c = 1/(2L)).** Figure 1 shows a 12-layer post-norm model at LR 1e-2: with c = 1 the hidden-state variance explodes and token alignment (rank collapse) climbs until divergence; with small c, rank collapse reverses and variance contracts. The paper proves scaling the skip branch by a and the residual branch by c are equivalent (LN(ax + cF(x)) depends only on a/c), but chooses residual scaling because raising a would *increase* the variance entering LayerNorm, defeating the regulariser.

**Component 2 — variance regularisation.** A sporadic *second* variance expansion can occur later in training even with residual scaling (visible in the 96-layer ablation). The penalty mean_l ReLU(σ(z_l)² − 1), averaged over batch, sequence, and sublayer dimensions and added with weight λ = 1, only activates when variance exceeds 1, and reuses LayerNorm's variance computation. An ablation shows that applying the same regulariser to GPT-2 pre-norm *increases* instability (Appendix C.8), confirming it is not simply a generic training trick.

**Component 3 — scalarised double-normalisation.** Following KEEL's double-norm structure LN(X + F(LN(X))), KiteNorm replaces vector-valued LN parameters γ, β ∈ R^d with scalars γ′, β′ ∈ R (initialised 1 and 0), giving the optimiser a minimal set of parameters to directly adjust skip/residual branch input scales. Ablations show scalarisation beats standard LayerNorm, with the largest gains in deeper models.

**Experimental setup.** Decoder-only Transformers on SlimPajama-627B with the Chinchilla ratio (20 tokens/param): base 12-layer d_model=512, seq len 2048, vocab 50280, RoPE (base 500k), GLU/SiLU FFN, no biases, untied embeddings, AdamW with fixed β1=β2=0.95, gradient clipping 1.0, bf16, batch 128, linear warm-up (6%) + cosine decay; learning rate is the tuned variable on grids like (1e-3, 2e-3, 5e-3, 1e-2, 2e-2). Baselines: GPT-2 pre-norm (chosen as the strongest pre-norm variant via Appendix C.1), LayerNorm-Scaling, peri-norm, vanilla post-norm, KEEL (GLU variant, since its equal-parameter MLP version is worse, App C.2), and mix-norm with partial GPT-2 init. Scale-up tests: a Pythia-1B config (L=16, d=2048, batch 1024, 15B tokens, warm-up-then-constant schedule) and a Llama-3.2-1B-style 1.3B config (32 heads, GLU factor 4, 22B FineWeb tokens). Metrics: validation loss/perplexity plus zero-shot ARC-Easy and PIQA via the LM Evaluation Harness. Total compute: ~1000 A100-80GB GPU-hours plus ~250 B200 GPU-hours; code is released at github.com/l-trochelmann/KiteNorm.

## Results & Highlights

- **48-layer main comparison (SlimPajama-627B validation perplexity, tuned LR, 3 seeds):** KiteNorm achieves 19.080 best / 19.156 worst vs GPT-2 pre-norm 20.240 / 20.343 — a **1.16-ppl improvement at both best and worst seed** — and also beats LayerNorm-Scaling (19.834/19.970), KEEL (20.316/20.420), peri-norm (21.365/21.699), mix-norm (22.011/22.211), and vanilla post-norm (24.807/24.882). KiteNorm's optimal LR is 5e-3, where several baselines are unstable.
- **Depth scaling:** at 96 layers, KiteNorm's best validation perplexity on SlimPajama-627B is **16.189 vs 16.755 for GPT-2 pre-norm (Δ = 0.57), 16.889 for KEEL, and 17.133 for LayerNorm-Scaling**; at 12 layers the gap is larger (24.831 vs 26.660 for GPT-2 pre-norm, Δ = 1.83). KiteNorm maintains its lead across the full 3→96-layer sweep (Figure 3).
- **Llama-style 1.3B models on FineWeb-22B (validation ppl across LR grid):** KiteNorm's best is **14.929 at LR 1e-3 vs 15.464 for GPT-2 pre-norm at 5e-4** (Δ = 0.54); GPT-2 pre-norm shows loss spikes at LR 1e-3/2e-3 and **diverges at 5e-3, while KiteNorm stays stable across the entire grid**. Gradient L2 norms of the GLU output projections are slightly more homogeneous across layers for KiteNorm.
- **1B Pythia-config robustness and downstream:** KiteNorm converges at LR 5e-3 where GPT-2 pre-norm diverges; at the shared LR 2e-3, zero-shot ARC-Easy acc_norm is **0.473 vs 0.443** and PIQA acc_norm 0.675 vs 0.670.
- **48-layer downstream (zero-shot, best checkpoints):** ARC-Easy accuracy 0.455 / acc_norm 0.404 for KiteNorm vs 0.426 / 0.383 for GPT-2 pre-norm (PIQA differences are near noise, as the authors themselves note for these small models).
- **Ablations (48-layer validation ppl unless noted):** removing the variance regulariser causes the 96-layer model to **diverge at LR 5e-3 late in training**; λ = 1 is optimal (19.080 vs 19.190 at λ = 0.1 and 19.431 at λ = 10); the residual-scale rule 1/(2L) beats 1/L (19.080 vs 19.125); scalarised LN beats standard LN increasingly with depth; dropping the inner (double) LayerNorm degrades a 12-layer model. Interestingly, removing KEEL's first-layer exceptions *improves* KEEL (19.612 vs 20.420 at LR 5e-3), yet even this improved KEEL still trails KiteNorm's 19.080.
- **Other scaling axes:** on 12-layer models, KiteNorm's advantage holds across training budget (0.25×–6× Chinchilla, with the largest relative gains at low budgets), batch size (128→1024), and width (halved/doubled d_model).

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from the ELLIS Institute Tübingen, the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems, and the Tübingen AI Center — a strong European ML theory/systems group. Shiwei Liu is a co-author of Mix-LN (Li et al., 2024), one of the baselines, so the team has direct prior expertise in normalisation-placement research; Antonio Orvieto is a established researcher on optimisation and training dynamics (the fixed β1 = β2 AdamW setting follows his work with Gower). The paper states it is a preprint (not yet peer-reviewed). Credibility signals are good: code is released (github.com/l-trochelmann/KiteNorm), all hyperparameters are exhaustively specified (Appendix Tables 1–2), baseline implementations follow their original specifications with deviations explicitly justified in appendices (C.1–C.3), and the compute budget (~1000 A100 + 250 B200 GPU-hours) is transparently disclosed. Seed robustness (3 seeds at tuned LR) and learning-rate grids for every method are above the norm for architecture papers at this scale.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The individual ingredients are mostly known — residual scaling dates to GPT-2/DeepNet, double-normalisation comes from KEEL (Chen & Wei, 2026). The genuinely new pieces are (a) the clean Theorem 1 bound tying variance to gradient contraction, (b) the empirical variance↔rank-collapse coupling experiment, (c) the ReLU variance regulariser, and (d) scalarised LayerNorm. As a *synthesis* it is incremental but coherent, and it is the first post-norm method in this setting to clearly beat strong pre-norm baselines at tuned learning rates rather than only at initialisation.
- **Rigor:** Above average. Tuned LR grids per method, three seeds for headline numbers, fairness checks (the ReLU regulariser applied to GPT-2 pre-norm *hurts* it, App C.8; KEEL evaluated with GLU because its equal-parameter MLP variant is worse, App C.2), and sensitivity sweeps over λ, scaling rules, RMSNorm, warm-up, and Adam betas. A notable paper-specific finding is that KEEL's first-layer exceptions *reduce* performance (Table 6) — a careful dissection, though it implicitly questions KEEL's original claims, which the authors discuss rather than bury.
- **Weaknesses in evidence:** Scale is modest — 1.3B parameters and ≤22B tokens is far from frontier LLM training, and the authors honestly list this in Limitations. Downstream evaluation is limited to ARC-Easy and PIQA; PIQA scores at this scale are close to chance and the authors concede the differences are hard to distinguish from noise — the perplexity results carry the paper, and no harder benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, etc.) are attempted. The theory is only an upper bound that *motivates* variance control; it provides no guarantee against gradient vanishing (acknowledged in Section 6). Some results (data/batch/width scaling, Figure 4) are presented only graphically without tabulated numbers, and the variance threshold of 1 in the regulariser is a fixed, somewhat arbitrary constant.
- **Baseline selection nuance:** LayerNorm-Scaling actually beats GPT-2 pre-norm at 48 layers (19.834 vs 20.240), yet is dropped from the expensive scaling studies in favour of GPT-2 pre-norm based on 12/96-layer results (App C.5). The choice is justified and LNS still loses to KiteNorm head-to-head at 48 layers (19.834 vs 19.080), but the strongest-at-48L baseline not being carried through the scaling sweeps is a mild cherry-picking risk worth noting. DeepNet is discussed but not included in the decoder-only comparison table.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong — released code, full hyperparameter tables, disclosed LR grids and seeds, and stated GPU-hour budget make the main claims independently checkable at small scale.
- **Red flags:** None serious. The headline perplexity gaps (0.5–1.8 ppl) are consistent across depths and two datasets, which argues against cherry-picking; however, "post-norm is back" style conclusions at ≤1.3B should not be extrapolated to 7B+ models without further evidence, and the paper appropriately avoids such claims.

## Strengths

- Simple, interpretable theory (Theorem 1) that directly translates into a practical training-time mechanism, rather than an init-only fix.
- Identifies and addresses a failure mode prior work misses: sporadic *late-training* variance expansion that initialisation-based methods cannot control.
- Empirically thorough: six competitive baselines at tuned learning rates, multiple seeds, four scaling axes, two architectural style guides (Pythia and Llama), two datasets, and per-component ablations with sensitivity sweeps.
- Honest and transparent: openly reports where downstream differences are noise, discloses compute, releases code, and stress-tests its own fairness assumptions in appendices.

## Limitations

- Experiments cap at 1.3B parameters and 22B training tokens (SlimPajama-627B, FineWeb); whether the advantage persists at 7B+ scale and multi-trillion-token budgets is untested.
- Theoretical analysis provides no general guarantee against gradient vanishing; the residual-scaling rule 1/(2L) is chosen from intuition and sensitivity analysis, not derived from a complete theory.
- Downstream evaluation is limited to ARC-Easy and PIQA (binary-choice, near chance at this model scale); no harder reasoning or knowledge benchmarks.
- The variance threshold (σ² > 1) and λ = 1 are fixed constants; no adaptive or depth-dependent schedule is explored.
- Training configuration was tuned experimentally rather than via a principled parameterisation (e.g., Dey et al. 2025), which the authors themselves flag as a limitation.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does KiteNorm's advantage persist at 7B–70B parameters and multi-trillion-token budgets, where pre-norm's "deeper layers are lazy" pathology is known to worsen?
- How does variance regularisation interact with principled parameterisations such as μP/CompleteP — can the 1/(2L) rule and λ = 1 be derived rather than tuned?
- Would a depth- or training-time-adaptive variance threshold outperform the fixed σ² > 1 cutoff, and does the threshold interact with width (d_model) or normalisation choice (LN vs RMSNorm)?
- Does KiteNorm change the emergence of massive activations and attention sinks at scale, and what are the inference-time implications (KV-cache, quantisation robustness) of post-norm hidden states?
- Can the variance regulariser complement other stability mechanisms (QK-norm, different optimisers such as Muon), and does it help encoder-decoder or non-Transformer post-norm architectures?
