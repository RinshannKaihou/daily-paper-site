> **一句话总结：** 本文借用压缩感知把神经网络的"基本表征单元"（FRU）形式化为"表征原子"——在原子内积（AIP）下近似正交且满足 µ < 1/(2K−1) 的稀疏字典——用保真度（R²）与稳定性（q*）两个可计算指标统一评测神经元与 SAE 特征（各有短板），并证明双向阈值 SAE（BiJumpReLU）可同时达到两项指标最优（8 个模型-数据组合全部第一，如 Wikipedia/Gemma2-2B 上保真度 97.25 对 JumpReLU 94.91、稳定性 99.78 对 TopK 39.34），进而发现稳定性与单义性显著正相关（ρ=0.71），且 HowNet 义原极少由单个原子表征（单原子最佳 F1 仅 0.31–0.35）、而是由平均 54–62 个跨层原子组合恢复（F1 升至 0.78–0.82）。

> **TL;DR:** Seeking a principled definition of the "fundamental units" of neural representations, this CASIA team formalizes them as representational atoms — a K-sparse dictionary that is approximately orthogonal under a new atomic inner product — with two computable criteria: fidelity (R²) and stability (q*, the largest quantile where the compressed-sensing condition µ < 1/(2K−1) holds). They prove threshold-activated SAEs can exactly recover such atoms, instantiate BiJumpReLU (signed/learnable thresholds), and train ReLU/TopK/AbsTopK/JumpReLU/BiJumpReLU SAEs (~221K width) on 7 models spanning LLMs (Gemma-2-2B/9B, Qwen3.5-9B, Olmo-Hybrid-7B) and diffusion models (SD v1.5, FLUX.2-klein-9B) over Wikipedia/Wikidata/LAION-COCO: BiJumpReLU wins both metrics in all 8 model-dataset combos (e.g. on Wikipedia/Gemma2-2B fidelity 97.25 vs 94.91 for JumpReLU, stability 99.78 vs 39.34 for TopK with K=50; on LAION-COCO/FLUX.2 stability 98.47 vs 64.12 for JumpReLU). Stability correlates with monosemanticity (Spearman ρ=0.71 across 40 dataset-model-SAE combos), and against the expert-annotated HowNet sememe benchmark (2,135 sememes, ~123K entities) single atoms rarely encode a full human sememe (best-single-atom F1 0.314/0.349/0.340 on Gemma2-2B/Gemma2-9B/Llama3.1-8B) while greedy cross-layer atom combinations lift F1 to 0.809/0.820/0.783 (+0.44–0.50) — mostly via recall 0.23→0.99 at held precision — implying concepts are compositionally distributed over layers. Read it for the formal framing and the metrics; note the theory is an idealized exact-recovery result and the BiJumpReLU-vs-JumpReLU margin is often small.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化定义"表征原子"**：将内部表征建模为稀疏线性组合 m = Dδ（||δ||₀ ≤ K），原子须同时满足可表征性、稀疏性与（近似）可分性；关键是提出**原子内积（AIP）**⟨dᵢ, dⱼ⟩ = dᵢᵀ S̃dⱼ（S̃ = (DDᵀ)⁻¹）——因为表征只在线性重参数化下可辨识，欧氏内积不是规范的几何；AIP 在动态、依赖输入的表征空间中运作，区别于 Park 等人的因果内积。
- **两个可计算的评测指标**：**保真度**（R²，重建质量）与**稳定性**（q* = sup{q : µ_q < 1/(2K_q−1)}，即经验稀疏-相干对满足可分辨条件的最大分位数），把压缩感知的唯一性定理（µ < 1/(2K−1) ⇒ 单表征性/单义性的必要结构条件）变成可实测的量，从而首次在统一框架下定量比较神经元与 SAE 特征：神经元高保真低稳定，Gemma Scope/Llama Scope 特征高稳定低保真，二者都不接近理想原子。
- **TSAE 可识别性定理 + BiJumpReLU 实例化**：证明带双向阈值的 SAE（W_dec = D, W_enc = DᵀS̃, σ 在 |x| 超阈值时透传否则置零）在适当条件下可精确恢复原子集；理论直接指出 ReLU SAE 缺阈值故不足、TopK/JumpReLU 单侧激活隐含任意的正方向偏好；BiJumpReLU 用可学习的正负阈值（σᵢ(z) = z·1[z < τᵢ⁻ ∨ z > τᵢ⁺]）消除符号偏置，在全部 8 个模型-数据组合上双指标第一。
- **稳定性 ⇔ 单义性的实证验证**：40 个数据-模型-SAE 组合上 Spearman ρ = 0.71（P < 0.001），三个数据集内部分别为 0.74/0.79/0.95，八个模型级相关全部为正（0.37–0.93），支持"可分辨性是单义性前提"的理论链路。
- **原子组合编码人类概念结构**：用 HowNet（2,135 个义原、约 12.3 万英文实体、全层提取表征）做对齐分析：近乎一对一的义原-原子映射极罕见（单原子 F1=1 的仅约 1%），多数义原需 >6 个原子（三模型中占 79–82%）；跨层原子组合将义原 F1 从单原子的 0.31–0.35 提升到 0.78–0.82，且等价检验（|r| < 0.10）排除了"跨层宽度越大 F1 越高"的搜索伪影解释——说明模型概念是分布在多层原子上的**组合式**编码，并对知识编辑/干预、持续学习中的灾难性遗忘给出了结构性解释视角。

## Key Contributions

- **Formal definition of representational atoms.** Internal representations are modeled as sparse linear combinations m = Dδ (||δ||₀ ≤ K); atoms must satisfy representability, sparsity, and approximate separability. The crux is a new **atomic inner product (AIP)** with metric S̃ = (DDᵀ)⁻¹: since representations are identifiable only up to invertible linear reparameterization, the Euclidean inner product is non-canonical; unlike the causal inner product of Park et al., the AIP operates in the dynamic, input-dependent representation space.
- **Two computable evaluation criteria.** **Fidelity** (R² of reconstruction) and **stability** (q* = sup{q : µ_q < 1/(2K_q − 1)}, the largest quantile at which empirical coherence µ_q and sparsity K_q satisfy the compressed-sensing uniqueness condition), turning Theorem 9 (µ < 1/(2K−1) ⇒ monorepresentationality, a provable necessary condition for monosemanticity) into a measurable score. This lets neurons and pretrained SAE features (Gemma Scope, Llama Scope) be compared in one framework: neurons show high fidelity but low stability; features the reverse.
- **TSAE identifiability theorem + BiJumpReLU instantiation.** They prove bidirectional threshold-activated SAEs (W_dec = D, W_enc = DᵀS̃, activation passthrough only when z > τᵢ⁺ or z < τᵢ⁻) exactly recover the atom set under bounded-coefficient conditions; the theory explains why ReLU SAEs (no threshold) are insufficient and why one-sided TopK/JumpReLU impose an arbitrary positive orientation. BiJumpReLU, with learnable signed thresholds, ranks first on both metrics in all 8 model-dataset combinations.
- **Empirical stability–monosemanticity link.** Across 40 dataset-model-SAE combinations, stability positively correlates with monosemanticity (Spearman ρ = 0.71, P < 0.001), preserved within Wikipedia (0.74), Wikidata (0.79), LAION-COCO (0.95) and at all eight model levels (0.37–0.93).
- **Compositional encoding of human concepts.** Aligning atoms with HowNet sememes (2,135 sememes, ~123K English entities, representations from every layer of Gemma-2-2B/9B and Llama-3.1-8B): one-to-one sememe-atom matches are rare (~1% of sememes get F1 = 1 from a single atom; 79–82% of sememes need >6 atoms); greedy cross-layer atom combinations raise sememe F1 from 0.314/0.349/0.340 (best single atom) to 0.809/0.820/0.783, chiefly via recall 0.23–0.27 → 0.95–0.99 at held precision, with an equivalence test (|r| < 0.10) ruling out cross-layer width as the driver. Atoms thus behave as sub-concept semantic components composed across layers — with direct implications for knowledge editing, steering, and catastrophic forgetting.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds theory-first. (1) **Definition**: because any invertible linear reparameterization leaves the network's input-output function unchanged, internal representations and their units are only identifiable up to a linear equivalence class, so the Euclidean inner product (angles, orthogonality) is parameterization-dependent. The authors posit that the right geometry makes the (unknown) atom set itself orthonormal, deriving the atomic inner product S = c²(DDᵀ)⁻¹ (Theorem 2) and its normalized version S̃ = (DDᵀ)⁻¹ (Corollary 3); relaxed to ϵ-approximate orthogonality when |D| ≫ H (superposition). (2) **Evaluation**: representability is measured by R²; sparsity+separability are unified via classical coherence theory — a coherence-RIP bound (δ_K ≤ (K−1)µ, Theorem 8) and the uniqueness/exact-ℓ1-recoverability condition µ < 1/(2K−1) (Theorem 9, injectivity on K-sparse supports in Corollary 10). Stability q* is computed by binary search over quantiles of the empirical distributions of per-sample ℓ0 sparsity and pairwise NAIP coherence. (3) **Identification** (Theorem 11): if the data genuinely follow m = Dδ with near-orthogonal D and coefficients bounded away from zero, then a threshold-activated SAE with W_dec = D, W_enc = DᵀS̃ and bidirectional thresholds sandwiched in explicit intervals reconstructs every m exactly — hence TSAEs identify atoms in principle. BiJumpReLU instantiates this with per-coordinate learnable τᵢ⁻, τᵢ⁺, trained with the standard reconstruction + λ‖σ(z)‖₁ loss (λ = 5.0, straight-through estimator through thresholds, lr 2e-4, Adam, batch 4096), plus a trajectory-based early-stopping rule at the point where reconstruction loss turns from decreasing to persistently increasing while sparsity keeps improving. (4) **Experiments**: representations extracted from 6 layers of each LLM on 300K Wikipedia samples (1.8M vectors/model), 1.8M Wikidata entity vectors, and diffusion-model internals (SD v1.5 mid-block attention over 3 timesteps = 1.92M vectors; FLUX.2-klein-9B over 3 layers × 2 steps = 3.84M), with per-site normalization (std scaling after layernorm sites, RMS scaling for down-proj/unnormalized sites); SAE dictionary widths held at ~221K across models for comparability. For the semantic analysis, BiJumpReLU SAEs are trained at every layer, sememes and atoms are represented as overlapping entity sets, and per-sememe best single atoms vs greedy F1-maximizing atom combinations are compared via precision/recall/F1, with an equivalence test controlling for cross-layer width and manual case inspection of mismatched entities.

## Results & Highlights

- **BiJumpReLU sweeps both criteria on all 8 model-dataset combinations** (Table 1). On Wikipedia/Gemma2-2B: fidelity 97.25 (±1.44) vs 94.91 (±2.38) for the strongest baseline JumpReLU, 88.68 (±7.05) for TopK (K=50) and 75.52 for GemmaScope-Large; stability 99.78 (±0.13) vs 39.34 (±1.01) for TopK — a +60-point stability gap. On the hardest setting, LAION-COCO/FLUX.2-klein-9B: stability 98.47 (±2.48) vs 64.12 (±10.20) for JumpReLU and 41.56 (±14.95) for TopK, with fidelity 96.03 vs 88.33 (±8.43) for TopK. BiJumpReLU is the only architecture keeping stability above 98 everywhere.
- **Stability predicts monosemanticity** (Fig. 3): Spearman ρ = 0.71 (P < 0.001, N = 40 dataset-model-SAE combos), ρ = 0.74/0.79/0.95 within Wikipedia/Wikidata/LAION-COCO, and all 8 model-level correlations positive, from ρ = 0.37 (Olmo-Hybrid-7B on Wikidata, P = 0.041) to ρ = 0.95 (Qwen3.5-9B on Wikipedia, P < 0.001).
- **Human sememes are compositionally, not atomically, encoded** (Fig. 4 + Extended Data Table 3): on HowNet (2,135 sememes), the best single atom reaches F1 = 0.314 (Gemma2-2B), 0.349 (Gemma2-9B), 0.340 (Llama3.1-8B), while cross-layer atom combinations reach 0.809/0.820/0.783 — ΔF1 of +0.495/+0.470/+0.443 — driven almost entirely by recall rising from 0.232/0.268/0.266 to 0.989/0.986/0.954 at unchanged-to-better precision (0.691→0.691, 0.682→0.710, 0.624→0.672), using a mean of 62.0/54.4/57.6 atoms per sememe. Only ~1% of sememes admit a single-atom F1 of 1, and 79–82% require more than six atoms.
- **The composition gain is not a search artifact** (Fig. 5): Pearson correlation between cross-layer width and final sememe F1 is r = 0.025/0.006/−0.032 across the three models, and equivalence tests confirm |r| < 0.10 at P < 0.001.
- **Per-layer BiJumpReLU SAEs on HowNet hit near-ceiling quality** (Extended Data Table 4): fidelity 99.62/99.64/99.30 and stability 99.82/99.91/99.89 on Gemma2-2B/Gemma2-9B/Llama3.1-8B, with feature utilization 86.6–93.5%.
- **Existing units fall short of ideal atoms** (Fig. 2): neurons preserve fidelity but their stability stays far below 1, and Gemma Scope/Llama Scope features trade fidelity away for moderate stability gains — neither class approaches the ideal-atom reference (R² = q* = 1) across Gemma2-2B, Gemma2-9B, Llama3.1-8B at multiple widths (7×–37×).

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors (Chenhui Hu, Pengfei Cao, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao) are from the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences (CASIA/NLPR) and the School of AI, UCAS. Liu and Zhao lead a well-established NLP group, and the team's prior work is directly on-topic: "Knowledge in superposition" (AAAI 2025) on lifelong knowledge-editing failures, "The knowledge microscope" (ACL 2025) comparing features vs neurons as analytical lenses, query localization (ICLR 2025), and WiKE (ACL 2024 Findings) — this paper reads as the theoretical consolidation of that line. The manuscript is prepared in Nature-style format (main text + Methods + Extended Data + Supplementary, 48 pages), with disclosed ChatGPT use limited to language polishing, public data sources (Wikipedia 20231101.en, Wikidata, LAION-COCO, OpenHowNet), and code released at github.com/ChenhuiHu/representational_atoms. Experiments span 7 models across two modalities (5 LLMs including a hybrid-architecture model, 2 diffusion models) with confidence intervals reported throughout — an unusually broad footprint for an interpretability paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuinely good on the formal side — the AIP (making the atom set itself the orthonormal reference instead of borrowing Euclidean geometry) and the TSAE identifiability theorem are real contributions, and framing neurons vs SAE features under one fidelity/stability axis is clarifying. BiJumpReLU itself is architecturally a small delta over JumpReLU (signed learnable thresholds instead of one-sided), but it is theory-derived rather than ad hoc, and the paper honestly positions JumpReLU as the closest competitor.
- **Rigor caveats on the headline metric**: q* measures satisfaction of a sparsity-coherence inequality that BiJumpReLU's training objective (L1 sparsity + thresholding) directly optimizes the ingredients of — so near-ceiling stability numbers (99.8) are partly self-fulfilling; the paper mitigates this with the monosemanticity correlation and HowNet alignment, which is the right move, but readers should not read q* = 99.8 as "99.8% semantically clean."
- **The theorem is idealized**: Theorem 11 assumes data are exactly generated as m = Dδ with near-orthogonal D and coefficients bounded away from zero (δmin > B₀ + B±), and sets W_enc = DᵀS̃ — which presumes knowing the true atom set. It is a representability/exact-recovery result, not a learnability guarantee; the paper does not quantify how far trained SAEs sit from the theorem's conditions.
- **Small-scale, partly subjective monosemanticity protocol**: only 10 candidate FRUs per model-site with up to 10 activating instances each, scored by an LLM-as-judge (judge model unnamed in the main text) with manual verification for LMs, and manual-only scoring for diffusion models — a thin basis for the 40-point correlation, though the effect is consistent across 12 panels.
- **Baseline confounds**: Gemma Scope/Llama Scope comparisons use pretrained checkpoints at widths 16k–131k trained on different corpora vs locally trained ~221K SAEs, so their fidelity/stability deficits partly reflect training-distribution mismatch rather than architecture (the authors acknowledge this for fidelity); newer baselines (Gated, BatchTopK) are absent; and on LLMs the BiJumpReLU-over-JumpReLU margin is often ~1–3 points — the decisive stability gaps are against TopK/AbsTopK and on FLUX.2.
- **Compositional-search controls**: the greedy combination uses a mean of 54–62 atoms per sememe with candidate prefiltering; precision retention and the cross-layer-width equivalence test are good controls, but no random-combination or matched-size baseline is reported. Extended Data Table 5 itself shows noisy members inside high-F1 recoveries (e.g., "hardtack", "coleorhizae", "counterspy" inside the DISTRICT sememe at F1 = 0.983), which the authors partly attribute to HowNet annotation gaps — verified manually but selectively.
- **Reproducibility**: strong — public models, public datasets, released code, fixed hyperparameters (λ = 5.0, lr 2e-4, batch 4096) shared across all architectures, and per-layer SAE specs given. Significance: the compositional-distributed-sememe finding gives a concrete structural account of why single-direction edits steer incompletely, but all knowledge-editing/continual-learning implications remain argued, not tested.

## Strengths

- A clean, self-contained theory pipeline: parameterization-invariance argument → atomic inner product → coherence-based distinguishability → computable fidelity/stability metrics → an identifiability theorem that also explains *why* existing activation functions fall short (ReLU lacks thresholds; TopK/JumpReLU are one-sided; AbsTopK needs a preset K).
- Unusually broad empirical coverage for interpretability work: 7 models, 3 representation sources (continuous text, discrete entities, diffusion internals), 5 SAE families, ~221K-width dictionaries trained per layer — with mean ± 95% CI reported everywhere.
- The stability-monosemanticity link is tested at three granularities (global, per-dataset, per-model) rather than asserted.
- The HowNet analysis is a genuinely informative operationalization of "representation ↔ human concept structure," and the cross-layer-width equivalence test is a thoughtful control against the most obvious artifact; the annotation-error case studies are a bonus.
- Honest framing of monorepresentationality as provable-but-structural vs monosemanticity as empirical-and-interpretive.

## Limitations

- The identifiability theorem assumes noiseless K-sparse generation and bounded nonzero coefficients; no analysis of robustness to noise, near-dependencies, or distribution shift, and no estimate of how close trained TSAEs come to the theorem's threshold sandwich.
- q* is a distributional quantile condition, not a per-atom semantic quality measure; its near-1 values for BiJumpReLU partly mirror the training objective.
- Monosemanticity sampling is tiny (10 units × ≤10 instances per site) and judge-dependent; diffusion-model monosemanticity is manually scored only, and some model-level correlations are weak (ρ = 0.37, P = 0.041).
- Comparisons against Gemma Scope/Llama Scope confound architecture with width, training data, and training scale; CI half-widths on diffusion models are wide (up to ±21 on SD v1.5 fidelity across only 3 timesteps).
- The compositional claim rests on greedy F1-maximizing search averaging 54–62 atoms per sememe; without matched-size random-combination controls, "composition recovers concepts" and "union of overlapping sets recovers entity lists" are hard to fully separate, and HowNet's own entity-set coarseness (Chinese-oriented sememe taxonomy applied to English entities) inflates both matches and mismatches.
- No downstream utility evidence: the promised implications for knowledge editing, steering, and continual learning (superposition as a structural cause of forgetting) are discussed but untested.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是把 SAE/叠加(superposition)研究与压缩感知形式化统一起来的表征几何工作，提供了可复用的保真度/稳定性度量、BiJumpReLU SAE 变体与"概念=跨层原子组合"的实证结论，对后续做探针、特征引导与知识编辑的分析框架都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How far do trained BiJumpReLU SAEs actually sit from Theorem 11's threshold sandwich (δmin vs B₀ + B±), and can the margin be estimated from data as a practical identifiability certificate?
- Does the stability-monosemanticity correlation survive at scale — e.g., 1,000 features per site instead of 10, monosemanticity scored by a fixed open judge with published prompts, and automated (而非 manual) scoring for diffusion latents?
- Do matched-size random atom combinations (or matched-precision unions) recover HowNet sememes as well as the greedy 54–62-atom combinations, i.e., how much of the ΔF1 = +0.44–0.50 is structure rather than set-union coverage?
- If concepts are compositional over ~60 cross-layer atoms, what happens to single-direction steering and model editing (ROME/MEMIT-style) — can atom-composition-aware interventions measurably reduce collateral edits or incomplete edits?
- Does the AIP geometry (S̃ = (DDᵀ)⁻¹) change conclusions from prior neuron-vs-feature studies that used Euclidean geometry, e.g., for feature splitting or dictionary-learning scaling laws?
- Is the FLUX.2 stability collapse of JumpReLU (64.12 vs BiJumpReLU's 98.47) a modality property (vision tokens) or a sign-convention pathology that would also appear in other non-Transformer representation spaces?
