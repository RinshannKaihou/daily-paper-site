Why This Matters

Supplies a reusable, open-source arithmetic reference for bit-exact GPU numerics — instruction-set simulators, cross-platform reproducibility, SFU verification — with no GPU or giant tables needed.

Critical Edge

A functional equivalence, not silicon truth. The 64-segment / Q37 / S15 structure is an Occam-style inference from bit-agreement — and the 17 extended paths are validated on the A100 only.

Methodology — four falsifiable stages

  1. Draw the boundary. Compile minimal PTX, read the SASS: sin.approx.ftz.f32 = FMUL.RZ prescale by 1/2π + MUFU.SIN. Only the bare-MUFU register I/O is the target; prescales, LOP3 masks, and loads are modeled as separate surrounding ops.
  2. Segment. 25 effective phase bits; symmetry maps 4 quadrants × 2 functions onto one kernel at phase p or its bitwise mirror 0x7FFFFF − p. Even/odd-start pairing of error patterns uniquely selects 64 segments over 32/128 hypotheses (6-bit index + 17-bit coordinate ℓ).
  3. Constrain. Each FP32 output pins an integer interval on the internal value; feasibility forces fixed-point scale Q37 (Q36 leaves an empty interval) and pins per-segment integer pairs (cs, bs) within the first 1024 points of every segment.
  4. Close the residual. The quadratic term needs a truncated square: S15 keeps only partial products with m+n ≥ 14 of (ℓ≫2)², exactly reproducing the observed correction δ(v) ∈ {0..7}.

The same procedure yields 7 core families covering 14 MUFU forms (FP16/BF16 reuse FP32 cores; RCP64H pads to 20 bits and rebuilds the high word). Validation runs inline-PTX CUDA on device vs an independent C++ model, in chunks, requiring checked-count == domain size.

Strengths

  • Exhaustive, gap-checked, fault-injected validation over complete input domains — far beyond sampled testing.
  • Disciplined, falsifiable methodology that generalizes from SIN/COS to 14 MUFU forms.
  • S15 truncated-squaring rule turns an ad-hoc correction table into a structurally meaningful arithmetic law.
  • <10 KB of coefficients replaces ~128 GiB of tables; computes on demand, no GPU needed.
  • Shared coefficients across three architectures suggest stable SFU numerics for three generations.
  • Bare MUFU cleanly separated from compiler-generated ops via SASS disassembly; open-source release.

Limitations

  • Extended paths (non-.ftz, FP16x2, rounding-mode RCP/SQRT) validated only on the A100.
  • One SKU per architecture; H100 / RTX 50-series and future parts untested.
  • FP64 low input word fixed to zero — complete for the path, but not the full 2⁶⁴ space.
  • Functional equivalence, not confirmed micro-architecture; structural choices are Occam-style inferences.
  • No demonstrated integration into an instruction-set simulator; no model-speed numbers at simulation scale.
  • Rounding-mode paths verified at final output only — bare-MUFU intermediates not independently confirmed.
  • Surrounding ops are toolchain-dependent (CUDA 12.8-era); compiler updates could reshape paths.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Do H100, B200, RTX 50-series share the same 64-segment/Q37/S15 coefficients — and can divergence be detected with far fewer than 2³² probes?
  2. How do the extended paths behave on Ada and Blackwell, where the compiler may pick different surrounding-op sequences?
  3. Can the pipeline be automated (SASS diffing + interval solving) to recover a new architecture's SFU in hours?
  4. What is the cost of embedding SFU-Sim into a full ISS (GPGPU-Sim/AccelSim-class), and does bit-exact SFU modeling change reproducibility conclusions?
  5. Does the truncated squaring rule correspond to a real area/power optimization in NVIDIA's SFU — confirmable via silicon-level evidence?
  6. Do AMD / Intel GPU SFUs follow analogous piecewise-quadratic organizations recoverable with the same method?
