Methodology — how it works

  1. On-policy calibration

    The dense VLM greedily generates reasoning traces (≤512 tokens) over 256 ScienceQA samples; activations from prompts, multimodal inputs, and self-generated reasoning form the calibration matrix — matching the real inference-time state distribution (reasoning-state reconstruction error 10.27 → 8.88).

  2. Sensitivity-aware SVD

    Each token's reconstruction error is weighted by a Monte-Carlo Fisher estimate (K=8) of its policy-KL sensitivity — reasoning-critical tokens and attention sinks are orders of magnitude more sensitive than most visual tokens. Solved in closed form via Cholesky-whitened SVD.

  3. Global rank allocation

    Every singular component across all layers and K/V projections is scored by its estimated second-order policy-KL cost of removal; components compete for one shared budget (value projections and mid-to-late layers win most capacity).

  4. Quantization + fused Triton kernels

    Hadamard rotation + singular-value redistribution (α=0.7) cut quantization NMSE by 4–8%; fused kernels (key reconstruction + MRoPE + attention logits, latent-space value aggregation) convert memory savings into real decoding speedup.

Training-free · calibration ≈ 30 min for a 7–8B model on one L40S GPU

Strengths

  • Causal diagnosis before the fix: two controlled observations (calibration mismatch, sensitivity heterogeneity) each map to one component, and the ablation confirms both matter.
  • One principled objective: a single second-order policy-KL derivation yields the calibration choice, SVD weighting, and rank allocation — with honest appendix-level error analysis.
  • Practical end-to-end: memory savings become measured wall-clock speedups and peak-memory cuts via real fused kernels, not just accuracy tables.
  • Targets reasoning-specific failure modes: generation-length / truncation analysis shows the dense model's reasoning trajectory is preserved, not merely the final answer.

Limitations

  • Dense-trace objective: policy-KL is measured on dense-model rollouts; the compressed model's own state distribution is untested, so errors may accumulate over very long generations.
  • Calibration overhead: ~30 min per model plus 8 backward passes; generalization beyond 256 task-specific examples (agentic, long-form) is unvalidated.
  • Narrow benchmark coverage: three VQA suites only — no text-only reasoning, video, or long-context retrieval; token eviction untested in combination; LASER / BELT not compared.
  • Kernel-level baseline missing: speedups are measured against default HuggingFace dense inference; Palu's latency is never compared.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would compressing the compressed model's own rollouts — or iterative re-calibration — close the dense-vs-compressed state-distribution gap?
  2. Does the policy-KL sensitivity structure transfer to video frames, long-document VQA, or agentic tool-use traces where visual tokens may be less redundant?
  3. Can TraceKV combine multiplicatively with token eviction or cross-layer sharing (e.g., xKV), given visual tokens are both low-sensitivity and highly redundant?
  4. Does the sensitivity ranking of late reasoning states change when calibration traces scale from 512 to the full 4,096-token regime?
  5. Do the rank-allocation patterns (value > key, mid-to-late layers) hold for much larger or MoE reasoning VLMs — and can they be predicted without Monte-Carlo backward passes?
