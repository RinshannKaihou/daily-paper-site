Why this matters

Explains why rotation-based 4-bit quantization breaks on massive outliers — and fixes it at near-zero cost, drop-in across QuaRot / SpinQuant / DartQuant and MoE models.

⚠ Critical edge

The headline 6.60 → 5.88 conflates two changes; like-for-like, SignRot adds 6.16 → 5.88 — and stacked on DartQuant (LLaMA3-70B) it actually loses 0.37 accuracy points.

Methodology
0Why rotation breaks

At the FFN down-projection input, the rotated activation is the outlier term ±Mt/√Cin plus a Gaussian residual — a two-Gaussian mixture that turns bimodal whenever one channel holds more energy than all remaining channels combined (a Lindeberg-condition violation), inflating per-token quantization range and step. On LLaMA2-7B layer 2 (Cin=11,008): threshold ≈58, observed Mt=1510 — 26× over.

1Find the culprit channel

One calibration forward pass picks cmax = argmaxc maxt |xt,c|. Massive-outlier channels are stable across inputs — the dominant channel holds 87–99.9% of token energy.

2Flip signs

With n = sign(Hcmax,:), row cmax of H·diag(n) becomes uniformly +1/√Cin: the outlier degrades to a constant shift and the distribution collapses to unimodal. diag(n) is involutory, so its inverse fuses offline into Wd — online cost is element-wise sign flips only, no extra matmul.

3Pair with asymmetric quantization

The aligned outlier shifts the activation mean off zero, so per-token asymmetric quantization is integral, not optional. Asym-Symm matmul precomputes the zero-point correction zX(1ᵀWq) from weight column sums — O(T·Cout) per call vs. O(T·Cin·Cout) for the matmul itself.

  • LLaMA3-8B RTN: accuracy +2.83 pts (61.34 → 64.17), Wiki2 PPL 10.60 → 9.68. Gains shrink with LLaMA2 scale (7B/13B/70B: +2.70 / +1.26 / +0.39).
  • Generalization: MoE Qwen3-30B-A3B GPTQ accuracy 65.11 → 65.80, PPL 10.16 → 9.79; Mistral-7B PPL 5.80 → 5.71 with accuracy held; LLaMA2-13B matches DartQuant's PPL (5.32) with no rotation training.
  • Mechanism (App. C): cuts the first massive-outlier layer's KL by 36–61% (that layer is 2.3–3.6× the median cost; its token is an attention sink), dropping other tokens' KL by 31–64%; per-token activation range narrows a median 2.6–11.3× — up to 885× on near-one-hot Qwen3 tokens.
  • Efficiency (LLaMA2-7B, RTX 3090, batch 1): +1.37% prefill / +0.34% decode / +0.43% end-to-end latency, +0.02% peak memory — retaining 1.65× speedup and 3.19× memory savings vs. FP16. Quantization: ~3 min (RTN) / ~15 min (GPTQ) on one GPU; +5 min for 70B.
  • Robustness: swapping calibration set (Wiki2 vs. C4) and seeds moves PPL by ≤0.06–0.08 and accuracy by ≤0.9 pts (Table 5).
Strengths
  • Falsifiable diagnosis with a concrete number: bimodality threshold ≈58 vs. observed Mt=1510 on LLaMA2-7B layer 2 — explains when and why randomized Hadamard rotation fragments.
  • Near-zero inference cost: element-wise sign flips only (+0.43% latency, +0.02% memory); asymmetric correction reduced to a precomputed rank-1 term.
  • Broad compatibility: consistent gains stacked on three rotation pipelines (QuaRot, SpinQuant, DartQuant) across dense and MoE architectures.
  • Per-layer KL / attention-sink analysis explains why fixing ~1 token per sequence moves model-level metrics — more than a trick.
  • Fully training-free and fast to apply: 3–15 min for a 7B model on a single GPU.
Limitations
  • Highly model-dependent: large on LLaMA3 (esp. 70B), near-flat on LLaMA2-7B GPTQ PPL (6.10 → 6.09), occasionally negative (DartQuant stack; several individual tasks).
  • Only pays off with asymmetric activation quantization — under symmetric settings the shifted mean is not absorbed and gains can invert (66.54 → 66.33).
  • One sign vector per layer folds only one dominant channel; late layers with comparable second channels (M2/M1 ≈ 0.53–0.68) are only partially fixed.
  • W4A4 commonsense-only evaluation: no GSM8K/MMLU, no long-context, no sub-4-bit regime where fragmentation should bite harder.
  • Single-run LLaMA3-70B ablation; the bimodality threshold is exact only within the Gaussian-mixture approximation.
Questions for further exploration
  1. Does sign adjustment help more at W4A3 or W4A2, where the inflated step size from fragmentation matters far more than at W4A4?
  2. Can per-channel-group Hadamard + sign vectors fold the late layers (M2/M1 ≈ 0.5–0.7) — and finally make DartQuant+SignRot positive on LLaMA3-70B?
  3. Is the dominant massive-outlier channel stable under distribution shift (code, math, long context), or does the single calibration pass silently pick the wrong channel out-of-domain?
  4. Does the same polarity-induced fragmentation appear in KV-cache rotation paths or MoE router inputs (Qwen3's near-one-hot tokens, 885× range reduction)?
  5. Is there remaining headroom in jointly choosing QuaRot's random diagonal signs and SignRot's n?
