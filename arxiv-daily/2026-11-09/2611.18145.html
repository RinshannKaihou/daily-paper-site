Key Metric 4.2×

8-byte message rate vs NCCL GIN on H100 — 243 M msg/s at 64 CTAs (3.3× NVSHMEM), one doorbell per window instead of one per put.

Why This Matters

Attacks decode-time small-message latency in expert-parallel MoE serving — the flagship of replacing hand-tuned communication libraries with compilers. 直接命中 MoE 专家并行 dispatch/combine 的小消息延迟瓶颈；vLLM + Qwen3 端到端验证。

Critical Edge ⚠

Benefits stop at small/medium messages: DeepEP wins from 64 tokens/rank (up to 1.7×) and TTFT p95 is 1.4–2.0× worse.

Methodology — distilled

  • Triton plugin, not a library. obaleth.language builtins emit communication ops into TTIR; MLIR passes rewrite them through three levels.
  • Semantic ops — put / put_inline / signal / wait over endpoints & regions; tokens order operations; guarantees deliberately minimal so passes may reorder, merge, and elide.
  • Queue IR — a backend-agnostic schedule: one submission = one queue reservation + command chains + one doorbell + ≤1 completion. Passes query transport capabilities (ordering, remote atomics, inline payloads), so N optimizations cover M backends without N×M implementations.
  • Lowering — mlx5 (compile-time WQE offsets, strong signals as remote CAS / fenced writes), AWS EFA (retire payload before posting epoch), host proxy (32-byte commands to a pinned ring), peer memory (warp-cooperative copies).
  • Measurement — H100 + 200 Gb/s IB, B200 + ConnectX-7, MI355X + Pollara; median of 5 interleaved runs, ≥1000 iterations, payload verification, FP32-validated model outputs.

Where it loses (reported by the authors) Fused dispatch runs at 0.78–0.93× the separate-kernel speed (0.44–0.82× with FP8); NVSHMEM keeps the pure 16-queue streaming rate (257 vs 213 M msg/s); from ≥64 KiB/peer all-to-all is NIC-bound and device APIs converge within 4%.

Strengths

  • First system to put the full GPU-initiated transport path — WQEs, doorbells, completions, ordering — under compiler control, portable across mlx5 / EFA / proxy / peer-memory and both NVIDIA and AMD.
  • Every optimization removes a measured library cost (reservation atomics, system-scope fences, per-call completions), with an ablation quantifying each pass and its interactions.
  • Resource efficiency is a systems win beyond speed: 4–22 MiB/rank and 60 vs 128 registers/thread — attractive for multi-tenant serving.
  • Tiered adoption lowers the barrier: generated drop-in device library for CUDA/CuTe/Triton; frameworks state facts (token bounds, top-k, expert layout), never hand-written kernels.
  • Unusually transparent evaluation: library versions pinned and justified, losing regimes reported, outputs FP32-validated, AI assistance disclosed.

Limitations

  • Gains bounded to small/medium messages and latency-bound phases; from 64 KiB/peer everything converges to NIC-bound within 4%.
  • Throughput regime lost: DeepEP is faster from 64 tokens/rank (up to 1.7×) — register spills in the compiled NVLink send programs.
  • Tail latency: TTFT p95 is 1.4–2.0× worse than DeepEP; the fused dispatch form is slower than the separate packing kernel.
  • Platform-sensitive headline: part of the H100 lead over NCCL GIN comes from NCCL's host-forwarded doorbells — the B200 GPU-direct gap shrinks to 1.11–1.16×.
  • AMD/EFA evaluations preliminary; no training workloads, no congestion/fault stress tests, no formal correctness proof; Tier-2 requires Triton rewrites and multiplies JIT cache variants.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can layout-aware lowering — or hybrid switching to library-style bulk paths — fix the register-spill loss at large batches without giving up the decode advantage?
  2. What exactly drives the 1.4–2.0× TTFT p95 regression: shape-specialization recompilation, capacity-one decode plans, or the dispatch packing split?
  3. How do coalesced batches and deferred completions behave under real cluster congestion and incast (expert-hot ranks, NIC back-pressure, PFC)?
  4. Epoch elision lets receivers observe epochs up to k−1 iterations late — what is the end-to-end impact on flag-polling consumer kernels?
  5. Can the 45%-more-KV-cache storage plan be inferred automatically from Triton expert kernels, and does it survive TMA-based readers?
  6. Does Queue IR extend to upcoming transports (ULL, CX-9 dynamic-reparse WQEs, HBM-attached NICs) — and can it generate collective algorithms, not just point-to-point plans?
