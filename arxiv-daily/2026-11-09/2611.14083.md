> **一句话总结：** Sable 用 31 位小素域上"永不回绕"的约束系统 + 分块 LogUp 查表 + 流式两遍证明,在单张 RTX PRO 6000 GPU 上以 92.5 秒为 Qwen2.5-32B 全部 64 层、4,096 token 的推理生成逐位精确的密码学证明(比 zkLLM 每参数每 token 快 40–64 倍),其代价是整数化模型 FO 在 WikiText-2 上困惑度比 BF16 高 8.3%、贪心 token 一致率 86.3%。

> **TL;DR:** Sable is the first single-GPU cryptographic prover that bit-exactly certifies a full production LLM: on one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell it proves all 64 layers of Qwen2.5-32B for a 4,096-token sequence in 92.5 s (16,384 tokens in 397.5 s), a 41–50× overhead over the integer serving engine and 40–64× less time per parameter and token than the zkLLM baseline (LLaMa-2-13B on A100, 803 s for 2,048 tokens). It does this by fixing an integer-only function F_O (8-bit matrix products, table-looked-up softmax/SiLU, deterministic seeded sampling) and proving it wrap-free over the 31-bit KoalaBear prime with chunked LogUp (chunks < 2^30 lookups) and a streamed two-pass prover with a device-resident Fiat–Shamir transcript. The honest price: on WikiText-2, F_O's perplexity is 8.3% above BF16's (5.580 vs 5.151, where simulated FP8 loses only 0.7%), greedy-token agreement is 86.3%, and on MMLU/GSM8K it stays within 1.4/0.6 points of BF16. Proofs are 134.8–477 MiB and non-zero-knowledge, so as built this is an auditor's tool for open-weight models, with recursive compression (BLAKE3 circuit at 2.8 µs per compression) as future work. Code is MIT-licensed.

## 核心贡献

- **可证明的整数化 transformer(第 5 节)**:定义 F_O——一个无任何浮点步骤的整数推理函数,覆盖 Qwen2/Qwen3 架构(RMSNorm、GQA、RoPE、门控 SiLU MLP、QK-norm、128 专家 MoE 路由),每一次舍入、归一化、softmax 和采样都由输入完全决定;由确定性程序从 BF16 检查点(QuaRot 式随机旋转 + 8 位列指数量化)自动生成。
- **31 位小素域上的"无回绕"约束系统(第 6 节)**:KoalaBear 素数 p = 2^31 − 2^24 + 1 上,每个 gadget 都附带整数界,保证域上关系与整数关系等价(定理 6.1/6.2);所有静态查表进入一张 2^26 行的带标签表。
- **分块 LogUp + 域合并(第 7 节)**:证明超过 p 次查表(16K 序列约 7×10^11 次)时把查表切成 ≤2^30 的块各配独立重数列,一张 GKR 统一证明所有块(定理 7.2);"域"把零检查与 LogUp-GKR 叶层合并进单个 sumcheck,声明从输出向嵌入反向流动,证明器两遍流式扫描、每层重算见证。
- **单 GPU 证明器(第 9 节)**:逐约束程序生成的 sumcheck 内核、GPU 常驻的 Fiat–Shamir transcript(每 warp 一次 BLAKE3 压缩,证明过程零主机往返)、同构层 CUDA graph 回放;见证与权重全部装进一个由 rate-1/2 Reed–Solomon + 混合高度 BLAKE3 Merkle 树 + Fp8 上 BaseFold 组成的多线性承诺。
- **面向递归的验证器(第 10 节)**:公列闭式求值、定表承诺、最终打开不进 transcript 等手段把验证器哈希量降 4.2 倍(1×4K 证明从 17.8M 降到 4.2M 次 BLAKE3 压缩),证明体积从 360 MiB 降到 134.8 MiB;并给出同一证明系统内的 BLAKE3 电路(每压缩 2.8 µs)。
- **诚实的精度与开销账本(第 11 节)**:系统量化 F_O 相对 BF16 / FP8 / SmoothQuant INT8 的困惑度、MMLU、GSM8K 差距并逐项归因(8-bit Q/K 是主要损失源;Qwen3 上免费的 Q/K 指数分配即可消除 23–53% 困惑度差距),另有逐项安全预算(轮轮可靠性误差 2^−103)。

## Key Contributions

- **A provable integer transformer (Sec. 5):** F_O, an integer-only function for Qwen2/Qwen3-style decoders (up to QK-norm and 128-expert MoE routing) in which every rounding, normalization, softmax and sampling decision is input-determined; a deterministic program maps a BF16 checkpoint to F_O via a QuaRot-style random orthogonal rotation fused into the weights, 8-bit matrix products with power-of-two scales kept off the reduction axis, and correctly-rounded lookup tables (exp, SiLU, rsqrt, recip) pinned by a circuit digest.
- **A wrap-free constraint system over a 31-bit field (Sec. 6):** every gadget carries explicit integer bounds under which the F_p relation is equivalent to the relation over Z (Theorem 6.1) and has exactly one satisfying output (Theorem 6.2) — closing the classic small-field loophole where a prover could "wrap" mod p and still verify; all static lookups go through one tagged table of 2^26 rows.
- **Chunked LogUp (Sec. 7.3):** the observation that LogUp only proves inclusion modulo the field characteristic, so >p lookups are unsound; lookups are split into chunks of at most 2^30 with per-chunk multiplicity columns and one GKR over the table proves all chunks — necessary because a 16K proof makes ~7×10^11 lookups (>300p).
- **Domains (Sec. 7.2):** the zerocheck and the leaf layer of all LogUp-GKR trees of a set of columns are folded into a single sumcheck of degree 8–10, with challenges in F_p4.
- **A streamed, single-GPU prover (Sec. 9):** two passes over the model (commit pass forward keeping only the residual stream; prove pass backward recomputing each layer's witness), a device-resident Fiat–Shamir transcript (one warp per BLAKE3 compression, no host round trips), CUDA-graph replay of identical middle layers, and generated per-constraint sumcheck kernels; all tensors and weights live in one multilinear commitment (rate-1/2 Reed–Solomon, BLAKE3 Merkle trees of mixed height, mixed-length BaseFold over the degree-8 extension).
- **A recursion-affordable verifier (Sec. 10):** public columns evaluated in closed form, fixed tables pre-committed, unhashed final openings and shared Merkle paths cut verifier hashing 4.2× (17.8M → 4.2M BLAKE3 compressions for a 4,096-token proof) and the proof from 360 to 134.8 MiB; a BLAKE3-in-circuit gadget (80 columns per G-call, LogUp memory wiring) proves compressions at 2.8 µs each as the first recursive component.
- **An honest evaluation (Sec. 11):** full accuracy accounting of F_O against BF16 and two deployed 8-bit schemes with paired bootstrap intervals, per-step accuracy attribution (which integer step loses what), a scan showing F_O is never undefined on 3.45M tokens of real text plus 160 adversarial sequences, message-level corruption tests, and a pre-implementation cost model checked against measurements.

## Methodology

The statement proved is: given a weight commitment C_W, a decoding policy, a prompt x and a client nonce n_c, the committed integer model F_O produced exactly the claimed tokens y with seed r = SHA256(seed ∥ n_c ∥ C_W ∥ SHA256(x) ∥ policy), including greedy or inverse-CDF sampling and the stopping rule (so truncated answers cannot verify). Because F_O is deterministic and causal, all P+G−1 positions are proven in one teacher-forced pass.

**The integer function F_O.** The residual stream holds 24-bit integers (A = [−2^23, 2^23)); matrix-product inputs are 8-bit with one power-of-two exponent per activation row (dynamic, Requant8) and per weight column (static); scales live off the reduction axis so the accumulator Σ t_amt·b_nt is an exact integer with |acc| < 2^29 and the combined scale is applied once by a shift. Nonlinearities become correctly-rounded table lookups (τ_exp for softmax over a 2^23-entry table, τ_SiLU, τ_recip, τ_rsqrt with 16-bit mantissas); RMSNorm is integer NormScale with a shared rsqrt table; RoPE is RoPE8 on 8-bit inputs; attention computes scores, a per-row max, 10-bit probabilities, a blocked P·V product (blocks of 2^12 keep partial sums < 2^29 for contexts up to 2^20) and an exact division by the softmax denominator via τ_recip. MoE routing is argmax-top-k with a renormalized expert softmax, and all k selected experts are folded into one requantized row so a single down-projection accumulator serves all slots. Calibration runs every vocabulary token at position 0, widens exponents as needed, and refits weight columns whose shifts would leave range (for Qwen2.5-32B this changes only 134 of 458,752 QKV columns).

**The proof system.** Each operation is a gadget with committed witness columns, row-wise polynomial constraints and lookups into the tagged 2^26-row table; the design rule is that every relation has both sides below p in absolute value (≤2^30.9), so F_p equality implies integer equality — this is what makes the small field safe. Domains merge the zerocheck and the LogUp leaf layer into one sumcheck; static lookups are chunked (≤2^30 per chunk) with one GKR over the table proving all chunks' multiplicities simultaneously; claims flow backward from the sampling gadgets to the embedding, so the prover makes exactly two passes and never holds more than one layer's witness (a 16K trace is ~6×10^11 committed elements and 7×10^11 lookups — far beyond GPU or host memory). Everything is committed in one multilinear commitment built from a rate-1/2 Reed–Solomon code over multiplicative subgroups, BLAKE3 Merkle trees of mixed height, and BaseFold openings over F_p8, batched across tensor sizes. Fiat–Shamir uses a custom sequential BLAKE3 mode (argued indifferentiable) run entirely on the GPU; soundness is 2^−103 round-by-round in the random-oracle model, counting a 30-bit proof of work on queries, with every term of the budget tabled.

**Engineering.** Sumcheck round kernels are generated per constraint program; the first round runs on base-field tables; F_p4 multiplies use one modular reduction per coordinate; one middle layer's prove pass is captured as a CUDA graph and replayed for the other layers; the prover peaks at 36.8 GiB GPU / 101 GiB host for 1×4K. A companion Triton serving engine computes F_O bit-for-bit (verified token-by-token) so the 41–50× overhead is measured against an engine that actually serves the proven function.

## Results & Highlights

- **End-to-end proving (Qwen2.5-32B, 64 layers, one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell):** 92.5 ± 0.0 s for one 4,096-token sequence, 253.1 s for four sequences batched in one proof, 397.5 s for one 16,384-token sequence; per-token cost is nearly flat (22.6 vs 24.3 ms/token from 1×4K to 16K). Qwen3-32B (QK-norm) proves 4,096 tokens in 121.8 s and the MoE Qwen3-30B-A3B in 84.7 s.
- **vs. prior systems:** 0.48–0.75 ps per parameter and token against zkLLM's 30.2 ps for LLaMa-2-13B on an A100 (803 s / 2,048 tokens) — 40 to 64× less, of which the authors honestly attribute part (roughly 3.5×) to having more CUDA cores; the concurrent FlashProve (Llama 4 Maverick, 400.7B MoE, H100) achieves 0.56 ps per active parameter and token, i.e. 6–11× less than Sable's MoE proofs, but at 1,024-token context and without checking sampling.
- **Overhead and cost:** a proof takes 41–50× the time of the F_O serving engine for the same work (about 90× against FP8 in SGLang); at $2.50/GPU-hour a 4,096-token proof costs 6.4 cents, a 16K proof 28 cents.
- **Accuracy (the price of exactness), Qwen2.5-32B:** on WikiText-2, F_O perplexity 5.580 vs BF16 5.151 — +8.3% excess (paired 95% CI 7.3–9.5%), against +0.7% for simulated FP8 and +1.6% for SmoothQuant INT8; greedy-token agreement with BF16 86.3% (13.7% of positions differ); on MMLU 81.8% vs BF16 83.2% (−1.4 pts, where FP8/INT8 lose ≤0.2), on GSM8K 88.0% vs 88.6%.
- **Accuracy attribution:** 12-bit queries/keys alone remove 37% of the WikiText-2 perplexity gap and raise agreement to 88.6%; a float softmax, +4-bit SiLU and 16-bit up-projection each move <3% (and none of these variants is provable as it stands — 12-bit Q/K would cost 1.30×/1.68× proving time at 4K/16K by the cost model). On Qwen3, the free Q/K exponent split removes 53% (Qwen3-30B-A3B) and 23% (Qwen3-32B) of the excess perplexity, leaving F_O within 1.6 pts of BF16 on MMLU and 1.7 pts on GSM8K on both models.
- **Proof size and verification:** proofs are 134.8 / 239.0 / 345.7 MiB at the three shapes; verifier hashing was cut 4.2× (17.8M → 4.2M BLAKE3 compressions for a 4,096-token proof) and a compiled-core verifier checks all three models' proofs on one CPU core in 19–56 s (18.8–40.2 s for the three Qwen2.5 shapes).
- **Robustness of F_O's definition:** over the full WikiText-2 test split, all 1,319 GSM8K prompts, 2,000 random MMLU prompts and 160 adversarial 4,096-token sequences (3.45M+ tokens), F_O never hit any of its undefinedness conditions — the honest prover would never have aborted.
- **Serving F_O:** the exact integer engine decodes Qwen2.5-32B at 27.5 tok/s at batch 1 (vs 25.3 FP8 / 21.0 BF16 in SGLang on the same GPU) and 428.5 tok/s at batch 32 (79% of FP8's 542.0).

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Tomer Solberg, at MoonMath.ai (a small research group/startup focused on cryptography), with acknowledged external discussants (Omer Shlomovits, Miki Asa, Karthik Inbasekar, Suyash Bagad). The paper is unusually self-punishing for a systems paper: it quantifies its own accuracy loss against two deployed 8-bit quantization baselines with paired bootstrap confidence intervals, runs a message-by-message corruption test suite (every corrupted proof must be rejected, including targeted forgeries of no-wrap bounds, QK-norm head rows and the MoE router), verifies that the GPU transcript and graph-replayed prover produce byte-identical proofs to the host versions, and compares measurements to a cost model written before implementation. The prover, verifier and the scripts behind every table are released under MIT (github.com/moonmath-ai/Sable), and the README maps each command to each table. This is an arXiv preprint (cs.CR, cross-listed cs.AI/cs.LG, Nov 2026) and not yet peer-reviewed; the protocol-level proofs (LogUp over prime fields, chunk soundness, wrap-freedom, determinism) are given in appendices but, unlike the concurrent FlashProve (proved in Lean), are not machine-checked.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** genuine and substantial — the combination of (i) a fully-specified integer semantics for a served 32B-class LLM including sampling and stopping, (ii) proven wrap-freedom over a 31-bit field with per-gadget integer bounds, and (iii) chunked LogUp for >p lookups is new, and the 40–64× improvement over zkLLM is a real systems milestone. The chunking observation (LogUp proves inclusion only modulo the characteristic) is a crisp technical contribution beyond engineering.
- **Rigor:** strong on protocol (explicit round-by-round soundness budget of 2^−103 with every term tabled, forgery tests that specifically target wraps the bounds exclude) and honest about scope (non-zero-knowledge, ~1.9×10^4 proofs leak the weights, auditor-scale 106–477 MiB proofs). One caveat: the Fiat–Shamir transcript uses a custom, non-standardized sequential BLAKE3 mode whose indifferentiability is argued from the compression function being ideal rather than from a standardized mode — defensible but an extra trust surface.
- **Weak spots in the evaluation:** (1) The FP8/SmoothQuant baselines are *simulated* by quantize-dequantize in BF16 rather than run as deployed kernels, which slightly flatters the comparison against F_O's measured serving engine. (2) The headline zkLLM comparison mixes hardware with methodology — the authors concede ~3.5× of the 40–64× is CUDA-core count, and the systems prove different statements (zkLLM is zero-knowledge over BLS12-381 with approximate non-linearities), so the "×" is an upper bound on the algorithmic gap. (3) The Qwen3-32B 4×4K runs show an unexplained 462/482/512 s spread the authors "did not locate" — a small but candidly-admitted reproducibility wart. (4) The accuracy-attribution variants (12-bit Q/K, unrounded down input) are evaluated but none is provable as they stand, so the paper cannot report both best accuracy and proving cost in one configuration; the best provable tweak (9-bit down input) improved perplexity/MMLU but *lowered* GSM8K, a drop the authors admit they could not separate from answer-style changes.
- **Data quirks worth flagging:** calibration uses 1,024 tokens of "English technical text" that is literally the beginning of the LaTeX spec of Sable itself — a charming but arbitrary choice (they show WikiText-2 recalibration changes nothing, which mitigates it); and on Qwen3-32B GSM8K, 527 of 1,319 F_O answers switch into a step-by-step style vs 92 for BF16, meaning a chunk of the "accuracy loss" is style flips, not arithmetic errors — the paper dissects this rather than hiding it.
- **Reproducibility:** excellent — MIT code, per-table commands, warm-run protocols, ±std over three runs, Nsight profiles, and a stated ~2% run-to-run variance. The cost-model-vs-measurement gap (2.45× at 1×4K, outside the model's own [0.5×, 2×] band at the small shape, attributed to latency-bound small sumchecks) is disclosed rather than tuned away.
- **Significance:** this moves verifiable inference from "GPT-2-scale demo" to "production 32B model on one GPU," but as built it is an auditor's instrument for open weights, not a per-request consumer proof — the missing pieces (zero knowledge, full recursion, sub-MiB proofs) are all still future work, and the 8.3% perplexity gap means the audited function is noticeably degraded relative to the deployed BF16 model.

## Strengths

- First bit-exact proof of full production-size LLM inference (Qwen2.5-32B / Qwen3-32B / Qwen3-30B-A3B) on a single GPU, with the whole model — every rounding, normalization, softmax, sampling and stopping decision — inside the proved statement.
- Principled small-field design: wrap-freedom proven per gadget rather than assumed, and chunked LogUp that stays sound when lookup counts exceed the field characteristic by 300×.
- Serious prover engineering with measured ablations: device-resident transcript, CUDA-graph replay, per-program kernels, per-layer reduction — Table 10 traces the 92.5 s result through ~20 individually measured optimizations, including negative results that were reverted.
- Exemplary honesty: quantifies the accuracy cost of exactness against strong 8-bit baselines, attributes it step by step, reports where it could not locate a discrepancy, releases all measurement scripts.
- The verifier is explicitly designed for the missing recursion (4.2× less hashing, closed-form public columns, a working BLAKE3 circuit at 2.8 µs per compression), rather than punting compression entirely.

## Limitations

- **Not zero-knowledge:** proofs reveal trace evaluations; ~1.9×10^4 proofs suffice to recover the weights by linear algebra, so closed-weight serving is out of scope until ZK layers exist.
- **Auditor-scale proofs:** 106–477 MiB per proof and 19–56 s single-core verification — impractical to attach to every API response; recursion is only begun (the BLAKE3 circuit's trace commitment is not yet measured).
- **Accuracy gap:** +8.3% perplexity vs BF16 on WikiText-2 (5.580 vs 5.151) with 86.3% greedy agreement — much worse than FP8's +0.7%; ~63% of the gap is unexplained by the ablations (8-bit activations/V are the suspects), and the remedies that work are either unprovable or cost 1.3–1.68× more proving time.
- **Overhead:** 41–50× over the F_O engine (~90× over FP8 SGLang), and F_O's own serving engine is 2.7× slower than FP8 SGLang at batch-32 prefill, so a verifiable deployment pays at both ends.
- **MoE cost:** per-active-parameter proving time (3.36–6.27 ps/pt) is 6–11× worse than FlashProve because attention and the 30.5B-weight commitment do not shrink with the selected experts.
- **Soundness scope:** random-oracle model with proven proximity bounds, but no machine-checked protocol proof; F_O is a partial function, and although no undefined input was found, an adversary with the weights could search for one (the server may then refuse, detectably).
- **Generality:** evaluated on Qwen-family decoders only; other architectures "change the gadget composition," which is asserted rather than demonstrated.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中"极致性能优化下的可靠性"方向:它把激进低精度(8-bit 整数量化)推理的数值正确性问题变成可密码学验证的命题,逐 gadget 证明约束不回绕、量化/舍入/采样每一步都确定,正是量化误差与数值栈可信性研究的硬核形态;同时"可验证推理"为推理侧静默损坏(服务端换权重、截断回答、偷换采样随机性)提供了一种不依赖信任的检测手段。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the remaining ~63% of F_O's WikiText-2 perplexity gap be closed with provable formats (e.g., 9-bit down input adopted, or score rounding before the exponential table) at bounded proving-cost inflation, and would that also fix the GSM8K style-flip penalty?
- How much would sliding-window attention in most layers shrink the 16K proof, given that every causal score entry costs one committed probability, one lookup and two constraint terms?
- What is the end-to-end cost of the full recursive stack — 4.2M BLAKE3 compressions at 2.8 µs is ~12 GPU-seconds of circuit work per compression batch, but the trace commitment of the BLAKE3 circuit is unmeasured; does the recursion with F_p-friendly algebraic hashes (further assumption) become cheaper than keeping BLAKE3 everywhere?
- Can a hiding weight commitment be layered on without destroying the plain-Merkle "anyone can recompute C_W from the open checkpoint" property that makes the registry trust-free today?
- Does the wrap-free-bounds methodology transfer to other small-field proofs of numeric programs (e.g., scientific kernels, fixed-point RL rollouts), where the same "relation holds mod p but not over Z" forgery class exists?
- The 8.3%-perplexity F_O is what gets audited — for a regulator comparing an auditor's proof against a black-box API, what detection rate could statistical fingerprinting (TOPLOC-style) achieve on the same budget, and where exactly does the cryptographic guarantee pay for its 50× overhead?
