> **一句话总结：** 本文指出"巨大激活"（massive activations，来自注意力汇聚机制的离群维度）使残差流高达 99.9% 的方差集中在第一主成分 PC1 上，而 PC1 在 TopK 稀疏目标下没有唯一的稀疏分解——每个训练种子都用一组不同的"贴砖"特征重建 PC1，导致 SAE 最常用的两大评测（消融重要性、方差解释率）同时失真：GPT-2 上前 50 个最重要特征平均有 47 个跨种子不可复现（随机 50 个仅约 27 个无匹配），Qwen-2.5-7B 的 SAE 报告解释 99.96% 方差却留下 33% 的 PC1 之外"内容方差"未解释（低估 890 倍）；作者提出把 PC1 从 SAE 输入中剔除、推理时再加回的 bypass 方案，使受影响模型的前 50 特征跨种子共享率从 4–39.5% 升至 73–99.5%，同等 k=64 稀疏度下内容重建误差最多降低 33.9%（Qwen-2.5-7B），模型拼接代价最多降低 43.8%。

> **TL;DR:** Massive activations (attention-sink outlier dimensions) concentrate up to 99.9% of residual-stream variance on PC1, and because an isolated rank-one signal has no unique sparse decomposition under TopK, independently seeded SAEs "tile" PC1 with different features that then dominate ablation-importance rankings — on GPT-2 (SAEs trained on 5M WikiText-103 tokens at the middle block, TopK k=64, width 4d), 47 of the 50 most important features fail to recur across seeds vs only ~27 unmatched for a random 50, and dictionary-wide sharing (47–63%) inverts at the top (5–11%). Reported FVU also hides content error by the dominance factor 1/(1−w1): Qwen-2.5-7B SAEs report 99.96% variance explained while leaving 33.0% of content (non-PC1) variance unexplained — an 890× understatement, echoed in released dictionaries (median content/total FVU = 98.8 for unfiltered Qwen Scope, 2.1 for Gemma Scope 2). The fix — train the SAE on PC1-removed activations and add PC1 back at inference — lifts top-50 sharing to 73–99.5%, cuts held-out content FVU by up to 33.9% (Qwen) at the same k=64 budget, and reduces splice-in cross-entropy cost by up to 43.8% (Qwen, 0.115 → 0.065 nats/pred) versus the standard TopK SAE; a post-hoc variant (drop features with PC1 alignment ≥ 0.01 from the ranking) reaches 77.5–96% sharing with no retraining. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示 SAE 评测的双重失效根源**：证明巨大激活（attention sink 的离群维度）使中间层残差流 70–99.9% 的方差落在 PC1 上；由于孤立的秩一信号在 TopK 目标下存在无穷多组精确稀疏分解（附录 C 给出论证），不同种子的 SAE 用不同的"贴砖（tiling）"特征重建 PC1，这些贴砖特征占据了重要性排名的顶端——最重要特征恰恰最不可复现，与直觉相反。
- **量化两大评测指标的失真程度**：(1) 重要性指标——五个强各向异性模型（GPT-2、Qwen-2.5-7B、Pythia-160M/1.4B/6.9B）中前 50 重要特征平均 43.5–48 个跨种子无匹配，而随机抽 50 个仅 19–27 个无匹配；top-50 共享率与 PC1 占比的 Pearson 相关达 −0.93。(2) 保真度指标——推导并验证 FVU_content/FVU_total ≈ 1/(1−w1)（在所有模型上相对误差 <0.2%），报告的"99.96% 方差已解释"可以掩盖 33% 的内容方差误差（890 倍低估）。
- **建立特征的三分类taxonomy**：按解码器与 PC1 的对齐度 cos²(d_j, v1) 把特征分为普通特征（对齐 <0.01，按字典基线共享率复现）、贴砖特征（中间对齐 0.01–0.6，共享率仅 0–2.6%，每模型 38–106 个、约占存活特征 1%，却承担 2–66 倍于其数量占比的消融重要性）、典范特征（对齐 ≥0.6，凡出现必跨种子复现）。
- **定位成因并给出三种修复**：(1) 给 TopK 加幅度惩罚 β‖z‖₁（β=0.3 时 GPT-2 top-50 共享率 6.4%→94.9%，L0 与重建几乎不变）；(2) PC1 bypass——在剔除 PC1 的激活上训练 SAE、推理时加回，top-50 共享率 4–39.5%→73–99.5%，内容 FVU 全面下降；(3) 事后过滤——把 a_j ≥ 0.01 的特征移出重要性排名池（仅剔除 0.72–3.92% 的存活特征），无需重训即可达 77.5–96% 共享率。
- **界定混淆的边界与普适性**：ReLU+ℓ1 目标完全不受影响（各模型 top-50 共享率 95–100%）；Gemma-2 两模型（PC1 占比仅 0.70/0.78）无此反转；混淆延伸到已发布字典（Gemma Scope 2 内容/总 FVU 比中位数 2.1，未过滤 Qwen Scope 高达 98.8，过滤极端范数激活后降至 ~1.03）、视觉 Transformer（DINOv2-base 总 FVU 0.013 vs 内容 FVU 0.343）以及 crosscoder（Pythia-1.4B top-50 共享 3–4%→83–93%）。
- **开源**：代码（github.com/nathandelisle/massive-saes）与全部 SAE 字典（huggingface.co/nathandelisle/massive-saes）已发布。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies massive activations as the source of SAE evaluation failure.** Massive activations drive 70–99.9% of middle-layer residual-stream variance onto PC1; since an isolated rank-one component admits infinitely many exact sparse reconstructions under TopK (formal argument in Appendix C), each training seed "tiles" PC1 with a different feature set, and these seed artifacts rise to the top of ablation-importance rankings.
- **Quantifies both standard evaluations being confounded.** Importance: in five highly anisotropic models, 43.5–48 of the top-50 features by zero-ablation importance fail to recur across seeds, versus 19–27 unmatched for a random 50; top-50 sharing anti-correlates with PC1 dominance (Pearson r = −0.93). Fidelity: FVU_content/FVU_total ≈ 1/(1−w1), verified to within 0.2% relative error — a Qwen-2.5-7B SAE can report 99.96% variance explained while leaving 33.0% of content variance unexplained (890× understatement).
- **A three-category feature taxonomy by PC1 alignment** (cos² of decoder with v1): ordinary features (<0.01) share at dictionary baseline; tiles (0.01–0.6; 38–106 per model, ~1% of alive features, but 2–66× their share of total ablation importance) share at 0.0–2.6%; rare canonical features (≥0.6) recur in every run where present.
- **Cause verified by intervention.** Adding a magnitude penalty β‖z‖₁ to TopK raises GPT-2 top-50 sharing from 6.4% to 94.9% (β=0.3) with <1% change in L0 (64.00→63.47) or content FVU (0.1394→0.1387); canonical initialization raises it from 5.5% to 68%. Across objectives, TopK/BatchTopK/JumpReLU show the inversion while ReLU+ℓ1 does not (95–100% sharing everywhere).
- **Two repairs, one retraining-free.** PC1 bypass (train on PC1-removed activations, add PC1 back at inference) lifts top-50 sharing from 4–39.5% to 73–99.5% and cuts held-out content FVU by 0.8–33.9% at identical k=64 sparsity; post-hoc exclusion of features with a_j ≥ 0.01 from the ranking reaches 77.5–96% sharing while removing only 0.72–3.92% of alive features from an existing dictionary.
- **Scope established beyond the lab setting.** The confound appears in released dictionaries (median content/total FVU: 98.8 unfiltered Qwen Scope → ~1.03 after extreme-norm filtering; 10.9→1.03 for OpenAI's GPT-2 SAEs in original vs normalized space), in DINOv2-base vision activations, and in crosscoders; it disappears where PC1 dominance is low (Gemma-2, Pythia-1.4B final layer, Llama-3.1-8B with the BOS position excluded: 97%→3%).

## Methodology

**Setup.** TopK SAEs (Gao et al., 2025) trained on residual-stream activations at the middle transformer block of GPT-2, Pythia-70M/160M/1.4B/6.9B (deduped), Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B/70B, and Gemma-2-2B/9B, over 5M WikiText-103 tokens; dictionary width m = 4d, sparsity k = 64, Adam, 20k steps, batch 4096, decoder renormalized to unit norm; only initialization varies across seeds. Cross-seed reproducibility uses the Paulo & Belrose (2025) decoder-similarity criterion: a feature is "shared" if max cosine with any decoder direction in another run is ≥ 0.7.

**Metrics.** FVU = E‖x−x̂‖²/E‖x−x̄‖²; content FVU is the same ratio after projecting both x and x̂ orthogonal to PC1 (v1 from centered training activations). Feature importance I_j is the cross-entropy increase from zeroing feature j in the SAE reconstruction, measured relative to the model running the non-ablated SAE. Splice cost is the cross-entropy increase from replacing activations with their SAE reconstruction.

**Diagnosis.** (1) Rank features by importance and compare top-50 sharing vs dictionary-wide sharing; regress against PC1 variance share w1. (2) Group features by PC1 alignment to isolate tiles vs canonical features; verify that ablating only the PC1-orthogonal component of a top feature reproduces 88–100% of the full ablation damage — the damage comes from breaking cancellation between tiles outside PC1, not from removing PC1 itself. (3) Derive and empirically verify the dominance-factor approximation for FVU.

**Causal test.** Because TopK penalizes only which features fire (not magnitudes), partially aligned tiles need larger activations to contribute the same PC1 signal; an ℓ1 penalty on activations therefore prefers aligned/canonical features. Tested at β ∈ {0, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3} on GPT-2 (5 seeds), plus canonical-feature initialization, dictionary-width doubling, and an added coefficient penalty for Gated SAEs (whose magnitude parameters normally receive no sparsity gradient).

**Repairs.** Bypass: train on x − (v1·x)v1, and at inference add the removed PC1 component (computed directly from the input, outside the sparsity budget) plus the training mean back before feeding the model — preserving the attention-sink signal while freeing SAE capacity (on massive-activation tokens a standard SAE spends ~63 of its 64 coefficients on PC1-aligned features in GPT-2/Pythia-1.4B/Qwen). Post-hoc: exclude a_j ≥ 0.01 features from importance rankings of already-trained dictionaries. Evaluated on held-out content FVU, splice cost, automated interpretability (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct ↔ Mistral-Small-24B-Instruct as explainer/scorer, roles swapped), sparse probing (SST-2, AG News, Amazon Polarity, Emotion), and behavioral steering on subject–verb agreement (32,000 constructed prompts).

**Generalization probes.** Released dictionaries (Gemma Scope 1/2, Llama Scope, EleutherAI Pythia, Qwen Scope, OpenAI GPT-2); layer variation in Pythia-1.4B; DINOv2-base patch activations with BatchTopK SAEs and register tokens; crosscoders on Pythia-1.4B activation pairs.

## Results & Highlights

- **Top-50 cross-seed sharing (importance-ranked features, GPT-2):** only 5.5% shared across seeds vs a 63% dictionary-wide sharing baseline — i.e., 47 of the 50 most important features unmatched vs ~27 unmatched for a random 50; across the five PC1-dominated models, mean pairwise top-50 sharing is 5–11% vs dictionary-wide 47–63%.
- **Reported vs actual fidelity (Qwen-2.5-7B, middle block, WikiText-103 held-out):** 99.96% of total variance explained while 33.0% of content (non-PC1) variance is unexplained — the reported total FVU understates content error by ~890×; the dominance-factor prediction FVU_content/FVU_total ≈ 1/(1−w1) holds to within 0.2% relative error on all measured models.
- **PC1 bypass repair:** top-50 sharing rises from 4–39.5% to 73–99.5% in affected models; held-out content FVU falls by 33.9% on Qwen-2.5-7B (and 0.8–4.9% on the other models, e.g., −2.2% GPT-2, −4.9% Llama-3.1-8B) at the same TopK k=64 budget.
- **Downstream splice cost (nats/prediction, 256 held-out validation sequences):** Qwen-2.5-7B standard SAE 0.11548 → bypass 0.06485, a 43.8% reduction; other models improve 0.6–10.6% (GPT-2 −8.3%, Pythia-6.9B −10.6%, Gemma-2-9B −2.8%).
- **Magnitude-penalty fix (GPT-2, 5 seeds):** top-50 sharing 6.4% → 94.9% at β=0.3 while mean L0 moves 64.00 → 63.47 and content FVU 0.1394 → 0.1387 (<1% change).
- **Objective sweep (Table 1, top-50 sharing %):** on Qwen-2.5-7B TopK 6.0 / BatchTopK 33.0 / JumpReLU 32.0 vs ReLU+ℓ1 97.0; on GPT-2 5.5 / 17.5 / 11.5 vs 100.0 — the inversion is specific to magnitude-agnostic objectives; ReLU+ℓ1 exceeds its dictionary-wide rate in every model.
- **Released dictionaries:** median content/total FVU ratios of 98.8 for unfiltered Qwen Scope drop to ~1.03 after removing ~0.8% extreme-norm activations; OpenAI's GPT-2 dictionary is 10.9 in raw activation space but 1.03 in its normalized space; Gemma Scope 2 sits at 2.1.
- **Beyond language models:** DINOv2-base block-8 BatchTopK SAEs (3 seeds): total FVU 0.013 vs content FVU 0.343 (~26× gap; PC1 = 96.2% of patch variance); bypass improves content FVU to 0.333 vs 0.344 for a random-direction control. Crosscoders on Pythia-1.4B: top-50 sharing 3–4% → 83–93% with PC1 bypass.
- **Steering (subject–verb agreement, 5 features):** standard top-50 features achieve 0% edit success on GPT-2/Pythia-1.4B/Qwen-2.5-7B; post-hoc selection gives 9.0%/31.9%/4.2% and bypass 7.1%/37.5%/2.0% (Gemma-2-2B constant at 64–65%); automated-interpretability AUROC on Pythia-1.4B rises from 0.551→0.732 (Qwen explaining, Mistral scoring) and 0.669→0.743 with roles swapped.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: high and well-scoped.** Seed-instability of SAE features was known (Paulo & Belrose 2025 reported ~70% feature churn; Sun et al. 2025 noticed PC1-aligned dense latent pairs in Gemma Scope), but this paper is the first to (a) explain *why the most important features are the least stable* via the rank-one tiling argument, and (b) show the same PC1 dominance silently corrupts the *fidelity* metric through the 1/(1−w1) dominance factor. The connection to massive activations/attention sinks is convincing and mechanistic rather than correlational.
- **Rigor: strong overall, with honest controls.** The authors themselves report the control that weakens the generality of the *importance* half of the claim: under support-preserving resample ablation, GPT-2's top-50 sharing is already 83.0% *before* bypass (though Pythia-1.4B remains at 27.5%), so part of the observed instability is specific to the zero-ablation importance metric rather than the features themselves. They disclose this in Section 6.1/Appendix B.4 rather than hiding it — a good sign.
- **The "up to 44%" headline deserves scrutiny.** The splice-cost table (Appendix E.4, Table 32) uses a single seed (seed 42) and 256 validation sequences per model; the 43.8% reduction is the Qwen-2.5-7B best case, while the median reduction across the nine models is ~4.7% and Gemma-2-9B gains only 2.8%. The abstract's "degrades the model up to 44% less" is technically accurate but cherry-picks the extreme model.
- **Downstream utility evidence is mixed.** Sparse probing shows no average benefit (<1 percentage point across SST-2/AG News/Amazon Polarity/Emotion, per-model deltas −2.44 to +3.13); automated-interpretability AUROC *falls* on Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-2B after bypass; and steering success after repair remains low in absolute terms (9.0% GPT-2, 4.2% Qwen, and Pythia-160M stays at 0% even post-repair). The reproducibility and content-FVU gains are solid, but the practical "you get better features" story rests mainly on Pythia-1.4B steering (31.9–37.5%) and one interpretability result.
- **Missing ablations/settings.** Main experiments fix k=64, width 4d, the middle block, and a single 5M-token WikiText-103 training set — quite small by modern SAE standards (public dictionaries train on orders of magnitude more data), so tile counts and sharing rates at other sparsities/widths/sites/data scales are only partially explored (layer variation only in Pythia-1.4B; one equal-budget variant in Appendix E.3). The interaction with decoder-normalization choices and with much sparser production dictionaries (e.g., Gemma Scope's low-L0 variants) is underexamined.
- **Reproducibility: good.** Code, SAEs, seeds, and precise metric definitions (cos ≥ 0.7 sharing threshold, FVU conventions) are released; the NeurIPS checklist is filled out with limitations pointed to Section 6.3 and Appendix C.
- **Significance: substantial for evaluation practice.** Even if one discounts the behavioral results, the FVU dominance-factor finding alone changes how released-dictionary fidelity numbers should be read (Qwen Scope's 98.8× content/total ratio is an actionable warning), and the post-hoc filter is a zero-cost fix any SAE user can apply today.
- **Red flags: minor.** Reliance on a single seed for the splice-cost headline; the two Gemma models show the repair barely matters there (0.8–1.2% content-FVU gain), which the authors state plainly rather than overclaiming.

## Strengths

- A crisp mechanistic explanation (rank-one signals have no unique sparse decomposition under magnitude-agnostic objectives) backed by a formal argument, targeted interventions that confirm it (ℓ1 penalty, canonical init), and a clean three-way feature taxonomy (ordinary / tiles / canonical) that makes the phenomenon directly observable in any trained dictionary.
- The FVU dominance-factor identity FVU_content/FVU_total ≈ 1/(1−w1) is simple, derived, and verified to 0.2% — an immediately usable diagnostic for anyone evaluating SAEs, requiring only the PC1 variance share.
- Genuine breadth: ten LMs (70M–70B), five public dictionary families, vision transformers (with register-token comparisons), crosscoders, multiple SAE objectives (TopK, BatchTopK, JumpReLU, Gated, ReLU+ℓ1), and layer-wise analysis, with negative results (Gemma inversion-free, final-layer Pythia unaffected) reported rather than suppressed.
- Practical, graduated remedies: a training-free post-hoc ranking filter, a cheap loss tweak, and full PC1 bypass — each with quantitative trade-offs (post-hoc top-50 mean ΔCE 0.0130 on GPT-2 vs 0.0141 for retrained bypass, both far below the confounded 0.0966).
- All code and trained dictionaries released; accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

## Limitations

- The importance-side confound is partly an artifact of zero-ablation as the importance metric: their own support-preserving resample control yields 83% top-50 sharing pre-bypass on GPT-2 (though only 27.5% on Pythia-1.4B), so how much of the instability lives in the features vs the metric is model-dependent and not fully resolved.
- Downstream benefits are uneven: no average sparse-probing gain, interpretability AUROC drops on two of eight models, steering success after repair stays ≤ 37.5% and near zero on GPT-2/Qwen; Pythia-160M is not repaired at all for steering.
- Narrow training regime in the main experiments: one site (middle block), k=64, width 4d, 5M WikiText-103 tokens, WikiText-based evaluation — production SAEs use far more data and wider sparsity ranges; tile statistics and bypass gains may differ there (the released-dictionary analysis partially mitigates this).
- PC1 bypass changes the learned features, so results from bypassed dictionaries are not directly comparable to the standard SAE literature; the post-hoc variant avoids this but leaves the dictionary's content-FVU problem unsolved.
- The splice-cost headline (43.8% on Qwen) rests on a single seed and 256 sequences; medians across models are far smaller (~4.7%).
- Only the first principal component is treated; models whose variance is spread over several rogue dimensions (a situation the Gemma models hint at) may need a multi-direction generalization, which is not tested.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是关于 SAE（稀疏自编码器）评测方法学与表征几何（PC1/巨大激活主导性）的核心工作，凡用消融重要性选特征、用 FVU 报告保真度的人都需要知道这一混淆及其零成本修复（事后过滤 a_j ≥ 0.01）。它也与"可靠性"兴趣相关——展示了被广泛引用的评测数字（如 Qwen Scope 的保真度）可能系统性低估 2–98 倍的真实误差，本质上是一种评测层面的"静默失效"。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the confound persist at the much sparser regimes (L0 ≪ 64) and larger training corpora used by production dictionaries like Gemma Scope, or does tile formation diminish with data scale?
- Can the bypass idea generalize to removing the top-m principal components (m > 1) for models whose variance is spread across several rogue dimensions rather than a single PC1?
- How does PC1 tiling interact with meta-SAEs, attribution dictionaries, and cross-layer training beyond the four Pythia-1.4B pairs tested — do stacked/cross-layer methods inherit or amplify the instability?
- Since support-preserving resample ablation largely removes the instability on GPT-2 (83% sharing pre-bypass), could a resample-based importance metric replace zero-ablation in feature-circuit workflows (Marks et al., 2025) without any retraining?
- Do canonical features (alignment ≥ 0.6) have interpretable content, and could canonical initialization plus a small magnitude penalty combine bypass-level reproducibility with better reconstruction than either alone?
- Is the 890× fidelity understatement actionable for downstream users of Qwen Scope-style dictionaries — e.g., does content-FVU error correlate with interpretability-pipeline failures (autointerpret AUROC, probing) once PC1 is accounted for?
