> **一句话总结：** 在无风扇 MacBook Air M2 上实测 Qwen2.5-Coder 全系 24 个 GGUF 量化文件在 542 个 EvalPlus 任务上的求解数与能耗，发现在 2 GB 预算处选 3B-q4_K_M 即可（366 题对 7B-q2_K 的 363 题，无显著差异），而 7B-q2_K 每解出一题要多花 2.03 倍电能——"更大模型+更低精度更好"的经验法则在此失效。

> **TL;DR:** An independent researcher benchmarked all 24 shipped GGUF files of Qwen2.5-Coder-Instruct (0.5B–7B, fp16/q8_0 down to q2_K) on 542 EvalPlus tasks (HumanEval+ 164 + MBPP+ 378) on a fanless MacBook Air M2, pricing each file in joules per solved task via rail-power logs. The result is a budget-indexed download guide: at 1 GB take 1.5B-q4_K_M (58.3% pass, 39.3 J/solve) and at 2 GB take 3B-q4_K_M (67.5%, 69.4 J/solve); the "larger model at lower precision" rule fails at the 2→3 GB step, where 7B-q2_K (3.16 effective BPW, below Song et al.'s ~3.5 threshold) matches 3B-q4_K_M's solves (363 vs 366, McNemar p=0.836) but needs 2.03x the energy per solve (141.2 vs 69.4 J; confirmed at 1.925 by five alternating-session pairs). A broken registry file (3B-q3_K_M, 7/542 solved, 4,708 J/solve) is also flagged as a trap.

## 核心贡献

- **按文件大小预算的选型指南**：以 Ollama 列出的文件体积（0.5/1/2/4/8 GB）为索引，给出每个预算下解题最多、能耗最低的量化文件，且每条建议都附带证据标签（重复实验支持 / 正确性检验 / 单次会话点估计）。
- **锚定步骤（2→3 GB）的定量否证**：7B-q2_K 相比 3B-q4_K_M 解题数无显著提升（363 对 366，精确 McNemar 检验 p=0.836，90% 置信区间把 3B 的劣势限制在 2.4 个百分点内），但每解一题的能耗是 2.03 倍（CPU/GPU/内存轨），整机口径下为 1.66–1.81 倍——"更大模型低精度更优"的规则在这一步不划算。
- **q4_K_M 从 1.5B 起即可放心用**：在 1.5B、3B、7B 三个规模上，q4_K_M 与该规模最高精度文件（fp16 或 q8_0）的差距在 3 个百分点以内（90% CI），而每题能耗显著更低（如 1.5B 上 39.3 J 对 fp16 的 69.1 J）。
- **按"解出一题"而非"每 token"计价**：证明每 token 能耗排名会误导选型——1.5B-q4_K_M 每 token 是 0.5B-q3_K_M 的 1.98 倍，但每解一题两者只差 1.2%；四个按 token 留在前沿的 0.5B 文件在按题计价的前沿上落选。
- **可复现的完整产物**：发布全部逐题正确性向量、逐题能耗、功率日志、文件摘要（digest）与分析脚本（Zenodo），脚本可从哈希校验的输入复现论文中的每一个数字。

## Key Contributions

- **A selection guide indexed by listed file size** (C1): for budgets of 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 8 GB, the guide names the best-solving file among the 24 tested, its J/solve, its in-budget runner-up, and an evidence label stating whether the pick rests on the paired repeat, a significance test, or an unreplicated point-estimate energy margin.
- **The 2→3 GB "anchor step" priced and repeated** (C2): 3B-q4_K_M solves 366/542 tasks vs 363 for 7B-q2_K (exact McNemar p=0.836; 90% CI on the difference −2.40 to +3.51 points), yet 7B-q2_K needs 2.03x the rail energy per solved task (95% CI 1.91–2.17), 1.66–1.81x under whole-system accounting, and 1.925 (95% CI 1.718–2.157) across five alternating HumanEval+ session pairs.
- **A regenerable price list for 24 shipped files** (C3): effective bits per weight (from runtime load logs, not tag names), pooled pass rate, J/solve and mJ/token for every file, with file digests so re-pushed registry tags can be detected.
- **A per-solve accounting argument**: energy per output token and per solved task diverge enough to change the frontier (four 0.5B files drop off), so leaderboards should print solve counts beside per-token energy.
- **A documented registry trap**: the 3B-q3_K_M tag ships a file that solves only 7/542 tasks at 4,708 J/solve — 68x its q4_K_M sibling — while ranking 14th of 24 per token, motivating smoke-tests of downloads and effective-BPW labeling by registries.

## Methodology

The study measures every GGUF file of Qwen2.5-Coder-Instruct at four sizes (0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B) that the Ollama registry lists across the k-quant ladder: q3_K_M, q4_K_M, q5_K_M, q6_K, q8_0 at all sizes, fp16 at 0.5B–3B (7B fp16 would need ~15 GB on the 16 GB machine), and q2_K only at 7B — 24 files total, each identified by digest. All files ran on Ollama 0.32.6 (llama.cpp on Metal) on a fanless MacBook Air M2 with 16 GB unified memory under macOS 26.5 on AC power, greedy decoding (temperature 0, fixed seed, repeat penalty 1.1), 2,048-token context, 768-token output cap, one sample per task, in a fixed order with one session per benchmark (the "main grid").

Correctness is measured on 542 EvalPlus tasks (HumanEval+ 164 + MBPP+ 378 pooled); a task counts as solved when its sanitized solution passes both base and plus tests, verified by serial re-evaluation of stored solutions. Energy comes from the macmon tool sampling CPU, GPU and memory rails once per second; marginal power is rail power above a 0.509 W idle median, and J/solve is pooled marginal energy divided by pooled solves, with 95% task-bootstrap intervals (B=100,000) and a whole-system-power view under three idle choices. Statistics use exact McNemar tests on discordant tasks with Holm correction (over all 276 file pairs for post hoc picks), 90% CI equivalence/non-inferiority at a ±3-point margin, and a pre-set 40% margin rule for headlining energy gaps. Because greedy decoding reproduced outputs byte-for-byte in every repeat, a paired repeat of the anchor pair (five alternating AB/BA blocks of 3B-q4_K_M vs 7B-q2_K on HumanEval+, with 5-minute cooldown gates to 45 °C) isolates session effects on energy: geometric mean of per-pair J/solve ratios with a 95% t-interval on log R, under pre-registered validity and flagging rules.

## Results & Highlights

- **Anchor step (RQ3)**: On the 542-task EvalPlus pool, 3B-q4_K_M solves 366 tasks (67.5%) vs 363 (67.0%) for 7B-q2_K — no significant difference (exact McNemar p=0.836; 90% CI limits any 3B deficit to 2.4 points) — yet 7B-q2_K costs 141.2 J per solved task (95% CI 131–153) vs 69.4 J (64–75) for 3B-q4_K_M, a ratio of 2.03 on rails (95% CI 1.91–2.17) and 1.66–1.81 under whole-system power; the five-pair HumanEval+ repeat gives 1.925 (95% CI 1.718–2.157) vs 1.956 in the main grid.
- **Within-size ladder (RQ1)**: From 1.5B up, q4_K_M trails the highest precision measured at its size by less than 3 points (90% CI): fp16 minus q4_K_M is −0.2 points [−2.6, +2.2] at 1.5B (equivalent) and −1.3 [−3.1, +0.6] at 3B (non-inferior); q8_0 minus q4_K_M is +0.7 [−1.1, +2.6] at 7B (equivalent); meanwhile q4_K_M costs 39.3 vs 69.1 J/solve at 1.5B, 69.4 vs 116.1 J at 3B, and 145.7 vs 187.1 J at 7B.
- **Budget picks (RQ2)**: 1.5B-q4_K_M at 1 GB solves 316 tasks (58.3%) at 39.3 J/solve; 3B-q4_K_M at 2 GB solves 366 (67.5%) at 69.4 J/solve, leading the 1.5B pick by 9.2 points (all-pairs Holm p<0.001) for 1.76x its energy per solve; 7B-q6_K at 8 GB solves 403 (74.4%) — its gain over the 2 GB pick stays significant under Holm over all 276 pairs (p=0.010) but costs 2.64x the energy.
- **Efficiency of the 2 GB pick**: 3B-q4_K_M reaches 90.8% of the best score measured (7B-q6_K, 74.4%) at 38% of that file's energy per solve; along the frontier each extra solved task costs about 259 J from 1.5B-q4_K_M to 3B-q4_K_M but about 1,077 J from 3B-q4_K_M to 7B-q4_K_M (~4x more).
- **Broken-file trap**: the 3B-q3_K_M registry file solves 7 of 542 tasks (1.3%) at 4,708 J/solve — 68x the energy per solve of 3B-q4_K_M — while ranking 14th of 24 files per output token, showing per-token rankings can mask non-functional files.
- **Per-token vs per-solve divergence**: 1.5B-q4_K_M costs 1.98x 0.5B-q3_K_M per output token, but the two sit within 1.2% per solved task, and four 0.5B files kept by a per-token frontier fall off the per-solve frontier.
- **Speed came free with the cheaper file**: in the paired repeat, 3B-q4_K_M decoded 2.03x as fast as 7B-q2_K (95% CI 2.00–2.06) and 7B-q2_K took 1.98x the wall time, so the cheaper pick gave up no response time.
- **Sanity anchor**: 7B-q8_0 scores 84.1% on HumanEval+ and 69.3% on MBPP+, in line with the official Qwen2.5-Coder technical report.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is single-authored by Aditi Patodiya, an independent researcher, and is submitted to GREENS 2027 (the 11th International Workshop on Green and Sustainable Software at ICSE 2027) — a workshop venue, not yet peer-reviewed at publication time. The author discloses that AI assistance (Claude, Anthropic) was used to implement the measurement and analysis scripts and to draft the manuscript under her direction, with the author claiming responsibility for design, verification against raw data, and content. The measurement hygiene is unusually careful for a single-author study: file digests pin registry artifacts, correctness was re-verified by serial re-evaluation (covering all failures plus a 3% sample of passes, flipping 9 outcomes, all fail→pass), repeat/validity/flag rules were fixed before the paired run in released scripts, and a Zenodo artifact (doi 10.5281/zenodo.24016482) regenerates every number in the paper from hash-checked inputs. The work builds directly on the author's prior registry-screening study (arXiv:2609.05881, "Broken on arrival"), which supplied the tuning/correctness observations for the same runs.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Moderate but genuine. Per-file (not per-model) energy-per-solved-task pricing with exact paired tests and a budget-indexed selection guide is a real increment over prior work (Cunha et al., Alizadeh et al. stop at q4 and report per-token/per-workload energy), and the 7B-q2_K anchor-pair falsification of the "bigger-at-lower-precision" rule at a specific budget is a crisp, reusable finding.
- **Rigor**: Strong on statistics (exact McNemar, Holm over all 276 pairs for post hoc picks, byte-identical greedy repeats, pre-registered repeat rules, equivalence testing with sensitivity checks over margins 2.6–3.1 points and thresholds 33–40%), but weakened by energy measurement provenance: rail power comes from Apple's IOReport estimates read by macmon, with no external power meter, and the rails cover only 28–53% of system power, so absolute joules are not comparable to RAPL/NVML studies and the paper rightly limits itself to ratios on one machine.
- **Reproducibility**: Very good — digests, released harness, a script that rewrites every number, and a fresh-directory rerun on the study machine claimed to reproduce results. The fixed-order main grid (size and recipe aligned with time of day, one session per benchmark per file) is the main caveat; only the anchor pair was repeated, so most cross-size energy comparisons rest on single sessions and the 40% headline rule is an exploratory screen set after the grid.
- **Significance**: Practical rather than scientific: it tells developers which Ollama tag to pull at each size budget (the 1 GB and 2 GB picks happen to be the registry defaults) and tells registries/leaderboards to print effective BPW and solve counts. External validity is narrow — one code family, one runtime, one fanless Apple machine, Python-only tasks, one zero-shot prompt, greedy decoding, 24 of 60 registry recipes (no q4_K_S/q4_0, no 14B).
- **Red flags / honest quibbles**: The 3-point equivalence margin and 40% rule were chosen after seeing the data (acknowledged in Threats); all five repeat blocks were flagged for heavy thermal pressure (90–97% of every block), so no unflagged subset exists; pair 1 overlapped macOS background work (post hoc exclusion gives 2.005); the broken 3B-q3_K_M file is kept in all statistics, inflating the 3B whole-ladder cost to +64.9 points; and under system power the 1.5B-q4_K_M frontier spot rests on one solved task over 1.5B-q6_K. AI-assisted implementation/drafting by a single independent author lowers (but does not eliminate) confidence in unspotted measurement bugs, though the artifact mitigates this.

## Strengths

- Prices exactly the decision a developer makes — one downloaded file under a size budget — instead of abstract model-level comparisons.
- Exact paired statistics task-by-task (McNemar + Holm over all pairs) rather than eyeballed score gaps; every guide pick carries an explicit evidence label admitting what is unresolved.
- The alternating-session paired repeat is well designed (cooldown gates, pre-registered validity rules, AB/BA balance) and directly supports the paper's headline claim (ratio 1.925 vs main-grid 1.956).
- Per-solve vs per-token accounting comparison exposes a concrete failure mode of per-token leaderboards (four 0.5B files; the 3B-q3_K_M trap ranking 14th/24 per token but 24th/24 per solve).
- Full artifact with digests, power logs, and a numbers-regenerating script; effective-BPW reporting per tag is a useful, adoptable recommendation.

## Limitations

- One machine (fanless MacBook Air M2), one runtime (Ollama 0.32.6/llama.cpp Metal), one model family (Qwen2.5-Coder-Instruct), Python-only benchmarks, a single zero-shot prompt, and greedy decoding with one sample per task — picks may move for other families, runtimes, cooling designs, languages, or sampling regimes.
- Energy from estimated IOReport counters without external validation; rails cover 28–53% of system power, so absolute joules are machine-relative only.
- The main grid runs each file in one session per benchmark in a fixed order, confounding size/recipe with time of day; only the anchor pair is repeated, leaving most energy rankings unreplicated below the 40% margin.
- Post hoc statistical choices (3-point margin, 40% rule) and a flagged thermal environment for all repeat blocks; 24 of 60 registry recipes covered (no q4_K_S, q4_0, or 14B files that list under 8 GB).
- The broken 3B-q3_K_M file is retained in every aggregate, which distorts the "whole ladder cost" reading at 3B; correctness data is reused from the author's prior screening study rather than regenerated.

## Relevance

对关注高效 LLM 推理与量化的研究而言，这篇论文提供了一个罕见地把"量化文件选型"与"每解出一题的能耗"直接挂钩的实测基准和可复用的选型指南，其"按 solved task 而非按 token 计价能耗"的方法论对绿色 AI 与端侧推理（Ollama/llama.cpp 生态）的评估工作有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the picks (q4_K_M sweet spot from 1.5B up; 3B-q4_K_M as the efficiency knee) transfer to other code families (DeepSeek-Coder, CodeLlama, Qwen3-Coder), other runtimes (vLLM, MLX, llama.cpp CPU-only), and actively cooled or desktop-class hardware?
- How does the picture change under sampling-based decoding (temperature > 0) or agentic multi-turn use, where outputs no longer repeat byte-for-byte and repeats must buy correctness precision as well as energy precision?
- Would a 14B-q2_K or 14B-q3_K_M file at the 8 GB budget beat 7B-q6_K's 74.4% pass rate, i.e., does the larger-model rule fail again one rung higher on the ladder?
- Can the effective-BPW vs tag-name discrepancy (e.g., "q4_K_M" files storing 4.91–5.08 effective bits) be standardized into registry metadata, and does Song et al.'s ~3.5 BPW performance threshold hold for coder families below 7B?
- What does the per-solve energy picture look like on other languages (beyond Python EvalPlus) and for repo-level tasks (SWE-bench-like), where longer generations shift the per-token vs per-solve trade-off?
