> **一句话总结：** ETH Zurich 的 HARE 通过在剖析时故意压缩 DRAM PRE-to-ACT 时序（fastHammer）来加剧读扰动，从而把单行 RDT（读扰动阈值）剖析加速 31.2%、模块最小 RDT 平均压低 21.7%，并在 180 颗真实 DDR4 芯片上验证它翻转 RowHammer 所翻转的 >95% 的单元且保持行排序，使在线剖析器在 36 种配置下系统性能提升 0.9%–54.4%。

> **TL;DR:** Read disturbance (RowHammer/RowPress) mitigations must be configured with the read disturbance threshold (RDT), but RDT drifts unpredictably over time, and exhaustive online profiling of a 16-bank DDR4 module takes hundreds of seconds. HARE intentionally violates tRP between PRE and ACT commands ("fastHammer") so the first bitflip appears at a lower activation count: on 180 real DDR4 chips (25 modules, 4 manufacturers) via DRAM Bender, fastHammer cuts per-row RDT by 27.0–33.5% and profiles a row 31.2% faster than the conventional RowHammer profiler, while flipping >95% of the same cells and keeping the RowHammer-weakest row within the top 5.6% of its ranking. In a 1M-trial Monte Carlo error model, adding HARE-Direct online profiling drops the uncorrectable-error probability from 99.9% (no profiler, mean time to failure 8.5 min) to 6.2% (66.8% → 0.67% at 20% guardband); in Ramulator 2.0 over SPEC CPU2006/2017, TPC, MediaBench, and YCSB mixes, HARE beats the conventional RowHammer profiler by 0.9–54.4% geomean weighted speedup across PRAC/Graphene/PARA mitigations.

## 核心贡献

- **首次刻画"降时序加剧读扰动"现象（fastHammer）**：在 180 颗商用 DDR4 芯片（25 根内存条，涵盖 SK Hynix、Micron、Samsung、Nanya 四大厂商）上，系统性证明把双面锤击序列中的 PRE-to-ACT 延迟从标称值压到 7.5–12 ns，可以让每行 RDT 平均下降 27.0%–33.5%（依厂商不同），且降低越激进、RDT 下降越多。
- **证明 fastHammer 是 RowHammer 的"超集"且保序**：fastHammer 在所有测试时序下翻转 RowHammer 所翻转单元的 95% 以上（7.5 ns 时 95.7%，15 ns 时 98.9%），翻转单元数最多达 RowHammer 的 3.9 倍；同时行级 RDT 排序基本保持——每个模块 RowHammer 最弱行都落在 fastHammer 排序的前 5.6% 以内（12 ns 时只需前 0.7%）。
- **提出首个在线 RDT 剖析技术 HARE**：两个变体——HARE-Direct 直接报告 fastHammer 测得的保守最小 RDT（天然自带保护带）；HARE-Filter 先用 fastHammer 给一批行排序、只对排名最前的少量行用常规 RowHammer 重剖析，从而恢复真实的最小 RowHammer RDT。两者都实现为类似刷新的周期性剖析，持续更新底层缓解机制的 RDT 配置。
- **建立可变读扰动（VRD）的概率错误模型**：基于实测分布参数化、1M 次蒙特卡洛试验，证明仅做一次性剖析的静态配置系统在 99.9% 的试验中出现不可纠正错误（平均失效时间仅 8.5 分钟），要把错误率压到接近零需要 50% 的保护带（RDT 减半）；HARE-Direct 把无在线剖析器系统在 20% 保护带下的错误率从 66.8% 降到 0.67%。
- **端到端系统评估**：在 Ramulator 2.0 上跨 36 种配置（PRAC/Graphene/PARA 三种缓解 × 三种 RDT 值 × 四种剖析间隔），HARE-Direct 与 HARE-Filter 相对常规 RowHammer 剖析器分别提升 0.9%–54.4% 与 1.5%–47.5% 的几何平均加权加速比，并给出硬件开销（37.2 KiB 存储、0.17 mm² 面积）与能耗（省 34.8%）分析。

## Key Contributions

- **First characterization of read disturbance under reduced timing constraints as a profiling accelerator.** Using the DRAM Bender FPGA infrastructure on 180 COTS DDR4 chips (25 modules) from SK Hynix, Micron, Samsung, and Nanya, the authors show that shrinking the PRE-to-ACT latency (tRP) in a double-sided hammer pattern ("fastHammer") reduces per-row RDT by 33.5% / 28.4% / 30.81% / 27.0% on average at 7.5 ns for SK Hynix / Micron / Samsung / Nanya respectively, and shifts the whole RDT distribution toward lower values (medians drop 16.6–28.2% at 7.5 ns).
- **Evidence that fastHammer disturbance is a superset of RowHammer disturbance.** Cell overlap with RowHammer-flipped cells exceeds 95% at every tested latency (95.7% at 7.5 ns, 98.9% at 15 ns), and fastHammer flips up to 3.9× more cells. The authors hypothesize (supported by prior 3D TCAD simulations) that shorter PRE-to-ACT gaps leave more trap-emitted electrons near the just-closed aggressor wordline for the opposite aggressor's field to sweep into the victim cell.
- **Ranking preservation enables filtered reprofiling.** In all 25 modules, the RowHammer-weakest row lands within the top 5.6% / 3.4% / 1.6% / 0.7% of the fastHammer ranking at 7.5 / 9 / 10.5 / 12 ns, so the module's true minimum RowHammer RDT can be recovered by exhaustively reprofiling only a small candidate head of the ranking.
- **HARE, the first online RDT profiling technique.** HARE-Direct reports the minimum fastHammer RDT (a conservative estimate that inherently guardbands the mitigation); HARE-Filter ranks batches of rows by fastHammer RDT and reprofiles only the top rows with the conventional RowHammer pattern. Both run as periodic profilers (analogous to refresh windows) that keep tightening the configured RDT of the underlying mitigation.
- **A probabilistic error model for variable read disturbance.** Parameterized from the empirical characterization and run over 1M Monte Carlo trials per configuration, the model shows one-time (static) profiling is unreliable — 99.9% of simulated banks hit an uncorrectable error with MTTUE of 8.5 minutes at zero guardband, and only a 50% guardband (halving the configured RDT) brings P(UE) near zero (0.5%).
- **End-to-end evaluation of reliability, performance, energy, and cost.** Across 36 configurations spanning three state-of-the-art mitigations (PRAC, Graphene, PARA), three RDT values (4K/1K/256), and four profiling intervals, HARE beats the conventional RowHammer profiler in every configuration; HARE-Filter costs only 37.2 KiB of storage and 0.17 mm² of synthesized area.

## Methodology

**Real-chip characterization (Sections 4–5).** The authors use DRAM Bender, an FPGA-based DDR4 testing platform, to issue DRAM commands with cycle-level timing control to 180 COTS DDR4 chips in 25 modules (H1–H6 SK Hynix, M1–M10 Micron, S1–S7 Samsung, N1–N2 Nanya; 4–16 Gb densities, x8/x16 organizations). To isolate circuit-level read disturbance they (1) disable periodic refresh so in-die TRR cannot mask flips, (2) bound test execution inside one refresh window so retention failures do not interfere, (3) verify the modules have neither rank-level nor on-die ECC, and (4) reverse-engineer the logical-to-physical row mapping. RDT is measured for a double-sided pattern (hammer cycle = ACT/ PRE to the two aggressors around a victim) with a bisection search terminated at 1% relative tolerance, repeated 5 times per row with the minimum reported. Tests use the worst-case data pattern among 0x00/0xFF/0xAA/0x55 (aggressors filled, victims negated) at 80 °C, sampling 3,000 rows per bank (1,000 each from the beginning, middle, and end). The fastHammer pattern simply violates tRP — issuing the next ACT 7.5–12 ns after PRE instead of the nominal latency. They deliberately avoid going below 7.5 ns because prior work shows multiple rows then activate simultaneously with different bitflip characteristics (different leakage paths), which would no longer profile RowHammer vulnerability. fastHammer is additionally validated on 10 HBM2 chips.

**HARE design (Section 6).** Per row: initialize victim+aggressor patterns, double-sided fastHammer to a target hammer count, read back the victim to check for bitflips. The hammer count search starts from H_init = 2× the previous profiling window's module minimum (10K initially), grows additively with a doubling step (Δ=10K), shrinks by factor β=1.2 if the initial count already flips, then bisects geometrically to tolerance ε=1% (≥10 hammers), repeated N_search=5 times with the minimum taken. HARE-Direct reports the module's minimum fastHammer RDT directly (conservative since fastHammer lowers RDT, e.g., by 21.7% average at the chosen 9 ns operating point). HARE-Filter profiles a batch (N_profile_batch), re-profiles the top-ranked rows (N_reprofile) with the true RowHammer pattern, and reports their minimum. Both are periodic: one profiling operation per bank every t_PROFI, sized so all rows are covered within a profiling window t_PROFW, analogous to refresh. The chosen deployment configuration is 9 ns PRE-to-ACT, balancing RDT/hammer-time reduction against cell- and row-overlap quality.

**Reliability modeling (Section 7).** A Monte Carlo simulator draws per-row RDTs and per-row drift ratios from the empirical distributions, assumes drift only lowers effective RDT (conservative), models a generic counter-based mitigation with RDT_conf = min(measured)×(1−g) for guardband g, distributes bitflips uniformly within a row, and declares an uncorrectable error when 2+ flips land in one 128-bit word under (136,128) on-die SEC ECC. Each of 1M trials simulates a 65,536-row bank under a pessimistic benign access pattern that hammers random victims' aggressors up to 10^14 activations, tracking P(UE ≤ t) and MTTUE (restricted mean).

**System evaluation (Section 8).** Ramulator 2.0 cycle-level simulation of a 4-core, 4.2 GHz system with DDR5 (1 channel, 2 ranks, 64K rows/bank), running 10 quad-core mixes of memory-intensive workloads (>10 row-buffer misses/kilo-instruction) from SPEC CPU2006, SPEC CPU2017, TPC, MediaBench, and YCSB. HARE (both variants) is compared against a conventional RowHammer profiler the authors construct (same periodic scheme, nominal-timing pattern) — a necessary baseline since no prior online RDT profiler exists — across 36 configurations (PRAC/Graphene/PARA × RDT 4K/1K/256 × four profiling intervals). Energy is measured on a real DDR4 module at the VDD and VPP supply rails; HARE-Filter's lowest-RDT table is synthesized in Verilog for area/latency.

## Results & Highlights

- **Per-row profiling latency:** For a hammer count of 4K, fastHammer profiles a DRAM row's RDT **31.2% faster** than the conventional RowHammer access pattern (measured with tightly scheduled DRAM commands), and reduces a module's minimum RDT by **21.7% on average at 9 ns** (28.3% at 7.5 ns) across all 25 modules — directly shrinking both the per-hammer time and the number of hammers needed to trigger the first bitflip.
- **Reliability (Monte Carlo, 1M trials):** A mitigation configured from one-time profiling with zero guardband hits an uncorrectable error in **99.9% of trials with MTTUE of only 8.5 minutes**; static configurations need g=50% (halving RDT) to reach P(UE)=0.5%. HARE-Direct with a 6.75 ms profiling interval cuts P(UE) to **6.2% at g=0%** (vs 99.9% no-profiler) and **0.67% at g=20%** (vs 66.8% no-profiler); HARE-Filter reduces P(UE) by up to **12.1×** vs no profiler (750-row batch) — though at 750-row batches its P(UE) is 33.3% higher than HARE-Direct's due to slower RDT updates.
- **System performance (Ramulator 2.0, SPEC/TPC/MediaBench/YCSB mixes):** Across all 36 configurations (PRAC, Graphene, PARA × RDT 4K/1K/256 × four profiling intervals), HARE-Direct improves geomean weighted speedup over the conventional RowHammer profiler by **0.9%–54.4%** and HARE-Filter by **1.5%–47.5%**; the largest gain is PRAC at RDT=256 with t_PROFI=0.5 ms, where HARE-Direct reaches **54.4%** (HARE-Filter 39.7%, up from 12.5%/8.8% at t_PROFI=4 ms), and HARE-Direct cuts total execution time by **35%** in that configuration.
- **Fidelity of fastHammer to RowHammer (180 DDR4 chips):** cell overlap with RowHammer-flipped cells is **>95% at every tested PRE-to-ACT latency** (95.7% at 7.5 ns, ≥98% at ≥9 ns; 98.8% on HBM2 at 10 ns), fastHammer flips up to **3.9× more cells**, and the RowHammer-weakest row of every module falls within the top **5.6%** of the fastHammer ranking at 7.5 ns (top 0.7% at 12 ns; top 0.1% in HBM2).
- **Energy and hardware cost:** hammering to 32K RowHammer-equivalent hammers costs **273 µJ with fastHammer vs 419 µJ with RowHammer (34.8% savings)** measured at the supply rails; HARE-Filter's per-bank lowest-RDT table costs **37.2 KiB storage, 0.17 mm² area, 260 ns worst-case update** (off the memory-controller critical path). Scaling with bank density: HARE-Direct's advantage over the RowHammer profiler grows from 47.7% at 64K rows to **89.5% at 128K rows** (PRAC), and stays at ~48% even when only 25% of a 256K-row bank is profiled.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper comes from the SAFARI Research Group at ETH Zurich, led by Onur Mutlu, whose group practically defined the empirical RowHammer literature — the original "Flipping Bits in Memory Without Accessing Them" (ISCA 2014), RowPress (ISCA 2023), Revisiting RowHammer (ISCA 2020), and the variable-read-disturbance study this paper builds on. Co-authors (Olgun, Luo, Yüksel, Kakolyris, et al.) are the core team behind many of these DRAM characterization works and the DRAM Bender infrastructure used here. The paper thanks anonymous HPCA 2026 reviewers, indicating a top-tier architecture venue submission. The experimental base — 180 real DDR4 chips across all four major DRAM manufacturers plus 10 HBM2 chips, on an FPGA platform with refresh disabled, on-die-ECC verified absent, and reverse-engineered row mapping — matches the methodological rigor the group is known for. Funding sources (Google Security and Privacy Research Award, Microsoft Swiss Joint Research Center) are disclosed.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuinely strong.** Reduced-timing DRAM testing itself is not new (the paper itself cites two prior works,Refs. [103,105], that reduce timing parameters), but nobody previously asked whether the resulting bitflips *preserve RowHammer's* vulnerable cells and row ranking — the cell/row-overlap analysis across 25 modules is the key novel evidence, and HARE is legitimately the first online RDT profiler. The counterintuitive design principle ("make the disturbance worse so you can measure the real threshold faster") is a real idea, not an increment.
- **Rigor: high on hardware, model-dependent on system claims.** The characterization is exemplary (WCDP discipline, 5× repeated bisection, interference elimination, physical row mapping reverse-engineered). But two headline numbers rest on modeling rather than measurement: the reliability numbers come from a Monte Carlo model whose access pattern is *benign-by-construction* (the authors' own footnote 5 concedes an adversary with oracular knowledge of vulnerable rows could yield higher error rates), and the 0.9–54.4% speedups are Ramulator simulations of DDR5 parameterized by DDR4 measurements — no end-to-end hardware prototype of the online profiler is demonstrated. The performance baseline (the "RowHammer profiler") is also constructed by the authors since no prior online profiler exists, so the comparison is HARE vs a strawman-of-necessity rather than a state-of-the-art system.
- **A real internal tension the paper handles honestly but does not resolve:** HARE-Filter measures the true RowHammer RDT more accurately yet *increases* P(UE) by 33.3% vs HARE-Direct (750-row batch) because batched updates delay configuration tightening — meaning the more accurate profiler is the less safe one, and the ranking-preservation property it relies on degrades exactly where fastHammer helps most (top-50 row overlap drops from 99.0% at 12 ns to 91.5% at 7.5 ns). The chosen 9 ns operating point is a sensible compromise, but the safety argument for HARE-Filter's candidate-set completeness rests on the single-weakest-row statistic, not on coverage of the top-N weak rows.
- **Reproducibility: good.** Standard patterns (0x00/0xFF/0xAA/0x55), 80 °C, bisection tolerance, and all simulator parameters (Table 2/3: 65,536 rows, 45 ns tRC, (136,128) SEC ECC, guardband sweep 0–50%, 6.75–128 ms intervals) are stated. HARE's hyperparameters (H_init=10K doubling, Δ=10K, β=1.2, ε=1%, N_search=5) are disclosed. DRAM Bender and Ramulator 2.0 are the group's released tools. That said, the characterization samples 3,000 rows per bank, not all 65,536 — and their own Fig. 4 shows the minimum RDT does not stabilize below 9K rows, so measured "module minimums" are lower bounds of what full profiling would find.
- **Significance: high if adopted.** The paper convincingly shows static RDT configuration is untenable (99.9% UE at g=0) and that profiling speed is the binding constraint for online protection, which PRAC-era systems genuinely face. The discussion of memory-controller tRP programmability (Intel TC_PRE, AMD configurable tRP) and in-DRAM deployment via Self-Managing DRAM makes the path to practice plausible.
- **Red flags: modest.** (1) No adversarial access pattern in the error model; (2) DDR4-only empirical base for a DDR5-simulated system, with HBM2 in characterization only; (3) 80 °C single temperature point for the main results (temperature sensitivity is not swept in the main text); (4) HARE-Direct's conservative estimate silently increases mitigation preventive actions — quantified as negligible (<1%) for PRAC/Graphene but "noticeable" for PARA, i.e., the worst-case mitigation pairing amplifies the cost.

## Strengths

- Scales a real-hardware study to 180 DDR4 chips from all four major manufacturers plus 10 HBM2 chips, giving the fastHammer observations strong cross-manufacturer generality (27.0–33.5% RDT reduction everywhere).
- The core insight is elegant and doubly beneficial: violating tRP both shortens each hammer cycle and lowers the activation count needed for the first bitflip, attacking both sources of profiling cost simultaneously.
- The cell-overlap (>95%) and row-ranking (weakest row in top 5.6%) analysis is exactly the evidence needed to justify using an "incorrect" disturbance pattern as a profiling proxy — the paper proves the substitution is sound rather than assuming it.
- The probabilistic VRD error model turns a vague worry ("RDT drifts") into a quantitative design argument: static profiling has 8.5-minute MTTUE, and halving the configured RDT is the only static fix — a strong motivation for any online profiler.
- Comprehensive evaluation surface: reliability (1M Monte Carlo trials), performance (36 simulator configurations × 3 mitigations), energy (real supply-rail measurements), hardware cost (synthesized Verilog), and bank-density scaling up to 256K rows/bank.
- Honest reporting of unfavorable results — HARE-Filter's higher P(UE) than HARE-Direct, and the row-overlap degradation at aggressive latencies, are stated plainly rather than buried.

## Limitations

- The error model uses a benign uniform-random access pattern and assumes drift only lowers RDT and that bitflips persist without scrubbing; adversarial workloads with knowledge of weak rows (acknowledged by the authors) could invalidate the 6.2%/0.67% P(UE) numbers.
- The system-performance results are cycle-level simulations (Ramulator 2.0, DDR5) seeded by DDR4 measurements; no prototype memory controller implements HARE, and fastHammer was not system-evaluated on HBM2 despite being characterized there.
- Row-overlap quality degrades with more aggressive latency reduction (top-50 coverage 91.5% at 7.5 ns) and HARE-Filter's batch structure delays RDT updates, so the accuracy-focused variant is the reliability-worse variant — a trade-off without a dominated solution.
- Characterization covers 3,000 rows per bank at a fixed 80 °C with WCDP only; the authors' own data (Fig. 4) shows module-minimum RDT has not stabilized by 9K rows, and temperature/data-pattern sensitivity of the fastHammer-overlap property is not explored in the main results.
- fastHammer cannot go below 7.5 ns PRE-to-ACT (multi-row activation changes bitflip characteristics), capping the achievable speedup; the 31.2% single-row profiling speedup is bounded by that floor.
- Deployment relies on memory-controller (or in-DRAM) support for issuing commands with reduced tRP and on avoiding PRAC-counter pollution during profiling — feasible per the cited industry mechanisms, but integration cost is argued rather than measured.

## Relevance

这项工作直接命中"DRAM/内存系统的可靠性与安全"方向：它把 RowHammer/RowPress 缓解的 RDT 配置问题转化为"如何快速在线剖析"的系统问题，并给出真实芯片刻画 + 错误建模 + 系统仿真的全链路方案，对研究硬件安全、内存可靠性以及近内存架构的人都值得精读。其"故意加剧扰动以加速测量"的思路对其他需要在线阈值剖析的硬件可靠性问题（如保持时间剖析）也有方法论上的借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the fastHammer cell-overlap and row-ranking properties behave under adversarial access patterns that adapt to the profiler's schedule, and under temperature/voltage corners other than 80 °C nominal?
- Can HARE-Filter's delayed-update weakness be fixed (e.g., streaming per-row updates for the top-ranked rows as they complete) so that accuracy no longer costs reliability?
- Does the reduced-tRP exacerbation effect persist at modern DDR5 / LPDDR5 / HBM3 process nodes, where the paper's own motivation says read disturbance is worst, and where nominal tRP is already much shorter than in DDR4?
- What is the interaction between HARE profiling traffic and on-die ECC / in-die TRR in production chips — can an in-DRAM HARE (via Self-Managing DRAM) reliably read raw cell data without ECC masking weak flips?
- Could the Monte Carlo error model be extended with scrubbing, non-uniform access locality, and multi-tenant adversarial attackers to give a worst-case rather than benign P(UE) bound?
- Is there a principled way to pick the PRE-to-ACT operating point (or adaptively vary it) per module to jointly optimize profiling speed, cell coverage, and ranking fidelity, rather than the fixed 9 ns empirical choice?
