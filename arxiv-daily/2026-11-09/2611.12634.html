Methodology

  • The identity under test. KL(base ‖ quantized) ≈ ½ΔTFΔ, with Δ = α(Ŵ−W) and F the true Fisher over sampled 256-token continuations. One streamed pass yields three predictions for free: diagonal q = ½Σg²Δ², tensor-block ½Σdℓ², and the full form. Headline statistic: R = measured KL / prediction.
  • Pre-registered scope. Primary Qwen3-8B (int8, fp8, group-int4; α ∈ {1/8 … 1}); frozen extensions to Qwen3-4B, OLMo-2-7B, Qwen3-32B, MoE Qwen3-30B-A3B; gradients in fp32 and bf16. Every rule, prediction, and file checksum logged before GPU runs.
  • Failure forensics. Hill tail estimators; α-scan KL(α) = aα² + bα³ + cα⁴ for OLMo-2; a Chernoff bound (p < 1.1×10⁻⁶) plus per-column projections for the 32B tensor; layer-by-layer backprop localization for bf16 blow-ups.
  • The screener. One fp32 Fisher pass (1.88 s/seq on GH200), 5%-trimmed tensor-block predictions, frozen band [0.75, 1.50], an alignment flag, and a greedy per-layer int4→int8→bf16 allocator that writes vLLM checkpoints.

Strengths

  • Pre-registration with a published ledger of frozen predictions — including failures — rare experimental hygiene for systems/ML evaluation.
  • Separates relative validity (format ranking — holds) from absolute validity (KL budgeting — heavy-tailed, needs trimming), changing how Hessian-aware screens should be used.
  • Every failure mechanistically localized: one tensor / two channels; the layer-15 bf16 jump; a quartic term.
  • Actionable output: a working screener that writes deployable vLLM checkpoints, validated end-to-end on an unseen family in under 1 GPU-hour.

Limitations

  • Alignment flag & block form validated by a single cell; no tensor flagged on the unseen Mistral test — real-world firing rate unknown.
  • MoE result officially inconclusive (dense control failed); α0.63 scaling is an observation, not a confirmed finding.
  • Weight-only quantization, three schemes, one GPU type (GH200), 256–512 sequences; the frozen band’s transferability is untested.
  • Mixed-format throughput gains were 4–8% at the tested operating point — the allocator’s serving benefit is undemonstrated.
  • Key localizations are post hoc; the 5% trim was fixed on the primary model before one out-of-sample round.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the gradient-aligned rounding coherence appear at 70B+ scale — and does it connect to known super-weight channels?
  2. Is MoE non-analytic scaling (KL ∝ α0.63) universal across sparse models; can a routing-flip correction extend the screen?
  3. Can the alignment flag fire before quantization, from top Fisher eigen-directions vs. candidate rounding patterns?
  4. Do the [0.75, 1.50] band and 5% trim survive larger calibration sets, other GPUs, and activation-aware schemes?
  5. Should stochastic rounding (15× KL cut on the culprit tensor) be the default for int4 last-layer projections?
