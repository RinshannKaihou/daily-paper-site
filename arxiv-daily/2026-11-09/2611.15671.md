> **一句话总结：** 该工作在 Cloudflare 的两个决策模型 Clef-flash (9B) 与 Clef (27B) 上，用同一组 2,400 条公共决策记录，逐一量化了量化评测中被混入的四种"非量化"噪声源（vLLM 重启 0–22 次翻转、换引擎 6–16 次、batch 大小 8–15 次、Gated DeltaNet 内核静默切换 10–14 次），并把 8/4-bit 量化效应表述为超出该噪声底的倍数：4-bit 效应（3.50–5.79%）在两个模型上区间均排除一切噪声源，而 8-bit 在 32 组对比中有 3 组与噪声底不可分；净精度变化几乎为零的 Q4_K_M 构建实际改变了 27B 模型 3.20%（1,000 条）的决策、其中 436 条变差。

> **TL;DR:** A rigorous pre-registered measurement study that isolates the "noise floor" of quantization evaluations — server restarts, engine swaps, batch-size changes, and silent kernel selection — on 2,400 paired public decisions from Cloudflare's Clef-flash (9B) and Clef (27B) decision models (12 tasks incl. MMLU, CLINC150, ANLI, RAGTruth; 31,275 questions total) on an NVIDIA GB10. Non-quantization sources flip 0–0.92% of decisions (llama.cpp restarts: exactly 0 flips, bit-identical on both models; vLLM NVFP4 restart: up to 22 of 2,400), while 4-bit quantization flips 3.50–5.79% — every 4-bit bootstrap interval excludes every noise source — and 8-bit flips 0.88–2.12%, inseparable from the floor in 3 of 32 comparisons. Two of five pre-registered predictions failed on both models: FP8 (2.12% flips on Clef-flash vs. a 0% BF16 restart floor) is NOT inside restart noise, and an engine change (0.25–0.67%) moves fewer decisions than 8-bit quantization. On the full 31,275-question set, Clef's Q4_K_M accuracy delta is a statistically-zero -0.02 pp while it flips 1,000 decisions (436 worse, 430 better). Essential reading if you quantize production classifiers and currently trust single-run comparisons.

## 核心贡献

- **提出并执行了量化对比的"分解协议"**：在同一批 2,400 条配对决策记录上，逐一测量重启、引擎、batch 大小、内核选择四种非量化漂移源，将每个量化效应表述为"超出噪声底的超出量/倍数"，而非只报准确率。配对翻转率差 + 固定种子（20261015）的 2,000 次配对 bootstrap 区间，零翻转对使用精确 Clopper-Pearson 区间。
- **三个引擎 × 五种权重格式 × 两个模型（9B/27B）的完整实测矩阵**（单台 NVIDIA GB10 / DGX Spark）：transformers 5.16.1（参考）、vLLM 0.29（BF16/FP8/NVFP4）、llama.cpp b11671（GGUF BF16/Q8_0/Q4_K_M），全部逐条输出开源。
- **量化了此前无人测过的噪声源**：llama.cpp 决策服务器重启在两个模型上均逐位一致（0 翻转）；vLLM 重启漂移依赖格式且与模型相关（Clef-flash：BF16/FP8/NVFP4 为 0/5/20 次翻转；Clef：4/0/22）；transformers 5.16.1 对 Gated DeltaNet 层按"哪些包能 import"静默选择内核，仅此一项就翻转 10–14 条决策。
- **预注册的 11 条假设 + 5 条预测（N1–N5）及其结果全部如实报告**，包括失败的两条：FP8 超出重启噪声（不支持 N1）、换引擎比 8-bit 量化动得少（不支持 N3）；同样的两条在 27B 上复测仍失败。
- **第三个服务器实例的机制探针**（探索性）：排除了"两个离散启动状态（如 torch.compile 计时选内核）"解释——第三实例与两个旧实例在 12.0% 的 NVFP4 记录上不同；NVFP4 重启底在 4–22 次之间随运行条件变动 5 倍以上，而量化效应本身（对 ref 137/139/137 次翻转）稳定在 ±2 以内。
- **"净精度掩盖翻转"的系统证据**：把每条全量翻转拆成变差/变好/错→错；提出可操作的评测协议（同引擎内对比、量化构建跨重启重复、记录实际运行的内核、翻转按方向拆分报告、以"相对噪声底的倍数"报告并写明分母）。

## Key Contributions

- A **decomposition protocol** for quantization comparisons: paired flip-rate differences with bootstrap intervals on a fixed record set (2,400 records = first 200 of each of 12 public tasks), measuring every non-quantization source (restart, engine, batch size, kernel selection) on the same records, and stating each quantization effect as the excess over — and ratio to — that floor.
- A **complete measured matrix** of three engines (transformers 5.16.1, vLLM 0.29, llama.cpp b11671) × five weight formats (BF16, GGUF Q8_0, GGUF Q4_K_M, FP8 W8A8, NVFP4 W4A4) × two models (Clef-flash 9B, Clef 27B, both Qwen3.5-hybrid architecture with Gated DeltaNet layers) on one NVIDIA GB10, with per-record outputs, quantization recipes, and the pre-registration released (github.com/Level5Tech/clef-quant-bench; weights on HuggingFace/Level5Tech).
- **First measurement of production-engine restart floors on decision models**: llama.cpp's single-sequence decision server is bit-identical across restarts on both models (0/2,400 flips); vLLM's restart floor is format- and model-dependent (0–22 flips), and does not follow a "fewer bits → noisier" ordering across models.
- **Exposure of a silent kernel-selection hazard**: transformers 5.16.1 routes Gated DeltaNet layers through a decorator that tries a hub kernel, then flash-linear-attention, then a PyTorch fallback, decided only by which packages import, with nothing reported; they made the choice observable (blocking/asserting imports) and measured it at 10–14 flips — comparable to a vLLM restart.
- **Pre-registered hypothesis testing with honest failures**: 5 predictions (N1–N5) written 2026-10-17 before any quantization analysis, plus 11 hypotheses (H1–H11) registered 2026-10-15; two predictions failed on both models (FP8 is not inside restart noise; engine change < 8-bit quantization effect).
- **Direction-split flip analysis** showing accuracy is nearly blind to quantization damage: e.g., Clef Q4_K_M's net accuracy change over 31,275 public questions is -0.02 pp (six questions) while 436 decisions get worse and 430 get better.

## Methodology

Thirteen configurations per model: a transformers BF16 reference (`ref`), vLLM BF16 (`bf16-srv`), FP8 W8A8 and NVFP4 W4A4 builds quantized with llm-compressor (calibration drawn only from task training splits, never overlapping scored records; joint schema head, vision tower, norms, and GDN gate projections kept in BF16), llama.cpp GGUF BF16/Q8_0/Q4_K_M, restart instances (`-r2`, and exploratory `-r3` for Clef's FP8/NVFP4), a batch-8 rerun (`ref-r2`), and a forced-fla-kernel run (`ref-fla`). All runs on one NVIDIA DGX Spark (GB10, 128 GB unified) in NGC's vLLM 26.09 container; quality runs used 16 requests in flight for vLLM, batch 1/8 for transformers, single-sequence for llama.cpp.

The comparison unit is the **top-1 decision flip** (change in the deployed top option of a fixed-choice decision model), with mean total-variation distance, max absolute probability difference, and bit-identical share reported alongside. All decomposition comparisons use the same 2,400 records (first 200 of each of 12 public tasks: BANKING77, CLINC150, ANLI R1–R3, ContractNLI, MMLU, ARC-Challenge, BoolQ, RAGTruth, When2Call, SST-5; 31,275 questions total). Intervals are percentile 95% from 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples over records with a shared resample matrix (so pair-vs-pair differences are themselves paired); zero-flip pairs get exact Clopper-Pearson bounds (0/2,400 → upper bound 0.15%). The silent transformers kernel choice was made observable by blocking the `fla` import in torch mode and wrapping fla entry points with counters plus post-warm-up assertions; every result line records the kernel tag. Full-set accuracy/macro-F1/Brier/ECE tables and per-task deltas are reported separately, and every full-set flip is split into got-worse / got-better / wrong-to-wrong against the gold label (exploratory). A third-instance probe on Clef's FP8 and NVFP4 builds tests the two-state (kernel-selection-at-startup) explanation of the restart floor. Latency (4,800 isolated requests per config) and throughput under the quality-run regime are in Appendix B.

## Results & Highlights

- **Non-quantization noise floor, 2,400 paired records (flips, both models)**: llama.cpp BF16 restart = 0 (bit-identical on 100%/95.3% of records); vLLM restarts on Clef-flash = 0 (BF16) / 5 (FP8) / 20 (NVFP4) and on Clef = 4 / 0 / 22 (max 0.92%); engine change at BF16 = 6–16 flips; batch 1→8 = 8–15; GDN kernel fallback→fla = 10–14. So any single-run "quantization comparison" that also changes engine/instance silently carries up to 0.92% decision drift that is not quantization.
- **Quantization inside one engine (flip rate vs. same-engine BF16 baseline)**: Clef-flash / Clef — Q8_0: 0.88% vs. a 0.00% llama.cpp restart floor (+0.88 pp [0.54, 1.29]); FP8: 2.12% vs. 0.00% vLLM BF16 restart floor on Clef-flash (pre-registered test +2.12 pp [1.54, 2.71] — N1 failed) and 1.62% vs. 0.00% FP8-restart floor on Clef (+1.62 pp [1.12, 2.12]); Q4_K_M: 5.79% / 3.50%; NVFP4: 5.79% / 5.67%. **Every 4-bit interval excludes every non-quantization source on both models** (weakest 4-bit-vs-noise difference +2.58 pp [1.79, 3.46], Bonferroni-safe); 8-bit clears the floor in only 29 of 32 comparisons (e.g., Clef Q8_0's 25 flips vs. llama.cpp engine change's 16 are inseparable).
- **Accuracy hides the damage (full 31,275 public questions, vs. own BF16 ref)**: Clef Q4_K_M Δaccuracy = -0.02 pp [-0.20, +0.16] (net six questions) while flipping 1,000 decisions (3.20%), of which 436 got worse / 430 better / 134 wrong-to-wrong; Clef-flash Q8_0 flips 183 decisions (0.59%) with Δaccuracy -0.00 [-0.08, +0.08]; Clef-flash FP8 loses only 0.20 pp accuracy while changing 374 decisions (1.20%). NVFP4 is the only build with unambiguous full-set accuracy loss: -0.95 pp [-1.17, -0.71] on Clef-flash and -0.65 pp [-0.87, -0.42] on Clef.
- **Effect-to-floor ratios (largest non-quantization source as denominator)**: on the 2,400-record unit, Q8_0 sits at 1.05× (Clef-flash) and 1.14× (Clef) of the floor, FP8 at 2.55×/1.77×, and 4-bit at 6.95×/3.82× (Q4_K_M) and 6.95×/6.18× (NVFP4); on the full set the 4-bit ratios reach 14.77× (Clef-flash Q4_K_M) and 9.63× (Clef NVFP4). A single restart repeat can understate the floor by >5×: Clef's NVFP4 gave instance-pair flips of 4, 22 and 22.
- **Flips concentrate at low reference confidence (pre-registered H3, Clef-flash)**: questions with ref top-probability < 0.7 are 16.3% of the set but contain 100% of Q8_0 flips, 98% of FP8's, 90% of Q4_K_M's and 86% of NVFP4's; neither 8-bit build flipped any question where the 27B reference was ≥ 90% confident.
- **Practical equal-memory comparison (exploratory)**: at 16.6–20.0 GB, the 27B at Q4_K_M is 1.45–1.46 pp more accurate than the 9B at BF16 (e.g., +1.45 [+1.08, +1.86] vs. transformers ref) and 0.82–0.84 pp ahead at NVFP4; a confidence-threshold cascade (H10) recovers 99.5–99.7% agreement with BF16 on public tasks while escalating 17–18% of decisions (26–30% on the authors' private data).

## Authors & Credibility

Two authors (Aleks Blagojevich, Trevor Sunderland) from Level5, a consultancy; contact and a general company address are given. Credibility signals are unusually strong for an engineering measurement paper: hypotheses H1–H11 were pre-registered on 2026-10-15 and predictions N1–N5 on 2026-10-17, before any quantization result was analyzed, and failed predictions are reported as failed on both models; code, quantization recipes, build scripts, per-record outputs, and the pre-registration text (PREREGISTRATION.md) are public at github.com/Level5Tech/clef-quant-bench, with quantized weights on Hugging Face (Level5Tech) — six of twelve tasks are shipped as full records and the license-restricted six as pinned record IDs with a rebuild script. The related-work search is documented with dates (2026-11-03 and 2026-11-10, 20 keyword queries, 191 Clef derivatives' model cards) and their full-set flip rates cross-check against independent community measurements (TurinTech's 27B Q4_K_M 96.9% agreement vs. their 96.8%; kurcontko's FP8/NVFP4 98.8%/94.3% vs. their 98.8%/95.4%). The paper candidly discloses that Claude (via Claude Code) wrote the benchmark/sweep/analysis code, operated the sweep, and drafted the text, with the authors fixing the thesis and hypotheses beforehand and taking responsibility. No peer-review status is stated (arXiv v1, 11 Nov 2026).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Real and well-scoped. Flip metrics (Dutta et al. 2024), backend variability (Masoudian et al.; Pape et al.), and serving nondeterminism (He 2025; vllm#58899) exist separately; the novelty is (a) measuring restart/engine/batch/kernel floors *on the same paired records* as the quantization effect for decision models, and (b) the silent GDN kernel-selection-by-import measurement, which they credibly claim is unmeasured anywhere.
- **Rigor**: High — pre-registration, paired bootstrap with a shared resample matrix, exact Clopper-Pearson for zero-flip pairs, kernel observability enforced by blocking/asserting imports with three independent checks for the six untagged early ref sets, and a third-instance probe that actually falsifies their own working hypothesis (the two-state kernel-selection story). But some choices limit inference: the 2,400-record unit is the *first* 200 records per task, not a random sample, and the authors themselves show it distorts rates (full-set rates run 0.8–1.6× differently, and equal task weighting alone inflates flip rates 1.26–1.36×); the `ref-r2` batch row is a rerun-plus-batch-change confound whose batch-1 bit-reproduction check was only run on the 9B, not the 27B; and vLLM "restart" comparisons ran with 16 requests in flight, so they mix instance drift with batch-composition drift.
- **Reproducibility**: Excellent on software (everything but private data released, pinned revisions, per-record outputs, recipes) but weak on hardware generality — every floor is measured on one GB10 whose FP8/NVFP4 kernels the authors call "young"; the paper itself cites Pape et al. finding up to 16.6-point backend effects elsewhere, so the specific floors should not be quoted as constants. Restart replicates are thin: one restart pair per build (a third instance only for two of Clef's builds), which the authors flag as the least certain term in every ratio.
- **Significance**: For anyone quantizing deployed decision/classification models, the operational protocol (same-engine comparison, per-build restart repeats, record the kernel, split flips by direction, report floor ratios) is immediately actionable and cheap (~a few thousand extra inferences per config). For generative-LLM quantization work the transfer is plausible but unproven.
- **Red flags / caveats**: (1) Both models are one family (Qwen3.5-based hybrids), and the headline "restart floor" pattern already failed to replicate between the 9B and 27B — a third model could move it again; (2) the equal-task-weighting and first-200-records unit choices mean the 2,400-record flip rates should not be compared directly to full-set community numbers; (3) some pre-registered decision rules involve judgment (what counts as "within the range" for N4) and the H2 combination rule was amended after seeing 27B results — disclosed, but a reviewer should note it; (4) the anomalous Clef-flash Q4_K_M +1.26 pp gain on ContractNLI is unexplained; (5) much of the text and code was AI-generated under author direction (disclosed), and the private-data results (13,415 records) are unverifiable by design; (6) H8 (equal-memory 4-bit-27B vs 8-bit-9B) is not evaluable within this sweep — the advertised equal-memory comparison uses a different pairing than pre-registered.

## Strengths

- Measures the exact confounds that every practical quantization comparison contains (engine, instance, batch, kernel) on the same records as the quantization effect, instead of assuming them away.
- llama.cpp's exactly-zero restart floor and the format-dependent, model-inconsistent vLLM floor are genuinely useful operational facts, as is the >5× swing in measured floor depending on which two instances (and run conditions) are compared.
- The failed pre-registered predictions are the most informative results: 8-bit quantization is *larger* than both the engine change and the restart floor, contrary to the authors' own expectations, on both models.
- The wrong/better/wrong-to-wrong flip split turns "accuracy barely moved" into "hundreds of individual decisions changed," which is the quantity an operator actually cares about.
- Full release: code, recipes, per-record outputs, pre-registration, quantized weights — plus their numbers reconcile with independent community model-card measurements.

## Limitations

- Two models from one family on one device (GB10); neither the restart-floor ordering nor the floor magnitudes can be extrapolated, and the authors explicitly refuse to extrapolate the effect-to-floor ratio with model size.
- Only one restart pair per build (plus a third instance for two Clef builds); the floor — the denominator of every ratio — is the least-certain measured quantity.
- The 2,400-record decomposition unit (first 200 records/task, equal task weights) differs systematically from the full-set rates (0.8–1.6×), so the two tables answer slightly different questions.
- The batch-size row is confounded with a rerun, and its clean attribution (bit-identical batch-1 reproduction) was verified only on Clef-flash.
- vLLM restart floors embed batch-composition variance (16 requests in flight), so they are deployment-realistic restart drift, not isolated instance drift.
- H8 (the pre-registered equal-memory 4-bit-vs-8-bit cross-model question) could not be formed from the builds made; the restart mechanism remains open beyond ruling out two states and FlashInfer FP4 tuning; no kernel-level profiling of the latency findings.

## Relevance

对量化评测方法学与推理服务可复现性方向高度相关：它给出了"量化效应必须相对噪声底报告"的可操作协议（同引擎对比、跨重启重复、记录内核、按方向拆分翻转），直接可用于本地部署决策模型/分类器时的 8-bit 与 4-bit 选型。其对 batch 非不变性、内核静默选择和 vLLM 重启漂移的量化，也为任何在生产服务栈上做模型压缩评测的人提供了必备的对照组设计模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the vLLM restart floor generalize beyond the GB10 (e.g., H100/B200 with mature FP8/NVFP4 kernels), and does the format-dependence ordering stabilize with five or more instances per build as the authors propose?
- What is the actual mechanism of the vLLM restart drift once two-state selection and FlashInfer FP4 autotuning are excluded — cold graph compilation, request-stream composition, or timing-dependent state?
- Would the 4-bit-vs-floor separation hold for generative workloads (long-form token flips) where per-token compounding could shrink decision margins differently than a single classification forward pass?
- Can the flip-concentration-at-low-confidence result (98–100% of 8-bit flips below 0.7 reference confidence) be turned into a cheap predictor of quantization damage from the BF16 model's own softmax, avoiding any quantized inference at all?
- How do mixed-precision choices interact with the floor — the builds keep GDN gate projections, norms, and the schema head in BF16 (conservative per Kozyrev & Maiboroda); does quantizing those layers push 8-bit builds below the noise floor?
- Is the unexplained +1.26 pp ContractNLI gain under Clef-flash Q4_K_M a real regularization effect of quantization noise or a split artifact worth investigating?
