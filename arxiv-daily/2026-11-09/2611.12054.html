Low-rank Nyström GP training with FITC correction matches reference accuracy on all nine UCI datasets — up to 1,120× faster, in pure fp32.

Low-rank Low Precision Training of Gaussian Processes

Moritz Poguntke, Eda Oktay, Yuanzhe Xi, Erin Carson, et al.  ·  TU Chemnitz · MPI Magdeburg · Emory · Charles University  ·  2026-11-09

Key metric · raw fit-time speedup

1,120×

on Buzz (n = 524.9K): 25,436 s → 22.7 s, while matching the reference RMSE (0.2672 vs 0.300). Range across nine datasets: 3.4×–1,120×.

Why this matters

It removes the kernel-matrix bottleneck from large-scale GP training and maps where fp32, fp16, and TF32 numerically break — numerical linear algebra serving ML.

原文：将大规模 GP 训练中的核矩阵瓶颈通过“几何自适应低秩近似 + FITC 修正 + 全解析梯度”彻底消除，并系统刻画 fp32/fp16/TF32 在病态核矩阵下的数值边界。

Critical edge

Headline speedups rest on cross-hardware A40-vs-V100 timings — normalized gains shrink to ~1.4× — and the crucial rank r is chosen retrospectively, with no a-priori rule.

Methodology — the ANGP pipeline

Exact GP training needs an O(n³) Cholesky of the dense kernel matrix. ANGP never forms one.

  1. AnchorNet landmarks (§4): low-discrepancy points (Halton / adaptive tensor grids) scattered over a volume-weighted partition of the data's bounding boxes, then snapped to nearest training points — chosen in O(mdn) with zero kernel evaluations, held fixed throughout optimization.
  2. Stabilized Nyström + FITC (§3): K ≈ K₂₁K₁₁⁻¹K₁₂ with escalated jitter; FITC diagonal D = Λ + σ²I restores the marginal variances Nyström underestimates.
  3. r×r-only gradients: via Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury and the matrix-determinant lemma, every dense factorization is r×r (M = Iᵣ + WD⁻¹Wᵀ ⪰ Iᵣ); ARD length-scale derivatives assemble from n×3r / 3r×3r factors — no n×n matrix or derivative tensor is ever formed.
  4. Optimization & prediction: Adam, 100 iterations, cosine decay, softplus positivity, no validation split; prediction via the low-rank mean or preconditioned CG (KeOps), with fp64 reference solves.
  5. Precision study (§7): only 4 large GEMMs are reducible to fp16-input/fp32-accumulate or TF32; triangular solves stay fp32.
O(nr(r+d)) per objective + gradient evaluation

Nine UCI datasets from Maddox et al. via bayesian-benchmarks; five splits per configuration; timings on an NVIDIA A40 (48 GB) vs the reference's V100 (32 GB).

  • Required rank spans two orders of magnitude — r = 50 (Buzz) to r = 3000 (3DRoad, Song) — unexplained by n or d, and chosen retrospectively.
  • Low-rank predictive mean trades accuracy for cost: median +27% RMSE vs the full-kernel mean (≥24% worse on Buzz at all ranks), but ≤ 4.1 s instead of hours.
  • fp32 full-kernel CG is the accuracy bottleneck: stagnates on 3DRoad, split-dependent on Buzz, fails on HouseElectric (κ grows ~1/σ²); reference-quality predictions need fp64 solves (up to ~24 h).
  • Below-fp32 is a poor trade: fp16/TF32 GEMMs give only 1.04–1.10× end-to-end; factorization fails for 7/8 (Buzz) and 9/11 (HouseElectric) tested ranks where κ₂(M) ≈ 1.3×10⁶–1.2×10⁷, with gradient errors up to 650×.

Credibility: numerical-linear-algebra teams (Chemnitz, Magdeburg, Emory, Prague); all numbers mean ± std over five splits; every tested rank tabulated; code released at github.com/moritzp23/AnchorNet-GP.

Strengths

  • Clean, fully derived analytic gradients — only r×r dense factorizations; no n×n matrix ever formed.
  • Kernel-free, geometry-adaptive landmark selection (AnchorNet) at O(mdn), fixed before optimization — no hyperparameter/inducing-point coupling.
  • Exceptional experimental honesty: all ranks tabulated, five splits, hardware normalization disclosed, reference typo identified.
  • A genuinely useful negative result: quantifies exactly why fp16/TF32 yields only ~1.1× and when it breaks (κ₂(M) ≳ 10⁶).
  • Released code and a reproducible protocol — same kernel, initialization, optimizer, budget; only r varies.

Limitations

  • No a-priori criterion for the rank r — it spans 50–3000 across datasets and must be tuned by trial.
  • Speedups are cross-hardware (A40 vs V100) and exclude the predictive solve; fp64 solves can take ~24 h.
  • The low-rank predictive mean is not uniformly accurate: median +27% RMSE vs the full-kernel mean.
  • Below-fp32 precision is impractical here: ≤1.10× best case; unreliable for κ₂(M) ≳ 10⁶.
  • No SVGP/SKI baselines, no FITC-vs-plain-Nyström ablation; one kernel family; fixed 100-iteration Adam budget.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Can a cheap a-posteriori indicator (effective rank of K₁₁, spectrum decay, FITC objective gap) predict the required rank r before training?
  2. How does ANGP compare at matched ranks against random/k-means inducing points, SKI, or GPyTorch SVGP under the same budget?
  3. Would an iterative or LDLᵀ-based solve for M make fp16 training robust where κ₂(M) ≈ 10⁶–10⁷?
  4. Can the ill-conditioned fp32 predictive solve be rescued in pure fp32 — iterative refinement, mixed-precision preconditioning, or factorization-based preconditioner application?
  5. Does the FITC correction meaningfully change hyperparameters or RMSE vs plain Nyström (Λ = 0), and how sensitive are results to the ε schedule and Adam budget?
