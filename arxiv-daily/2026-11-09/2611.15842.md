> **一句话总结：** 在一个对 OLMoE-1B-7B 执行真实计时 CPU→GPU 专家拷贝的卸载测试台上，本文证明"更少的路由切换"并不等于"更少的权重拷贝"：所有靠固定专家集合来加速的策略都至少付出 +0.067 nats 的质量损失，而完全不动路由、只把缓存驱逐规则从 LRU 换成 Belady 的"最远未来使用"规则，就能把每 token 专家拷贝数从 9.6 降到 4.6(2.1 倍降幅，距理论最优仅 3.5%)且质量零损失。

> **TL;DR:** In a PyTorch harness that performs real, timed blocking CPU-to-GPU expert copies on OLMoE-1B-7B (8 of 16 MoE layers offloaded, 64 bf16 experts per layer, 32 GPU cache slots, L40S with τ = 0.51 ms/copy), the standard LRU offloading baseline decodes at 38.3 tok/s with 9.6 expert copies per token; simply evicting the cached expert whose next use lies farthest in the future (Belady's 1966 rule, applied offline) cuts copies to 4.6 per token (2.1× fewer, within 3.5% of the provable minimum of 14,587 copies) with ΔNLL = 0.000 — while every tested policy that restricts routing to go faster than LRU costs at least +0.067 nats next-token NLL. A reimplementation of the learned option-critic routing controller copies 2.7× more experts than the simple Dwell-16 heuristic at similar quality, and a GRPO router fine-tuned for simulated cache hits actually copies slightly more experts than the untuned base (32,050 vs 31,915) — so on this benchmark suite, eviction, not routing, is the right lever.

## 核心贡献

- **直接测量而非代理指标**:构建了一个执行真实、阻塞性 CPU→GPU 专家拷贝并计时的 PyTorch 测试台(OLMoE-1B-7B,16 层 MoE 中卸载 8 层,每层 64 个 bf16 专家、GPU 缓存 32 槽),在同一次运行中同时记录质量(下一 token NLL)、拷贝数与解码速度,从而把"速度-质量"权衡从"路由切换次数"这一代理指标中解放出来。
- **概念澄清:切换 ≠ 拷贝**:用反例证明更少的路由切换未必带来更少的权重拷贝(A, B, A, B… 每 token 都切换却只 miss 两次;A, A, B, B, C, C… 切换减半却每次都 miss)。解码时间服从 ms/token = c + ρ·τ,其中 ρ 是每 token 拷贝的专家数、τ 是单次拷贝耗时(在不同 GPU 间相差约 5 倍)。
- **无损替代 LRU 的驱逐规则**:当缓存满时驱逐"下一次使用距现在最远"的专家(Belady 规则),完全不改变任何路由决策,却将每 token 拷贝数从 LRU 的 9.6 降到 4.6(2.1 倍),并证明其距理论最少拷贝数仅差 3.5%(15,087 对下界 14,587)。
- **前瞻性量化分析**:16 个 token 的未来需求即可恢复 LRU→Belady 降幅的 96%(32 个 token 达 99.7%),而基于过去请求计数的因果规则只能恢复 27%,加入路由概率的学习型打分器也只有 32–33%——把"可部署的驱逐学习"明确定义为一个尚待解决的预测问题。
- **对学习型路由方法的一系列负结果**:复现的 option-critic 式学习控制器在 6 个阈值中的 4 个被更简单的策略追平或超越;用 GRPO 针对"模拟缓存命中率"微调的路由器在真实测量中拷贝数不降反微升(32,050 对 31,915);MMLU 5,000 题上三个学习种子(平均保留 85.2% ± 1.3)全部低于同等切换率下的 Dwell-32 启发式。

## Key Contributions

- **Measured offloading, not proxies.** A harness that performs and times real blocking CPU-to-GPU copies for OLMoE-1B-7B, reporting quality (next-token NLL), throughput, and copy counts from the same run. Six cache policies are compared, including a reimplementation of the learned temporally-extended-routing controller.
- **A lossless alternative to LRU.** Farthest-future (Belady) eviction changes only which cached expert is evicted, never the routing decision, so the model's computation is bit-identical to full residency. It copies 2.1× fewer experts than LRU (9.6 → 4.6 per token) and is proven within 3.5% of the fewest copies possible on the recorded trace.
- **The switch/copy dissociation.** Formally shows that fewer routing switches need not mean fewer copies (a two-slot cache counterexample), and decomposes decode time as ms/token = c + ρ·τ — the basis for predicting the fastest policy on any platform from one measurement of compute time c and copy time τ.
- **A foresight curve that frames the open problem.** Sixteen tokens of future demand recover 96% of the LRU→Belady reduction; causal rules built from past-use counts recover 27%, and learned scorers with router probabilities 32–33% (worth about two tokens of foresight). The gap between causal and 16-token lookahead is the concrete prediction target.
- **Negative results on learned routing.** At four of six thresholds the learned controller is matched or beaten by a simpler schedule; a GRPO-fine-tuned router rewarded for simulated cache hits copies as many experts as before (slightly more, in fact); and fine-tuning the controller's objective with the measured copy time τ does not reach the frontier of the simple policies.

## Methodology

The authors freeze OLMoE-1B-7B (whose router was first trained to reuse experts, following ReMoE/Zhu et al. 2026) and offload 8 of its 16 MoE layers (layers {4,5,6,7,8,11,12,13}, chosen by a one-layer-at-a-time probe). In each offloaded layer, all 64 bf16 experts (12.6 MB each) live in pinned CPU memory while the GPU holds a cache of C = 32 slots. Policies differ only in how they manage those 32 slots:

- **LRU (lossless):** copies every missing expert on demand, evicting the least-recently-used expert not needed by the current token — preserves every routing decision.
- **Dwell-k / Static:** hold the cache fixed for k tokens (refilling from the most-requested experts in a window) or for the whole sequence (from the first 64 tokens) — the router must choose among cached experts, which can change the output.
- **Learned βµ controller:** a reimplementation of Shen & Henderson's temporally extended routing (option-critic style): a termination head β triggers cache replacement when it exceeds a threshold; a selection head µ picks the new set.
- **Farthest-future (Belady) eviction:** when eviction is required, evict the resident expert whose next use lies farthest in the future. Applied offline to the request trace recorded under LRU — valid because a lossless eviction rule provably never changes the requests the model makes.

Every policy decodes the same 16 held-out sequences (≤384 tokens, teacher-forced, batch size 1), recording next-token NLL, tokens/s, and steady-state expert copies (cache-filling copies excluded). Decode time is modeled as ms/token = c + ρ·τ and validated by artificially repeating each copy 10× and 50×. Correctness is checked by verifying that lossless runs match the all-64-expert baseline NLL within 5×10⁻³, and that the base NLL matches an independent batched evaluation within 4×10⁻⁴. A lower bound B_flat on the minimum number of copies for any lossless schedule (with or without prefetching) is derived by adapting Belady's MIN optimality to the k-experts-per-token setting, and greedy farthest-future eviction was verified optimal by exact dynamic programming on 4.2×10⁴ random instances.

## Results & Highlights

- **Farthest-future eviction vs LRU (main result):** on the L40S harness, farthest-future eviction lowers steady-state expert copies from 9.6 to 4.6 per token — a 2.1× reduction (exact ratio bounded in [2.1, 2.2]) — with ΔNLL = +0.000 vs the full-residency baseline, versus LRU's 9.6 copies/token at 38.3 tok/s. Offline, the rule makes 15,087 copies against a provable lower bound of 14,587, i.e., within 3.5% of the fewest copies possible.
- **No free lunch from holding expert sets fixed:** on the native L40S copy cost (τ = 0.51 ms), every tested policy that decodes faster than LRU (38.3 tok/s, ΔNLL 0.000) raises next-token NLL by at least +0.067 nats: Dwell-16 reaches 42.2 tok/s at +0.067, Static reaches 44.0 tok/s at +0.110, and the learned controller at threshold 0.9 reaches 43.6 tok/s at +0.350 — all against the all-64-expert baseline of 49.0 tok/s.
- **Foresight quantification:** an oracle given only the next k tokens of demand recovers 17% (k=1), 31% (k=2), 51% (k=4), 76% (k=8), 96% (k=16), and 99.7% (k=32) of the LRU→Belady gap; the causal decayed-frequency rule recovers 26.9% (27,285 steady transfers vs LRU's 31,776 and the oracle's 15,087), and a learned scorer with router probabilities 32–33% — equivalent to only about two tokens of foresight.
- **Learned controller underperforms:** at threshold 0.5 the controller's ΔNLL (+0.076) is similar to Dwell-16's (+0.067), but it copies 2.7× more experts (ρ = 12.3 vs 4.5) and decodes slower than LRU (33.8 vs 38.3 tok/s). On MMLU (5,000 questions, matched 3.1% switch rate), the three learned seeds score 42.2/41.5/40.1% accuracy (mean 41.3%, retention 85.2% ± 1.3) — all below the fixed-set-from-recent-use heuristic at 43.0% (88.9% retention) and far below free routing at 48.4%.
- **Cache-hit-reward router fine-tuning fails to transfer:** the GRPO-fine-tuned router (reward = simulated 16-slot LRU hit rate, per Calanzone et al. 2026) copies 32,050 experts vs the base model's 31,915 under lossless LRU serving (+0.4%), and 10,963 vs 10,954 at its own trained layer and cache size — despite training logs reporting higher simulated hit rates. Retraining the βµ controller with the measured τ in its objective still loses 0.16 nats at 4.0 copies/token, where Dwell-64 loses 0.085 at 3.9 and farthest-future loses nothing at 4.6.
- **Platform-aware policy selection:** copy time τ varies roughly 5× across GPUs (H100/H200 PCIe 5: 0.24 ms, 49.7 GiB/s → RTX 8000 PCIe 3: 1.17 ms, 10.0 GiB/s); Equation (1) fed with measured c and τ correctly picked the fastest policy when copies were made 10× and 50× slower (at 50×, Dwell-16/-64 are predicted and confirmed 2.0–2.5× faster than LRU).
- **Deployable lossless win:** decayed-frequency (LFU) eviction — fully causal — removes 12–15% of transfers for every router variant without changing any selected expert (e.g., 31,915 → 27,275 for the base model).

## Authors & Credibility

Pierre-Luc Bacon (CarbonForge, Mila, Université de Montréal) is a known reinforcement-learning researcher — co-author of the option-critic architecture (AAAI 2017) that underpins the learned controller this paper evaluates. Diego Calanzone (Mila, Université de Montréal) is an author of the Calanzone et al. 2026 cache-aware-routing work that Appendix E critically re-measures, and Jean-Maxime Larouche is at CarbonForge. The paper is accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 AXIOM workshop (Foundations of Efficient Deep Learning). Credibility signals are strong: code, traces, and raw results are released (github.com/carbonforgeAI/moe-expert-eviction); exactness checks tie the harness to the base model within 5×10⁻³ NLL; and the authors subject their own prior method (the GRPO cache-hit router) to the same unsparing measurement — reporting a negative result against their own training logs. A notable honesty caveat they state themselves: the original trained heads of the learned controller were unavailable, so their controller results test their reimplementation, not the published checkpoint.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Applying Belady eviction to expert caches is individually old (Belady 1966; Hawkeye/Parrot learn it for processor caches — both cited). The genuinely novel parts are (a) the measured harness that separates quality from speed with real timed copies instead of switch counts, and (b) the systematic negative evidence that routing-side learning (both option-critic control and GRPO cache-hit rewards) does not improve the measured trade-off. The paper is best read as a careful measurement study plus a well-framed open prediction problem.
- **Rigor:** High. Lossless invariants are checked every run (±5×10⁻³ NLL vs full residency; base NLL vs independent batched eval within 4×10⁻⁴); the optimality bound adapts Belady's proof to k-experts-per-token with a formal proposition; greedy optimality is confirmed by exact DP on 4.2×10⁴ random instances; imitation-learning results include a shuffled-label control (−136%, proving gains come from oracle labels). However, several raw artifacts were not kept — the second text shard's logs, MMLU per-question outputs, the GRPO simulator output, and the layer-selection probe — so those comparisons rest on aggregate summaries only, and the authors explicitly show no error bars and claim no statistical significance (16 sequences, one model).
- **Reproducibility:** Good. Public code, traces, and raw results; appendix documents weight layout, copy timing via CUDA events, warm-start details (without which dwell's penalty was inflated by up to half), and the exact restricted-layer set. The main irreproducible piece is the learned-controller comparison (original heads unavailable), which the authors flag rather than hide.
- **Significance:** Practically relevant for MoE serving on bandwidth-constrained GPUs: it tells practitioners that swapping the eviction rule is a free 2.1× copy reduction (and a causal LFU swap is a free 12–15%), and that fine-tuning routers for cache locality — a currently popular direction — did not pay off under honest measurement. The foresight curve (96% recovery at 16 tokens) cleanly defines the remaining research target.
- **Red flags:** (1) The headline eviction rule is an offline oracle — no deployable policy in the paper comes close (27–33% recovery), so the "2.1×" is an upper bound on what eviction learning could deliver, not a shipping speedup. (2) Scale is small: one 1B-active model, 16 sequences of ≤384 tokens, batch-1 teacher forcing — the GRPO failure could partly stem from the train/measure mismatch the authors themselves hypothesize (reward on self-generations vs teacher-forced fixed text). (3) The controller comparison uses a reimplementation with hand-set loss weights, not the published checkpoint. (4) At 10×/50× costs, the validation confirms only the winner's identity among six policies (Dwell-64 and threshold 0.9 were not run at those costs), not the accuracy of predicted times.

## Strengths

- Measures the thing that matters (timed CPU→GPU copies) instead of the proxy everyone reports (routing switches), and proves the two can diverge with a two-slot counterexample.
- The farthest-future result is lossless by construction — quality claims are exact, not statistical, and it comes with a 3.5%-of-optimal certificate via a lower-bound proof adapted to multi-expert requests.
- Equation (1) turns platform choice into a measurable decision rule (one measurement of c and τ at startup picks the policy), validated at 10× and 50× copy costs.
- Negative results are reported with the same care as positive ones, including against the authors' own prior work — with controls (SFT control, shuffled labels, cache-size tag) that rule out trivial explanations.
- The foresight curve (1→2→4→8→16→32 tokens vs % of gap recovered) gives future work a precise, quantified target.

## Limitations

- The best eviction rule requires future requests; the paper's own deployable approximations recover only 27–33% of its benefit, and closing this gap is left open.
- Single model (OLMoE-1B-7B), batch-1, teacher-forced, ≤384-token sequences, with blocking (non-overlapped) copies — overlapping copies would move latency from c + ρτ toward max(c, ρτ), shrinking gains on fast links, and batching effects are untested.
- The learned-controller results are for a reimplementation whose original trained heads were unavailable, so the negative verdict on Shen & Henderson's method is provisional.
- Several supporting results (second-shard replication, MMLU 5k, GRPO hit-rate gains) rely on aggregate summaries whose raw logs were discarded; there are no error bars or significance tests (acknowledged by the authors).
- Belady's optimality proof does not directly cover k-experts-per-token requests; the paper bounds greedy within 3.5% empirically/structurally, but whether greedy always attains the true optimum B* is stated as open.

## Relevance

对"大规模 ML 计算基础设施"方向高度相关:这是 MoE 推理在带宽受限 GPU 上卸载 serving 的核心问题,给出了可直接落地的结论——驱逐策略(而非路由微调)才是减少 CPU→GPU 专家拷贝的正确杠杆,并量化了 PCIe 带宽(τ 在 0.24–1.17 ms 间变化 5 倍)对策略选择的影响。其对学习型方法的严格负结果也为"模拟指标 vs 真实系统测量"的评估方法论提供了一个警示性范例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can router logits, hidden states, or speculative decoding drafts predict expert demand 8–16 tokens ahead, closing the gap between causal eviction (27–33% recovery) and the 96%-recovery lookahead oracle?
- How do the results change under batched decoding and asynchronous copy overlap, where latency approaches max(c, ρτ) — does eviction still dominate routing on high-bandwidth GPUs (H100/H200, τ = 0.24 ms)?
- Does the switch≠copy dissociation and the eviction ranking hold for other MoE families (Mixtral, DeepSeek-V3-style fine-grained experts) whose routing locality differs, per Liang et al. 2026?
- Is greedy farthest-future eviction provably optimal for the k-experts-per-token batching case, or can a smarter lossless schedule beat it (the paper's B* question)?
- Could prefetching guided by short-horizon demand forecasts eliminate blocking misses entirely without exceeding the proven lower bound on insertions?
