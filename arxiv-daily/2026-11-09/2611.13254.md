> **一句话总结：** 该工作提出一个"episode record"轨迹标注框架（用 P0–P7 运行阶段 × R1–R6 状态域定位故障的进入、传播、检测与响应），并对 Who&When 与 MAD 语料中 95 条多智能体失败轨迹做了全量精读，发现故障中位数在轨迹 18% 处即进入且多为对任务状态的错误决策或捏造，72% 的失败轨迹中早已存在与错误相矛盾的证据但 88% 的情况下无任何 agent 利用它，而最有效的控制族（独立裁判 74% 覆盖、任务契约 67%）恰恰不是人们常建的本地 checkpoint（仅 1%）；另发现 45% 的手工 Who&When 失败运行实际终结于 provider 内容过滤崩溃后 harness 代发的"最终答案"。

> **TL;DR:** An empirical dependability study that reads 95 failed multi-agent LLM runs (from the Who&When corpus on GAIA/AssistantBench questions and the MAD/MAST human-labelled traces) in full using a 38-field "episode record" protocol that ties each fault to its origin phase/state-domain, propagation hops, unused contradicting evidence, detection/response, and the control families that would have reached it. Compared to the Who&When corpus's human "decisive step" baseline labels, the annotated fault origin is earlier in 37/88 runs (within one step in 59%); contradicting evidence existed before the failure in 72% of runs but was acted on in only 7; and a deterministic scan reveals 45% (26/58) of hand-crafted Who&When runs actually ended with a provider content-filter crash after which the harness emitted a final answer no agent produced — a failure class invisible to both MAST symptom taxonomies and responsible-agent labels. The practically actionable finding: controls that read the ignored evidence (independent judges, 74% episode reach; admission-time task contracts, 67%) dominate local checkpoint/restore (1% reach; restoring agent state suffices in only 35/95 runs).

## 核心贡献

- **提出以轨迹为中心的可依赖性框架**：定义 "episode record" E=⟨O, I_E, L, D, C, M, σ, U⟩，把候选故障与其起源（origin）、传播链（propagation）、检测（detection）、响应（response）和后果（consequence）绑定，并用运行阶段（P0–P7，从授权、规划到审计）与状态域（R1–R6，从模型/算力到人类/组织控制）双坐标定位每个事件，外加 H1–H7 七种错误动力学（污染、漂移、级联等）刻画错误的存续与扩散。
- **把框架落成可复现的标注协议并规模化执行**：将 episode record 写成 38 个字段、固定词表的书面标注协议，对 Who&When（184 条：58 条手工 Magentic-One + 126 条算法生成的 AG2 CaptainAgent，任务来自 GAIA 与 AssistantBench）与 MAD（MAST 标注）两个公开语料中 95 条失败轨迹逐条全量精读（每条轨迹由独立的 LLM 标注实例先读全文再填写）。
- **三个核心实证发现（RQ1–RQ3）**：(1) 故障进入极早（中位在轨迹 0.18 处，距症状中位 0.67 的轨迹长度），且在 88 条 Who&When 轨迹中有 37 条早于人工标注的"决定性步骤"；起源多为对任务/知识状态（R2，71/95）的错误决策（42%）或捏造（26%）。(2) 证据通常在场但被无视：72% 的失败运行在症状前就含有与错误信念相矛盾的观察/消息，其中 88% 无人行动；31% 的运行有偏差被标记，但没有任何一次响应真正移除了偏差状态。(3) 能覆盖这些 episode 的控制族不是人们常建的：独立裁判（74%）、准入期任务契约（67%）、环境再验证（43%）、来源溯源（38%）排前面，本地 checkpoint 只能覆盖 1%——恢复 agent 自身状态仅在 35/95 条运行中足够，56 条中故障在计划/任务简报里，恢复只会重放它。
- **揭露隐藏在归因标签背后的 harness 级故障**：对全部 184 条原始 Who&When 轨迹的确定性文本扫描发现，45%（26/58）的手工运行以 orchestrator 的 progress-ledger 调用触发 provider 内容过滤 HTTP 400 崩溃告终，其中 25 条的"最终答案"由实验脚本（harness）代发、没有任何 agent 产生过它；而语料把 25 条中的 21 条归因于 orchestrator 之外的 agent——这意味着在该 split 上评估的归因方法有一部分是在给"provider 的 HTTP 400 找 agent 替罪羊"。

## Key Contributions

- **A trajectory-centered dependability framework.** Defines an *episode record* E = ⟨O, I_E, L, D, C, M, σ, U⟩ that ties a candidate fault to its origin, affected invariants, dependency links, detections, responses, manifestations, evaluation scale, and uncertainty. Every event is located on two orthogonal coordinates — operational phase (P0 scope/authorize … P7 audit/govern) and state domain (R1 model/compute … R6 human/organizational) — with seven error dynamics (H1–H7: repeated exposure, contamination, drift, cascade, external impact, observability debt, resource exhaustion) describing how a deviation persists or spreads.
- **A written, mechanically validated annotation protocol.** The record is operationalized as a ~4-page protocol with 38 fields and fixed vocabularies (including a decision tree for fault *kind*: specification / fabrication / integration / decision / interface / environment / coordination). Every step-valued field must point to a concrete step or line of the normalized trace so any claim can be checked; all records passed schema validation.
- **A full-read empirical study at a scale nobody had attempted.** Applied to 95 failed runs of six multi-agent systems from the public Who&When corpus (GAIA and AssistantBench questions; hand-crafted Magentic-One runs and algorithm-generated AG2 CaptainAgent runs) and the MAD (MAST) human-labelled split — reading each trajectory end-to-end rather than sampling symptoms.
- **Evidence-vs-response and control-reach measurements.** Quantifies, for the first time on released trajectories: how early faults enter relative to human "decisive step" labels; how often contradicting evidence existed and was ignored; how often detection produced an *effective* response (never); and which of 11 control families would have observed and reached each episode — finding that evidence-reading controls (independent judges, environment re-verification, admission-time task contracts, provenance) dominate checkpoint/restore.
- **A harness-fault discovery with benchmark-hygiene implications.** A deterministic text scan (independent of annotation) shows 45% of hand-crafted Who&When runs ended with a provider content-filter crash and a harness-emitted final answer attributed by the corpus to the wrong agent — a failure class represented neither by symptom taxonomies nor responsible-agent labels.
- **Released artifacts.** Protocol, all annotation records, and the scripts that compute every number in the paper are included as ancillary files, making the analysis repeatable and extensible.

## Methodology

The paper is an empirical annotation study, not a systems build. Its pipeline:

1. **Corpus selection and normalization.** Two public corpora of *complete released trajectories* of failed multi-agent runs: Who&When (184 failed runs — 58 hand-crafted Magentic-One orchestrator teams, 126 algorithm-generated AG2 CaptainAgent teams — each with human labels naming the responsible agent and decisive mistake step, used only as an outside reference) and the MAD human-labelled split (19 traces from AG2, MetaGPT, ChatDev, HyperAgent, AppWorld, Magentic-One, OpenManus with majority MAST failure-mode labels). Trajectories were normalized to numbered text files; seven traces over 200,000 characters were dropped ("nobody, human or model, reads those in one pass").
2. **Protocol development with a pilot.** Version 0.1 was applied to 20 length-stratified traces; annotator reports surfaced 14 vocabulary misfits (e.g., correct-answer-but-failed-process runs, provider crashes coinciding with fabricated answers), resolved in v0.2. This pilot-driven refinement of the fault-kind decision tree (specification / fabrication / integration / decision / interface / environment / coordination) is a deliberate methodological choice to reduce annotator disagreement.
3. **Annotation execution.** Each normalized trajectory was annotated by one independent LLM instance (Claude, Anthropic) — instructed with the protocol, field schema, and one finished example — that read the whole trajectory before writing one primary episode (earliest candidate fault whose consequences reach the final failure) plus any causally independent secondary episode. Trajectories were treated strictly as data, never as instructions (a prompt-injection safeguard). Records were checked mechanically against the schema; the annotator self-rated confidence (high 38, medium 55, low 2). 96 records were completed; one MAD run with a correct final answer was excluded from outcome statistics, leaving 95 failed runs.
4. **Annotator-independent checks.** (a) Origin steps compared against Who&When's human decisive-step labels on all 88 annotated Who&When runs — agreement within one step in 59%, with disagreement direction analyzed case by case (all 11 "later" cases hand-checked); (b) the harness-fault result computed by a deterministic text scan over all 184 raw Who&When trajectories, no annotation involved.
5. **Counterfactual control mapping.** For each episode, every control family from a 6-dimension comparison table (task contract, runtime policy, information-flow separation, environment-verified state, provenance, tests, independent judge, checkpoint/restore, coordination protocol, stall rules, human review) is listed if its *inspection basis* would have seen the deviation and its *boundary* reaches the affected state; a separate "restore reach" field asks whether restoring the agent's own state to just before the origin would have sufficed.

## Results & Highlights

- **Faults enter early and travel far (vs. the human decisive-step baseline).** On Who&When, the annotated origin sits at a median of 0.18 of the run length, with a median origin-to-symptom distance of 5.5 steps (mean 17.9, maximum 126) = 0.67 of the run. Versus the corpus's human-labelled decisive step (n = 88): origins coincide in 40 runs (45%) and fall within one step in 52 (59%); the paper's origin is *earlier* in 37 runs (median 3 steps earlier) and later in only 11 — i.e., the wrong belief is typically written into a plan or fact sheet several steps before the step humans call decisive.
- **The evidence is usually there and usually ignored.** 68/95 failed runs (72%) contain an observation or message contradicting the erroneous belief before the symptom (in 32 of them already present when the fault entered); agents acted on it in only 7 runs — so 88% of evidence-bearing runs failed with the disconfirming evidence sitting in the trajectory (41 times in an agent's input and never mentioned; 19 times explicitly acknowledged and then ignored, e.g., a verifier noting distances were "simulated" yet reporting gyms as verified). A deviation was flagged in 29/95 runs (31%) and a response followed in 34, but **not one response removed the deviated state** (4 removed part of it) — the typical replan changes the next action while keeping the belief that caused the loop.
- **The controls that reach these episodes are not the ones people mostly build.** Judged by episode reach: independent judges reading unused evidence 74% (70/95), admission-time task contracts 67%, environment re-verification of beliefs 43%, stall/termination rules 40%, provenance on stored facts 38% — versus checkpoint/restore at 1% (primary control in 0 runs). Correspondingly, restoring the agent's own state would have sufficed in only 35/95 runs; in 56 runs the fault lived in the plan, task brief, or stop rule, which a restore would simply replay.
- **A harness-level failure hidden behind attribution labels.** A deterministic scan of all 184 raw Who&When trajectories finds content-filter (HTTP 400) crashes in 26/58 hand-crafted runs (45%) — 25 ending with a harness-emitted FINAL ANSWER no agent produced — and 0/126 algorithm-generated runs; the corpus attributes 21 of those 25 fallback runs to an agent other than the orchestrator (mostly the web surfer), so attribution methods trained/scored on this split are partly fitting blame for a provider crash.
- **System-level contrast (Table 5).** Magentic-One (n=29): long runs, contradicting evidence in 90%, deviation detected in 41% (its orchestrator ledger flags loops), fabrication origin only 7%, harness fallback 55%, median origin-to-symptom 18 steps. CaptainAgent (n=59): short runs, fabrication origin 39%, 20% failing already at admission (P0) because the manager handed the group a garbled task, detection 25%, median distance 5 steps.
- **Symptom labels under-determine controls.** Each frequent MAST mode (avg 2.9 modes per run) maps to 4–11 distinct phase×domain origin cells; e.g., mode 3.3 "incorrect verification" (44 runs) has five origin kinds (16 wrong decisions, 16 fabrications, 6 corrupted handoffs, 4 misread observations, 2 defective briefs), and the P1/R2 cell appears among the top cells of all ten modes — a symptom label alone says neither where a control must act nor what it must inspect.

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors (Shaohuang Wang, Jinghao Wen, Xulei Zhang, Xun Jiao) are from the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Villanova University, USA; the work reads as an academic dependability/systems group turning classical fault-tolerance vocabulary (Avižienis et al.'s fault/error/failure taxonomy, checkpointing, compensation, redundancy) onto LLM multi-agent trajectories. Credibility is strengthened by: anchoring every step-valued annotation field to a concrete trace line; mechanical schema validation of all records; two checks that do not depend on the LLM annotator (comparison with human decisive-step labels; the deterministic harness-fault scan); a disclosed pilot with protocol revisions; explicit threats-to-validity (below); and release of the protocol, records, and every script that produces the paper's numbers. The acknowledgments transparently state that AI-assisted tools were used for the annotation itself and for editing/figures, with authors responsible for claims. The paper is a 37-page arXiv document (cs.AI, 9 Nov 2026) in ACM survey/measurement style with substantial appendices (dependability background, control-family catalog, evaluation methodology).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: high for the measurement, moderate for the framework.** Episode records with phase/state coordinates are a sensible synthesis of classical dependability concepts; the genuinely new contributions are the quantitative measurements (evidence-present-but-unused rates, control-reach frequencies, harness-fault prevalence) — the authors themselves note that "as far as we know, nobody has measured these things on released trajectories at any scale," which appears accurate.
- **Rigor: the biggest structural weakness is single-annotator LLM annotation.** All 96 records come from Claude instances with no human inter-annotator agreement in this version; the authors admit the fault-kind labels were "the hardest part of the protocol" and that a human subset check "would not be surprised" to see kind labels move. The mitigations (line-anchored fields, schema validation, 59% within-one-step agreement with human decisive steps, an annotation-free harness scan) are real but partial — kind-level percentages (e.g., decision 42% vs. fabrication 26%) should be read as provisional.
- **Reproducibility: strong on paper.** Every number is script-computed from released records, the protocol is included, and the deterministic harness-fault scan is trivially recomputable from the public corpora. What is *not* reproducible-as-measured is the control analysis: "would have reached" is an expert counterfactual judgment constrained by control contracts, not an intervention — the authors candidly state they never re-ran agents with controls in place, so the headline "judges reach 74%, checkpoints 1%" is analytic reach, not measured prevention.
- **Significance: the harness-fault finding alone is benchmark hygiene gold.** 45% of the hand-crafted Who&When split ending in a content-filter crash with a harness-fabricated final answer — mis-attributed in 21/25 cases — directly contaminates any failure-attribution benchmark built on that split; this deserves independent replication. The 72%-evidence/88%-ignored result also gives guardrail designers a concrete, testable target.
- **Red flags / caveats to keep in mind.** (a) Sampling was budget-driven (96 of 203 normalized traces chosen by balanced-character batches, not at random), though Who&When run-length statistics match the corpus; (b) the MAD slice is tiny (7 usable runs) and skewed short by the 200k-character cap, so "six multi-agent systems" overstates the effective coverage — the quantitative results are effectively a two-system (Magentic-One, CaptainAgent) study; (c) zero runs touch shared or third-party systems, so external impact (H5) — one of the framework's motivating dynamics, e.g., committed payments — is entirely untested here, meaning the checkpoint-is-useless conclusion does not transfer to settings with irreversible external effects; (d) all tasks are QA/programming sandboxes with no humans in the loop (R6 origins = 0), so findings may not extend to deployed agents.

## Strengths

- Reframes LLM-agent failure analysis from *symptom labels* (MAST modes, responsible-agent attribution) to *episode structure* — origin, propagation, unused evidence, ineffective response — which is exactly the information a control designer needs.
- The evidence result (72% present / 88% ignored / 0 effective responses) is a single memorable, actionable number with a clear design implication: a control earns its place by reading the evidence the worker left on the table.
- The control-family analysis (Table 4 + Table 10) converts anecdotes into a coverage argument and deflates the local-checkpoint/replay intuition with data (sufficient in only 35/95 runs).
- The harness-fault discovery is annotation-independent, deterministic, and directly actionable for the Who&When benchmark and for anyone evaluating attribution methods on it.
- Exceptional transparency: pilot failures disclosed, threats to validity enumerated honestly, protocol/records/scripts all released; trajectories treated as data (never instructions) during annotation.

## Limitations

- Single-LLM annotation with no human inter-annotator agreement; kind-level statistics are the least trustworthy layer of the results (acknowledged by the authors).
- The counterfactual control-reach judgments were never validated by re-running agents with the controls installed — "reaches" is analytical, not empirical.
- Corpus coverage is narrow: QA (GAIA/AssistantBench) and programming sandboxes; no run has external side effects (H5 untestable), no human-in-the-loop state (R6 empty), and the MAD contribution is only 7 runs.
- 96 of 203 traces annotated, selected by batch balancing rather than random sampling; conclusions about the full corpora are extrapolations.
- The study diagnoses *where* controls would attach but provides no prototype, cost analysis, or measured false-positive rates for any control family — engineering guidance remains qualitative (though contracts in Table 10 are carefully specified).

## Relevance

对于关注多智能体 LLM 系统可靠性与长程任务失败分析的方向，这篇论文提供了一个可直接复用的轨迹级故障标注框架与首批量化证据（证据在场却被无视、checkpoint 基本无效、harness 级故障污染归因基准），对设计 guardrail/验证器/失败归因方法的研究者具有直接的指导意义；其"控制必须读取 worker 忽略的证据"的结论可作为构建独立裁判与环境再验证机制的实证依据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the 72%-evidence-present / 88%-ignored / 0-effective-response pattern hold on corpora with irreversible external effects (τ-bench, TheAgentCompany trajectories), where checkpoint/restore might also fare differently?
- Can the counterfactual control-reach numbers (JUDGE 74%, TC 67%) be validated by actually deploying each control family and re-running the 95 failures — what are the real prevention rates, false-positive rates, and costs?
- How much do the fault-kind shares (decision 42%, fabrication 26%) move under human double-annotation, given the authors flag kind labels as the hardest part of the protocol?
- What is the causal relationship between the content-filter crashes and the harness's decision to emit a fabricated FINAL ANSWER — and should Who&When re-release a corrected attribution split that marks harness-terminated runs?
- Can "restore reach" be predicted online (i.e., at failure time) from observable trajectory features, so an agent could decide whether rollback, replan, or escalation is the right recovery without human diagnosis?
- Does the finding that replans preserve the contaminated belief generalize to single-agent long-horizon systems, or is it specific to orchestrator-worker multi-agent topologies with shared coordination state (R5)?
