Key Finding · arXiv 2611.13548 · NeurIPS 2026

Massive activations force independently seeded SAEs to “tile” PC1 with different features — making the most important features the least reproducible.

巨大激活（注意力汇聚的离群维度）使残差流高达 99.9% 的方差集中在 PC1 上，而 PC1 在 TopK 稀疏目标下没有唯一稀疏分解——导致 SAE 两大评测（消融重要性、方差解释率）同时失真。

Massive Activations Confound Sparse Autoencoders ↗

Nathan Delisle · Chenhao Tan — University of Chicago  ·  Nov 10, 2026

Sparse Autoencoders Mechanistic Interpretability SAE Evaluation Massive Activations Feature Reproducibility
Key Metric
0×

understatement of true content error in reported SAE fidelity.

Qwen-2.5-7B SAE reports 99.96% variance explained while leaving 33.0% of content (non-PC1) variance unexplained. Rule: FVUcontent/FVUtotal ≈ 1/(1−w₁), verified to <0.2% error.

Why This Matters

Anyone ranking SAE features by ablation importance or reporting FVU needs this confound and its zero-cost fix — widely cited fidelity numbers can understate real error 2–98×.

Critical Edge

The 43.8% splice-cost headline rests on a single seed and 256 sequences — the median gain across nine models is only ~4.7%.

Methodology

  • Training. TopK SAEs (width 4d, k=64, 20k steps, batch 4096) on middle-block residual streams over 5M WikiText-103 tokens; only the initialization varies across seeds. Ten LMs (GPT-2, Pythia 70M–6.9B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B/70B, Gemma-2), plus DINOv2 and crosscoders.
  • Reproducibility criterion. A feature is “shared” if its max decoder cosine with any direction in another run is ≥ 0.7 (Paulo & Belrose, 2025).
  • Feature taxonomy by PC1 alignment cos²(d_j, v₁): ordinary (<0.01), tiles (0.01–0.6, only 0–2.6% shared), canonical (≥0.6, always recur).
  • Causal test. An ℓ1 magnitude penalty on TopK activations should prefer aligned/canonical features — tested at β ∈ {0, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3}.
  • Repairs. PC1 bypass (train on PC1-removed activations, re-add PC1 at inference, outside the sparsity budget) and a retraining-free post-hoc filter excluding features with alignment a_j ≥ 0.01 from importance rankings.

  • Splice cost (Qwen-2.5-7B): 0.115 → 0.065 nats/prediction (−43.8%, single seed); median across nine models ≈ −4.7%.
  • Confounds extend beyond LLMs: DINOv2-base total FVU 0.013 vs content FVU 0.343 (~26× gap); crosscoders on Pythia-1.4B: top-50 sharing 3–4% → 83–93% with bypass.
  • Steering after repair remains modest in absolute terms: ≤ 37.5% edit success (Pythia-1.4B); near zero on GPT-2 and Qwen.

Strengths

  • Mechanistic, formally backed explanation — rank-one signals have no unique sparse decomposition under magnitude-agnostic objectives — confirmed by causal interventions (ℓ1 penalty, canonical init).
  • The dominance-factor identity is simple, derived, and verified to 0.2% — an immediately usable diagnostic requiring only the PC1 variance share.
  • Genuine breadth with negative results reported: 10 LMs (70M–70B), 5 public dictionary families, vision transformers, crosscoders, 5 SAE objectives.
  • Graduated remedies down to a zero-cost post-hoc filter; code and all trained dictionaries released.

Limitations

  • The importance-side confound is partly an artifact of zero-ablation: support-preserving resample ablation already yields 83% top-50 sharing on GPT-2 pre-bypass (27.5% on Pythia-1.4B).
  • Downstream benefits uneven: no average sparse-probing gain; interpretability AUROC drops on 2 of 8 models; steering stays ≤ 37.5% after repair.
  • Narrow main regime — k=64, width 4d, one site, 5M tokens — whereas production SAEs train on orders of magnitude more data.
  • PC1 bypass changes the learned features, so bypassed dictionaries aren’t directly comparable to the standard SAE literature.
  • Only PC1 is treated; models with variance spread over several rogue dimensions need an untested multi-direction generalization.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does tiling persist at production sparsity (L0 ≪ 64) and data scales, or diminish with corpus size?
  2. Can bypass generalize to removing the top-m principal components for multi-rogue-dimension models?
  3. Do meta-SAEs, attribution dictionaries, and cross-layer methods inherit or amplify this instability?
  4. Could resample-based importance metrics replace zero-ablation in feature-circuit workflows without any retraining?
  5. Do canonical features (alignment ≥ 0.6) carry interpretable content, and does content-FVU error predict interpretability-pipeline failures once PC1 is accounted for?
