Why This Matters

Quantifies the gains and risks of reusing long-prefix caches across rolling model upgrades — directly informing RAG / long-context serving and cross-version cache management.

Critical Edge

The 5× headline assumes a warm, importable cache already exists — end-to-end costs of cache storage, versioning, and page-format migration are never modeled.

Methodology

  • Three paired configurations per (prefix, question): Target (new model does prefill + generation), Source (old model throughout), KV Reuse (old model's prefill cache handed to the new model, which processes the question suffix and generates). Identical token IDs; greedy decoding.
  • Retrieval compatibility: RULER-style single-token NIAH at 512–32K-token prefixes (192 queries per length; queries at 10/50/90% depth, phase-balanced), extended to 64K–512K for V4.1-Flash (1,920 queries). Metrics: answer accuracy + harmful flip rate.
  • QA quality & latency: 585 fixed examples (300 BABILong, 100 HotpotQA, 100 2WikiMQA, 85 MultiFieldQA-en); paired median TTFT with source cache pre-loaded, on SGLang, 8×H100, TP=8.
  • Functional Cache Divergence (FCD): scaled Jensen–Shannon divergence between next-token distributions under reused vs. native caches — 12,288 matched positions.
  • Mechanistics: gold-logit-margin analysis, Indexer top-k overlap, and crossed input/cache interventions at layers 27/37/42 to localize drift.
  • Baseline: closed-form Ridge Mapper (Heo et al. 2026) adapted to DeepSeek's composite cache, calibrated on 500 FineWeb-Edu documents.

Strengths

  • Clean paired design — identical token IDs and decoding; every comparison is within-pair, not cross-run.
  • Four independent real updates, not one cherry-picked pair: exposes the full spectrum from near-native (V4.1) to severely degraded (Qwen).
  • FCD separates drift from accuracy — yielding the most interesting finding: retrieval drift grows with prefix length while natural-text drift stays flat.
  • Mechanistic depth: margin-vs-survival, Indexer overlap, crossed interventions show drift rides incoming hidden states (layer-37 NMSE 0.473 vs 0.019 local), not local caches.
  • Unusually transparent — 512K key-pool reuse, non-additive interventions, and the Qwen Source 0% artifact are disclosed in the paper itself.

Limitations

  • Only four updates, all same-architecture/same-size pairs; update magnitude, architectural tweaks, and open-weights replication unstudied.
  • TTFT accounting excludes source prefill, cache storage, versioned cache management, and page-format migration across serving-stack upgrades.
  • No per-query flip predictor before deployment; multi-hop QA loses up to −7.0 F1 — reuse is not safe for reasoning-heavy workloads at long prefixes.
  • Best results on 750B/552B models with excellent native retrieval — the regime where reuse matters least and the hardware barrier is highest.
  • Mechanistic coverage is thin: three inspected layers, 96+48-query cohorts; FCD at a single answer position.
  • Greedy decoding only; sampling and generations beyond ~48 tokens untested.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can update magnitude (KL between old/new outputs) predict per-prefix reuse safety before deployment — turning post-hoc FCD into an admission-control signal?
  2. Does the retrieval-vs-continuation FCD gap (30.62 vs 3.51 @32K) persist for multi-hop reasoning, code completion, and agentic tool-call traces?
  3. Would hybrid policies — direct reuse for natural text, selective recomputation for retrieval-critical regions — recover the lost 3–7 F1 while keeping most of the speedup?
  4. Since drift propagates through hidden states rather than local caches, can an early-layer "drift firewall" (recomputing a few shallow layers) cheaply bound divergence?
  5. Do the findings transfer to open-weights models — and does MoE expert-assignment drift explain part of the Qwen failure mode?
  6. What is the end-to-end systems cost of maintaining versioned prefix caches (storage, format migration, staleness policy)?
