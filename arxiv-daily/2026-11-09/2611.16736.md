> **一句话总结：** Google DeepMind 的理论工作证明：把贪心解码或自回归采样直接嵌入序列级损失函数得到的两种"自然"损失都是无下界的（可发散到 −∞），并借自回归 Gumbel 技巧揭示二者本是一体；对采样过程施加停止梯度得到的"矩匹配方向"虽不是任何损失的梯度、却是函数空间中 AR 负对数似然的有效下降方向，但其蒙特卡洛方差随序列长度增长——因此监督学习阶段 teacher forcing 可能"就是你所需要的一切"。

> **TL;DR:** A theory-only paper (no experiments, no benchmarks) proving that the two natural sequence-level losses built by plugging greedy decoding or autoregressive sampling into a cumulant are both unbounded below — explicit T=2, V=2 counterexamples drive the greedy loss to 1−α→−∞ and the sampling loss to (3/2)log 2 + (1/2)log(1+e^α)−α→−∞. An autoregressive Gumbel trick unifies them: the sampling loss equals the expected greedy loss under Gumbel perturbations. A stop-gradient "moment matching" direction is shown (via 7 fully proved propositions) to be a valid function-space descent direction for the teacher-forcing AR NLL against the exact-gradient baseline (one forward pass, no sampling), but suffers high variance — concluding teacher forcing may be "all you need" for the supervised phase, with exposure bias better addressed by changing the data distribution (on-policy distillation) or RL post-training.

## 核心贡献

- **证明两种"自然"的解码感知序列级损失在根本上有缺陷**：基于贪心解码的损失 $L^{greedy}_q = A^{greedy}_q(x) - R_q(x,y)$ 是不连续、非凸且**无下界**的（命题 2）；基于自回归采样的损失 $L^{sampling}_q = A^{sampling}_q(x) - R_q(x,y)$ 虽然光滑，但同样非凸且无下界（命题 3）。二者都会使损失发散到 −∞，因此**完全不能用于学习**。
- **用自回归 Gumbel 技巧统一两种缺陷损失**（命题 4）：给打分函数加上 i.i.d. 平移 Gumbel 噪声后做贪心解码，恰好等价于从 $p^{AR}_q$ 做祖先采样；且 $L^{sampling}_q = \mathbb{E}_Z[L^{greedy}_{q+Z}]$——采样损失就是贪心损失在 Gumbel 扰动下的期望，二者是同一枚硬币的两面。
- **提出并刻画"矩匹配方向" $d_q$**：对采样过程施加停止梯度后得到的更新方向在序列级对比模型样本与真值（介于 token 级 teacher forcing 梯度与 EBM 序列级梯度之间）。证明了它**不是任何损失函数的梯度**（命题 5），但通过软 Bellman 方程定义的 AR→EBM 映射 $\mathcal{M}$，它是 AR 梯度的预条件子形式（命题 6），因而是函数空间中 AR 负对数似然的**有效下降方向**（命题 7）。
- **给出明确的实践结论**：矩匹配方向需对 $V^T$ 规模的分布做蒙特卡洛估计、方差随序列长度 $T$ 增长，而 teacher forcing 一次前向传播即可得到**精确**梯度——监督学习阶段 teacher forcing 可能"就是你所需要的一切"；要真正解决 exposure bias，应改数据分布（如 on-policy 蒸馏）或用 RL 后训练（REINFORCE），而非改损失函数。

## Key Contributions

- **Impossibility results for decoding-aware losses.** The greedy-decoding loss $L^{greedy}_q$ (built from the greedy max cumulant $A^{greedy}_q$, computable in $O(VT)$ vs $O(V^T)$ for exact maximization) is discontinuous, nonconvex, and unbounded below (Prop. 2). The sampling-based loss $L^{sampling}_q$ (built from $A^{sampling}_q(x) = \mathbb{E}_{Y\sim p^{AR}_q}[R_q(x,Y) - \log p^{AR}_q(Y|x)]$, a variational lower bound of the intractable sequence-level log-sum-exp) is smooth but likewise nonconvex and unbounded below (Prop. 3). Both would diverge to −∞ during optimization.
- **Unification via autoregressive Gumbel tricks (Prop. 4).** Greedy decoding of Gumbel-perturbed scores $q+Z$ produces exactly ancestral samples from $p^{AR}_q$, and $A^{sampling}_q(x) = \mathbb{E}_Z[A^{greedy}_{q+Z}(x)]$, hence $L^{sampling}_q = \mathbb{E}_Z[L^{greedy}_{q+Z}]$. Greedy decoding is the deterministic limit; autoregressive sampling the stochastic counterpart — one shared pathology.
- **Analysis of the stop-gradient repair: the moment matching direction.** Because the pathology stems from the decoded sequence's dependence on parameters, the authors apply stop_gradient to the sampling, yielding $d_{q_w}(x,y) = \mathbb{E}_{Y\sim p^{AR}_{q_w}}[\nabla_w R_{q_w}(x,Y)] - \nabla_w R_{q_w}(x,y)$, which contrasts model samples against ground truth **at the sequence level** (unlike teacher forcing, which contrasts at the token level with ground-truth prefixes). They prove: (i) for $T\ge 2$ it is the gradient of no loss function (Prop. 5); (ii) via the soft-Bellman AR-to-EBM mapping $r=\mathcal{M}(q)$ of Blondel et al. (2025), $d_q$ equals the EBM functional gradient in reparameterized space and the AR gradient preconditioned by $(\partial\mathcal{M}(q)^*)^{-1}$ (Prop. 6); (iii) $-d_q$ is nevertheless a descent direction for the AR NLL in function space (Prop. 7).
- **Interpretability of the direction.** $d_q$ is exactly the mathematically-incorrect "reparameterized" (path) gradient of $\mathbb{E}_Z[L^{greedy}_{q_w+Z}]$; equivalently it drops the non-stationarity term that the envelope theorem kills in EBMs but not in AR models; equivalently it is a GAN-like scheme where the model is **its own critic** (shared parameters, stop-gradient on the generator). Remark 1 shows this does not transfer to RL post-training, where the reward is a separate function and REINFORCE remains unavoidable.
- **A sober practical verdict.** $d_q$ requires Monte-Carlo estimation over $\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{V}^T$ with variance growing in $T$ (correlated sampled prefixes), while teacher forcing yields the exact gradient in a single forward pass — so teacher forcing may be "all you need" for supervised learning of AR models from static datasets.

## Methodology

The paper works in the function-space (tabular) setting with a next-token scoring function $q(s_t,y_t)$ (causal Transformer in practice), next-token distribution $\pi_q(y_t|s_t) \propto \exp q(s_t,y_t)$, and sequence distribution $p^{AR}_q(y|x) = \prod_t \pi_q(y_t|x\oplus y_{<t})$. The cumulative score is $R_q(x,y)=\sum_{t=1}^T q(s_t,y_t)$, and the teacher-forcing NLL decomposes as $L^{AR}_q = \sum_t (V_q(s_t) - q(s_t,y_t))$ with $V_q$ the token-level log-sum-exp.

The methodology is a sequence of definitions, propositions, and complete proofs (Appendix A):

1. **Construct candidate losses by replacing intractable oracles with decoding procedures.** Exact maximization and sequence-level log-sum-exp over $\mathcal{Y}$ both cost $O(V^T)$, so the paper substitutes the greedy max $A^{greedy}_q$ (per-step argmax, $O(VT)$) and the sampling cumulant $A^{sampling}_q$ (ground-truth prefix replaced by model-sampled prefixes, then averaged), each paired with $-R_q(x,y)$ à la structured prediction (perceptron / Fenchel-Young losses).
2. **Disprove usability by explicit counterexample.** For both losses, minimal $T=2, V=2$ constructions exhibit discontinuity (the greedy loss jumps from 1 to 2 as $\epsilon$ crosses 0), nonconvexity (a Jensen-inequality violation for the sampling loss), and unboundedness (families $q_\alpha$ with $\alpha\to\infty$).
3. **Connect the two losses with a new autoregressive Gumbel trick**, proved by applying the categorical Gumbel trick step by step along the perturbed greedy prefix.
4. **Repair via stop-gradient and characterize.** The moment matching direction $d_q$ is derived by treating the sampling distribution as constant; its status is pinned down by a non-self-adjoint-Jacobian argument (not a gradient, Prop. 5), the invertible soft-Bellman map $\mathcal{M}$ from the companion paper (preconditioning, Prop. 6), and an inner-product computation (descent in function space, Prop. 7).
5. **Situate the results** against structured prediction (early/max-violation updates, beam-search optimization, finite-candidate-set losses with pseudo-references à la Edunov et al. 2018 / best-of-n distillation), scheduled sampling (shown to be inconsistent because ground-truth tokens are paired with mixed prefixes), and RL post-training.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported. The paper contains no experiments, benchmarks, or simulations; all evidence is theoretical — seven propositions with complete proofs in Appendix A. The key results are:

- **Greedy-decoding loss is unusable (Prop. 2).** On a $T=2, V=2$ construction, the loss is discontinuous (limit 1 from the left vs 2 from the right as the first-step logit gap $\epsilon\to 0$), nonconvex, and driven to −∞ by the family $q_\alpha$ with $L^{greedy}_{q_\alpha}(x,y) = 1-\alpha \to -\infty$: the loss is minimized by making the greedy **search error** ($R_q(x,y) > R_q(x,\hat{y}^{greedy})$) arbitrarily large, not by matching the ground truth.
- **Sampling-based loss is equally unusable (Prop. 3).** A Gumbel-trick computation gives the closed form $L^{sampling}_{q_\alpha}(x,y) = \frac{3}{2}\log 2 + \frac{1}{2}\log(1+e^{\alpha}) - \alpha \to -\infty$; nonconvexity is shown by an explicit Jensen-inequality violation at the midpoint of two configurations $q_1, q_2$.
- **Unification (Prop. 4).** $\hat{y}^{greedy}_{q+Z}(x) \sim p^{AR}_q(\cdot|x)$ and $L^{sampling}_q(x,y) = \mathbb{E}_Z[L^{greedy}_{q+Z}(x,y)]$: the sampling loss is the expected greedy loss under Gumbel perturbations.
- **Moment matching direction characterized (Props. 5–7).** For $T\ge 2$ no loss has $d_q$ as its gradient; $d_q = \nabla_r L^{EBM}_{R_r}|_{r=\mathcal{M}(q)}$ and $\partial\mathcal{M}(q)^* d_q = \nabla_q L^{AR}_q$; and $\langle -d_q, \nabla_q L^{AR}_q\rangle < 0$, i.e., $-d_q$ is a descent direction for the AR NLL in function space.
- **Complexity contrast quantifying the trade-off.** Brute-force exact maximization or sequence-level log-sum-exp over $\mathcal{Y}$ costs $O(V^T)$, greedy decoding $O(VT)$, and teacher forcing's exact gradient needs a single forward pass with no sampling — while estimating $d_q$ requires Monte-Carlo sampling over a $V^T$-sized space whose variance grows with $T$.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The unboundedness results are genuinely useful negative results — they close off the most obvious way to build exposure-bias-aware supervised losses (plug a decoding oracle into a cumulant), and the AR Gumbel trick connecting greedy and sampling losses is elegant and, in this loss-function context, new (Kool et al. 2019 used Gumbel perturbations for diverse beam outputs, a different goal, which the authors correctly distinguish). The "model as its own critic" and non-stationarity-gap interpretations give real insight into why the AR case differs from EBMs.
- **Rigor:** High on the theory side — every claim is a proved proposition, and the counterexamples are explicit and checkable. But the proofs of unboundedness rest on minimal $T=2, V=2$ instances, which suffices for impossibility yet says nothing about severity on real vocabularies ($V\sim10^5$, $T\sim10^3$).
- **The most conspicuous weakness is the total absence of experiments.** The practical crux of the paper — that the moment matching direction $d_q$ has prohibitively high variance while teacher forcing gives an exact gradient — is asserted analytically (correlated prefixes across time steps) but never measured: there is not even a small-scale synthetic experiment plotting the variance of $d_q$ against sequence length, or a toy run showing the greedy/sampling losses actually diverging. For a paper whose headline conclusion is practical ("teacher forcing may be all you need"), this is a real gap.
- **The descent guarantee is weaker than it sounds.** Proposition 7 holds in function space only; the authors themselves note that in parameter space the Gram operator $J(w)J(w)^*$ deforms inner products, so descent is not implied even for a linear parameterization $q_w = \Phi w$. The positive result is thus a structural characterization, not an optimization guarantee.
- **Partial non-self-containment and assumptions.** The key AR→EBM mapping $\mathcal{M}$ is imported from the companion arXiv:2512.15605, and the fixed-length assumption $\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{V}^T$ delegates variable-length handling to that same paper — a reader must consult two papers for the full picture.
- **Overclaiming risk, mostly managed.** The "all you need" conclusion is carefully scoped to supervised learning from static datasets and the paper explicitly concedes exposure bias is "real in principle" (recommending on-policy distillation or RL post-training instead), but the catchy framing invites misreading as a dismissal of exposure bias generally. Also, the negative results cover exactly the two plug-in losses; the bounded finite-candidate alternatives (pseudo-reference / best-of-n distillation losses) are set aside on the grounds that they need a distance or reward, i.e., fall outside the studied setting — a boundary the reader should keep in mind.

## Strengths

- Complete, self-contained proofs for every claim (Appendix A.1–A.7), with explicit counterexample constructions instead of hand-waving.
- The Gumbel unification (Prop. 4) turns two separate flaws into one understood phenomenon — a genuinely clarifying result.
- The moment matching direction is thoroughly characterized from three complementary angles (not-a-gradient via non-self-adjoint Jacobian; preconditioned AR gradient via the soft-Bellman map; expected perturbed greedy loss / self-critic view), connecting EBM theory, structured prediction, GANs, and scheduled sampling in one framework.
- Honest negative-result framing with actionable guidance: change the data distribution (on-policy distillation, Agarwal et al. 2024) or use RL post-training, rather than patching the loss.
- Useful positioning against prior art: explains precisely why violation-fixing updates (Collins & Roark 2004; Huang et al. 2012; Wiseman & Rush 2016) avoid the pathologies (clipping at zero, pseudo-references) and why scheduled sampling is inconsistent (ground-truth targets paired with mixed prefixes).

## Limitations

- **No empirical validation of any kind** — no benchmarks, no synthetic experiments; the variance-based dismissal of $d_q$ and the divergence of the flawed losses are never demonstrated numerically.
- **Descent guarantee in function space only** (Prop. 7); parameter-space descent can fail even for linear parameterizations, as the authors acknowledge in Section 5.5.
- **Fixed-length, finite-vocabulary setup** ($\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{V}^T$), with variable-length handling outsourced to the companion paper.
- **Dependence on companion work** (arXiv:2512.15605) for the soft-Bellman mapping $\mathcal{M}$ that underpins Props. 6–7.
- **Scope of the impossibility:** only the two natural plug-in losses are proven flawed; bounded surrogates (finite candidate sets, best-of-n distillation) are discussed but not analyzed within the paper's setting, and require external distance/reward signals.

## Relevance

这篇论文从数学上解释了为什么 LLM 监督预训练至今仍用 token 级交叉熵（teacher forcing）而非序列级损失——任何直接把贪心解码或采样塞进损失的想法都会导致无下界的目标；对关注训练目标设计、exposure bias 与 RL 后训练衔接的研究者，这是理解"何时必须离开监督范式"的理论基础。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the variance of the moment matching direction $d_q$ be tamed at small scale — e.g., via control variates, baseline subtraction, or truncated Gumbel-Top-k perturbations — and does its growth with sequence length match the analytic argument empirically?
- Does the unboundedness result extend to losses built on beam-search cumulants (beam width $k$ interpolates between greedy and exact maximization) — are they unbounded below for every finite $k$?
- Since $d_q = (\partial\mathcal{M}(q)^*)^{-1}\nabla_q L^{AR}_q$ is a preconditioned AR gradient, could an approximation of this preconditioner accelerate ordinary teacher-forcing training even if $d_q$ itself is never used?
- How do the finite-candidate pseudo-reference losses (Edunov et al. 2018; best-of-n distillation) behave as the candidate count $n\to\infty$ — do they converge to the unbounded sampling loss, and is there a principled bounded interpolation?
- For RL post-training with an external (separate) reward, the paper says REINFORCE is unavoidable — can the self-critic / shared-parameter view of $d_q$ inspire reward-free sequence-level objectives that sidestep the impossibility results?
