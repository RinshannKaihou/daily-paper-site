> **一句话总结：** 在一个 30B（激活 3B）的多语言 MoE 模型上，作者追踪两条从同一 SFT 检查点出发的 GRPO 分支：发布的分支在 MATH 上暴涨 36.07 分，却在 XQuAD-Indic 上丢失 19.32 F1；分解显示 MATH 增益的 95.2% 来自"更会包 \boxed 答案"而非真正解题能力，而加入 Indic 指令跟随奖励的兄弟分支能找回 Indic 抽取分数（+19.14 EM），但以 MMLU-Pro −13.92、LiveCodeBench −13.06 为代价，后续判官训练还会让抽取能力崩塌（F1 50.44→17.67），只能靠无奖励蒸馏恢复到 47.13。

> **TL;DR:** A production 30B/3B-active multilingual MoE team dissects what GRPO post-training breaks outside its reward's purview: relative to the SFT init, the released branch gains 36.07 points on MATH yet loses 19.32 F1 on XQuAD-Indic (while XQuAD-English improves 11.71 under the identical scoring rule), and a decomposition attributes 95.2% of the pooled MATH gain (33.13 of 34.80 points) to boxed-answer emission rising from 55.2% to 91.6% rather than to accuracy among boxed answers (91.1%→92.6%). A sibling branch with an Indic instruction-following reward (R_task + 0.3·R_lang − 0.3·R_rep) recovers the regressing Indic benchmarks by +11.24 to +26.95 points over RL-BASE but pays −13.92 MMLU-Pro and −13.06 LiveCodeBench, and a later judge-reward round collapses XQuAD-Indic F1 from 50.44 to 17.67 before reward-free distillation from the earlier checkpoint restores it to 47.13. Essential reading for anyone who trusts benchmark deltas as evidence of capability.

## 核心贡献

- **系统刻画了"奖励视野之外"的能力漂移**：在同一个 30B 总参/3B 激活的多语言 MoE（英语 + 8 种印度语言）上，从同一 SFT 检查点分出两条 GRPO 分支；发布分支（RL-BASE）在 MATH 上 +36.07 分的同时，XQuAD-Indic 掉了 19.32 F1、IndicQA −8.64、IndicIFEval-ground −7.47，而同一评分规则下的 XQuAD-English 反而 +11.71 F1 —— 说明这不是抽取任务本身的退化，而是语言 × 任务交互的失败。
- **拆穿了 MATH 分数暴涨的"形式水分"**：把 MATH 增益分解为答案形式与条件准确率两部分，发现 boxed 答案的产出率从 55.2% 升到 91.6%，贡献了 34.80 分池化增益中的 33.13 分（95.2%）；而 boxed 答案的条件准确率几乎没动（91.1%→92.6%，仅贡献 4.0%）。
- **提出了"奖励覆盖不对称"的解释并做了初步检验**：保留的混合脚本轮次中，拉丁字母答案平均多拿 0.168 奖励（主要来自结构化输出和工具环境），且判官（能"看见"整个答案）训练反而让 Indic 抽取 F1 暴跌 —— 说明"奖励看得见该行为"并不等于"奖励会保护该行为"。
- **给出一条可复用的修复路径及其代价清单**：带语言感知塑形的兄弟分支找回全部四个退化的 Indic 基准（相对 RL-BASE +11.24～+26.95 分）且保住 MATH，但牺牲竞赛数学、代码和 MMLU-Pro；脚本合规（翻译版 IndicIFEval 的母语脚本输出 95.1%→84.6%）并未恢复。
- **三轮后训练的纵向案例**：判官奖励轮把 XQuAD-Indic F1 从 50.44 打到 17.67（EM 32.00→1.91），随后的**无任务奖励在线蒸馏**（RL-OPD，从 RL-INDIC 早期检查点蒸馏，前向+反向截断 KL 等权）把 F1 恢复到 47.13，答案长度和脚本占比也基本回归。

## Key Contributions

- Documents **capability drift outside the reward's purview** in a real production pipeline: from one shared SFT checkpoint of a 30B-total/3B-active multilingual MoE (English + 8 Indic languages), the released GRPO branch (RL-BASE) gains 36.07 points on MATH while losing 19.32 F1 on XQuAD-Indic, 8.64 on IndicQA, and 7.47 on IndicIFEval-ground — yet XQuAD-English improves 11.71 F1 under the identical extractive scoring rule, isolating a language-by-task interaction rather than a generic extraction failure.
- **Decomposes the headline MATH gain into convention vs. capability**: boxed-answer emission rises from 55.2% (SFT) to 91.6% (RL-BASE) and accounts for 33.13 of the 34.80-point pooled gain (95.2%), while accuracy-given-boxed moves only 91.1%→92.6% (1.39 points, 4.0%). A benchmark score can rise while underlying accuracy is nearly flat.
- **Proposes and probes asymmetric reward coverage as the mechanism**: within retained mixed-script prompt groups, Latin-script answers earn 0.168 more reward on average (bootstrap interval reported; driven by structured-output and workplace environments); a generative judge that reads the whole answer still fails to protect Indic extraction — visibility alone does not preserve a behavior.
- **A bundled recovery recipe with an honest cost accounting**: RL-INDIC adds an Indic instruction-following environment with shaped reward R = R_task + 0.3·R_lang − 0.3·R_rep (language scored only on the post-reasoning answer; malformed answers get −0.25) plus a divisor floor max(σ, 0.05). It recovers all four regressing Indic benchmarks by +11.24 to +26.95 points over RL-BASE while preserving MATH (84.76→86.76), but drops MMLU-Pro by 13.92, LiveCodeBench by 13.06, HMMT Feb 2025 by 10.41, and AIME 2025 by 8.96 — and still does not restore script compliance (in-script output on translated IndicIFEval falls 95.1%→84.6%).
- **A three-round longitudinal trace of loss and reward-free recovery**: judge training collapses XQuAD-Indic from F1 50.44 / EM 32.00 to 17.67 / 1.91 in twenty steps; RL-OPD, which distills RL-INDIC into JUDGE-I+ with no reward model, verifier, or KL-to-reference (equal-weight forward/reverse truncated divergence over the teacher's top-k), restores F1 to 47.13 and EM to 33.52 with answer length back near the teacher's.

## Methodology

Two GRPO branches start from SFT-INIT, an export taken partway through the final pass of a supervised curriculum (15.2M rows, 64.0% English / 36.0% Indic across Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu; native and romanized script; ~17,205 H200 GPU-hours). RL-BASE — released as gnani-evon-v3.3-30B-A3B — trains on judged final-answer mathematics, JSON schema conformance, an iterative tool-call workplace assistant, and MCQA, with **no** language, script, length, grounding, or repetition term. RL-INDIC adds an Indic instruction-following environment whose reward scores task completion (fraction of K instruction constraints satisfied) plus 0.3 × language compliance (fraction of answer words in the target script, gated by language ID) minus 0.3 × repetition; only the answer after the final reasoning block is language-scored, so English internal reasoning goes unpenalized. An Indic-only mixture with an Indic-only reward produced Indic-script answers to English prompts, so the final mixture keeps a 9.9% English guardrail pool.

Both branches use GRPO with a leave-one-out baseline; RL-BASE samples 16 completions per prompt, RL-INDIC 8. RL-INDIC floors the advantage divisor at max(σ, 0.05) — a floor that binds for 44% of groups (39% of nonzero-spread groups) in the Indic environment, i.e., it shapes a substantial share of the training signal, not just edge cases.

Round two replaces verifiable graders with a learned generative judge: a 26B-total/4B-active MoE trained with GRPO on preference pairs from Indic data and HelpSteer3 (each pair in both orders), with rewards for side-agreement, calibration, and self-consistency; the export is selected on calibration because pairwise accuracy saturates early. Under greedy decoding averaged over both presentation orders it scores above a 235B-parameter reference on JudgeBench and RM-Bench (largest margin on the style-adversarial RM-Bench slice). Policy optimization against the frozen judge uses a circular judging ring (each rollout judged twice, canceling positional bias), group-relative length control, truncation penalties, and — in the "+" configuration — a think-length penalty on a pseudo-random fifth of groups over a 5,000-token budget plus repetition and script-consistency terms. Round three (RL-OPD) starts from JUDGE-I+ at step 20 and distills from RL-INDIC with no reward, matching the student's next-token distribution over the teacher's top-k with forward and reverse truncated divergences equally weighted, chosen because the targeted regressions are "collapse-shaped."

Evaluation uses a matched campaign (Table 8: 55 metrics on one harness/model mode/decoding configuration) comparing SFT-INIT vs RL-BASE, plus a directly measured RL-INDIC-vs-RL-BASE comparison (comparison B); cross-comparison with the SFT column assumes campaign comparability, which the authors flag.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline trade-off (MATH vs XQuAD-Indic)**: RL-BASE vs SFT-INIT gains +36.07 points on MATH (48.69→84.76) while losing −19.32 F1 on XQuAD-Indic and −8.64 on IndicQA; in the same matched campaign XQuAD-English *improves* +11.71 F1 under the same extractive scoring rule.
- **MATH gain decomposition**: boxed-answer emission 55.2%→91.6% (SFT→RL-BASE) contributes 33.13 of the 34.80-point pooled gain (95.2%); accuracy given a boxed answer 91.1%→92.6% contributes 1.39 points (4.0%); credit for unboxed answers adds 0.28 (0.8%). Nearly the entire celebrated math gain is answer-convention learning.
- **Indic recovery bundle (RL-INDIC vs RL-BASE, comparison B)**: XQuAD-Indic EM +19.14 (12.86→32.00), IndicIFEval-ground +26.95 (33.05→60.00), IndicQA EM +11.24 (12.22→23.46), IFEval-Hindi +21.70 (50.59→72.29); MATH rises further to 86.76 (+2.00). Costs vs RL-BASE: MMLU-Pro −13.92, LiveCodeBench −13.06, HMMT Feb 2025 −10.41, AIME 2025 −8.96, GPQA-Diamond −4.99.
- **Judge round collapse and distillation rescue (XQuAD-Indic, 20 steps each)**: round 1 RL-INDIC F1 50.44 → round 2 JUDGE-I+ 17.67 (EM 32.00→1.91) → round 3 RL-OPD 47.13 (EM 33.52); the share of English answers rises from 8.7% to 91.7% under the judge round, and median post-reasoning span balloons from 18 to 210 characters (JUDGE-B+ step 40) before distillation brings it back to 26 with script share 83.2%.
- **Script vs task decoupling**: on translated IndicIFEval, in-script output falls 95.1%→84.6% under RL-INDIC even as task accuracy improves; on XQuAD-Indic the sibling's answers are 80.0% at-least-half-Indic vs unmeasured for RL-BASE, and a prose-tolerant regrade barely changes F1 — the language shift, not extraction strictness, is the failure.
- **Reward differential evidence**: within retained mixed-script prompt groups, Latin-script answers receive 0.168 more reward on average (mainly from structured-output and workplace environments; bootstrap interval in Table 12) — an association, not a controlled effect.
- **Tool-policy side effects**: on BFCL v4, parallel-call categories collapse to ~0 (0.0–0.5%) while multi-turn base accuracy rises 34.0→58.0 (second turn supplies the missing call in 95/96 examined cases); vs RL-BASE, TauBench telecom +26.90 (9.36→36.26) but SWE-bench Verified −10.60 (22.80→12.20).

## Authors & Credibility

All six authors (Sutanshu Saksena, Raj Purusharth Malik, Pasunuti Prasanjith, Daksh Maan, Parijat Chatterjee, Avinash Benki) are at Gnani.AI, an Indian enterprise-voice/LLM company; this is a production post-training team reporting on their own released checkpoint (gnani-evon-v3.3-30B-A3B on Hugging Face), which strengthens credibility — the regressions are self-reported against a shipped artifact. The paper is a NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper ("Foundations of LLM Post-Training in Changing Environments"), which fits its observational, single-lineage scope. Credibility positives: the base model is cited (NVIDIA Nemotron 3, arXiv:2512.20848), the released checkpoint is public, the appendix openly documents a corrected baseline mistake (Table 6: an earlier comparison used the wrong SFT epoch — e.g., AIME 2025's "earlier delta" of +40.00 became +1.46 after correction, caught by recorded model paths rather than score agreement), and threats-to-validity get a dedicated section. The unreleased sibling, judge, and distilled checkpoints limit independent verification of the central comparisons, though RL-BASE itself is downloadable.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The framing — reading benchmark deltas alongside the *response forms they reward* — is genuinely useful, and the MATH decomposition (95.2% of the gain from boxed-emission) is a sharper, more quantified version of the "format vs. capability" concern than most post-training papers offer. The judge-visibility result (a reward that sees the behavior still fails to protect it) is the most conceptually interesting finding.
- **Rigor**: Honest to a fault in places — single seed for the main branch comparisons, no per-item uncertainty on benchmark deltas (only the 0.168 reward differential carries a bootstrap interval), and the authors repeatedly downgrade causal language ("an explanation to test, not a mechanism established"). But the central intervention is **bundled**: RL-INDIC changes the environment set, reward terms, English guardrail pool, group size (16→8), and the advantage floor simultaneously, so the paper cannot attribute the recovery to language-aware shaping versus simply adding Indic task data — the authors name this ablation as future work rather than running it.
- **Measurement quirks that matter**: a harness change after August 2026 made exact match more permissive, so EM is not comparable across rounds and the authors read the judge-round recovery on F1 only; XQuAD-Indic F1 comparisons with the SFT column assume campaign comparability across campaigns; IndicIFEval-ground's construction is *unverified* by the authors' own admission (question marks in their Table 1 taxonomy), and XorQA-Indic's passage status is unknown — meaning two of the load-bearing "grounded extraction" cells in the proposed taxonomy are unverified.
- **Reproducibility**: RL-BASE is public, but RL-INDIC, the judge, and RL-OPD are unreleased research artifacts; the SFT recipe (256 H200s, ~17k GPU-hours) is documented in unusual detail (packing efficiency, iteration accounting), which is commendable, but nobody outside the company can rerun the branch comparison.
- **Significance**: As a workshop case study of one model family it generalizes cautiously; the value is the diagnostic pattern (decompose score gains into emission vs. conditional accuracy; track extraction and script separately from task scores) more than the specific recipe.
- **Red flags**: mild. The MATH decomposition itself is incomplete by the authors' admission (boxed subset composition may shift; no robust regrading of unboxed answers was run — "we have not run that test"), and the three-round recovery trace is scored on a single benchmark (XQuAD-Indic) rather than the matched suite, so RL-OPD's side effects are unmeasured.

## Strengths

- Quantifies, rather than asserts, the gap between benchmark scores and capability: the boxed-emission decomposition is the paper's strongest and most reusable analytical move.
- A real production lineage with a public released checkpoint, including an unusually transparent correction log (Table 6) for a previously wrong SFT baseline.
- Clean separation of language effect from task-shape effect via the within-XQuAD contrast and the same-round grounded-IF-vs-grounded-extraction split.
- The reward-free distillation recovery (F1 17.67→47.13) is a practical, mechanism-plausible remedy for collapse-shaped regressions introduced by judge rewards.
- Dedicated threats-to-validity section that pre-empts the obvious objections (bundling, single seed, harness drift, unverified benchmark construction).

## Limitations

- No reward-term ablation: the recovery intervention bundles data, reward shaping, group size, and advantage floor, so no causal claim about the language reward itself survives.
- Single seed, single model family, and single benchmark (XQuAD-Indic) for the three-round trace; no per-item statistical intervals on benchmark deltas.
- Script compliance is not restored by any intervention tried, and the script differential estimate is sensitive to length filtering, reward scale, and rollout selection.
- Two of the grounded benchmarks underpinning the taxonomy (IndicIFEval-ground, XorQA-Indic) have unverified construction.
- The MATH decomposition stops short of semantic regrading of unboxed answers, leaving the "true capability" question open.
- Only RL-BASE is released; the sibling, judge, and distilled checkpoints are internal artifacts.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中"RL 后训练的奖励设计与其副作用"这一核心兴趣：它用生产级 30B MoE 的真实流水线展示了 GRPO 奖励覆盖之外的能力遗忘（多语言/脚本合规、抽取式 QA），并给出了可操作的测量手段（把分数增益分解为答案形式 vs 条件准确率）与修复手段（语言感知奖励塑形、无奖励蒸馏）。对任何关心多语言 LLM、RLHF/GRPO 奖励设计或"基准分数≠能力"评估方法论的工作都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Which single component of the bundle — Indic task data, the 0.3·R_lang term, the repetition penalty, the smaller group size, or the advantage floor — drives the Indic recovery? The authors flag this as the key missing ablation.
- Would semantic regrading of unboxed MATH answers (the test the authors did not run) confirm that conditional accuracy is truly flat, or does the boxed subset composition shift hide real capability gains?
- Does the judge's preference for elaborated answers over short verbatim spans (the hypothesized cause of the extraction collapse) hold measurably, and would a length-controlled judge eliminate the regression?
- Can distillation-based recovery (RL-OPD) preserve other capabilities lost in round two, given it was only scored on XQuAD-Indic and inherits the teacher's limits by construction?
- Does the Latin-script reward differential (0.168) persist under normalized GRPO advantages across environments with different reward scales, and would a per-language reward baseline neutralize it?
- How general is the emission-vs-conditional-accuracy decomposition — e.g., does IFBench/IFEval improvement in RL-BASE similarly decompose into format learning rather than instruction-comprehension gains?
