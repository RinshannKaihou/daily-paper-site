A rigorous audit of interpretability metrics delivers sharp stability constants and sound certificates — yet 55% of feature verdicts flip across seeds.

一句话总结：数学审计两个广泛使用的可解释性度量——特征维度与特征恢复率（FRR）——证明紧的 Lipschitz 稳定性常数并给出“特征已恢复”的可验证证书；但在不同训练种子之间，FRR 在标准阈值下 55.47% 的特征判定随种子翻转，证书无法非平凡地认证任何特征。

Why this matters

Evaluations claiming “features recovered” via FRR or dimensionality sum rules rest on metrics proven fragile to thresholds, perturbations, and training seeds — with stable, certified replacements now available.

Critical edge

All proofs are AI-drafted and AI-checked — no independent human mathematician has verified them — and every experiment is toy-scale, with no real language model tested.

Methodology

Proof-driven, with experiments confined to known-ground-truth settings. Two objects are audited: Elhage et al.'s feature dimensionality Di (and its sum rule, expected to equal model width d) and Chen et al.'s feature recovery rate FRRt.

  • Discontinuity diagnosis (§4): direct perturbation analysis pins the exact jump set Zi; a 2×2 extremal matrix family is reused three times to prove lower bounds.
  • Ridge regularization (§5): Di → D̃i via a λ-penalized denominator; Lipschitz bounds proved twice independently (variational reduction + segment argument), with constants matched by explicit extremal constructions — sharp, not asserted.
  • Certification layer (§6): line-angle geometry yields margin-count certificates C11 / C11-loc bounding FRR change between dictionaries.
  • Verification: every statement carries a blind-check status marker; experiments span 12 ReLU toy models, tied-weight SAEs (1024 atoms) on a synthetic 100×384 ground truth (3 seeds), and perturbed synthetic dictionaries.

Certificates are sound but vacuous where it matters: across seeds the dictionary distance H ≈ 1.2 (near maximum 2), so all 384 features go uncertified — atoms move ~7× more than the margins the certificates require. On small controlled perturbations (εnl = 0.1) the local angular form does fire, certifying 96.5 of 384 features.

Strengths

  • Sharp, not asserted: every constant comes with a matching lower bound from an explicit extremal construction.
  • Repairs a real artifact: a τ = 0.2 norm threshold manufactures a 24.8% sum-rule “violation”; the ridge-regularized replacement returns one stable verdict across the entire pre-registered λ range.
  • Right conceptual move: converts “is this feature recovered?” from a bare threshold crossing into a checkable margin condition.
  • Exceptional epistemic hygiene: provenance markers on every theorem, a per-result novelty audit, pre-registered PASS/FAIL expectations, negative results reported in the abstract itself.

Limitations

  • Toy-scale only: a 30×400 ReLU toy model and a synthetic 100×384 ground truth with 1024-atom SAEs, 3 seeds — no real language model, one training seed per density.
  • Certificates are post-hoc: they need the second dictionary, verifying a change was safe rather than predicting robustness ahead of time.
  • Vacuous on trained dictionaries: nothing certified non-trivially across seeds or even between two checkpoints of one run (H ≈ 1.19–1.21; motion ~20° vs ~3° margins).
  • Open loose ends: eight statements keep unresolved reviewer points; code was “to be released” at submission — every number marked “not reproduced by anyone else.”

Questions for further exploration

  • Can certificates become predictive by bounding local distances from training dynamics alone — turning post-hoc verification into a priori guarantees?
  • Would subspace-based certificates stay informative when atoms move ~20° across seeds, the exact regime where current ones are vacuous?
  • Does the 55% seed-instability (and stable area c̄) replicate on real LLM-derived dictionaries, where features are not i.i.d. uniform on the sphere?
  • Should SAE evaluation protocols replace single-threshold FRR with the Lipschitz area c̄ or smoothed FRRt,β as the primary metric?
