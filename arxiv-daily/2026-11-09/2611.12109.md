> **一句话总结：** SkillDuet 在 RL 训练中让模型参数与外部文本技能协同演化——从在线轨迹提取局部技能（Local）做 token 级 on-policy 蒸馏，并用"Strength"分数（技能引起的模型响应概率偏移幅度）每 10 步筛选局部技能合并进全局技能库（Global）——使 Qwen3-4B 在 ALFWorld 上达到 98.57%、WebShop 上达到 92.97% 的成功率，分别超过匹配的 SEED-API 基线 2.86 和 5.47 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** From the Qwen Business Unit of Alibaba, SkillDuet couples LLM parameter updates with the evolution of a persistent textual skill set (Global) during RL: trajectory-extracted Local skills supervise token-level on-policy distillation (OPD) at every step, and a "Strength" score — the magnitude of the response log-probability shift a skill induces on held-out trajectories — decides which Local skills are consolidated into Global every 10 steps. With Qwen3-4B it reaches 98.57% success on ALFWorld and 92.97% on WebShop, beating the matched SEED-API baseline by 2.86 and 5.47 percentage points respectively (and beating GRPO by 3.13 pp on WebShop success). The paper's most interesting finding is diagnostic: fixed skills that give +26.98 pp early in GRPO training decay to −2.00 pp by steps 101–150, and SkillDuet's learned Global helps its own model (+20.95 pp when added back at test time) but actively hurts a differently trained SEED-API model (−4.05 pp) — skill value is model-specific, justifying co-evolution.

## 核心贡献

- **模型–技能协同演化框架（SkillDuet）**：在单一交互循环中同时进行参数更新与持久技能库更新。智能体带着全局技能集（Global）交互，外部分析器从在线轨迹中提取局部技能（Local），Local 既用于每步的 on-policy 蒸馏（OPD）监督参数更新，其优选子集又周期性地合并进 Global 指导后续交互——内部化与技能巩固互相强化，构成技能层面的递归自我改进（RSI）。
- **基于模型响应反馈的技能选择（Strength 分数）**：将"模型响应反馈"从即时蒸馏扩展到长期技能保留。Strength 用技能在留出轨迹上引起的响应 log 概率偏移幅度（对轨迹取绝对值后平均）来排序候选 Local 技能，无需额外环境交互即可评估技能对当前策略的价值。
- **技能效用随训练衰减的实证动机**：在 ALFWorld 上用固定技能做 150 步 GRPO 训练的探针实验显示，同样的技能指导在训练早/中/晚期带来的平均增益分别为 +26.98、+9.64、−2.00 个百分点——固定技能会随策略进步失效，必须随训练更新。
- **技能价值取决于内容与模型兼容性的证据**：固定参数的对照实验表明，SkillDuet 学到的 Global 给自己用提升 20.95 个百分点，但迁移给 SEED-API 训练的模型反而降低 4.05 个百分点，直接证明"技能无模型不可迁移"，支持联合演化。
- **系统性消融**：验证了 Global 巩固优于仅 Local OPD、Strength 选择优于随机/效用/对比性准则、Top50% 保留率最优、以及直接部署优于执行验收（Commit）检查。

## Key Contributions

- **A model–skill co-evolution framework (SkillDuet).** One interaction loop drives both learning channels. The agent acts with a persistent Global skill set in its prompt; an external Qwen3.8-27B analyzer compares a successful vs. failed trajectory of the same task (or analyzes single trajectories) to extract Local skills; Local skills provide privileged-context teacher scores for token-level OPD alongside the RL objective, and the top-Strength Local skills are merged into Global every 10 steps. The updated model and Global jointly shape subsequent experience.
- **Strength: response-feedback-based skill selection for long-term retention.** A skill that barely shifts the current model's response probabilities likely encodes already-internalized knowledge; a large shift flags content worth keeping. Strength averages |Δ_r(L)| over held-out trajectories, where Δ_r(L) is the trajectory-mean log-prob shift of adding Local L versus token-length-matched neutral text. Scoring reuses sampled trajectories — no extra environment rollouts.
- **Empirical motivation: skill utility decays during training.** With fixed skills injected alongside 150 steps of GRPO on ALFWorld, mean success-rate gains fall from +26.98 pp (steps 1–50) to +9.64 pp (51–100) to −2.00 pp (101–150), even though the skill text never changes.
- **Evidence that skill value is model-dependent.** With parameters frozen, SkillDuet's learned Global raises its own ALFWorld success from 76.67% to 97.62% (+20.95 pp, bootstrap 95% CI [14.07, 27.96]), but transferring the same Global to the SEED-API model lowers it from 93.81% to 89.76% (−4.05 pp, CI [−8.45, 0.47]).
- **Thorough ablations** covering Global consolidation, selection criterion (Strength vs. Utility vs. Contrast vs. Random), retention ratio (Top25/50/75%), an optional execution-based Commit acceptance check, and controlled text-injection probes (neutral / shuffled / intact / antonym-replaced skills).

## Methodology

SkillDuet interleaves two coupled paths at every training step (8 trajectories sampled per task):

1. **Local skill extraction.** An external analyzer (Qwen3.8-27B) receives a successful and a failed trajectory of the same task, locates the earliest divergent decision, and writes one shared Local skill; groups without an eligible pair yield one Local per trajectory. Shared skills supervise OPD for the whole group; the remaining ≤6 trajectories form the held-out scoring set.
2. **Parameter learning via Local-guided OPD.** The student branch scores a sampled response under (context C, Global G); adding Local L to the same context yields the teacher branch. Their token-level log-probability difference, weighted by a sigmoid confidence gate (β = 5), gives the OPD loss added to the PPO outcome objective: L = L_PPO + λ_OPD·L_OPD + L_reg, with λ_OPD = 0.05 (ALFWorld) / 0.03 (WebShop). Teacher scores are cached before each actor update.
3. **Skill evolution via Strength selection and Global synthesis.** Each scorable Local is scored once at generation: Δ_r(L) = mean over tokens of [log π(a|C,G,L,a<i) − log π(a|C,G,NL,a<i)] on held-out trajectories, where NL is token-length-matched neutral filler text; Strength = mean_r |Δ_r(L)|. Every 10 steps, candidates are deduplicated, the top 50% by cached Strength are kept, and a synthesizer merges them with the current Global (hierarchical batch compression, up to 5 levels, if input exceeds a 24,000-byte context budget). The new Global (≤6 skills, ≤2,400 chars) is deployed for the next window; the window then clears. ALFWorld maintains one Global per task category (6 total); WebShop uses a single shared Global.
4. **Training setup.** Qwen3-4B backbone, no task-specific SFT, 300 steps, batch size 16, lr 1e-6, temperature 1.0. Evaluation: ALFWorld 140 trajectories (success rate, plus per-category rates); WebShop catalog1000 with mean terminal reward (Score) and complete-purchase success rate (Succ.).

The matched baseline SEED-API keeps SEED's per-trajectory skill extraction and OPD pipeline, uses the same external analyzer and the same initial checkpoint, but maintains no Global — isolating the co-evolution contribution.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main result (ALFWorld, Qwen3-4B):** SkillDuet reaches **98.57% overall success** (138/140 trajectories) vs. **95.71%** for the matched SEED-API baseline — a **+2.86 pp** gain; it hits 100% on five of six task categories (Pick, Look, Clean, Heat, Pick2; Cool = 90.00%), including Heat rising from SEED-API's 83.33% to 100%.
- **Main result (WebShop, catalog1000):** SkillDuet reaches **92.97% complete-task success** and **95.18 Score** vs. SEED-API's **87.50% / 91.31** — gains of **+5.47 pp success** and +3.87 Score; it also beats GRPO (92.96 Score / 89.84% Succ.) by +2.22 Score and +3.13 pp.
- **Motivating decay result (ALFWorld, GRPO + fixed skills):** mean guided-vs-unguided success gains of **+26.98 pp (steps 1–50), +9.64 pp (51–100), and −2.00 pp (101–150)** — fixed skill guidance becomes actively useless as training progresses.
- **Selection ablation (WebShop, peak validation Succ.):** SEED-API 87.50%, Paired Local-only 87.50%, SkillDuet with Random Top50% selection 89.06%, SkillDuet with Strength 92.97% — Strength beats Random by **3.91 pp**; in the historical ALFWorld pool, Strength achieves the best peak (97.9% vs. Utility 95.7%, Random 95.0%, Contrast 90.0%) and best AUC (88.26% vs. Random 78.97%, +9.29 pp).
- **Controlled Global study (ALFWorld, parameters fixed, 420 episodes):** SkillDuet with learned Global scores 97.62% vs. 76.67% without it (**+20.95 pp**, bootstrap 95% CI [14.07, 27.96]) and vs. 75.24% with length-matched neutral text (**+22.38 pp**, CI [15.93, 29.23]); the same Global transferred to SEED-API drops it from 93.81% to 89.76% (**−4.05 pp**, CI [−8.45, 0.47]).
- **Retention ratio and Commit ablations:** Top50% retention is best overall (ALFWorld 97.14%, WebShop 92.97% peak validation) vs. Top75% (93.57% / 82.81%); execution-based Commit32 acceptance underperforms direct deployment at Top50% (96.43% / 85.94%).
- **Cost:** the main WebShop run made 35,036 Local HTTP calls + 145 Global API calls over 37.09 hours on a single 8-GPU node; Strength scoring adds model-scoring compute but no extra environment interaction.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is authored by Xingguang Ji, Ziqiang Dong, Yubo Zhang, Xi Leng, Ninghua Yang, Yang Yang, and Guanjun Jiang of the Qwen Business Unit of Alibaba — an industrial lab with strong LLM-agent RL infrastructure, which lends credibility to the substantial training runs (300-step RL with per-step skill extraction, an external 27B analyzer, and response-scoring passes). The methodology sections are unusually transparent about limitations: the appendices disclose that the analyzer endpoint alias does not pin an immutable backend weight version, that the GPU model is not fully documented, and that complete deployment records are unavailable for some earlier configurations. Evaluation protocols for published baselines (D2Skill, ATOD, ICRL) are carefully separated from reproduced results, and uncertainty on the key Global-removal comparisons is quantified with 10,000 stratified, game-clustered paired bootstrap draws. The main comparisons rest on single training runs per configuration, which the authors also state plainly. No code release is mentioned.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Moderate-to-good. The core ingredients — on-policy distillation from skill-conditioned contexts (SEED), skill libraries during RL (SkillRL/D2Skill), response-based skill evaluation (UniSkill) — all exist; the contribution is the specific coupling: using response-shift magnitude (not direction, not reward) to decide which skills survive consolidation, synchronized with parameter updates. The framing as "skill-level recursive self-improvement" is more a rebranding of co-evolution than a new capability, and the paper never demonstrates compounding multi-generation improvement beyond the training loop itself.
- **Rigor:** The matched-baseline design (SEED-API sharing analyzer, checkpoint, and OPD coefficients) is the strongest part, and the controlled Global study with token-length-matched neutral text is exemplary. However, headline gains over the matched baseline are modest (+2.86 pp ALFWorld, +5.47 pp WebShop), the ALFWorld 98.57% vs. 95.71% comparison is 138/140 vs. 134/140 on a single evaluation of a single training run — within plausible run-to-run variance given no training seeds. Each configuration is one training run; the three "seeds" in the controlled study are execution seeds of fixed checkpoints, not independent trainings.
- **Reproducibility:** Questionable despite the detailed appendices. The external analyzer is a non-pinned internal endpoint ("edu-vlm-rm"), so the skill-extraction model cannot be exactly reproduced; no code or skill artifacts are released; and the paper itself admits deployment records are incomplete for some configurations. Numbers also shift across evaluation configurations (Top50% ALFWorld is 97.14% in the validation sequence but 98.57% under a later re-evaluation with new task order and RNG), which is disclosed but complicates comparison.
- **Significance:** The compatibility finding is the most valuable scientific content: SkillDuet-without-Global (76.67%) is far below SEED-API-without-Global (93.81%), showing the policy becomes adapted to Global-conditioned prompts, and skills actively hurt a differently-trained model (−4.05 pp). This is honest, important evidence that retained skills are model-specific — but it also means the framework's benefit is partly prompt-dependence rather than pure internalized capability.
- **Red flags:** (1) Hyperparameters (window = 10, Top50%, λ_OPD) appear tuned on the same two benchmarks used for reporting. (2) Only two benchmarks and one 4B backbone. (3) The Commit ablation shows their own execution-based acceptance check hurts performance and is silently dropped from the main configuration. (4) Strength ignores shift direction, so a skill that reliably degrades responses could score highly in principle; the paper deflects rather than resolves this. (5) All published-baseline comparisons in Table 1 (e.g., beating D2Skill-with-teacher-SFT's 95.3% ALFWorld) mix different initializations and aggregation protocols, so cross-paper rows are context, not evidence.

## Strengths

- Clean co-evolution design with a genuinely matched baseline that isolates the contribution of skill consolidation and selection.
- The Strength criterion is cheap (reuses sampled trajectories, no extra environment interaction) and principled (skills that no longer shift responses are already internalized).
- The motivating fixed-skill decay experiment (+26.98 → −2.00 pp over training) directly establishes the problem the method solves.
- The controlled Global study is exemplary methodology: token-length-matched neutral text, identical initial states, paired outcomes, and game-clustered bootstrap CIs.
- Exceptionally transparent appendices — cost accounting (35,036 analyzer calls, 37.09 h), worked synthesis example, and candid disclosure of missing deployment records and baseline protocol differences.
- Ablations go beyond the headline: selection criterion, retention ratio, Commit check, and shuffled/antonym text probes all probe the mechanism rather than just the score.

## Limitations

- Single training run per configuration; no variance over training seeds for the main comparisons, so the +2.86 pp ALFWorld gain over SEED-API could be within noise.
- Only Qwen3-4B and two interactive benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop); no math/coding/open-ended tasks, no larger backbones, no cross-domain transfer of the Global sets.
- Depends on an external 27B analyzer for every trajectory group — 35k+ API calls in one WebShop run — making the loop expensive and dependent on an unverifiable internal endpoint.
- The trained policy is dependent on Global-conditioned prompts: removing Global drops SkillDuet to 76.67%, below SEED-API without skills (93.81%), i.e., internalization alone underperforms the non-co-evolving baseline.
- Strength measures shift magnitude, not benefit; harmful but response-shifting skills are not filtered out by the score itself.
- Skills are model-specific by the paper's own evidence — the learned Global transfers negatively to SEED-API — limiting reuse of the skill artifact.

## Relevance

本文直接命中"自进化智能体 + RL 训练"这一兴趣方向：它把文本技能库的演化与策略参数更新在同一个反馈回路里耦合，并给出了技能价值随训练动态衰减的定量证据，对设计带记忆/技能库的 agent 训练管线（何时保留、何时淘汰经验）有直接的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the +2.86 pp ALFWorld gain over SEED-API survive multiple independent training seeds, given that the headline comparison rests on single runs?
- Can Strength be replaced by a directional metric that distinguishes beneficial from harmful response shifts, without reintroducing the trajectory-quality confound the authors identify in Utility/Contrast?
- Does co-evolution reduce or worsen prompt-dependence — can the Global-conditioned policy be gradually weaned off Global (e.g., annealed prompt dropout) so capability is fully internalized?
- How does the method scale to stronger backbones (does a larger model internalize skills so fast that Global consolidation becomes redundant, as the decay experiment suggests)?
- Can Global skill sets transfer across domains or be composed from multiple models' Globals, given the demonstrated negative transfer to a differently trained model?
- What happens when the external analyzer is replaced by the policy itself (self-analysis), trading analyzer cost against skill quality?
