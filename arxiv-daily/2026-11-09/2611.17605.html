Key metric · energy per solved task
1.00×

7B-q2_K needs 2.03× the rail energy per solve vs 3B-q4_K_M — 141.2 J vs 69.4 J (95% CI 1.91–2.17; 1.925 in a five-pair repeat).

Why this matters

A rare measured benchmark tying quantized-file choice to energy per solved task — directly reusable for green-AI and on-device inference evaluation.

把"量化文件选型"与"每解一题的能耗"直接挂钩的实测基准，对绿色 AI 与端侧推理评估有直接参考价值。

Critical edge ⚠

Absolute joules rest on Apple’s estimated IOReport rails — just 28–53% of system power, no external meter — so only same-machine ratios hold.

01Methodology

  • 24 GGUF files, digest-pinned — every Qwen2.5-Coder-Instruct file (0.5B–7B) the Ollama registry lists across the k-quant ladder: q3_K_M → q8_0 at all sizes, fp16 at 0.5B–3B, q2_K at 7B.
  • Fixed rig — Ollama 0.32.6 (llama.cpp on Metal), fanless MacBook Air M2 / 16 GB unified memory, AC power; greedy decoding (temp 0, fixed seed), 2,048-token context, 768-token output cap, one session per benchmark.
  • Correctness — 542 EvalPlus tasks (HumanEval+ 164 + MBPP+ 378); “solved” = passes base + plus tests, re-verified by serial re-evaluation of stored solutions.
  • Energy — macmon samples CPU/GPU/memory rails at 1 Hz; marginal power above a 0.509 W idle; J/solve = pooled marginal energy ÷ pooled solves, with 95% bootstrap intervals (B=100,000) and a whole-system cross-check.
  • Statistics — exact McNemar on discordant tasks, Holm-corrected over all 276 file pairs; ±3-point equivalence at 90% CI; a pre-set 40% rule gates headline energy gaps; the anchor pair re-run in five alternating AB/BA blocks with 45 °C cooldown gates.

  • The anchor step is a tie — 366 vs 363 solves (McNemar p=0.836; 90% CI caps any 3B deficit at 2.4 pts), yet the bigger file pays double energy and decodes 2.03× slower: the cheaper pick gives up nothing.
  • q4_K_M is safe from 1.5B up — within 3 pts of fp16/q8_0 at every size (90% CI) while costing 39.3 vs 69.1 J (1.5B) and 69.4 vs 116.1 J (3B) per solve.
  • Frontier marginal cost quadruples — ≈259 J per extra solve from 1.5B→3B, ≈1,077 J from 3B→7B.
  • Per-token rankings mislead — 1.5B-q4_K_M costs 1.98× per token vs 0.5B-q3_K_M but only +1.2% per solve; the broken 3B-q3_K_M ranks 14th/24 per token while solving almost nothing.
  • Sanity anchor — 7B-q8_0 hits 84.1% HumanEval+ / 69.3% MBPP+, matching the official Qwen2.5-Coder report.

03Strengths

  • Prices the exact decision a developer makes — one downloaded file under a size budget, not abstract model-level comparisons.
  • Exact paired statistics task-by-task (McNemar + Holm over all 276 pairs); every guide pick carries an explicit evidence label.
  • The alternating-session repeat (cooldown gates, pre-registered rules, AB/BA balance) independently supports the headline: 1.925 vs 1.956 in the main grid.
  • Per-solve vs per-token accounting exposes a concrete failure mode of per-token leaderboards.
  • Full artifact — digests, power logs, and a script that regenerates every number in the paper (Zenodo).

04Limitations

  • One fanless machine, one runtime, one model family, Python-only tasks, one zero-shot prompt, greedy decoding with a single sample.
  • Energy from estimated IOReport counters, no external validation; rails cover 28–53% of system power — absolute joules are machine-relative only.
  • Fixed-order main grid confounds size/recipe with time of day; only the anchor pair is repeated, leaving most energy rankings unreplicated.
  • Post hoc statistical choices (3-pt margin, 40% rule) and thermally flagged repeat blocks; 24 of 60 registry recipes covered (no 14B).
  • The broken 3B-q3_K_M file stays in every aggregate, distorting the whole-ladder cost reading; correctness data reused from the author’s prior study.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Do the picks — q4_K_M sweet spot from 1.5B up, 3B-q4_K_M as the efficiency knee — transfer to other code families, runtimes (vLLM, MLX, CPU-only), and actively cooled hardware?
  • How does the picture change under sampling-based decoding or agentic multi-turn use, where repeats must buy correctness precision too?
  • Would a 14B-q2_K / 14B-q3_K_M at 8 GB beat 7B-q6_K — does the larger-model rule fail one rung higher?
  • Can effective-BPW (vs tag names) be standardized into registry metadata, and does the ~3.5 BPW threshold hold below 7B for coder models?
  • What does per-solve energy look like beyond Python EvalPlus — other languages and repo-level (SWE-bench-like) tasks?
