> **一句话总结：** 作者通过对真实 GPU 的黑盒逆向工程，完整恢复了 NVIDIA SFU 特殊函数单元（MUFU）的分段二次插值结构与截断乘方等有限位宽运算规则，构建了 SFU-Sim 计算模型；在 A100、RTX 4060 与 RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell 三块卡上对所有测试 PTX 路径做全输入域穷举比对，FP32 路径覆盖全部 2^32 个输入编码，模型输出与硬件逐位一致（0 处不匹配），且 8 个 FP32 函数的系数总存储不足 10 KB（对比全输入查找表约 128 GiB）。

> **TL;DR:** NVIDIA only publishes ULP error bounds for its special-function (MUFU) instructions, which do not fix output bit patterns, and a PTX instruction compiles to a SASS sequence (e.g., `sin.approx.ftz.f32` = FMUL.RZ prescale by 1/2π + MUFU.SIN). SFU-Sim black-box reverse-engineers the bare MUFU on real GPUs — recovering input reduction, 64-segment quadratic tables, Q37 fixed-point arithmetic, and a truncated partial-product squaring rule — then models the surrounding compiler-generated operations. Validated exhaustively against three GPUs (A100/sm_80, RTX 4060 Laptop/sm_89, RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell/sm_120) as the "benchmark": 0 mismatches on all 12 common PTX paths over full input domains (all 2^32 FP32 encodings, all 2^16 FP16 encodings), plus 2 BF16 paths (0 mismatches over 2^16 encodings) and 17 extended paths on the A100 (all 2^32 encodings, 0 mismatches). Unlike prior lookup-table reproduction (Cankaya's GB-scale tables) or envytools' obsolete G80 register reads, SFU-Sim computes outputs arithmetically from <10 KB of coefficients (vs ~128 GiB for a full FP32 output table) with no GPU access needed.

## 核心贡献

- **裸 MUFU 的位级算术模型**：通过独立的黑盒逆向工程，首次对现代 NVIDIA GPU（Ampere/Ada Lovelace/Blackwell）的多功能单元（MUFU）指令恢复出可计算的位精确模型，覆盖 12 种常见形式（8 种 FP32：EX2/LG2/SIN/COS/TANH/RCP/RSQ/SQRT；2 种 FP16：EX2/TANH；2 种 FP64 高位字：RCP64H/RSQ64H）以及 Blackwell 上的 EX2.BF16/TANH.BF16，并清晰界定了"裸 MUFU 行为"与"PTX 周边编译操作"的边界。
- **完整的逆向工程方法论（以 SIN/COS 为案例）**：给出从 SASS 反汇编剥离周边指令、确定分段结构（偶/奇起始配对误差模式判定为 64 段）、利用输出编码的整数区间约束恢复定点规模（Q37，Q36 不可行）与常数/线性系数、再用二次残差分析恢复二次项系数与截断部分积平方规则 S15（仅保留 m+n ≥ 14 的列）的四步可证伪流程。
- **全输入域穷举验证**：在三代架构的三块真实 GPU 上，对每条测试 PTX 路径在规定输入域上穷举比对——FP32 路径覆盖全部 2^32 个编码、FP16/BF16 覆盖全部 2^16 个编码、FP64 高位字路径覆盖全部 2^32 个高字（低位固定为零、比较完整 64 位输出），全部 0 不匹配；还通过单比特故障注入确认比较器确能检出错误。
- **跨架构通用性与极小存储**：三块设备共用同一套系数与运算规则、无需按设备重新拟合；14 种 MUFU 形式归并为 7 个数值核族（窄格式复用 FP32 核）；8 个 FP32 函数的系数总计不足 10 KB，而存全部 2^32 输入的输出约需 128 GiB。
- **扩展 PTX 路径建模**：在 A100 上额外验证了 7 条非 .ftz FP32 路径（如利用 2^x = (2^(x/2))^2 处理次正规输出）、2 条 FP16x2 打包路径、以及 RCP/SQRT 在 RN/RZ/RM/RP 四种舍入模式下的 8 条路径，每条覆盖全部 2^32 编码，均 0 不匹配。

## Key Contributions

- **Bit-level arithmetic models of bare MUFU instructions.** Via independent black-box reverse engineering on real GPUs, SFU-Sim recovers computable, bit-exact models of the NVIDIA multi-function unit covering 12 common forms — 8 FP32 (EX2, LG2, SIN, COS, TANH, RCP, RSQ, SQRT), 2 FP16 (EX2, TANH), 2 FP64 high-word (RCP64H, RSQ64H) — plus EX2.BF16/TANH.BF16 on Blackwell, with an explicit boundary between bare MUFU behavior and the surrounding PTX-compiled operations.
- **A complete, falsifiable reverse-engineering methodology (SIN/COS case study).** Disassembly-based separation of surrounding ops (the FMUL.RZ prescale by FP32 1/2π); segment-count discrimination among 32/64/128 hypotheses via even/odd-start pairing error patterns (only 64 segments predicts "even-start errors stay below odd-start errors for every pair"); integer-interval constraints from output encodings that pin the fixed-point scale to Q37 (Q36 leaves at least one empty interval) and the constant/linear coefficients; and quadratic-residual analysis that recovers both the quadratic coefficients and a truncated partial-product squaring rule S15(v) (keep columns m+n ≥ 14, v = ℓ ≫ 2), which structurally explains the small shared correction δ(v) ∈ {0..7} as omitted low-weight partial products.
- **Exhaustive multi-device validation.** On A100 (sm_80), RTX 4060 Laptop (sm_89), and RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell (sm_120), every tested PTX path is compared over its full specified input domain — all 2^32 FP32 encodings, all 2^16 FP16/BF16 encodings, all 2^32 FP64 high words — with zero mismatches; single-bit fault injection confirms the comparator detects errors.
- **Cross-architecture generality with tiny storage.** The same coefficients and rules hold on all three devices with no per-device refitting; the 14 forms collapse into 7 numerical core families (narrow formats reuse FP32 cores); all 8 FP32 functions need <10 KB of coefficients versus ~128 GiB to tabulate outputs for all 2^32 inputs.
- **Extended PTX paths.** On the A100: 7 non-.ftz FP32 paths (e.g., ex2.approx.f32 computes x/2 → MUFU.EX2 → square, exploiting 2^x = (2^(x/2))^2 to reach subnormal outputs), 2 FP16x2 packed paths (all half-word combinations), and 8 RCP/SQRT paths under RN/RZ/RM/RP rounding predicted from final PTX semantics — each covering all 2^32 encodings with 0 mismatches.

## Methodology

SFU-Sim's target is the *observable* bit-level behavior of NVIDIA special-function PTX paths, not a circuit description. The method has four falsifiable stages (Fig. 1):

1. **Define the bare MUFU boundary.** PTX is a virtual ISA; a special-function instruction may compile to one MUFU or to a sequence with surrounding scalar ops. The authors compile minimal PTX entries and inspect SASS (e.g., `sin.approx.ftz.f32` → `FMUL.RZ t, x, 0x3e22f983` then `MUFU.SIN y, t`), treating the register-level input/output of the single MUFU.∗ instruction as the observation boundary; prescales, masks (LOP3.LUT for BF16), loads (LDG.E for RCP64H), and low-word zeroing (HFMA2.MMA) are modeled separately as surrounding operations.

2. **Input reduction and segmentation.** For SIN/COS: the in-period phase has exactly 25 effective bits (adjacent inputs differing only below bit 25 always agree); the top 2 bits give the quadrant and the low 23 bits the in-quadrant phase p. Trigonometric symmetry reduces 4 quadrants × 2 functions to one magnitude kernel K evaluated at p or its mirror — and the mirror is proven to be the *bitwise inversion* p′ = 0x7FFFFF − p, not endpoint reflection (they differ by 1 LSB; only inversion matches all observations). Segment count is decided by fitting quadratics to even-start vs odd-start pairings of 2^16-point blocks: the measured pattern (even-start errors below odd-start errors in every pair) uniquely selects 64 segments over the 32/128 hypotheses, yielding a 6-bit index + 17-bit in-segment coordinate ℓ.

3. **Fixed-point scale and coefficient recovery.** A measured FP32 output o_i only constrains the internal value to [o_i, next(o_i)). Assuming a Q-format internal value y/2^Q, each observation gives an integer interval [L_i, H_i]; feasibility requires Q ≥ max_i Q_min(i), which evaluates to Q = 37 across all 2^23 phase points (Q = 36 fails). Near segment starts the quadratic term vanishes, so a pure line c_s + b_s ℓ must satisfy all intervals — this pins unique integer pairs (c_s, b_s) per segment via intersection of Eq. (3) constraints; the linear window holds for the first 1024 points of every segment.

4. **Quadratic term and truncated squaring.** Residual intervals R_i (Eq. 4) constrain the quadratic term. No single quantized full square satisfies all segments; instead the square input is v = ℓ ≫ 2 with main term v² ≫ 15 minus a correction δ(v) ∈ {0..7} keyed to the low 14 bits of v. Summing only partial products with weight m+n ≥ 14 reproduces δ(v) exactly at all 2^15 square inputs (column 14 kept as guard for carry) — the "truncated square" S15(v). The final kernel is y = c_s + b_s·ℓ + a_s·S15(v) in Q37, normalized, truncated to FP32, with sign from quadrant and input sign, plus special-value bypasses (NaN → 0x7fffffff with payload dropped; ±0/subnormal: SIN → ±0, COS → 1).

The same observation-and-constraint procedure applied to the other Table I entries yields the full model set (Table IV), organized into 7 core families sharing segment coefficients and evaluation rules: e.g., RCP uses 7+16 bits with 128 segments; RSQ/SQRT split the exponent into even/odd (r ∈ {0,1}) with two 64-segment tables; TANH uses 11 exponent-selected intervals with 51 coefficient sets; FP16/BF16 widen into and reuse the FP32 cores; RCP64H/RSQ64H pad the 20-bit mantissa with three zeros, run the FP32 model, and rebuild the high word. FP32 results are truncated except RCP/RSQ (round-to-nearest-even); EX2/RCP subnormal results are flushed to zero in .ftz forms. Validation (Fig. 5) enumerates each path's domain in chunks, runs inline-PTX CUDA programs on the device, computes model outputs independently in C++, and compares full bit patterns, requiring checked-count == domain size and zero mismatches.

## Results & Highlights

- **Bit-exactness on three architectures (Table V):** all 12 common minimal PTX paths show **0 mismatches** on the A100, RTX 4060 Laptop, and RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell over their full specified input domains — FP32 paths (EX2, LG2, RCP, RSQ, SQRT, SIN, COS, TANH) over **all 2^32 encodings**, FP16 paths (EX2, TANH) over **all 2^16 encodings**, and FP64 high-word paths (RCP, RSQ) over **all 2^32 high words** with the full 64-bit output compared. The hardware is the only baseline that matters here, and the mismatch count against it is exactly zero.
- **BF16 on Blackwell:** EX2.BF16 and TANH.BF16 achieve **0 mismatches over all 2^16 BF16 encodings** on the RTX PRO 5000 (sm_120); these forms do not exist on the other two devices.
- **Extended paths on the A100 (Table VI):** 7 non-.ftz FP32 approximate paths, 2 FP16x2 packed paths (all 2^32 half-word combinations, not one-half-fixed scans), and 8 RCP/SQRT paths under RN/RZ/RM/RP rounding — each covering all 2^32 encodings — all with **0 mismatches per path**, for 17 additional bit-exact paths beyond the minimal set.
- **Storage efficiency:** the coefficients of all 8 FP32 functions fit in **<10 KB total**, whereas storing their outputs for all 2^32 inputs would take **~128 GiB** — a 7-order-of-magnitude reduction versus exhaustive tabulation, while computing outputs on demand with no GPU or table access.
- **Recovered micro-architecture constants (SIN/COS):** 25-bit effective phase; 64 segments (6-bit index, 17-bit coordinate); smallest feasible fixed-point scale **Q37** (Q36 leaves at least one point with an empty integer interval); linear-only window of the first **1024 points** per segment; truncated square S15 retaining partial products with **m+n ≥ 14** out of a 15×15 grid (105 terms kept, 105 discarded, 1 guard column). One set of coefficients and rules serves all three devices with no per-device refitting.
- **Validation rigor:** acceptance requires checked inputs == domain size with no gaps or duplicates, and an injected single-bit fault is always detected by the comparator, guarding against a trivially passing comparison.

## Authors & Credibility

Haozhi Xie and Tao Su are with the School of Electronics and Information Technology, Sun Yat-sen University (Guangdong, China). The paper is a v1 arXiv preprint (cs.AR, 10 Nov 2026). Credibility is strengthened by: (1) an exhaustive rather than sampled validation protocol (full 2^32/2^16 encodings per path, with coverage counting and fault injection); (2) explicit falsifiability at each reverse-engineering step (each hypothesis has a test that sends the authors back on failure); (3) honest epistemic framing — the authors repeatedly state the model is functionally equivalent and "does not imply the physical implementation inside the chip"; (4) open-source release of SFU-Sim and all validation programs for independent reproduction; and (5) correct positioning against prior art: envytools (G80/Tesla-era register dumps, inapplicable to modern devices), Cankaya's GB-scale lookup tables (no arithmetic model), MMA-Sim (matrix units, not piecewise special functions), and the classic Oberman/Siu and Piñeiro et al. interpolator designs (organization known, parameters unpublished). The work sits in a credible lineage (the authors' prior MMA-Sim, arXiv:2511.10909, is cited as [6]).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuinely strong for modern GPUs.** Bit-exact special-function reproduction on Ampere/Ada/Blackwell appears to be new; the closest prior work either targets 2006-era hardware (envytools' G80 SFU table reads via device registers) or sidesteps arithmetic entirely with GB-scale lookup tables (Cankaya). The truncated-partial-product squaring rule S15 — deriving that δ(v) is exactly the sum of discarded low-weight columns — is an elegant, verifiable insight.
- **Rigor: high, with unusual honesty.** Exhaustive full-domain scans (2^32 per FP32 path) with coverage checks and fault injection exceed typical practice. The authors explicitly note that the zero-mismatch result on the 8 RCP/SQPT rounding-mode paths "only validates the prediction of the final PTX output" and is "not an independent validation of the MUFU intermediate results," since those paths are predicted from PTX rounding semantics rather than by replaying SASS — a caveat many papers would omit.
- **Reproducibility: good.** Open-source code and validation programs; the pipeline (compile → inspect SASS → enumerate → compare) is mechanically checkable. Caveat: results depend on specific CUDA toolchain behavior (PTX 8.7 / CUDA 12.8-era compilation); a different compiler version could change surrounding operations on some paths, though the bare-MUFU models should be stable.
- **Coverage gaps in validation scope.** (a) The extended paths (non-.ftz, FP16x2, four rounding modes) are validated **only on the A100** — their zero-mismatch status on Ada/Blackwell is asserted for the minimal set only, not demonstrated. (b) One device per architecture (a data-center A100, a laptop-class 4060, a workstation Blackwell); whether H100/RTX 5090-class parts or future drivers share the same coefficients is untested — the cross-arch sharing found is suggestive but based on 3 data points. (c) The FP64 "domain" fixes the low input word to zero; this covers all inputs the path can distinguish, but the headline "all 2^32" for FP64 should not be read as the full 2^64 FP64 space.
- **Inference risk: functional equivalence vs. silicon truth.** The 64-segment/Q37/S15 structure is the *simplest* model consistent with observations chosen by structural compactness once bit-agreement holds; the authors are careful about this, but readers citing "NVIDIA's SFU uses 64 segments" should note this is an inferred equivalent, not disclosed hardware fact. Likewise, the partial-product-omission explanation for δ(v) is a plausible structural story, not a die-level confirmation.
- **Significance: solid niche impact.** For instruction-set simulators, bit-exact numerical reproducibility studies, and SFU hardware verification, this removes the need for physical GPUs or huge tables. It will not matter to users satisfied by documented ULP bounds, and no end-to-end simulator integration (e.g., plugging into GPGPU-Sim/AccelSim-class tools) is demonstrated — the claimed "component for instruction simulators" remains a promise rather than a built system.
- **Red flags: minimal.** No cherry-picked benchmarks are possible (the domain is exhaustive), and no overclaiming was found; the main weaknesses are the A100-only extended-path validation and the single-SKU-per-architecture sample.

## Strengths

- Exhaustive, gap-checked, fault-injected validation over complete input domains (all 2^32 FP32 encodings per path) on three GPU architectures — a far stronger standard than sampled testing.
- A disciplined, falsifiable methodology (boundary definition → segmentation → interval-constrained coefficient recovery → residual-constrained squaring rule) that generalizes beyond SIN/COS to 14 MUFU forms and 7 core families.
- The truncated-squaring discovery (S15, columns m+n ≥ 14 with a carry guard) turns an ad-hoc correction table into a structurally meaningful arithmetic rule.
- Extreme efficiency: <10 KB of coefficients replaces ~128 GiB of tabulated outputs, with on-demand computation and no GPU dependency.
- One shared coefficient set across Ampere, Ada Lovelace, and Blackwell — no per-device refitting — suggesting the SFU numerics have been stable for three generations.
- Clean separation of bare MUFU from compiler-generated surrounding operations, grounded in SASS disassembly rather than guesswork; open-source release.

## Limitations

- Extended paths (7 non-.ftz FP32, 2 FP16x2, 8 rounding-mode RCP/SQRT) are validated only on the A100; their behavior on Ada/Blackwell is unverified.
- One GPU model per architecture; generalization to other SKUs (H100, RTX 50-series consumer parts) and future architectures is untested.
- FP64 high-word validation fixes the low 32 input bits to zero — complete for what the path can distinguish, but not the full FP64 input space.
- The recovered model is a functional equivalence, not a confirmed micro-architectural description; structural choices (e.g., the partial-product-omission explanation) are Occam-style inferences from bit-level agreement.
- No demonstrated integration into an existing instruction-set simulator, and no performance numbers for model evaluation speed at simulation scale.
- Rounding-mode RCP/SQRT paths are validated only at final-output level, so the bare-MUFU intermediate behavior on those paths is not independently confirmed (authors acknowledge this).
- Compilation is toolchain-dependent: surrounding operations were recovered for specific PTX/SASS compilations (CUDA 12.8-era); different compiler versions could alter path structure even if the MUFU core is stable.

## Relevance

对需要 GPU 数值行为可复现与位精确模拟的方向（指令集模拟器、跨平台数值一致性验证、SFU 硬件功能验证）而言，本文提供了可直接复用的算术参考模型与开源实现，是底层浮点/SFU 逆向工程与数值分析兴趣的直接命中；若关注点仅在高层次模型或应用层算法，则相关性有限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do H100, B200, RTX 50-series consumer parts, and future architectures share the same 64-segment/Q37/S15 coefficients, or do any SKUs diverge — and can SFU-Sim's interval-constraint method detect a divergence with far fewer than 2^32 probes?
- How do the extended paths (non-.ftz, FP16x2, four rounding modes) behave on Ada and Blackwell, where the compiler may choose different surrounding-operation sequences?
- Can the reverse-engineering pipeline be automated (SASS diffing + interval solving) so that a new architecture's SFU is recovered in hours rather than the manual case-study effort described?
- What is the throughput and accuracy cost of embedding SFU-Sim into a full instruction-set simulator (GPGPU-Sim/AccelSim-class), and does bit-exact SFU modeling change conclusions in numerical-reproducibility studies that previously blamed only MMA units?
- Does the truncated partial-product squaring rule (columns m+n ≥ 14, carry guard at 14) correspond to an actual area/power optimization in NVIDIA's SFU, and would silicon-level evidence (e.g., power side channels) confirm the omitted-columns hypothesis?
- Do AMD CDNA/RDNA and Intel GPU SFUs follow analogous piecewise-quadratic organizations recoverable with the same method, enabling vendor-crossing bit-exact special-function simulation?
