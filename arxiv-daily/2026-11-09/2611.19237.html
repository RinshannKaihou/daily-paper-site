Why this matters

As LLM agents enter production, the question is whether human approval is a real control boundary — or an illusion of control.

⚠ Critical edge

Many headline cells are tiny (3-task ADK matrix, 4-episode banking rows) and the flagship prompt fix is exploratory — McNemar p = .25: a localization probe, not a validated cure. And “zero leakage” is bounded at 3.7% (Wilson upper bound), not proven.

No public URL for the approval-audit-toolkit appears in the paper.

Methodology, distilled

Unit of analysis: a scoped decision — versioned approvals, denied effects, conditions — traced across three execution boundaries: model call selection → harness scheduling & resume → workflow routing.

  • Paired design. Identical upstream business state; only the decision flips (Approve vs Reject), so both obligations are measured on the same state.
  • Native witnesses, not LLM judges. Isolated native executions and state predicates prove an effect happened — eliminating LLM-as-judge circularity; every number is re-derivable from execution evidence.
  • Model layer. τ-bench / τ²-bench Retail (pinned commit) × GPT-6.1-Sol, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Claude Haiku 5.5 — 100 paired checkpoints each + 1,100 approval-only episodes across 5 prompt arms; 10–18-turn horizons.
  • Harness layer. 2,352-episode CrewAI 1.15.37 / Agno 3.1.12 screening matrix, a Google ADK 2.14.0 reply-mode matrix, Mastra suspend/resume, plus OpenAI Agents SDK and LangGraph replays.
  • Workflow layer. 5,606 public n8n graphs screened to 59 policy-comparable Approve/Reject pairs, replayed with intercepted services; abstain-all / execute-all baselines bound the test.

Strengths

  • Reframes approval as two independently verifiable obligations, exposing the failure mode most HITL evaluations miss: approved work that silently never happens.
  • Native business-state witnesses + paired decisions make every reported number re-derivable — no LLM-judge circularity.
  • Clean three-layer attribution; each mechanism (confirmation aggregation, restoration scheduling, decision-insensitive routing) comes with a demonstrated repair.
  • Honest statistics: exact McNemar, Wilson intervals, explicit non-independence caveats, no deployment-prevalence claims.
  • Directly usable deliverables: triage flowchart, offline audit toolkit, 8 runtime preservation targets, a one-sentence design invariant.

Limitations

  • Mechanism-diagnostic panels, not population statistics — no deployment prevalence is estimated.
  • Small denominators throughout (6-case variant cells, 3-task ADK matrix, 4-episode banking rows); the 4/59 n8n rate carries a 2.7–16.2% CI.
  • All three models are proprietary endpoints — no open-weight model tested; per-model protocol adaptations weaken strict comparability.
  • The n8n “policy-comparable” funnel rests on only fair AI annotation agreement (κ 0.190–0.477).
  • Prompt-arm fixes are unvalidated generalizations (p ≥ .25); the CrewAI repair is evaluated as a bundle, never ablated.
  • Horizons capped at 18 turns — long-horizon decay and multi-approver settings untested.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Do completeness gaps grow beyond 18 turns, multi-dependency workflows, or more than two concurrently approved actions?
  2. Can the contract be enforced at runtime — a scheduler-level pending-obligation ledger that blocks completion claims until a native witness discharges them?
  3. Which CrewAI repair component carries the 336-omission fix — join bookkeeping or sibling/router draining — and does it transfer to LangGraph interrupts and AutoGen?
  4. What is the post-Reject execution prevalence across the full 5,606-graph n8n archive, and can static verification plus native replay scale to it?
  5. Do open-weight models show the same omission profile (44 GPT-6.1-Sol vs 6 DeepSeek unfinished), and is scope restatement provider-specific or universal?
  6. How should the contract treat non-binary effects — downstream failures, partial batches, or effects needing a fresh decision after a disclosed condition change?
