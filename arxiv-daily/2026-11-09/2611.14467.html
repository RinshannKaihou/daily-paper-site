REINFORCE with exact gradients and any fixed KL reference (even Cbase ≤ 6) hits this lower bound; a reset-based PPO variant is exponentially faster (Thm 2.1 vs Cor 3.1).

Why this matters 02

First unified convergence theory for why DeepSeek/ProRL KL-reference resets and Kimi K1.5/K2 squared losses actually work — with actionable tuning rules.

And a falsifiable prediction: fixed-reference online DPO sits on the wrong side of the lower bound.

Critical edge 03

The lower bound is a worst-case single-prompt tabular construction — the paper offers no evidence coverage collapse occurs in real RLVR runs, and runs no LLM experiments.

Methodology · distilled

Setting. One-shot contextual bandit abstraction of RL post-training: a policy maps a prompt to a distribution over full responses. Base-model quality is coverage Cπ = E[1/π(Y*|x)] — expected generations to hit an optimal response.

The bar. “Coverage-preserving” = ε-suboptimal with steps and samples poly(Cbase, …) — what Best-of-N achieves; no prior policy-gradient guarantee met it.

Lower bound. A single prompt, m+2 responses (m = ⌈1/ε⌉ zero-reward distractors), Cbase = 6. Exact two-dimensional logit-dynamics tracking shows reward improving monotonically while the optimal response’s probability collapses to exp(−Θ(1/√ε)).

The fix — APMD. Each outer iteration resets the KL reference to the current policy and approximately solves a strongly-regularized (η = Θ(1)) subproblem π*η,t ∝ πt·eηr. The mirror-descent rate log(2Cbase)/(ηT) survives approximation, and every iterate keeps ≥ ½ of the base model’s coverage.

Instantiations. PPO-NoClip (KL + resets), PPO-Lite (asymmetric clipping as variance control), and squared-loss fitting of log-ratios via (1/η)·log(π*/πt) = r − b*(x) — yielding KimiPO and Rebel-OnPolicy.

Results · beyond the headline

All theorem-level guarantees on a softmax-linear testbed (Assumption 2.1); no LLM benchmark experiments.

Reset frequency matters: REINFORCE crawls through a long coverage-collapse plateau; the same instance with a reference reset every 10 steps is ~14× faster.

Strengths

  • Isolates coverage collapse under the most favorable assumptions (exact gradients, tabular, good coverage) — an intrinsic property of the RL objective, not an implementation artifact.
  • Genuinely unifying: reset-based PPO/GRPO (DeepSeek, ProRL), Kimi squared losses, and Rebel are all APMD instances; fixed-reference methods land on the wrong side of the lower bound.
  • Separates the reference policy’s two conflated roles — optimization anchor (central) vs. importance weighting (secondary).
  • Quantifies design trade-offs precisely: mean vs. normalizer baseline (steps vs. samples), clip thresholds tied to η.
  • Exceptional transparency: explicit constants, exact simulation hyperparameters, honest limitations, AI-usage disclosure.

Limitations

  • All end-to-end guarantees assume softmax linear policies over frozen features with linear rewards — real transformers and token-level structure are out of scope.
  • One-shot bandit abstraction: no multi-turn/agentic RL, no credit assignment, no advantage estimation (stylized PPO uses raw rewards).
  • The lower bound is worst-case; no empirical measurement of collapse in production-scale runs.
  • PPO-Lite’s inner loop (non-smooth clipped objective) is left abstract and assumes realizability.
  • Theory covers amplifying base-model coverage only — silent on expanding capability beyond it.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does collapse occur in production? Track probability mass on verifier-passing responses across a DeepSeek-R1/Kimi-style run for the predicted plateau signature.
  2. Advantage estimators as baselines? Do GAE / GRPO group-normalized advantages act like the mean baseline (biasing but convexifying) or like paired differences (unbiased)?
  3. Clip prescriptions vs. practice. Do DAPO-style asymmetric ranges (τ ≈ 0.2) sit near the prescribed [e−2ηRmax, e2ηRmax]?
  4. Adaptive reset schedules. K = 10 beats K = 100 by 5.6× — is there a provably good adaptive K (or η)?
  5. Token-level MDPs. Can the coverage-preserving guarantee survive where response-level ratios explode and per-token KL budgets rule?
  6. Test the prediction. Online DPO vs. Rebel-OnPolicy from the same base model, tracking coverage dynamics end to end.
