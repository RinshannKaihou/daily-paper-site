> **一句话总结：** 该论文发现用 NVFP4（4 位浮点）从零预训练的模型，在验证损失几乎追平 BF16（仅差 0.5%–2%）的情况下，学会一条罕见事实所需的曝光次数却是 BF16 的 1.32–1.52 倍；作者通过受控噪声实验证明这一代价主要来自梯度噪声经 Adam 归一化后对更新信噪比的持续损害（约占三分之二），且调大学习率无法挽回，并提出用"曝光乘数"与无损拟合的检查点梯度统计（Spearman 0.96）来补足损失曲线看不到的评估维度。

> **TL;DR:** Training transformers from scratch with emulated NVFP4 (4-bit) matrix multiplications, the authors show on synthetic biographies (Allen-Zhu & Li bioS setup) and a FineWeb-Edu mixture that NVFP4 needs 1.32× (synthetic) and 1.52× (natural text) as many exposures per fact as the BF16 baseline to reach the same recall at matched training tokens, while final validation loss differs by only 0.1–0.9% and 1.9–2.1% respectively (about 1.2× at matched loss). Controlled noise injections localized to Adam's moments show ~2/3 of the cost is a persistent signal-to-noise penalty in the update that raising the learning rate does not remove, and a fit-free checkpoint diagnostic ranks 17 configurations' costs with Spearman 0.96. Practical implication: judge FP4 recipes by rare-fact recall alongside loss — an adapted NVIDIA recipe closes 60% of the loss gap but only 42% of the exposure cost.

## 核心贡献

- **测量（曝光乘数）：** 提出并形式化了"曝光乘数"（exposure multiplier）指标——低精度模型要达到与参考模型相同召回率所需的额外曝光次数倍数。在合成传记与混入 FineWeb-Edu 网页文本两种设定下，NVFP4 相对 BF16 的乘数为 1.32×（合成）与 1.52×（自然文本，匹配 token 数），而验证损失差距只有 0.5%–2%；匹配损失时约 1.2×。损失曲线基本看不到这一代价。
- **机制（Adam 如何放大梯度噪声）：** 通过向 Adam 的一阶/二阶矩分别注入受控噪声证明：仅进入二阶矩的零均值噪声等价于降低学习率，其代价随训练消退；进入更新本身的噪声则降低信噪比，其代价贯穿训练始终并占约三分之二。按预设判据调大学习率（补偿缩小了的平均步长）对噪声与 NVFP4 均无效。
- **实践（诊断与补救评估）：** 给出无需拟合的检查点诊断——用 BF16 检查点上的梯度统计量按 Eq.(1) 预测各配置代价，在 17 种配置上与实测排序的相关达 Spearman 0.96；同时实测各种补救（适配版 NVIDIA 配方、BF16 冷却、FP8 反向等）都更快恢复损失而非事实习得（损失恢复 60% vs 曝光代价仅降 42%），并建议在评估低精度训练配方时把罕见事实习得与损失并列报告。

## Key Contributions

- **Measurement — the exposure multiplier.** A formalized metric (Definition 1) capturing the horizontal shift, in log2 exposures, between a low-precision run's recall curve and its BF16 reference, averaged over recall levels 20–80%. It isolates a cost that validation loss hides: token-averaged loss weights frequent facts (Proposition 3 shows the loss sees the delay weighted by the learning-curve slope under a frequency-tilted distribution), so a model can match loss while learning rare facts markedly slower.
- **Mechanism — two distinct costs of gradient noise through Adam.** A split-moment intervention (feeding the noisy gradient to only Adam's first or second moment, via a second BF16 backward pass) separates a step-size effect (noise inflates the second-moment normalizer, acting like a lower learning rate; its cost fades over training) from a signal-to-noise effect (noise occupies the update itself; its cost persists through the final learning-rate decay and carries ~2/3 of the total at twice the NVFP4 error). Pre-registered learning-rate compensation tests rule out the "uniformly shrunken step" explanation.
- **Practice — a fit-free diagnostic and honest remedy accounting.** Eq. (1), evaluated purely from BF16-checkpoint gradient statistics (v_i from 32 batches, s_i² per configuration, g_f for 96 facts), ranks the costs of 17 formats/partial-quantizations/noise controls with Spearman 0.96 without fitting — though it ranks without calibrating (it underestimates small errors). All tested remedies (adapted NVIDIA NVFP4 recipe, BF16 cooldown, FP8 backward GEMMs, simplified recipe) recover loss faster than factual acquisition, and none removes the cost.

## Methodology

- **Controlled-fact corpora.** Synthetic biographies follow the bioS construction (Allen-Zhu & Li, 2024): N persons × 6 attributes; one fact = one person's attribute value; one exposure = one six-sentence biography from 5 templates. Persons are split into nine bins seen 2^0…2^8 (1–256) times, fixed across seeds and configurations. Recall is probed (200 persons/bin) with the first training template cut before the value, scored as first-value-token accuracy. In the natural-text regime, 20,000 persons' biographies are inserted at random document boundaries into FineWeb-Edu, forming a 1B-token mixture with 7.2% biography tokens (16k byte-level BPE); validation loss is measured on held-out web text with no inserted biographies.
- **Models and quantization.** Pre-norm transformers (RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, untied embeddings) at 12M/40M/100M (96.6M total params at the largest), trained with AdamW, WSD schedule (2% warmup, linear decay over last 20%), batch 64×512 tokens, on a single RTX 4090. FP4 GEMMs are emulated: operands quantized exactly to the format and multiplied in FP32, isolating rounding from kernel effects. NVFP4 = E2M1 elements in 16-element blocks with E4M3 scales + FP32 tensor scale, stochastic rounding in backward, random 16-point Hadamard transform on weight-gradient inputs; MXFP4 = E2M1 in 32-element blocks with power-of-two scales; FP8 = per-tensor E4M3 forward / E5M2 backward. Embeddings, output head, attention scores, norms, and optimizer state stay high precision; master weights are FP32.
- **Statistical protocol.** The multiplier is the geometric mean over seeds (6–8 seeds for primary comparisons, ≥3 for secondary), paired by seed, with 95% t-intervals on the log2 scale; robustness is checked across recall levels, readouts (exact match, BF16-forward evaluation, correct-value probability, top-5), and fixed-fact resampling. The noise-mechanism experiments inject Gaussian noise shaped like block-scaled rounding (per-tensor RMS of the actual NVFP4 error, scaled per 16-element block) into backward-GEMM outputs of otherwise-BF16 training.
- **Theory.** Proposition 2 derives, under fixed local statistics, that zero-mean gradient error dilutes per-exposure first-order progress by m_f = Σ g²/√v / Σ g²/√(v+s²) (a step-size effect) and lowers the update's mean-to-std ratio by √(1+ρ_i) (a signal-to-noise effect); Proposition 3 shows validation loss weights the learning delay toward frequent facts, explaining why the deficit barely moves loss.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline cost vs. invisible loss gap:** On the 40M synthetic-biography benchmark, NVFP4 needs **1.32× [1.16, 1.50] as many exposures as BF16** to reach the same recall at matched tokens (every seed above 1; sign test p = 0.03) while final validation loss differs by only **0.1–0.9%**; on the FineWeb-Edu natural-text mixture the shift is **1.52× [1.35, 1.71]** with a loss gap of just **+1.9 to +2.1%**. At matched validation loss the multipliers are 1.25× [1.13, 1.38] (synthetic) and 1.22× [1.10, 1.36] (natural). Reading NVFP4 weights with BF16 GEMMs (removing evaluation-time quantization) still leaves 1.26×/1.30×.
- **Format ordering:** MXFP4 costs more than NVFP4 (1.61× [1.38, 1.88] vs. 1.32× at matched tokens; 1.41× vs. 1.25× at matched loss), and NVFP4 costs more than FP8 (paired ratio 1.19× [1.02, 1.40]); FP8 itself shows a small 1.11× cost whose interval touches 1.
- **Backward pass is the invisible part:** Quantizing only backward GEMMs moves validation loss by only **+0.1%** on average yet costs **1.13× [1.04, 1.21]** exposures; forward-only costs 1.18×, and the two approximately multiply to the full effect (1.18 × 1.13 = 1.33 vs. observed 1.32×). Zero-mean block-shaped noise at the NVFP4 error RMS reproduces the backward-only cost (1.11×; 0.99× [0.94, 1.04] relative to backward-only quantization), while i.i.d. noise of equal RMS costs more (1.25×), matching prediction P3's ordering.
- **Split-moment decomposition:** At twice the NVFP4 error, noise in Adam's second moment only matches a pre-registered uniform learning-rate cut (η/A with A = 1.356), ending at 1.02× [0.93, 1.13] of reduced-rate BF16 with its cost fading; noise in both moments costs an additional **1.21× [1.05, 1.39]** over second-moment-only (p = 0.017, 7/8 seeds) — **two-thirds of the 1.33× log-cost** — and does not fade, even during LR decay to zero. Raising the rate by A leaves the cost unchanged (noise: 1.08× [0.95, 1.24] of its default-rate multiplier vs. 0.75 predicted by the shrunken-step hypothesis; NVFP4: 0.99× [0.87, 1.13] vs. 0.76).
- **Checkpoint diagnostic:** Eq. (1) computed from BF16-checkpoint gradient statistics, with nothing fitted to observed multipliers, ranks the observed costs of 17 configurations with **Spearman 0.96 [0.84, 0.99]** and lands inside the observed 95% interval for 15/17 — though it underestimates small errors (1.13 predicted vs. 1.25× observed for 1× i.i.d. noise).
- **Loss-weighting anatomy:** Stratified by exposure bin, NVFP4 raises value-token loss by **0.67–0.85 nats for facts seen 1–4 times** but at most 0.07 nats for facts seen ≥32 times; equal-weight-per-fact the gap is 0.32 nats, but frequency-weighted as in the validation stream it is only 0.04 nats — quantitatively instantiating Proposition 3.
- **Remedies recover loss, not facts:** The adapted NVIDIA NVFP4 recipe removes **60% [29, 79] of the validation-loss gap but only 42% [26, 56] of the log2 exposure cost** (difference 18 pp [2, 29]); on natural text it removes ~an eighth (12% [−75, 45]). Every remedy's token-matched multiplier stays above 1 with intervals excluding 1. The BF16 cooldown does not repair previously learned facts and facts seen only during the cooldown keep 54% [36, 70] of the deficit — evidence of a training-history effect.
- **Robustness:** The cost persists at 100M (1.30× [1.17, 1.45] matched tokens), under linear-decay schedules (1.42×), and after per-format learning-rate selection (1.27× [1.02, 1.58] on selection-free seeds); at 100M the matched-loss cost grows with training budget (1.05× at 0.67 → 1.22× at 2.3 tokens/parameter). A lenient top-5 readout preserves 71–92% of the log-exposure shift across nine seed/configuration pairs.

## Authors & Credibility

Chang Lu (Independent Researcher, corresponding author) and Mengjiao Zhang; a preprint dated November 10, 2026, with no institutional affiliation listed. Despite the independent status, the experimental hygiene is unusually strong for a preprint: predictions and test criteria for the learning-rate compensation and split-moment experiments were fixed before the runs (stated twice, with details in Appendix E); comparisons are paired by seed with 6–8 seeds for primary results; multiple-comparison caveats are stated explicitly for secondary results; and the Limitations section (Section 10) is specific and candid. The work builds directly on established literature (Allen-Zhu & Li's physics-of-language-models bioS setup, NVIDIA's published NVFP4 recipe, Kandpal/Mallen long-tail knowledge results) and cites the current FP4 training wave (NVIDIA 2025, Quartet, FP4 all-the-way, MXFP4 training) accurately.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel angle. Prior low-precision evaluations are loss- or aggregate-benchmark-based; the closest works study post-training quantization knowledge loss (Jaiswal et al. 2024) or ternary-training collapse (Paudel & Sheshappanavar 2026). This paper instead measures *exposure efficiency during training* with production FP4 formats, showing slower learning without collapse, and adds an optimizer-level mechanism with interventional (not just correlational) support.
- **Rigor:** High. Pre-registered predictions, seed-paired designs, robustness across readouts/recall levels/resampled facts, and honest interval reporting (e.g., admitting loss-matched intervals include 1 under correct-value probability and BF16-forward readouts on natural text). The theory is modest in scope and the paper says so (Eq. 1 "prices only the expected step").
- **Reproducibility:** Good in principle — emulated GEMMs, synthetic data with a published generator (bioS), public corpus (FineWeb-Edu), and one GPU (RTX 4090) make the setup cheap to replicate; no code release is mentioned, however, and the exact noise-shaping and split-moment implementations have many details that matter.
- **Significance caveats:** The scale gap is the main one — ≤96.6M parameters and ≤1B tokens (natural-text runs ≈24 tokens/parameter) versus the 12B/10T-token NVFP4 production recipes the paper comments on; whether the multiplier shrinks or grows at production scale is explicitly open, and the paper does not overclaim here. The mechanism evidence (zero-mean noise) strictly covers the backward-pass part; full FP4 training adds biased gradient error, so the two-thirds attribution may not transfer — the authors state this.
- **Weak points to note:** (1) A known confound the authors flag but do not resolve: the BF16-cooldown result (facts seen only in BF16 keeping 54% of the deficit) suggests delayed formation of recall-supporting circuits, an alternative to the per-exposure noise account that the experiments cannot separate. (2) The Eq. (1) diagnostic underestimates small errors and depends on how the normalizer is estimated, and was tested at only one model size. (3) All runs share one data order, so seed intervals understate total variability. (4) The loss-matched control on natural text compares BF16 at step 28,700–28,900 (mid-decay) — different training stages, biasing that multiplier toward 1, which the authors disclose but which slightly weakens the headline "1.2× at matched loss." (5) Synthetic-template probes (no paraphrases) may overstate what "recall" means for real long-tail knowledge.

## Strengths

- A crisp, answerable question ("does matching loss mean matching fact acquisition?") with a metric (exposure multiplier) that is invariant to monotone recall transforms (Proposition 1) and easy to reuse.
- Interventional mechanism analysis: splitting gradient noise between Adam's moments is a clean causal design, and the pre-specified learning-rate compensation tests are the right falsification move.
- The fit-free checkpoint diagnostic (Spearman 0.96 across 17 configurations) is immediately actionable for practitioners screening FP4 recipe candidates without retraining.
- Quantitative anatomy of *why* loss is blind (0.67–0.85 nats for 1–4-exposure facts shrinking to 0.04 nats after frequency weighting) makes Proposition 3 concrete rather than abstract.
- Transparent statistics: geometric means with 95% intervals, sign tests, paired t-tests with Holm correction, Fieller intervals for share estimates, and disclosed fragility of secondary readouts.

## Limitations

- Scale: models ≤96.6M parameters, ≤1B tokens, one GPU; no native FP4 hardware (no Blackwell kernel validation, no throughput numbers), so the wall-clock economics of FP4 — the very reason for its use — are outside the analysis.
- External validity: facts are synthetic biographies (fixed templates, probed with a seen template, no paraphrased questions) rather than naturally occurring long-tail knowledge; only AdamW is tested (SGD-with-momentum analysis is theoretical; Muon untested); one alternative LR schedule; a three-point learning-rate sweep with three seeds cannot exclude an untested rate closing the gap.
- Mechanistic scope: the zero-mean-noise account covers the backward pass; full FP4's biased gradient error is handled only diagnostically. The linearized model's link from weight spread to recall is heuristic; forgetting between exposures, curvature, and fact interactions are omitted.
- Estimation caveats: one shared data order across seeds; Eq. (1) depends on normalizer estimation and uses final-checkpoint statistics rather than trajectory statistics; loss-matched multipliers are readout-sensitive (intervals include 1 under some readouts).

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the exposure multiplier shrink, hold, or grow at production scale (e.g., ≥10B parameters, ≥10T tokens), where gradient-noise-to-signal ratios and tokens-per-parameter both differ substantially from these runs?
- Would Muon or other matrix-based optimizers, whose normalization differs from Adam's coordinatewise second moment, avoid the signal-to-noise cost the paper attributes ~2/3 of the damage to?
- Can the training-history effect suggested by the BF16-cooldown experiment (facts seen only in BF16 keeping 54% of the deficit) be separated from per-exposure slowing — e.g., by measuring when recall-supporting attention circuits form under each precision?
- Does the deficit extend to naturally distributed long-tail knowledge (rare entities, low-resource languages) and to paraphrased or multi-hop probes, where extraction difficulty may interact with precision?
- Since the cost acts approximately as a constant exposure delay, what is the optimal re-weighting of knowledge-dense data (sampling it m× more often) in an FP4 run, and what does that do to non-factual capabilities and to loss?
- Could error-shaping tricks (making quantization noise follow the gradient's per-coordinate scale, as block vs. i.i.d. noise suggests) be built into FP4 rounding schemes to attack the ρ_i = s_i²/v_i ratio directly?
