> **一句话总结：** 来自蚂蚁国际与浙江大学的 VbD（Verify by Doing）在单个任务内、模型权重冻结的条件下，让 Actor 执行 harness 与 Evaluator 验证 harness 协同演化——Evaluator 用工具主动检查工件与环境状态（如读取电子表格单元格、运行探针），把"验证发现"转化为既指导输出选择、又指导双 harness 更新的内部信号，并通过比较式评审与独立采纳规则防止不完整验证误导更新——在 AutomationBench、τ³ Banking 和 GDPval 子集上分别以 3.3、4.1、2.3 个百分点超过最强无外部反馈基线（DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 主干）。

> **TL;DR:** VbD tackles test-time agent self-improvement when benchmark scores and reference answers are unavailable: an Evaluator harness actively inspects artifacts/environment state with tools to produce verification findings, which (a) ground an internal preference signal for selecting among candidates and (b) drive edits to both the Actor's execution harness and the Evaluator's own checking procedure within one task, with a comparative two-order review gating Evaluator changes and strict fresh-trial support gating Actor changes. On DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 it scores 37.0% overall on AutomationBench (vs 33.7% for the strongest baseline RQGM, +3.3 pp), 49.5% on τ³ Banking (vs RHI's 45.4%, +4.1 pp), and 82.4% GDPval rubric quality (vs RQGM's 80.1%, +2.3 pp); gains hold on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (48.4% / 57.4% / 84.7%). The most instructive experiment holds BoN candidates fixed: VbD's evolved Evaluator lifts pass rate from 31.2% to 35.8% purely by selecting better from the same six outputs — improved verification alone recovers successful work without generating anything new. An external-feedback reference still leads by 5.6 pp on AutomationBench, honestly quantifying the remaining gap.

## 核心贡献

- **把"验证本身"作为测试时改进的对象（Verify by Doing）**：不同于只用工具收集信息或只改执行流程的做法，VbD 让 Evaluator 用工具主动检查工件与环境状态（读取工作簿单元格、运行探针、检索源记录），把验证从"看轨迹"变成"做实验"，为判断任务完成提供证据。
- **证据驱动的 Actor-Evaluator 双 harness 协同演化**：同一条"验证发现"同时流入两个方向——既变成 Actor 的可复用技能/指令（例如"再试一次受支持的读取"），也变成 Evaluator 的持久检查项（例如"核实'资源不可用'的声明"）；任务需求与模型权重全程冻结，只有 harness 在变。
- **比较式评审 + 独立采纳规则，防止不完整验证误导更新**：Evaluator 的提案要在相同候选集上与保留版对比评审（两种呈现顺序都必须支持提案，抑制位置偏差）；Actor 的采纳则要求提案 harness 自己的新鲜输出获得严格支持——"选中早先的成功输出"不会误判为"Actor 变强了"，输出选择与 harness 采纳是两个独立决策。
- **无需外部反馈的内部信号替代基准分数**：在 AutomationBench、τ³ Banking、GDPval 子集三个任务族、两个主干模型上全面领先所有无外部反馈的对比方法；固定候选池实验证明改进后的验证能从既有输出中"找回"成功结果，消融分别支持主动验证（−2.7 pp）与 Evaluator 演化（−1.6 pp）的贡献。

## Key Contributions

- **Active verification as a first-class target of test-time improvement.** Instead of judging only from the Actor's trace, the Evaluator interacts with the environment (cell reads, probes, source retrieval) and records structured findings — candidate, proposition, observations/sources, and a scoped assessment (Field / Relation / Aggregate / Task scopes) with uncertainty — so evidence, not vibes, grounds the internal signal.
- **Within-task co-evolution of execution and verification harnesses.** A fixed editor translates each finding into joint proposals: the same observation can become an Actor instruction (how to act) and an Evaluator check (how to judge), so a locally discovered fact (e.g., a supposedly unavailable contact sheet was actually retrievable) turns into both a better way to execute and a better way to verify.
- **Comparative review and separate retention rules.** Proposed Evaluators are reviewed against the retained version on common candidates in both presentation orders (position-bias control, Eq. 3); Actor adoption additionally requires strict support for the proposal's own fresh output (Eq. 5). This decouples "selecting the best available output" from "adopting a changed Actor" — an often-conflated distinction in self-improvement loops.
- **Empirical validation without external feedback.** Highest overall scores among compared no-external-feedback methods across three task families and two backbones, with ablations isolating active verification from Evaluator evolution, and a fixed-candidate-pool experiment showing selection-only gains.

## Methodology

VbD operates on a single task x (request, materials, permitted tools) with model weights frozen. Two harnesses are maintained: an Actor harness H (instructions, skills, working rubrics, persistent check plans) and an Evaluator harness Φ (instructions, skills, rubrics). The retained state comprises an incumbent candidate z, candidate set G, and history D; everything resets between tasks and the final output is frozen before offline scoring.

Each of three rounds runs four steps:

1. **Actor Trials.** The current and proposed Actor harnesses each execute the task once (two fresh candidates per round; both get the same temporary checks, only the proposal gets persistent edits), preserving execution record, resulting state, and deliverable for later inspection.
2. **Agentic Evaluation.** Both Evaluators independently inspect a common pool B of candidates with tools, emitting findings F_Φ(B) = {(candidate, proposition, observations/sources, scoped assessment)}. A fixed selector Π interprets findings against the request to produce decision d — a preference among candidates that constitutes the internal signal (Eq. 2).
3. **Comparative Selection.** (i) *Evaluator adoption*: a fixed reviewer compares the proposed vs retained Evaluator's reports on the same candidates (evidential support, criterion applicability, counterexamples); both orders must favor the proposal. (ii) *Output selection*: the retained Evaluator re-inspects eligible candidates and the selector picks the output. (iii) *Actor retention*: the proposed Actor is adopted only if its own fresh output wins strict comparisons (s1 ∧ s2 ∧ z+ = b) — selecting an earlier output leaves the Actor unchanged.
4. **Harness Co-evolution.** A fixed editor L_edit consumes findings, decisions, and history to propose updates to either or both harnesses, each with an expected effect and a check for the next trial; rejected trials and observation sources stay in history to inform later proposals.

Experimental setup: main backbone DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 (284B total / 13B activated) plays all roles; Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (125B / 6B) as second backbone; offline grading with GPT-5.6-sol (xhigh). Benchmarks: AutomationBench (600 tasks, 6 workflow domains, scored via saved application state), τ³ Banking (97 tasks, dialogue+action policy compliance), and 133 of GDPval's 220 public gold tasks (selected for text/document-tool support, 9 sectors, 41 occupations; local rubric scores, not official pairwise win rates). Baselines: Base (Pass@1), BoN-6 with VbD's initial Evaluator, self-preference, HGM, RHO, RHI, and RQGM — the latter adapted from their original external-feedback formulations to within-task observations — plus an unranked external-feedback reference (HGM search with benchmark scores).

## Results & Highlights

- **AutomationBench (600 tasks, pass rate, DeepSeek backbone):** VbD reaches 37.0±1.9% overall vs the strongest baseline RQGM at 33.7±2.2% (+3.3 pp); BoN is 31.2±2.1% and the base model 22.3±1.6%. Its Round-2 mean (34.3%) already exceeds RQGM's final score at lower cumulative token cost. The external-feedback reference is 42.6% — VbD closes most of, but not all, the gap (+5.6 pp remaining).
- **τ³ Banking (97 tasks, pass rate):** VbD scores 49.5±2.1% vs RHI's 45.4±2.1% (+4.1 pp over the strongest baseline; RQGM gets 41.2±1.0%), a +14.4 pp gain over the base model's 35.1%.
- **GDPval subset (133 tasks, rubric quality):** VbD attains 82.4±1.4% overall vs RQGM's 80.1±1.7% (+2.3 pp), leading in professional deliverables across sectors; with Qwen3.8-Flash-Next it reaches 84.7±2.0% vs RQGM's 83.1±1.6%, and AutomationBench/Banking become 48.4% and 57.4% (vs RQGM's 45.1% and RHI's 53.6%).
- **Selection-only gains on fixed candidate pools (600 AutomationBench tasks):** given the same six BoN outputs per task, VbD's evolved Evaluator selects a pass rate of 35.8% vs 31.2% for BoN's initial Evaluator (+4.6 pp) and 33.4% for RQGM's (+2.4 pp), with partial credit 69.8% vs 67.4%/68.5% — improved verification recovers successful outputs without any new generation.
- **Actor update gating quality:** labeled by offline partial credit, VbD rejects 89.3% of regressions (TNR) vs 71.2% for RQGM (+18.1 pp) at essentially unchanged acceptance precision (77.6% vs 77.0%).
- **Ablations:** removing active verification drops AutomationBench overall by 2.7 pp (37.0 → 34.3) and removing Evaluator evolution by 1.6 pp (→ 35.4), with both variants lower on all three task sets; partial credit rises 66.35% → 70.26% (+3.91 pp) across rounds, and attempts containing state verification grow from 20.4% (Round 1) to 25.3% (Round 3).

## Authors & Credibility

The work is from Ant International (a major fintech with strong applied-agent incentives and prior agentic-evaluation publications among the citations) and Zhejiang University, with equal contributions by the first two authors. Credibility signals are good: three-run mean ± SD reporting on every table, explicit separation of the unranked external-feedback reference from ranked comparisons, careful baseline adaptations documented against their original formulations (Appendix B.3, Table 7), a labeled-gating analysis (TP/FP/TN) rather than only end scores, per-round cost-accuracy curves, and honest failure case studies (e.g., Finance024 selecting a failing output after misreading a file timestamp as a business date). All benchmarks are public (AutomationBench v1.0.5, τ³ Banking, GDPval gold tasks), and the method is model-agnostic given harness-editable agents, though harness prompts and role specifications (Appendix E) are needed for exact reproduction.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Real, though incremental relative to a crowded 2026 self-improvement literature. The distinctive move is making the verification procedure itself evolvable within a task with no external signal — RQGM co-evolves agents and evaluators but replaces evaluators using held-out ground truth, and RHO/RHI/TTHE evolve only the execution side. The separate-retention rule (output selection ≠ Actor adoption) is a clean, well-motivated contribution that others conflate.
- **Rigor:** Generally strong (3 runs with SD, two backbones, two-order review against position bias, fixed-pool selection isolation), but there are soft spots. All comparisons rest on 3 runs with SDs of 1.4–3.5 pp against margins of 2.3–4.1 pp — mostly beyond one SD, but no formal significance testing in the main tables (behavioral bootstrap intervals are relegated to Appendix D.3). The GDPval evaluation uses only 133 of 220 public gold tasks, selected by the authors' own tool-support criteria and scored with local rubrics by GPT-5.6-sol rather than the official expert pairwise win rate — a defensible but non-standard protocol that both shrinks the benchmark and replaces its native metric.
- **Baseline fairness:** HGM and RQGM are authors' adaptations — their benchmark-utility/labeled-anchor mechanisms are substituted with "permitted task observations," which may understate them in their native (cross-task, external-feedback) regime; conversely, the comparison setting is the paper's point, and the adaptations are documented in unusual detail.
- **Reproducibility:** Public benchmarks, frozen weights, named models, and configuration tables (Tables 4–6) help, but the system has many moving parts (editor, reviewer, selector, eligibility/acceptance rules, harness templates in Appendix E); the no-code-release status at review time makes exact reproduction a substantial engineering effort.
- **Red flags / overclaiming:** Largely avoided — the paper repeatedly quantifies the remaining external-feedback gap (5.6 pp on AutomationBench, 6.9 pp on Banking) instead of hiding it. One nuance worth noting: VbD is not uniformly best per domain — on AutomationBench-HR it scores 9.7% vs RQGM's 10.7% — and Banking's user simulator plus policy-prerequisite access are shared scaffolding that eases verification relative to fully opaque environments.

## Strengths

- Frames and addresses the genuinely under-specified problem of incomplete self-verification misguiding self-improvement, with a mechanism (finding → joint Actor/Evaluator edits) that is concrete and traceable in case studies.
- The fixed-candidate-pool experiment is the cleanest evidence in the paper: it isolates pure selection quality (31.2% → 35.8% pass rate) from generation improvements, directly supporting the "verification is a target of improvement" thesis.
- Design hygiene throughout: two-order comparative review to blunt position bias, separate retention rules to decouple selection from adoption, regression-rejection (TNR 89.3%) reporting alongside end scores, and cost-aware round-by-round curves (Round-2 beats RQGM's final at lower token cost).
- Honest negative evidence: the Finance024 timestamp-misreading case and the explicit gap to the external-feedback reference make the claims easier to trust.

## Limitations

- Verification depends on inspectable, isolatable state: irreversible actions, non-resettable environments, delayed effects, or inaccessible state escape the method — a real constraint for exactly the deployment domains (production systems) the authors care about.
- Actor, Evaluator, editor, and reviewer share one backbone, so correlated misinterpretations of evidence can survive comparative review (as Finance024 shows); no ensemble or external check breaks this loop.
- Only three rounds of within-task adaptation with six total executions are tested; cross-task transfer of evolved harnesses, longer-horizon evolution, and the risk of verification criteria overfitting single tasks are untested.
- Substantial inference cost (tens of millions of tokens per task on the AutomationBench cost curve) and added latency from inspection, review, and re-execution; per-domain losses (HR on AutomationBench) suggest the gains are not universal across task types.

## Relevance

这与我关注的"无需外部反馈的 agent 自我改进/测试时适应"方向高度相关：VbD 把验证器本身变成可演化的对象，正是解决自改进循环中"反馈不可靠"这一核心痛点的直接尝试，其"输出选择与 harness 采纳分离"的设计对任何做 agent 自评估、self-play 或 harness 搜索的工作都有借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do evolved Evaluator harnesses transfer across tasks or domains, or do the learned checks overfit the specific task's artifacts (the paper resets harnesses between tasks and leaves transfer untested)?
- How much of the +3.3/+4.1/+2.3 pp gain survives when the reviewer/editor use a different (weaker or stronger) backbone than the Actor/Evaluator, breaking the shared-error correlation?
- Can the comparative-review gate be replaced or supplemented with cheap statistical controls (e.g., consistency across paraphrased criteria) to cut the tens-of-millions-of-tokens-per-task inference cost?
- The external-feedback reference still leads by 5.6 pp on AutomationBench — what fraction of that gap is verification quality vs. search guidance, and does combining VbD's evolved Evaluator with occasional ground-truth checks (every k-th task) close it?
- How does VbD behave in environments with irreversible actions or non-resettable state, where repeated trials are impossible — could counterfactual replay or simulator surrogates substitute for fresh trials?
