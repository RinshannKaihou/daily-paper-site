Key Finding · arXiv 2611.15842

Leave the router untouched: swapping LRU eviction for Belady’s farthest-future rule cuts expert copies 2.1× with zero quality loss.

Why This Matters

For MoE serving on bandwidth-starved GPUs, eviction — not router fine-tuning — is the lever that cuts CPU→GPU expert copies; a cautionary tale for simulated metrics.

Critical Edge

The headline 2.1× comes from an offline oracle — no deployable policy in the paper recovers more than 27–33% of its benefit.

Read the number as an upper bound, not a shipping speedup

Methodology

model OLMoE-1B-7B 8/16 MoE layers offloaded 64 bf16 experts / layer 32 GPU cache slots L40S, τ = 0.51 ms/copy

The router is frozen (pre-trained to reuse experts, ReMoE-style); policies differ only in how they manage the 32 cache slots:

  • LRU — lossless: copies every missing expert on demand, never alters routing.
  • Dwell-k / Static — hold the set fixed for k tokens or the whole sequence; the router must pick cached experts (can change output).
  • Learned βµ controller — reimplemented option-critic routing: termination head β triggers replacement, selection head µ picks the new set.
  • Farthest-future (Belady) — evict the resident expert whose next use is farthest away; applied offline to the LRU trace, valid because lossless eviction provably never changes requests.

All policies decode the same 16 held-out sequences (≤384 tokens, teacher-forced, batch 1), logging next-token NLL, tok/s, and steady-state copies. Decode time is modeled as ms/token = c + ρ·τ and validated by repeating each copy 10×/50×. Lossless invariants are checked every run (±5×10⁻³ NLL vs full residency); greedy Belady is verified optimal by exact DP on 4.2×10⁴ random instances.

Beyond the headline

  • GRPO router fine-tuning fails to transfer: rewarded for simulated cache-hit rate, it copies slightly more than the untuned base (32,050 vs 31,915, +0.4%) — despite better training logs.
  • MMLU-5k: learned seeds retain 85.2% ± 1.3 of free-routing accuracy vs 88.9% for the simple Dwell heuristic (free routing: 48.4%).
  • Free, deployable win: causal decayed-frequency (LFU) eviction removes 12–15% of transfers losslessly, for every router variant.
  • Platform rule: copy time τ spans 5× across GPUs (0.24 ms H100/H200 → 1.17 ms RTX 8000); one startup measurement of c and τ picks the fastest policy — confirmed at 10×/50× costs.

Strengths

  • Measures the thing that matters — timed CPU→GPU copies — not the switch-count proxy, and proves the two diverge with a counterexample.
  • The Belady result is lossless by construction: ΔNLL = 0.000 exactly, with a 3.5%-of-optimum certificate (lower-bound proof adapted to k-experts-per-token; greedy checked by exact DP).
  • Equation (1) turns platform choice into a measurable startup decision, validated at 10× and 50× copy costs.
  • Negative results reported with the same care as positive ones — including against the authors’ own GRPO router — with controls (shuffled labels, SFT control) ruling out trivial explanations.

Limitations

  • The best rule needs the future: deployable approximations recover only 27–33% of its benefit; closing the gap is left open.
  • Small scale: one 1B-active model, 16 sequences ≤384 tokens, batch-1, teacher-forced, blocking (non-overlapped) copies; batching effects untested.
  • The learned-controller verdict is provisional — a reimplementation with hand-set loss weights, not the published checkpoint.
  • Several raw logs were discarded (second shard, MMLU per-question, GRPO simulator): aggregate summaries only, no error bars, no significance tests.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can router logits, hidden states, or speculative-decoding drafts predict expert demand 8–16 tokens ahead, closing the 27–33% → 96% foresight gap?
  • Under batched decoding and async copy overlap (latency → max(c, ρτ)), does eviction still dominate routing on high-bandwidth GPUs (τ = 0.24 ms)?
  • Does the switch≠copy dissociation hold for other MoE families — Mixtral, DeepSeek-V3-style fine-grained experts — whose routing locality differs?
  • Is greedy farthest-future eviction provably optimal for k-experts-per-token requests (the open B* question)?
  • Could prefetching from short-horizon demand forecasts eliminate blocking misses without exceeding the proven lower bound?
