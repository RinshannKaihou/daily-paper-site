> **一句话总结：** 本文发现低比特世界模型中"确定性的舍入误差"会被迭代式采样规划器（MPPI/CEM）系统性利用而导致规划崩溃（Meta-World 上成功率最多掉 100 分），而误差大小并不预测损害；提出免训练的 sh-iter 方案——同一次迭代内所有候选动作共享一份随机 dither（随机舍入）、每次前向重抽——在全部 19 个受影响的 Meta-World 单元中至少消除一半损害（中位数 95%），且 W8A4 整数核在 CUDA graph 下比 FP32 快 1.9–3.3×（dither 开销 ≤2.3%）。

> **TL;DR:** Round-to-nearest activation quantization of TD-MPC2/DINO-WM world models can collapse planning by up to 100 success points on Meta-World even when its latent one-step error is *smaller* than a stochastic-rounding variant that plans fine — the damage comes from the error being persistent across planner iterations and shared across candidates, which the planner exploits. A training-free fix (sh-iter: share one dither draw across all candidates, redraw every forward call) removes ≥50% of the damage in all 19 affected TD-MPC2 Meta-World cells (median 95%, e.g. det loses 44.6 points on average vs 4.0 for sh-iter) and in 8/8 tasks of a 317M multi-task TD-MPC2, but fails on DMC (helps in only 5/12 cells, harmful on walker at 3 bits). An integer W8A4 kernel with CUDA graphs runs rollouts 1.9–3.3× faster than FP32 (though slower than pre-converted BF16), with shared dither costing ≤2.3% latency; pre-registered hypotheses with failed tests reported.

## 核心贡献

- **现象（量化规划崩溃与误差大小脱钩）**：在相同比特宽度下，round-to-nearest（det）激活量化可使 TD-MPC2 规划成功率下降多达 100 分（mw-button-press，2 比特），但在 36 个受影响单元中有 29 个，det 的潜空间一步预测误差并不比规划良好的随机舍入方案 sh-iter 更大——离线预测误差无法暴露这类规划损害。
- **机制（规划器利用"持续且共享"的误差）**：确定性舍入误差在一次规划迭代内对所有候选共享、且跨迭代持续存在，迭代式规划器因此把采样分布漂向误差偏袒的候选。正对照实验证明：把同一份共享 dither 固定在一个控制步或整个 episode 内（sh-step/sh-ep），即可复现 det 漂移的 70–130%；而 sh-iter 只保留 det 漂移的至多 24%。收益随 MPPI 迭代次数增长（12 次迭代时 sh-iter 损害仅 0–11%，det 保持 43–47%），并随候选数量增长（优化者诅咒的剂量响应）。
- **处方（免训练修复 sh-iter）**：同层共享一份 dither 抽样（迭代内比较公平，等价于 common random numbers）、每次前向调用重抽（规划器无法跨迭代利用）。在 TD-MPC2 5M 的全部 19 个受影响 Meta-World 单元中至少消除一半损害（中位数 95%）且从不有害；在 DINO-WM 的 5 个单元中消除 4 个；在 317M 多任务模型的全部 8 个受影响任务中消除 75–104% 的损害。
- **部署（整数核与 FP4 边界）**：fused Triton W8A4 核（int8 权重、4 比特激活码、GPU 上抽共享 dither）以 CUDA graph 复现伪量化结果，世界模型 rollout 在 A40 上比 FP32 快 1.9–3.3×（整步 1.28–1.49×，dither 开销 ≤2.3%）；块缩放 FP4 格式（NVFP4/MXFP4）大幅缩小损害面，将失败归因于带共享缩放的粗网格。
- **方法论（预注册实证研究）**：所有假设、阈值、种子与分析代码在数据产生前冻结进带哈希的设计文档；失败的假设（H2a/H2b、H4b、L2c、S2、F1 的 NVFP4 部分、H3b 的 DINO-WM 部分）全部如实报告。

## Key Contributions

- **Phenomenon**: At fixed bit width, round-to-nearest (det) activation quantization causes planning collapse (up to 100 success points on Meta-World) that the world model's latent one-step error does not reveal: in 29 of 36 affected cells det's one-step error is no larger than that of sh-iter, a stochastic variant that plans well (e.g. mw-pick-place at 3 bits: error 0.22 vs 0.39, yet det loses 37.8 points vs 7.8).
- **Mechanism**: The planner exploits error that *persists* across iterations and is *shared* across candidates. Positive controls that fix one shared dither for a control step (sh-step) or an episode (sh-ep) reproduce 70–130% of det's favorable drift; sh-iter retains ≤24% of it. The benefit requires an iterating planner (useless at 1 MPPI iteration) and grows with candidate count (optimizer's-curse dose-response) and with dither-table size (damage shrinks as a fixed table of independent offsets grows; a single coarse offset per layer collapsed Wall at 3 bits by +37 points).
- **Theory**: For planners averaging fresh unbiased noise the expected drift is zero; a persistent error field instead pulls the population toward the minimizer of the smoothed model cost (sample-average-approximation bias). Derived selection and drift terms matched 922k simulated MPPI/CEM runs with fitted slopes 0.92–1.05.
- **Prescription (sh-iter)**: Training-free, model-free fix — share one dither draw per layer across all candidates (fair within-iteration comparison) and redraw it at every forward call (denies cross-iteration exploitation). Removes ≥50% of damage in 19/19 affected Meta-World cells (median 95%), 4/5 DINO-WM cells, and 8/8 affected tasks of a 317M multi-task TD-MPC2.
- **Deployment**: A W8A4 integer TD-MPC2 world model (int8 weights, 4-bit activations, int8 tensor-core GEMMs, first layer FP32) captured as CUDA graphs runs planning rollouts 1.9–3.3× faster than FP32 in all 12 conditions on an A40 (5M–317M params, N ∈ {512, 2048, 8192}) and the whole planning step 1.28–1.49× faster, with shared dither adding at most 2.3% (rollout) / 0.7% (step) latency. Block-scaled FP4 shrinks the failure surface: det damages only 2 of 12 tasks under NVFP4 vs 71.6% return loss on cheetah-run with a uniform 4-bit grid.
- **Registered-report methodology**: Hypotheses, thresholds, seeds and analysis code frozen in hashed design documents before each stage's data; ~147 GPU-hours (RTX A5000) for the main study; failed hypotheses reported in Table I.

## Methodology

The study is a controlled, pre-registered empirical comparison of rounding rules for activation quantization of learned world models used by sampling-based planners.

**Models and planners.** (1) TD-MPC2 official 5M single-task checkpoints (three seeds) on 8 Meta-World tasks (success rate) and 4 DMC tasks (return), planned with the default MPPI (512 samples including 24 policy trajectories, 64 elites, 6 iterations, horizon 3); (2) the official 317M multi-task checkpoint (mt80) on the same 8 Meta-World tasks; (3) DINO-WM official PushT/Wall/PointMaze models with open-loop CEM (300 samples, top 30, horizon 5, 30 iterations on PushT, 10 otherwise).

**Quantization.** Only hidden activations are quantized: the four hidden layers of the TD-MPC2 dynamics and reward MLPs (after Mish) and the six MLP outputs of the DINO-WM predictor (after GELU), to k bits on a uniform [min, q99.99] grid calibrated per layer on held-out data. Inputs, weights and all other modules stay in floating point except in the Sec. VI integer kernels. A pre-fixed rule picks the primary bit width per task as the largest k at which det loses ≥20 points of success (≥20% return on DMC), and k−1, k, k+1 are tested (36 cells at 5M, 9 for DINO-WM, primary k of 3–5 bits at 317M).

**Rounding variants** (all stochastic variants share the same unbiased dither, hence the same per-entry error distribution; they differ only in how u is shared and how long it persists): `det` (round-to-nearest), `ind` (independent draw per candidate per call), `sh-iter` (one draw per layer shared across candidates, redrawn every forward call), `sh-step`/`sh-ep` (same shared draw held fixed within a control step / an episode; coincide as `sh-plan` in open-loop CEM), `det+shift` (round-to-nearest on a grid shifted by a fresh offset each call).

**Statistics.** Every variant is paired with the unquantized model on the same episode seeds and planner noise (90 pairs per TD-MPC2 cell, 100 per DINO-WM cell). Damage D is the paired drop from the FP baseline; a cell is "affected" if det's drop is real under a one-sided paired bootstrap (10^4 resamples) with Holm correction; damage-removed fraction red = (D_det − D_v)/D_det; a variant is harmful if the 95% CI of D_v − D_det lies above 5 points. The optimizer exploitation index OEI = e(a_exec) − ē_first decomposes exactly into a selection term and a drift term. Latency is measured with a fused Triton W8A4 kernel replayed as CUDA graphs (matching TD-MPC2's compiled planner), and FP4 formats (NVFP4: blocks of 16 with E4M3 scale; MXFP4: blocks of 32 with power-of-two scale; both rounding to the E2M1 grid) are tested on an RTX 5090.

## Results & Highlights

- **Planning collapse invisible to one-step error (Meta-World, TD-MPC2 5M)**: det costs up to 100 success points (mw-button-press at 2 bits); across the 19 affected Meta-World cells the mean drop is 44.6 points under det vs 4.0 under sh-iter and 4.0 under det+shift, while the persistence controls lose 50.1 (sh-ep), 34.9 (sh-step) and 29.2 (ind) points. Example: mw-pick-place at 3 bits, det's latent one-step error 0.22 vs 0.39 for sh-iter, yet det loses 37.8 success points vs 7.8 for sh-iter.
- **Prescription holds across scales**: sh-iter removes ≥50% of det's damage in all 19 affected Meta-World cells (median 95%), 4 of 5 DINO-WM cells (exception: PushT at 5 bits, where det only loses 5 points), and 8 of 8 affected tasks of the 317M multi-task TD-MPC2 — removing 75–104% of damage (mw-peg-insert-side: 98.9 points lost under det vs 4.4 under sh-iter), with the 95% CI of D_sh-iter − D_det below zero in every task. The weakest Meta-World cell (window-open, 4 bits) keeps 47% of the damage.
- **Honest negative on DMC**: sh-iter removes ≥half the damage in only 5 of 12 DMC cells and is harmful on walker at 3 bits (78.9% of return lost vs 56.9% under det). A rule added after the first stage and validated on new seeds explains this: the persistent share of det's favorable error (drift/OEI) is 0.33–0.52 on DMC vs 0.54–0.73 on Meta-World, and predicts damage removed across the 12 TD-MPC2 tasks with Spearman 0.83.
- **Mechanism evidence**: sh-iter keeps at most 24% of det's favorable drift (ratios 0.10, 0.24, 0.03, −0.01 across the four mechanism tasks) and det+shift at most 15%; fixing the shared dither for a step/episode brings back 70–130% of det's drift. At 12 MPPI iterations sh-iter's damage is 0–11% while det stays at 43–47% and sh-step at 18–47%; with 1 iteration sh-iter is no better than det. Derived selection/drift terms match 922k simulated MPPI and CEM runs (fitted slopes 0.92–1.05).
- **Latency (A40, CUDA graphs)**: the W8A4 integer rollout is 1.9–3.3× faster than FP32 in all 12 conditions (5M–317M params, N ∈ {512, 2048, 8192}), replicated on a second A40; shared dither changes rollout latency by −0.2% to +2.3%. The whole planning step gains 1.28–1.49× over FP32 (dynamics+reward are only 40–52% of the step). However, pre-converted BF16 is faster than the integer path (rollouts take 1.07–1.52× its time; with a TF32 first layer, post hoc, 0.72–0.96×, not evaluated in closed loop), and the eager integer kernel was 18% slower than FP32 (kernel-launch bound).
- **FP4 boundary (RTX 5090)**: an NVFP4 GEMM at the 317M layer width is up to 5.3× faster than BF16. In closed loop, det damages only 2 of 12 tasks under NVFP4 (cheetah-run 9.9%, walker 1.7% — below the pre-registered threshold of 3) and 4 of 12 under MXFP4 (cheetah-run 31.3%, window-open 10.0 points), vs 71.6% return loss on cheetah-run with a uniform 4-bit grid; in all six damaged cells det's one-step error was 0.48–0.63× sh-iter's, the same signature as uniform grids.

## Authors & Credibility

Two authors from Chungbuk National University (Republic of Korea): Yong Hwi Song (School of Information and Communication Engineering) and Keon Myung Lee (School of Software; kmlee@cbnu.ac.kr, a long-tenured professor there). This is not a frontier-lab or big-budget effort — the main study used about 147 GPU-hours on RTX A5000 GPUs, with extensions on cloud A40/RTX 4090/RTX 5090 — which makes the statistical care more notable: pre-registered hypotheses frozen in hashed design documents, paired bootstrap with Holm correction, positive controls (sh-step/sh-ep, det+shift, dither-table size), a dose-response manipulation (candidate count), and explicit reporting of failed hypotheses (Table I) and post-hoc analyses. Code, design documents and hashes are promised with the final paper. The bibliography mixes canonical works (Roberts' and Schuchman's dither theory, Smith & Winkler's optimizer's curse, stochastic rounding of Gupta et al./Croci et al.) with a dense cluster of 2026 arXiv/OpenReview preprints on world-model quantization, indicating a very fast-moving niche that this paper enters with unusual methodological discipline.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The ingredients are classical (dither, unbiased stochastic rounding, common random numbers, the optimizer's curse, SAA bias), and the authors say so. The genuine novelty is the controlled *factorial separation* of sharing vs persistence at a fixed per-entry error distribution inside closed-loop planners, and the demonstration that a training-free change of the rounding rule fixes what bit width and offline error metrics cannot see. It is a diagnostic-plus-patch paper, not a new algorithm family — but the patch is cheap and the diagnosis is the kind practitioners rarely establish.
- **Rigor**: Above average for an empirical ML-systems paper: pre-registration with hashes, paired seeds/planner noise, one-sided bootstrap with Holm correction, 90–100 pairs per cell, positive controls that isolate persistence, and honest reporting that the original OEI hypotheses (H2a/H2b) failed and the claim was narrowed to drift. Two caveats: the DMC explanatory rule (persistent share) was added after the first stage (disclosed with a dagger, tested on new seeds), and the reward-error separation was explicitly "observed after the fact".
- **Conditioning to note**: the headline numbers are conditioned on *affected* cells — the primary bit width per task is pre-defined as the largest k where det loses ≥20 points, so cells where det does not hurt are excluded by construction. This is appropriate for studying the failure, but "median 95% removed" should not be read as an expectation over arbitrary quantization configs.
- **Reproducibility**: Good in principle (official checkpoints, official planner defaults, variants differing in one factor, promised code + design docs). Remaining frictions: the 317M results come from a single checkpoint, the FP4 results from fake quantization of the 5M models rather than the fused kernels, and the latency map from a single GPU type — with the strongest competitor (pre-converted BF16) never evaluated in closed loop, so the practical speed story is not yet end-to-end.
- **Significance**: The prescription matters wherever iterating sampling planners sit on top of quantized world models — exactly the on-robot efficiency path the field is pushing. But the deployment message is more muted than the abstract: the integer path is *not* faster than pre-converted BF16 (1.07–1.52× its rollout time; the TF32-first-layer fix at 0.72–0.96× is post hoc and closed-loop-untested), and the fix is useless for planners with few iterations (their own L-test logic) and fails on half of DMC.
- **Red flags**: The quantization-aware-training baseline is admittedly weak (so no conclusion about training-time alternatives); on top of the best post-training quantization the marginal gain of sh-iter is borderline in TD-MPC2 (10/19 cells) and absent in DINO-WM (1/3); no physical-robot experiments; and six of the closest related works are concurrent anonymous ICLR 2027 submissions, so the novelty margin against [5] (calibration-based recovery of 4-bit planning) is argued on the axis of action (rounding rule vs calibration objective) rather than demonstrated head-to-head.

## Strengths

- Identifies and cleanly names a failure mode — deterministic rounding error that is persistent and shared gets *exploited* by iterating planners — that standard offline metrics (latent one-step error, bit width) provably miss, and supports it with a mechanism, not just correlations.
- The fix (sh-iter) is training-free, model-free, adds ≤2.3% latency in the fused kernel, requires no calibration data at deploy time, and transfers unchanged from 5M single-task to a 317M multi-task checkpoint.
- The experimental design is exemplary for this genre: pre-registered hypotheses with hashed design docs, paired statistics with multiplicity correction, positive controls (sh-step/sh-ep, det+shift, coarse dither tables, single-offset collapse) that isolate persistence as the causal variable, and a candidate-count dose-response tied to the optimizer's curse.
- Intellectual honesty is a strength in itself: Table I reports failed hypotheses (H2a/H2b, H4b, L2c, S2, F1-NVFP4, H3b on DINO-WM), the DMC failure is presented as a boundary with a validated explanation (persistent-share rule, Spearman 0.83 on new seeds), and the limitations section names the weak QAT baseline and the single-checkpoint 317M result.
- The FP4 section ties the failure to a concrete numerical cause — coarse uniform grids with a shared scale — and shows block scaling (NVFP4) removes most of the failure surface, giving practitioners a hardware-aligned path.

## Limitations

- The prescription assumes an iterating planner: with a single MPPI iteration sh-iter is no better than det, and on DMC it removes half the damage in only 5 of 12 cells while being outright harmful on walker at 3 bits (78.9% of return lost vs 56.9% under det) — the paper honestly keeps this in scope as a boundary.
- Gains shrink when combined with strong post-training quantization: on top of the best PTQ (per-channel ranges), sh-iter's extra benefit is borderline in TD-MPC2 (10 of 19 cells) and absent in DINO-WM (1 of 3); dither was never combined with calibration objectives such as the concurrent work [5].
- The QAT baseline was weak, so nothing can be concluded about whether training-time approaches already solve this; the 317M results rest on one checkpoint; the FP4 closed-loop results use fake quantization of the 5M models, not fused FP4 kernels.
- Deployment evidence is incomplete: eager integer kernels are 18% slower than FP32 (launch-bound), the CUDA-graph speed-ups (1.9–3.3× over FP32) lose to pre-converted BF16 (1.07–1.52× its time; 1.01–1.12× for the whole step), and the TF32-first-layer variant that would beat BF16 was only timed, never run in closed loop.
- Scope is simulation-only (Meta-World, DMC, PushT/Wall/PointMaze); there are no physical-robot experiments, and the latency map comes from one GPU family (A40) plus one Blackwell consumer card (RTX 5090) for the FP4 GEMM microbenchmark.

## Relevance

直接命中"极端性能优化下的可靠性"方向：这是低精度量化（激活量化到 2–5 比特）在闭环推理系统中引发静默正确性失效（规划崩溃而离线误差指标毫无察觉）的完整案例研究，并给出了一个免训练、微延迟开销（≤2.3%）的数值层修复。其对"数值误差被上层优化循环系统性放大/利用"的机制分析，对量化推理、低精度核与混合精度系统的可靠性评估具有普遍借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would sh-iter protect other closed-loop consumers of quantized models — e.g. denoising loops in diffusion policies/VLA models, beam or best-of-N search over quantized LLMs, or MPC with learned dynamics in real robotics — where a selection mechanism repeatedly queries a model with correlated deterministic error?
- Does the reward-channel observation generalize: is one-step *reward* error (larger under det in all 19 affected Meta-World cells and 8/8 at 317M) a reliable pre-deployment diagnostic of exploitable quantization error, as the planned extensions propose?
- Can dither be combined with calibration objectives (e.g. the concurrent 4-bit calibration approach) multiplicatively, or do they act on the same error mass — and does the answer change at weight-plus-activation W4A4?
- Why does DMC resist the fix — is the smaller persistent share (0.33–0.52) a property of the return-based tasks, the longer horizons, or the dynamics landscapes, and can a per-task persistent-share estimate be computed cheaply before choosing a rounding rule?
- Does cross-unit correlation of deterministic rounding error (the untested explanation for why scalar reward error separates det) predict damage within a bit width, and would low-discrepancy dither (as used for recurrent KV caches) beat uniform dither at the same persistence schedule?
