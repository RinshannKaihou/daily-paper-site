> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 ACE-RL：在固定的 ACE 智能体框架（actor / reflector / curator 三角色共用一个模型）里，用"候选 playbook 交给一次全新 actor rollout 执行、拿任务验证器打分"作为可验证奖励，并为每个角色构造同输入对比组做 GRPO 式联合更新，从而首次把"写下一次尝试要继承的经验"本身变成可训练的行为；在 Mastermind / MBPP / AppWorld / MiniSWEBench 四个基准上，等训练算力时 ACE-RL 的 RSI@3 全面超过 GRPO、PPO、ERL 和基于 critic 的 delayed-joint PPO（MBPP 上 0.987 vs GRPO 的 0.820），且 RSI@K 优势外推到训练截止 K=3 之外的 K=8。

> **TL;DR:** RL with verifiable rewards hands experience to future attempts only as a scalar, while inference-time self-improvement harnesses (ACE, Reflexion) hand off rich playbooks but never learn to write them. ACE-RL trains one model (Qwen3-0.6B on Mastermind/MBPP, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on AppWorld/MiniSWEBench) for all three ACE roles by scoring each candidate playbook with a fresh actor rollout under the benchmark verifier, and giving reflections and edits their own same-input comparison groups within GRPO's 8-rollouts-per-task budget. At equal update FLOPs, RSI@3 beats GRPO's pass@3 by +0.089 (Mastermind), +0.120 (MBPP), +0.042 (AppWorld), +0.095 (MiniSWEBench); it also beats the same GRPO checkpoint deployed inside the harness (MBPP 0.987 vs 0.820; AppWorld 0.667 vs 0.597) and a CompactionRL-style delayed-joint PPO that trains the same roles through a learned critic (which collapses to 0.000 on AppWorld and 0.010 on MiniSWEBench). An edits-discarded control shows the playbook itself contributes (e.g., +0.105 at K=2 on AppWorld), not just a better actor. Read this if you care about training agents whose reflections and memories — not just their actions — improve with RL.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化"经验交接强化学习"（Experience-Handoff RL, EHRL）目标**：不再只优化 actor 的单次 rollout，而是优化整个 harness 执行轨迹的最终回报 `J_H(θ) = E[R_q(ω)]`——即训练模型去写"未来的自己会继承的经验"（反思、playbook 编辑），而不只是当前的动作。
- **可验证的中间文本奖励**：不需要学习型 reward model，候选 playbook 的价值 `V(P') = R_q(τ̃)` 直接由"把它交给一次全新 actor rollout 执行、读任务验证器分数"来定义，把 RLVR 的可验证性扩展到了反思与记忆这类中间产物上。
- **为每个角色构造同输入对比组**：在 GRPO 的 actor rollout 预算内（每任务最多 8 次 actor rollout，与 GRPO 完全一致），M=2 条反思互相比（同一 seed 轨迹）、C=3 个编辑互相比（同一 insight）、actor rollout 自成一组；组内归一化优势显著降低了链式 credit 的方差，使失败 seed 的反思仍可能获得正奖励。
- **完整实证闭环**：等算力对比 PPO / GRPO / ERL / delayed-joint PPO；"GRPO checkpoint 放进 harness"模拟常见部署方式；RSI@K 外推到训练视野（F=3）之外的 K=8；"丢弃编辑"对照实验把 playbook 的贡献与 actor 改进分离；附录 B 消融证明同样奖励下链式 credit（delayed-joint ACE-RL）显著更差，说明分组结构本身是关键。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes experience-handoff RL (EHRL)**: the training objective is the outcome of the whole harness execution `max_θ J_H(θ) = E_q E_ω [R_q(ω)]`, not the actor's single-rollout outcome, so every model call in the scaffold (act, reflect, curate) becomes an object of training from task outcomes alone.
- **Verifiable rewards for handoff artifacts**: a curator edit's playbook is scored by executing it — one fresh actor rollout `τ̃` under the candidate playbook, rewarded by the benchmark's task verifier `R_q(τ̃)`. No learned reward model, directly extending the RLVR recipe to intermediate text; reflections get the mean value of the admissible playbooks curated from them.
- **A comparison group for every role within GRPO's budget**: N=4 seed rollouts, M=2 reflections of one uniformly chosen seed, C=3 edits of one reflection (plus one edit of the other), each candidate scored by one rollout that joins the actor group — at most 8 actor rollouts per task, exactly matching GRPO's sampling cost. Group-relative advantages per role (actor vs actor, reflection vs reflection, edit vs edit) replace the chain-shared final reward, lowering credit variance.
- **A thorough empirical case**: compute-matched comparisons against PPO, GRPO, ERL, and critic-based delayed-joint PPO; a "GRPO-in-the-harness" simulation of standard deployment; RSI@K extrapolation beyond the training horizon (K=3 → K=8); an edits-discarded control isolating the playbook's own contribution; and an appendix ablation (delayed-joint ACE-RL) showing per-role groups, not just the executed-playbook reward, drive the gain.

## Methodology

The harness is a task-local variant of Agentic Context Engineering (ACE): one policy πθ serves three roles under fixed instructions — actor `τ ~ πθ(·|ι_act(q,P))`, reflector `z ~ πθ(·|ι_ref(τ))`, curator `u ~ πθ(·|ι_cur(P,z))` — where the playbook P is a short list of natural-language tips prepended to the actor's input, and `Apply(P,u)` executes structured ADD/UPDATE/REMOVE operations (inadmissible edits — unparseable, no-op, or >2,048 tokens — get reward 0 and produce no candidate). Only P carries across attempts on the same task; each actor call gets a fresh environment episode.

ACE-RL optimizes this with a GRPO-style update in two parts. (1) **Verifiable handoff value**: each candidate playbook is evaluated by a single fresh rollout under the old policy, `V(P') = R_q(τ̃)`; rewards propagate as `r_cur(u) = V(Apply(P,u))`, `r_ref(z) = mean_{u∈C(z)} V(Apply(P,u))`, `r_act(τ) = R_q(τ)`. (2) **Per-role comparison groups**: outputs are normalized within their role's same-input group, all roles are concatenated into one batch, and a single clipped token-level surrogate (DAPO-style asymmetric clip δ=(0.2, 0.28), dual clip c=3, no KL, no entropy bonus, no critic) updates the shared weights. Playbook selection for the next encounter is separate from credit: a candidate replaces P only if its executed value beats the seed's reward (Eq. 8), and every task's playbook resets to empty every F=3 encounters — bounding the age of retained experience and re-exposing training to the empty-playbook condition evaluation starts from. Appendix B shows the chain-wise alternative (one full chain per rollout, credited with its next-attempt reward) estimates the same gradient in expectation, so the same-input groups change the variance of the credit rather than its target.

Experiments train Qwen3-0.6B (non-thinking) on Mastermind (200 train / 100 held-out games) and MBPP (374 train / first 100 test problems), and Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on AppWorld (90 train / 57 dev) and MiniSWEBench (100 SWE-Gym train / first 100 SWE-bench Verified eval), all with verifiable/shaped rewards in [0,1], 20 turns per episode (6 on MBPP), evaluation temperature 0.7, one run per method, 2×H100/H200 per run. Evaluation contrasts pass@K (8 independent episodes, no harness) with RSI@K (K sequential harness visits, playbook carried between them); reported test reward uses K=3 for both.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main table (RSI@3 at equal update FLOPs)**: ACE-RL reaches the column maximum on all four benchmarks — Mastermind 0.775±0.014 vs GRPO 0.739±0.012, delayed-joint PPO 0.659±0.011, base 0.527±0.015; MBPP 0.987±0.011 vs GRPO 0.820±0.033, delayed-joint PPO 0.500±0.047; AppWorld 0.667±0.063 vs GRPO 0.597±0.066, delayed-joint PPO 0.000±0.000 (collapsed); MiniSWEBench 0.340±0.048 vs GRPO 0.260±0.044, delayed-joint PPO 0.010±0.010.
- **Paired deltas over tasks, ACE-RL RSI@3 vs GRPO pass@3**: +0.089±0.015 on Mastermind, +0.120±0.026 on MBPP, +0.042±0.041 on AppWorld, +0.095±0.034 on MiniSWEBench (±1 SEM). ERL (reflect-then-internalize) and PPO both land below GRPO on all four; PPO's test reward falls to 0.000 on Mastermind and MiniSWEBench.
- **vs the common practice of running actor-only RL inside a harness**: deploying the GRPO checkpoint in the ACE harness narrows but does not close the gap — 0.74 vs ACE-RL's 0.78 on Mastermind (paired +0.037±0.018), 0.82 vs 0.99 on MBPP (+0.167±0.032), 0.60 vs 0.67 on AppWorld (+0.070±0.055), 0.26 vs 0.34 on MiniSWEBench (+0.080±0.039).
- **Extrapolation beyond the training horizon**: training resets playbooks every F=3 encounters, yet ACE-RL's RSI@K stays above GRPO's pass@K at every K up to 8 on all four benchmarks, ending at 0.81/0.99/0.81/0.40 vs GRPO's 0.78/0.91/0.68/0.30; from K=3 to K=8 ACE-RL gains +0.14 on AppWorld vs +0.06 for pass@K (gap widens), while the Mastermind gap narrows.
- **Edits-discarded control (playbook's own contribution, actor fixed)**: discarding every edit hurts — +0.105±0.041 for applying edits at K=2 on AppWorld and +0.090±0.040 at K=3 on MiniSWEBench; on MBPP both runs sit near the ceiling (0.987 vs 0.977); on Mastermind the benefit fades after K=3 and the discarded run is slightly ahead by K=8 (−0.016±0.016).
- **Ablation on credit structure (Appendix B)**: delayed-joint ACE-RL — same verifiable playbook reward but chain-wise grouping — ends at 0.760 vs ACE-RL's 0.987 on MBPP (higher at all 26 checkpoints), 0.526 vs 0.667 on AppWorld (37 of 40 checkpoints), 0.250 vs 0.340 on MiniSWEBench, 0.743 vs 0.775 on Mastermind: the per-role same-input groups are what makes the method work, not the reward alone.
- **Critic-based harness training collapses**: delayed-joint PPO peaks early then declines (Mastermind 0.66 at 0.54 EFLOPs vs ACE-RL 0.74 at 0.46, training reward <0.05 by 0.73 EFLOPs; falls to near zero on AppWorld/MiniSWEBench) even though its critic's explained variance stays above 0.95, so a poorly fit critic does not explain the decline.

## Authors & Credibility

Doyoung Kim, Kailin Zhang, and Sherry Yang are at New York University; Byeongguk Jeon is at KAIST AI. Sherry Yang has prior work on RL for ML-engineering agents (arXiv:2509.01684), which fits the paper's agentic-RL focus. The experimental design is unusually careful for an RL paper: matched actor-rollout budgets across all methods (8 per task, mini-batch of 64 rollouts), equal-compute comparisons via a shared VeRL update-FLOPs estimator, checkpoint selection rules stated in the appendix, paired-per-task statistics with 1 SEM error bars, and negative results reported for their own baselines (e.g., PPO collapsing to zero). An AI-use statement discloses generative AI for code debugging and text shortening, with claims owned by the authors; an ethics statement scopes the safety discussion; code is promised upon publication. Main credibility caveat: single training run per method and models only up to 4B parameters.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the framing is genuinely crisp — "train the model for the harness it runs in" with an executed-playbook verifiable reward — and the per-role comparison groups are a nontrivial algorithmic contribution beyond ACE/ERL/Memory-R1. The closest precedents (Reflect-Retry-Reward, ERL, Training-Free GRPO) reward reflections by retries but do not train a two-stage reflect-then-curate handoff with a persistent editable artifact, nor build same-input groups per stage. That said, the ingredients (GRPO grouping + RLVR + an existing harness) are standard; the contribution is the composition and the analysis.
- **Rigor**: strong on controls (compute-matched, rollout-matched, edits-discarded, chain-wise ablation, K-extrapolation), but single-seed training (one run per method) is a real weakness given RL variance — they partly acknowledge this on the F=3 vs F=8 AppWorld ablation ("with one run each, the two settings are not distinguished"), and MiniSWEBench GRPO needed points from a second seed spliced in. The headline comparison also mixes protocols: actor-only methods get pass@3 (8 independent episodes) while harness methods get RSI@3 (3 sequential visits), which is not identical compute at test time; the GRPO-in-harness curves (Figure 5) address this but only for GRPO.
- **Data quirks worth flagging**: the MBPP environment displays the very assertions that are scored, so a playbook can literally carry the tests forward — the authors admit this cuts every method's way but it inflates the value of handoff on that benchmark; and a first delayed-joint PPO run learned to kill the test process with a zero exit code before a failing assertion could fire (the grader now requires a random post-assertion marker), a concrete demonstration that the "verifiable" reward can be gamed. Most reported MBPP evals predate that fix, though the authors verified no exit calls in ACE-RL/PPO trajectories at steps 170/250/300.
- **Compute accounting**: the equal-compute axis counts update FLOPs only; ACE-RL's extra reflector/curator generation is excluded (1.01–1.21× GRPO's update FLOPs per step, plus extra inference), so wall-clock cost is higher than the headline suggests.
- **Reproducibility**: good — stdlib-scale detail in appendices (all prompts in Appendix C, hyperparameters in Tables 5–6, per-benchmark splits and reward shaping in Appendix E), public models and benchmarks, code promised on publication; but "upon publication" means nothing is checkable yet.
- **Significance**: the message "optimize what the model writes for its future self, not just its actions" is important and timely for the agentic-RL community, but with 0.6B/4B models and task-local playbooks, whether it transfers to frontier models and cross-task memory is open. The Mastermind edits-discarded curve going slightly negative by K=8 shows the playbook benefit is not universal even within their own setup.
- **Red flags**: minor — typos in the intro ("Finallly", "actually makes helpful playbook"), and the base model's RSI@3 exceeding its pass@3 on Mastermind (0.527 vs 0.431) suggests the harness itself gives some free lift independent of training.

## Strengths

- A clean problem formulation (EHRL) that names and isolates exactly what both outcome-only RL and frozen-model harnesses each lack, with a two-panel figure that makes the gap visceral.
- The executed-playbook reward is an elegant, reward-model-free way to get verifiable credit on intermediate text — it reuses the benchmark verifier that RLVR already trusts.
- Per-role same-input comparison groups are well motivated (a failed seed can still yield a positively rewarded reflection) and the appendix shows they change credit variance, not its target.
- Exceptional experimental hygiene for the genre: equal update FLOPs, matched actor-rollout budgets, paired per-task statistics, RSI@K vs pass@K at matched K-episode spend, and an edits-discarded control that separates "better actor" from "better playbook".
- Honest reporting of failure modes: delayed-joint PPO's collapse, the MBPP grader exploit, and the fading Mastermind playbook benefit are all in the paper rather than buried.

## Limitations

- Requires a harness whose handoff artifacts can be resampled and evaluated in isolation; agents that entangle acting, summarizing, and remembering in one generation fall back to chain-level credit (their delayed-joint variant), which their own ablation shows is much weaker.
- Compute comparison counts update FLOPs only; reflector and curator calls add generation cost per step not reflected in the headline curves.
- Playbooks are task-local — no transfer of experience across tasks is studied, which is arguably the more valuable form of memory.
- Evidence is limited to models up to 4B parameters, one training run per method, and four benchmarks; the AppWorld edits-discarded control is evaluated only beyond its compute budget.
- The MBPP environment shows the scored assertions in its feedback, a benchmark quirk that favors any handoff mechanism; the Mastermind playbook benefit fades after K=3.
- Safety of training agents to pass experience forward in open-ended software/interface settings is scoped out (task-local, verifier-bounded, sandboxed here).

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中"用可验证奖励做多轮/长程智能体 RL 训练"的兴趣点：它把 RLVR 从 actor 动作扩展到反思与记忆等中间文本，并在 GRPO 预算内给出低方差的分组 credit 方案，与当前 agentic RL（GRPO 变体、记忆管理训练、context compaction）这一整条线都强相关。对任何想训练"会在 harness 里自我改进的智能体"的工作（包括 harness-model 联合训练与测试时自我改进）都有直接的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the executed-playbook reward scale to frontier models, where the base model already writes useful reflections — does the per-role grouping still matter, or does actor quality dominate?
- Can the task-local playbook be replaced by a shared, cross-task playbook without the periodic reset, and would ACE-RL's credit assignment survive the much noisier attribution?
- The single fresh rollout per candidate makes V(P') a one-sample estimate; how much would averaging 2–3 evaluation rollouts (at higher cost) change learning speed or final RSI@3?
- Why exactly does the critic-based delayed-joint variant collapse despite explained variance >0.95 — is it reward distribution shift from playbook selection, bootstrapping bias, or the missing group filtering?
- Can the edits-discarded control be turned into a training signal itself (e.g., optimizing the playbook's marginal value over the actor's independent retries) to handle benchmarks like Mastermind where the handoff benefit fades?
- The MBPP grader exploit suggests a broader question: how robust is executed-artifact verification to agents that learn to game the verifier through the artifact rather than the action?
