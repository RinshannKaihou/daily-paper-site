Key finding

Disagreement between a model's quantized copies is statistically indistinguishable from free answer entropy as an error signal.

One Model, Two Precisions: Does Disagreement Between Quantized Copies Tell You When a Small Model Is Wrong? ↗

Mohammed Nayeem Khan · Allan Kevin T — Intel  ·  2026-11-09  ·  arXiv 2611.16478 (cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG)

Key metric
+0.001AUROC

Gain of the best precision-disagreement score over free answer entropy (0.684 vs 0.682, p = 0.760) on the held-out test half — n = 4,199 questions, Qwen3-0.6B. A whole extra forward pass bought one thousandth of a point.

Why this matters

Free entropy + recalibration is already near the ceiling for local deployment — don't pay for an extra low-precision pass; the control design is a reusable playbook.

Critical edge

The disagreement score's 0.832 rank correlation with entropy shows it mostly repeats what confidence already said — the novelty buys almost nothing.

Methodology

  • Setup. Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B (bf16, Apache-2.0) answer 8,398 four-way MCQs — ARC-Easy (2,376), ARC-Challenge (1,172), an MMLU subset (2,850), a HellaSwag subset (2,000) — zero-shot; the answer is the option letter with the largest next-token logit.
  • Simulated quantization. In-memory round-to-nearest (3–6 bit, groups 32–256) on every decoder projection; the 8-bit g128 copy is the "deployed" answerer, bf16 the noise floor (the 8-bit copy flips 5.0% of answers). RTN only — no GPTQ/AWQ, no wall-clock.
  • Signal zoo. Three free confidence scores (entropy, 1 − max-prob, margin) vs five disagreement scores (flip, pdrop = 1 − plow[y], JSD, KL, margin drop) across 9 low-precision copies (0.6B; 3 for 1.7B) — plus option-order disagreement from one extra 8-bit pass with cyclically shifted options.
  • Statistics. Dev/test split (seed 14): configuration chosen on dev, headline numbers on test; paired bootstrap over questions; cost counted in weight bytes per question, normalized to one 8-bit pass.
  • Controls. Matched-energy Gaussian weight noise and feature shuffling within task × entropy-decile cells — experiments designed to break the paper's own hypothesis.

  • Not quantization-specific. Matched-energy Gaussian weight noise scores 0.597 vs 0.550 for actual 4-bit rounding; across all 14 perturbed copies AUROC tracks answer-flip rate (rank corr −0.895). At 3 bits the signal reverses (JSD 0.410).
  • Free recalibration wins on calibration. A logistic map of 3 confidence features cuts ECE 0.181 → 0.014 — better than adding disagreement features (0.020).
  • Routing 0.6B → 1.7B. Escalating the top-20% most uncertain lifts accuracy 49.5% → 56.7% (option order) — but at equal total weight traffic, free-entropy routing reaches 61.3% and beats both extra-pass scores.
  • Re-asking. Averaging the two option-order readings lifts 0.6B accuracy 49.5% → 52.3%; four option orders give 55.4%.
  • More copies don't scale. Four 4-bit copies voting collapse to AUROC 0.574 (ensemble mutual information at chance). At 1.7B the story mirrors: entropy 0.767 vs disagreement 0.766, and task-level gains flip sign vs 0.6B.

Strengths

  • Exceptional hygiene: out-of-sample score selection, paired bootstraps, and controls built to destroy its own hypothesis — reported plainly when they do.
  • A concrete cost model (weight bytes per question) that makes "cheap low-precision pass vs full 8-bit pass" comparable — the equal-traffic routing finding only falls out of it.
  • Actionable output: a deployment decision table and an explicit "When it does not help" section with worked false-alarm/miss examples.
  • Clear mechanisms: why graded pdrop beats flip/JSD, why 3-bit disagreement reverses, why option order adds more (entropy correlation 0.472 vs 0.832).
  • Reproducible end-to-end: cached per-question logits + a builder that regenerates every table and figure from scratch (~400 LOC, fixed seeds).

Limitations

  • Narrow scope: two small models of one family; no 4B confirmation (6 GB GPU cap); plain RTN only — GPTQ/AWQ/imatrix could shift the balance; MCQ letter-logit scoring only.
  • One-sided band: the useful 5–6 effective-bit region was observed from below — no copy between 6.25 and 8.25 bits was ever run.
  • Statistical caveats: no multiplicity correction across many tables; intervals resample questions only — not noise seeds, prompts, or checkpoints.
  • Cost is a proxy: byte-reads excluding the ~156M-weight embedding/LM head; no wall-clock or memory numbers; self-consistency baseline cut for compute budget.
  • Label-noise ceiling: ambiguous or mislabelled questions look uncertain to every score and count as errors for all.

Deployment recipe paper's Table 10, distilled

No spare compute

Free logistic recalibration of 3 confidence features: ECE 0.181 → 0.014. Best value at zero cost.

Low-precision copy at hand

Add it as a feature: +0.009 AUROC, concentrated on ARC. Marginal.

One 8-bit pass affordable

Shifted-option re-ask, average two readings: accuracy 49.5% → 52.3%, AUROC 0.732.

Bigger model available

Escalate the top 20–30% by free entropy: 61.3% at equal traffic — beats both extra-pass scores.

Open questions

  1. Would a 7-bit copy (the untried 6.25–8.25 effective-bit band) dominate, and does the optimum track the copy that flips ~5–10% of answers?
  2. Do learned quantizers (GPTQ, AWQ, imatrix, K-quants) give a stronger or weaker signal than RTN, given the −0.895 damage trend?
  3. Does the conclusion transfer to 4B–70B models, where quantization damage is much smaller and entropy AUROC is already higher (0.767 at 1.7B)?
  4. Can option-order disagreement run on a low-precision copy — the exact-cost variant that was never tried — match the 8-bit shifted pass?
  5. Do these signals survive free-form/generative QA, where letter logits don't exist?
  6. A cost-matched comparison against self-consistency (k-sample voting) remains open — it was cut for compute budget.
  7. Could multiple noise seeds at low energy replace holding a quantized copy, given matched Gaussian noise performs about as well as rounding?
