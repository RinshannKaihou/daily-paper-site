> **一句话总结：** Meta 与 AMD 合作，在 AMD MI350X 上为两个生产级广告排序模型实现了原生 MXFP8/MXFP6 GEMM 内核（自研 FlyDSL 内核 + Inductor 融合激活量化），首次在 0.01% NE（归一化熵）这一严格上线门槛内将推荐模型量化推到 8 位以下：全线性层 GEMM 相对 FP16 提速 1.73–1.99×（MXFP6），端到端吞吐在 FP8 生产配置之上再提升 1.5–14.3%，且模型 A 的 6 个、模型 B 的 3 个预测任务全部满足 NE 容差。

> **TL;DR:** Meta+AMD evaluate OCP microscaling formats (MXFP8 E4M3 and the first native 6-bit MXFP6 E2M3 GEMMs that quantize both weights and activations and compute directly on 6-bit elements) for two real production ads ranking models on AMD MI350X, where MXFP6 has 2x the peak throughput of FP8. Using real production traffic replayed through actual kernels (not simulated quantization), MXFP8 matches row-wise FP8 accuracy (median per-call-site QSNR gap only 0.1 dB, 35.4 vs 35.6 dB median on Model A) while MXFP6 sits only ~1 dB below MXFP8 yet delivers 1.73-1.99x GEMM speedup over the FP16 baseline (vs 1.49-1.63x for production row-wise FP8) across all supported linear layers, and up to 2.90x on Model A's DCN down-projection. Selective MXFP6 covering 80.8%/72.9% of linear weights (Models A/B) keeps all 6/6 and 3/3 prediction tasks within the strict 0.01% NE launch bar while improving end-to-end QPS over the production FP8 configurations by 5.3%/1.5% (Model A) and 14.3%/7.6% (Model B) at batches 1024/2048. Read this if you care about sub-8-bit inference for accuracy-critical industrial recommendation systems or hand-tiled GEMM kernel engineering on non-power-of-two (6-bit) data types.

## 核心贡献

- **首次实现原生 MXFP6 GEMM 加速**：据作者所知，这是 R&R 与 LLM 领域第一篇报道原生 MXFP6 GEMM（权重和激活同时量化为 6 位、直接在 6 位数据上计算）显著加速且不损失模型质量的工作。此前 6 位方案（如 FP6-LLM）需将权重反量化回 FP16 再计算，在计算受限场景下优势消失。
- **张量级量化误差分析（真实内核 + 真实流量）**：在两个生产模型（模型 A：313 个线性调用点 / 47 种形状；模型 B：202 个 / 46 种）上，用真实生产推理流量和实际低精度内核测量每个线性层的量化信噪比（QSNR）。结论：MXFP8 与生产中的逐行 FP8 精度几乎相同（中位逐点差距仅 0.1 dB），MXFP6 与 MXFP8 的中位逐点差距仅约 1 dB（远小于每位尾数 6 dB 的理论差，因为 E2M3 保留了 E4M3 的全部 3 位尾数，只牺牲指数范围）。
- **MI350X 上的 MXFP8/MXFP6 内核工程**：用 FlyDSL 实现两类 GEMM 内核，解决 6 位元素占 0.75 字节（4 元素 3 字节）破坏 2 的幂假设的难题——深度流水（3 个 LDS 缓冲、权重预洗牌驻留寄存器环、K-tile-major 激活布局再提速 6–8%）、XCD 感知的 L2 工作组重映射、split-K 等；并扩展 PyTorch Inductor 将激活量化（abs-max、E8M0 缩放、打包）融合进生产者内核，避免独立量化内核使总时间反而变慢（未融合时 MXFP8 竞态下曾比 FP16 慢 4.6–5.2×）。
- **在 0.01% NE 上线门槛内的选择性 MXFP6 范式**：提出按层选择性应用 MXFP6（模型 A 覆盖 80.8%、模型 B 覆盖 72.9% 的线性层权重），全部预测任务（6/6 与 3/3）的相对 NE 增幅保持在 0.01% 容差内，同时端到端吞吐超过生产 FP8 配置。
- **格式选择的实践指导**：MXFP8 是逐行 FP8 的可行替代（精度几乎相同，可按 GEMM 形状选更快内核）；最大层 MXFP6 比 MXFP8 快 1.31–1.40×；但最小的请求级层（M 仅 25–51 行）上 FP8 反而最快（1.31–1.32×，在 19 个形状中 14–16 个胜过 MXFP6）——没有一种格式在所有层都最优。

## Key Contributions

- **First native MXFP6 GEMM speedups.** To the authors' knowledge, the first paper across R&R and LLM domains to report significant speedups from native MXFP6 GEMMs (both operands quantized to 6-bit E2M3, compute performed directly on 6-bit elements) without sacrificing model quality. Prior FP6 work (FP6-LLM) dequantizes weights to FP16 before the matmul, so its benefit evaporates in the compute-bound large GEMMs that dominate R&R inference.
- **Tensor-level quantization analysis with real kernels and traffic.** QSNR measured per linear call site on two production models (Model A: 313 call sites / 47 unique shapes; Model B: 202 / 46) using captured production inference inputs and the actual low-precision GEMM kernels (fake-quantized references agree within 0.15 dB at the median). Key findings: MXFP8 (E4M3) is accuracy-equivalent to production row-wise FP8 (median per-call-site gap 0.1 dB), and MXFP6 (E2M3) is only ~1 dB below MXFP8 at the median — far less than the ~6 dB per mantissa-bit rule because E2M3 keeps all three mantissa bits of E4M3 and only gives up exponent range under the E8M0 block scale.
- **MI350X kernel engineering for sub-byte formats.** FlyDSL-implemented MXFP8/MXFP6 GEMM kernels tackling the core difficulty that a 6-bit element occupies 0.75 bytes (four elements in three bytes), breaking power-of-two addressing: K-tiles of 192 elements (144 bytes) split into three 64-element sub-tiles feeding the scaled matrix instruction, deep software pipelining with three LDS activation buffers, offline weight preshuffling so B fragments live in a register ring (6–12 fragments ahead) and bypass LDS, K-tile-major activation layout worth 6–8% kernel time, XCD-aware workgroup remapping for L2 reuse, split-K for small output grids, and a branch-free main loop (branching costs ~25% GEMM time). AlphaKernel (an AI agent) tuned tiling/pipeline configurations per production shape.
- **Fused activation quantization via extended PyTorch Inductor.** Standalone quantization kernels re-read activations several times and outweigh GEMM gains (an untuned sweep showed summed linear time of 1.7x FP16 for MXFP6 and 4.6–5.2x for MXFP8); the authors extended Inductor so abs/max/mul/clamp/byte-packing are fused into producer kernels, making MX preparation at least as efficient as the row-wise FP8 preparation used in production.
- **Selective MXFP6 within the 0.01% NE launch bar.** Replacement rules covering 80.8% (Model A) and 72.9% (Model B) of linear-layer weights keep the relative NE increase within 0.01% for every evaluated prediction task (6/6 for Model A, 3/3 for Model B), while beating the production FP8 configurations end-to-end — the first work to push R&R quantization below 8 bits under this bar.

## Methodology

The paper combines a measurement study, kernel implementation, and end-to-end benchmarking on an AMD MI350X GPU (PyTorch 2.16, AOTInductor, HIP 7.0.51831).

1. **Formats.** Four candidates (Table 2): FP16 baseline; row-wise FP8 (E4M3) as deployed in production today; MXFP8 with E4M3 elements; MXFP6 with E2M3 elements. OCP MX formats share one E8M0 (power-of-two, 8-bit) scale per block of 32 consecutive elements along the reduction dimension. E4M3 is chosen over E5M2 and E2M3 over E3M2 because mantissa bits dominate error under block scaling (each extra mantissa bit is worth ~6.02 dB QSNR; the E8M0 scale only shifts exponents). They adapt Overflow-Aware Scaling (OAS) from prior MXFP4 work: a variant OAS-S for E4M3 that skips the NaN-reserved all-ones encoding and bounds block-maximum saturation error to 3.4%, and regular OAS for E2M3 (which has no NaN encoding).
2. **Accuracy metric.** Per-call-site QSNR = 10·log10(||y_ref||²/||y − y_ref||²) with y_ref the FP16 output and y the low-precision kernel output, computed over all captured batches with the open-source QuantSuite framework. QSNR is computed with real kernels, not simulated quantization.
3. **Workloads.** Two real production ads ranking models (DLRM-style: embedding tables + interaction backbone of DCN/DHEN/Wukong-like layers + MLPs). Model A: 53 captured batches, 69–638 candidate ads each, ~17.9k user–ad examples, 313 linear call sites, 47 unique GEMM shapes. Model B: 63 batches, 39–877 ads, ~19.9k examples, 202 call sites, 46 shapes. Comparisons use the call sites all formats support (FP8/MXFP8 alignment constraints exclude 20 Model A sites and 3 Model B heads; MXFP6 supports all).
4. **Kernel benchmarking.** GEMM time per call at batches 1024 and 2048, same day and same build, at each layer's real M; L2 flushed before each timed call; median of 50 calls. FP16 baseline is PyTorch F.linear; FP8 baseline is torch.scaled_mm (hipBLASLt). Low-precision kernels are timed on pre-quantized activations, with preparation cost analyzed separately. MXFP6 numbers use the deep-pipelined kernel with configuration + split-K factor (1–16) search keeping only outputs matching an FP32 reference.
5. **End-to-end.** Selective MXFP6 (27 GEMMs per forward for Model A, 49 for Model B, confirmed by traces) vs the production FP8 configuration on identical request-batched inputs (Model A: benchmark-generated IBB inputs; Model B: real IBB inputs), 20 warmups / 200 timed iterations, 7 alternating timing pairs per comparison; QPS gain = geometric mean of paired FP8/MXFP6 latency ratios minus 1, with 95% intervals from 10,000 paired bootstrap samples; both variants must pass an output-agreement numerical check at relative tolerance 0.01 against the unquantized model.

## Results & Highlights

- **GEMM speedup over FP16, all supported linear layers (total summed time).** Model A batch 1024: FP8 1.49×, MXFP8 1.43×, MXFP6 1.83×; batch 2048: 1.63× / 1.55× / 1.98×. Model B batch 1024: FP8 1.49×, MXFP8 1.33×, MXFP6 1.73×; batch 2048: 1.63× / 1.49× / 1.99×. MXFP6 is 1.17–1.23× faster than the production row-wise FP8 scheme on total GEMM time.
- **20 most compute-intensive layers (67% of Model A's and 76% of Model B's GEMM FLOPs).** MXFP6 total GEMM speedup over FP16 reaches 2.27× / 2.29× (Model A, batches 1024 / 2048) and 2.21× / 2.31× (Model B), vs 1.58–1.82× for FP8 and 1.67–1.72× for MXFP8; MXFP6 is fastest on every one of these shapes in both models at both batch sizes, peaking at 2.85× (batch 1024) and 2.90× (batch 2048) on Model A's DCN down-projection (K=1.6e5 → N=1.0e3). On the largest compute band MXFP6 is 1.31–1.40× faster than MXFP8, capturing much of the 2:1 peak-throughput ratio.
- **Accuracy (QSNR vs FP16, real kernels).** Median per-call-site gap between MXFP8 and row-wise FP8 is only 0.1 dB (Model A medians: 35.4 vs 35.6 dB; Model B: 36.4 vs 36.6 dB). Median MXFP6-vs-MXFP8 gap: 1.0 dB (Model A, range 0.2–3.1 dB below) and 0.9 dB (Model B, from 3.9 dB below to 1.6 dB above) — far smaller than the 6 dB per mantissa bit. The FP16 baseline itself is 72–74 dB from FP32 at the median, so low-precision noise dominates baseline noise by >35 dB. Lowest QSNRs are in small user-sequence layers (22.3–23.6 dB in Model A, 28.0–28.5 dB in Model B).
- **End-to-end throughput within the 0.01% NE bar.** Selective MXFP6 covering 80.8% (Model A) / 72.9% (Model B) of linear weights improves full-model QPS over the production FP8 configurations by +5.3% / +1.5% (Model A) and +14.3% / +7.6% (Model B) at batches 1024 / 2048 (95% bootstrap intervals: +4.8–6.1% / +1.3–1.8% and +13.2–16.0% / +7.1–8.4%), with relative NE increase within 0.01% on all 6/6 (Model A) and 3/3 (Model B) prediction tasks.
- **Per-format shape dependence.** For Model B at batch 1024, MXFP8 is slower than FP16 on 18 of 45 shapes (per-shape geomean 1.04×), while MXFP6 is slower on only 4; on the smallest request-level layers (M = 25–51 rows), row-wise FP8 is actually fastest (1.31–1.32× total, beating MXFP6 on 14/19 shapes at batch 1024 and 16/19 at 2048) — motivating per-shape format selection.
- **Fusion is essential.** Without producer fusion and tuning, summed linear time was 1.7× FP16 for MXFP6 and 4.6× (Model A) / 5.2× (Model B) for MXFP8 — activation preparation alone can erase the entire GEMM gain.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuinely strong on the systems side.** Native (weight-and-activation) MXFP6 GEMMs with measured speedups appear to be a first, enabled by MI350X being (per the authors) the only GPU where MXFP6 has 2× the FP8 peak throughput; the 0.75-byte element packing, 192-element K-tiles, register-ring weight staging, and XCD-aware L2 remapping are real engineering contributions. The accuracy finding that MXFP8 ≈ row-wise FP8 (0.1 dB median gap) and MXFP6 is only ~1 dB below is a useful, non-obvious empirical result.
- **Rigor: high for performance, thinner for the quality claim.** The NE result — the paper's central justification — is reported as a summary ("within 0.01% for every evaluated prediction task", 6/6 and 3/3 tasks) without the underlying NE numbers, offline evaluation datasets, or the procedure used to derive the per-layer replacement rules (which layers were excluded and why is only implied by the QSNR tables). No online A/B or production-launch metrics are shown despite framing around a "launch bar"; everything is offline replay. QSNR is a proxy, and its link to task-level NE is asserted rather than modeled.
- **Reproducibility: limited.** The models are proprietary production ads rankers; the kernels are FlyDSL/MI350X-specific and tuned by an internal AI agent (AlphaKernel) on production shapes; no code or kernel release is promised (QuantSuite, used for QSNR, is open source). Independent replication on other R&R stacks or GPUs is essentially impossible; results on NVIDIA GB300 (where MXFP6 has the same 2× peak as FP8) would likely be much less favorable, and the paper is candid about this hardware dependence.
- **Weak/awkward spots the authors do disclose:** (a) MXFP8 is slower than FP16 on 18/45 Model B shapes at batch 1024, so the MXFP8 story is really "a per-shape alternative," not a win; (b) the E2E benchmarks for Model A use benchmark-generated (synthetic) IBB inputs rather than real traffic; (c) the E2E configuration uses row-major packed activations, so the headline kernel gains (2.2–2.3× on top layers) do not fully reach the model level (+1.5–14.3% QPS) — the K-tile-major producer fusions are explicitly future work; (d) bootstrap intervals cover within-process variation only, not compilation or day-to-day variation; (e) two Model B layers need K zero-padding to a multiple of 32, and the FP8/MXFP8 alignment rules exclude 20 Model A call sites from the common comparison set.
- **Missing baselines/ablations:** no comparison against INT8 GEMMs, no QAT (quantization-aware training) alternative to test whether full-model MXFP6 could pass the NE bar, no NVFP4/MXFP4 datapoint on these models, and no sensitivity analysis of the 0.01% margin (how close to the bar do the tasks actually land?). Gains also shrink with batch size (5.3% → 1.5% for Model A), and the paper does not explore larger batches where the trend presumably continues.

## Strengths

- First measured, quality-preserving native MXFP6 GEMM deployment story, on real production ads ranking models with real captured traffic rather than synthetic proxies.
- Measurement discipline is excellent: actual kernels (not fake quantization) for QSNR, median-of-50 with L2 flush for GEMMs, paired alternating E2E timings with bootstrap CIs, output-agreement checks, same-day same-build comparisons.
- Honest, fine-grained reporting: per-shape speedups vs totals, speedup-by-GEMM-size bands, disclosure that MXFP8 loses to FP16 on many small shapes and FP8 wins the smallest request-level layers, and an explicit accounting of the kernel-to-E2E gap.
- Practical takeaway generalized cleanly: pick the format per GEMM shape (MXFP6 for large compute-bound layers, FP8-family for tiny request-level layers), which is immediately actionable for other serving teams.
- The fused activation-quantization finding (preparation can cost 1.7–5.2× FP16 if unfused) is a valuable negative result for anyone adopting MX formats.

## Limitations

- All evidence comes from exactly two proprietary Meta ads models on one GPU (AMD MI350X); generalization to other recommendation stacks, model architectures, or NVIDIA hardware (where MXFP6 peak = FP8 peak) is untested.
- The 0.01% NE compliance is asserted at the task level without published NE deltas, evaluation-data details, or the layer-selection rule; no online production A/B results are included.
- End-to-end gains (+1.5–5.3% Model A, +7.6–14.3% Model B) are modest relative to the 1.7–2.3× kernel-level gains, because K-tile-major producer fusions are not yet integrated into the production Inductor workflow; the full potential remains unrealized.
- QPS gains decrease with batch size, and the paper does not study regimes beyond batch 2048 or non-IBB (non-request-batched) serving paths.
- Kernel tuning depends on an internal AI agent (AlphaKernel) and production GEMM shapes; no public artifact is provided, limiting external verification.

## Relevance

本文直接命中“低精度量化 × 推荐系统推理效率”的交叉兴趣：它把 OCP 微缩放格式（MXFP8/MXFP6）从 LLM 领域带到对精度极其敏感的工业级排序模型，并给出从内核实现、量化误差分析到端到端 QPS/NE 权衡的完整实证，对研究低精度格式在计算受限稠密层中的实际收益非常有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How close to the 0.01% NE bar do the individual tasks actually land (per-task NE deltas), and does the margin hold on online A/B traffic, not just offline replay?
- Could quantization-aware training (or GPTQ-style weight rounding adapted to MX blocks, e.g., MR-GPTQ) push MXFP6 coverage beyond 80.8%/72.9% of weights, or even enable MXFP4 on the least sensitive layers?
- How much of the remaining kernel-to-E2E gap closes once K-tile-major producer fusions land in PyTorch Inductor, especially at batch 2048 and larger batches where the QPS gain shrinks (1.5% for Model A)?
- Do the results transfer to NVIDIA GB300, where MXFP6 has the same 2× peak throughput as FP8 and thus no 4× headroom over FP8 — i.e., is MXFP6 worth it without AMD's asymmetric rate?
- Can the per-shape format-selection policy (MXFP6 / FP8 / MXFP8 per GEMM) be automated at compile time from shape and traffic statistics, e.g., extended LoKA-style profiling, and does it generalize beyond these two models?
- Why exactly are the small user-sequence layers so quantization-sensitive (22.3–28.5 dB QSNR) — is it input distribution, small K, or interaction with the sequence attention, and would Hadamard rotations (QuaRot-style) fix it?
