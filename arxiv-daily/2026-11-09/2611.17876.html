Early temporary weight decay in Muon pretraining hurts at removal yet wins at the final budget — effects keep evolving after the intervention ends.

临时 weight decay 具有延迟效应：撤除时更差，训练结束时反而更好——撤除时刻的评估会得出完全错误的结论。

Delayed Effects of Temporary Weight Decay in Muon Pretraining
Md Rifat Arefin, Ravid Shwartz-Ziv, Ernie Chang, Chinnadhurai Sankar, et al. (9 authors — Meta · Mila · Samsung SAIL)
Muon weight decay pretraining optimization schedules LLM training

Key metric — sign reversal vs no decay

−0.262 nats ────────▶ +0.0275 nats

1.4B model, FineWeb100B: a λ=0.15 window over the first 44% (selected at 162M, transferred unretuned) is 0.2616 nats worse than no decay at removal, overtakes at update 20,440, and finishes 0.0275 nats better — beating the best constant-decay baseline by 0.0264.

Why this matters

An evaluation principle for any temporary training intervention — LR schedules, regularization windows, data curricula: judge at the final budget, not at removal. Otherwise you may discard the winning run.

Critical edge

The headline 1.4B and 3.4B tables are single-seed, and the authors' own best sampled schedules beat the celebrated 44% window — the transfer story is about robustness, not optimality.

Methodology — distilled

Decoder-only Transformers at 162M–3.4B trained with Muon on FineWeb100B / FineWeb-Edu (plus OpenWebMath, GitHub-Code, mC4 for domain studies), batch 1,024×1,024 tokens, compute-optimal budgets (3,200–66,000 updates).

Intervention: decoupled weight decay Wt+1 = (1 − ηtλt)Wt − ηtUt applied only inside a window [s, e), embeddings/head exempt — systematically varying start (0–60%), duration (5–100%), coefficient, and evaluation point, including matched-total-shrinkage (D ≈ 2.1) grids.

Mechanism + causal controls: relative step size αt = ‖P⊥Ut‖F/‖Wt‖F under Muon's near-constant update norms; tested via fixed-norm replays with trajectory swaps, norm resets at removal, and opposite-sign LR boosts; a structurally different Gram penalty probes generality.

robustness: 20× budgets · warmup · SwiGLU · AdamW · LR 0.005–0.04 · 4 data domains · 3.4B zero-shot (HellaSwag, ARC-E, PIQA, LAMBADA, WinoGrande)

Results — beyond the headline

SettingFinding
162M · 5 seeds 320-update window (λ=0.3) improves final loss +0.0172 nats over no decay (3.1701 ± .0027 vs 3.1873 ± .0028); Gram penalty + combination: −0.0142 / −0.0191.
Start time Flips the sign at every scale: 10% window from init helps; the same window starting at 60% hurts. At 1.4B: 2.6506 vs 2.6754 (no decay).
Duration vs dose Duration moves final loss by 0.026–0.035 nats vs only 0.007–0.010 for coefficient; at matched shrinkage D≈2.1, a 3,564-update window more than doubles the gain of a 700-update one. Interior optimum: 50% (162M), 20% (405M).
Causal replay Fixed-norm replays: post-window steps carry 0.0181 of 0.0221 nats of the gap; norm resets recover/remove 96%/97% of the gain at 162M. Larger steps alone aren't sufficient (continued λ=0.10 → only −0.0071).
3.4B scale Every early schedule beats all references on loss and accuracy: transfer reaches 2.4638 (−0.0194), zero-shot 66.90% vs 65.30%; both tested constants hurt.

Downstream caveat: at 405M/1.4B, zero-shot accuracy differs by ≤0.11 points from constant decay — the validation-loss win only becomes a clear downstream win at 3.4B.

Strengths

  • Clean, surprising, under-documented phenomenon — sign reversal long after removal — turned into an actionable evaluation principle.
  • Exceptional experimental hygiene: matched seeds, paired stats, matched-shrinkage comparisons, trajectory swaps, norm resets, opposite-sign perturbations.
  • Mechanism is quantitative and predictive (LR-boost offset matches within 8% for Wup/Wdown), and honestly bounded — it explains persistence, not benefit.
  • Broad robustness sweep (4 scales, 20× budgets, 4 domains) with negative results reported, not buried.

Limitations

  • Single-seed results dominate at 1.4B/3.4B; several margins (0.001–0.01 nats) are comparable to the ±0.0028-nat seed noise.
  • Coefficients are non-universal across scales; Gram penalty needs retuning at 1.4B — a phenomenon and protocol, not a drop-in schedule.
  • Mechanism established mostly at 162M; cannot explain why early-decay trajectories help while equally large steps from continued decay or late windows do not.
  • A simple linear taper over the first 20% beats the abrupt window at both scales (Appendix I) — the headline schedule family isn't the strongest decay schedule tested.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Why do early-decay step trajectories help while late windows and continued decay hurt — is there a link to critical learning periods?
  2. Does the delayed effect persist under data curricula, dropout, or interventions that don't primarily change weight norms?
  3. Does the crossover point move predictably with schedule shape (linear decay phase, cyclic/cosine)?
  4. Can the 1/α² recurrence forecast the final-loss delta from a removal-time norm deficit, without running the continuation?
  5. Would transfer hold for the linear-taper schedules that beat abrupt windows, and does optimal duration scale between 162M and 3.4B?
  6. Does an analogous delayed effect exist for AdamW at larger scales, or was the 405M failure a grid artifact?
