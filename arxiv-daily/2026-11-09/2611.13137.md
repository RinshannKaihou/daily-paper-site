> **一句话总结：** 本文将 Muon 优化器（动量正交化）与鲁棒聚合（bucketed RFA）结合用于拜占庭容错的分布式学习，在非欧信任域框架下证明了非凸与星凸目标的收敛保证（含异构误差下界与不精确 Newton–Schulz 正交化扩展）；在 CIFAR-10（ResNet-18/ViT-Tiny）、Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-100 及 TinyStories 38M 字节级语言模型的模拟联邦实验中，Muon-NS 在五种攻击下的攻击态精度（如 ResNet-18 自适应攻击下 74.0% vs 符号更新的 41.3%）与攻击态困惑度（TinyStories 1.938 vs AdamW 2.358）均优于 SGD/归一化 SGD/符号更新/AdamW 基线。

> **TL;DR:** The authors combine worker-local momentum, robust aggregation (bucketed RFA / smoothed geometric median), and Muon's momentum orthogonalization into "Byzantine-Robust Muon," proving non-convex and star-convex convergence guarantees in a non-Euclidean trust-region framework with a first-moment dual-norm heterogeneity assumption, an unavoidable Byzantine error floor, and an inexact-Newton–Schulz extension. In simulated federated training with 20% Byzantine workers (Dirichlet-0.1, 25 workers), Muon-NS on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 reaches 88.0% clean / 74.0% under an adaptive attack vs 41.3% for sign updates (the next-best geometry), and on TinyStories a 38M byte-level LM reaches 1.875 benign / 1.938 attacked (Dir. 3) byte perplexity vs 2.268 / 2.358 for a tuned AdamW baseline — though the paper is careful to note these are mechanism checks under specified bounded attacks, not worst-case security guarantees.

## 核心贡献

- **提出拜占庭鲁棒 Muon 算法**：每个诚实 worker 维护本地动量缓冲并发送给服务器，服务器用鲁棒聚合器（替代简单平均）聚合后执行 Muon 式正交化更新；鲁棒性与更新几何是模块化、可分离的。
- **非欧信任域统一框架**：将归一化 SGD、符号更新、Muon 统一为"在任意范数球上最小化线性模型"的信任域步骤；Muon 对应算子范数（对偶为核范数）几何，理论分析不依赖 i.i.d. 假设。
- **非凸收敛保证（Theorem 4.1）**：在 (δ, c)-鲁棒聚合器与相对一阶矩异构性假设下，平均对偶范数梯度以 O(1/K) 收敛到含拜占庭项 4cδζ/(1−4cδB) 的驻点邻域，该异构误差下界与已知不可能性结果一致。
- **星凸线性收敛（Theorem 4.3）**：带权重衰减的变体以 (1−β/2)^K 的速率线性收缩到随机噪声与拜占庭噪声的邻域，且无需对 δB 设上界。
- **不精确 Muon 扩展（Theorem 4.4）**：在加性 LMO 误差条件下，不精确正交化仅使进度按 (1−δ̄) 折减、分子中的非凸拜占庭项不变；另有外推法在二阶光滑下将无攻击复杂度改进到 O(ε^−3.5)。
- **系统性实验验证**：CIFAR-10（ResNet-18/ViT-Tiny，5 种攻击、5 种子）、Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-100/MNIST 迁移实验（3 种子）、TinyStories 38M 字节级语言模型（独立故事不相交测试集），以及固定诚实 worker 对照、共享检查点几何对照、RFA 查询攻击对照和大量消融。

## Key Contributions

- **Byzantine-Robust Muon algorithm**: honest workers maintain local momentum buffers (worker-momentum design of Karimireddy et al. 2021 / Farhadkhani et al. 2022, so the method uses temporal information absent from memoryless permutation-invariant schemes); the server robustly aggregates the buffers and takes a Muon-style orthogonalized step. Robustness (aggregation rule) and geometry (update norm) are modular and independently swappable.
- **Unified non-Euclidean trust-region formulation**: the update solves min ⟨m_k, x⟩ s.t. ‖x − x_k‖ ≤ η; choosing the operator/nuclear-norm geometry recovers Muon, while other norms recover normalized SGD and sign-type momentum methods — placing all tested baselines in one framework.
- **Convergence theory without i.i.d. assumptions**: a (δ, c)-robust aggregator definition in the dual norm (first-moment variant of Gorbunov et al. 2023), plus a (B, ζ) heterogeneity condition expressed in the trust-region geometry. Non-convex case: O(1/K) to a stationarity neighborhood whose Byzantine floor 4cδζ/(1−4cδB) cannot be removed by tuning η or α (consistent with Karimireddy et al. 2022 lower bounds); at δ=0 the tuned rate matches normalized/trust-region SGD with momentum (O(K^−1/4)).
- **Star-convex linear convergence with weight decay**: Algorithm 2 searches the trust region around (1−β)x_k, contracting at rate (1−β/2)^K to a noise neighborhood without any upper bound on δB (heterogeneity history absorbed by the contraction); β must satisfy β < α, an explicit trade-off rather than free acceleration.
- **Inexact-Muon and extrapolation extensions**: under an additive oracle-error condition (Shulgin et al. 2025), all guarantees survive with progress deflated by (1−δ̄) and the adversarial numerator constants unchanged; gradient extrapolation under H-Lipschitz Hessian improves the tuned non-convex complexity at δ=0 to O(ε^−3.5).
- **Multi-pronged empirical study**: 25-worker simulated federated CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18/ViT-Tiny under five attacks (sign-flip, ALIE, IPM, mimic, adaptive directional) with five seeds; transfer to Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-100/MNIST; a 38.15M-parameter byte-level LM on TinyStories with an independent story-disjoint test set; fixed-honest controls isolating the aggregator and the update geometry from shared checkpoints; ablations of Byzantine fraction, heterogeneity, worker momentum α, aggregator, bucketing, and nearest-neighbor mixing.

## Methodology

The paper targets distributed finite-sum minimization min f(x) = (1/G) Σ_{i∈G} f_i(x) where up to δn < n/2 of the n workers are Byzantine and may send arbitrary vectors. Algorithm 1 ("Byzantine-Robust Muon") works as follows: each honest worker keeps a momentum buffer m_i^{k+1} = (1−α) m_i^k + α g_i^{k+1} and sends it; the server computes m_k = ARAgg(m_1, …, m_n) with an agnostic (δ, c)-robust aggregator that approximates the honest average with dual-norm error ≤ cδr proportional to the honest pairwise dispersion r; the server then solves the linearized trust-region subproblem x_{k+1} = argmin ⟨m_k, x⟩ s.t. ‖x − x_k‖ ≤ η. Under the operator-norm geometry on matrix parameters, the exact LMO solution is the negative polar (orthogonal) factor of the aggregated momentum — exactly Muon applied to momentum — approximated in practice by five Newton–Schulz iterations (Muon-NS). Algorithm 2 adds decoupled weight decay by centering the trust region at (1−β)x_k.

The analysis uses four building blocks: (i) unbiased stochastic gradients with bounded variance σ²; (ii) Lipschitz gradients in the chosen norm; (iii) a norm-equivalence constant κ (e.g., κ = min{d1, d2} for matrix blocks, and κ = sqrt(Σ_b r_b + J) for the tensorwise product norm actually implemented); (iv) the (B, ζ)-heterogeneity condition: average dual-norm deviation of honest gradients from the global gradient is ≤ B‖∇f‖* + ζ, with the non-convex theorem requiring 4cδB < 1. Aggregating momenta rather than raw gradients damps stochastic noise by a factor α (noise scales VG = κσ√(α/G(2−α))), which is the provable variance-reduction benefit of worker momentum. Extensions cover approximate aggregation with an additive certified residual (realized by a smoothed geometric median with a Weiszfeld-iteration objective certificate, Lemma B.2), a second-moment aggregation route (Appendix D, condition-free), gradient extrapolation under second-order smoothness (Appendix E), and the additive-error inexact LMO oracle (Appendix F).

Experiments: 25 simulated workers, Dirichlet-0.1 non-IID CIFAR-10 partitions across honest workers, 20% Byzantine (5/25) in attacked runs, bucketed RFA (smoothed geometric median, bucket size 2, following Karimireddy et al. 2022 / Pillutla et al. 2022), worker momentum α = 0.1, 500 rounds, learning rates selected on validation data (0.01 for SGD/sign/Muon-NS; 0.03 for normalized SGD); the LR is the trust-region radius η for the normalized geometries. The SGD row is exactly the worker-momentum method of Karimireddy et al. 2021 with bucketed RFA, so the geometry comparison sits on top of an established robust baseline. Attacks forge messages from the honest mean ḡ and coordinate-wise std σ (sign-flip: −ḡ; IPM: −0.1ḡ; ALIE: ḡ − 0.1σ; mimic: copy a fixed honest worker; adaptive directional: ḡ − 0.1√G·sign(ḡ)⊙σ); the stronger RFA-query attack picks among four candidates after querying the current bucketed RFA output. Careful controls include fixed-honest runs (identical 20 honest partitions; benign extra messages copy ḡ), shared-checkpoint geometry comparisons (both geometries continue from the same benign Muon-NS checkpoint with matched honest streams, 10 paired seeds), an independent TinyStories test split (100 fixed windows from 4000 disjoint unused stories), and a controlled 512×512 matrix quadratic verifying the extrapolation mechanism and the certified-RFA residual.

## Results & Highlights

- **CIFAR-10 / ResNet-18 (Table 2, 5 seeds, 20% Byzantine)**: Muon-NS attains 88.0% ± 1.4 clean and 74.0% ± 3.3 under the adaptive attack, vs 41.6–54.3% (sign, the next-best geometry) and 25.1–39.8% (worker-momentum SGD) across the same columns; under ALIE Muon-NS keeps 86.4% ± 0.8 (a 43.1-point margin over sign's 43.3%).
- **CIFAR-10 / ViT-Tiny (Table 2)**: Muon-NS reaches 64.1% ± 2.1 clean / 47.1% ± 1.1 adaptive, vs 46.2% / 35.0% for sign and 42.4% / 32.9% for globally normalized SGD.
- **TinyStories language model (Table 6, 5 seeds, round 1200, independent byte-level test perplexity, lower is better)**: Muon-NS achieves 1.875 ± 0.003 benign and 1.938 ± 0.004 under directional strength 3, vs 2.268 ± 0.053 and 2.358 ± 0.061 for tuned AdamW — a 0.39–0.42 perplexity advantage — and 5.038/7.034 for SGD.
- **RFA vs mean under an RFA-query attack (Table 4, 6 paired seeds)**: from near-identical benign accuracy (88.7% vs 88.1%), bucketed mean collapses to 10.0% while RFA retains 36.5% ± 6.6 — RFA cuts the attack-induced loss by 27.0 points (paired 95% interval 20.3–33.8).
- **Geometry isolated from a shared checkpoint (Table 5, 10 paired seeds)**: with the benign gap closed (Muon-NS 91.4% vs tensorwise ℓ2 90.8%), Muon-NS stays ahead under Dir. 3 (83.7% vs 82.3%) and Flip 5 (85.0% vs 82.4%), with smaller attack-induced drops (1.9 points smaller under Flip 5, paired 95% interval 0.6–3.3; directional interval −0.4–1.9 includes zero).
- **Fixed-honest AdamW comparison on ViT-Tiny (Table 3)**: attack-induced loss is smaller for Muon-NS by 5.6 points under Dir. 3 (25.3% vs 18.0%) and 8.4 points under Flip 5 (31.7% vs 21.5%), paired 95% intervals 0.8–10.3 and 4.9–12.0.
- **Transfer benchmarks (Table A.4, 3 seeds)**: CIFAR-100/ResNet-18 Muon-NS 65.4% clean / 61.2% sign-flip vs 29.8%/15.9% (normalized SGD) and 19.1%/6.2% (SGD); Fashion-MNIST/CNN 90.9%/88.7% — the geometry ordering matches CIFAR-10 in every column.
- **Mechanism sweeps**: worker momentum matters non-monotonously — under the adaptive attack Muon-NS gets 51.0% at α=0.03, 74.0% at 0.1, 82.7% at 0.3, 73.4% at α=1; at nominal 30% corruption under IPM, accuracy is 69.1% at Dirichlet 0.1 but 86.9–90.1% at concentrations 1–10, consistent with a heterogeneity-dependent Byzantine floor.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The individual ingredients (worker momentum for Byzantine robustness, RFA/geometric-median aggregation, Muon orthogonalization, non-Euclidean trust-region analysis) all exist; the contribution is the synthesis plus the first-moment dual-norm analysis in arbitrary norms, which genuinely extends Yang et al. 2024 (i.i.d., Euclidean) and goes beyond Bolatov et al. 2026, who only discuss arbitrary-norm LMO as an extension without proving it. The inexact-oracle extension (Theorem 4.4) is timely and practically relevant since real Muon uses ~5 Newton–Schulz iterations.
- **Rigor**: Theory is stated under explicit assumptions with constants in appendices; the paper honestly flags that 4cδB < 1 constrains only relative heterogeneity B, that the ζ-floor is unavoidable, and that five Newton–Schulz iterations do not themselves certify a small δ̄. Experiments use validation-based LR selection, seeds with sample standard deviations, and paired t-intervals. However, the paper itself admits the neural results "neither measure the theorem constants nor verify those assumptions" — the theory and experiments are parallel tracks, not a validation of one by the other.
- **Baseline caveats (acknowledged by the authors)**: the AdamW comparison changes temporal state (α = 1 vs α = 0.1) and the ResNet-18 benign gap (88.1% vs 66.7%) precludes an intrinsic robustness interpretation there; SGD's language-model learning rate sits on the boundary of its tuning grid even in the extended study; sign-flip and IPM send negative scalar multiples of the honest mean, which scale-invariant updates (Muon, sign, normalized SGD) absorb more easily — indeed Table A.5 shows plain bucketed mean matches or exceeds RFA in all twelve mild-attack cells, so the headline Table 2 gaps under sign-flip/IPM owe much to geometry-scale-invariance rather than the robust aggregator.
- **Attack strength**: attacks are bounded, specified strategies; the RFA-query adversary considers only four candidates and is not an exhaustive adaptive search. The authors explicitly state this is "not deployment-level Byzantine-security" — and the 51.7-point RFA drop in Table 4 shows how far from security the tested defenses remain.
- **Data quirks**: attacked runs partition data across 20 honest workers while clean runs use 25, so clean and attacked data volumes per worker differ; the Byzantine-fraction sweep conflates corruption with honest data volume (fixed-honest controls mitigate but do not remove this for the main table). Macro-accuracy masks worst-class failure: Muon-NS ResNet-18 worst-class accuracy is 72.4% (clean) and 25.1% (adaptive) against macro 88.0%/74.0%.
- **Reproducibility**: detailed protocol (rounds, grids, seeds 0–4/6/10/3 by experiment, attack formulas, hardware: NVIDIA H200 single-GPU trials), with per-seed summaries promised alongside the source; no code link is given in the main text, which is the main reproducibility gap.

## Strengths

- Clean modular design: robust aggregation and update geometry are separated, so the same aggregator analysis covers Muon, normalized SGD, and sign geometries — and the experiments exploit this to isolate the geometry effect (shared-checkpoint control).
- Honest, self-critical evaluation: the paper designs controls specifically to break its own headline claims (fixed-honest, RFA-query, shared-checkpoint) and reports the adverse results (mean ≥ RFA under mild attacks; directional paired interval including zero).
- Theory covers exactly what practitioners use: heterogeneous non-IID data, inexact Newton–Schulz orthogonalization, weight decay, and an additive certified aggregation residual.
- Breadth: five attacks, three model families (CNN, ViT, byte-level LM), five seeds in primary comparisons, transfer datasets, plus a controlled matrix quadratic that directly probes the extrapolation mechanism and certifies the smoothed-RFA residual (1.90 × 10⁻⁵ nuclear norm; Weiszfeld certificate satisfied on all rounds).

## Limitations

- No worst-case adversary: all attacks are specified bounded constructions; the adaptive attack still costs Muon-NS ~14 points on ResNet-18 (88.0% → 74.0%), and even RFA loses 51.7 points under the four-candidate RFA-query attack.
- Theory–experiment gap: the theorems' constants (cδ, B, ζ, δ̄) are never measured on the neural workloads; the neural results are mechanism checks, not verification.
- Conditions may be hard to check in practice: 4cδB < 1 and the first-moment (δ, c)-aggregation premise (worst case cδ ≥ 1/2) limit how tight the guarantees are; the implemented smoothed Euclidean RFA is not the exact dual-norm geometric median the remark describes.
- Confounded baselines: AdamW differs in temporal state; SGD's LM learning rate is at the grid boundary; clean-vs-attacked runs differ in honest data volume.
- Simulated federated training on a single GPU per trial — no real cross-institution deployment, system faults, or asynchrony; only 25 (vision) and 9 (language) workers, and macro-accuracy hides severe worst-class degradation (25.1% under the adaptive attack).

## Relevance

与"LLM Training Stability & Observability"中分布式训练鲁棒性与故障恢复的方向高度相关：该工作直接研究大规模分布式/联邦训练中恶意或故障 worker（拜占庭节点）发送损坏更新时的训练稳定性，提供了从鲁棒聚合到 Muon 类优化器的可证明容错方案，可用于训练异常（如掉卡节点发送坏梯度）场景下的检测与缓解设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the theorem constants (cδ, B, ζ, κ) behave empirically on real LLM pretraining workloads — can the Byzantine floor 4cδζ/(1−4cδB) be measured, and does it predict the accuracy drop at 30% corruption seen in Figure 1?
- Does the first-moment (δ, c)-aggregator premise hold for any practical aggregator (bucketed RFA, NNM variants) on matrix-valued momenta in the nuclear norm, given that worst case cδ ≥ 1/2 and the implementation uses a smoothed Euclidean median?
- How does Byzantine-Robust Muon interact with communication compression and asynchrony, both standard in real federated/cross-silo LLM training — does robust aggregation plus orthogonalization remain stable under quantized or delayed momentum?
- Can a stronger adaptive adversary (e.g., gradient-aware or query-unlimited, or attacking individual Newton–Schulz block geometry) close the 14-point adaptive-attack gap on Muon-NS, and would per-block Byzantine filtering help?
- Does the smaller attack sensitivity of matrix (spectral) geometry transfer to optimizer-stateful baselines like AdamW with worker momentum matched, isolating geometry from temporal state as the shared-checkpoint control did for tensorwise ℓ2?
