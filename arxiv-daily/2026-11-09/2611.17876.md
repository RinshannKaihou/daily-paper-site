> **一句话总结：** 本文发现 Muon 预训练中"只在早期施加、随后撤除"的临时 weight decay 具有延迟效应：在 1.4B 模型（FineWeb100B，28,000 步）上，从 162M 迁移而来的 λ=0.15/前 44% 训练的 decay 方案在撤除时比无 decay 差 0.26 nats，但到 73% 训练进度时反超，最终反而好 0.0275 nats 并优于最佳恒定 decay 基线 0.0264 nats；机制上，早期 decay 留下持久的权重范数亏缺，使撤除后的相对步长持续偏大，固定范数重放实验证明这种相对步长轨迹足以复现收益。

> **TL;DR:** Meta/Mila researchers show that a temporary weight-decay window applied only early in Muon-pretrained Transformers (162M–3.4B, FineWeb100B/FineWeb-Edu) can be harmful at removal yet beneficial at the final budget: a schedule selected at 162M and transferred unretuned to 1.4B is 0.26 nats worse than no decay when the window ends, overtakes it after 73% of training, and finishes 0.0275 nats better while beating the best sampled constant-decay baseline by 0.0264 nats. Early decay leaves a persistent weight-norm deficit (12–16% of no-decay norms at removal, still 60–66% at the end) that keeps relative step sizes elevated long after removal; fixed-norm replays of the measured relative-step trajectories reproduce the ordering and magnitude of the gain. The practical lesson: evaluate temporary schedules at the final training budget, not at removal.

## 核心贡献

- **发现并量化"延迟效应"**：临时优化干预（早期 weight decay 窗口）在撤除时的验证损失与最终预算下的验证损失可以符号相反。最极端的例子：1.4B 模型上，从 162M 未经调参直接迁移的 decay 方案在撤除时比无 decay 差 0.26 nats，训练进行到 73% 时才反超，最终却好 0.0275 nats，且优于最佳采样恒定 decay 基线 0.0264 nats。
- **分离了三个常被混淆的概念**：干预何时起作用（起始时间）、撤除后训练多久（评估视角）、以及最终训练预算。实验表明：起始时间能反转效果符号（10% 窗口从初始化开始有益，从 60% 开始有害）；评估视角能反转方案排序（405M 上早窗口在共同终点评估与等量撤除后评估下的排序相反）；在总压缩量 D≈2.1 匹配时，窗口越长收益越大（700 → 3,564 步收益翻倍以上），说明总压缩量不能单独决定效果。
- **机制解释——持久范数亏缺与相对步长**：早期 decay 使权重范数降至无 decay 的 12–16%（撤除时）且到训练结束仍只有 60–66%；由于 Muon 更新范数近似恒定，范数更小意味着相对步长（⊥ 分量/范数）持续偏大。一个近似递推式（1/a²t+1 − 1/a²t ≈ 1 + 2κt）解释了该差异为何收敛缓慢：1/α²t 从更新 400 到 2,800 增长约 4 倍，而两运行之间的差距几乎不变，相对步长比值仅从 1.6–1.8 降到 1.1。
- **因果性验证（重放与重置）**：固定范数重放（Hyperball 变体）中，早期 decay 运行的相对步长轨迹比无 decay 轨迹好 0.022–0.023 nats（原始 Muon 差距 0.0171），且轨迹交换实验将 0.0221 nats 重放差距中的 0.0181 归因于撤除后的步长；撤除时刻的范数重置实验在 162M 恢复/消除 96%/97% 的收益、405M 恢复/消除 91%/71%。反向扰动（早期学习率 boost）按同一递推预测提高了范数并使后续相对步长偏小，三个 boost 均以更高损失收尾。
- **跨规模与条件稳健性 + 第二种干预**：10% 早期窗口在 162M/405M/1.4B/3.4B 全部尺度优于无 decay 与最佳采样恒定 decay（405M 三个种子下比无 decay 好 0.0247–0.0262 nats）；在 20× 计算最优预算（64,000 步）、warmup、SwiGLU、AdamW(162M)、四种数据域下现象依然存在（AdamW 405M 除外）。结构不同的 Gram 惩罚干预呈现相同的时序依赖（0%/20%/60% 起始分别 −0.0147/−0.0080/+0.0209 nats），表明机制不限于乘性 weight decay。

## Key Contributions

- **Documents and quantifies "delayed effects"**: a temporary intervention (early weight-decay window) can have opposite-signed effects at removal versus at the final budget. The headline: a 162M-selected schedule (λ=0.15, first 44% of training) transferred unretuned to 1.4B is 0.26 nats worse than no decay at removal, overtakes it only after 73% of training, yet finishes 0.0275 nats better and beats the best sampled constant-decay baseline by 0.0264 nats.
- **Disentangles three conflated notions** — when the window acts, how long training continues after removal, and the final evaluation budget: start time reverses the sign (a 10% window from initialization helps; the same window starting at 60% hurts at every scale); evaluation horizon reverses rankings (at 405M, early-start schedules rank oppositely under final-checkpoint vs equal-post-removal comparison); at matched total log-shrinkage D≈2.1, lengthening the window from 700 to 3,564 updates more than doubles the final improvement, so total shrinkage alone cannot rank schedules.
- **Identifies a mechanism — persistent norm deficit and relative steps**: early decay drops weight norms to 12–16% of no-decay values at removal (still 60–66% at the end at 1.4B), and because Muon updates have approximately controlled magnitude, relative step size stays elevated after removal. An approximate recurrence (1/a²t+1 − 1/a²t ≈ 1 + 2κt) explains the slow convergence: 1/α²t grows roughly fourfold from update 400 to 2,800 while the between-run gap stays nearly fixed, so the relative-step ratio falls only from 1.6–1.8 to 1.1.
- **Causal tests via replay and reset**: in fixed-norm replays (a Hyperball variant holding all norms at initialization), the early-decay relative-step trajectory beats the no-decay trajectory by 0.022–0.023 nats across three seeds (original Muon difference: 0.0171), and a trajectory swap attributes 0.0181 of the 0.0221-nat replay difference to post-removal steps. Norm resets at removal recover/remove 96%/97% of the gain at 162M and 91%/71% at 405M. An opposite perturbation (early learning-rate boost) raises norms, leaves relative steps smaller — as the same recurrence predicts — and all three boosts finish with higher loss.
- **Robustness across scale and conditions, plus a second intervention**: a first-10% window beats both no decay and the best sampled constant at all scales (162M, 405M, 1.4B, 3.4B); at 405M across three seeds it improves on no decay by 0.0247–0.0262 nats and on constant 0.02 by 0.0172–0.0194 nats. The effect survives 20× compute-optimal training (64,000 updates), warmup, SwiGLU, AdamW at 162M, and four data domains (but not AdamW at 405M). A structurally different Gram-penalty intervention shows the same timing dependence (10% windows starting at 0%/20%/60% change final loss by −0.0147/−0.0080/+0.0209 nats), indicating the dynamics are not unique to multiplicative decay.

## Methodology

The authors train decoder-only Transformers at 162M/405M/1.4B/3.4B parameters (widths 768–3,072; ReLU² MLPs of width 4d; pre-norm blocks, QK-norm, RoPE base 10,000; 50,304 vocab; untied embeddings) with Muon (LR 0.01, momentum 0.95, spectral-norm adjustment, linear decay to zero over the last 10% of updates, no warmup by default; embeddings/head use AdamW). Batch is 1,024 sequences × 1,024 tokens; budgets are compute-optimal (~20 tokens/param: 3,200–66,000 updates). Main timing/scale experiments use FineWeb100B; replicated mechanism experiments use FineWeb-Edu; domain studies add OpenWebMath, GitHub-Code-Clean, and multilingual C4. The intervention is decoupled weight decay Wt+1 = (1 − ηt λt)Wt − ηt Ut applied only inside a window [s, e), with embeddings and the head exempt. Losses are next-token validation cross-entropy in nats, always compared within matched groups sharing initialization, data order, and LR schedule.

The experimental design systematically varies window start (0–60%), duration (5%–100% of training), coefficient, and evaluation point, including a 4×3 duration/coefficient grid at 162M with 16,000 updates and matched-total-shrinkage (D ≈ 2.1) comparisons. Mechanism work defines relative step size αt = ∥P⊥Ut∥F/∥Wt∥F, derives an approximate recurrence for norm dynamics under roughly equal-norm Muon updates, and validates it with three causal controls: (1) fixed-norm replays that prescribe measured αt trajectories while holding norms fixed (a Hyperball variant), including trajectory swaps at the removal boundary; (2) norm resets at removal that rescale each matrix family of one run to the counterpart's norms while preserving directions and optimizer state; (3) early learning-rate boosts as an opposite-sign perturbation. A second intervention — an off-diagonal Gram penalty on {WV, WO, Wdown} — tests generality, with radial/tangential gradient decomposition and copied-Gram-state controls. Robustness is probed via warmup, SwiGLU, AdamW, learning-rate sweeps (0.005–0.04), gradual (linear-taper) decay schedules, 20×-longer training, four data domains, and 3.4B runs with zero-shot evaluations (HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, PIQA, LAMBADA, WinoGrande).

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline delayed reversal (1.4B, FineWeb100B, 28,000 updates)**: the transferred schedule (λ=0.15 for the first 44%) is 0.2616 nats worse than no decay at removal, first overtakes no decay at update 20,440 (73% of training), and finishes 0.0275 nats better; it also beats the best sampled constant (0.003) by 0.0264 nats, while the best schedule sampled directly at 1.4B improves on it by only 0.0020 nats.
- **Replication at 162M (five matched seeds, FineWeb-Edu, 3,200 updates)**: a 320-update early-decay window (λ=0.3) improves final loss by 0.0172 nats over no decay (3.1701 ± 0.0027 vs 3.1873 ± 0.0028) and by 0.0074 nats over the best sampled constant (λ=0.05); the Gram penalty gives −0.0142 and the combination −0.0191.
- **Start time flips the sign at every scale**: 10% windows starting at initialization beat both references, while the same windows starting at 60% of training are worse than both — at 1.4B, first-10% (λ=0.2) reaches 2.6506 vs 2.6754 (no decay, −0.0248) and 2.6743 (best constant); at 405M the improvement over no decay is 0.0247–0.0262 nats across three seeds.
- **Duration matters beyond total shrinkage (162M, 16,000 updates)**: varying duration changes the final improvement by 0.026–0.035 nats versus only 0.007–0.010 for the coefficient; at matched D ≈ 2.1, going from a 700-update to a 3,564-update window more than doubles the gain; 10 of 12 grid cells beat the best constant (0.05), and the best cell (λ=0.15, first 44%) improves on it by 0.0335 nats. There is an interior optimum (50% at 162M, 20% at 405M); full-run decay is worse than no decay at 405M (+0.0880 nats, Table 6).
- **Mechanism quantified**: norms at 1.4B fall to 12–16% of no-decay values at removal and remain 60–66% at the final checkpoint; in fixed-norm replays the early-decay αt trajectory improves final loss by 0.0221–0.0233 nats over the no-decay trajectory across three seeds (vs 0.0171 between the source Muon runs), and 0.0181 of the 0.0221-nat replay difference comes from post-window steps; norm resets recover 96%/remove 97% of the gain at 162M and 91%/71% at 405M; larger relative steps are not sufficient — continuing decay at 0.10 after the window keeps steps larger but yields only −0.0071 vs −0.0171 nats.
- **Robustness and 3.4B**: with 10% warmup, temporary decay is still 0.0237 nats worse at update 8,000 but 0.0373 better at 14,400 of a 16,000-update 162M run; at 20× budget (64,000 updates) the first-10% window beats the best constant by 0.0180 nats; at 3.4B every early schedule beats all references on both loss and accuracy — the 12,320-update transfer reaches 2.4638 (−0.0194 vs no decay) with mean zero-shot accuracy 66.90% vs 65.30%, whereas both tested constants hurt loss (0.001: +0.0011; 0.003: +0.0063).
- **Downstream caveat**: at 405M and 1.4B, mean zero-shot accuracy differs by at most 0.11 points between early decay and constant 0.01 (at 1.4B early decay is +0.65 over no decay); the validation-loss advantage does not translate into a clear downstream advantage until 3.4B.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core observation — that an intervention's effect keeps evolving after removal, to the point of sign/rank reversal — goes meaningfully beyond Golatkar et al. (2019), who showed regularization matters disproportionately early but did not study post-removal dynamics; the transient (non-equilibrium) analysis of norm deficits under Muon is complementary to equilibrium analyses (Kosson et al. 2023; Wen et al. 2026 Hyperball; Liu et al. 2026). The methodological point (evaluate at final budget) is genuinely useful for anyone doing schedule search. That said, the ingredients — norm deficit → larger effective steps — are individually known; the contribution is the careful causal chaining and evaluation-time analysis, not a new algorithm.
- **Rigor**: High. Five-seed replication for the headline 162M comparison, three seeds at 405M, three replay seeds, paired comparisons sharing initialization and data order, opposite-sign perturbations (LR boosts), trajectory swaps, norm resets, learning-rate sensitivity sweeps (Appendix J), and a second structurally different intervention (Gram penalty). The trajectory-swap control (post-window steps account for 0.0181 of 0.0221 nats) is a particularly clean localization.
- **Weak spots**: (1) Many headline comparisons — including the entire 1.4B and 3.4B tables — use a single seed; at 162M the seed standard deviation is ±0.0028 nats, so 0.002–0.01-nat single-seed margins (e.g., the 0.0020-nat gap between transferred and directly sampled 1.4B schedules, or the 0.0011-nat 3.4B constant-decay result) are within plausible noise. (2) The best sampled schedule at each scale actually beats the transferred and fixed first-10% schedules (Table 7: 3.3510/3.0180/2.6459 vs 3.3710/3.0287/2.6506), so the celebrated 44%-window is not the strongest configuration the authors themselves found — the transfer story is about robustness, not optimality. (3) Baselines are "best sampled" constants from small grids, not fully tuned schedules; a well-tuned cosine/truncated decay schedule was not run (the gradual-decay appendix shows a linear 20% taper beats the abrupt window at both scales, suggesting the paper's headline schedule family is not special). (4) Downstream gains at 405M/1.4B are within 0.11 points of constant decay — the practical payoff over a decent constant baseline rests on ~0.007–0.03 nats of validation loss. (5) The mechanism is established almost entirely at 162M; at 1.4B the recurrence could not be tested directly (missing relative-step logs), and the recurrence overpredicts norm growth for WV and WO even at 162M. (6) The paper explicitly cannot explain why early-decay trajectories help and late ones hurt — norms explain persistence, not benefit.
- **Reproducibility**: Reasonable. Public datasets (FineWeb, FineWeb-Edu, OpenWebMath, GitHub-Code-Clean, mC4), public base code (microsoft/dion), standard model sizes, and exact hyperparameters in Appendix A. But the many custom windows/coefficient grids, single-seed configurations, and one hardware fault (the 405M no-decay norm-reset source run stopped at update 5,700) will make exact number-matching laborious.
- **Red flags**: No fabrication or cherry-picking concerns; the 0.26-nat "worse at removal" figure is prominently disclosed as part of the phenomenon. The main overclaim risk is implicit: the abstract's "0.028 nats better" final margin on a single 1.4B seed, versus a ±0.003-nat seed spread measured at 162M, is a thin margin for the strongest transfer claim — though the 405M three-seed and 3.4B results lend it support.

## Strengths

- Identifies a clean, surprising, and previously under-documented phenomenon (sign reversal of an intervention's effect long after its removal) and turns it into an actionable evaluation principle.
- Exceptional experimental hygiene for a systems/optimization paper: matched seeds, paired statistics, matched-total-shrinkage comparisons, trajectory swaps, norm resets, opposite-sign perturbations, and a second intervention that reproduces the dynamics.
- The mechanism analysis (norm deficit → elevated relative steps → slow 1/a² convergence) is quantitative, predictive (the LR-boost offset matches within 8% for Wup/Wdown), and honestly bounded (it explains persistence, not benefit).
- Broad robustness sweep: four scales up to 3.4B, 20× training budgets, warmup, SwiGLU, AdamW, LR sweeps, four data domains, and zero-shot accuracy — with negative results reported (AdamW at 405M, Gram at 1.4B needing retuning).
- Clear writing with the methodological takeaway stated crisply: evaluate temporary schedules at the intended final training budget, not at removal.

## Limitations

- Single-seed results dominate outside the replicated 162M/405M comparisons, including the 1.4B and 3.4B headline tables; several reported margins (0.001–0.01 nats) are comparable to the measured seed noise of ±0.0028 nats at 162M.
- Decay coefficients are explicitly non-universal across scales and settings, and the Gram penalty requires retuning at 1.4B (μ=10⁻³ hurts, +0.0017; μ=10⁻⁴ helps, −0.0054), so practitioners get a phenomenon and an evaluation protocol rather than a drop-in schedule.
- The mechanism (norms/relative steps) is demonstrated mostly at 162M; the paper does not establish that norms are the only persistent state, nor why early-decay step trajectories are beneficial while equally large steps from continued decay (λ=0.10 → only −0.0071 nats) or late windows (+loss) are not.
- Downstream (zero-shot) benefits are negligible at 405M/1.4B (≤0.11 mean-accuracy points vs constant 0.01) and only become consistent at 3.4B; validation-loss deltas of 0.01–0.03 nats may not matter for many applications.
- Baselines are sampled constants and windowed schedules, not fully tuned schedules: Appendix I shows a linear-taper decay over the first 20% beats the abrupt window at both scales (3.3689 vs 3.3708 at 162M; 3.0257 vs 3.0287 at 405M), so the abrupt-window family examined in most of the paper is not the strongest decay schedule even within the paper's own experiments.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 预训练与优化器（尤其是 Muon）研究的人高度相关：它直接研究 weight decay 调度与 Muon 更新动力学的交互，并给出了一个对任何临时训练干预（LR 调度、正则窗口、数据课程）都适用的评估原则——必须在最终训练预算处评估，而非撤除时。若你的兴趣涵盖训练效率与优化器设计，这篇论文的范数-相对步长机制分析和跨尺度迁移结论都是可复用的实证依据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why do early-decay relative-step trajectories help while late windows (same shrinkage) and continued decay (larger steps) hurt? Is there an interaction with the critical-learning-period literature the paper cites but does not connect mechanistically?
- Does the delayed effect persist under data curricula, dropout, or other temporary interventions that do not primarily change weight norms, or is the norm channel essentially the only carrier under Muon?
- How does the phenomenon interact with the final linear LR decay phase and with cyclic/cosine schedules — does the crossover point move predictably with the schedule shape?
- Can the 1/a² recurrence be turned into a predictive tool that, given a removal-time norm deficit, forecasts the final-loss delta without running the full continuation?
- Would the transfer results hold for the linear-taper schedules that beat abrupt windows in Appendix I, and does the optimal window duration follow any scaling trend between 162M and 3.4B?
- Since Muon's norm-controlled updates are central, does an analogous delayed effect exist for AdamW at larger scales, or was the 405M AdamW failure a learning-rate/coefficient-grid artifact rather than an optimizer difference?
