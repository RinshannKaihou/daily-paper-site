Key Finding

The classic active-learning strategy — experiment where models disagree most — steers AI scientists away from undiscovered phenomena, losing to random sampling.

越严格跟随“在模型分歧最大处做实验”，越容易被引离新现象——两个前沿 AI 科学家进入隐藏峰区的频率都低于完全不拟合方程的随机采样。

Asking the right question is key to AI discovery in a chain of hypotheses

Key Metric · Peak-Region Entry
1/32

GPT-6.1-Sol entered the hidden peak region W in just 1 of 32 trials (3%) — random sampling that fits no equations at all entered 82% of the time.

Sonnet 5.5: 19/32 (59%) · bisection: 32/32
Why This Matters

Directly informs building and benchmarking autonomous AI scientists: score where agents choose to measure, not just the equations they write.

⚠ Critical Edge

The failure may belong to this stripped-down harness — no code execution, no tools, n = 1 model per family — not to hypothesis-guided search itself.

Methodology

y = (V·x₁)/(K+x₁) · 1/(1+x₂/J) + A·exp(−(x₂−c)²/2S²) + ε, σ=0.08
  • Task — closed-loop equation discovery on a synthetic 3-input system hiding a narrow Gaussian peak (up to 31σ) inside region W = 7% of the x₂ range; invisible (<1σ) in starting data.
  • Trials — 32 paired runs, identical starting data (4 conditions outside W) and shared noise seeds; peak centers stratified across [2.5, 8.5].
  • Agents — Claude Sonnet 5.5 & GPT-6.1-Sol, high reasoning effort, no code, calculator, or tools. 12 rounds: see all data + 12 BIC-ranked equations, propose ≤3 equations, pick 2 experiments (measured twice each).
  • Fitting — bounded nonlinear least squares with agent guesses + 3 random restarts; BIC = χ² + k·ln N.
  • Success — E = RMSE/σ ≤ 1.05 on 4,096 held-out points; peak-free curves bottom out at E = 3.9–4.8.
  • Baselines & stats — random x₂ sampling (no oracle), interval bisection (oracle); exact binomial / McNemar / Fisher / Wilcoxon with Wilson & bootstrap CIs.

Strengths

  • Maximally clean causal demo: feature invisible in starting data (<1σ), unrepresentable in the BIC-winning class until observed — failure traced round-by-round, not just end-to-end.
  • Paired trials with shared noise seeds + exact paired tests make the between-agent comparison tight (0.92 vs 0.48 disagreement-following, all 31 shared trials).
  • Constructive recipe, not just failure: ≥10σ early anomaly + ≥3 positions + enough rounds → 10/10 recovery; agents revise well once confronted with evidence.
  • Actionable benchmark guidance (BoxingGym, NewtonBench, LLM-AutoSciLab): score measurement placement, avoid integer-aligned features, include space-filling baselines.
  • Human-science parallels (confirmation bias, streetlight effect, Chamberlin) drawn carefully, not rhetorically.

Limitations

  • One synthetic 3-variable system, one narrow 1-D peak, fixed 12-round × 2-point budget — external validity argued, not shown.
  • Two agents, one harness each, no prompt/scaffolding ablations; the Sonnet-vs-GPT behavioral gap can't be generalized to model families.
  • No-tools/no-code protocol strips safeguards real deployments have; practical severity of the blind spot is unknown.
  • Timing result (post-Round-9 hits fatal) partly a budget artifact; ≥10σ threshold and 7% peak width are single operating points.
  • Small sub-cells (2-position bin, n=5); no code or transcript release mentioned.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Q1Would the blind spot persist with code execution or a prompt suggesting space-filling / Latin-hypercube exploration — is the failure in the agents' inductive biases or the harness?
  2. Q2Can the two-stage strategy (broad early coverage → anomaly-focused follow-up) be scaffolded or rewarded to close the gap to 82%/100% baseline entry rates without hurting fit quality?
  3. Q3Does the agent ranking flip on spread-out (non-localized) hidden structure, where disagreement-guided search should be near-optimal?
  4. Q4How does failure scale with dimensionality — does entry probability collapse as unmeasured volume grows, and can disagreement signals be regularized (ensemble over model classes) to recover coverage?
  5. Q5Is the ≥10σ "surprise" trigger stable across noise models (heteroscedastic, heavy-tailed) and across agents, or is it Sonnet 5.5-specific behavior?
