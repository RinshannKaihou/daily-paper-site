Why This Matters

Self-improvement overhead can cost 3–4× the tokens of the tasks themselves — a near-free 2,048-token note reaches the same accuracy.

from Relevance: RSI Tax “可能数倍于任务执行本身”，为 agent 持续学习提供新度量框架与关键警示

Critical Edge

The expensive rivals ran as narrowed streaming adaptations — so the real verdict is “these adaptations aren’t worth the cost,” not the original published pipelines.

And it’s single-run point estimates: headline gaps like 78.7% vs 77.5% sit within plausible run-to-run noise.

Methodology

Six methods — Static, ReasoningBank, EvoSkill, Meta-Harness, RRSI, and the proposed SRN — run on two ordered task streams: SWE-Chain-Evo (7 repo chains × 15 positions = 105 tasks, state reset at chain boundaries) and Terminal-Bench 2.1 (all 89 terminal tasks, one fixed order, persistent learned state). Two backbones — DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GPT-6 Luna — under a shared KIRA agent (262,144-token context, ≤100 turns): 2,328 scored method–task pairs.

Streaming protocol: every task is scored before its feedback becomes available for updates — accuracy is never inflated by re-running already-scored tasks. Updates see only public instructions, visible trajectories, and scalar outcomes.

Cost accounting: each model call is tagged task-execution or self-improvement (request-ID dedup, cached input included). The tax rate is ρ = N_RSI / N_task; SRN’s own note revisions total just 1.21M tokens. RSI spend is decomposed into Verify / Explore / Write and tagged by whether its artifact is ever used by a later task.

SRN vs Static: +4.7 pts accuracy for +3.6% tokens; SRN’s updates are <0.4% of its total budget (tax rate 0.04–0.36%).

  • Omitted taxes invert rankings: on execution tokens alone Meta-Harness looks cheaper than SRN (184.13M vs 214.37M) — its 578.45M RSI Tax flips totals to 762.57M vs 214.65M.
  • Verification dominates the tax: 74.8% (Meta-Harness), 78.1% (EvoSkill), 93.6% (RRSI); tax rates reach 314–393% of task-execution tokens on Terminal-Bench.
  • 23.7–63.6% of RSI tokens buy artifacts never used later; EvoSkill spent 497.45M re-scoring its old frontier vs 182.26M serving all 89 tasks.

Strengths

  • RSI Tax is a clean, auditable metric — it charges even rejected updates.
  • SRN is an elegant control: isolates cheap cumulative note-keeping from expensive search + validation.
  • Verify/Explore/Write + use-attribution turns “RSI is expensive” into an actionable target — verification, ~98% of it input tokens.
  • Honest hedging throughout: “observed accuracy,” explicit macro-averaging, candid limitations.
  • Current, deployment-relevant streams (SWE repo chains, terminal tasks) with two frontier-class backbones.

Limitations

  • No repeated runs or variance: 1–3-point accuracy gaps could be noise.
  • Only 7 of 50 SWE-Chain-Evo chains and one fixed Terminal-Bench ordering — sensitivity untested.
  • Rivals are streaming adaptations, not the original published procedures.
  • Token counts ≠ money: cached input (~98.1% of the tax) counted at full weight; latency ignored.
  • SRN itself is not ablated — note size, update cadence, failure-only selection are all fixed.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would predicting expected future use before validating (cheap retrieval-likelihood screening) eliminate most of the verification-dominated tax?
  2. Does the tax persist under original unrestricted Meta-Harness search and EvoSkill v2 — or is it an artifact of validation-heavy streaming adaptations?
  3. How much of the “never-used” spend is causally removable? Controlled deletion of those calls would test the attribution.
  4. Since ~98.1% of the tax is input tokens, would cheaper verifier models or log-prob scoring retain selection quality?
  5. Do SRN’s gains survive longer streams, where a 2,048-token note saturates but skill libraries keep growing?
