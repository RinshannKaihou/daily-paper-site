What the Loss Curve Misses: FP4 Pretraining Slows the Acquisition of Rare Facts ↗
Chang Lu, Mengjiao Zhang · 2026-11-09 · Independent preprint · pre-registered criteria · seed-paired (6–8 seeds)
arXiv 2611.12832 low-precision training FP4 quantization knowledge acquisition evaluation methodology
Key Metric
1.52×

Exposures needed per rare fact vs BF16 on natural text (1.32× synthetic) — while the validation-loss gap is only 1.9–2.1%.

Why This Matters

Loss-only evaluation systematically overestimates low-precision training quality; the exposure multiplier and fit-free checkpoint diagnostic are directly reusable evaluation tools.

⚠ Critical Edge

All evidence comes from models ≤96.6M parameters on ≤1B tokens — whether the multiplier shrinks or grows at production scale (12B, 10T tokens) is explicitly open.

Methodology

  • Controlled-fact corpora. Synthetic biographies (bioS, Allen-Zhu & Li 2024): one fact = one person's attribute, binned to 1–256 exposures. Natural regime: 20k biographies embedded in a 1B-token FineWeb-Edu mix (7.2% bio tokens).
  • Models. Pre-norm transformers at 12M–100M params, AdamW, WSD schedule, single RTX 4090.
  • Emulated quantization. Operands quantized exactly to format, multiplied in FP32 — isolates rounding from kernel effects. NVFP4 = E2M1 in 16-element blocks with E4M3 scales; embeddings, head, and norms stay high precision.
  • Causal noise experiments. NVFP4-shaped Gaussian noise injected into backward outputs; a second BF16 backward routes it into only Adam's first or second moment.
  • Statistics. Multiplier = geometric mean over 6–8 seed-paired runs, 95% t-intervals on the log2 scale; learning-rate compensation criteria pre-registered.

  • Mechanism: noise entering Adam's update itself carries ~2/3 of the cost and never fades — raising the learning rate does not remove it (pre-registered test).
  • Fit-free diagnostic: Eq. (1) from BF16-checkpoint gradient stats alone ranks 17 configurations at Spearman 0.96 [0.84, 0.99], inside 15/17 observed intervals.
  • Why loss is blind: +0.67–0.85 nats for facts seen 1–4×, ≤0.07 nats for ≥32×; frequency-weighting shrinks the gap to 0.04 nats.
  • Remedies recover loss, not facts: adapted NVIDIA recipe closes 60% of the loss gap but only 42% of the exposure cost; facts seen only during BF16 cooldown keep 54% of the deficit.
  • Robust: persists at 100M (1.30×), under linear decay (1.42×), and after per-format LR selection (1.27×).

Strengths

  • Crisp, answerable question with a reusable, monotone-invariant metric (exposure multiplier).
  • Interventional mechanism design: split-moment noise routing plus pre-registered learning-rate falsification tests.
  • Fit-free checkpoint diagnostic (Spearman 0.96, 17 configs) is immediately actionable for screening FP4 recipes without retraining.
  • Quantitative anatomy of loss-blindness makes Proposition 3 concrete, not abstract.
  • Transparent statistics: paired seeds, 95% intervals, Holm correction, disclosed fragilities of secondary readouts.

Limitations

  • Scale: ≤96.6M params, ≤1B tokens; no native FP4 hardware, so FP4's wall-clock economics are outside the analysis.
  • Facts are synthetic, fixed-template biographies — no paraphrased or naturally occurring long-tail probes.
  • Only AdamW tested; one LR schedule; a three-point sweep cannot exclude an untested rate closing the gap.
  • Zero-mean noise account covers the backward pass; full FP4's biased gradient error is handled only diagnostically.
  • One shared data order across seeds; Eq. (1) underestimates small errors; loss-matched multipliers are readout-sensitive.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the multiplier shrink, hold, or grow at production scale (≥10B params, ≥10T tokens)?
  2. Would Muon or other matrix-based optimizers avoid the signal-to-noise cost attributed to Adam?
  3. Is the BF16-cooldown deficit (54% retained) delayed circuit formation rather than per-exposure slowing?
  4. Does the deficit extend to natural long-tail knowledge and paraphrased or multi-hop probes?
  5. What is the optimal re-weighting of knowledge-dense data in an FP4 run — and what does it cost other capabilities?
  6. Could error-shaping be built into FP4 rounding schemes to attack the ρi = si²/vi ratio directly?
