Why This Matters

Reusable fidelity/stability metrics, a working BiJumpReLU recipe, and the finding that concepts are cross-layer atom combinations — directly informing probing, feature steering, and knowledge editing.

Critical Edge

The headline stability metric is partly self-fulfilling — BiJumpReLU's training objective directly optimizes q*'s ingredients. Don't read 99.8 as “99.8% semantically clean.”

— Critical Assessment, on the q* metric

Methodology 方法论

1

Geometry first. Representations are identifiable only up to invertible linear reparameterization, so Euclidean angles are parameterization-dependent. The fix: an atomic inner product S̃ = (DDᵀ)⁻¹ that makes the (unknown) atom set itself the orthonormal reference — relaxed to ε-approximate orthogonality under superposition (|D| ≫ H).

2

Two measurable criteria. Fidelity = reconstruction R²; stability q* = the largest quantile where empirical coherence and sparsity still satisfy the compressed-sensing uniqueness condition µ < 1/(2K−1), found by binary search over quantiles.

3

Identifiability theorem. If data truly follow m = Dδ with near-orthogonal D, a threshold-activated SAE (W_dec = D, W_enc = DᵀS̃, bidirectional thresholds) reconstructs every m exactly. BiJumpReLU instantiates it with learnable signed thresholds (λ = 5.0, lr 2e-4, batch 4096, straight-through estimator, trajectory-based early stopping).

4

Breadth. Representations from 6 layers of each LLM (1.8M Wikipedia vectors/model), 1.8M Wikidata entity vectors, and diffusion internals (SD v1.5, FLUX.2-klein-9B); dictionaries held at ~221K width for comparability; HowNet sememes aligned via overlapping entity sets.

Results 结果与亮点

  • Stability predicts monosemanticity: Spearman ρ = 0.71 (P < 0.001, N = 40 combos); 0.74 / 0.79 / 0.95 within Wikipedia / Wikidata / LAION-COCO; all 8 model-level correlations positive (0.37–0.93).
  • Hardest setting (LAION-COCO · FLUX.2-klein-9B): stability 98.47 vs 64.12 for JumpReLU and 41.56 for TopK; fidelity 96.03 vs 88.33 for TopK.
  • Neither neurons nor pretrained Scope features are ideal atoms: neurons show high fidelity but low stability; Gemma/Llama Scope features the reverse — neither approaches R² = q* = 1.
  • Composition gain is not a search artifact: |r| < 0.10 (P < 0.001) between cross-layer width and sememe F1; only ~1% of sememes admit a single-atom F1 = 1, and 79–82% need > 6 atoms.

Strengths

  • Self-contained theory pipeline: invariance argument → atomic inner product → coherence distinguishability → fidelity/stability metrics → an identifiability theorem that also explains why ReLU (no threshold), TopK/JumpReLU (one-sided), AbsTopK (preset K) fall short.
  • Unusually broad footprint for interpretability: 7 models, 3 representation sources (text, entities, diffusion internals), 5 SAE families — mean ± 95% CI reported throughout.
  • The stability–monosemanticity link is tested at three granularities (global, per-dataset, per-model) rather than asserted.
  • HowNet analysis operationalizes “representation ↔ human concept structure,” with a thoughtful cross-layer-width equivalence control and honest framing of provable monorepresentationality vs empirical monosemanticity.

Limitations

  • Theorem 11 assumes noiseless K-sparse generation with coefficients bounded away from zero — no robustness analysis and no estimate of how close trained SAEs sit to the theorem's conditions.
  • q* is a distributional quantile condition whose near-1 values partly mirror BiJumpReLU's own training objective — not a per-atom semantic quality measure.
  • Monosemanticity sampling is tiny (10 units × ≤10 instances per site) and LLM-judge dependent; some model-level correlations are weak (ρ = 0.37, P = 0.041).
  • Gemma/Llama Scope comparisons confound architecture with width, training data, and scale; Gated/BatchTopK baselines absent; LLM margins over JumpReLU often just ~1–3 points.
  • The compositional claim rests on greedy 54–62-atom searches without matched-size random-combination controls; knowledge-editing and continual-learning implications remain argued, not tested.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can the gap to Theorem 11's threshold sandwich (δmin vs B₀ + B±) be estimated from data as a practical identifiability certificate?
  2. Does the stability–monosemanticity correlation survive at scale — 1,000 features per site, a fixed open judge with published prompts, automated scoring for diffusion latents?
  3. How much of the ΔF1 = +0.44–0.50 is structure rather than set-union coverage — do matched-size random atom combinations recover sememes as well?
  4. If concepts span ~60 cross-layer atoms, can composition-aware interventions measurably reduce collateral or incomplete edits in ROME/MEMIT-style editing?
  5. Does the AIP geometry change conclusions from prior Euclidean-geometry studies — feature splitting, dictionary-learning scaling laws?
  6. Is JumpReLU's FLUX.2 stability collapse (64.12 vs 98.47) a vision-token modality property or a sign-convention pathology?
