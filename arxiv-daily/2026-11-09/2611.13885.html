Methodology

Models: Qwen3-0.6B / 1.7B, Llama-3.2-1B, Gemma-3-1B (base checkpoints). A “neuron” is one coordinate of a gated-MLP activation at a given layer; all interventions act only at the last prompt token.

Four interventions: zero ablation, mean ablation, resampling (activation from a different prompt), and patching (from a designated source prompt). Metric: margin m = logit(correct) − logit(alternative); effect Δ(S) = m(clean) − m(S), normalized by clean margin m₀.

Group selection: candidates ranked by first-order AtP*-style estimates, then measured individually; the frozen 12 = 6 strongest measured + 6 best estimates — so weak-alone neurons are not excluded a priori.

The exhaustive step: for each frozen group, Δ(S) is measured for all 2¹² = 4,096 subsets, yielding additivity fits, pairwise interactions, minimal effective sets, disjoint alternative pairs, and transfer correlations. Controls: 12 layer-matched random neurons, identical intervention, fixed seed.

Rigor scaffolding: measured bfloat16 noise floor (0.125–0.50 logits), interaction thresholds at max(5% of effect, 3× noise), 95% bootstrap CIs, pre-specified decision rules with a public deviation log (Appendix B).

Strengths

  • Exhaustive, not sampled — necessity/uniqueness claims are complete relative to the candidate group; no statistical extrapolation.
  • Study 1 is a genuinely useful negative control for the field: the standard evidence bundle can be produced by a content-agnostic group, and the paper names the control that catches it.
  • Cross-family replication on an identical 7-chain set with a pre-frozen protocol (flips without random flips, small sufficient subsets, strong same-chain / weak cross-chain transfer).
  • Exceptional transparency: pre-specified rules, a public post-hoc-change log, measured noise floors, a repo that recomputes every number.
  • Hypothesis-generating bridge between circuit structure and model uncertainty (low margin ↔ disjoint alternatives).

Limitations

  • Pilot scale both ways: 0.6–1.7B models (32B did not fit in GPU memory) and ≤ 11 analyzed chains per model, all author-written with pop-culture/geography skew.
  • Selection bakes in additivity: candidates chosen as strongest single neurons + best first-order estimates — purely synergistic circuits would be missed by design.
  • Last-token only: interventions never touch earlier positions; multi-token answers judged by first token.
  • Noise floor up to 0.50 logits (bfloat16) may hide small interactions; margin cut-off m₀ = 4 chosen after seeing data, confounded with chain identity.
  • One random group per question (fixed seed), not a distribution; disjoint-alternative claim rests on margin criterion, not generation checks.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Do the findings hold at 8B–32B scale and on Goodfire’s actual 18-neuron group, where margins and redundancy may differ?
  2. Would candidate selection admitting synergistic neurons (greedy pair search, higher-order estimates) change the additivity and minimal-set conclusions?
  3. What explains the Study 1 group mechanistically — positional/format signal, a “question-answering mode” feature, or an artifact of two-option three-shot prompting?
  4. Is low-margin ↔ disjoint-alternatives a general property, and does it survive interventions at earlier or multiple token positions?
  5. How do subset statistics interact with backup heads / the Hydra effect — does ablating a minimal subset trigger downstream self-repair?
  6. Can a content-matched control be standardized into circuit-discovery pipelines (ACDC, attribution graphs, transcoders) by default?
