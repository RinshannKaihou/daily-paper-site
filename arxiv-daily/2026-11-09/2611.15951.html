Shard orthogonally inside the GPU, not deeper across it — chiplet partitioning becomes up to 4× faster decode attention.

一句话总结：针对 AMD Instinct 的 chiplet/NUMA 分区模式（CPX+NPS4）提出"正交分片"原则：在 GPU 内部沿正交维度新增一层分片——序列维度切分 KV cache 消除复制、二维 GEMM 分片保持算术强度、分层 collective 利用 USR 相对 XGMI 的 10 倍带宽差——在 MI300X 上使 decode 阶段 self-attention 内核最高提速 4 倍，而朴素分片反而因 KV 复制导致 OOM。

Starscream: Reimagining LLM Sharding on AMD Instinct GPUs with GPU Partitioning ↗
Sumanth Gudaparthi · Smeet Chheda · Ganesh Dasika — AMD Research & Advanced Development
LLM Inference Tensor Parallelism Chiplet NUMA AMD MI300X Hierarchical Collectives
Key Metric
4.0×

Self-attention decode kernel speedup on MI300X in CPX/NPS4 vs the SPX/NPS1 unpartitioned baseline — Llama3-70B/405B & GPT-OSS-120B, 16K–128K context, batch 1–1024.

Naive sharding in the same partition mode: consistently slower than the baseline — and OOM at batch sizes where both competitors still run.

Why This Matters

Chiplet NUMA is now a first-class GPU property. A direct engineering blueprint for multi-die LLM serving, long-context decode latency, and NUMA-aware parallelism.

Critical Edge

All quantitative evidence is per-kernel — end-to-end gains are asserted, not measured — and the 4× headline is a small-batch best case, shrinking to ~1.08× in the throughput regime that matters for serving economics.

MMethodology — the orthogonal recipe

  • Topology first. MI300X = 8 XCDs on 4 IODs joined by an on-package USR ring (~10× the bandwidth of inter-GPU XGMI), 5.3 TB/s HBM3. CPX slices compute into 8 virtual GPUs; NPS4 splits HBM into 4 NUMA domains.
  • Why naive fails. vLLM / Megatron / FSDP treat virtual GPUs as independent devices: deepening 1-D TP replicates the KV cache 8×, collapses tile arithmetic intensity, and inflates flat all-reduce (21× more movement at 64-way).
  • The fix. Keep inter-GPU TP untouched; add an intra-GPU dimension chosen by three tradeoffs — kernel tile shape, memory redundancy, USR-vs-XGMI bandwidth.
  • Mechanisms. Sequence-length KV sharding with phase-aware all-gather (query side <0.1% at decode; 16×-smaller KV side at prefill); Row↔Column-parallel interchange for square-ish 2-D GEMM tiles (2× arithmetic intensity); three-phase hierarchical all-reduce (USR reduce-scatter → peer-XCD all-reduce over XGMI among 8 physical GPUs → USR all-gather); symmetric-memory fusion writes outputs straight into the destination NUMA region.
  • Evaluation. Triton + vLLM v0/v1 attention kernels, Stream-K GEMMs; MI300X & MI355X; 200+ GEMM shapes; SPX-tuned configs reused without CPX re-tuning — results framed as conservative lower bounds.

Read the 4× with care: it is a best-case small-batch regime; gains narrow at high batch as FP16 working sets exceed per-XCD L2 capacity. Naive CPX sharding OOMs because KV replication amplifies a cache that already costs 3× parameter memory in long context.

Strengths

  • Real, timely problem — chiplet NUMA is now first-class, and frameworks are provably topology-oblivious (down to concrete OOM failures).
  • General principle — the three-tradeoff recipe (tile shape, redundancy, communication) applies to TP, SP, FSDP, and future architectures, not just one kernel trick.
  • Phase-aware collectives grounded in the GQA head-count asymmetry, with quantified justification (<0.1% vs 4.4% data movement).
  • Correct by construction — the hierarchical all-reduce is mathematically equivalent to the flat collective.
  • Honest evaluation — explicitly labeled lower bounds, 1D-vs-2D ablation, cross-generation validation on MI355X.

Limitations

  • No end-to-end serving numbers — throughput, TTFT/TPOT, goodput all absent; every quantitative claim is per-kernel.
  • Gains shrink at high batch — ~1.08× for GEMMs in the throughput regime; diminishing beyond batch 2048 (per-XCD L2 thrashing).
  • Training untested — the FSDP2 pathology is analyzed persuasively but never implemented or benchmarked.
  • AMD-only scope — MI355X only in 2-way DPX/NPS2; no NVIDIA or tuned-baseline comparison, so generality is argued, not shown.
  • Code unreleased at submission (pledged upon acceptance); per-figure geomeans never stated in text.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How much of the 4× kernel-level attention speedup survives at the full-model level (tokens/s, TTFT/TPOT at fixed SLOs)?
  2. Can the orthogonal-sharding recipe be instantiated for FSDP2 training, and at what speedup on pretraining or fine-tuning?
  3. What is the optimal split of sequence/head dimensions when composed with Ring Attention, DeepSpeed-Ulysses, or Helix as outer layers?
  4. Does it extend to MLA (DeepSeek-style) and clustered-head attention, where the KV/query asymmetry differs from GQA — and to FP8/FP4 working sets?
  5. How much of the advantage is kernel fusion vs topology-aware sharding alone — would the RCCL CPX tuner capture part of it?
  6. As XCD counts grow, does the two-tier decomposition generalize to a recursive N-tier collective before orchestration overhead dominates?
Provenance & Credibility

All three authors are at AMD Research & Advanced Development — the vendor of the evaluated hardware, giving deep access to partitioning modes and ARC tooling. A vendor paper by construction (AMD-only scope), but claims are tied to concrete kernel-level measurements rather than projections; one author continues an ongoing multi-GPU performance-tax research program (arXiv:2511.02168), and code is pledged open-source upon acceptance.
