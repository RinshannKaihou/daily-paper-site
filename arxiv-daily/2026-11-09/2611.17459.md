> **一句话总结：** 针对可分数据上的 logistic / 多类交叉熵这类"指数尾"损失（不存在有限最小化子），宾夕法尼亚大学团队用"曲率定义的参考子空间"（即最大间隔方向）取代此前理论依赖的最小化子流形，证明大步长梯度下降虽先经历不稳定振荡，但三阶耦合项会把振荡能量转化为沿最大间隔方向的前进、按指数压低曲率（不稳定相 λ₁ ≲ λ₁(θ₀)e^{−ct}），自稳定后正交分量经"非扩张→线性收缩(1−ε*)ᵗ→多项式衰减 O(t^{−γ})"三阶段阻尼、末次迭代损失 O(1/(1+t−t_stab))——并进一步证明：当支撑向量的正交分量不张成数据子空间时，大步长会放大并永久留下某些正交残差（瞬态隐式偏置），使有限时间内迭代偏离最大间隔方向。

> **TL;DR:** A University of Pennsylvania theory paper (Tarmoun, MacDonald, Emmanouilidis, Xu, Vidal) that unifies the two main lines of edge-of-stability (EoS) theory — Damian et al.'s self-stabilization mechanism (mechanism but no rates, assumptions verified only numerically) and Wu et al.'s convergence rates for logistic-like losses (rates but no mechanism) — by extending MacDonald et al.'s minimizer-manifold analysis (NeurIPS 2025, least squares only) to exponential-tail losses on separable, pair-symmetric data, where the loss infimum is never attained so no minimizer manifold exists. The substitute is a curvature-defined reference subspace (the max-margin direction), around which a second-order gradient Taylor expansion yields a coupled two-equation system whose third-order coupling term −(η²/2)θ⊥ᵀH′θ⊥ converts orthogonal oscillation energy into forward parallel motion, i.e., implicit gradient descent on the sharpness. Theorem 1 gives iterate-level rates: during the unstable phase the restricted sharpness decays exponentially, λ₁(θ∥_t) ≲ λ₁(θ∥_0)e^{−ct} (classical descent-lemma theory guarantees nothing once ηλ_max > 2), and after finite t_stab the orthogonal component goes through nonexpansion → linear contraction (1−ε*)^{t} with ε* = ρ_tail(1−δ_r)/(1+δ_r) → polynomial decay O((t−t_poly)^{−γ_r}), with last-iterate logistic loss O((1+t−t_stab)^{−1}) (vs Wu et al.'s sharper logarithmic averaged rate log(1+ηT)/(ηT), which the unified envelope intentionally sacrifices for generality). The genuinely new result is Theorem 2: when support vectors do not span the orthogonal subspace (conditioning ratio ρ(θ∥_t) ≍ t^{−(α_{j*}−α₁)/α₁}), the cumulative contraction budget stays finite, so weak orthogonal components are only partially damped — a transient implicit bias. In a controlled R³ non-spanning logistic experiment (η/η_c up to 3, T = 150), the support-vector component decays to ~10⁻¹³ while the weak component plateaus and grows with η; a spur-flip distribution-shift test (T = 20,000, 10 step sizes) shows the residual cuts effective test margin by 2|θ_spur|. All experiments are d = 3 synthetic validations — no deep nets, no real datasets.

## 核心贡献

- **用"曲率定义的参考子空间"取代最小化子流形，统一了两条 EoS 理论路线**：此前 Damian et al.（ICLR 2023）给出自稳定机制但无收敛速率、假设只能数值验证；Wu et al.（COLT 2024）对 logistic 型损失给出速率但不揭示机制；MacDonald et al.（NeurIPS 2025）在过参数化最小二乘上统一两者，但其分析根本依赖最小化子流形——而 logistic / 交叉熵在可分数据上的损失下确界在任何有限参数处都取不到。本文用最大间隔方向张成的一维子空间 M 作为几何锚点，把统一分析推广到指数尾损失。
- **把自稳定机制写成显式的耦合动力系统**：对梯度在平行方向点做二阶 Taylor 展开，得到"正交振荡方程 + 平行漂移方程"的二方程系统；其中三阶耦合项 −(η²/2)θ⊥ᵀH′(θ∥)θ⊥ 把正交振荡能量转化为沿 M 的前进、进而降低曲率——相当于对曲率（sharpness）做隐式梯度下降，这正是自稳定机制的定量形式。
- **五条结构化假设 + 在具体损失上的解析验证**：将机理要素提炼为"谷几何"（子空间对齐、相对曲率可比性、损失–曲率兼容性）与"衰减轮廓"（局部曲率衰减、指数尾）两类共五条假设，并在成对对称 logistic 回归（命题 1 给出全部显式常数：尾速率 β_tail = a_min、容差 δ_r = cosh(u_max·r)−1 ≈ ½(u_max·r)²、条件常数 ρ_tail = λ_min(Σ*)/λ_max(Σ)）以及正则单纯形对称数据上的多类交叉熵（附录 D）上逐条解析验证——相比之下 Damian et al. 的条件此前只有数值验证。
- **迭代点级别的收敛速率与三阶段阻尼结构（定理 1）**：不稳定相内曲率指数衰减 λ₁(θ∥_t) ≲ λ₁(θ∥_0)e^{−ct}、平行坐标至少线性前进、t_stab 有限；稳定相内平行坐标 Ω(log t) 增长、曲率 O(1/t)，且正交分量依次经历非扩张、速率 (1−ε*) 的线性收缩、指数 γ_r 的多项式衰减三个阶段；推论 B.1 进一步给出损失速率（不稳定相平均损失 O(1/T)、稳定相末次迭代损失 O((1+t−t_stab)^{−1})）。
- **发现大步长的"瞬态隐式偏置"（定理 2）**：当支撑向量的正交分量不张成活跃正交子空间 U 时，条件数比 ρ(θ∥_t) ≍ t^{−(α_{j*}−α₁)/α₁} 衰减，累计收缩预算 Σρ·λ₁ 有限（张成情形则发散），弱方向只被部分阻尼——尽管 GD 渐近方向收敛到最大间隔解，有限时间内正交残差可被不稳定相放大并持续存在，且对步长 η 的依赖可增、可减、也可非单调，由数据几何决定。

## Key Contributions

- **Replaces the minimizer manifold with a curvature-defined reference subspace, unifying two EoS theory lines.** Prior work splits: Damian et al. [26] give the self-stabilization mechanism with no rates and numerically-verified-only assumptions; Wu et al. [27, 28] derive rates for logistic-like losses via direct gradient recursions with no mechanism; MacDonald et al. [29] unify both for overparameterized least squares but fundamentally rely on a minimizer manifold — absent for exponential-tail losses on separable data, whose infimum is not attained at any finite parameter. This paper anchors the analysis on the one-dimensional subspace M = span{v} (the max-margin direction) and extends the unified geometric analysis to logistic and multiclass cross-entropy losses.
- **Makes self-stabilization quantitatively explicit via a coupled two-equation system.** A second-order Taylor expansion of the gradient around the parallel point θ∥_t·v splits GD into an orthogonal oscillation equation θ⊥_{t+1} ≈ (I − ηH(θ∥_t))θ⊥_t and a parallel drift equation whose coupling term −(η²/2)·θ⊥ᵀH′(θ∥_t)θ⊥_t (a third-order derivative of the loss) converts orthogonal oscillation energy into forward progress along M, which lowers the restricted sharpness — literally implicit gradient descent on curvature, the engine of self-stabilization.
- **Five structural assumptions, verified analytically (not just numerically) for concrete losses.** "Valley Geometry" (Subspace Alignment, Relative Curvature Comparability, Loss–Curvature Compatibility) plus "Decay Profile" (Local Curvature Decay, Exponential Tail). Proposition 1 verifies all of them for pair-symmetric logistic regression with fully explicit constants — tail rate β_tail = a_min (the support-vector margin), tubular tolerance δ_r = cosh(u_max r) − 1 ≈ ½(u_max r)² valid for r < arccosh(2)/u_max, and conditioning constant ρ_tail = λ_min(Σ*|_U)/λ_max(Σ|_U) under a spanning condition; Appendix D does the same for multiclass cross-entropy on a centered regular simplex, where the softmax covariance of class-logit perturbations plays the role of the scalar logistic curvature and inactive directions are provably frozen.
- **Iterate-level convergence rates with a three-phase damping structure (Theorem 1).** Unstable phase: tubular-neighborhood invariance ∥θ⊥_t∥ ≤ r, at-least-linear parallel drift, exponential sharpness decay λ₁(θ∥_t) ≲ λ₁(θ∥_0)e^{−ct}, and finite stabilization time t_stab. Stable phase: parallel growth θ∥_t − θ* = Ω(log t), sharpness λ₁ = O(1/t), and — under uniform conditioning ρ_tail — three regimes for the orthogonal component: nonexpansion on [t_stab, t_lin], linear contraction ∥θ⊥_t∥ ≤ (1−ε*)^{t−t_lin}∥θ⊥_{t_lin}∥ with ε* = ρ_tail(1−δ_r)/(1+δ_r), then polynomial decay O((t−t_poly)^{−γ_r}) once the corrected sharpness η(1+δ_r)λ₁ falls below 1. Corollary B.1 converts these into loss rates: O(1/T) averaged over the unstable phase and last-iterate loss L(θ_t) = O((1+t−t_stab)^{−1}).
- **Identifies a transient implicit bias of large step sizes in non-spanning geometries (Theorem 2).** If the support vectors' orthogonal components span U, ρ stays bounded away from zero and the three-phase structure controls the full orthogonal component; if not (j* > 1), ρ(θ∥_t) ≍ t^{−(α_{j*}−α₁)/α₁}, the cumulative contraction budget Σ_t ρ(θ∥_t)λ₁(θ∥_t) stays finite (it diverges in the spanning case), and weak orthogonal directions receive only a finite total contraction — so components amplified during the unstable phase persist at any finite horizon despite asymptotic directional convergence to the max-margin solution. The dependence of the residual on η can be increasing, decreasing, or non-monotone depending on data geometry and initialization.

## Methodology

The paper studies fixed-step gradient descent θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η∇L(θ_t) in the regime where the corrected sharpness η(1+δ_r)λ₁ exceeds 2, i.e., beyond the classical stability threshold where the descent lemma and linearized stability analysis provide no guarantee.

**Warmup (Section 3.1).** The 2D toy loss L(x, y) = e^{−x}y² admits an exact multiplicative recursion y_{t+1} = (1 − 2ηe^{−x_t})y_t: while 2ηe^{−x_t} > 2 the y-component oscillates and grows, but the same growth accelerates x (the first equation is GD on e^{−x} with effective step ηy²), which decays the curvature until the oscillation self-stabilizes. This isolates the three ingredients: oscillation in sharp directions, slow drift in flat directions, and emergent curvature-reducing coupling.

**General framework (Sections 3.2–4).** For a unit vector v along which curvature decays, set M = span{v}; for classification losses the gradient lives in the fixed data span N ⊇ M, and U = N ∩ M⊥ collects the active orthogonal directions. Every iterate splits as θ = θ∥v + θ⊥. A second-order Taylor expansion of the gradient around θ∥v gives the coupled system: θ⊥_{t+1} = (I − ηH(θ∥_t))θ⊥_t + O(∥θ⊥∥²) and θ∥_{t+1} = θ∥_t − ηℓ′(θ∥_t) − (η²/2)θ⊥ᵀH′(θ∥_t)θ⊥_t + O(∥θ⊥∥³), where ℓ is the loss restricted to M and H its Hessian restricted to U. Convergence is then proven under two assumption families: Valley Geometry (subspace alignment ∇L(θ∥v) ∈ M and Π_U∇²L(θ∥v)v = 0; curvature comparability in a tubular neighborhood of radius r up to factor 1 ± δ_r; forward parallel drift that decreases loss and curvature) and Decay Profile (curvature decreases at definite rate β_J on compacts — no plateaus; parallel gradient and sharpness both decay as e^{−β_tail θ∥}). An admissibility condition carves out a *moderately unstable* window for (η, r, θ0): η(1+δ_r)λ₁(θ∥_0) > 2 (genuinely unstable start) but the Taylor remainder must not overwhelm the curvature-reduction mechanism (χ = β − η(1+δ_r)c_{∥,r}r/ρ > 0), and Appendix B.2 proves this window is non-empty.

**Instantiations (Section 5, Appendices C–D).** Pair-symmetric logistic regression: N reflected pairs x±_i = ±a_i·v + u_i. Proposition 1 verifies all assumptions with the explicit constants quoted above; the spanning condition U = span{u_i : i ∈ I*} (support vectors' orthogonal components span U) decides uniform conditioning. Multiclass cross-entropy: class directions are the vertices of a centered regular simplex, each index i contributes a K-class orbit sharing one off-axis vector u_i; the parallel ray is generated by the Crammer–Singer hard-margin solution, inactive directions are frozen (Proposition 7), and the softmax covariance of logit perturbations replaces the scalar logistic curvature. Theorem 2 then splits geometries by the nested subspaces U^{(j)} = span{u_i : a_i ≤ α_j}: j* = 1 (spanning) gives the full three-phase decay; j* > 1 gives ρ ≍ t^{−(α_{j*}−α₁)/α₁} and componentwise rates under simultaneous diagonalizability.

**Experiments (Section 6, Appendix E).** Controlled synthetic d = 3 pair-symmetric logistic problems, not large-scale validation: (i) a spanning configuration (N = 24 pairs, dim U = 2, η = 15, T = 100) checking the predicted phase boundaries and decay exponents against the observed stability product η(1+δ_r)λ₁(θ∥_t); (ii) a non-spanning configuration (support vectors along e₂ only, weak layer along e₃, T = 150) sweeping η/η_c ∈ {1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3} with η_c := 2/λ_max(∇²L(θ0)|_U), tracking the support-vector component, weak component, conditioning ratio, and angle to the max-margin line; (iii) a distribution-shift test (T = 20,000, ten learning rates from 0.01 to 10) where the spur coordinate's sign is flipped at test time, reducing effective margin by 2|θ_spur|; and (iv) an asymmetric perturbation (ε_asym = 0.04) decomposing along the hard-margin SVM direction, showing the stability product still self-stabilizes while a *centered* residual contracts (illustrative only, not covered by the theorems).

## Results & Highlights

- **Unstable phase (Theorem 1.I), pair-symmetric logistic setting:** the iterates remain in the tubular neighborhood, the parallel coordinate advances at least linearly (θ∥_t − θ∥_0 ≳ t), and the restricted sharpness decays exponentially, λ₁(θ∥_t) ≲ λ₁(θ∥_0)e^{−ct} for a constant c > 0, so t_stab < ∞. For comparison, classical descent-lemma theory gives *no* convergence guarantee anywhere in this regime (it requires η < 2/L), and Damian et al.'s self-stabilization analysis provides this mechanism without any rate.
- **Stable phase (Theorem 1.II):** parallel growth θ∥_t − θ* = Ω(log t) and sharpness λ₁(θ∥_t) = O(1/t); the orthogonal component obeys the three-phase schedule — nonexpansion up to t_lin, then linear contraction ∥θ⊥_t∥ ≤ (1−ε*)^{t−t_lin}∥θ⊥_{t_lin}∥ with contraction margin ε* = ρ_tail(1−δ_r)/(1+δ_r), then polynomial decay ∥θ⊥_t∥ = O((t−t_poly)^{−γ_r}) once η(1+δ_r)λ₁ ≤ 1.
- **Loss rates (Corollary B.1):** averaged loss over the unstable phase is O(1/T) uniformly for 1 ≤ T ≤ t_stab + 1, and last-iterate loss after stabilization is L(θ_t) = O((1+t−t_stab)^{−1}). Remark 4 is explicit that Wu et al.'s margin-based analysis achieves the sharper logarithmic averaged rate log(1+ηT)/(ηT) under an additional loss–parallel-gradient comparability condition — the unified framework deliberately trades sharpness for mechanism and generality.
- **Explicit verification constants (Proposition 1):** for pair-symmetric logistic regression the exponential tail rate equals the support-vector margin, β_tail = a_min; the tubular tolerance is δ_r = cosh(u_max r) − 1 = ½(u_max r)² + O(r⁴) (valid for r < arccosh(2)/u_max); and under spanning, ρ_tail = λ_min(Σ*|_U)/λ_max(Σ|_U).
- **Spanning experiment (Figure 3; R³, N = 24 pairs, dim U = 2, η = 15, T = 100):** the theory's constants evaluate to δ_r = 0.037, ρ_tail = 0.200, ε* = 0.186, and the predicted polynomial exponent γ_r = 0.84 matches the observed decay; the observed maximum tubular radius is 0.13 < the prescribed r = 0.15; the linear-contraction bound is tight initially and loosens as ηλ₁ shrinks below the worst-case threshold — exactly the predicted crossover to the polynomial tail.
- **Non-spanning experiment (Figure 4; 5 support pairs α₁ = 0.1 along e₂, 5 weak pairs α₂ = 1.0 along e₃, T = 150, η/η_c ∈ {1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3}):** the support-vector component |c_sv| contracts through the three-phase mechanism to numerical precision (~10⁻¹³), while the weak component |c_wk| plateaus at a nonzero value; crucialially, larger η amplifies |c_wk| during the unstable phase and the tail dynamics cannot remove it, producing a larger finite-time angle to the max-margin line. Appendices E.2 shows the η-dependence is geometry-governed, not universal: with α₂ = 4 larger η *suppresses* the residual, with α₂ = 3 the dependence is non-monotone with an interior minimum.
- **Theorem 2 quantifies the split:** in the non-spanning case the conditioning ratio decays as ρ(θ∥_t) ≍ t^{−(α_{j*}−α₁)/α₁}, so the cumulative contraction budget Σ_t ρ(θ∥_t)λ₁(θ∥_t) is finite, versus divergent (driving ∥θ⊥_t∥ → 0) in the spanning case — partial damping of weak directions is the mechanism behind the transient implicit bias.
- **Downstream distribution-shift test (Figure 7; R³, T = 20,000 steps, η ∈ {0.01, …, 10}, M = 160 vs 240 signed features):** under a spur-sign-flip shift the effective test margin shrinks by 2|θ_spur|; the non-spanning geometry retains a systematically larger final spur residual than the spanning control, with the gap widening at large η where unstable amplification is strongest, confirming that top-eigenvalue stabilization alone does not uniformly contract all orthogonal components.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuinely fills a hole.** The combination "mechanism + iterate-level rates for exponential-tail losses" did not exist: Damian et al. give the self-stabilization mechanism with abstract conditions verified only numerically and no rates; Wu et al. give rates without a mechanism and need a bespoke analysis per loss family; MacDonald et al. need a minimizer manifold that classification losses lack. The curvature-defined subspace is the right abstraction, and the transient implicit bias in non-spanning geometries (persistent, η-dependent, sometimes amplified orthogonal residuals) is a genuinely new observation that loss-level analyses cannot see.
- **Rigor: high.** Full proofs in Appendices B–D; the assumptions are *verified analytically* for the named losses with explicit constants (β_tail = a_min etc.), which is rarer and more valuable than the usual "assume regularity" move; non-emptiness of the admissible (η, r, θ0) window is itself proven (Appendix B.2).
- **Scope caveats (paper-specific, not generic):** (i) the verified models are *symmetric* — pair-symmetric logistic (reflected pairs) and regular-simplex multiclass orbits; real data is not pair-symmetric, and the asymmetric extension (Appendix F, ε_asym = 0.04) is explicitly "illustrative and not covered by the theorem." (ii) The admissible step size is only *moderately* unstable — conditions (A1)–(A2) require 2/((1+δ_r)λ₁(θ∥_0)) < η < ρβ/((1+δ_r)c_{∥,r}r), so arbitrarily large η (the catapult regime) is out of scope, and the upper bound involves constants (c_{∥,r}, β_J) that are hard to compute outside toy models. (iii) The sharpest three-phase theorem needs uniform conditioning ρ_tail > 0, which the authors themselves show *fails* in exactly the non-spanning geometries where the new bias phenomenon lives — there the guarantee weakens to componentwise/partial damping (Theorem 2ii needs an extra simultaneous-diagonalizability assumption).
- **Sharpness concession:** the unstable-phase averaged-loss bound O(1/T) is an envelope estimate; Wu et al.'s specialized margin recursions achieve the logarithmic rate log(1+ηT)/(ηT) — the paper admits this in Remark 4. The linear-contraction rate is also a worst-case bound that visibly loosens in the spanning experiment.
- **Experimental evidence is thin by design:** all experiments are d = 3 synthetic constructions with 20–24 pairs (or 160–240 signed features in the shift test) and hand-layered margins; the authors state plainly that these are "controlled tests of the predicted dynamics rather than large-scale validation on deep networks," and that identifying the reference subspace in modern networks "remains an open problem." No real benchmark appears anywhere.
- **Reproducibility:** good in principle — seeds, step sizes, initialization, and data construction are fully specified, and the theory is self-contained; no code link, so the numerical figures would need reimplementation.
- **Red flags:** none serious. There is no overclaiming; the generalization/robustness implications of the transient bias are explicitly deferred to future work rather than asserted.

## Strengths

- True unification: one framework recovers the self-stabilization mechanism *and* delivers iterate-level (not just loss-level) rates for logistic and cross-entropy losses, replacing the unavailable minimizer manifold with a curvature-defined reference subspace.
- The coupling term −(η²/2)θ⊥ᵀH′θ⊥ gives self-stabilization a clean quantitative identity: implicit gradient descent on the sharpness, converting oscillation energy into forward progress.
- Assumptions are verified analytically for concrete losses with explicit constants — a meaningful upgrade over the numerically-verified conditions of Damian et al.
- The spanning vs. non-spanning dichotomy (Theorem 2) turns a geometric data property into a sharp prediction about which orthogonal components are controlled, and the three η-dependence regimes (amplifying, suppressing, non-monotone) are all exhibited experimentally.
- The spur-flip distribution-shift test gives the transient implicit bias a concrete operational meaning — a persistent spurious-feature component that costs 2|θ_spur| of test margin under covariate shift — connecting the theory to shortcut-learning concerns.
- Honest positioning: Table 1 maps the prior landscape precisely, and Remark 4 concedes where prior specialized analyses are sharper.

## Limitations

- Symmetric, linearly separable, linear models only: pair-symmetric logistic and regular-simplex multiclass cross-entropy; no neural networks, and the asymmetric-data extension is a numerical sketch (Appendix F) outside the theorems.
- All validation is low-dimensional synthetic (d = 3, ≤ 24 pairs); no real datasets and no deep-network experiments — the authors flag identifying the reference subspace in modern networks as an open problem.
- The admissible step-size window is bounded above, covering "moderately unstable" dynamics; constants defining the window (c_{∥,r}, β_J) are impractical to estimate, so the theory cannot yet certify a learning-rate schedule for real training.
- The three-phase decay theorem requires uniform conditioning ρ_tail > 0, which fails precisely in the non-spanning geometries where the transient implicit bias arises — the most interesting case gets the weakest guarantees (partial damping, componentwise rates only under extra diagonalizability).
- The averaged unstable-phase loss rate O(1/T) is intentionally non-sharp versus Wu et al.'s logarithmic rate; the linear-contraction rate is worst-case and empirically loose after early stable iterations.
- The connection between the transient implicit bias and generalization or robustness is left entirely to future work; only a single controlled shift test is offered.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does an analog of the curvature-defined reference subspace exist for deep networks, and can it be estimated online during training using only Hessian-vector products, so the three-phase schedule becomes a monitoring signal for real runs?
- How do stochastic gradients, momentum, and Adam interact with the third-order self-stabilization coupling — does mini-batch noise wash out the persistent weak components of the transient implicit bias, or does it re-inject them?
- Can the non-spanning amplification mechanism be detected on real datasets as learning-rate-dependent reliance on spurious features (shortcut learning), and does the predicted non-monotone η-dependence (the α₂ = 3 regime of Appendix E.2) survive finite-sample, non-symmetric data?
- On non-separable data or with label noise, the loss regains finite minimizers — does the curvature-subspace analysis then degenerate gracefully into the minimizer-manifold theory of MacDonald et al., or is there an intermediate regime with new behavior?
- At practical training horizons (e.g., token-budget-limited LLM pretraining), is the polynomial tail O(t^{−γ_r}) slow enough that the transient implicit bias measurably affects test-time margins and generalization?
