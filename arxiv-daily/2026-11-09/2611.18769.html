Key Finding

FlipQuant learns a per-tile E2M1/E0M3 format map for Blackwell tensor cores — closing 42.5% of NVFP4's perplexity gap at 1.9% GEMM overhead.

一句话：不改模型权重，为 Blackwell 张量核的每个 MMA 权重瓦片端到端学习 E2M1/E0M3 二进制格式选择图。

Key Metric

42.5%

of the NVFP4 → BF16 perplexity gap closed, averaged over 6 models × WikiText-2/C4 — 3.2× the best natively-executable baseline (FourOverSix, 13.3%), at 1.9% / 1.7% median GEMM overhead (SM120 / SM100).

Why This Matters

A hardware-feasible, near-free route to clawing back W4A4 accuracy on Blackwell — orthogonal to, and stackable with, GPTQ and Hadamard-rotation PTQ.

Critical Edge

Headline gains are perplexity-centric: downstream wins are uneven — sometimes within noise — and the SM120 kernel depends on undocumented opcode encodings a CUDA/PTX update could break.

01 · Methodology — how the map is learned

  • What runs W4A4. Every linear layer except the output head; embeddings, norms, and non-linear ops (Mamba-2 scan, Gated DeltaNet recurrence) stay BF16. FlipQuant changes only how each tile's 4-bit codes are interpreted.
  • Two frozen candidates. W(0) = NVFP4 (FourOverSix-scaled E2M1); W(1) = E0M3, Blackwell's undocumented uniform grid {0, ±1…±7}. Identical 4.5 bits/weight storage and tensor scale — the map is a per-tile decode tag, stored free in an unused scale sign bit (SM120).
  • Objective. Bucketed KL to a frozen BF16 teacher (top-1,000 tokens + merged tail). One forward/backward yields every tile's exact gradient ∂L/∂mu = Σ G̃ijΔij (identity STE through activation quantizers) — weights elements by downstream sensitivity, not weight-space MSE.
  • Learning the map. Latent θu per tile + hard threshold + BinaryConnect-style STE, Adam (η=0.02, 640 steps, OpenWebMath+CodeParrot); gradients recomputed after each update — flipping all 359,840 most-negative-gradient tiles at once raises held-out KL by 69.4×10⁻³.
  • Kernels. SM120: one branch per K-tile into pre-compiled format arms (1.47–1.57× faster than per-MMA predication). SM100: one descriptor register write per MMA; 256×64 tiles preserve arithmetic intensity (N=8 is 40× slower otherwise).

Spot numbers · WikiText-2 perplexity ↓

Llama-3.2-1B: FlipQuant 14.39 vs FourOverSix 15.32 · NVFP4 15.54 · BF16 13.16.  Qwen3.8-27B: FlipQuant 7.08 ≈ BF16's 7.05 (NVFP4: 7.57).

Downstream accuracy

7-task log-likelihood mean 70.20% (GPTQ 69.74, FourOverSix 69.60); GSM8K 73.2% — highest of all quantized methods — but per-model swings range −0.3 to +5.5 pts: real, less uniform than the perplexity story.

Inference & calibration cost

SM120 prefill within +0.1–1.1% of FourOverSix on every model (Llama-3.1-8B: 61.7 vs 61.6 ms; both ~2.1× faster than BF16's 131.2 ms); GB200 256×64 adds only +0.2–0.4%. Calibration: 3.7–170.4 min on one RTX PRO 6000 (≤91 GiB).

Trust checks

Native matches simulated in 29/30 (SM120) and 11/12 (SM100) cells, PPL diff ≤0.10%. Learned maps flip only 2.3–14.8% of tiles; random maps flipping the same count gain nothing. Different seeds → different maps (Jaccard 0.34–0.38) but equal ΔNLL; web-text calibration keeps 82–103% of the gain.

03 · Strengths

  • Identifies and cleanly measures the exact hardware constraint — the MMA operand tile is the only native unit of format adaptivity — that invalidates prior per-block schemes on real GPUs (>99.999% of tiles are mixed-preference).
  • End-to-end, sensitivity-weighted tile gradients replace local reconstruction error — and every weaker alternative (random, activation-weighted, one-shot gradient, weight-MSE) is ablated, not just asserted.
  • Real kernels with measured, small overheads on both Blackwell variants, plus native-vs-simulated correctness verification.
  • Broad, honest evaluation: 6 models (incl. 2 hybrid architectures), 2 corpora, 7 log-likelihood tasks + GSM8K, paired statistics, thorough robustness appendix.
  • Composable with GPTQ and Hadamard rotations — orthogonal to weight-improvement PTQ, not competing with it.

04 · Limitations

  • NVIDIA Blackwell only; the SM120 path patches undocumented E0M3 opcode encodings — exposed to CUDA/PTX toolchain breakage, not portable to Hopper or other vendors.
  • Downstream gains are uneven and sometimes within noise per model/task; the clearest wins are in perplexity — don't deploy on the 42.5% headline alone.
  • Only W4A4 with E2M1 activations studied; activation-format flipping, FP8/INT8 mixes, and W4A8 comparisons are unaddressed; output head stays BF16.
  • SM100 users are effectively locked to 256×64 tiles (narrower tiles up to 40× slower), giving up ~a quarter of the fine-tile perplexity gain.
  • Calibration needs up to 170 min and 91 GiB for the 27B hybrid; 4× more steps buys ≤2.6×10⁻³ nats/token — the recipe is near its ceiling.
  • Why only 2.3–14.8% of tiles prefer E0M3 is left mostly unexplored beyond aggregate statistics.

05 · Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would learning activation tile formats with the same machinery stack with weight-format maps, or does E2M1 become the binding constraint?
  • Can the flip map be predicted from weight statistics at deployment time, skipping per-model calibration entirely?
  • Why do only 2.3–14.8% of tiles prefer E0M3 — is there a structural signature (layer depth, outlier channels, weight distribution)?
  • How does FlipQuant interact with QAT or finer activation formats — would W4A8 + FlipQuant dominate W4A4 at equal memory?
  • Does the bucketed-KL objective transfer to non-language modalities (ViTs, MoE experts)?
  • Is there a middle ground between one-shot selection and 640-step calibration — coordinate descent, second-order corrections?
