> **一句话总结：** 本文将 Barron et al. (2026) 对 softmax/软交叉熵的数值稳定性分析推广到分类分布间的散度计算：在两个分布几乎相等时，前向 KL、反向 KL、广义 JSD、蒙特卡洛 KL 估计器和熵的标准实现会因舍入与抵消而丢失损失值和梯度；作者提出 8 种基于 expm1/log1p、非负余项 E2(x)=e^x−1−x 和 logit 差重构的数值稳定改写，并用 Hypothesis 属性测试（对照 mpmath 80 位参考值）验证了 JAX 实现。

> **TL;DR:** This paper shows that when a categorical distribution q=softmax(z) is nearly equal to a reference p=softmax(z_ref) — exactly the regime at convergence of knowledge distillation (e.g., on-policy distillation objectives), KL-regularized RL (GRPO/TRPO-style penalties), and entropy bonuses — standard FP32 implementations of forward KL, reverse KL, generalized JSD, entropy, and Monte Carlo KL estimators (K1/K2/K3) catastrophically lose both the loss and its gradient: in the paper's property-based test setup (vs. 80-digit mpmath references, K∈{2,...,16}), basic implementations show relative errors up to ~10^6 while the proposed reformulations stay at ~10^-9–10^-12 down to perturbations of 10^-12; concrete failures include the entropy gradient at z=(0,10^-8) rounding to exactly zero (vs. an accurate entropy ≈ log 2) and the K3 estimator at log-ratio h=10^-8 returning identically zero value and derivatives, both fully recovered by the fixes. The trade-off, honestly documented: the reformulations are themselves unstable away from equality (overflow near e^100 at logit separation h=50 in FP32) and one rounding failure in the logit subtraction (h=2^-25) remains an open problem — no large-scale training experiments are included.

## 核心贡献

- **识别并系统归纳了 5 类数值问题**（Table 1）：当分类分布几乎相等时，(1) log-softmax 减法——分别计算的 log 概率被舍入到相同值，抹掉小 log-ratio；(2) KL 求和抵消——混合符号的求和项相互抵消，只剩二阶小量；(3) softmax 减法——q−p 的概率差被分别舍入的 softmax 输出抹掉；(4) K3 控制变量估计器中 expm1(−s)+s 的余项抵消与导数里 e^{−s} 舍入到 1；(5) JSD 中混合分布 log-ratio m/q 舍入到 1。这些问题同时影响前向值和梯度。
- **提出 8 种数值稳定改写**：统一使用三条经典技术——从 centered logit 差 δ 重构 log-ratio（继承 Barron et al.）、用非负余项恒等式 KL = Σ p·E2(s)（E2(x)=e^x−1−x，近零处用 Taylor 级数）替代带符号求和、以及零和修正项（u − q·Σu）消除归一化残差；反向 KL 梯度则用 Proposition 1（centered log-ratio ≡ centered logit 差）直接对 δ 居中。
- **覆盖蒙特卡洛 KL 估计器与熵**：K1/K2/K3（Schulman 2020 记号）的采样 log-ratio 通过重新组合 (z_a−z_ref,a)−(logZ−logZ_ref) 避免形成中间 log 概率——每个采样类别只需交换 2 个标量，顺带降低了分布式训练的通信量；熵梯度作为"到均匀分布的反向 KL"的特例修复。
- **可执行实现 + 属性测试验证**：为每个设置提供 JAX 示例代码（custom_gradient 显式前向/反向），用 Hypothesis 对照 mpmath 80 位十进制参考值做属性测试（覆盖相等、类别置换、可表示的公共 logit 平移、K∈{2,3,4,8,16} 的前向值与梯度精度）。
- **诚实的边界分析（Section 4.1.1）**：这些改写仅在近相等区域稳定——远离相等时指数中间量会溢出（需 guard + 混合回退），且 FP32 的 logit 减法本身仍可抹掉信号，作者明确将其列为开放问题。

## Key Contributions

- **A catalog of five numerical issues** (Table 1) in standard implementations of divergences between nearly equal categorical distributions, covering both forward values and gradients: (1) log-softmax subtraction — separately rounding log-probabilities erases the small log-ratio s; (2) KL-sum cancellation — mixed-sign summands cancel down to a much smaller quadratic remainder; (3) softmax subtraction — the q−p probability difference needed by forward-KL gradients is rounded away; (4) remainder cancellation plus a rounded exponential in the K3 control-variate estimator; (5) mixture log-ratio rounding in generalized JSD.
- **Eight numerically stable reformulations** built on three classical techniques: reconstructing the log-ratio from centered logit differences (extending Barron et al. 2026's fix), replacing signed KL sums with the nonnegative remainder identity KL(p‖q)=Σ pᵢ E₂(sᵢ) where E₂(x)=eˣ−1−x is evaluated by Taylor series near zero (Mächler's expm1x strategy), and a zero-sum correction that removes normalization residuals from gradients.
- **Proposition 1**, which shows centered log-ratios equal centered logit differences (s − q·s = δ − q·δ), letting the reverse-KL gradient avoid log-softmax subtraction entirely; the same rule fixes entropy gradients as reverse KL to the uniform distribution.
- **Stable Monte Carlo KL estimators**: regrouping the sampled log-ratio as (z_a − z_ref,a) − (logZ − logZ_ref) avoids forming intermediate log-probabilities and requires only two scalars per sampled category per model — a communication saving for distributed setups; K3 is repaired as k₃ = E₂(−s) with expm1-based derivatives.
- **Executable JAX implementations for every setting, validated by property-based tests** (Hypothesis) against 80-digit mpmath references, covering exact equality, category permutations, common logit shifts, and forward/gradient accuracy for K ∈ {2, 3, 4, 8, 16}.
- **An explicit pitfalls section (4.1.1)**: the reformulations can overflow away from equality and one FP32 rounding failure in the logit subtraction remains unsolved — stated as an open problem rather than hidden.

## Methodology

The paper is a numerical-analysis-style study of scalar arithmetic, not an empirical training study. Setup: logits z, z_ref ∈ R^K define q = softmax(z) (optimized) and p = softmax(z_ref) (fixed); the authors analyze the forward computation of each divergence and its gradient with respect to z (θ-gradients follow by the chain rule).

- **Diagnosis.** For each divergence, they decompose the standard evaluation into its elementary operations and identify where rounding or cancellation destroys small quantities. Example: near equality each KL summand satisfies −pᵢsᵢ ≈ (pᵢ−qᵢ) + (qᵢ−pᵢ)²/(2pᵢ); the linear parts cancel across categories (both distributions sum to one), leaving a quadratic remainder that roundoff easily overwhelms.
- **Repair template.** (a) Reconstruct the log-ratio from logit differences via s = d − log1p(Σ pᵢ expm1(dᵢ)) with d = δ − Σ pᵢδᵢ, using expm1/log1p to retain small increments; (b) convert signed KL sums to sums of nonnegative remainders E₂(x) = eˣ−1−x, evaluated by a degree-12 Taylor polynomial for |x| < 1/2; (c) attach explicit custom gradients with zero-sum corrections (gradient = u − q·Σuᵢ); (d) for reverse KL and JSD, center logit differences or use the stable mixture log-ratio h = −log1p(β·expm1(−s)).
- **Validation.** Basic vs. fixed FP32 evaluations on CPU are compared against mpmath references with 80 decimal digits, over perturbation sweeps h from 10⁻² down to 10⁻¹², plus property-based tests with Hypothesis (following Maaz 2026, whose tests found a NaN-gradient bug in v1 of Barron et al.).
- **Application mapping.** Section 2 ties each setting to real objectives: on-policy distillation (Agarwal et al. 2024), RL with reverse-KL penalties and entropy bonuses (Todorov; Geist et al.; Mnih et al.), proximal/mirror descent and TRPO-style trust regions (Teboulle; Schulman et al. 2015), and MC KL estimators as used in Kimi k1.5 (K2), DeepSeekMath's GRPO (K3), and Thinking Machines' StableReinforce (forward-KL K1/K3).

## Results & Highlights

All numbers are FP32 relative errors versus an 80-decimal-digit mpmath reference on the paper's test distributions (e.g., z_ref = (−1, 0, 1), z = (−1, h, 1)); the "baseline" is the standard direct implementation (naive KL sum / ordinary autodiff), the setting the paper aims to fix. The paper reports no standard benchmark-dataset training results — its evidence is these controlled numerical experiments:

- **Loss relative error reduced from ~10⁶ to ~10⁻⁹–10⁻¹²**: across Figures 1, 4, and 9 (forward KL, reverse KL, and generalized JSD with β = 1/2), the basic implementation's loss error climbs to 10⁶–10¹⁸ as the perturbation h shrinks to 10⁻¹², while the fixed reformulations hold gradient and loss errors near 10⁻⁹–10⁻¹² — a delta of roughly 15–27 orders of magnitude at the smallest perturbations.
- **Entropy gradient at z = (0, 10⁻⁸) in FP32**: the direct evaluation returns an accurate entropy (≈ log 2) but rounds the nonzero gradient to exactly zero; the fixed reverse-KL-derived rule retains it down to perturbations of 10⁻¹² (Figure 5) — i.e., a 100% gradient error in the baseline, ~machine-precision error after the fix.
- **K3 estimator at log-ratio h = 10⁻⁸** (with z_a = −h, z_ref,a = −2h, log Z = log Z_ref = 0): the direct value k₃ = expm1(−s)+s and both of its input derivatives evaluate to exactly zero (100% relative error) despite an accurate input log-ratio; the E₂(−s) reformulation preserves the small nonzero penalty and both derivatives (Figure 8).
- **K1 sampled log-ratio at h = 10⁻⁸** (z_a = h, z_ref,a = 0, log Z = log Z_ref = 1): direct log-probability subtraction returns 0 while the regrouped difference retains h — the regrouping fixes the arithmetic, though the paper shows it cannot recover differences already rounded away in the supplied log-normalizers (both estimators then scale linearly in h instead of the true quadratic KL).
- **Honest negative results for the fixes themselves**: (a) overflow — with z_ref = (−h, 0), z = (h, 0) at h = 50, the reconstruction forms an intermediate exponential near e¹⁰⁰ and overflows FP32 although the true log-ratio and KL are finite (Figure 2); (b) residual rounding — with z_ref = (0, h), z = (1, 1) at h = 2⁻²⁵, FP32 logit subtraction gives δ = (1, 1), so both the fixed forward KL and its gradient vanish although p ≠ q, and this failure passes the assert_close guard (Figure 3); the authors state that a simple FP32 reformulation without these trade-offs "remains an open problem."
- **Property-based test coverage**: all FP32 reformulations pass Hypothesis tests for K ∈ {2, 3, 4, 8, 16} covering equality, category permutations, representable common logit shifts, and forward/gradient accuracy.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate.** The individual numerical techniques are classical — expm1/log1p usage, Taylor evaluation of E₂(x) = eˣ−1−x dates back at least to Welinder (2005) and Mächler's expm1x, the nonnegative-remainder trick for small JSD appears in Raj & Wiggins (2008), and the logit-difference reconstruction and zero-sum correction are taken from Barron et al. (2026). The contribution is the systematic extension to KL/JSD/MC-KL/entropy with a clean issue taxonomy (Table 1), Proposition 1, and tested reference implementations — a solid follow-up note rather than a conceptual breakthrough.
- **Rigor: high on arithmetic, verified by construction.** Every claim is checked against 80-digit references under property-based testing, including edge cases the authors did not have to expose (their own reformulations' overflow at h = 50 and residual rounding at h = 2⁻²⁵ are demonstrated, not just admitted) — this level of self-skepticism is exemplary.
- **Practical significance: asserted, not measured.** The paper explicitly says "The examples isolate the arithmetic rather than measure large-scale training outcomes." There is no distillation run, no RLHF run, no measurement of parameter drift like Barron et al.'s BF16 soft-target experiment; the practical-impact argument is by analogy (Section 3) plus the observation that real systems (Kimi k1.5, DeepSeekMath GRPO, StableReinforce) use these exact estimators. A reader wanting "does this change my loss curve?" will not find out.
- **Missing ablations.** No runtime/throughput cost for the reformulations (extra expm1/log1p calls, a custom-gradient path that bypasses XLA fusion), no study of the guard limit (default span ≤ 1/2) or the switch threshold versus Barron et al.'s |log-ratio| < 1, no BF16/FP16/FP8 measurements of their own — the low-precision discussion is qualitative and borrowed, and compensated summation (Ogita et al.) is mentioned as a possible improvement but never evaluated.
- **Data quirks.** All demonstrations use toy categorical distributions of K = 2–3 (sweeps to K = 16 in tests) with hand-chosen logits like z_ref = (−1, 0, 1); there is no experiment at a realistic vocabulary size (e.g., K = 32k logits) where normalization error and summation order effects could behave differently, despite large-K being the paper's own motivation for Monte Carlo estimators.
- **Red flags: none serious.** Claims are carefully scoped ("can affect," not "does degrade"), and the entropy case even includes a note that its failure regime (near-uniform policies) may rarely matter in practice. The main risk is over-generalization by readers: near-equality failures are most relevant late in training or at high precision, and Adam's ε may or may not mask them.

## Strengths

- Clear, reusable taxonomy of five failure modes across eight settings, with forward and gradient issues cleanly separated (Table 1 is genuinely useful as a checklist for implementers).
- Every fix ships as a short, complete, executable JAX listing using only standard stable primitives (expm1, log1p, log_softmax, custom_gradient) — easy to port into production kernels.
- Verification methodology is best-in-class for this genre: property-based testing against 80-digit references, including permutation and shift invariances, following the practice that caught a real bug (NaN gradients) in v1 of the prior work it builds on.
- Unusual honesty: a dedicated pitfalls section demonstrating two ways the proposed fixes themselves fail, plus an explicitly stated open problem.
- Concrete relevance map to deployed systems (Kimi k1.5's K2 surrogate, DeepSeekMath GRPO's K3, StableReinforce's forward-KL K1/K3) and to the distributed-training setting, where the regrouped sampled log-ratio needs only two scalars per sampled category per model.

## Limitations

- No end-to-end training experiments: the practical impact on distillation, RLHF, or proximal optimization is argued by construction and citation (Barron et al.'s BF16 drift), never demonstrated, and the interaction with Adam's ε is left unquantified.
- The reformulations are valid only near equality: away from it they can overflow (e^100-scale intermediates at logit separation 50), so production use requires closeness guards plus a hybrid fallback — which the paper outlines but does not build or benchmark.
- One rounding failure in the underlying logit subtraction (the h = 2⁻²⁵ example) defeats all the KL/JSD reformulations and passes the closeness guard; the authors leave a clean FP32 solution as an open problem (alternatives like max/mean centering or anchoring "caused regressions or left rounding failures unresolved").
- Scope limited to categorical distributions and exact-sum or single-sample-decomposed estimators; continuous distributions, general f-divergences, Bregman divergences, and Sinkhorn/entropy-regularized OT are deferred to future work, and Monte Carlo sampling variance vs. numerical error is only conceptually separated.
- Precision coverage is FP32-only; the BF16/FP8/FP4 discussion is qualitative, extrapolated from Barron et al. rather than measured.

## Relevance

对任何做 LLM 蒸馏（student 逼近 teacher）、KL 正则化 RL（GRPO/PPO/TRPO 式 reference policy 约束）或熵正则训练的实现者，本文给出了在"分布几乎相等"这一收敛末期必然出现的 regime 下损失与梯度被浮点舍入清零的机理和可直接落地的 JAX 修复代码；若训练使用 BF16/FP8 等低精度格式，此类问题会更为严重，与大规模模型训练的数值稳定性直接相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do these arithmetic errors measurably change end-to-end outcomes — final reward, KL-to-reference, or student-teacher gap — in a real on-policy distillation or GRPO run, and does Adam's ε mask or amplify the residual updates as Barron et al. observed for soft cross-entropy?
- How do the failures and fixes transfer to BF16/FP16/FP8/FP4 training, where rounding dominates at larger distribution separations and the "nearly equal" regime widens?
- Can a principled hybrid guard (switching between the reformulation and direct evaluation, as Barron et al. do at |log-ratio| < 1) be derived for forward/reverse KL and JSD with a proven error bound, rather than the current span ≤ 1/2 heuristic?
- Is there an FP32 reformulation that survives the logit-subtraction rounding failure (the z_ref = (0, 2⁻²⁵) example), e.g., higher-precision accumulation of δ, compensated subtraction, or storing unnormalized logits — and what is its cost?
- How do the errors scale at realistic vocabulary sizes (K ≈ 10⁴–10⁵), where log-sum-exp normalization error, summation order, and kernel-level reductions may interact with the cancellation analysis?
- Do the same remainder/centering identities extend to continuous distributions, f-divergences, Bregman divergences, and Sinkhorn divergences with quantitative guarantees, as the conclusion sketches?
