Continual compilation: a hot-reloading persistent engine runs 252× more agent experiments — and beats hand-tuned megakernels by up to 2.6×.

Together Compiler: Continual Compilation for AI Inference ↗

Production-deployed (trillions of tokens/day) · beats the authors' own prior SOTA baselines (ThunderKittens / Megakernels) · arXiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed
Key Metric · 关键指标
0×

more agent experiments than engine-restart systems (11× less idle time) — a candidate forward pass hot-reloads in 17 s, no engine restart.

Why This Matters · 为什么重要

Agent-driven continual compilation already squeezes latency and throughput from frontier MoE models in production — directly relevant to anyone building inference-serving or agentic optimization systems.

Critical Edge · 批判视角

Outsiders can reproduce the benchmarks but not the system: TKCC stays closed, and headline speedups don't price in ~24 h × 2-replica agent compute per model.

Also: the 252× is measured vs engine-restart regimes, not faster offline kernel harnesses — the framing slightly flatters the number.

Methodology · 方法

  • Inputs — server launch command, traffic profile, hardware config. The loop: cost model seeds parallelism → engine loads model once → parallel agents submit candidate forward passes → tiers measure → best candidate verified & deployed.
  • Hot reload — each rank execs the agent's tkcc_forward.py, patches model.forward, recaptures CUDA graphs; kernel binaries cached across submissions.
  • Two tiers — fast tier: 12 layers, 17 s reload, CUPTI kernel traces, 3.9 crashes/1k screens candidates; slow tier: 60 layers, 0 crashes, confirms. Splitting prefill from decode cuts variance 6.6% → 2.7%.
  • Cost model — analytic pass-time estimate (FLOPs, bytes, collectives) at three fidelity levels, pruned by memory + SLA; found a Kimi K3 config worth +24% TPS / +18% TPM.
  • Agent setup — Claude Code / Opus 5.5 with the engine's raw source: no knowledge base, no custom IR, no prescribed strategies; edits kernels/forward-pass only.
  • Verification — KV-cache contamination checks + quality suites before production; bit-exactness incl. RNG state on scientific models.

Results · 结果

  • vs hand-tuned megakernels (Llama-3.2-1B, batch 1): up to 1.7× TPS — peaking at 2,118 TPS on B200 where persistent-kernel baselines drop below 300 TPS at OSL 65,536.
  • InferenceX / AgentX: up to 2.6× over the best open entries across 5 platforms — each compiled in <24 h, ≤2 nodes.

Notable discovered kernels
  • Lamport-style fused all-reduce ~2× faster than NCCL at decode (MiniMax M3.1 Flash).
  • Removing FlashMLA's 16→64 head padding — largest decode-attention gain on DeepSeek v4.1; PDL cuts decode fixed cost 9.7%.
  • NVFP4 MoE down-projection 23.7 µs vs vendor's 34.1 µs; DeepGEMM tiles 273 → 210 µs.
  • Selectivity: a MoE down-proj gains 2.8–5% at concurrency 22–78 but is 6.6% slower at 8 — TKCC applies it per active batch size.

Strengths · 优势

  1. Names the right problem — inference is now polymorphic (traffic, architectures, hardware all drift), so optimization must be continual, not one-shot; and demonstrates a working production loop.
  2. Throughput–fidelity tradeoff diagnosed with evidence, not hand-waving: 761 s engine cold starts; random benchmark data activates 13–38% more experts than real traffic.
  3. The Tiered Persistent Engine is load-bearing — decoupling engine lifetime from forward-pass evaluation is what makes second-scale agent iteration possible at all.
  4. Refreshingly honest ablations that contradict the authors' own earlier designs (custom IRs, knowledge hubs, expert strategies) — rare, useful data for agentic-optimization builders.
  5. Breadth — 5 hardware platforms, 3 engine codebases, 8 non-LLM scientific models, all bit-exactness verified.

Limitations · 局限

  1. No public release — results depend on Together's internal traffic profiles, cluster, and the Claude Code/Opus 5.5 harness; exact reproduction is effectively impossible for outsiders.
  2. Optimization compute excluded from headline speedups (2× replica, 24 h, many parallel agents); for low-traffic deployments the amortization math may not favor continual recompilation.
  3. Measurement stays the binding constraint — agents spend 52.7–57.0% of time waiting on gates, queues, or re-measuring; authors call throughput "the primary outstanding bottleneck."
  4. Fast tier can mis-rank data-dependent kernels (12 layers; their own anecdote: 20 µs on random data vs 62 ms at 70K context on real data).
  5. Bitter-lesson ablations tied to one harness version — untested across agents; closed-model safety refusals and 9 GPU-reset incidents (2 machines lost) show operational fragility.

Questions for Further Exploration · 待探索

  • Does the 252× experiment-throughput gain survive on a single node — what is the break-even traffic volume for continual compilation?
  • Is the knowledge-base negative result an artifact of Opus 5.5's pretraining already containing kernel-optimization knowledge?
  • What would a verification IR that doesn't restrict the optimization space look like, and could it subsume today's post-hoc checks?
  • Would predictive pre-screening from kernel traces (or more tiers) beat the measurement bottleneck, or does variance ultimately bound useful throughput?
  • Do concurrency-dependent optimizations (helping at 22–78, hurting at 8) remain correct under adversarial traffic mixtures?
