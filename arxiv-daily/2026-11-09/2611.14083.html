01Methodology

What is proved: given a weight commitment, decoding policy, prompt and client nonce, the committed integer model FO produced exactly the claimed tokens — the sampling seed, greedy/inverse-CDF decisions and the stopping rule are all inside the statement, so truncated answers cannot verify.

  • FO is float-free: 24-bit residual stream, 8-bit matrix products with power-of-two scales kept off the reduction axis, correctly-rounded lookup tables for exp / SiLU / rsqrt / recip.
  • 31-bit KoalaBear prime p = 2³¹ − 2²⁴ + 1: every gadget carries explicit integer bounds so no value wraps mod p — Fp equality implies integer equality, closing the classic small-field loophole.
  • Chunked LogUp: a 16K proof makes ~7×10¹¹ lookups (>300p), so lookups are split into ≤2³⁰ chunks proved together by one GKR over the table.
  • Streamed two-pass prover (commit forward, prove backward) never holds more than one layer's witness; the Fiat–Shamir transcript stays GPU-resident; identical layers replay as CUDA graphs.
  • One multilinear commitment — rate-1/2 Reed–Solomon + BLAKE3 Merkle trees + BaseFold openings; round-by-round soundness 2⁻¹⁰³.

03Strengths

  • First bit-exact proof of full production-size LLM inference (Qwen2.5-32B / Qwen3-32B / Qwen3-30B-A3B) on a single GPU — every rounding, softmax, sampling and stopping decision inside the proved statement.
  • Principled small-field design: wrap-freedom proven per gadget, not assumed; chunked LogUp stays sound 300× beyond the field characteristic.
  • Serious engineering: Table 10 traces the 92.5 s result through ~20 individually measured optimizations — including negative results that were reverted.
  • Exemplary honesty: quantifies its own accuracy loss against deployed 8-bit baselines, attributes it step by step, discloses unexplained spreads, releases all scripts (MIT).
  • Recursion-ready verifier: 4.2× less hashing, closed-form public columns, working BLAKE3 circuit at 2.8 µs per compression.

04Limitations

  • Not zero-knowledge: ~1.9×10⁴ proofs suffice to recover the weights by linear algebra — closed-weight serving is out of scope.
  • Auditor-scale artifacts: 106–477 MiB proofs and 19–56 s single-core verification; recursion only begun (BLAKE3 circuit trace commitment unmeasured).
  • Accuracy gap: +8.3% perplexity vs BF16 with 86.3% greedy agreement; ~63% of the gap is unexplained, and working remedies are unprovable or cost 1.3–1.68× more proving time.
  • Double overhead: 41–50× proving overhead (~90× vs FP8 SGLang), and FO's own engine is 2.7× slower than FP8 at batch-32 prefill.
  • MoE cost: 6–11× worse per active parameter than concurrent FlashProve (which, unlike Sable, is Lean-verified but skips sampling checks).
  • Scope: random-oracle model, no machine-checked protocol proof; Qwen-family decoders only — other architectures asserted, not demonstrated.

05Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can provable formats close the remaining ~63% of the perplexity gap at bounded proving-cost inflation — and fix the GSM8K style-flip penalty?
  2. How much would sliding-window attention shrink the 16K proof, given every causal score entry costs a committed probability, a lookup and two constraint terms?
  3. What does the full recursive stack cost end-to-end once the BLAKE3 circuit's trace commitment is measured — and do Fp-friendly hashes beat BLAKE3-everywhere?
  4. Can a hiding weight commitment be layered on without destroying the trust-free "anyone can recompute CW" registry property?
  5. Does the wrap-free-bounds methodology transfer to other small-field proofs of numeric programs — scientific kernels, fixed-point RL rollouts?
  6. Against statistical fingerprinting (TOPLOC-style) on the same budget, where exactly does the cryptographic guarantee pay for its 50× overhead?
