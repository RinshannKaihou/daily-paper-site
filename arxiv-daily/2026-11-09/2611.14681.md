> **一句话总结：** Together AI 提出 Together Compiler（TKCC），首个面向推理优化的"持续编译"智能体循环：用可热重载的双层持久引擎（fast/slow tier）把单次实验的引擎启动开销解耦出来，使智能体实验吞吐提升 252 倍，再用解析+分层测量的代价模型挑选并行策略作为优化起点，并发现删掉知识库/专家策略/自定义 IR 反而更快（"苦涩教训"）；在 Llama-3.2-1B batch-size-1 上比 ThunderKittens/Mirage 手写 Megakernel 最高快 1.7×，在 SemiAnalysis InferenceX（AgentX）上比最强开源引擎基线最高快 2.6× TPS（24 小时、每模型 ≤2 节点），并刷新低并发 SOTA（B200 MiniMax M3.1 Flash 1,111 TPS、GB300 DeepSeek v4.1 Flash 1,205 TPS）。

> **TL;DR:** A production system from Together AI that replaces one-shot kernel benchmarking with a *continual* agentic loop: a Tiered Persistent Engine hot-reloads agent-written forward passes into a live engine (252x more agent experiments than engine-restart systems like Apple's RSI-Inference Bench; 11x less idle time), and an analytical+tiered-measurement cost model picks parallelism configs (found a Kimi K3 config worth up to +24% TPS / +18% TPM). On Llama-3.2-1B batch-size-1 it beats the hand-tuned ThunderKittens/Mirage megakernel baselines by up to 1.7x TPS (e.g., 2,118 vs ~1,649 TPS at OSL 256 on B200); on SemiAnalysis' InferenceX AgentX benchmark it beats the best open-source engine entries (as of Oct 27, 2026) by up to 2.6x TPS across Kimi K3, GLM 5.3, DeepSeek v4.1 Flash, and MiniMax M3.1 Flash on B200/B300/GB300/MI355X/Trainium2, using under 24 hours and at most 2 nodes per model — including batch-size-1 SOTA of 1,111 TPS (MiniMax M3.1 Flash, B200; 1.8x the best published 633) and 1,205 TPS (DeepSeek v4.1 Flash, GB300; vs 813). It also generalizes beyond LLMs with bit-exact 1.5–6.4x speedups on weather/biology/time-series/PDE models, where a plain Claude Code baseline reaches only 1.2–4.4x and outright fails on AIFS-ENS 2.0. Notably, ablations show expert-curated knowledge bases *hurt* (25–31% lower speedup after 12h) — the bitter lesson holds for performance optimization.

## 核心贡献

- **持续编译的问题定义与动机**：推理流量形状持续漂移（行为趋势、突发性、渐进迁移——如某实验室模型的流量 ISL 在两周内从 10K 涨到 18K、缓存命中率跳升 8 个百分点），静态优化一次性编译不再适用；现有智能体优化系统陷入"吞吐-保真度"两难——离线 kernel 基准（随机数据）保真度低（随机数据比真实流量多激活 13–38% 的专家；Cursor 的 MoE megakernel 在真实流量下比均衡负载慢 6–15%），端到端引擎优化又被每次 10 分钟以上的引擎启动拖垮（TensorRT-LLM 冷启动 761 秒，其中模型加载 265 秒、autotune 192 秒、CUDA graph 捕获 226 秒）。
- **双层持久引擎（Tiered Persistent Engine）**：引擎只启动一次，通过替换 `model.forward` 函数指针 + 重捕获 CUDA graph 实现"热重载"，把引擎启动与前向计算解耦；fast tier（12 层、17 秒热重载、0.05% 测量方差）高频筛选候选，slow tier（60 层、0 崩溃）提供高保真信号。相比每次重启引擎的系统，智能体实验吞吐提升 252×、空闲时间降低 11×；双 tier 比单 slow tier 多评估 8.1× 候选（1,354 vs 168）并找到快 7.2% 的解。
- **并行策略代价模型**：解析模型（Tpass = Σ max(计算时间, 字节/带宽) + 集合通信时间）+ 三层性能估计（理论硬件规格 / 实测 microbenchmark / 实测端到端）区分"硬件极限"与"软件不成熟"，配合生产流量分布（MTP 接受率、专家路由不均衡）排序 top-k 并行配置，避免昂贵的网格搜索；为 Kimi K3 生产部署找到 TPS +24%、TPM +18% 的配置。
- **性能优化的"苦涩教训"**：两年三代系统迭代（Megakernels → Torch Compile/Inductor 增强 → 专家管理上下文）后，发现给智能体原始引擎源码 + 简单提示（"让前向尽可能快"）胜过一切显式抽象：知识库使 12 小时后加速降低 25–31%；朴素集成（6.2×）胜过专家分工策略（4.9×）；研究日志数据结构吃掉高达 43% 的工具调用；高频智能体通信使优化快 4.7×。
- **跨领域泛化与生产部署**：在天气（AIFS-ENS 2.0、Aurora 1.5）、生物（Boltz-2、Evo 2）、时序（Chronos-2、Panda-72M）、PDE（Walrus、Poseidon-T）模型上取得 1.5–6.4× 逐位精确（bit-exact）加速；TKCC 每天服务数万亿 token 的生产集群。

## Methodology

TKCC takes a server launch command, traffic profile, and hardware configuration as input, and runs a loop: cost model selects seed parallelism config → persistent engine loads model once → parallel agents (constantly communicating) submit candidate forward passes as jobs → tiers measure and feed back → best candidate deployed after full verification (KV-cache contamination, quality tests). Key components:

1. **Hot reloading** (Figure 3): each agent writes `tkcc_forward.py` and POSTs it via RPC; every rank execs the file, patches `model.forward`, recaptures CUDA graphs, and restores on teardown. Prefix-cache reuse of a fixed request set cuts measurement latency another 2.6x on Kimi K3; kernel binaries are cached across submissions.
2. **Tiered measurement**: the fast tier runs fewer layers (12 vs 60), starts in 3 min (vs 11), hot-reloads in 17 s (vs 45), and traces kernels (CUPTI) for kernel-level reports, but crashes 3.9/1,000 jobs (vs 0) — so agents weed out IMA/hang-prone candidates on the fast tier first. Variance is controlled by splitting prefill from decode (back-to-back variance 6.6% → 2.7%) and tuning the minimal number of measured layers, since identical forward passes can vary 4–6% while real wins can be as small as 1%.
3. **Cost model** (Appendix D): enumerates admissible schemes over MoE (EP/TP), dense TP, and attention (TP/DP/CP) up to 4,096 chips; prunes by weight fit, head-dimension divisibility, and SLA (1.5 s TTFT, 40 TPS decode); estimates per-layer prefill/decode time from FLOPs, bytes, and collective traffic; finds max batch fitting HBM and latency; inverts into tokens-per-minute-per-chip (TPM). It correctly predicts crossovers, e.g., wide EP64 for Kimi K3 throughput regimes vs higher TP as per-user TPS demands rise, and TP8 over TP4 for MiniMax M3.1 Flash at batch size 1 (decode is memory-bound; TP8 halves per-GPU weight traffic).
4. **Agent setup**: Claude Code with Opus 5.5 (reasoning high) given the engine's raw source code, no custom IR, no knowledge base, no prescribed strategies; multiple parallel agents load-balance across tier queues. Edit surface is kernels/forward-pass only, no quantization changes.
5. **Verification**: candidates must pass correctness suites before production (bit-exactness including RNG state on the scientific models; 8,640-sample/240-trajectory checks for Poseidon-T).

## Results & Highlights

- **vs hand-tuned megakernels (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, batch size 1, ISL 32, OSL up to 64K)**: TKCC reaches up to **1.7x higher TPS** than the best baseline. On B200, TKCC peaks at ~2,100 TPS (Figure 6b, 2,118 TPS), and the up-to-1.7x margin over the strongest megakernel baseline comes at the longest output lengths, where persistent-kernel approaches that pre-allocate the full KV cache fall off sharply (several baselines drop below 300 TPS at OSL 65,536). Post-mortem: fine-grained memory paging is worth only 3.1% of the forward pass, and PDL + CUDA graphs + L2 prefetching recover persistent-kernel benefits without megakernels.
- **InferenceX / AgentX leaderboard (393 agentic-coding traces, pinned AIPerf 1.0.12 load generator; protocol reproduces published numbers exactly, P90 within ~2%)**: TKCC delivers **up to 2.6x higher TPS** over the best open configurations (as of Oct 27, 2026) across Kimi K3 (B300), GLM 5.3 (B200, MI355X), DeepSeek v4.1 Flash (B200, GB300), MiniMax M3.1 Flash (B200), and MiniMax-M3 on Trainium2 — each compiled in **under 24 hours with at most 2 nodes** per model.
- **Batch-size-1 SOTA**: TKCC generates **1,111 TPS on B200 MiniMax M3.1 Flash** — 1.8x the best published result of 633, achieved by the cost model switching TP4 → TP8 — and **1,205 TPS on GB300 DeepSeek v4.1 Flash** vs the InferenceX best of 813 (GLM 5.3 B200: 671 vs 633; Kimi K3 GB300: 943 vs 417).
- **Experiment throughput**: the tiered persistent engine processes **252x more agent experiments** than engine-restart systems (11x lower idling per agent); two tiers evaluate 8.1x more candidates than one slow tier (1,354 vs 168) and find a 7.2% faster solution on B200 GLM 5.3.
- **Beyond LLMs (bit-exact speedups over baseline forward passes, Table 2)**: Poseidon-T **6.4x** (Claude Code 4.4x), Walrus **4.3x** (3.2x), Aurora 1.5 **3.7x** (3.0x), AIFS-ENS 2.0 **3.5x** (Claude Code: invalid — fails to find a correct solution), Chronos-2 **2.6x** (2.2x), down to Evo 2 and Panda-72M at 1.6x/1.9x (ties with Claude Code). Speed translates to accuracy: in fixed wall-clock, AIFS ensembles go from 4 → 13 samples (temperature RMSE 0.97 → 0.94 K, CRPS 0.50 → 0.46) and Aurora from 4 → 14 (RMSE 0.60 → 0.55 K, CRPS 0.25 → 0.21).
- **Continual adaptation**: on Kimi K3 a persistent MoE down-projection gains 2.8–5% at concurrencies 22–78 but is 6.6% *slower* at concurrency 8 — TKCC selectively applies optimizations by active batch size. Notable discovered kernels: Lamport-style fused all-reduce ~2x faster than NCCL at decode (MiniMax M3.1 Flash); removing FlashMLA's 16→64 head padding (4x redundant work) was the largest decode-attention gain on DeepSeek v4.1; PDL cut DeepSeek v4.1 decode fixed cost by 9.7%; an NVFP4 tcgen05 MoE down-projection at 23.7 µs vs the vendor kernel's 34.1 µs; DeepGEMM tile tuning 273 → 210 µs.

## Authors & Credibility

Twelve authors at Together AI Research, led by Reyna Abhyankar and Dylan Lim (equal contribution) with Simran Arora (correspondence). The team built ThunderKittens and Megakernels at Hazy Research / Together — i.e., they are beating their own prior state-of-the-art baselines, which makes the megakernel comparison credible and the "bitter lesson" self-criticism unusually candid. The system is deployed in production serving trillions of tokens per day, and the paper commits to unusually reproducible protocols: pinned docker image sha256 hashes per baseline, the exact AgentX load-generator version and trace seed, a re-run confirming they reproduce InferenceX published throughput exactly (P90 within ~2%), and per-model bit-exactness evals. The coding harness is named concretely (Claude Code, Opus 5.5, reasoning high). No peer review yet (arXiv preprint, industry lab).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core ideas (persistent engine + hot reload; tiered fidelity; analytical cost model with hardware-spec vs measured-software separation) are engineering-native rather than academically novel, but the *composition* into a continual loop is genuinely new, and the negative results on knowledge bases/IRs run against the grain of most 2026 agentic-optimization systems (GEAK, KernelAgent, AccelOpt) — valuable, falsifiable information.
- **Rigor**: Strong on measurement hygiene (variance quantified at 4–6%, reduced to 2.7% by prefill/decode splitting; baseline protocol reproduced before being beaten; pinned artifacts). The ablations (knowledge hub −25–31%, expert strategies 4.9x vs naive 6.2x, logging overhead 43% of tool calls) are the paper's most intellectually honest section, as are the failure-mode disclosures: GPU resets (Xid 154, 9 times during GLM 5.3 runs, 2 machines removed from the cluster), megakernel formation yielding only 0.22% on AMD, and attention-kernel fusion being a persistent money pit.
- **Reproducibility**: Protocol details are excellent (hashes, seeds, versions), but the system itself is not open-sourced as far as the paper states, and it depends on a proprietary production fleet's traffic profiles; outsiders can reproduce the *benchmarks* but not the *system*.
- **Weak spots in the comparisons**: (1) "Up to 2.6x" is against open InferenceX entries frozen at Oct 27, 2026 — a moving leaderboard, and closed-source lab-internal engines are absent; (2) TKCC's search spends 2x replica size × 24 hours of agent compute per model, a cost axis the headline speedups don't price in (dollars-per-trial appears only in an appendix figure); (3) the Claude Code baseline for the diverse-workload table was deliberately denied the persistent engine, model adapter, and correctness tests — an acknowledged but confounded comparison of scaffolding rather than agent ability; (4) the Trainium2 result uses a synthetic 70k-ISL multi-turn dataset instead of AgentX, so it is not directly comparable to the other four platforms (justified as avoiding CPU-offloading noise).
- **Red flags**: The 252x throughput claim is measured against the engine-restart regime (RSI-Inference-style), not against offline kernel harnesses, which are faster still but low-fidelity — the framing slightly flatters the number. The fast tier crashes 3.9/1,000 jobs and runs only 12 layers, so early-tier rankings can mislead for data-dependent kernels (their own top-k anecdote: 20 µs on random data vs 62 ms at 70K context on real data). Closed-model agents occasionally refused to continue optimizing for safety reasons, and a ban on editing the recorded GPU-step sequence permanently killed some known-good techniques — a real but under-quantified operational limitation. Production claims ("trillions of tokens") are unverifiable.

## Strengths

- Identifies and names the right problem: inference is now "polymorphic" (traffic, architectures, and hardware all drift), so optimization must be continual rather than one-shot — and demonstrates a working production loop for it.
- The throughput-fidelity tradeoff is diagnosed with concrete evidence (761 s engine cold start; random data activating 13–38% more experts; quadratic top-k blowup on real ReLU-zero distributions), not hand-waving.
- The Tiered Persistent Engine is an elegant, load-bearing idea: decoupling engine lifetime from forward-pass evaluation is what makes second-scale agent iteration loops possible at all.
- Refreshingly honest ablations that contradict the paper's own earlier design choices (custom IRs, knowledge hubs, expert strategies) — rare and useful data for anyone building agentic optimization systems.
- Breadth: five hardware platforms (B200, B300, GB300, MI355X, Trainium2), three engine codebases (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM), and eight non-LLM scientific models, all with bit-exactness verification.

## Limitations

- No public release of TKCC itself; results depend on Together's internal traffic profiles, cluster, and Claude Code/Opus 5.5 harness, so exact reproduction is effectively impossible for outsiders.
- Optimization compute (2x replica, 24 h, many parallel agents) is excluded from the headline speedups; for low-traffic deployments the amortization math may not favor continual recompilation.
- Measurement remains the binding constraint even after the 252x improvement: agents spend 52.7–57.0% of their time waiting on gates, queues, or re-measuring marginal results (Table 6) — the authors themselves call throughput "the primary outstanding bottleneck."
- The fast tier's 12-layer, 3.9-crashes-per-1000 design means early filtering can still mis-rank data-dependent kernels; the two-tier scheme mitigates but does not eliminate fidelity gaps.
- The knowledge-base and expert-strategy ablations were run on specific harness versions; as coding agents improve, the bitter-lesson conclusion may need re-liming — the authors acknowledge harness dependence but do not test multiple harnesses.
- Closed-model safety refusals and GPU-reset incidents (9 Xid 154 events, 2 machines lost) show the loop is operationally fragile at fleet scale despite the guardrails.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"高效 LLM 推理/服务系统优化"高度相关：它展示了智能体驱动的持续编译如何在实际生产集群上同时压榨 MoE/稀疏注意力模型（GLM 5.3、DeepSeek、Kimi、MiniMax）的延迟与吞吐，其热重载持久引擎、并行策略代价模型与"删抽象反而更快"的智能体上下文管理消融，对任何构建 agentic 性能优化或推理编译系统的人都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the 252x experiment-throughput gain survives on a *single* node or a low-budget setting — i.e., is continual compilation economical for smaller serving fleets, and what is the break-even traffic volume?
- Can the knowledge-base negative result be replicated across different coding agents and harness versions, or is it an artifact of Opus 5.5's pretraining already containing kernel-optimization knowledge?
- The authors propose "an IR for verifying agent-generated kernels without restricting the optimization space" as future work — what would such a verification IR look like, and could it subsume the KV-cache contamination and bit-exactness checks now done post hoc?
- Since >50% of agent time is spent waiting on measurement gates, would speculative candidate evaluation (e.g., predictive pre-screening from kernel traces) or more tiers change the optimum, or does variance ultimately bound useful throughput?
- How do the TKCC-generated forward passes behave under distribution shift beyond what was recompiled for — do the selectively-applied concurrency-dependent optimizations (like the MoE down-projection that helps at concurrency 22–78 but hurts at 8) remain correct under adversarial traffic mixtures?
