> **一句话总结：** 针对 MX 量化推理中"操作数已低位、累加器仍 BF16"的硬件瓶颈，UC Berkeley 团队提出 MIAO 软硬件协同设计框架：先校准 MX 块尺度偏移 d 以压低部分和幅值，再在权重驻留脉动阵列中为每一行独立配置渐进加宽的浮点累加器，配合"综合面积/能耗模型 + 舍入噪声损失模型 + 位精确端到端验证"的自动搜索；在 TinyLlama（WikiText-2）上于 MXFP8/6/4 三种格式下将 PE 阵列面积最多降低 38.7%、每操作能耗最多降低 36.1%（相对全 BF16 累加基线），相对纯操作数量化仅增加 ≤1.06% 困惑度，且在 TinyLlama 上选出的配置迁移到另外 5 个 LLM 无需重调、附加困惑度 ≤1.6%。

> **TL;DR:** MX (microscaling) quantization shrinks LLM operands to FP8/6/4 but systolic-array accumulation usually stays BF16, costing >50% of PE area and up to 54% of PE energy. MIAO (UC Berkeley) calibrates the MX block-scale shift d away from the OCP default (e.g., d=0 vs OCP d=8 for MXFP8) to shrink partial sums, then assigns each row of a weight-stationary array its own progressively wider accumulator (e.g., e5m4×11 + e5m5×4 for MXFP8 16×16), searched via a synthesis-calibrated cost model plus a rounding-noise loss predictor and certified by bit-exact end-to-end evaluation. On TinyLlama-1.1B / WikiText-2 it cuts PE-array area by 32.0–38.7% and whole-accelerator energy per op by 23.7–36.1% versus an all-BF16-accumulation datapath, while adding at most 1.06% perplexity over operand-only MX quantization; TinyLlama-selected configs transfer to Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-2-7B/13B, and Qwen2.5-14B with ≤1.6% additional perplexity.

## 核心贡献

- **按阶段（按行）分配累加器精度**：首次针对 MX 脉动阵列提出"级数式"浮点累加——同一阵列中靠前的行（部分和还小、舍入敏感度低）用窄累加器（如 e5m3），靠后的行渐进加宽（至 e5m5/e5m6），取代全阵列统一 BF16 累加的传统做法；配合乘积格式选择（如 e4m5/e4m4）一起优化。
- **MX 块尺度偏移校准**：指出 OCP 规范固定 d = emax（MXFP8 为 8）会使部分和幅值膨胀（TinyLlama 上达 2^20）并造成饱和截断（14–16% 的激活块被削顶）；MIAO 逐格式扫描 d，选取困惑度与最优统计不可区分的最小 d（MXFP8→d=0、MXFP6→d=2、MXFP4→d=2），从源头压缩中间精度需求。
- **自动化搜索与最优性认证**：构造非降精度"梯子"搜索空间（8×8/16×16/32×32 分别有 330/3,876/52,360 条梯子），用"可行性筛查 → 均匀宽度夹逼 + 两步梯子 → 舍入方差损失模型引导精炼 → 剩余更廉价候选逐一验证"的流程，把 MXFP8 的搜索空间相对穷举缩小 177×（16×16）和 3,009×（32×32），并在受限空间内**认证**所选配置的最优性，而非仅启发式挑选。
- **可信的软硬件评估基础设施**：位精确模拟器（与 FireSim FPGA 仿真在 MXFP8 的 65,536 个元素上逐位一致）+ 16 nm 逻辑综合导出的面积/能耗模型 + Triton 加速的端到端困惑度评测（TinyLlama 上快 2.2–7.4×），损失模型拟合 11 条梯子即可预测 319 条未见梯子（RMS 0.0066，低于成对运行标准误 0.0085）。
- **跨模型可迁移性的实证**：仅用 TinyLlama-1.1B 调优的 16×16 配置直接应用于 5 个 7B–14B 模型，中间精度化带来的附加困惑度 ≤1.6%，零样本任务与 HumanEval 代码生成均无统计显著退化。

## Key Contributions

- **Stage-dependent accumulator precision for MX systolic arrays**: replaces the uniform accumulator format with a non-decreasing "ladder" of per-row floating-point formats (narrow in early rows, wider in later rows) in weight-stationary arrays, jointly optimized with a shared product format — exploiting the spatial mapping of accumulation stages to distinct hardware (TPU/Trainium-style arrays).
- **MX block-scale shift calibration**: identifies that the OCP-mandated d = emax both inflates partial-sum magnitudes (up to 2^20 element units for MXFP8 on TinyLlama) and causes operand saturation clipping (14–16% of activation blocks for MXFP8, average clipping magnitude 8%); calibrating d per format (d=0/2/2 for MXFP8/6/4) reduces intermediate dynamic range while keeping perplexity within noise of the best.
- **Certified automated search**: an optimization pipeline minimizing a normalized area·energy product under an end-to-end perplexity constraint PPL(θ) ≤ (1+ε)·PPL_BF16, combining feasibility screening, two-step ladder bracketing, a Widrow-style rounding-noise loss model (variance ∝ E[S_r²]·4^−m_r, log-PPL increase ≈ Σ w_r(4^−m_r − 4^−7)), and explicit certification that no cheaper candidate in the restricted space meets the target — reducing the MXFP8 search space by 177× (16×16) and 3,009× (32×32) versus exhaustive enumeration.
- **Bit-exact, synthesis-grounded evaluation stack**: a configurable arithmetic simulator validated bit-identical against FireSim FPGA simulation (65,536 elements, MXFP8), a 16 nm / 2 ns synthesis-derived PE area and energy model (extends open-source Gemmini), and a Triton emulation path 2.2–7.4× faster for repeated perplexity evaluation; the loss model predicts 319 unseen ladders from 11 fitted ones with RMS error 0.0066 (below the 0.0085 paired-run standard error).
- **Cross-model transfer evidence**: configurations tuned only on TinyLlama-1.1B applied unchanged to Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-2-7B, Llama-2-13B, and Qwen2.5-14B, adding at most 1.6% perplexity beyond operand-only MX quantization, with zero-shot and HumanEval accuracy statistically unchanged.

## Methodology

MIAO is a hardware–software co-design framework operating in five stages:

1. **Block-scale calibration (software)**. For each MX format (MXFP8 E4M3, MXFP6 E3M2, MXFP4 E2M1, 32-element blocks with shared E8M0 scale), it sweeps the scale shift d ∈ {0,…,d_OCP} and measures end-to-end perplexity with *lossless* intermediate arithmetic (isolating operand error from intermediate error). It picks the smallest d whose perplexity is statistically indistinguishable from the minimum. This directly shrinks product/partial-sum magnitudes: for MXFP8, d=0 vs OCP d=8 removes a nominal 2^16 amplification of products.

2. **Precision-configurable datapath (hardware)**. Built on Gemmini's weight-stationary systolic array: each PE multiplies an incoming activation by a stationary weight, rounds the product to a shared format f_p, and adds it to the partial sum from the row above, rounded to that row's format f_a,r. Inter-tile accumulation and attention stay BF16; tile outputs are rescaled by the MX block scales and requantized to MX before the next layer.

3. **Problem formulation**. θ = (f_p, f_a,1 … f_a,n−1); minimize H(θ) = (A(θ)/A_BF16)·(E(θ)/E_BF16) subject to PPL(θ) ≤ (1+ε)·PPL_BF16, over non-decreasing ladders with accumulator exponents fixed at 5 bits (justified by profiling: partial sums for MXFP8 at d=0 grow from magnitude 8 to only 23.93 after 15 additions) and mantissas 3–7 bits.

4. **Search algorithm (Algorithm 1)**. Feasibility screening (operand-only PPL with exact products must meet the target) → candidate selection (uniform accumulator widths bracketing the target, then two-step ladders sweeping the transition row k) → loss-model-guided refinement (fit w_r ∝ r+1, validated by five-fold cross-validation against smoothness-regularized per-row fits; partial-sum profiling matches this trend within 9% across 67 layers) → certification (prune cheaper candidates that are no more precise than measured failures; bit-exactly evaluate the rest; certify only if all cheaper candidates are excluded).

5. **Cost and accuracy evaluation**. Area/energy from post-synthesis parameterized PEs (product formats 3–8 exp / 3–8 significand bits; accumulator formats 4–8 exp / 4–12 significand bits); accuracy from bit-exact end-to-end WikiText-2 perplexity (16 windows × 2,048 tokens), seven zero-shot tasks (first 500 examples each, lm-eval), and HumanEval (164 problems, 0-shot greedy). A key sensitivity finding drives the design: cutting accumulator mantissa m7→m5 saves 12.7% PE-array area and 13.9% energy (+0.05 PPL), whereas exponent e8→e5 saves only 2.6%/0.9% — mantissa width is where the money is.

## Results & Highlights

- **PE-array area (TinyLlama-1.1B, WikiText-2, vs all-BF16 accumulation datapath at same array size)**: MXFP8 −32.0% to −34.5%; MXFP6 −34.6% to −38.5%; MXFP4 −35.2% to −38.7% across 8×8/16×16/32×32 arrays (post-synthesis area; e.g., 16×16 MXFP8: 0.471 mm² vs BF16 baseline).
- **Whole-accelerator energy per operation**: MXFP8 −23.7% to −32.5% (32×32: 3.65 vs baseline pJ/op); MXFP6 −28.6% to −36.1% (32×32: 0.99 pJ/op); MXFP4 −26.0% to −36.1% (32×32: 0.78 pJ/op). BF16 accumulation alone accounts for ~65% of PE-array area in the baseline.
- **Accuracy cost of all this narrowing (TinyLlama, vs operand-only MX quantization with lossless products + BF16 accumulation)**: at most +0.50% (MXFP8), +1.06% (MXFP6), +0.89% (MXFP4) additional perplexity; absolute 16×16 perplexities 7.30/7.45/8.47 vs 7.23 all-BF16 (i.e., +0.98%/+2.96%/+17.10% total, the +17% for MXFP4 dominated by operand quantization, not MIAO's intermediate precision).
- **Cross-model transfer without retuning (16×16 configs, WikiText-2)**: worst-case additional perplexity vs operand-only is +1.6% (Llama-2-7B MXFP6); e.g., Llama-3.1-8B MXFP8 PPL 6.63 vs 6.52 BF16 (+1.7% vs BF16, +0.6% vs operand-only); Llama-2-13B MXFP8 4.68 vs 4.66 BF16.
- **Downstream tasks**: TinyLlama mean accuracy over 7 zero-shot tasks (ARC-e/c, HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande, BoolQ, LAMBADA) changes by ≤0.91 points vs all-BF16 for MXFP8/MXFP6 (e.g., 16×16 MXFP8: 57.49 vs 56.74, +0.74); HumanEval pass@1 (Llama-3.1-8B, 16×16): selected MXFP8 37.8% vs operand-only 34.1% (+3.7 points, within the ±5.6–6.7 point paired 95% interval), MXFP4 28.0% vs operand-only 27.4% (+0.6).
- **Search efficiency and validity**: MXFP8 search space reduced 177× (16×16) and 3,009× (32×32) vs exhaustive; on 8×8 the exhaustive sweep confirms the search's selections minimize hardware cost under the perplexity target; loss model RMS prediction error 0.0066 < 0.0085 run-to-run noise; simulator bit-identical to FireSim RTL over 65,536 elements. FireSim cycle simulation additionally shows −48.3% cycles for 16×16 MXFP8 vs a BF16 array (1.15×10⁹ vs 2.22×10⁹ on Mistral-7B prefill).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-scoped. Mixed-precision work (HAQ, AWQ, Atom, SliM-LLM, MixLLM, FGMP) allocates bits to *operands*; A2Q/A2Q+/AXE adapt quantization to *fixed* accumulator constraints; Abdel-Aziz et al. and He et al. optimize intermediate arithmetic but not per-stage FP accumulator widths in MX systolic arrays. The observation that a weight-stationary array's rows are physically distinct accumulators with distinct precision needs is simple, true, and previously unexploited. The block-scale calibration (moving d off the OCP default) is a smaller but practically useful contribution.
- **Rigor**: High. Bit-exact simulator validated against hardware (65,536 elements, MXFP8), synthesis-based cost model rather than analytic hand-waving, paired statistical tests for calibration ("smallest d statistically indistinguishable from best"), loss model validated by cross-validation and against 319 unseen ladders, and exhaustive 8×8 verification of the search's optimality. The paper honestly attributes MXFP4's +17–29% perplexity (vs BF16) to operand quantization, not its own technique.
- **Reproducibility**: Good on the software/simulation side (built on open-source Gemmini; standard models; exact eval protocol: WikiText-2, 16×2,048-token windows, seed 0, lm-eval for zero-shot). The 16 nm synthesis flow and FireSim setup are described but not trivially replicable; no code release is mentioned.
- **Significance**: The 32–39% PE-area savings are meaningful for MX accelerator design, but note the scope: accumulation is >50% of PE area only *within the PE array*; savings are for the array datapath, not the whole SoC or memory system (whole-accelerator energy deltas of 23.7–36.1% are model estimates, with some power figures derived from energy per op, marked * in Table 3).
- **Red flags / weaknesses**: (1) All tuning happens on TinyLlama-1.1B — a 1.1B model — and the authors themselves note "the TinyLlama accuracy constraints are not guaranteed to hold" on other models; the transfer study shows ≤1.6% but Qwen2.5-14B MXFP6 32×32 hits +1.88%, outside the original ε. (2) Attention, inter-tile accumulation, and non-linear ops stay BF16, and only MLP linears + lm_head are mapped to the array, so the accuracy story excludes quantized attention. (3) Statistical power on downstream tasks is thin: zero-shot tasks use 500 examples (±4 points uncertainty) and HumanEval's paired interval is ±5.6–6.7 points, so "+3.7 points vs operand-only MXFP8" is noise, which the authors correctly flag but readers may misread as improvement. (4) No direct experimental comparison against prior intermediate-precision work (e.g., Abdel-Aziz et al. 2021 or He et al. 2026) — the baseline is only all-BF16 accumulation, leaving unclear how much a uniform FP16/FP12 accumulator baseline would already capture. (5) The search space is heavily restricted by design (5-bit accumulator exponents fixed, non-decreasing mantissa ladders, 4-row transition granularity at 32×32), and certification of optimality holds only within that restricted space. (6) MXFP4 configurations deliver the biggest area savings (−38.7%) but at +17–30% perplexity vs BF16 — the headline saving and the headline accuracy number belong to different operating points.

## Strengths

- Attacks a real, quantified bottleneck (>50% PE area, up to 54% PE energy in accumulation) that operand quantization alone cannot touch, with a clean architectural insight (per-row precision in weight-stationary arrays).
- The software half (block-scale calibration) and hardware half (precision ladders) are co-designed and each is necessary: calibration shrinks dynamic range, ladders exploit it.
- Trustworthy evaluation methodology: bit-exactness verified against RTL-level simulation, synthesis-derived costs, certified search-space optimality, and exhaustive small-scale verification of the search.
- Honest accounting: separates operand-quantization error from intermediate-precision error in every table; explicitly marks estimated/derived power figures and noise intervals.
- Practical output: concrete per-format configurations (Table 2) an accelerator designer could adopt, plus evidence they transfer across model families and array sizes without retuning.

## Limitations

- Weight-stationary systolic arrays only; output-stationary arrays and adder trees are mentioned as generalizations but not studied, and non-systolic GPU-style tensor cores are out of scope.
- Tuning is per-operand-format and per-array-size on a single small model (TinyLlama-1.1B); per-model retuning or cross-model calibration is left to future work, and the ε-constraint is not re-imposed in the transfer experiments.
- Evaluation is perplexity-centric on WikiText-2 (16 windows); downstream coverage is seven zero-shot tasks with ±4-point uncertainty and one code-generation benchmark — no long-generation, agentic, or multimodal workloads.
- Hardware results are pre-silicon (16 nm synthesis + Accelergy-style estimation); no taped-out chip or measured end-to-end system latency/power, and memory-system effects (bandwidth, SRAM sizing for narrower datapaths) are not analyzed.
- The loss model assumes uncorrelated, zero-mean rounding noise with w_r ∝ r+1 (constant per-row loss sensitivity); models or layers violating this (e.g., outlier-heavy layers) could mislead candidate ranking, though measured end-to-end checks backstop the final choice.

## Relevance

与高效 LLM 推理加速和低精度量化方向高度相关：MIAO 补上了 MX/FP8/FP4 部署故事中常被忽略的"中间累加精度"硬件成本一环，提出的按行渐进加宽累加器与块尺度偏移校准可直接用于脉动阵列加速器（TPU/Trainium 类）的数据通路设计，也是量化算法与硬件协同设计的一个清晰范例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would per-layer or per-tensor ladders (instead of one ladder for all 67 linear layers) buy further area at the same perplexity, or does layer diversity wash out after block-scale calibration?
- How does the calibrated d interact with outlier-heavy layers (SmoothQuant/LLM.int8-style outliers) — is d=0 for MXFP8 stable across models with stronger activation outliers, e.g., at longer context lengths?
- Can the rounding-loss model be extended to guide *operand* precision too (joint MXFP-format selection per layer with intermediate precision), rather than taking the operand format as given?
- What happens with adder-tree (output-stationary / GPU tensor-core) topologies where accumulation stages are not spatially ordered — can a depth-ordered precision schedule be mapped onto tree reductions?
- Does the TinyLlama-tuned ladder remain certified under distribution shift (code, multilingual, long-context) where perplexity on WikiText-2 is a weak proxy, and what ε would be needed to guarantee zero-shot degradation bounds?
- Since BF16 inter-tile accumulation is retained, how much additional area/energy is left on the table, and does narrowing it require the same calibration machinery?
