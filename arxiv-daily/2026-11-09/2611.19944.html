⚠ Critical edge

Hardware claims rest on technology-mapped counts only — no routed timing, clock frequency, or power — so “10 vs 28 cycles” says nothing about wall-clock performance.

01 Methodology

  • Five datapaths reconstructed in SystemVerilog from a pinned Git revision (49350ee): direct 12-bit table, 11-bit chord interpolator, 8-bit table + two NR steps, 8-bit table + two Goldschmidt iterations, and the 9-bit interpolated-seed + 1 NR “Hybrid”. Original coefficient images were unavailable, so all ROMs were regenerated from their defining equations.
  • Exhaustive scoring, no sampling: every representable input in [0.5, 2.5] is compared against an exact integer reference — 131,073 codes (Q16.16), 33,554,433 (Q8.24).
  • The winning recipe: guarded chord seed (6/8 address bits, 4 guard bits) + one NR step + one integer residual check (compare 2R with ±k, adjust by at most one code).
  • Compact corrector: reuses the refinement product k·Z, shrinking the extra multiply’s operand to D = 2ᵍ·q₀ − Z — DSP slices 12 → 9 in Q8.24.
  • Verification: Verilator 5.48.0 scoreboard streams the entire domain at 1 input/cycle, checking every output and its cycle; Yosys 0.52 maps to Xilinx 7-series primitives. 306 configurations feed the budget-selection analysis.

  • Format sensitivity: +8 fractional bits (Q16.16→Q8.24) cuts max relative error 205–223× for refined paths (NR 220×, GS 205×, Hybrid 223×) but only 0.969× for the direct table — its address width pins the error near 0.1%.
  • Sampling risk made concrete: the prior 1,000,001-point sweep covered just 2.98% of Q8.24 codes and understated the direct table’s max error (32,716.07 vs 32,732.04 LSB).
  • RTL cost of exact rounding: 9 DSP slices / 10 cycles for the compact Hybrid vs 0 DSPs / 28 cycles (and the largest logic cost) for the exact radix-2 control.
  • Budget payoff: under a 4-LSB budget, the 3-product guarded Hybrid needs 1,056 / 6,016 coefficient bits vs 14,848 / 335,872 for the 1-product interpolator.

03 Strengths

  • Exhaustive, exact-integer verification on complete input grids — 33.5M points for Q8.24 — with worst-case codes recorded; no statistical hand-waving.
  • A clean, provably sufficient, exhaustively confirmed correct-rounding recipe at modest ROM (2,560 / 13,824 bits) and 4 products.
  • The product-reuse corrector (D = 2ᵍ·q₀ − Z) is an elegant trick — DSPs 12→9 — surviving full-domain RTL verification at 1 input/cycle.
  • Unusually honest scoping: reconstruction caveats stated up front, and the paper quantifies exactly how misleading the earlier sampled sweep was.
  • The budget-selection framework (least memory vs fewest products, with dominance-sensitivity tests) turns a benchmark study into a reusable design tool.

04 Limitations

  • Domain is strictly positive [0.5, 2.5]: no normalization stage, output range checks, or negative-input handling — or their costs — are analyzed.
  • Hardware evidence is Yosys mapped counts on one 7-series family: no place-and-route, no fmax, no power/energy, no vendor-IP head-to-head.
  • The five “reconstructed” paths use regenerated coefficients (originals unavailable); the Q16.16 Goldschmidt model deviates from the public repo module.
  • Baselines are re-scored numerically only; the degree-4 polynomial’s Q8.24 collapse (1,663 LSB) is reported but not diagnosed.
  • Correct rounding shown only for 32-bit Q16.16/Q8.24 on the tested interval; no end-to-end application case study.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Does the seed + 1 NR + residual-check recipe extend to the full positive axis with a 2ᵉ normalization stage — and does the residual condition remain sufficient?
  • Would Vivado place-and-route preserve the 9-DSP / 10-cycle vs 0-DSP / 28-cycle trade-off — and how does AMD’s Divider Generator IP compare in the same flow?
  • Can the compact product-reuse corrector generalize to full division x/y, reciprocal square root, and other function-evaluation datapaths?
  • Why does the Lunglmayr degree-4 correction degrade from 6.7 LSB (Q16.16) to 1,663 LSB (Q8.24) — coefficient width, F+4 internal rounding, or interval mapping?
