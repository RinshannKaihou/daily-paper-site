> **一句话总结：** 本文用两个尺度/旋转不变的集体坐标——文档可区分度 u 与文档维度 d_C——把 Transformer 前向传播表示为相图上的"推理流"，发现 8 个训练好的模型（Qwen2.5/3.5、Mistral、Falcon3、Granite）的推理流全部进入并驻留中央"工作相"，而 156 个随机初始化未训练网络的推理流则漂向坍缩区或噪声区；用离散 Langevin 方程拟合后，两者差别归结为 P*=(1.08, 117) 处有效力是鞍点还是稳定结，并在 Pythia-1.4B 的 19 个训练检查点上观察到第 256–512 步之间从漂移相到工作相的类相变跳变。

> **TL;DR:** A physics-style phase diagram for language models: each document's layer-by-layer residual-stream state is mapped to two rotation/scale-invariant coordinates (document distinguishability u, document dimension d_C), and a model's "inference flow" over a fixed 96-document corpus (PG-19 books + arXiv papers + GovReport, 32 each) either drifts or converges. All 8 trained models (5 architecture families, depths 28–48) enter and stay in a central "working phase" (entry at median relative depth 0.11; late-half dispersion 0.085 dex) versus 156 untrained random-init baselines that drift toward collapse/noise (dispersion 0.3–1.4 dex); a discrete Langevin fit gives a stable node at P* = (1.08, 117) for trained models (contraction rate −ln ρ = +0.011 per layer) versus a saddle for untrained runs (−ln ρ = −0.022), and on Pythia-1.4B's 19 checkpoints the working-phase fraction of deep-layer states jumps from ~0 to the vast majority between training steps 256 and 512. Worth reading if you care about representation geometry, layer-wise dynamics, or physics-of-learning framings of LLMs; note it links geometry to a document-identification readout, not to downstream benchmark performance.

## 核心贡献

- **定义了两个集体坐标并构建了语言模型的首个"相图"**：文档可区分度 u = |δ|²/√(tr C²)（文档均值偏移相对文档内方差的强度）与文档维度 d_C = (tr C)²/tr C²（文档内协方差矩阵的参与率，即 token 表示变化的有效方向数）。二者均为旋转与整体缩放不变的量，因此任意网络——不管表示空间维度 D 与基如何——都能被映射到同一张 (u, d_C) 对数坐标相图上，仅凭一次前向传播即可测量。
- **发现训练与未训练网络的"推理流"落在两个截然不同的相**：156 个随机初始化未训练网络的推理流随深度持续漂移，按初始化尺度 σ 分流至坍缩区（u 大、d_C 小，对应秩坍缩）或噪声区（u 小、d_C 大，对应熵坍缩）；而 8 个来自 5 个架构家族（Qwen2.5、Qwen3.5、Mistral、Falcon3、Granite）、由 4 家机构用不同语料训练的模型，其推理流全部在最初几层内进入中央"工作相"并驻留——这意味着仅凭表示的传播方式（无需解码）即可区分训练好与未训练的模型。
- **用离散 Langevin 方程给出两相的动力学判据**：v_{t+1} = α v_t + F(x_t) + η_t。未训练组惯性系数 α = 0.287 且 P* 处有效力为鞍点；训练组 α ≈ 0（准静态）且有效力为稳定结。两相之别归结为收缩率 −ln ρ 的符号（训练组 +0.011，未训练组 −0.022）。
- **把训练过程解释为相变**：在 Pythia-1.4B 从初始化到训练结束的 19 个检查点上，深层状态落入工作相的比例与惯性系数在第 256–512 步之间同步突变（杠杆规则拟合复现 α 的骤降），收缩率随后转为正。
- **提供既有观察的"字典"与可复现工件**：秩坍缩、神经坍缩、探测精度随深度上升、有效秩训练中先降后升等现象均可映射为相图上的位置或轨迹变化；代码（MIT）与全部复现数据（CC BY 4.0）公开在 GitHub 与 HuggingFace。

## Key Contributions

- Defines two collective coordinates — document distinguishability u = |δ|²/√(tr C²) and document dimension d_C = (tr C)²/tr C² (the participation ratio of the within-document covariance) — both invariant under rotation and rescaling, so any network regardless of width D or basis can be placed on one logarithmic (u, d_C) phase diagram from a single forward pass.
- Shows the first phase diagram of language models built from internal representation measurements: the "inference flows" (per-document layer-by-layer trajectories) of 156 untrained random-initialization runs drift toward a collapse region (large u, small d_C) or a noise region (small u, large d_C) depending on init scale σ ∈ [0.005, 1], consistent with known rank-collapse and entropy-collapse phenomenology in random Transformers.
- Shows that the inference flows of 8 trained models from 5 architecture families (Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B/14B, Qwen3.5-4B/9B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Falcon3-7B, Granite-3.1-8B; depths 28–48, D from 1536 to 5120; 4 institutions) all enter and reside in a central working phase — a decoding-free, statistical signature that separates trained from untrained networks.
- Fits a discrete Langevin equation v_{t+1} = α v_t + F(x_t) + η_t pooled over each group: untrained flows have inertia α = 0.287 and a saddle-shaped effective force at P*; trained flows are quasistatic (α ≈ 0) with a stable-node force, so the phase distinction reduces to the sign of the per-layer contraction rate −ln ρ.
- Interprets training as a phase transition with the training step as control parameter: on 19 Pythia-1.4B checkpoints, the working-phase fraction of deep-layer states and the inertia coefficient jump simultaneously between steps 256 and 512, after which the contraction rate turns positive.
- Provides a "dictionary" mapping prior observations (rank collapse, neural collapse, probe-accuracy depth trends, effective-rank dynamics during pretraining) onto the diagram, and releases all code and data.

## Methodology

The unit of analysis is a document, not a single token, because attention mixes document-level features (topic, coreference, style) into token representations. For each document d at each layer, they compute the document-mean offset δ = μ_d − μ_ref (where μ_ref is the mean token representation of the other 95 documents) and the within-document covariance C = ⟨(h − μ_d)(h − μ_d)^T⟩_d, then form the two invariant coordinates u and d_C. States are analyzed in log10 coordinates (dex).

Measurement protocol: a fixed corpus of 96 coherent long documents — 32 books (PG-19), 32 arXiv papers, 32 government reports (GovReport) — each truncated to its first 16,384 tokens (2,048 for Pythia-1.4B, whose context is 2,048) and tokenized with each model's own tokenizer. The residual stream is measured at the outputs of blocks 1 through L−1 (embedding and final block excluded as vocabulary-tied); outlier-norm tokens are discarded; relative depth is (t−1)/(L−2). A document's per-layer states form a trajectory; all 96 trajectories form the model's inference flow; per-layer medians over documents give the document-median trajectory.

Untrained baselines: 156 runs built by replacing only the parameters inside the Transformer blocks of the same 8 architectures with random samples (two init families — F: N(0, σ²) matrices with unit norm gains; G: all parameters including norm gains drawn from N(0, σ²) — with σ from 0.005 to 1 and multiple seeds), keeping trained input embeddings and final norm.

Phase assignment is empirical: late-half (relative depth ≥ 1/2) states are pooled into untrained and trained point clouds; the central region occupied by trained flows is the working phase (representative point P* = (1.08, 117), the median of late-half medians), the outer ring is the drifting phase, with a smoothed working-phase-fraction contour band (0.16–0.84) as the coexistence region.

Dynamics: a discrete Langevin equation v_{t+1} = α v_t + F(x_t) + η_t in (log10 u, log10 d_C), with one inertia coefficient α per group and the effective force F estimated cell-by-cell on a 0.1-dex grid over 0.3 ≤ u ≤ 3, 30 ≤ d_C ≤ 400, then linearized at P*: F ≈ a + J(x − P*). Eigenvalues of the propagation matrix M = [[I+J, αI],[J, αI]] give the per-layer contraction rate −ln ρ; its sign determines whether flows near P* contract. Fluctuation strength is summarized by a Péclet number Pe = |F/s| per cell. Training dynamics use 19 Pythia-1.4B checkpoints (steps 0 to 143,000, roughly log-spaced), measuring working-phase fraction, α, and binned −ln ρ at each; a lever-rule mixture model reproduces the α trajectory.

## Results & Highlights

- Phase separation and tight convergence of trained models: the document-median trajectories of all 8 trained models enter the working phase at a median relative depth of 0.11 and remain there at every subsequent layer; the dispersion of their late-half median positions is 0.085 dex, versus 0.3–1.4 dex for groups of untrained runs sharing an initialization scheme but differing in architecture (on the same 96-document corpus) — trained flows from different families collapse onto nearly the same region around P* = (1.08, 117).
- Dynamical distinction (baseline = 156 untrained runs): at P*, the per-layer contraction rate is −ln ρ = +0.011 (+0.019/−0.006) for the trained group (stable node, flows contract) versus −ln ρ = −0.022 (+0.003/−0.008) for the untrained group (saddle, flows do not contract and separate along the diverging direction); errors are 16–84% bootstrap intervals. The inertia coefficient likewise separates: α = 0.287 (+0.041/−0.030) untrained vs α = −0.001 (+0.033/−0.021) trained.
- Force vs fluctuation regime: within the fit range (0.3 ≤ u ≤ 3, 30 ≤ d_C ≤ 400), the median Péclet number is 1.99 for the untrained group (directed, force-driven drift per layer) versus 0.49 for the trained group (fluctuation-dominated slow motion near P*).
- Phase transition during Pythia-1.4B training (19 checkpoints, steps 0–143,000): the fraction of deep-layer states (96 documents) falling in the working phase jumps from almost zero to the vast majority between steps 256 and 512, with the inertia coefficient dropping abruptly to ≈0 in the same interval (lever-rule fit reproduces the drop); −ln ρ is ≈0 in the drifting phase and becomes positive after entry into the working phase. Notably, at step 0 the deep-layer states happen to lie inside the working phase, yet α still matches the drifting phase — position alone cannot determine phase; dynamics can.
- Geometry-to-readout link: across 155 blocks of 5 trained models, single-token document-identification accuracy (96-way logistic regression on PCs of token representations) rises with E = u/(√d_C + u) as d′ ∝ E^0.42 with R² = 0.90, connecting the collective coordinates to a measurable linear-readout quantity.
- Robustness ablation: estimating J per trained model separately (instead of pooled) gives median −ln ρ = 0.027 with all 8 values positive, confirming contraction is not an artifact of pooling.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel framing. Individual ingredients are known — residual networks as discretized ODEs, rank/entropy collapse in random Transformers, representation convergence across models, participation-ratio coordinates — but nobody has combined them into an empirical, cross-architecture phase diagram with a fitted dynamical law and a training-step control parameter. The step-0 observation (states inside the working phase yet dynamically drifting) is a nice demonstration that phase is dynamical, not positional.
- **Rigor:** Mostly high — invariant coordinates, bootstrap errors, ablations over the fit range, force-evaluation point, and per-network estimation (Appendix D 2, Table IV). However, several choices are under-validated: the phase boundary and P* are empirical and admittedly corpus-dependent, and the paper does not test how much the working phase moves with, e.g., a different 96-document corpus or shorter inputs.
- **A concrete confound:** input length differs across models — all models see 16,384 tokens except Pythia-1.4B, which sees only 2,048 because of its context limit. The training-transition analysis (the paper's most causal claim) rests on exactly this shortest-context model, and the paper itself flags (Fig. 5 caption) that its binned −ln ρ values are "not compared value by value" with the two reference groups because of input-length and model differences. The transition is also bracketed only coarsely: Pythia has no checkpoint between steps 256 and 512, so "the boundary lies in this interval" is a wide bracket, and the phase-transition claim rests on a single model's single training run.
- **Missing links to behavior:** despite the abstract's promise of a gateway to "language-model behavior," no downstream capability, loss, or perplexity is correlated with phase residence — the only behavioral proxy is 96-way document identification, a readout task far from benchmark performance. Nothing shows that two models at different loss plateaus occupy distinguishable positions, so the diagram currently separates trained from untrained, not good from bad.
- **Group heterogeneity:** the "trained" group mixes 7 pretrained base models with 1 post-trained model (Qwen3.5-9B), and 3 of 8 models are Qwen2.5 family, so "5 architecture families" overstates diversity somewhat; all models are English-centric and the corpus is English-only (PG-19/arXiv/GovReport).
- **Overclaim watch:** phrases like "unified gateway" and "the fate of the flow determines the phase" (the latter is stated as an assumption) are stronger than what 8 models + 156 synthetic runs establish. The mapping of prior literature (Appendix A dictionary) is argued by analogy rather than measured head-to-head.

## Strengths

- A single, cheap, decoding-free measurement (one forward pass over 96 shared documents) that cleanly separates trained from untrained networks across architectures, widths (1536–5120), depths (28–48), and training recipes.
- The two collective coordinates are basis- and scale-invariant, which is what makes cross-model comparison legitimate — a real methodological contribution beyond the specific findings.
- The discrete Langevin formalism converts a descriptive observation into a predictive dynamical criterion (saddle vs stable node; sign of −ln ρ), and the step-0 Pythia result proves the dynamical criterion catches cases positional criteria miss.
- Honest uncertainty reporting throughout (bootstrap 16–84% intervals on α, −ln ρ, lever-rule fit), plus public code and data with pinned revisions and seeds — unusually reproducible for interpretability work.
- The dictionary (Appendix A) constructively unifies previously disjoint observations (rank collapse, neural collapse, probe-depth trends, effective-rank dynamics) in one coordinate system.

## Limitations

- Only 8 trained models, all 1.5B–14B dense English models from 5 families; no test of whether the working phase persists for frontier-scale, MoE, multilingual, or multimodal models, nor for non-Transformer architectures.
- The training-as-phase-transition evidence comes from a single model (Pythia-1.4B) with no checkpoint between steps 256 and 512, so the transition window is bracketed, not resolved; no hysteresis or order-parameter analysis is possible with the released checkpoints.
- The effective force F is fit only in a window around P* (0.3 ≤ u ≤ 3, 30 ≤ d_C ≤ 400); dynamics deep in the collapse/noise regions are extrapolated from trajectory fates, not fitted.
- The phase boundary is empirical (smoothed working-phase-fraction contours), corpus-dependent, and admits a wide coexistence band attributed to finite depth — the authors expect it to narrow with depth but do not demonstrate this with deeper models.
- No connection to task-level behavior, capability, or loss; the "behavior" link is via a 96-class document-identification readout only.
- Pooling across networks to fit one (α, J) per group assumes layer actions depend only on state; the per-network ablation is done only for the trained group, where it is easiest (most untrained runs never pass through the working phase, so per-run fits would be biased).

## 相关性

这篇论文把"表示几何如何随深度演化"变成了可测量的相图与动力学系统问题，直接契合对 LLM 可解释性、表示几何与训练动力学的核心兴趣；其"训练初期即发生漂移相→工作相的类相变"结论，也为理解模型训练早期发生了什么提供了一个不依赖下游评测的新视角。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does residence position within the working phase (distance from P*) correlate with training loss, downstream benchmark performance, or data quality — i.e., can the diagram separate good models from merely trained ones?
- How do post-training stages (SFT, RLHF/DPO) move the inference flow: does P* shift, does the contraction rate tighten, or does the flow stay fixed?
- Does the transition between steps 256 and 512 in Pythia-1.4B coincide with known early-training events such as induction-head formation, and can finer checkpoints resolve whether the jump is continuous or a true discontinuity?
- Do the collapse/noise fates of random inits predict trainability in practice — e.g., do models initialized near the saddle's stable manifold (near P*) reach a given loss with fewer tokens, as the "edge of chaos" literature would suggest?
- What do higher-order collective coordinates (beyond u and d_C) capture that this two-dimensional projection averages away, and does the phase diagram survive multilingual, code-heavy, or non-document (conversation) corpora?
