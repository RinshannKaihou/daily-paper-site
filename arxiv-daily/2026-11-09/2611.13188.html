Critical Edge

Headline tables use idealized perfect routing; layer-level microbenchmarks only, with no end-to-end serving numbers — and the 2.4× partly conflates the comm win with a different GEMM backend.

01 Methodology — how it works

Three observations drive the design: in DP-attention + EP-MoE serving each rank knows its tokens’ destinations locally; a single NVLink domain (B300 board, GB200 NVL72 rack — up to 72 GPUs at 900 GB/s) can map peers’ memory into one symmetric region where every aligned 32-bit word arrives whole or not at all; and at decode batch sizes the round trip costs tens of µs although payload transfer would take <1 µs — the gap is fixed synchronization cost, so the fix must be structural.

Dispatch: each source rank writes the quantized token (hidden state, scales, all k expert ids + router weights) directly into its own region of every target’s workspace — one slot per target rank regardless of how many of that rank’s experts are selected (min(k, P) sends instead of k). Slots are taken by atomic counter (FENCE) or deterministic landing, token i → slot i (SENTINEL, Lamport-style: the data itself is the arrival flag, sentinel-initialized words).

Compute & combine: indexing → FC1/FC2 grouped GEMMs read tokens in place via TMA gather4 slot indices (no per-expert permute) → finalize applies router weights and writes partial sums back at the token’s original slot, so combine is also metadata-free. Two sync points per layer; both protocols time out and trap rather than hang.

Evaluation: routed layers of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (384 experts, top-6) and Kimi-K3 (896, top-16), 1–4096 tokens/rank, perfect + Zipf routing, on 8×B300 and one GB200 NVL72 (8→64 ranks); CUPTI + in-kernel timestamps, L2 flushed per iteration, outputs bitwise- or L2-verified.

03 Strengths

  • Attacks the right bottleneck: fixed synchronization and layout-conversion cost, not raw bandwidth — IKET traces show SENTINEL shortens the work around the peer wait.
  • Compounding rank-major win: de-duplicates sends to min(k, P) per token and shrinks workspaces ~6×, while staying GEMM-compatible via TMA gather4.
  • Clean interfaces: quantization-format- and backend-agnostic behind FlashInfer’s MoEEpCommunication; CUDA-graph + PDL capturable; timeouts trap instead of hanging.
  • Wide ablation surface: two protocols, two models (k=6 vs k=16), 1–4096 tok/rank, Zipf skew, payload-format sweep, 8→64-GPU scaling.

04 Limitations

  • Single NVLink domain only: relies on direct peer stores and whole-word arrival that RDMA cannot provide; cross-node EP out of scope; workspace grows linearly with EP size.
  • Loses to fused MegaMoE from 256 tok/rank (up to 35% slower at 4096) — separate kernels cannot overlap comm with Tensor Core work.
  • SENTINEL overhead grows with batch (poll/copy/reset per slot), forcing the 256-tok/rank protocol switch.
  • Layer-level, synthetic routing: no end-to-end serving numbers; no accuracy table for FP8 combine; Blackwell-only hardware (no Hopper/NVLink4 results).
  • Backend constraint: Prims-TS GEMM needs ≥k experts per rank — this silently blocked the Kimi-K3 64-GPU config (14 < 16).

05 Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can SENTINEL-style in-band completion adapt to GPU-initiated RDMA, where word-arrival guarantees are absent — or is a hybrid NVLink+RDMA tier the only path to multi-node?
  • What are the end-to-end serving gains under a real SLO, where router drift, KV-cache pressure, and uneven per-rank tokens replace perfect routing?
  • How large is the accuracy cost of FP8 combine (per-element E4M3) across tasks and larger k (Kimi-K3’s 16 partial sums)?
  • Would a fused one-sided variant keep the metadata-free push while closing the ≥256-token/rank gap to MegaMoE?
  • How does the expert-id slot broadcast scale to NVL144-class domains (P·T̂ poll/reset cost grows quadratically)?
  • Can deterministic landing (token i → slot i) survive dynamic expert replication or capacity-factor overflow under extreme skew without metadata re-introduction?
