> **一句话总结：** 该文系统研究"新版模型直接复用旧版模型生成的 KV cache"是否可行：在 GLM-5.2→5.3、DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash→Flash-1017、DeepSeek-V4-Flash→Flash-0731、Qwen3-30B-A3B→Instruct-2507 四次模型更新上，检索兼容性随模型检索能力差异极大（32K 前缀下有害翻转率从 0.53% 到 78.30%）；以 DeepSeek-V4-Flash→Flash-0731 为主案例，源 cache 就绪时三个长前缀 QA 基准的中位 TTFT 加速 2.10–5.08 倍（HotpotQA F1 仅降 3.0 点），并提出 Functional Cache Divergence（缩放 JS 散度）刻画复用对下一 token 分布的扰动——检索任务随前缀变长发散度从 2.87 升至 30.62，而自然文本续写稳定在 3.04–3.64。

> **TL;DR:** An empirical study of *direct* KV-cache reuse across four model updates (e.g., DeepSeek-V4-Flash → Flash-0731): the updated model consumes its predecessor's prefix cache with no mapping or recomputation. Compatibility is highly update-dependent — on single-token NIAH retrieval at 32K prefixes, harmful flip rates range from 0.53% (V4.1-Flash→Flash-1017, which keeps 97.92% accuracy vs Target's 97.92%) to 78.30% (Qwen3→Instruct-2507, accuracy 55.21%→13.02%). For the main Flash→Flash-0731 pair on 585 QA examples, prepared-cache reuse gives median TTFT speedups of 5.08× on HotpotQA, 2.10× on 2WikiMQA, and 2.51× on MultiFieldQA-en, at an F1 cost of −3.0 (HotpotQA, 69.3→66.3) and −7.0 (2WikiMQA, 61.7→54.7) while MultiFieldQA-en F1 is preserved (45.9→46.2). A new metric, Functional Cache Divergence, shows next-token distribution drift grows with prefix length for retrieval (2.87→30.62 from 512 to 32K) but stays flat (~3.0–3.6) for FineWeb-Edu natural-text continuation. Task utility and behavioral fidelity are distinct criteria; read it if you care about serving-stack cache invalidation across model upgrades.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统研究"跨模型更新的直接 KV cache 复用"**：在不做映射、不做选择性重算的前提下，让更新后的模型直接消费前代模型（同架构同规模，仅后训练不同）生成的前缀 cache，并在四次真实模型更新（GLM-5.2→5.3、DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash→Flash-1017、DeepSeek-V4-Flash→Flash-0731、Qwen3-30B-A3B→Instruct-2507）上统一评测检索、答案质量、TTFT 与生成行为。
- **核心发现：cache 的"任务有用性"与"行为保真度"是两个独立标准**——cache 能保留有用的任务信息（甚至让 Flash-Base 的 NIAH 准确率从 0% 升到 91.15%@2K），却不忠实复现新版模型的原生行为（首 token 一致也不保证答案内容与停止行为一致）。
- **提出 Functional Cache Divergence (FCD)**：在固定后续输入与预测位置的前提下，比较复用 cache 与原生 cache 下目标模型的完整下一 token 概率分布（缩放 Jensen–Shannon 散度），能捕捉"选词相同但概率已漂移"的隐性变化。
- **机制层面的解释**：答案 token 的 logit 裕度（margin）分析解释了正确检索为何能在内部状态大幅漂移下幸存（Flash-0731 中位裕度变化 −1.00 logit 仍修复 14 个答案）；交叉干预实验证明漂移主要沿层间输入表征传播（layer 37 处替换输入隐状态的 NMSE 0.473 ≫ 替换本地 cache 的 0.019），而非局限于单层 cache。
- **实用结论**：检索能力更强的大模型（GLM-5.3、V4.1-Flash-1017）复用更可靠，V4.1 复用在 512K 前缀下仍保有 61.46% 准确率；直接复用在长前缀上还优于适配版 Ridge Mapper（512K 处 61.46% vs 40.10%）。

## Key Contributions

- First systematic study of **direct KV-cache reuse across successive model updates** (unchanged architecture and size, post-training-only changes), evaluated on four real updates: GLM-5.2→GLM-5.3 (750B), DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash→Flash-1017 (552B), DeepSeek-V4-Flash→Flash-0731 (284B, main case study), and Qwen3-30B-A3B→Instruct-2507 (30.5B), across retrieval accuracy, QA quality, TTFT, and generation behavior.
- Central conceptual finding: **task utility and fidelity to native inference are distinct compatibility criteria** — a transferred cache can carry useful task state (raising Flash-Base's NIAH accuracy from 0% to 91.15% at 2K with frozen weights) without faithfully reproducing the updated model's behavior (even first-token agreement does not guarantee matching answer content or stopping).
- Introduces **Functional Cache Divergence (FCD)**, a scaled Jensen–Shannon divergence between the target's next-token distributions under reused vs. native caches with subsequent tokens and prediction positions held fixed — revealing probability drift even when the selected token is unchanged.
- Mechanistic account linking state drift to decisions: **gold-token logit-margin analysis** explains why correct retrievals survive large state changes, and **crossed input/cache interventions** at layers 27/37/42 show drift propagates mainly through incoming hidden representations (layer-37 NMSE 0.473 for input replacement vs 0.019 for local-cache replacement), so later-layer cache inspection misses propagated effects.
- Practical serving evidence: with source caches prepared, reuse yields **median TTFT speedups of 2.10–5.08×** on longer-prefix QA benchmarks with task-dependent quality trade-offs, and direct reuse **beats an adapted closed-form Ridge Mapper** at long prefixes (61.46% vs 40.10% at 512K).

## Methodology

The paper compares three inference configurations on every (prefix, question) pair: **Target** (target model does prefill + generation), **Source** (source model throughout), and **KV Reuse** (source-model prefill cache handed to the target model, which processes the question suffix and generates). Prompt token IDs and decoding settings are identical within each pair; free generation is greedy.

- **Retrieval compatibility**: controlled key–value NIAH tasks (RULER-style, following Hsieh et al. 2024) with 512/2K/4K/8K/16K/32K-token prefixes, 8 histories × 24 queries per length (192/length; ~1 record per 16 tokens; single-token keys and values; queries at 10%/50%/90% depth, phase-balanced against compression-window boundaries). The V4.1-Flash update is extended to 64K–512K (1,920 queries total). Metric: answer-prefix accuracy; also **harmful flip rate** (HFR) = fraction of Target-correct answers that become incorrect under reuse.
- **QA quality and latency**: 585 fixed examples (300 BABILong, 100 HotpotQA, 100 2WikiMQA, 85 MultiFieldQA-en from LongBench v1) for Flash→Flash-0731, measuring accuracy/token-F1 and paired median TTFT with the source prefix cache already loaded (cache loading/restoration included; source prefill and startup excluded). Serving stack: SGLang on eight H100 GPUs (TP=8).
- **Cache-transfer direction analysis**: Flash's caches injected into both Flash-Base (earlier, pretrained) and Flash-0731 (later), with layerwise NMSE traces of attention outputs / post-attention residual / layer outputs, gold-margin change vs native margin, and Indexer (sparse-attention entry-selection) top-k overlap analysis.
- **Functional Cache Divergence**: mean FCD (0–100 scale, natural-log JS divergence × 100/ln2) at the first NIAH answer position (1,152 queries) vs 32 matched continuation positions × 64 FineWeb-Edu documents across all six prefix lengths (12,288 paired positions).
- **Crossed interventions**: at layers 27/37/42, evaluate target attention F(X, C) over {Target/reuse input} × {Target/source cache} to separate local-cache effects from propagated input-representation effects; matched-perturbation cohorts (natural patch vs random directions vs structured rotation, BF16-norm-matched within 0.2%) validate the margin analysis.
- A **closed-form Ridge Mapper baseline** (Heo et al. 2026) is adapted to DeepSeek's composite cache (attention + compression + indexer states, pre-RoPE content coordinates, identity-transfer validated with exact logit agreement), calibrated on 500 FineWeb-Edu documents.

## Results & Highlights

- **NIAH retrieval across updates (32K prefixes)**: KV reuse under V4.1-Flash→Flash-1017 matches Target exactly at 97.92% accuracy and stays within 1.04 points at every length; GLM-5.2→5.3 reuse *improves* 32K accuracy from 86.46% (Target) to 90.62%; V4-Flash→Flash-0731 reuse improves Target from 25.52% to 31.25% at 32K (though below Source's 55.73%); Qwen3→Instruct-2507 reuse drops accuracy from 55.21% to 13.02% at 32K despite RoPE phase alignment.
- **Harmful flip rate at 32K**: 4.22% (GLM), 0.53% (V4.1-Flash), 26.53% (V4-Flash), 78.30% (Qwen) — stronger retrieval models preserve correct answers more reliably. V4-Flash reuse at 32K breaks 13 of Target's 49 correct answers but repairs 24 errors (net gain).
- **512K extension (V4.1-Flash→1017)**: reuse retains 61.46% accuracy at a 512K-token prefix (Target 66.15%, Source 57.81%); direct reuse beats the adapted Ridge Mapper by 19.79 and 21.35 points at 256K (72.40% vs 52.60%) and 512K (61.46% vs 40.10%), though the mapper wins at 32K on the V4-Flash pair (40.10% vs 31.25%, +8.85 points, 95% CI [5.21, 12.50]).
- **QA latency/quality (Flash→Flash-0731, 585 examples)**: median TTFT speedups of 5.08× (HotpotQA, median prefix 14.3K), 2.51× (MultiFieldQA-en, 7.7K), 2.10× (2WikiMQA, 6.4K); BABILong is 0.86× (slower) on its 1.7K median prefix because cache-access costs outweigh savings. F1 changes: HotpotQA 69.3→66.3 (−3.0, 95% CI [−6.6, −0.1]), 2WikiMQA 61.7→54.7 (−7.0, CI [−12.7, −1.5]); MultiFieldQA-en preserved at 45.9→46.2; BABILong accuracy 59.0→58.7.
- **Cache carries prompt-conditioned behavior**: Flash-Base scores 0% NIAH accuracy with its own cache (it restates the question under the instruction prompt) but 88.02%/91.15%/81.77% at 512/2K/4K with Flash's cache injected — weights unchanged; the transferred state encodes both records and response instructions.
- **FCD task contrast (Flash update)**: mean NIAH FCD rises from 2.87 (512 tokens) to 30.62 (32K), while FineWeb-Edu natural-text continuation stays within 3.04–3.64 across the same lengths (3.51 at 32K).
- **Margin mechanism**: for Flash-0731 at 4K, median gold-margin change is only −1.00 logit yet accuracy rises 87.50%→91.67% (14 repairs vs 6 losses); for Flash-Base, every query gains gold margin (median +11.77 logits, accuracy 0%→81.77%) even though Indexer top-k selection overlap with Target drops from 81.7% to 55.4% (late layers 50.0%→37.1% after chance correction).
- **Fidelity gaps**: MultiFieldQA-en shows 87.5% first-token agreement yet 46.2% of responses change later in the sequence while mean F1 is preserved (45.92→46.24); diagnostic cases show changed stopping ("Switchfoot [EOS]" → "Switchfoot is still together.", F1 1.00→0.40) and changed answers (Washington, D.C. → St. Louis, Missouri, F1 0.67→0.00).

## Authors & Credibility

Three authors (Zhikun Tao, Fangyong Jin, Junping Zhao, with Zhao as corresponding author) from **Rank Computing**, an industry lab. This is a technical report (v1, cs.DC/cs.AI) rather than a peer-reviewed venue paper, but the empirical rigor is well above typical reports: fixed snapshots with revision hashes for BABILong/LongBench, per-pair tokenized-prompt identity, history-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 replicates for sweeps), matched reruns for the mapper comparison (identity-transfer validation with exact cache and logit agreement), a full runtime matrix (SGLang vs PyTorch paths, chunk sizes, TP/PP settings, eight H100 GPUs), same-version control runs to isolate handoff effects, and explicit disclosure of construction quirks (e.g., the 512K term-pool exhaustion). No code release is mentioned.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Good and genuinely underexplored — prior cross-model cache work (KVComm, DroidSpeak, HeteroFold, Heo et al.'s ridge mapping, CacheBridge, RaReCache) focuses on *mapping* or *selective recomputation* between models; this paper isolates the *zero-mapping direct-reuse* question across real version-to-version updates and, unusually, connects it to mechanistic analysis (margins, NMSE traces, crossed interventions). The FCD metric is a modest but useful addition.
- **Rigor**: High — matched token IDs, paired bootstrap CIs, phase-balanced NIAH queries against compression-window boundaries, and honest reporting of where reuse *hurts* (2WikiMQA −7.0 F1; BABILong 0.86× slowdown). However, the "stronger retrieval models reuse more reliably" claim rests on only four updates in four different model families, so backbone size/strength, cache format, and update magnitude are confounded — it is a plausible hypothesis, not an established law.
- **Reproducibility**: The protocol is documented unusually well (dataset revisions, chunk sizes, perturbation-norm matching within 0.2%), but the study depends on proprietary or hard-to-obtain checkpoints (DeepSeek-V4-Flash family, GLM-5.2/5.3, Qwen3-30B-A3B pair) and no code or cache artifacts are released, so independent replication will be difficult.
- **Red flags / data quirks**: (1) The Qwen Source baseline scoring 0.00% on NIAH is a protocol artifact — its thinking-style output fills the eight-token budget before answering — which muddies the Qwen panel of Figure 1 even though the Target-vs-Reuse comparison stands. (2) The 512K NIAH histories violate the key/value disjointness used at all shorter lengths (65,536 distinct terms needed vs a 53,162-term pool, so a token can be both a key and a value), and the authors themselves flag that cross-length trends "describe the recorded settings... rather than isolate length". (3) TTFT speedups exclude source prefill and model startup — the benefit is real but conditional on a warm cache surviving an upgrade, which is exactly the deployment question and is not costed end-to-end. (4) Mechanistic claims rest on three inspected layers (27/37/42) and a 96-query cohort; the authors admit the crossed input-cache interventions "do not add up" and illustrate propagation rather than define a repair method. (5) The Flash→Flash-Base 0%→91% result partly reflects that Base was never instruction-tuned, making the "instruction transfer" interpretation hard to separate from raw capability rescue.
- **Significance**: Practically relevant to anyone serving frequently-updated models with long stable prefixes (system prompts, RAG corpora): direct reuse is sometimes free speed, sometimes a −7 F1 quality leak, and this paper gives the first honest map of when each happens.

## Strengths

- Clean three-configuration design (Target / Source / KV Reuse) with identical token IDs and decoding, making every comparison paired rather than cross-run.
- Evaluates *four* independent real-world model updates rather than one cherry-picked pair, exposing the full spectrum from near-native (V4.1) to severely degraded (Qwen) reuse.
- Introduces a metric (FCD) that measures distributional drift separately from accuracy, yielding the paper's most interesting finding: retrieval drift grows with prefix length while natural-text continuation drift stays flat.
- The mechanistic section goes beyond correlational curves — margin-vs-survival analysis, Indexer selection overlap, and crossed input/cache interventions give a coherent story of *why* answers survive state drift and *where* the drift originates (propagated inputs, not local caches).
- Unusually transparent about limitations: the 512K key-pool reuse, the non-additivity of interventions, and the Qwen Source 0% protocol artifact are all disclosed in the paper itself.

## Limitations

- Only four model updates, all within the same family per pair and all with unchanged architecture/size; no study of how *update magnitude* (amount of post-training) or architectural tweaks affect compatibility, and no smaller/open-weights replication.
- The headline TTFT speedups (2.10–5.08×) assume the source cache is already computed and importable; end-to-end cost of cache storage, versioned cache management, and page-format compatibility across serving-stack upgrades are not modeled.
- Quality regressions concentrate on multi-hop QA (2WikiMQA −7.0 F1), i.e., reuse is not safe for reasoning-heavy workloads at long prefixes, but the paper offers no predictor of *which* queries will flip before deployment (explicitly listed as future work).
- The strongest reuse results (GLM, V4.1-Flash) are on very large models (750B/552B) with excellent native retrieval — the regime where reuse matters least for correctness and the hardware barrier is highest; the 284B main case study is itself an older-model pair.
- Internal-state and intervention analyses sample a small set of layers (27/37/42) and cohorts (96 + 48 queries); FCD is measured at a single NIAH answer position, so within-sequence divergence dynamics remain unexplored.
- Greedy decoding throughout; behavior under sampling, and generation beyond ~48 tokens, is untested.

## Relevance

该文直接命中"LLM 推理效率与 KV cache 复用"这一核心兴趣：它量化了模型滚动升级时长前缀 cache 的直接复用在检索、QA 质量与 TTFT 上的收益和风险（2.10–5.08× 加速 vs 最多 −7 F1 的质量损失），并给出了 FCD 这类可复用的兼容性度量。对构建 RAG/长上下文服务系统、以及跨版本 cache 管理策略的设计非常有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can *update magnitude* (e.g., KL between old/new model outputs on calibration text) predict per-prefix reuse safety before deployment, turning the paper's post-hoc FCD into an admission-control signal?
- Does the retrieval-vs-continuation FCD gap (30.62 vs 3.51 at 32K) persist for multi-hop reasoning chains, code completion, and agentic tool-call traces, where "natural continuation" cues are weaker than FineWeb-Edu prose?
- Would hybrid policies — direct reuse for natural-text prefixes, selective recomputation or ridge mapping only for retrieval-critical regions flagged by margin analysis — recover the lost 3–7 F1 on HotpotQA/2WikiMQA while keeping most of the TTFT speedup?
- Since drift propagates through incoming hidden states rather than local caches (layer-37 NMSE 0.473 vs 0.019), can an early-layer "drift firewall" (recomputing a few shallow layers) cheaply bound downstream divergence?
- How do the findings transfer to open-weights models and MoE routing changes — does expert-assignment drift under the new checkpoint (not just attention-state drift) explain part of the Qwen failure mode?
- What is the end-to-end systems cost of maintaining versioned prefix caches (storage, page-format migration across serving-stack upgrades, staleness policy), which the prepared-cache TTFT accounting excludes?
