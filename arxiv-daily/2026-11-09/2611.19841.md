> **一句话总结：** 作者设计了一个闭合回路方程发现任务——在含隐藏窄高斯峰（占 x2 范围 7%）的三变量合成系统中让 Claude Sonnet 5.5 与 GPT-6.1-Sol 各做 12 轮"提假设→选实验"，结果两个 AI 科学家进入峰区的频率都低于随机采样（Sonnet 19/32 vs 随机 82% 二分法 100%；GPT 仅 1/32），越严格跟随"模型分歧最大处做实验"的 GPT-6.1-Sol 反而 32 次全败（Sonnet 解出 7/32），因为 BIC 复杂度惩罚让光滑背景曲线在未测区间上彼此一致，分歧引导的搜索被拉向 x2≈0 处的细枝末节——发现隐藏现象靠的是尽早探测未测空隙并沿异常做多步跟进，而非只在模型分歧处测量。

> **TL;DR:** In a controlled closed-loop equation-discovery benchmark (a synthetic 3-input system y=f(x1,x2,x3)+ε, σ=0.08, hiding a narrow Gaussian peak of up to 31σ inside an unmeasured region W covering 7% of the x2 range), two frontier AI scientists run without code or tools were each given 12 rounds to propose equations (BIC-ranked) and pick 2 experiments per round, scored on 4,096 held-out test points (solved = RMSE/σ ≤ 1.05). Both agents entered W *less* often than a random-sampling baseline that fits no equations at all (Claude Sonnet 5.5: 19/32 trials = 59%; GPT-6.1-Sol: 1/32 = 3%; random sampling: 82%; interval bisection: 32/32), and solve rates were 7/32 (22%) for Sonnet vs 0/32 for GPT-6.1-Sol (p=0.016). The mechanism: before any data lands in W, BIC penalizes peak parameters, the leading smooth curves agree across the gap, and their disagreement concentrates near x2 ≤ 1 (78%/64% of pre-entry rounds) rather than inside W (0.8%/2.0%) — so the agent that chased disagreement hardest (median chosen/max-disagreement ratio 0.92 vs 0.48, p=9×10⁻¹⁰) wasted its budget separating nearly identical background curves (median spread 0.68σ, below single-measurement noise). Recovery, by contrast, is deterministic once the chain starts: with ≥3 distinct x2 positions measured inside W, Sonnet solved 10/10 trials (vs 0/17 with 1 position, p=2×10⁻⁷), provided a ≥10σ anomaly arrived by Round 9 (9/11 solved vs 0/8 for later hits). The actionable lesson: space-filling early exploration plus anomaly-focused follow-up, and benchmarks should evaluate *where* agents measure, not just what equations they write.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示"分歧引导搜索"的盲区机制**：当初始数据漏掉窄特征时，BIC 的复杂度惩罚使峰项无法进入最优方程，拟合背景的多条光滑曲线在未测区间上彼此一致、只在别处（x2≈0）有分歧——于是"在模型分歧最大处做实验"这一经典主动学习策略反而把搜索引离新现象所在之处，类似人类科学家的确认偏误与"路灯下找钥匙"效应。
- **前沿 AI 科学家的实证失败率量化**：在 32 组配对试验（同初始数据、同噪声种子）中，GPT-6.1-Sol 进入峰区仅 1/32（3%）、解出 0/32；Claude Sonnet 5.5 进入 19/32（59%）、解出 7/32（22%）——两个 agent 都显著差于完全不拟合方程的随机采样（到第 12 轮进入率 82%；第 6 轮即显著低于随机，p=0.002 与 p=8×10⁻¹³）。
- **失败原因的逐轮解剖**：进入峰区前，两个 agent 的最优方程预测出 ≥2σ 局部峰的轮次仅 0.7%/0.5%；GPT-6.1-Sol 的"所选点分歧/最大分歧"比值中位数 0.92（Sonnet 0.48，p=9×10⁻¹⁰），但其所选点上前三方程的预测差中位数仅 0.68σ——低于单次测量噪声，即它一直在精测本质上相同的背景曲线。
- **发现成功的多步假设链条件**：只有测量落入 W 才可能恢复（未进入者 0/32 解出）；≥10σ 的强异常信号在 19 个进入试验中的 16 个触发峰项进入最优方程；在 W 内测 ≥3 个不同 x2 位置则 10/10 全部解出（仅 1 个位置 0/17，p=2×10⁻⁷）；信号须在第 9 轮前到达（9/11 解出 vs 第 10–12 轮 0/8）。
- **对 AI 科学家系统与基准的设计建议**：两阶段策略——早期用空间填充式实验覆盖宽未测区间以尽早撞上意外现象，再围绕异常集中跟进；发现类基准应同时评估 agent 选择测量的位置、使用非整数特征位置、并纳入简单空间填充基线。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies and demonstrates a blind-spot mechanism in disagreement-guided closed-loop discovery**: with a narrow feature missing from early data, BIC's complexity penalty keeps peak terms out of the top-ranked equations, so fitted smooth curves agree across the unmeasured gap and disagree only elsewhere — steering the classic "measure where models disagree most" strategy (Box–Hill, query-by-committee lineage) away from where the new phenomenon lies, an LLM-scale analog of confirmation and streetlight bias in human science.
- **Quantifies the failure on two frontier AI scientists**: across 32 paired trials (identical starting data, shared noise seeds), GPT-6.1-Sol entered the hidden peak region W in 1/32 trials (3%) and solved 0/32; Claude Sonnet 5.5 entered 19/32 (59%) and solved 7/32 (22%, p=0.016) — both below a random-sampling baseline (82% entry by Round 12; significantly below by Round 6) and far below interval bisection (32/32, though that baseline gets oracle knowledge of which variable carries the peak).
- **A round-level mechanistic account of why**: before entering W, neither agent's top equation predicted a ≥2σ local peak in more than 1% of rounds; disagreement among the top three equations concentrated at x2 ≤ 1 (78% of rounds for Sonnet, 64% for GPT-6.1-Sol) and almost never inside W (0.8%/2.0%); GPT-6.1-Sol followed this (partly spurious) disagreement signal most faithfully (median chosen/max-disagreement ratio 0.92 vs 0.48, p=9×10⁻¹⁰) yet the spread at its chosen points was a median 0.68σ — under single-measurement noise.
- **Characterizes the multi-step hypothesis chain needed after entering the gap**: no recovery without a measurement inside W (0/32); a ≥10σ anomaly launched a peak term in the top-ranked equation in 16/19 such trials; ≥3 distinct x2 positions across W gave 10/10 solves vs 0/17 with one position (p=2×10⁻⁷); timing matters — a ≥10σ hit by Round 9 yielded 9/11 solves, Rounds 10–12 hits 0/8.
- **Concrete, transferable design guidance**: two-stage exploration (space-filling early coverage of wide unmeasured intervals, then concentrated follow-up around anomalies), and benchmark-design recommendations — score *where* agents measure alongside the equations they propose, use non-integer feature locations, and include simple space-filling baselines.

## Methodology

The paper builds a fully controlled closed-loop equation-discovery task. The synthetic ground truth is y = (V·x1)/(K+x1) · 1/(1+x2/J) + A·exp(−(x2−c)²/(2S²)) + ε with V=10, K=5, J=2, A=0.279, S≈0.149 and Gaussian noise ε~N(0, σ²), σ=0.08: y saturates in x1 ∈ [0,50], decays smoothly in x2 ∈ [0,10], ignores x3, and hides a narrow Gaussian peak along x2 centered at c. The peak region W = (c−0.35, c+0.35] covers 7% of the x2 range; at x1=40 the peak raises y by up to 31σ inside W while contributing <2σ anywhere outside, so the exact peak-free curve scores E = RMSE/σ = 3.9–4.8 and the solve threshold E ≤ 1.05 (evaluated on 4,096 fixed uniform random test points) is unreachable without finding the peak.

32 paired trials were constructed with peak centers drawn one from each of 32 equal-width bins across [2.5, 8.5] (23 W regions contain an integer x2, 9 lie strictly between integers); each trial starts from four random conditions (one per quarter of each input range, redrawn until none falls in W, each measured twice). Both agents — Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1-Sol, run at high reasoning effort with no code execution, calculator, or external tools — receive identical starting data and share the measurement-noise seed. Each of 12 rounds shows the agent all measurements so far plus up to 12 previously proposed equations ranked by BIC (= χ² + k·ln N, penalizing k adjustable parameters); the agent explains its reasoning, proposes up to three new equations (1–10 parameters each, with starting guesses and ranges), and selects two input conditions to measure (each measured twice). Equations are fitted by bounded nonlinear least squares with the agent's guesses plus three random restarts; invalid formulas are skipped (17% of GPT-6.1-Sol's proposals were invalid zero-parameter formulas vs 0.2% for Sonnet). After Round 12 the agent submits up to three final equations without new measurements. An additional 32 Sonnet-only trials (same stratified generation) give 64 Sonnet trials total for post-entry analysis. Baselines that fit no equations are random sampling over x2 (analytic entry probability 1−(1−0.07)^2k per round k) and interval bisection (midpoint of the widest unmeasured x2 interval, which presumes knowledge that x2 carries the peak). Statistics use exact binomial tests vs random sampling, exact McNemar tests for paired binary outcomes, Fisher exact for categorical recovery rates, Wilcoxon signed-rank for per-trial scores, with Wilson and bootstrap (10,000 trial-level resamples) confidence intervals.

## Results & Highlights

- **Peak-region entry (the headline negative result)**: by Round 12, interval bisection entered W in 32/32 trials (100%) and random sampling in 82% of trials, versus Claude Sonnet 5.5 in 19/32 (59%) and GPT-6.1-Sol in 1/32 (3%); both agents fell significantly below random sampling already by Round 6 (one-sided exact binomial p = 0.002 for Sonnet 5.5, p = 8×10⁻¹³ for GPT-6.1-Sol). Entry into the central half of W: 11/32 vs 0/32 (McNemar p = 0.001).
- **Solve rate on held-out test points (E = RMSE/σ ≤ 1.05)**: Sonnet 5.5 solved 7/32 trials (22%) vs 0/32 for GPT-6.1-Sol (exact McNemar p = 0.016); all of GPT-6.1-Sol's 32 finals were smooth peak-free curves, whose achievable error band is E = 3.9–4.8.
- **Disagreement-chasing explains the trap**: across the 31 shared trials, GPT-6.1-Sol's ratio of equation-disagreement at its chosen points to the maximum disagreement in the space had median 0.92 vs 0.48 for Sonnet (Wilcoxon p = 9×10⁻¹⁰), yet the top-three spread at GPT's chosen points was a median 0.68σ over 348 pre-entry rounds (1.25σ for Sonnet over 251 rounds) — below single-measurement noise; pre-entry disagreement sat at x2 ≤ 1 in 78%/64% of rounds and inside W in only 0.8%/2.0%.
- **Post-entry recovery is nearly deterministic given enough of the chain**: over 64 Sonnet trials, 0/32 trials that never entered W were solved; with entry, a ≥10σ signal appeared in 19/32 and produced a peak term in the top-ranked equation in 16/19 (only 2/13 sub-10σ trials solved); measuring ≥3 distinct x2 positions across W gave 10/10 solves (100%) vs 1/5 (20%) with two positions and 0/17 (0%) with one (Fisher p = 2×10⁻⁷); a ≥10σ signal by Round 9 gave 9/11 solves (82%) vs 0/8 for hits in Rounds 10–12 (p = 7×10⁻⁴).
- **Integer-stepping quirk quantified**: 78% (Sonnet) and 84% (GPT-6.1-Sol) of all 768 measured x2 values per agent were integers; GPT-6.1-Sol put 70% of its measurements at just x2 = 0 and x2 = 2, while Sonnet swept integers 0–10 — entering 19/23 (83%) of integer-containing peak regions but 0/9 regions lying strictly between integers.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is single-authored by Hu Cang of the Department of Developmental and Cell Biology at UC Irvine (cangh@uci.edu), an optical-imaging/microscopy group — hence the secondary physics.optics categorization — and is supported by NIH grant R01HG014004, with no competing interests declared. The experimental design is unusually careful for an LLM-agent evaluation: paired trials with identical starting data and shared noise seeds across the two agents; pre-specified thresholds (10σ anomaly strength, ≥5σ peak-term prominence, E ≤ 1.05) defined in Methods; exact nonparametric tests (binomial, McNemar, Fisher, Wilcoxon) with Wilson/bootstrap 95% CIs throughout; and honest accounting of protocol frictions (17% invalid equation proposals from GPT-6.1-Sol, 52 rounds with no new equations, exactly one unparseable reply across 1,248 steps). All system constants are disclosed, making the benchmark exactly reproducible in principle. No code or prompt release is mentioned, however, and the agent harness details (prompt wording) are summarized only briefly.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core theoretical point — that disagreement-based active learning fails under model misspecification when the data miss a localized feature, and that complexity penalties suppress the true hypothesis — is well established in the active-learning and Bayesian-design literature the paper itself cites (Sloman et al. 2022; Tang, Sloman & Kaski 2026). The genuine novelty is the clean empirical demonstration that *frontier LLM agents* inherit exactly this failure in a closed loop, plus the crisp decomposition of the recovery chain (10σ trigger → peak term → ≥3 positions → timing by Round 9).
- **Rigor**: Strong within its scope — paired seeds, exact tests, pre-registered-style thresholds, and mechanistic intermediate measurements (where the top-3 equations disagreed, round by round) rather than just end-point solve rates. The 100% bisection entry baseline is admittedly oracle-assisted (it is told which variable carries the peak), but random sampling needs no oracle and still beats both agents, so the headline claim stands.
- **Reproducibility**: The synthetic system is fully specified (all constants, W definition, noise, test protocol), and the shared-seed paired design is exemplary; but no prompt transcripts or code are stated to be released, and results for proprietary, fast-moving models (Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1-Sol at "high reasoning effort") will be hard to replicate exactly.
- **Prompt-harness confound**: agents received "only the three input ranges, the noise level, and the goal," with no code execution or tools. Real AI-scientist deployments let agents compute uncertainty maps or run space-filling designs; the demonstrated failure is therefore a property of *this* harness, and one cannot tell how much of GPT-6.1-Sol's 0/32 is "following disagreement" versus its separate competence problems in the same runs — 17% invalid (zero-parameter) equation proposals, 52 rounds proposing nothing, and 70% of measurements parked at x2 ∈ {0, 2}. n = 1 model per family also blocks generalization from "GPT-6.1-Sol chases disagreement" to model-family claims.
- **Scope of the evidence**: one synthetic 3-variable system, one hidden-feature type (a 1-D narrow Gaussian peak on one axis), one peak per trial, a fixed 12-round/2-point budget, and no code execution. The paper is upfront about this ("to isolate how hypotheses guide search") but the leap to "higher-dimensional and laboratory settings" in the closing paragraph is aspiration, not evidence.
- **Missing ablations**: no variation of prompt phrasing (e.g., telling the agent about space-filling design), number/diversity of starting points, round budget, peak width, or dimensionality; the integer-stepping artifact (9/32 peak regions strictly between integers, 0/9 entered) is nicely quantified but also means part of the reported failure is numeracy of LLMs under this harness rather than hypothesis-selection per se.
- **Red flags**: none serious — no overclaiming beyond the discussion's forward-looking paragraph; the abstract's "less often than random search" is fully supported.

## Strengths

- A maximally clean causal demonstration: the hidden feature is invisible in starting data (<1σ), unrepresentable in the BIC-winning model class until observed, and the failure mode follows from first principles that the paper verifies round-by-round (where the top-3 equations agreed vs disagreed).
- The paired-trial, shared-noise-seed design plus exact paired tests (McNemar, Wilcoxon) makes the between-agent comparison genuinely tight — the 0.92-vs-0.48 disagreement-following result across all 31 shared trials is a striking individual-difference result.
- Goes beyond "agents fail" to a constructive, quantified recipe for success: ≥10σ early anomaly + ≥3 positions across the feature + enough remaining rounds → 10/10 recovery, showing the bottleneck is exploration strategy, not equation revision, since current agents revise well once confronted with evidence.
- Actionable and self-aware recommendations for the emerging AI-scientist benchmark ecosystem (BoxingGym, NewtonBench, LLM-AutoSciLab): score measurement placement, avoid integer-aligned features, include space-filling baselines.
- The human-science parallel (confirmation bias, streetlight effect, Chamberlin's multiple working hypotheses) is drawn carefully, not just rhetorically.

## Limitations

- Single synthetic system with one narrow 1-D Gaussian peak; no test of broader feature types, multiple simultaneous anomalies, higher-dimensional inputs, or real laboratory data — external validity to "experimental science" is argued, not shown.
- Only two agents, one harness configuration each, no prompt or scaffolding ablations; the Sonnet-vs-GPT behavioral difference (broad integer sweep vs extreme concentration at x2 = 0 and 2) cannot be attributed to a general "disagreement-following" trait of either model family.
- The no-tools/no-code protocol isolates hypothesis-guided search but deliberately strips the safeguards real systems have (agents that could enumerate unmeasured gaps or compute space-filling designs); the practical severity of the blind spot in deployed AI scientists is therefore unknown.
- Fixed budget (12 rounds × 2 conditions) makes the timing result (hits after Round 9 are fatal) partly an artifact of that budget; the ≥10σ trigger threshold and 7%-of-range peak width are single operating points.
- 32 paired trials give adequate power for the large effects reported, but the sub-analyses (e.g., 2-positions bin, n=5) rest on small cells; no code or transcript release is mentioned.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM 驱动的自主科学家/闭合回路科学发现"这一研究方向：论文定量揭示了主动学习经典策略（在模型分歧最大处实验）与 BIC 简约性准则联合导致的探索盲区，并给出可操作的两阶段实验设计与基准设计建议，对构建或评估 AI Scientist agent 与科学发现基准均有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the blind spot persist if agents were given code execution or an explicit prompt suggesting space-filling/Latin-hypercube exploration before model discrimination — i.e., is the failure in the agents' inductive biases or in the harness's stripped-down protocol?
- Can the two-stage strategy (broad early coverage, then anomaly-focused follow-up) be implemented as a scaffold or reward signal and shown to close the gap to the 82%/100% baseline entry rates without hurting equation-fitting quality?
- Does the ranking of agents flip on tasks where the hidden structure is spread out rather than localized (broad non-Gaussian features), where disagreement-guided search should be near-optimal?
- How does the failure scale with dimensionality — does the probability of entering a hidden region collapse as the unmeasured-volume fraction grows, and can disagreement signals be regularized (e.g., ensemble-over-model-classes spread) to recover coverage?
- The ≥10σ anomaly threshold triggering hypothesis revision is a human-free operationalization of "surprise": is that threshold stable across noise models (heteroscedastic, heavy-tailed) and across agents, or does it reflect Sonnet 5.5's particular behavior?
