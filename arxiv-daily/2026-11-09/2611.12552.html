Why this matters

对任何做多智能体工作流优化或科学智能体的人都可直接借鉴。

Critical edge

A 12/18 vs 8/18 gap over 18 fixed-fixture attempts is suggestive, not established — no significance test is reported anywhere, and the GLM-5.3 “win” is a single attempt.

Methodology — distilled

Seven logical roles (analyzer, planner, coder, verifier, router, debugger, finalizer) wired through eight handoff interfaces execute a task as harness H=(P,C), or (P,C,M) with memory. The optimizer observes only public inputs, tool responses, and artifacts. Each adaptation round:

  1. Execute the current harness for reference evidence.
  2. Propose — independent scoped editors draft role-only, coordination-only (one route/contract), and memory-policy-only candidates; validators enforce schema, ownership, and evidence citations.
  3. Probe every candidate in isolated workspaces; held-out labels stay out of inputs and graph.
  4. Compose a successor only from already-probed options — no invented policies.
  5. Select among ≤4–5 outcomes via public checks; the official evaluator runs once, after final commitment.

Memory is a Mycelium-style evidence graph — six namespaces, typed provenance edges, immutable observations — whose contents are deliberately split from the policy that reads them. One round costs 3–5 team executions, charged to the same allowance as baselines.

Case study — BBBC002 cell counting (SAB #11): a path-search bug zeroes ten predictions (MAE 78.0). A role-only edit fixes it to MAE 6.17; adding one verifier→finalizer contract alone reaches MAE 11.8 — two orthogonal repair mechanisms for the same failure.

Note: the GPT-6-Astra headline cohort actually runs a two-agent worker–reviewer team covering the seven roles, not the full harness described in the method section.

Strengths

  • Contents-vs-policy split for memory, operationalized by a fixed-memory control, cleanly attributes gains to adapting the use of evidence, not merely storing it.
  • Leak-proofness is engineered, not asserted — official scores reserved for final evaluation, isolated probe workspaces, held-out labels excluded from graph and inputs.
  • Tested-option-only composition plus execution reuse with integrity checks disciplines the combinatorial blowup of harness search.
  • Honest scoping — the meta-optimizer never rewrites itself; edits are task-local; validators check compliance, not performance.
  • Monotone ordering across three benchmarks under matched compute; a rare trace-level case study of orthogonal repairs.

Limitations

  • The closed loop is closed only in simulation — data replay and a digital twin, no new wet-lab experiments.
  • Only two model cohorts; GLM-5.3 sits near the floor (2/18), so nothing is learned about mid-strength backbones.
  • Small, untested samples: 18 / 38 / 20 attempts, with 64/102 SAB tasks and most of AppWorld excluded.
  • No cross-task transfer attempted or measured — “does it compound?” stays open.
  • Real overhead — 3–5 full team executions per round — with no cost-effectiveness analysis.
  • Memory-policy space explored by a single LLM editor call per round, with no exploration strategy.

Questions for further exploration

  • Do task-local harnesses or reflector lessons transfer across tasks — can a cross-task library compound without leaking held-out information?
  • Why does the framework barely help GLM-5.3 (2/18 vs 1/18) — editor quality, probe fidelity, or a base team that can’t execute any harness well?
  • How do results scale with the number of outer rounds — does the 2-round budget leave most achievable adaptation on the table?
  • Can selection-time public probes be calibrated against official outcomes, so probe-based selection is provably correlated with final evaluation?
  • What happens with real wet-lab feedback latency and noise, where a bad probe cannot be undone?
  • Does the memory-policy editor converge to stable policies, or oscillate (e.g., retrieval limit swinging 0 ↔ 12) within a task?
