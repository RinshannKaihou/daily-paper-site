> **一句话总结：** 该文证明批量推理引起的 logit 扰动严格按浮点精度的尾数位数缩放（bf16/fp16 = 8.00×，fp16/fp32 = 8192×），并通过对模型自身 top-1/top-2 logit 间隙施加铰链惩罚（margin 设为实测噪声 99.9 分位数的两倍）进行微调，在 GSM8K 上将 token 翻转减少 35%、序列发散率从 53.8% 降至 38.1%，代价是 1.3–2.9 个准确率点；但训练无法移动最坏情况——确定性验证器的触发阈值 τ100 保持不变。

> **TL;DR:** Greedy decoding at temperature 0 is not batch-invariant: the same prompt can flip tokens depending on batch composition. This paper first characterizes the logit perturbation across six model families and three precisions (it scales exactly with representable precision — 8.00× bf16/fp16, 8192× fp16/fp32, matching mantissa-bit predictions — and batch invariance occurs accidentally at certain tensor shapes, e.g. zero flips at sequence length 512), then fine-tunes with a hinge penalty on the model's own top-1/top-2 logit gap, margin m = 2·Q0.999 calibrated from measured noise. On GSM8K with Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct vs. a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baseline, token flips drop 35% (197 → 128 mismatched steps) and sequence divergence falls from 53.8% to 38.1% (p = 0.004), at a cost of 2.88 accuracy points (35.94% → 33.06%). Crucially, the method reduces flip frequency but not the worst case: the gap threshold τ100 for full determinism does not improve, so margin training does not compose with worst-case verification.

## 核心贡献

- **扰动表征（测量贡献）**：在六个模型家族、三种数值精度、两种 GPU 架构（NVIDIA Turing/Ada）及 Apple Metal 上系统刻画批量引起的 logit 扰动，发现扰动尺度严格按可表示精度缩放——bf16/fp16 与 fp16/fp32 的 ulp 网格步长比值分别为 8.00× 和 8192.0×，与尾数位数（7/10/23 位）的 2^3、2^13 预测完全一致；用各自 ulp 归一化后 σ/ulp ≈ 1.35，分布跨精度一致。
- **"意外批量不变性"的发现**：批量不变性会在特定张量形状上偶然出现——同一模型在序列长度 512 时对所有测试批量完全不变（零扰动），在 320/384 时仅 B=32 不变；因此任何已发表的翻转率若不注明序列长度和批量大小就是不可解释的。
- **校准式 margin 训练方法**：在交叉熵之上对模型自身 top-1/top-2 logit 间隙施加铰链惩罚 L = L_CE + λ·max(0, m − g)，margin 不经调参、直接取实测噪声分位数的两倍 m = 2·Q0.999；效率（每单位困惑度代价的脆弱性下降）恰在该推导值处达到峰值，并在四个模型家族的六个模型上迁移（预注册预测 p ≈ 0.002）。
- **诚实的负结果（七项被否证假设）**：包括一个在六个模型上预注册成功、随后被第七个模型（DistilGPT-2 与 GPT-Neo-125M 共享同一诊断值 Q/g = 0.67 却结果相反）否证的"模型可用性诊断"；以及最重要的机理结论——margin 训练降低翻转频率但不改变最坏情况间隙（存活翻转的中位间隙在所有 arm 中均为 0.125，Q0.999 训练前后不变）。

## Key Contributions

- **Perturbation characterization across 6 model families, 3 precisions, and 2 GPU architectures**: the perturbation to the top-1/top-2 logit gap scales exactly with representable precision. Measured grid-step (ulp) ratios are 8.00× for bf16/fp16 and 8192.0× for fp16/fp32, matching the 2^3 and 2^13 predictions from the 7/10/23 mantissa bits; after ulp normalization σ/ulp is 1.35 (fp16) and 1.34 (bf16), i.e. the same distribution. Deployment precisions are "quantisation-dominated" (about half of perturbations round to exactly zero); fp32 is "accumulation-dominated" (Q0.999/ulp = 55) and produced 0 flips in 32,640 steps.
- **Accidental batch invariance at specific tensor shapes**: for Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct in bf16, sequence length 512 gives exactly 0% non-zero gap perturbations at every batch size tested, while 256/288 give ~54–56%; at lengths 320/384 the model is invariant at B=32 but not B=8/16. Any reported flip rate is meaningless without its sequence length and batch size.
- **A training-time margin objective calibrated from measured hardware noise**: L = L_CE + λ·max(0, m − (ℓ(1) − ℓ(2))) with m = 2·Q0.999 derived (not tuned) from a single profiling pass. Efficiency (fragility reduction per unit perplexity cost) peaks exactly at m = 2·Q0.999, and the peak location transfers across four model families under pre-registered prediction (6 correct predictions, p ≈ 0.002).
- **Seven rejected hypotheses reported with the evidence that killed them**, including a per-model viability diagnostic (Q/g ratio) that survived pre-registration on six models and was refuted by the seventh: GPT-Neo-125M and DistilGPT-2 share the identical diagnostic value Q/g = 0.67 yet one peaks at ratio 2 and the other fails.
- **The mechanistic boundary of the method**: margin training reduces how often flips occur but not the worst-case gap at which they can occur — the hinge is a soft penalty and the hardware's largest perturbation is unchanged by training, so margin training and worst-case (MarginGate-style) verification do not compose.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in two stages: measurement, then a training intervention calibrated by that measurement.

**Characterization protocol.** For a request q, the authors define the top-1/top-2 logit gap g_t = ℓ(1) − ℓ(2) at each decode step and the perturbation δ_t induced by running q inside a batch of size B instead of alone. The protocol runs N fixed-length sequences alone (recording top-5 logits and token IDs), then re-runs them alone (an identity control, which returned exactly zero flips in every valid run) and inside a batch, comparing logits at the token IDs from the first pass. They test nine candidate predictors of the perturbation scale σ (batch size, sequence position, activation norm, top-1 logit magnitude, token index, attention entropy, full-distribution entropy, near-tie count, and more) — none exceeds 1.3× variation; attention entropy is the cleanest null (ratio 0.94, ρ = +0.006, p = 0.39 over 24,480 samples), so σ is treated as a constant for a given model/precision/hardware.

**Training objective.** A hinge penalty on the model's own top-1/top-2 gap is added to cross-entropy: L = L_CE + λ·max(0, m − (ℓ(1) − ℓ(2))), with both terms masked to response tokens for instruction-tuned models. Two design choices are defended empirically: (1) hinging on the model's own top two rather than the label token vs. top competitor — the label-based variant moved the median gap from 1.125 to 0.625 and raised the fragile-step fraction to 35.2%, the opposite of the intent; (2) worst-case subtraction rather than noise injection — 49% of measured perturbations are exactly zero, so sampling from the distribution delivers no learning signal on roughly half of all training steps. The margin is calibrated so the correct token survives the worst case where the leader falls by Q0.999 while the runner-up rises by Q0.999, giving m = 2·Q0.999. Profiling is done in prefill (thousands of decision points per forward pass) and transfers to decode: Q0.999 = 0.2500 under both regimes for the tested model.

**Experimental setup.** Training experiments are restricted to models below 0.5B parameters (full fine-tuning with fp32 master weights + AdamW needs 16 bytes/parameter against a 22GB GPU); characterization extends to 7B. Hardware: NVIDIA L4 (Ada), with earlier runs on a T4 (Turing, noted because it lacks native bf16); Apple M-series under PyTorch MPS for cross-platform checks. Characterization data is WikiText-2; the systems experiment fine-tunes and evaluates Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on GSM8K with MBPP as an out-of-domain check. Training: 200 optimizer steps, effective batch 16, lr 1e-5 with linear decay, gradient clipping 1.0, λ = 1, fp32 master weights with bf16 autocast; evaluation in bf16 (deployment precision). Metrics: fragile-step fraction (primary — tens of thousands of steps, tight CIs), flip rate (supporting — only ~10^2 events), sequence-level divergence, verifier trigger rate at threshold τ, and GSM8K exact match on the full 1,319-question test set. Statistics use bootstrap over sequences/questions (not tokens) and McNemar's exact test for paired arm comparisons; the authors explicitly correct an earlier analysis that treated arms as independent.

## Results & Highlights

- **Precision scaling is exact (Qwen2.5-0.5B, L4, 32,640 prefill steps, B=1 vs B=32)**: measured grid steps are 0.0000010 (fp32), 0.0078125 (fp16), 0.0625 (bf16) — ratios of 8.00× (bf16/fp16) and 8192.0× (fp16/fp32), matching mantissa-bit predictions 2^3 and 2^13. Flips: 0 (fp32), 45 (fp16), 320 (bf16); zero-perturbation fractions 94.8% / 50.5% / 50.1%.
- **Risk is concentrated in the lowest gap bin (Qwen2.5-0.5B, fp16, B=1 vs B=32, 32,640 steps)**: 62 flips (0.19%) against an identity control of exactly zero; every flip occurred in the gap bin [0, 0.05), which holds only 4.3% of decision steps, consistent with the largest observed perturbation of 0.086.
- **Main systems result (GSM8K, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, m = 2·Q0.999 = 1.5, paired McNemar; divergence on n = 256 sequences, accuracy on n = 1,319)**: sequence divergence falls from 53.8% (SFT baseline) to 38.1% (margin arm), a 29% relative reduction at matched output length (119 vs 121 tokens), p = 0.004; mismatched steps fall from 197 to 128 (35% reduction); GSM8K accuracy drops from 35.94% to 33.06% (−2.88 points, p = 0.023).
- **Accuracy cost is monotone in the margin (GSM8K full test set)**: 0.5Q → 34.65% (−1.29, p = 0.326); 1.0Q → 33.51% (−2.43, p = 0.058); 2.0Q → 33.06% (−2.88, p = 0.023), all against the 35.94% SFT baseline.
- **Efficiency peaks at the derived margin (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 65,280 evaluation steps, two seeds)**: fragility reduction per unit perplexity cost peaks at m = 0.75 = 2·Q0.999 (efficiency 9.39; 20.5% fragility reduction at 2.2% perplexity cost), versus 6.85 at m = Q0.999 and 1.61 at m = 3.0 (where perplexity cost reaches 39.5%).
- **The worst case does not move**: median gap at surviving flips is 0.125 in every arm and the 95th percentile is flat; Q0.999 remains 0.75 before and after training; τ100 (threshold for full sequence determinism) stays at 0.75 for SFT and the 2.0Q arm, with verified-step fractions of 8.00% vs 6.51% (+18.5%, but one flip in the 1.0Q arm pushed its τ100 verification cost to 10.02%, i.e. −25.2%, so the authors make no claim here).
- **Supervised fine-tuning alone is a strong baseline**: ordinary in-domain fine-tuning with no margin term reduced flips by 22% while improving perplexity by 26% — so all method comparisons are against SFT, not the base model.
- **Accidental batch invariance (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, bf16, L4)**: sequence length 512 yields 0.0% non-zero gap perturbations at every batch size tested (B=8/16/32), while lengths 256/288 yield ~54–56% — a practitioner measuring at 512 would conclude, wrongly, that the problem does not exist in deployment.
- **Cross-platform**: the same operations that produce a 0.19% flip rate on CUDA are bit-identical under Apple Metal (verified for real forward passes at hidden sizes 896/2048/4096 in fp16 and bf16).

## Authors & Credibility

The paper has two authors — Charan Teja Nagisettygari and Dr. Siva Raja Sindiramutty — both from the School of Computer Science at Taylor's University, Subang Jaya, Malaysia (the first author's email is a student address, suggesting a student-led project under faculty supervision). It is a 9-page IEEE conference-format paper. Credibility signals are unusually strong for a small-team paper: predictions for the cross-model margin study were printed before training (pre-registration); paired statistical tests (McNemar's exact) are used and an earlier unpaired analysis is explicitly corrected in the text; an identity control (running the same sequences twice) is mandatory in every measurement; the abstract itself leads with what the method cannot do; seven rejected hypotheses are reported with the falsifying evidence; and the authors claim a public GitHub repository with the measurement code, the full experiment log including failed attempts, and per-attempt raw results. The main credibility limits are scale (all training below 0.5B parameters on a single 22GB GPU) and a single-institution review context.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core idea — a margin/regularisation term on logits — is not new (the authors cite MCEL's margin-augmented cross-entropy for bit-error tolerance and analog foundation models' noise-injection training). What is genuinely new is (a) calibrating the margin from the *measured* batch-induced noise quantile rather than treating it as a hyperparameter, and (b) applying it to batch-induced autoregressive nondeterminism rather than bit errors or analog noise. The characterization results (exact precision scaling; accidental batch invariance at specific tensor shapes) are independently valuable and, to my knowledge, new observations.
- **Rigor**: High. Pre-registered predictions, identity controls, paired tests with correction of their own earlier mistake, seed-replicated margin sweeps (spread 0.02–0.27 points, so the efficiency peak is signal), and honest treatment of the underpowered flip-rate metric (reported as supporting evidence precisely because only ~10^2 events per experiment exist). The distinction between quantisation-dominated and accumulation-dominated perturbation regimes is a clean insight backed by the ulp normalization.
- **Reproducibility**: Good on paper — stdlib-free protocol, named hardware (L4/T4/M-series), public code and experiment log claimed. But the headline phenomenon is kernel-library- and architecture-specific: the authors themselves warn that the shapes at which accidental invariance appears are a property of one kernel library on one architecture for one model and are untested for stability. Results on Hopper/Blackwell or non-NVIDIA accelerators other than Metal are absent, and σ transferred between their two GPUs while the maximum did not (0.0859 vs 0.1328), so worst-case claims may not carry across architectures.
- **Significance**: The honest framing cuts both ways. The paper's own data show the method does not compose with worst-case verification (τ100 unchanged), benefits are in-domain only (fragility reduction 0.87× in-domain vs 0.95–0.97× out-of-domain; on GSM8K→MBPP every verifier trigger ratio lies between 0.97 and 1.02 and divergence is slightly worse), and cost is a real 1.3–2.9 accuracy points — you trade task quality for a 29–35% reduction in nondeterminism, while fp32 or batch-invariant kernels buy full determinism. The practical takeaway may be more the measurement warnings than the method.
- **Red flags**: (1) The systems evaluation is a single task (GSM8K) on a single sub-0.5B model; the authors admit no second in-domain reasoning task was run. (2) The accuracy-vs-margin result contradicts the prior n=160 result they cite, and the 1.0Q point sits at p = 0.058 — barely non-significant — so the "statistically significant from m = 1.0Q" framing leans on 2.0Q. (3) The τ100 column is decided by a single worst step (one flip at gap 0.875 moved the 1.0Q arm from improvement to −25.2%), which the authors acknowledge. (4) The (λ, m) space was never jointly tuned (sweeps at fixed λ = 1), so the accuracy frontier may be improvable. (5) The failed viability diagnostic means a practitioner cannot predict in advance whether their model family (GPT-2/Pythia lineage failed) will benefit — the authors' own "most important open problem".

## Strengths

- Turns a folklore annoyance (batch-induced temperature-0 nondeterminism) into a precisely characterized measurement object, with two crisp, portable findings: perturbation scales exactly with mantissa-derived representable precision, and flip rates are uninterpretable without sequence length and batch size because invariance arises accidentally at certain shapes.
- The margin is derived (m = 2·Q0.999) from a worst-case argument rather than tuned, and the efficiency peak empirically confirms the derivation and transfers across four model families under pre-registration.
- Exceptional experimental hygiene: identity controls everywhere, seed replication, paired McNemar tests, bootstrap over sequences not tokens, and an explicit correction of their own earlier overconfident analysis.
- Radical transparency about negative results: seven rejected hypotheses with the falsifying evidence, including the demise of their own pre-registered diagnostic, and a clear mechanistic account (soft hinge + unchanged hardware perturbation ⇒ worst case fixed) of why the method cannot deliver determinism.
- The SFT-baseline observation (in-domain fine-tuning alone cuts flips 22% and improves perplexity 26%) is the kind of free-lunch result practitioners can use immediately, and the evaluation-setting warning (base-model-on-prose trigger rates ~88% vs 18.56% for instruct models on reasoning benchmarks) protects the field from comparing incomparable numbers.

## Limitations

- All training experiments use models below 0.5B parameters (a 22GB GPU cannot fit full fine-tuning with fp32 master weights at larger scale); whether the margin frontier retains its shape at 7B is untested, and the 7B characterization used 4-bit weight loading, which alters the arithmetic.
- Single hardware family for CUDA results (NVIDIA Turing and Ada); no Hopper, Blackwell, or non-NVIDIA accelerator besides Apple Metal; σ transfers across their two GPUs but the maximum perturbation does not.
- Characterisation is prefill-dominant (prefill yields ~1000× more decision points per unit compute); Q0.999 was verified identical across regimes and fragility reduction transfers (0.87× prefill vs 0.80× decode), but the decode flip-count comparison was underpowered at their sample size.
- The systems evaluation is GSM8K-only; MBPP served only as an out-of-domain check and showed no transfer, so in-domain generality beyond one math-reasoning task is unestablished.
- The model-dependence of the method is unexplained — all three failures are GPT-2-lineage or Pythia models, and the two GPT-2 failures get *worse* at small margins before (if ever) improving — but this pattern cannot be converted into a usable predictor.
- The hinge weight λ was fixed at 1 for all margin sweeps; the joint (λ, m) space is unstudied and could shift the accuracy-per-determinism trade-off. Training schedules were short (200 optimizer steps), so long-run behavior of the Pareto ordering is unknown.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 推理可靠性与可复现性的研究而言，本文给出了批量非确定性这一实际部署痛点的系统性测量框架与低成本训练侧缓解手段（margin 正则化），其"扰动按浮点精度精确缩放、翻转率必须连同序列长度/批量一起报告"的结论对任何做 LLM 评测与推理服务的人都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why do GPT-2-lineage and Pythia models fail to benefit (and initially get worse) from margin training, while OPT/SmolLM2/Qwen models peak at the predicted ratio 2? Could unembedding-layer activation scale or parallel-attention/FFN architecture explain it?
- Does the efficiency peak at m = 2·Q0.999 survive at 1B–7B scale and on Hopper/Blackwell GPUs with different kernel libraries, where the accidental-batch-invariance shapes presumably differ?
- Can the joint (λ, m) space, or a curriculum on m, recover part of the 1.3–2.9 accuracy-point cost while keeping the 29–35% divergence reduction?
- Since 49% of perturbations are exactly zero and δ behaves as a small set of arithmetic paths rather than noise, could an analytic model of the kernel's reduction order replace the empirical Q0.999 profiling pass?
- Would combining margin training with selective verification (MarginGate) at thresholds below τ ≈ 2 — where trigger rates drop 14–19% — yield a net serving-cost win, given the two techniques do not compose at the worst-case threshold?
- Do the accidental batch-invariance shapes persist across cuBLAS/CUDA versions, and could serving systems deliberately pad to those shapes as a zero-cost determinism heuristic (with the caveat the authors raise about kernel-library dependence)?
