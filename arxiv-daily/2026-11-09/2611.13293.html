Why this matters

Long-context, high-concurrency LLM serving is bottlenecked by interconnect bandwidth and KV capacity — TKNP is a direct systems answer to that infrastructure problem.

Critical edge

Some of the 1.7× over DCP may be recoverable by a better DCP — vLLM couples DCP to a TP group whose dense collectives cross InfiniBand, a coupling the authors themselves flag.

Methodology — how TKNP works

TKNP targets the decode pool of a prefill–decode-disaggregated stack: an external prefill server writes each request's initial KV cache directly onto its assigned decode owner.

One balanced copy of model weights is sharded into contiguous layer blocks (~1/N per node, balanced by weight bytes). The weight-owning node acts as active root: it runs QKV/output projections and MLP for the full batch with intra-node TP8, preserving weight-read amortization. Meanwhile every node owns a subset of requests: it holds their complete KV cache for all layers and computes full attention locally — exploiting the fact that attention over KV is parameter-free. No partial-softmax merging crosses nodes; weights and KV never move.

  • Capacity-aware scheduling: each request goes to the eligible node with the lowest KV load; ownership is fixed until completion — no KV migration.
  • Dual-active-root microbatching: a second, shifted weight copy (≈2/N per node) gives every block two roots; two microbatches overlap one's attention with the other's dense phase, adding up to 11.1% throughput.
  • Eval setup: vLLM 0.19.0 + FlashAttention-3 + NCCL 2.27.5; 4–8 nodes of 8× H100 over 8×400 Gb/s InfiniBand; RULER (32K/64K/96K, 1,024 out tokens, batch 4–128) plus a mixed-length L-Eval + LongBench v1/v2 pool under Poisson and burst arrivals.

  • Why the split works: at batch 128/32K, attention is ~70% of single-layer decode latency; TP1→TP8 cuts it 7.6× (5.38→0.71 ms) but TP64 adds nothing (KV-head limit = 8), while a batch-128 MLP all-reduce costs 6.5× more at TP64 than TP8.
  • The motivation in one number: a TP8 node spends 454 GiB on FP8 weights, leaving 106 GiB for KV — batch 128 at 32K needs 2,016 GiB (19× one node).
  • Honest crossover: DP still wins below the capacity-crossover point (small batches, short contexts); under greedy decoding, TKNP's output tokens match DP exactly.

Strengths

  • Crisp, measured diagnosis: dense layers amortize weight reads and belong in one NVLink domain; attention cost grows with resident KV — backed by single-layer latency breakdowns (attention 22× growth batch 1→128 vs 1.24× for MLP).
  • Design follows the diagnosis; every mechanism justified with concrete tensor-shape communication math, not hand-waving.
  • Dense all-reduces stay on NVLink and attention needs no cross-node merging — a genuinely clean point in the design space.
  • Real-scale evaluation: 405B params, 64× H100, FP8, four baseline families at matched GPU counts, capacity-matched DCP comparison, greedy-decoding equivalence check.
  • Honest reporting of the DP-favorable low-concurrency regime and of microbatching's memory cost.

Limitations

  • Only pays off for long-context, high-concurrency decode; at small batches / short contexts, per-layer scatter/gather sync can outweigh the benefit.
  • Dual-root microbatching trades KV capacity (second copy, 2/N weights) and adds scheduling complexity for a ≤11.1% gain.
  • Dense (non-MoE) models only; requests whose KV exceeds one node still need DCP-style sharding; MoE + expert parallelism left to future work.
  • Single hardware point (H100 + InfiniBand); larger NVLink domains would rebalance the hierarchy the design leans on.
  • Prefix sharing disabled in all experiments; long-context accuracy not addressed (Nemotron runs beyond native context); no prefill-side or TTFT analysis.

Questions for further exploration

  1. How much of the 1.7× over DCP survives once DCP is decoupled from vLLM's TP-group coupling — is request-parallel attention intrinsically better, or just better-placed?
  2. How does the design behave with realistic prefix sharing (shared system prompts), and can assignment become prefix-aware to recover KV dedup?
  3. What happens on Ethernet-only or lower-bandwidth interconnects — where is the bandwidth knee for the context-length-independent scatter/gather volume?
  4. Can adaptive schedulers jointly weighing KV occupancy and attention cost capture the dual-root overlap without the second weight copy?
  5. Does the parameter-free-attention argument extend to MoE serving with expert parallelism, removing weight replication in designs like Helix?
  6. How do speculative decoding and multi-token verification interact with the per-layer scatter/gather path — can draft tokens ride the same transfers?
