> **一句话总结：** 这篇 141 页的理论论文证明：即使基座模型覆盖良好（C_base ≤ 6）、使用精确梯度，REINFORCE（PPO/GRPO 的基础）也会在奖励上升的同时发生"覆盖塌缩"——最优响应概率跌至 exp(−Θ(1/√ε))——导致收敛需要 exp(Ω(1/√ε)) 步；而周期性把 KL 参考策略重置为最新策略（形式化为 APMD 元算法）可以全程保持覆盖，使带参考重置的 PPO 变体（PPO-NoClip）在 softmax 线性策略测试床上以 Õ(C_base·B³/ε³) 梯度步达到 ε-最优（同一实例上 REINFORCE 需 exp(Ω(1/√ε)) 步），并首次为 Kimi K1.5/K2 式平方损失算法（KimiPO，Õ(ε^{-7/2}) 步）和 Rebel 的在线变体给出端到端全局收敛保证。

> **TL;DR:** From the Kempner Institute (Harvard), UIUC, UCSD, and Microsoft Research NYC, this paper builds a theory of RL post-training around one question: which algorithms learn near-optimal policies at a cost governed by the base model's response coverage C_base? The negative result (Theorem 2.1) is sharp: on a tabular softmax bandit with a single prompt and C_base ≤ 6, REINFORCE with exact gradients and any KL regularization to the fixed base model (any η ∈ (0,∞], any learning rate α ≤ 1/5, ε ∈ (0,1/64]) drives the optimal response's probability to exp(−Θ(1/√ε)) while reward still improves, so convergence needs exp(Ω(1/√ε)) gradient steps. The fix, formalized as Approximate Policy Mirror Descent (APMD), is to periodically reset the KL reference to the most recent policy and solve each strongly-regularized (η = Θ(1)) subproblem approximately. On softmax linear policies, this yields Õ(C_base·B³/ε³) gradient-step and sample bounds for a KL-regularized, reset-based PPO variant (vs. Best-of-N's O(C_base/ε) generations and REINFORCE's exponential lower bound), analogous statistical bounds for a clipped variant (PPO-Lite, which isolates clipping's role as variance control with thresholds tied to η via [e^{-2ηRmax}, e^{2ηRmax}]), and the first end-to-end global convergence guarantees for the squared-loss algorithms used to train Kimi models (KimiPO, mean baseline, Õ(ε^{-7/2}) steps) and for an on-policy Rebel variant (Õ(ε^{-3}) steps, unbiased and globally convex). Illustrative exact-gradient simulations (ε = 0.01): REINFORCE first reaches reward 0.99 at step 7,364 after a long coverage-collapse plateau, while APMD gets there at step 2,911 (reset every K = 100) or 520 (K = 10). The theory explains why DeepSeek/ProRL-style reference resets and Kimi-style squared losses work, and predicts that fixed-reference online DPO is exposed to the same collapse.

## 核心贡献

- **覆盖塌缩下界（Theorem 2.1）**：在仅一个 prompt、C_base ≤ 6 的表格 softmax 老虎机实例上，REINFORCE 即使拥有精确梯度、任意固定参考 KL 正则（η ∈ (0,∞]）、任意学习率 α ≤ 1/5，训练途中覆盖也会退化到 C_πt = exp(Θ(1/√ε))（最优响应概率指数级变小），使收敛步数 T = exp(Ω(1/√ε))。这是首个在排除随机噪声、参数共享、多步动态等干扰因素后，单独分离出"覆盖塌缩"这一失效模式的结果——而同一实例上 Best-of-N 只需 O(C_base/ε) 次采样即可找到最优响应。
- **失效根因诊断**：覆盖塌缩源于 RL 目标特有的非凹性——收敛分析中的 Łojasiewicz 型系数 ‖π*/πt‖∞ 随覆盖恶化而指数级膨胀，使策略可以"近乎驻点但高度次优"；固定参考的 KL 正则必须弱到 1/η = O(ε) 才允许实质改进，恰恰放任了这种偏离。
- **APMD 元算法与覆盖保持定理（Theorem 3.1 / 4.1）**：把参考策略周期性重置为最新策略 πt，每个外层迭代近似求解目标 π*_{η,t} ∝ πt·e^{ηr} 的 KL 正则子问题。只要内层达到 KL 容差 1/(64·C_base·T²·log(eT))（或平方损失的对应 MSE 容差），最终策略即满足镜像下降速率 J* − J(πT) ≤ log(2C_base)/(ηT)，且所有迭代都保持 πt(Y*_x|x) ≥ ½·π_base(Y*_x|x)。强正则（η = Θ(1)）让每个子问题好解，参考重置把局部改进累积成全局最优。
- **PPO/GRPO 变体的首个覆盖保持保证**：PPO-NoClip（KL 正则 + 重置、无裁剪，Algorithm 1）以 Õ(C_base·B³/ε³) 梯度步收敛——在下界实例（C_base·B³ = O(1)）上为 Õ(ε^{-3})，与 REINFORCE 的 exp(Ω(1/√ε)) 形成指数级差距；PPO-Lite（Algorithm 2，加入非对称裁剪 [1−τ−, 1+τ+]）在任意参数化下以 Õ(C_base·R_max³(log|Π|+log(1/δ))/ε³) 采样收敛，首次分离出裁剪的统计功效（控制重要权重的方差代价，而非改善优化本身），并给出裁剪区间应随正则强度收紧的定量处方 [e^{-2ηRmax}, e^{2ηRmax}]。
- **Kimi 式平方损失算法与 Rebel 的全局收敛（首个端到端保证）**：基于恒等式 (1/η)log(π*/πt) = r − b*_t(x)，KimiPO（均值基线，即 Kimi K1.5/K2 的目标，G = 1 个额外响应即可）以 Õ(C_base^{5/4}·B^{7/2}/ε^{7/2}) 步/样本收敛；KimiPO-Norm（归一化基线，G = Θ̃(1/ε)，η = 1/(3R_max+4D⋆)）以 Õ(ε^{-3}) 梯度步收敛但多花 1/√ε 的采样；Rebel-OnPolicy（配对响应消去基线，无偏且目标全局凸）以 Õ(C_base·B³/ε³) 步收敛，样本量对 d 和 1/δ 仅对数依赖。
- **统一视角与实践处方**：DeepSeek-R1/ProRL 式参考重置 PPO/GRPO、Kimi K1.5/K2、OAPL、KARL、Rebel-OnPolicy 都是 APMD 实例；而固定参考的 online DPO、A*-PO、SPO 因解与固定参考 KL 目标相同，理论上暴露于同一覆盖塌缩。处方：内层应视为对参考的局部改进，正则宜强、内层宜解到足够精度（而非常见的 1–4 步），裁剪阈值、内层步数、批大小应与 η 联合调参。

## Key Contributions

- **Coverage-collapse lower bound (Theorem 2.1).** On a single-prompt tabular softmax bandit with C_base ≤ 6 and R_max ≤ 1, REINFORCE with exact gradients, any KL regularization to the fixed base model (η ∈ (0,∞]), and any learning rate α ≤ 1/5 passes through an iterate whose coverage is C_πt = exp(Θ(1/√ε)), so ε-suboptimality requires T = Ω(C_πt/(α√ε)) = exp(Ω(1/√ε)) gradient steps. This is the first result isolating coverage collapse per se: no stochastic noise, no parameter sharing (tabular), no multi-step dynamics — while Best-of-N on the same instance needs only Θ(C_base/ε) generations.
- **Diagnosis of the failure.** Coverage collapse stems from the nonconcavity particular to the RL objective: the Łojasiewicz-type coefficient ‖π*/πt‖∞ in prior analyses explodes exponentially, so a policy can be near-stationary yet highly suboptimal. Fixed-reference KL regularization cannot help because it must be weak (1/η = O(ε)) to allow real improvement — exactly the freedom that lets coverage collapse.
- **APMD meta-algorithm and coverage-preservation theorems (Theorems 3.1 and 4.1).** Reset the KL reference to the most recent policy πt each outer iteration and approximately solve for π*_{η,t} ∝ πt·e^{ηr}. If each inner loop meets KL tolerance ε_in = 1/(64·C_base·T²·log(eT)) (or the analogous MSE criterion), the final policy achieves the mirror-descent rate J* − J(πT) ≤ log(2C_base)/(ηT), and every iterate preserves coverage: πt(Y*_x|x) ≥ ½·π_base(Y*_x|x) with high probability over prompts. Strong regularization (η = Θ(1), vs. η = Ω(1/ε) forced by a fixed reference) makes each subproblem easy; resets accumulate local improvements into global optimality.
- **First coverage-preserving guarantees for PPO/GRPO-style variants.** PPO-NoClip (KL + resets, no clipping; Algorithm 1) converges in Õ(C_base·B³/ε³) gradient steps — Õ(ε^{-3}) on the lower-bound instance where C_base·B³ = O(1), an exponential separation from REINFORCE's exp(Ω(1/√ε)). PPO-Lite (Algorithm 2, asymmetric clipping) converges with Õ(C_base·R_max³(log|ΠΘ|+log(1/δ))/ε³) samples for any parameterization, isolating for the first time a statistical benefit of clipping (bounding the cost of large importance weights) and prescribing clipping intervals [e^{-2ηRmax}, e^{2ηRmax}] that tighten with stronger regularization.
- **First end-to-end global convergence for Kimi-style algorithms and online Rebel.** Via the identity (1/η)·log(π*_{η,t}/πt) = r − b*_t(x): KimiPO (mean baseline, the Kimi K1.5/K2 objective, needs only G = 1 extra response) is coverage-preserving with Õ(C_base^{5/4}·B^{7/2}/ε^{7/2}) steps and samples; KimiPO-Norm (normalizer baseline, G = Θ̃(1/ε), η = 1/(3R_max + 4D⋆)) saves a 1/√ε factor in gradient steps at a 1/√ε cost in responses (Table 1); Rebel-OnPolicy (paired responses cancel the baseline; unbiased, globally convex quadratic objective) achieves Õ(C_base·B³/ε³) steps with only logarithmic dependence of samples on d and 1/δ.
- **A unifying lens with actionable design rules.** DeepSeek/ProRL-style reset-based PPO/GRPO, Kimi's squared losses, OAPL, KARL, and Rebel-OnPolicy are all APMD instances, while fixed-reference methods (online DPO, A*-PO, SPO) provably inherit the collapse-prone fixed-reference objective. Practical guidance: view the inner loop as a strongly-regularized local improvement step solved to sufficient accuracy (common 1–4-step recipes under-solve it), and co-tune clipping thresholds, inner steps, and batch size with η.

## Methodology

The paper works in a contextual-bandit abstraction of one-shot RL post-training: a policy πθ maps prompts x to distributions over complete responses y; J(π) = E[r(x,y)]; quality of the base model is measured by response coverage C_π = E_x[1/π(Y*_x|x)], the expected number of generations to hit an optimal response. An algorithm is **coverage-preserving** if, for every ε and δ, it returns J* − J(π) ≤ ε with probability ≥ 1−δ using both gradient steps and samples bounded by poly(C_base, comp(ΠΘ), 1/ε, log(1/δ)) — matching what Best-of-N (Θ(C_base/ε) generations) and filtered SFT already achieve, a bar no prior policy-optimization guarantee met.

**Lower bound (Section 2, Appendix C).** The construction is a single prompt with m + 2 responses (m = ⌈1/ε⌉ zero-reward responses, r(1) = 1, r(2) = 1 − 3ε), base model π_base(1) = 1/6, π_base(2) = 1/3, so C_base = 6. A careful analysis of exact gradient ascent on the KL-regularized objective shows reward improves monotonically while the probability of response 1 first collapses, then recovers only after exponentially many steps; the proof tracks the two-dimensional logit dynamics exactly.

**APMD (Section 3.1).** Each outer iteration resets the reference to πt and targets π*_{η,t}(y|x) ∝ πt(y|x)e^{ηr(x,y)}. Exact mirror descent iterates would satisfy πt ∝ π_base·e^{tηr} with J* − J(πt) ≤ log(C_base)/(ηt) and monotone coverage improvement. Theorem 3.1 shows these properties survive approximation error: if each subproblem is solved to KL tolerance 1/(64·C_base·T²·log(eT)), the final policy keeps the mirror-descent rate log(2C_base)/(ηT) and all iterates retain at least half the base model's optimal-response probability. Theorem F.1 extends this to general comparator policies.

**Instantiations.** (i) PPO-NoClip: K steps of full-batch gradient ascent on the importance-weighted KL-regularized objective over a pre-collected batch from πt (stylized PPO/GRPO without advantage estimation); under Assumption 2.1 (softmax linear policies over frozen features φ with linear reward r = φᵀθ⋆, B = B_φ‖θ⋆‖), strong regularization η = 1/B keeps all policy ratios bounded by e^{8}, e^{4} throughout the inner loop, giving the Õ(ε^{-3}) result. (ii) PPO-Lite: clipping with asymmetric thresholds; the statistical analysis applies to any parameterization given an ε_opt-optimal inner solve, and the inner loop is deliberately left abstract (the clipped objective is non-smooth). (iii) Squared-loss route (Section 4): fitting centered log-ratios (1/η)log(πθ/πt) to shifted rewards r − b*_t via least squares, with an MSE criterion (Theorem 4.1) replacing the KL criterion; the Kimi-style mean baseline introduces a bias that decomposes as L^Kimi = MSE + (1/η²)E[D_KL(πt‖π)²] and inflates MSE by O(η⁴B⁶), forcing stronger regularization (η = Θ̃(√ε)) but yielding a globally convex objective; the normalizer baseline trades this for more responses; paired differences (Rebel-OnPolicy) cancel b*_t exactly, giving an unbiased, globally convex quadratic. Illustrative exact-gradient simulations (Figures 2–3, Appendix C.1) visualize collapse, the plateau, and the APMD landscape on the modified lower-bound instance (ε = 0.01, α = 1, 10,000-step budget).

## Results & Highlights

All results are theorem-level guarantees on the softmax-linear testbed (Assumption 2.1) plus exact-gradient simulations; there are no LLM benchmark experiments.

- **Exponential lower bound vs. polynomial upper bound (core separation).** On the tabular instance of Theorem 2.1 with C_base = 6, REINFORCE with exact gradients requires T = exp(Ω(1/√ε)) gradient steps to reach ε-suboptimality (intermediate coverage C_πt = exp(Θ(1/√ε))), for any η ∈ (0,∞] and α ≤ 1/5. On the same instance, PPO-NoClip (Corollary 3.1, η = 1/B) needs only Nstep = Õ(ε^{-3}) gradient steps and Nsample = Õ((d + log(1/δ))/ε³) samples — an exponential improvement. For calibration, Best-of-N attains an optimal response with Θ(C_base/ε) generations per prompt.
- **PPO-Lite (clipping) sample complexity (Corollary 3.2).** With η = 1/R_max and clipping interval [e^{-2ηRmax}, e^{2ηRmax}], any parameterization (finite ΠΘ, π*_{η,t} ∈ ΠΘ) gives Nsample = Õ(C_base·R_max³·(log|ΠΘ| + log(1/δ))/ε³); clipping bounds the batch size independently of how large the importance ratios become during training — a statistical role not previously isolated in PPO theory.
- **KimiPO — first global convergence for a Kimi-style algorithm (Corollary 4.1).** With G = 1 baseline response and η = Θ̃(√ε): Nstep = Õ(C_base^{5/4}·B^{7/2}/ε^{7/2}), Nsample = Õ(C_base^{5/4}·B^{7/2}·(d + log(1/δ))/ε^{7/2}). The worse ε-exponent versus PPO-NoClip's ε^{-3} (7/2 vs. 3) is attributed precisely to the mean-baseline bias O(η⁴B⁶) forcing stronger regularization and more outer iterations T.
- **Rebel-OnPolicy (Corollary 4.2).** With η = log(2C_base)/(2B): Nstep = Nsample = Õ(C_base·B³/ε³), with sample complexity depending only logarithmically on dimension d and 1/δ; its objective equals exactly 2·MSE_t and every empirical objective is a globally convex quadratic in θ minimized by the APMD target.
- **Baseline trade-off quantified (Table 1, Corollary K.1).** KimiPO-Norm with η = 1/(3R_max + 4D⋆) and G = Θ̃(1/ε) achieves Õ(C_base·D⋆²(R_max + D⋆)/ε³) gradient steps but Õ(C_base·D⋆⁴(d + log(1/δ))/ε⁴) total responses: versus KimiPO it saves a 1/√ε factor in gradient steps and pays a 1/√ε factor in generations (which can be collected in parallel).
- **Simulation (exact gradients, ε = 0.01, α = 1, 10,000 steps; Appendix C.1).** REINFORCE (η ≈ 23.645, the strongest fixed-reference regularization still permitting 0.9ε-suboptimality) first reaches reward 0.99 at step 7,364 after a prolonged coverage-collapse plateau; APMD/PPO-NoClip (η ≈ 0.781) reaches reward 0.99 at step 2,911 with a reference reset every K = 100 steps, and at step 520 with K = 10 (best of the K ∈ {1,5,10,50,100} sweep).
- **Coverage-preservation guarantee (Theorem 3.1).** Under the stated inner tolerance, all iterates satisfy πt(Y*_x|x) ≥ ½·π_base(Y*_x|x) with probability ≥ 1 − 1/(2√log(eT)) over prompts, while final suboptimality obeys the mirror-descent rate log(2C_base)/(ηT).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High. The coverage-preserving criterion is a new and discerning yardstick that no prior policy-gradient guarantee meets; Theorem 2.1 is the first lower bound isolating coverage collapse with exact gradients and tabular parameters (prior failures involved noise, parameter interference, or multi-step dynamics); Corollary 4.1 is the first end-to-end global convergence guarantee for any Kimi-style algorithm; the statistical role of clipping isolated in Corollary 3.2 (variance control of importance weights, not optimization improvement) had not been isolated before.
- **Rigor:** Very high. Precise theorem statements with explicit constants, a clean lower-bound construction whose dynamics are tracked exactly, a meta-theorem (Theorem 3.1) that cleanly separates the "what" (inner KL tolerance) from the "how" (per-algorithm inner loops), and 13 appendices of proofs. The informal main-text corollaries are backed by fully explicit versions (e.g., Corollary G.1 specifies η, T, α, K, N numerically).
- **Reproducibility:** For a theory paper, good: algorithms are given as pseudocode and the simulations are described with exact hyperparameters (η ≈ 23.64513 vs. 0.78087, α ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1} sweep, K sweep), making Figures 2–3 reproducible. No code release is mentioned, and the theorems themselves are the artifact to verify.
- **Significance:** Potentially large for methodology: it gives a principled explanation for why DeepSeek/ProRL reset the KL reference and why Kimi's squared loss with an updated denominator works, plus concrete prescriptions (strong η = Θ(1) regularization with resets; solve inner loops to sufficient accuracy rather than the common 1–4 steps; co-tune clip thresholds with η). The prediction that fixed-reference online DPO shares REINFORCE's collapse-prone objective is falsifiable and testable.
- **Red flags / honest weaknesses:** (1) The stylized algorithms drop key components of practice — the PPO/GRPO variants use observed rewards rather than advantage estimates, and KimiPO differs from the actual Kimi K2 recipe in replacing the Muon optimizer with gradient descent and using separate responses for the baseline — so the guarantees cover simplified cousins, not the deployed methods. (2) The end-to-end results require Assumption 2.1: softmax linear policies over frozen features with a reward linear in the same features; real transformers with token-level parameter sharing are out of scope, a limitation the authors acknowledge. (3) The lower bound is a worst-case single-prompt tabular construction (m = ⌈1/ε⌉ near-duplicate zero-reward responses with a deliberately small 3ε reward gap); the paper offers no evidence of how frequently coverage collapse occurs in real large-scale RLVR runs — it cites the qualitative ProRL observation that a fixed-reference KL penalty can dominate the loss, which is suggestive but not a demonstration of collapse. (4) Rates are asymptotic with heavy constants — e^{Θ(1)} prefactors, ε^{-7/2} and ε^{-3} exponents, and T = Θ̃(B/ε) outer iterations — so the polynomial-vs-exponential separation is the meaningful content, not the specific rates. (5) PPO-Lite's inner optimization loop is left abstract (non-smooth objective at clip thresholds), and requires realizability (π*_{η,t} ∈ ΠΘ) plus a finite class (relaxed to covering numbers). (6) No experiments on any real LLM or benchmark — Figures 2–3 are exact-gradient illustrations on the authors' own construction. None of these invalidate the core contributions; they bound the strength of the practical claims.

## Strengths

- Identifies and cleanly isolates a real failure mode (coverage collapse) that survives the most favorable assumptions (exact gradients, tabular parameters, good base coverage, KL regularization allowed), making it an intrinsic property of the RL objective rather than an implementation artifact.
- The APMD framing is genuinely unifying: reset-based PPO/GRPO (DeepSeek, ProRL), Kimi-style squared losses, OAPL/KARL, and Rebel-OnPolicy all become instances of one meta-algorithm, while fixed-reference methods (online DPO, A*-PO, SPO) are placed on the wrong side of the lower bound — a sharp dividing line.
- Separates the two roles practitioners conflate: the reference policy as an anchor for optimization (central) versus its role in importance weighting/batch reuse (secondary), and shows clipping is about statistical cost control, complementing rather than replacing KL regularization.
- Quantifies design trade-offs precisely: mean vs. normalizer baseline (1/√ε steps vs. 1/√ε samples, Table 1); stronger regularization buys an easier inner problem at the price of more outer iterations (Eq. 9); clipping intervals should shrink as η grows.
- Exceptional transparency and rigor: explicit constants, exact simulation hyperparameters, honest limitations section, and an AI-usage disclosure statement.

## Limitations

- All end-to-end guarantees are restricted to softmax linear policies over frozen features with rewards linear in those features (Assumption 2.1); nonlinear transformer parameterizations, token-level sequence structure, and variable-length responses are out of scope, and the authors flag that full-response policy ratios can be large in practice.
- The one-shot contextual-bandit abstraction ignores multi-turn/agentic RL, credit assignment across tokens, and advantage estimation (the stylized PPO variants use raw rewards).
- The lower bound is worst-case; the paper provides no empirical measurement of coverage collapse in production-scale post-training runs, only an illustrative simulation on its own construction.
- PPO-Lite's analysis assumes the inner loop solves the (non-smooth) clipped objective to a prescribed tolerance without analyzing how, and assumes realizability of the per-round target.
- Guarantees are about amplifying capabilities the base model already covers (C_base); the theory is silent on genuinely expanding capability beyond base-model coverage, which the authors identify as an important open question.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM 后训练/RLHF/RLVR 方法论及其理论基础"这一研究方向：论文为 PPO/GRPO 的 KL 正则与参考重置（DeepSeek-R1、ProRL 的做法）以及 Kimi K1.5/K2 的平方损失目标给出了统一收敛理论，对设计后训练算法（正则强度、内层步数、裁剪阈值的联合调参）有可操作的指导意义。若你关注后训练的理论分析或复现/改进这些训练配方，这是一篇值得精读的工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does coverage collapse measurably occur in production RLVR runs? One could track the probability mass on optimal (verifier-passing) responses and the empirical coverage C_πt across a DeepSeek-R1- or Kimi-style run and check for the reward-plateau/coverage-collapse signature predicted by Theorem 2.1.
- How do advantage estimators (GAE, GRPO group-normalized advantages) interact with the APMD framework — do they act like the mean baseline (biasing but convexifying) or like paired differences (unbiased)?
- The theory prescribes clipping intervals [e^{-2ηRmax}, e^{2ηRmax}] tied to the KL strength; do practically used asymmetric clip ranges (DAPO-style, τ ≈ 0.2) sit near this prescription, and does co-tuning them with η measurably stabilize training?
- The simulation shows reset frequency matters a lot (K = 10 reaches reward 0.99 in 520 steps vs. 2,911 for K = 100); is there an adaptive schedule for K (or for η) that provably or empirically dominates fixed choices?
- Can the coverage-preserving guarantee be extended to token-level MDPs and multi-turn agentic settings, where response-level policy ratios explode and per-token KL budgets are the practical proxy?
- The paper predicts fixed-reference online DPO is collapse-prone; a controlled empirical comparison of online DPO vs. Rebel-OnPolicy from the same base model, tracking coverage dynamics, would directly test the theory.
