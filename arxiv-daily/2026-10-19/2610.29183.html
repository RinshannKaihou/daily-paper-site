03WHY THIS MATTERS

Inference-level fault tolerance for extreme-edge hardware — its checkpoint-granularity and recovery-I/O-scheduling lessons transfer directly to recoverable inference under ground-side power loss and preemption.

对地面断电/抢占场景下的可恢复推理系统有直接借鉴意义。

04CRITICAL EDGE

All three baselines are the authors’ re-implementations, ported onto GPU regimes they were never designed for — the headline 4.07× partly measures baseline-platform mismatch, not just algorithmic superiority.

— Critical Assessment · SONIC is CPU-only, HCache not open-sourced; no vLLM/SGLang-native baseline included
05METHODOLOGY

Two coordinated mechanisms

9,617 lines Python + 1,741 CUDA/C++ on HuggingFace transformers; single-request / single-GPU to match payload constraints.

  • Phase-aware checkpoints. Prefill is split into a G-layer × C-token grid of recoverable units; only KV plus boundary hidden states persist (G=4 → ~2.04×10³ token-layers lost, 8.96 GiB for Qwen3-VL 2B). ViT checkpoints layer-wise (bidirectional attention); decode persists KV in D-token batches.
  • Crash consistency. Contiguous per-layer records + append-ordered KV, checksummed commit footers written via pwritev2() on O_DIRECT (AWUPF-aligned) — half-written records are never accepted.
  • Recovery as scheduling. Completed blocks form a “staircase frontier”; precedence-constrained I/O+compute scheduling (NP-hard) reduces, via three dependency observations, to a layer-order subset DP in O(L·2^L) — optimal plan in <100 ms (Qwen3-VL 8B, G≥2).
  • Setup. Jetson Orin NX / AGX Orin mirroring the two payloads; Qwen3-VL 2B/4B/8B + MiniCPM-V 4.5 (W4A16); LRS-VQA, 32K/64K-token contexts; five interruption traces from Sat-1/Sat-2 telemetry + NASA Dellingr (36.4–121.7 s mean windows).

SSD endurance is a scarce resource on satellites — writes matter as much as time.

  • Recovery latency: 1.22×–6.97× faster across devices, models, and 20–80% prefill progress (AGX · Qwen3-VL 4B @ 20%: baselines need 6.85× / 6.36× / 4.40× as long).
  • Normal path stays cheap: uninterrupted inference within 5% of no-checkpoint latency, at 3.75–4.74% extra energy.
  • Optional KIVI / TurboQuant KV compression cuts decode-stage recovery latency by up to 5.02× — but its accuracy impact is never measured.
  • In orbit: 72 h · 2 satellites · 91 shutdowns (47 + 44) · 41 of 50 requests interrupted — all 50 completed within the 20-min timeout.
07STRENGTHS

What holds up

  • Problem grounded in first-party on-orbit telemetry — 48 s to thermal cutoff, worst-case once-per-minute FDIR reboots — not synthetic interruption models.
  • Clean design insight, quantified: KV = long-lived context, hidden states = short-lived boundary; the storage–recompute trade-off is measured, not hand-waved.
  • Elegant reduction: NP-hard two-machine scheduling → O(L·2^L) subset DP with a hard <100 ms bound that fits 36 s execution windows.
  • Five-axis evaluation (time, recovery, writes, DLWA, energy) plus real crash-consistency engineering.
  • Rare validation: 72 h on two actual satellites surviving 91 unplanned shutdowns.
08LIMITATIONS

Where it stops short

  • Single-request, single-GPU prototype — no batching or multi-tenant serving, the regime projected “space data centers” will face.
  • Baselines are author re-implementations running outside their design domains (SONIC: CPU/embedded; HCache: datacenter).
  • No task-accuracy evaluation after recovery — none at all for the 2-bit KIVI compressed path.
  • G/C calibration is per model–device pair on LRS-VQA; sensitivity to distribution shift unexplored.
  • Scale ceiling: ≤8B models, ≤64K contexts; months of flash wear-out extrapolated; radiation-induced storage corruption explicitly out of scope.
09PROVENANCE

Authors & credibility

  • PKU + BUPT groups with a long track record in mobile/edge AI systems and satellite computing (corresponding author: Daliang Xu).
  • Traces from their own two LEO satellites plus NASA’s public Dellingr; hardware mirrors the real payloads.
  • Claims carefully hedged; NASA Prithvi and ESA Phi-Sat-2 correctly credited as agency initiatives.
  • No code or artifact release; Sat-1/Sat-2 telemetry is private — the in-orbit results are effectively unauditable.
10OPEN QUESTIONS

Where this leads next

  1. Does compressed-KV recovery (2-bit KIVI / TurboQuant) preserve VQA accuracy bit-for-bit, or degrade outputs after repeated recovery cycles?
  2. Does the staircase-frontier DP generalize to concurrent multi-request serving on a shared payload GPU — and what does checkpoint isolation cost?
  3. Can the planner stay within 100 ms as layer-groups grow with 30B+ models, or does O(2^L) need an approximation?
  4. What is long-run SSD/eMMC endurance under months of checkpoint churn as devices age in radiation and thermal stress?
  5. Could orbital-state-aware shutdown prediction beat the fixed calibrated G/C policy — e.g., coarsening checkpoints ahead of predicted eclipses?
