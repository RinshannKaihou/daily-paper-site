Don't Skip the Baseline: A Controlled Comparison of Dynamic Expert Skipping in MoEs
Jakob Steimle, Thomas Elsken, Daniel Ehrhardt, Lukas Rinder, et al. (6 authors · TUM / ETH Zurich / Ulm / Tensordyne) · 2026-10-19
mixture-of-experts expert-skipping inference-efficiency controlled-benchmark-study multimodal-llm
Key metric — best dynamic gain
≤2.6pp

The largest edge any dynamic method (NAEE, occasionally Ada-K) holds over the static top-k baseline at matched measured Keff — and it appears only where the model has already lost 50–65% of its original capability.

Why this matters

Dynamic skipping's promised savings can silently vanish at runtime — calibrated Keff drifts up to 6× from measured. Matched-compute static controls should be mandatory.

⚠ Critical edge

Only coarse-grained k=8 MoEs of ~26–30B were tested. The verdict may not transfer to fine-grained MoEs (128+ experts), 100B+ models, or the long-context / agentic workloads where dynamic skipping might genuinely help — and Ada-K's reproduction is unvalidated.

Methodology 方法

  • Isolation by design. Post-hoc expert skipping is studied alone — no pruning, no rerouting — so every quality difference is attributable to the skip decision itself.
  • Calibration-free baselines. Native softmax top-k with statically reduced k, plus a per-modality variant (separate ktext, kvision) for vision-language models.
  • Four methods, by signal complexity. NAEE (cumulative routing-mass threshold, ~0.5 h calib) → DiEP (+CKA expert-redundancy, ~0.5 h) → MoDES (per-modality gate-probability × layer-importance thresholds, ~20 h) → Ada-K (PPO-trained hidden-state Allocator, ~2.5 h). All reimplemented — original releases are incomplete or absent — and validated against the original papers' reported operating points.
  • Honest compute accounting. Keff = mean routed experts per token (all layers), counted during evaluation rather than trusted from calibration; methods compared at matched measured values along full quality–compute frontiers (every tunable parameter swept, both realistic and best-case calibration).
  • Evaluation. Text: lm-eval on Qwen3-30B-A3B & Gemma4-26B-A4B (ARC-C, HellaSwag, GSM8K, HumanEval). Multimodal: LMMS-Eval on Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct over six VQA benchmarks. Runtime grounding: vLLM + GuideLLM on one A100 80GB.

Authors & credibility

TUM (Daniel Cremers' lab) · ETH Zurich · Ulm University · startup Tensordyne (first author) — a practical serving motivation.

  • Every reimplemented method validated against the original authors' numbers before benchmarking (NAEE: 5.3% vs 7.4% reported relative drop; MoDES TextVQA within 0.1–0.6 pts).
  • Standard harnesses (lm-eval, LMMS-Eval, vLLM); conclusions stress-tested on a held-out benchmark, modality, and model family.
  • Explicit AI-use disclosure; code with all reimplementations promised — but not yet public at preprint time.

  • Dense-model reality check. At Keff ≤ 2.5 on Gemma4-26B-A4B, the best skipping strategy is on average worse than dense Gemma4-E4B — below Keff≈3, just use a smaller dense model.
  • Task-type asymmetry. Multiple-choice benchmarks degrade smoothly toward random-guess floors; generative GSM8K collapses sharply below Keff≈2 — the safe operating point is dictated by the generative task, not the average.
  • Vision tokens are cheap. Even kvision=0 (vision tokens skip experts entirely) retains >80% quality (19.1% drop), while a modality-unaware k=1 loses >30%.
  • Runtime reality. Cutting Keff 8→1 on vLLM/A100 lifts throughput only 1.22× (batch 1) / 1.39× (batch 256) — attention and KV-cache overheads cap real gains far below the 8× compute cut.
  • Calibration cost asymmetry. Static: 0 h · NAEE/DiEP: ~0.5 h · Ada-K: ~2.5 h · MoDES: ~20 h per operating point.

Strengths 优点

  • Compares at matched measured compute — and surfaces the ≥6× calibrated-vs-measured drift as a previously underreported failure mode of threshold-based methods.
  • Full frontier sweeps of every method's tunable parameter, under both realistic and best-case calibration — no cherry-picked operating points.
  • Honest about the one genuine dynamic win: MoDES uniquely reaches Keff<1 (skipping the MoE block for some tokens); the MME-Cognition outlier is reported, not buried.
  • The modality decomposition (Fig. 6) mechanistically explains why MoDES only matches the static baseline — both concentrate skipping on vision tokens, while NAEE perversely allocates slightly more experts to vision than text.
  • Transfer validated on a held-out benchmark, modality, and model family, plus real serving-stack latency measurements.

Limitations 局限

  • Only coarse-grained k=8 MoEs of ~26–30B studied — fine-grained architectures (128+ experts, DeepSeek-style) and 100B+ models untested beyond GLM-4.6V.
  • Ada-K has no public code or checkpoints: its Allocator is an unvalidated RL reproduction, excluded from all multimodal evaluation.
  • Skipping evaluated in isolation — composition with pruning or rerouting/caching (as NAEE/DiEP ship) is out of scope; small-batch bandwidth-bound regimes not evaluated.
  • Narrow workloads: no long-context, instruction-following, or agentic generation; accuracy-style metrics only.
  • Small calibration sets (1024 samples), English/Chinese/code/math-centric; the headline k-recommendation was selected and validated on the same VQA family, with only MVBench/GLM-4.6V truly held out.

Questions for further exploration 开放问题

  • Does the static-k conclusion hold for fine-grained MoEs (k=4–8 of 128–512 experts) and 100B+ models with better-separated routing statistics?
  • Could a per-layer static k schedule — still calibration-light — capture most of MoDES's sub-1 Keff advantage?
  • Is calibrated-vs-measured Keff drift a general pathology of threshold-based skipping, and could a closed-loop runtime controller bound it?
  • Do dynamic methods gain an edge on long-context or agentic workloads, where per-token expert requirements plausibly vary more?
  • Would training-time exposure to variable-k routing shift the frontier in dynamic methods' favor?
  • How does the comparison change when skipping is composed with pruning or rerouting/caching in bandwidth-bound serving?
