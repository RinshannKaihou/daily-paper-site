> **一句话总结：** SQuAT 把 LLM 权重分解为方向（shape）和增益（gain），对两者分别使用直通估计器（STE）进行格向量量化感知训练，等价于在球面上做黎曼优化；配合分布式精确最近邻分配（12.8× 加速），首次把精确码本 VQ-QAT 扩展到十亿参数模型和 100 亿 token 训练预算，在 2.25 bpw 下全面超越 INT-QAT。

> **TL;DR:** SQuAT is a shape-gain vector-quantization-aware training (VQ-QAT) method that quantizes weight directions with an E8-derived spherical lattice codebook and gains with a chi-quantile scalar quantizer, applying *separate* straight-through estimators to the two quantizers — which provably induces tangent-space (Riemannian) updates on the sphere. Combined with a distributed exact nearest-neighbor scheme (62.5 → 4.9 s/it, a 12.8× speedup on LLaMA-3.2-1B), it scales exact VQ-QAT to 10B training tokens. On LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen3-4B-Instruct and base Qwen3-1.7B at 2.25 bpw, SQuAT beats the SEQ INT-QAT baseline on WikiText2 perplexity at every training budget (e.g. 13.85 vs 15.07 at 1B tokens on LLaMA-3.2-1B) with MMLU gains up to +7.9 points, reduces LLaMA-3.2-1B WikiText2 PPL from 17.90 (PTQ init) to 13.74 after 10B tokens, and beats the most recent VQ-QAT method LC-QAT on Qwen3-1.7B CSR (61.2 vs 59.8).

## 核心贡献

- **球面化的 VQ-QAT 训练规则**：将权重块分解为方向 s（单位球面上的 shape）和增益 γ（gain），在反向传播中分别对两个量化器施加各自的 STE。数学分析表明，这会在方向参数上诱导出"切空间步进 + 归一化回缩（retraction）"的球面（黎曼）更新，而标准联合 STE 则完全忽略 shape-gain 结构。
- **新型 E8S 球面码本**：从 E8 格的等范数壳（norm shells）枚举码字，用最远点采样（farthest-point sampling）补齐部分壳以达到目标码本大小 K = 2^⌊8·bpw⌋，再归一化到单位球面。E8S 支持 1.75 / 1.875 / 2.25 等分数比特率，突破了 E8P12 固定 2.0 bpw 和整数标量量化的比特率粒度限制。
- **分布式精确最近邻分配**：各 data-parallel worker 对互不重叠的权重块做精确 NN 分配、只同步离散整数索引，配合融合归一化/选择/重建的 Triton kernel 和跨梯度累积步的索引缓存，把 LLaMA-3.2-1B 的训练时间从 62.5 s/it 降到 4.9 s/it（12.8×），逼近 INT2（4.4 s/it）和 BF16（4.3 s/it）。
- **SOTA VQ-QAT 精度**：在完全相同的量化器和 PTQ 初始化下，SQuAT 稳定优于标准 VQ-QAT STE；在 2.25 bpw 下于几乎所有模型 × 指标 × 训练预算上匹敌或超过成熟的标量 INT-QAT（SEQ）。
- **系统性的消融**：用相同码本 + 相同初始化隔离训练规则的贡献，并给出 3 个随机种子的非重叠区间结果；还消融了 chi 分位数增益量化、增益比特分配、显式 vs 隐式增益参数化。

## Key Contributions

- A **spherical training rule for shape-gain VQ-QAT**: separate STEs through the shape and gain quantizers yield surrogate gradients ∂L/∂s ≈ γ̂g and ∂L/∂γ ≈ ŝᵀg; the exact Jacobian of normalization produces the projector Ps = I − ssᵀ, so the one-step SGD update on the direction parameter is a Riemannian (tangent-space) gradient step on S^(d−1) followed by retraction via renormalization — in contrast to a standard joint STE, whose identity-Jacobian approximation is provably blind to the shape-gain structure.
- A **new E8S spherical codebook**: built from equal-norm E8 lattice shells (240 / 2160 / 6720 / 17520 points in the first nonzero shells), accumulated in increasing-norm order up to K = 2^⌊8·bpw⌋ with farthest-point sampling of the overflow shell; redundant shell pairs (S(2)/S(8), S(4)/S(16)) that collapse to identical directions after normalization are discarded. E8S unlocks fractional bitwidths (1.75–3.0 bpw), a Pareto region inaccessible to scalar quantization.
- A **distributed exact nearest-neighbor assignment scheme** with fused Triton kernels, weight caching across gradient-accumulation steps, and index-only all-gather communication: 62.5 → 4.9 s/it (12.8×) on LLaMA-3.2-1B while preserving *exact* NN assignment, making billion-parameter, 10B-token VQ-QAT tractable for the first time.
- **State-of-the-art VQ-QAT accuracy**: consistent wins over standard joint-STE VQ-QAT under matched quantizers and initialization, and wins over SEQ INT-QAT at 2.25 bpw across nearly all models, metrics, and budgets.
- Thorough **ablations and statistical rigor**: seed-level non-overlapping ranges for the SQuAT-vs-Standard gap, codebook (E8S vs E8P12), gain quantizer (chi-quantile vs uniform, 0-bit vs 1-bit), explicit vs implicit gain, and gain-bit Pareto analysis.

## Methodology

SQuAT targets weight-only quantization-aware training with lattice vector quantizers. Each 8-dimensional weight block w ∈ R^8 is decomposed in polar form as w = γs with gain γ = ‖w‖₂ and shape s = w/γ ∈ S^7. The forward pass quantizes the two parts independently and reconstructs ŵ = γ̂ŝ:

- **Shape quantizer**: exact nearest-neighbor assignment on the unit sphere, j⋆ = argmax_j ⟨s, c_j⟩, against a normalized E8-derived codebook (either the QuIP#-style E8P12 or the new E8S). E8S enumerates lattice shells in increasing squared norm, drops shells whose normalized directions duplicate others (integer multiples in the lattice collapse to the same direction), and farthest-point-samples the final partial shell to hit K = 2^⌊8·bpw⌋ exactly.
- **Gain quantizer**: a closed-form chi-quantile scalar quantizer — 2^bγ levels placed at evenly spaced quantiles of the χ_d distribution (block norms of approximately Gaussian weights follow χ_8). No learned codebook parameters; the gain consumes exactly bγ/d bits per weight.

The core of the paper is *where the STE is inserted*. Standard VQ-QAT keeps Euclidean shadow weights w and applies one STE to the whole quantizer Q(w), i.e. treats the Jacobian as the identity — the shape-gain factorization of the forward map then has zero effect on gradients. SQuAT instead parameterizes shadow weights as an unnormalized direction u plus gain γ (s = u/(‖u‖+ε)), applies the STE separately at each quantizer (∂ŝ/∂s ≈ I, ∂γ̂/∂γ ≈ 1), and differentiates *exactly* through the smooth normalization and reconstruction maps. The resulting surrogate gradient on u is (γ̂/(‖u‖+ε))·Ps·g with Ps = I − ssᵀ — exactly the Riemannian gradient on the sphere up to a positive scalar — and the post-step renormalization acts as the retraction. Appendix B dissects the other two combinations (spherical params + joint STE; Euclidean params + factored STE) to show that both the parameterization and the STE factorization materially change the effective update.

**Scalability.** Exact NN search is the bottleneck of VQ-QAT (O(NKd) per step, repeated every forward pass). The implementation fuses normalization, codeword selection, and reconstruction into a Triton kernel, caches quantized weights across gradient-accumulation steps so assignment runs once per optimizer step, and shards weight blocks across data-parallel workers that sync only integer indices. **Training setup**: QAT fine-tunes from PTQ checkpoints (QuIP#-style GPTQ reconstruction + OmniQuant scale calibration, blockwise Hadamard rotations) using DCLM-Edu with Jensen–Shannon distillation from a frozen FP16 teacher, batch 256, context 1024, 0.25B–10B tokens; the scalar baseline is SEQ INT-QAT; models are LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, and base Qwen3-1.7B; evaluation via LM Eval Harness on WikiText2 PPL (length 4096), CSR (six-task average, QuIP# protocol), MMLU, GSM8K, IFEval, and LongBench-E.

## Results & Highlights

- **Training-rule ablation (Table 1, identical E8S quantizer + PTQ init, 1B tokens):** SQuAT improves WikiText2 PPL on every model — 14.22 → 13.85 on LLaMA-3.2-1B (−0.37), 11.04 → 10.96 on LLaMA-3.2-3B, 10.17 → 10.03 on Qwen3-4B — and GSM8K by up to +4.9 points (14.7 → 18.0 on LLaMA-3.2-1B); MMLU on LLaMA-3.2-1B rises 39.3 → 40.3. Three-seed per-seed ranges do not overlap on any metric at 1B tokens, so the gap exceeds run-to-run noise.
- **QAT vs its PTQ initialization at 2.25 bpw / 10B tokens:** WikiText2 PPL drops from 17.90 → 13.74 (LLaMA-3.2-1B), 15.00 → 10.83 (LLaMA-3.2-3B), and 12.52 → 9.90 (Qwen3-4B); on Qwen3-4B, MMLU improves from 54.2 (PTQ) to 69.5 after QAT vs 72.6 for FP16.
- **vs INT-QAT (SEQ) at 2.25 bpw:** SQuAT matches or surpasses SEQ on nearly all model×metric×budget combinations at 10B tokens (e.g. LLaMA-3.2-1B PPL 13.74 vs 14.33; Qwen3-4B MMLU 69.5 vs 67.8), with MMLU gains up to +7.9 points at 1B tokens (40.3 vs 32.4 on LLaMA-3.2-1B); the single exception is Qwen3-4B CSR at 10B tokens (70.7 vs 70.9 for SEQ). At 2.0 bpw the two are broadly comparable — SQuAT wins at <5B tokens while SEQ pulls ahead with longer training (e.g. LLaMA-3.2-3B at 5B tokens: 11.08 vs 11.03 PPL).
- **vs the latest VQ-QAT method LC-QAT** on Qwen3-1.7B at 2.0 bpw with 1B training tokens: SQuAT reaches 61.2 vs 59.8 CSR (+1.4); vs QTIP and QuIP# (52M tokens), SQuAT sometimes has higher WikiText2 PPL (e.g. 14.33 vs QTIP's 14.47 on L1B at 10B is lower, but 10.83 vs 10.12 on L3B is higher) while achieving substantially stronger downstream accuracy (L3B MMLU 55.5 vs 49.4).
- **Systems speedup (Figure 3, LLaMA-3.2-1B):** vanilla VQ-QAT 62.5 s/it → 20.8 s/it with fused kernels (3.0×) → 6.6 s/it with index caching (9.5×) → 4.9 s/it with sharded assignment (12.8×), approaching INT2 QAT at 4.4 s/it and BF16 at 4.3 s/it, while keeping assignment exact.
- **Codebook and gain ablations (Tables 3, 9, Figure 4):** at 2.25 bpw / 1B tokens on LLaMA-3.2-1B, E8S-SG gives 13.85 PPL / 58.8 CSR / 40.3 MMLU vs 13.94 / 58.7 / 38.9 for E8P12-SG and 14.24 / 58.4 / 38.2 for E8S without shape-gain; explicit gain beats implicit gain on PPL (13.85 vs 14.01); a fixed chi-based scale with 0 gain bits outperforms 1-bit uniform integer gains; optimal gain allocation is 0.125 bpw below 2.0 total bpw, 0.25 bpw up to 2.75, and 0.375 bpw at 3.0.

## Authors & Credibility

The work is a collaboration between Sebastian Loeschcke (University of Copenhagen, work done during a Qualcomm AI Research internship) and six Qualcomm AI Research researchers (Koryakovskiy, van der Ouderaa, Del Chiaro, van Baalen, Whatmough, Nagel) — the team behind GPTVQ, PV-Tuning, and much of the LLM vector-quantization literature (QuIP#, QTIP, PVQ are cited as their own prior work). This is exactly the right group to scalably implement exact lattice VQ-QAT, and the engineering claims (fused Triton kernels, distributed index-only sync) carry weight from this lab. The paper includes unusually strong hygiene for a quantization paper: matched-quantizer/matched-initialization ablations that isolate the training rule, a per-seed table with non-overlapping ranges, an explicit AI-use statement (LLMs used only for code assistance and prose editing; all numbers from the authors' own experiments), and a reproducibility statement promising code release upon acceptance.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core insight — that applying separate STEs at the shape and gain quantizers, rather than a joint STE at the reconstruction, changes the surrogate gradient from a Euclidean step to a genuine tangent-space-plus-retraction Riemannian step — is a clean, well-derived observation, and connecting it to the spherical-optimization literature (DoRA, hyperspherical constraints) is well motivated. That said, each ingredient (shape-gain VQ, E8 lattices, STE-based QAT, spherical parameterizations) is individually established; the contribution is the synthesis and the scaling machinery, not a fundamentally new primitive.
- **Rigor**: The Table 1 design (same E8S quantizer, same PTQ init, three seeds with non-overlapping metric ranges at 1B tokens) is a genuinely controlled experiment, better than most QAT papers. But the SQuAT-vs-Standard gap *shrinks* with budget — at 5B tokens on LLaMA-3.2-3B CSR the Standard STE actually wins (66.8 vs 66.4), and PPL gaps compress from −0.37 to −0.13 — so the training-rule advantage is largest exactly where budgets are small, which the headline framing underplays.
- **Reproducibility**: Hyperparameters (learning-rate sweeps, optimizer, scheduler) are promised in App. C and evaluation protocols follow LM Eval Harness, but the code is only planned "upon acceptance," so the Triton/distributed-assignment engineering — the paper's practical bottleneck-solver — is not yet verifiable. The 12.8× speedup is measured on LLaMA-3.2-1B only; no per-iteration timings for the 3B/4B models are given.
- **Significance**: Beating a mature scalar INT-QAT (SEQ) at 2.25 bpw on WikiText2 PPL across *all* budgets is a real result, and the fractional-bitwidth E8S codebook opens a compression regime scalar methods cannot reach. However, "SOTA VQ-QAT accuracy" is claimed within a thin field: the only recent head-to-head is LC-QAT, compared on a single model (Qwen3-1.7B) and a single metric family (CSR), because LC-QAT's 1B-token checkpoint reports no PPL/MMLU. The QuIP# rows in Table 2 are taken from HuggingFace with unspecified training budget (footnote a), an uneven protocol.
- **Red flags**: Mild. MMLU at low budgets on small models is still far from FP16 (40.3 vs 45.4 on LLaMA-3.2-1B; 55.8 vs 59.6 on 3B); seed-level MMLU variance for Standard VQ-QAT spans 36.5–39.3, so some downstream deltas are within noise even where PPL is stable; and SQuAT sometimes loses to QTIP on perplexity while claiming downstream wins, a trade-off the paper is honest about but that complicates the "consistently outperforms" narrative. No wall-clock totals or total-compute comparison for the full 10B-token runs is reported, only per-iteration overhead ratios.

## Strengths

- Clean theoretical identification of *why* STE placement matters in shape-gain VQ, with the Riemannian tangent-plus-retraction interpretation derived explicitly (Eqs. 9–12) rather than asserted.
- Controlled ablation methodology: identical quantizer and PTQ initialization across training rules, plus multi-seed significance checks — rare and commendable in this literature.
- Practical systems work that solves the actual blocker (exact NN assignment cost) rather than switching to approximate assignment, with measured 12.8× end-to-end training speedup approaching scalar-QAT cost.
- E8S's fractional bitwidths (1.75, 1.875, 2.25 bpw) give operators a genuinely finer compression-accuracy dial, with a Pareto analysis of where to spend gain bits.
- Honest limitations section: the authors themselves flag the Qwen3-4B CSR exception, the E8S dequantizer gap, and the 4B/10B-token scale ceiling.

## Limitations

- Evaluation caps at 4B-parameter models and 10B training tokens; 7B+ models, other architectures, and higher-bit hierarchical schemes are untested (acknowledged by the authors).
- E8S currently lacks an efficient dequantizer/kernel implementation — E8P12 has a custom CUDA path, but is stuck at a fixed 2.0 bpw — so the fractional-bitwidth advantage is not yet realizable in deployment.
- At 2.0 bpw, INT-QAT (SEQ) often overtakes SQuAT with longer training, so the practical win is concentrated at 2.25 bpw and short budgets.
- The spherical-vs-standard advantage narrows (and occasionally reverses on CSR) as training budgets grow, suggesting much of the gain is faster early optimization rather than a better final solution.
- WikiText2 perplexity remains above FP16 at all budgets (13.74 vs 12.15 on LLaMA-3.2-1B at 10B tokens), and low-budget MMLU/GSM8K on 1B models degrade steeply (MMLU 40.3 vs FP16's 45.4).
- No total-training-compute accounting, and the distributed-assignment timing evidence is single-model (LLaMA-3.2-1B).

## Relevance

该论文直接命中"极端性能优化下的可靠性"方向：它系统研究了低比特（2.0–2.25 bpw）向量量化与量化感知训练中的精度损失问题，展示了量化器结构与梯度估计（STE 放置位置）如何共同决定训练稳定性与最终模型质量——对理解激进数值压缩引入的误差及其正确性边界有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the factored-STE/Riemannian advantage persist at 7B+ scale and 50B+ tokens, or does it wash out as the PTQ initialization matters less relative to the training budget?
- Would VQ-specific stabilization tricks ported from INT-QAT (learned clipping, elastic step-size schedules) widen the SQuAT-vs-SEQ gap at 2.0 bpw where SEQ currently catches up with longer training?
- How much of the SQuAT gain over Standard VQ-QAT comes from the tangent-space update geometry versus simply re-scaling the gradient by γ̂/‖u‖ — i.e., is there an ablation against a Euclidean update with matched preconditioning?
- Can the E8S codebook get a compact decoder (e.g., Leech-lattice-style structured arithmetic) to make its fractional bitwidths deployable, and what is the inference-time memory-bandwidth win at 1.875 vs 2.25 bpw?
- How does SQuAT behave under activation quantization or KV-cache quantization, where the shape-gain decomposition's Gaussianity assumption on block norms may fail?
- Does the index-only distributed assignment scheme extend to codebook *learning* (e.g., AQLM-style learned codebooks) or to non-lattice codebooks where exact NN is much more expensive?
