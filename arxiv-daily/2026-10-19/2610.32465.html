Key metric — the cost
+3%

per-epoch overhead vs Muon — T+1 gradient evaluations over T steps, where SAM-style methods need 2T. No extra memory buffer; drop-in for any Muon codebase.

what it buys — Pythia-70M INT4 AWQ ppl degradation: 10.96% → 10.55% (FP-SOMA: 10.20%)
Why this matters

Train with Muon and quantize afterwards? ~3% training overhead buys 4-bit quantization robustness — plus the first SAM-family convergence guarantee under heavy-tailed gradient noise.

「以约 3% 的训练开销，为 Muon 训练的模型换取量化鲁棒性。」

Critical edge

The theory is direction-agnostic — a random spectral perturbation matches SOMA on the paper's own synthetic problems, undercutting the "friendly direction" design story.

Methodology, distilled

How SAM meets Muon without a second gradient pass

The gap

SAM variants were built for SGD; Muon's only SAM pairing (the unpublished spectral SSM) doubles gradient cost and lacks theory — and no SAM analysis covers heavy-tailed noise, despite its prevalence in LLM training.

The trick

Reuse what Muon already computes. The perturbation combines the previous iteration's perturbed gradient Gt−1 with momentum (1−β)Mt−1 acting as a free EMA:

Et = ρ·NSq( Gt−1 − γ(1−β)·Mt−1 )

Gradient is evaluated at Wt−1+Et, then fed to the standard Muon update (momentum → Newton-Schulz, q=5). Lemma 1: the stale gradient costs only bias ≤ L(ρ+η).

The family

FP-SOMA (fresh gradient, 2 evals) · OP-SOMA Pre/Post-NSq (project out alignment with the last Muon step) · MSOMA (negative momentum) · Frobenius- vs spectral-geometry controls · random perturbation (RSM).

The theory

Under (ν,L)-smoothness and p-BCM noise (1<p≤2), SOMA reaches ε-stationarity in nuclear norm with an irreducible, noise-independent floor O(χqLρν), recovering Muon's rate at ρ=0 — the first such guarantee for any SAM variant.

The audit

15 optimizers across: synthetic teacher-student with controlled Pareto tails (α∈{1.1…3}), nanoGPT-124M & Pythia-70M continued pretraining on FineWeb-Edu, CIFAR-10 Airbench, 4-bit AWQ PTQ, and catastrophic forgetting on 4 fine-tuning datasets.

Results, beyond the headline

Five benchmarks, one failure case — numbers as reported (3 seeds)

nanoGPT pretraining SOMA matches Muon's val ppl 24.40 vs 24.41; two-gradient SSM/FP-SOMA worse (24.54–24.55) at 2.4–2.9× cost; AdamW 24.60, SGD 25.75.
Pythia-70M · 4-bit AWQ The clearest win: relative ppl degradation 10.96% (Muon) → 10.55% (SOMA) → 10.20% (FP-SOMA); best INT4 ppl 37.08 vs Muon's 37.28.
Heavy-tail synthetic (α=1.1) Muon-family reaches ~38–40% val acc vs ~36% SGD and ~29% AdamW; SOMA cuts Muon's optimality gap by up to 40% — the base optimizer, not the perturbation, sets robustness.
Catastrophic forgetting FineWeb loss rise vs Muon: −16% FP-SOMA, −11% SSM, −8% OP-SOMA-Pre, −4% SOMA (nanoGPT); all Muon-based methods forget equally on Pythia-70M.
CIFAR-10 Airbench AdamW wins val accuracy (87.87% vs Muon's 87.27%) while Muon trains fastest with lowest loss (0.266 vs 0.316) — SSM's vision edge doesn't transfer to language.

Read across the rows: sharpness awareness never improves final perplexity. The base optimizer sets the ceiling; the payoff concentrates in PTQ robustness and forgetting.
Strengths
  • First convergence theory for any SAM-type method under heavy-tailed (p-BCM) noise — noise-independent floor, Muon's rate recovered at ρ=0.
  • Genuinely cheap: T+1 vs 2T gradient evals, ~3% epoch overhead, no extra buffer, drop-in for existing Muon codebases.
  • Well-controlled audit: exact tail-index control, 15 optimizers, radius sweeps, 3 seeds, deployment-relevant axes (PTQ, forgetting).
  • Refreshingly honest — negative and null results (MSOMA divergence, no ppl gain, AdamW's CIFAR win) stated in the main text, not buried.
Limitations
  • Nothing beyond 124M parameters tested; Muon's real adoption is at far larger scale, where PTQ and forgetting effects could differ either way.
  • PTQ gains are moderate (10.96→10.55%) and convincing on one of two models; perplexity-only evaluation; only weight-only 4-bit AWQ tested.
  • Direction-invariant theory cannot explain why spectral/friendly perturbations beat Frobenius/random in some settings — the mechanism stays empirical.
  • Forgetting protocol inherits an optimizer-mismatch confound (AdamW-pretrained base); a 4-dataset average may hide large per-dataset variance.
Questions for further exploration
  1. Do the PTQ robustness gains persist at 1B+ scale, where loss-landscape geometry and quantization sensitivity differ substantially from 70M–124M?
  2. Does fine-tuning a Muon-pretrained model with SOMA — avoiding the optimizer-mismatch confound — change the catastrophic-forgetting picture?
  3. Why does a random spectral perturbation (RSM) match SOMA on synthetic problems — is the "friendly residual" direction ever measurably better, or is spectral normalization doing all the work?
  4. Can the theory's direction-invariance be tightened (e.g., under Hessian anisotropy) to actually distinguish friendly from random perturbations?
  5. Does SOMA interact with other PTQ schemes (GPTQ, low-rank, FP8) or with quantization-aware training the same way as with AWQ?
