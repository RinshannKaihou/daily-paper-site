arXiv 2610.25836 · 2026-10-19 · RL post-training diagnosis

RL training failures localize onto a handful of diagnostic n-grams — and a 35M-token corpus retrieved with those phrases repairs them.

一句话总结 — GRPO/RLVR 训练中的失败高度局部化：少数特征 n 元组（如 "Let's recheck"、"the inverse of A"）能强烈预测 rollout 失败且整个 RL 过程中持续存在；作者用这些“失败 n 元组”检索仅 3500 万 token（比常规 mid-training 小 3–4 个数量级）做定向继续预训练再重跑同样的 RL，在 11 个设置中的 10 个上超过同等预算的随机文本对照。

Why this matters

If your RL run keeps failing at the same spots, a 35M-token patch — mined from the failures themselves — is now a reproducible, cheap fix.

Critical edge — read before citing

Most “wins” are single-seed within a ~1-point noise band — and the paper itself concedes the gain cannot be attributed to the diagnosed states alone.

rigor: mixed (authors’ own framing) · no comparison exceeds 2σ · misses disclosed

01 · Methodology — mine, diagnose, repair

1
MINE failure n-grams

N-grams (n = 3–6) from the RL policy’s own reasoning rollouts, kept if seen ≥20 times in ≥0.1% of rollouts; digit/formatting junk dropped, near-duplicates merged at Jaccard ≥ 0.9. Ranked by conditional accuracy a(g) = Pr[correct | rollout contains g]; the bottom 4–20 spans form the failure set (listed verbatim in App. C).

2
DIAGNOSE as low-value states

Failure n-grams mark (not cause) low-value states: a(g) ≈ V^π(s_g), at ~6–26%. GRPO only learns from rollouts it samples at a state — almost none succeed there — so RL cannot self-repair: entry into failure states stays flat at 5–7% across 210 steps while overall accuracy climbs 0.2% → 47%. For OLMo2-1B on GSM8K, failure five-grams hit ~7% of late rollouts (24% success vs 44% elsewhere).

3
REPAIR with targeted CPT

Each n-gram becomes an exact-match infini-gram query over the pretraining corpora (Olmo-mix / Dolmino-mix); 2k-token windows around matches are sampled to a 35M-token budget — 3–4 orders of magnitude below standard mid-training. Continued pretraining (inserted before or after mid-training), then the identical RL recipe re-runs. Control: same pipeline and budget with content-neutral queries (“step 1”, “1”, “first”).

Evaluated on OLMo2-1B/7B and Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B across GSM8K, a multi-task mixture (GSM8K : Social IQA : MMLU : TriviaQA = 8:2:1:1), and General Reasoner (8-benchmark mean). Qwen3 retrieval borrows the OLMo2 corpora (Qwen’s are closed) — doubling as a transfer test.

03 · Strengths

  • Interpretable diagnosis — failures predictable from a handful of surface n-grams: reflection spirals for weak models, specific missing operations for strong ones; not a black-box score.
  • Extremely data-efficient repair — 35M tokens, 3–4 orders below mid-training, with a matched-control design that separates data selection from the generic effect of CPT.
  • Honest, thorough analysis — state-level statistics, pass@k decompositions, insertion-point and budget ablations, verbatim query listings, quantified seed-noise appendix.
  • Cross-family transfer — a corpus mined from OLMo2-1B’s failures helps Qwen3-0.6B/4B, suggesting the text encodes general skills, not model quirks.

04 · Limitations

  • Modest, noisy effects — 0.1–2.6 points, mostly single-seed; several claimed wins sit inside the ~1-point noise band; no comparison exceeds two standard deviations.
  • Uneven repair — missing operations jump 0% → 50–62%, but unproductive reflection (the common weak-model archetype) moves only 1–2 points.
  • Corpus access required — retrieval needs the base model’s pretraining data; effectively OLMo2-only. Exact match misses paraphrases; mixture artifacts currently removed by hand.
  • Narrow regime — only “RL almost works” is studied; genuine coverage gaps and repeated diagnose–repair–RL cycles are open.
  • Baseline narrows the win — curated MegaMath-Web-Pro matches the targeted corpus on already-mid-trained models, leaving the edge to pre-mid-training insertion.

05 · Open questions — for further exploration

  1. Would embedding-based retrieval seeded by the failure n-grams — or filtering windows for completed resolutions — fix the “trigger present, no recovery” weakness the authors identify?
  2. Entry rate doubles after targeted CPT while per-phrase success barely moves — does the gain come from the diagnosed states at all, or from generic in-distribution regularization?
  3. Why does scaling to 1B tokens hurt by 19 points when replaying the pretraining distribution does not — repetition of narrow text, distribution shift, or forgetting?
  4. Does iterating diagnose → repair → RL compound, or do successive failure sets collapse onto the same unrepairable reflection behaviors?
  5. Does the failure-n-gram signal stay diagnostic under PPO or offline RL, and can closed-corpus models substitute synthetic traversals?
