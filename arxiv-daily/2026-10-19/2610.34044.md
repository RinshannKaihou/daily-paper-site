> **一句话总结：** 该文证明并实证了一个"符号定律"：当 RLVR 的 rollout 用 top-k/top-p 截断或非 1 温度采样而学习器仍按原始策略求导时，恒定优势平移会以由采样器"锐度"决定的固定符号改变策略熵——正平移在更锐的采样器下令熵下降、在更平的采样器下令熵上升；掩盖（mask）与判败（fail）超长回复恰好把组内 token 加权净优势推向相反符号，在 2B 模型上造成 −9.4 vs +11.9×10⁻³ nats/步 的熵斜率分裂，在 9B 推理模型上 10 步内使未修正规则损失 8.2 个百分点的留出集精度，而零化净优势或学习器重归一化可完全恢复。

> **TL;DR:** In RLVR, whether policy entropy rises or falls can hinge on a seemingly neutral choice — masking over-length responses versus counting them as failures. The authors trace this to sampler mismatch (rollouts drawn with top-k/top-p truncation or a temperature the learner does not apply) and prove a sign law: a constant advantage shift lowers entropy at first order under any sampler sharper than the policy (top-k, top-p, min-p, T<1) and raises it under flatter samplers (T>1). The baseline-controlled part of the drift equals the token-weighted net advantage N (negative under FAIL, positive under MASK), so the two rules push entropy in opposite directions. On Qwen3.5-2B with MATH training prompts, the entropy slope is linear in N (−106.8×10⁻³ nats/step per unit N under truncation, vs flat under exact sampling), and on the MiMo-V2.6-9B reasoning model over 10 steps, adding the net-zero (NZ) baseline to FAIL+PEN lifts held-out MATH accuracy from 75.3% to 83.5% (+8.2 points) and benchmark-average from 56.4% to 67.6% (+11.2), with no difference under exact sampling (84.0% vs 84.2%). Token-level importance ratios leave 98% of the drift; support renormalization (equivalent to score centering) or a gap-weighted baseline removes it, and in 100-step 2B runs only the gap-weighted baseline and renormalization prevent a delayed entropy explosion (3.6–4.6 nats) that NZ alone only postpones.

## 核心贡献

- **符号定律（Theorem 1）**：在采样器失配（rollout 分布 μ ≠ 策略 π）下，恒定优势平移对 softmax 策略熵的一阶期望效应符号由采样器决定：凡比策略"更锐"的采样器（top-k、top-p、min-p、T<1、截断+降温），正平移降低熵、负平移升高熵；"更平"的采样器（T>1、与均匀分布混合）方向相反。该符号由"锐度间隙" δ(μ) 的符号唯一决定，并在 14,400 个随机分布 + 4,920 个对抗构造分布上数值验证无一例外。
- **批次级分解（Proposition 1）**：组式 RLVR 中 token 级损失的一阶熵变可分解为净优势项 D_net（唯一可被 baseline 改变的部分，符号由 token 加权净优势 N 决定）、间隙协方差项 D_cov（优势与采样器锐度间隙的协方差，与 baseline 无关、可长期正反馈）和 on-policy 项 D_on。
- **解释了 mask/fail 之谜**：组均值 baseline 下 N = Cov(r, L)/L̄，长回复更易失败，故 FAIL/PEN 使 N < 0（熵上升）；MASK 引入非负项 (n_mask/G)·r̄M 使 N > 0（+0.30 @2B，+0.16 @9B，熵下降）。看似中立的工程选择因此系统性扭转熵方向。
- **修正方案的等价性与失效分析（Proposition 2）**：OPO 的长度加权 baseline（NZ）恰好令 N=0；新提出的间隙加权 baseline GAPW 可对消两个失配项；学习器在采样支撑上重归一化等价于 score centering，可整体消除漂移；而截断下的 token 级重要性采样比仅按保留质量 π(S)≈0.99 重缩放漂移——保留 98%，不能作为修正。
- **实践普查**：审查 18 个 RL 框架的 19 个训练器和 123 份技术报告/配方，在 55 份披露训练采样器的报告中 7 份（13%）存在未修正的截断/温度失配（如 ROLL 默认 T=0.95、top-p 0.7、top-k 50 且学习器不除温度）。

## Key Contributions

- **A sign law (Theorem 1)**: under sampler mismatch, a constant advantage shift changes softmax policy entropy at first order and in expectation with a sign fixed solely by the sampler — sharper-than-policy samplers (top-k, top-p, min-p, T<1, temperature-then-truncation) make positive shifts sharpen the policy and negative shifts flatten it; flatter samplers (T>1, uniform mixing) reverse both. The sign reduces to that of a "sharpness gap" δ(μ), verified on 14,400 random + 4,920 adversarial distributions with zero exceptions.
- **A batch-level decomposition (Proposition 1)**: the first-order entropy change of token-level group losses splits into a net-advantage term D_net (the only part any baseline controls, signed by the token-weighted net advantage N), a gap covariance D_cov (advantage–sharpness correlation, baseline-independent and self-amplifying over long horizons), and the standard on-policy covariance D_on.
- **Resolves the mask-vs-fail asymmetry**: with a group-mean baseline N = Cov(r, L)/L̄, so FAIL/PEN give N<0 (entropy rises) because long responses fail more, while MASK adds a nonnegative (n_mask/G)·r̄M term giving N>0 (+0.30 at 2B, +0.16 at 9B; entropy falls). A neutral-looking pipeline choice flips the drift sign.
- **A taxonomy of fixes (Proposition 2)**: OPO's length-weighted baseline (NZ) zeroes N; the proposed gap-weighted baseline GAPW cancels both mismatch terms per group; learner-side support renormalization is proven identical to score centering and removes the whole drift; token-level importance ratios merely rescale it by the kept mass — 98% of the drift survives exact ratios, so importance sampling is not a correction for truncation.
- **Controlled evidence plus a practice survey**: dose-response, six-sampler sign sweep, aggregation-change out-of-sample test, 100-step horizon runs on Qwen3.5-2B, a 9B accuracy experiment on MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B, an OLMo-3-7B replication, and a survey of 19 trainers across 18 RL frameworks and 123 technical reports (7/55 = 13% of disclosed recipes train with uncorrected mismatch).

## Methodology

The analysis is first-order in the gradient step under a softmax parameterization. At a single position, a gradient step on A·log πa changes entropy by −ηA·Φ(a) + O(η²), where Φ(a) = u_a − E_π[u]; under exact sampling the expectation of Φ vanishes (classical baseline invariance, Williams 1992), but under sampler μ a constant shift c gives E_μ[ΔH] = −ηc·δ(μ), where the sharpness gap δ(μ) = E_μ[u] − E_π[u] measures how much the sampler reweights tokens toward high-probability ones. Theorem 1 proves δ(μ)>0 for any sampler whose likelihood ratio to π is increasing in token probability ("sharper"), via a layer-cake decomposition of μ into truncations over level sets plus a lemma that truncating to any upper set leaves a positive gap; flatter samplers decompose over complements and flip the sign.

For group-based RLVR (GRPO-style: G=8 rollouts per prompt, group-mean baseline, token-level surrogate, one optimizer step per batch so the PPO ratio is 1), Proposition 1 aggregates the per-position identity into ΔH = D_net + D_cov + D_on, where D_net is proportional to each group's token-weighted net advantage N_g times its mean gap, and D_cov is the within-group covariance between advantages and per-response mean gaps. Table 3 derives N_g in closed form for common rules: group-mean baseline gives Cov(r,L)/L̄ (negative under FAIL/PEN), MASK adds a nonnegative masked-mass term (making N positive in their runs), OPO's length-weighted baseline gives exactly zero, and sequence-mean aggregation zeroes N for FAIL but not MASK — an out-of-sample prediction the experiments confirm. Because both mismatch terms scale with the gap, and the gap grows as the policy mass moves into the discarded tail, D_cov feeds back on itself; a top-k cap lets this loop run away while top-p alone eventually self-limits as the nucleus grows.

Corrections are analyzed in Proposition 2: NZ zeroes D_net; GAPW sets the group baseline to the gap-weighted mean reward so the two mismatch terms cancel per group (reducing to NZ when gaps are equal, and having the form of variance-optimal baselines); learner-side support renormalization yields the centered score of Marek & Ryabinin (2026), making the learner's effective distribution equal μ and eliminating both terms; and exact token-level ratios multiply each prefix's drift by its kept mass π(S), preserving sign. Experiments use vLLM rollouts, entropy measured by teacher forcing on a fixed 64-prompt probe set (independent of batch composition), paired seeds so arms differ in exactly one factor, and the training seed as the unit of replication with t-based 95% intervals. Models are Qwen3.5-2B (15 or 100 steps) and MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B forked from step 20 of a reproduced public RLVR run (10 steps, 32 groups/step), both trained on 7,496 MATH problems graded by math-verify with 750 held out; OLMo-3-7B-Instruct serves as a second family.

## Results & Highlights

- **Sign separation on 2B (Table 1)**: under truncated sampling (top-k 20, top-p 0.95) the entropy slope over 15 steps is −9.4±0.2 for MASK (N≈+0.30) versus +11.9±0.6 for FAIL (N≈−0.08), with FAIL+NZ in between at +2.4±0.4 (all ×10⁻³ nats/step, 3 seeds); the paired MASK−FAIL difference collapses from 100% (reference) to −3% under exact sampling and −4% under learner renormalization — the cause is the mismatch, not truncation itself.
- **Dose-response linearity**: shifting each group's advantages by −λN_g (21 runs, 2B) gives an entropy slope of (−106.8 ± 0.7)×10⁻³ nats/step per unit N under truncated sampling, versus +1.1 ± 2.7 (indistinguishable from zero) under exact sampling; across 12 truncated runs with logged gaps, the first-order drift D_net + D_cov predicts the measured slope with correlation 0.95.
- **Sampler sets the sign (Figure 2b, Table 8)**: the MASK−FAIL difference tracks the measured sharpness gap across six samplers, from −5.9 (top-p 0.99) to −36.5 (top-p 0.8) and −35.2/−32.6 at T=0.7, and reverses sign at the only flatter sampler T=1.2 in all three seeds (MASK +21.5, FAIL −11.6, difference +32.4 to +34.3×10⁻³ nats/step). Exact token-level importance ratios leave 98% of the drift (predicted by mean kept mass 0.99) and ratios inside the clipped objective leave 103%; a learner applying the sampling temperature cuts it to 2% at T=0.7.
- **9B accuracy (Tables 2 and 9)**: from a fork at 78.7% held-out accuracy, truncated FAIL+PEN falls to 75.3% held-out / 56.4% benchmark average (MATH-500, AIME24, AIME25, AMC23) with probe entropy +0.215 nats over 10 steps, while FAIL+PEN+NZ reaches 83.5% / 67.6% with +0.016 nats — differences of +8.2 points held-out (95% CI [4.2, 12.1], p=0.007) and +11.2 on the benchmark mean (AIME24 +11.9: 36.9→48.8; AMC23 +15.8: 71.7→87.5); under exact sampling the same comparison is +0.2 points (84.0% vs 84.2%), and a renormalized learner recovers 84.2% (+8.8 over uncorrected, positive in every seed) — the accuracy loss is caused by the mismatch.
- **Long-horizon feedback (Figure 3, Table 10)**: in 100-step 2B runs FAIL pushes entropy past 0.5 nats by step 17 and to 3.6–4.6 by step 43 (held-out accuracy 45.5%→32.7% at step 25); FAIL+NZ only delays the explosion (crosses 0.5 at steps 33/37, peaks 3.8/4.6, accuracy 53.7%→24.3% at step 50) while exact sampling never exceeds the initial entropy and reaches 68–71%; GAPW holds entropy between 0.18 and 0.31 nats for all 100 steps with no gap growth and 68.7% held-out accuracy (renormalized learner: 69.2%).
- **Survey**: of 55 technical reports/recipes disclosing their training sampler, 7 (13%) train with uncorrected truncation or temperature mismatch (e.g., ROLL defaults to T=0.95/top-p 0.7/top-k 50 with no learner-side temperature); corrections landed in several frameworks only during 2026 (torchtitan rejects top-p<1; TRL AsyncGRPOTrainer renormalizes).

## Authors & Credibility

The paper lists three independent authors — Zhiyuan Wang, Jacob Steeves (email domain bittensor.com), and Chang Liu — with a "Preprint, October 27, 2026" stamp and no institutional affiliation block, which is unusual for work of this rigor and suggests an independent/small-lab effort. Credibility is nonetheless strong: the theory is proven rather than asserted (full proofs in Appendix A, plus a numerical falsification attempt over ~19,000 distributions that found no counterexample), claims are matched with controls that isolate exactly one factor (paired seeds, exact-sampling and renormalization controls, optimizer-state-reset controls), negative or inconvenient results are reported in place (MASK lying off the dose-response line, the unexplained OLMo magnitude gap, seed-level discrepancies after optimizer reset), and the practice survey of 18 frameworks at pinned commits is the kind of ground-truth engineering audit few academic papers do. Experiments ran on 8×H100 and H200 nodes. The work engages carefully with prior theory (Le Roux 2016; Arnal et al. 2025; Marek & Ryabinin 2026 score centering; Cui et al. 2025 entropy covariance; OPO's optimal baseline) and positions its novelty precisely: a sign statement for general policy-derived samplers plus the batch-level quantity controlling the drift in group-based RLVR.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuine and well-scoped. The baseline-dependence of drift under off-policy sampling is known (Le Roux 2016), and score centering already fixes it (Marek & Ryabinin 2026); the new results are the clean sign characterization for ordered samplers (Theorem 1), the N_g decomposition identifying the token-weighted net advantage as the baseline-controlled knob, and the demonstration that masking versus failing flips its sign. GAPW itself is a modest incremental construction (a gap-weighted variance-optimal-style baseline), and its main evidentiary role — showing D_cov exists and feeds back — could arguably have been served by renormalization alone.
- **Rigor**: high for the theory, decent for experiments, but the empirical scope is narrow. The headline 9B accuracy result covers only 10 steps from a fork of a single model (MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B) with 4 seeds (MASK: a single seed), one domain (MATH), one sampler configuration (top-k 20 + top-p 0.95), and evaluations always performed with the truncated sampler. The paper says plainly that the theory "predicts signs, not sizes": Adam amplifies the drift about twelvefold relative to SGD, and MASK (N=+0.30, slope −9.4) sits far above the dose-response line's prediction (≈−29), so the first-order account demonstrably fails to explain the magnitude for the most practically common rule — the authors acknowledge this but do not resolve it.
- **Reproducibility**: good. Models are public checkpoints, the 9B runs fork a reproduced public run with weights and optimizer state, seeds/probes/statistics are specified, and the framework survey pins commits. However, no code release is mentioned, GAPW requires the learner to compute per-response gaps under the sampling rule (an implementation detail easy to get subtly wrong), and several 100-step runs ended early at steps 43–78 due to a node failure, with only the renormalized arm rerun to step 100 — the long-horizon comparison is therefore partially unbalanced.
- **Significance**: the diagnosis is immediately actionable — an 8-point held-out accuracy loss from a default-looking sampler configuration is a large silent failure, and the survey shows real frameworks (ROLL) still ship it. But external validity beyond 10–100 steps and 2B–9B math-only RLVR is unestablished: the OLMo-3-7B replication shows the mask-fail effect at only about one-eighth of the Qwen magnitude despite a similar gap, a model-dependence the paper flags but does not explain, and no long 9B run tests whether NZ/GAPW benefits persist or whether, as the 2B runs suggest, NZ alone merely postpones an explosion at scale.
- **Red flags**: minor. The 13% prevalence figure describes public recipes, not production pipelines (most large-model reports do not disclose samplers — the paper is honest about this); the interesting FAIL+NZ-with-optimizer-reset entropy drop ("we did not test why") and the single-seed 9B MASK arm both warrant more evidence; and one 100-step fixed-shift seed recovers only 33.3% held-out accuracy at step 100, a warning that delay is not stability, which the text slightly underplays relative to the GAPW result.

## Strengths

- A crisp, provable, falsifiable law (sign fixed by sampler sharpness) where prior work offered verbal arguments, single-sampler observations, or fix-first papers — and the numerical search over ~19,000 distributions plus a tabular RLVR simulation (decomposition predicts realized entropy change within 3% over 300 steps) actively tried to break it.
- The mask-vs-fail explanation is the kind of result practitioners feel immediately: it turns "entropy mysteriously went up/down" into a measurable batch statistic (N and the mean gap) that can be logged and alarmed on.
- Exceptional control discipline: paired seeds isolating one factor, exact-sampling and learner-renormalization controls from both sides, sequence-mean aggregation as an out-of-sample test of the theory, dose-response over 21 runs, and honest reporting of where the first-order account fails (MASK off the line, Adam's 12× amplification).
- The framework survey at pinned commits (Table 11) is independently valuable — it documents which of 18 real RL frameworks expose the failure mode by default and which corrections landed when.

## Limitations

- The theory is first-order only and predicts signs, not magnitudes; absolute sizes do not transfer across optimizers (Adam ≈ 12× SGD) or rules, and the paper's own MASK data point violates the linear prediction by roughly 3×.
- Small experimental scale: 15-step main runs at 2B, 10-step 9B runs from a fork, 2–4 seeds per cell, math-only verifiable rewards, one sampler family; long-horizon claims rest on 2B-only runs, several truncated by a node failure.
- GAPW and NZ change the effective baseline, so their accuracy benefit could partly reflect baseline variance-reduction effects rather than drift removal alone — the renormalization arm (which changes no advantage) mitigates but does not fully close this concern, since renormalization also changes the update.
- The 13% prevalence estimate is limited to disclosing public recipes; the true production rate is unknown, and the OLMo replication suggests effect size varies substantially across model families for unexplained reasons.
- Evaluations consistently use the truncated sampler; accuracy under exact or alternative evaluation sampling is not reported.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the NZ/GAPW accuracy benefit persist over hundreds of steps at 9B+, or does NZ merely postpone the 2B-style explosion at scale, making learner-side renormalization the only durable fix?
- Why is the mask-fail effect on OLMo-3-7B only about one-eighth of Qwen3.5-2B's at a similar sharpness gap — is it the probe-response distribution, pretraining sharpness, or the format-failure grading quirk (34% strict-grading failures)?
- Can the twelvefold Adam amplification of the drift be folded into a predictive (not just sign-level) theory, e.g., via a diagonal-preconditioner-weighted gap, and does the same law hold under asynchronous staleness combined with truncation beyond the tabular check in Appendix G?
- How does the sign law interact with clip-higher/entropy-bonus and other deployed anti-collapse remedies, which shift effective advantages and might themselves set a mismatch drift?
- Do agentic RLVR settings (long tool-call trajectories with near-deterministic prefixes, where 60% of positions are top-token-dominated and carry only 12% of the gap) shrink the drift in practice, or does the discarded-tail feedback dominate at their much longer horizons?
