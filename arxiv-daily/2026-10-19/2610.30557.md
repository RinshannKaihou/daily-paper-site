> **一句话总结：** 提出 Steward——一个由九个 LLM 智能体组成的自动投资系统，其核心设计是把自我进化的边界画在类型系统里（代码级风控规则与纪律智能体的提示词不可被自我修改触及），代码而非 LLM 负责给每笔交易打分，季度反思产生的提示词改写经确定性门控与重放验证后可逆地应用；在 2026 年 4–9 月共 102 个交易日的真实账户部署中，该系统把半导体板块 24.6% 的回撤控制为仅 0.3% 的损失并抵御了全部提示注入攻击，且重放实验证明单一模型在同数据下五天跑输大盘 1.35 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** Steward is a nine-agent LLM trading system whose self-evolution is bounded by its type system: an invariant core of order arithmetic, blocking risk rules, and discipline-agent prompts that no self-edit can reach, with code (not the LLM) grading every trade and a deterministic, reversible gate applying quarterly prompt learnings verified by input replay. Over a five-month live paper deployment (102 trading days, 1,632 LLM calls, $455 API cost), it returned +5.0% vs SPY's +12.4% (beta 0.16, max drawdown −6.0% vs −4.5%) but contained a 24.6% semiconductor-sector crash to a 0.3% loss; a 2015–2026 backtest shows the core alone cuts momentum-strategy max drawdown from −61% to −33%. Replay of 20 recorded mornings shows the joint design loses nothing while a single-model baseline on the same raw data underperforms SPY by −1.35±0.44 points over 5 days — the value lies in synthesis and governance, not in any individual signal (Technical Analyst rank IC is only 0.031 at 20 days, t=1.2).

## 核心贡献

- **把自我进化的边界画进类型系统，而不是提示词。** 订单算术、阻断性风控规则（20% 单一持仓上限、40% 行业上限、90% 净敞口上限、3% 日亏熔断）以及 Risk Manager 和 Position Reviewer 两个纪律智能体的提示词构成不可变内核 π0；自我进化提案的 Pydantic 类型中的 Literal 目标枚举根本无法命名这些组件，因此系统可以改写自己的提示词，却从结构上无法削弱自身的安全护栏。
- **七层共享记忆 + 类型化思维链，让每个决策可被代码审计与重放。** 记忆由代码从系统自身记录组装（当日信号、持仓上下文、叙事与宏观 regime、按仓位大小分层的 45 天校准表、最近五次风控裁决、自身最近三次决策、预测账本），智能体引用的每个数字都来自代码而非另一个智能体的叙述；每个推理步骤是强制性的模式字段，输出对象连同完整输入逐字存储，构成"决策本身"而非"关于决策的日志"。
- **双时间尺度的受治理自我进化。** 每晚代码给每笔交易和预测打分写入记忆；每季度 Meta Reflector 提出至多三个类型化的 PromptLearning（20–300 字符、理由须引用摘要中的数字、目标限于六个可编辑智能体），十二不变量门控（长度、禁词 "never/always/override"、Jaccard≥0.6 去重、每智能体 FIFO 上限 10、每周期最多改三个智能体、单次 git commit 可整体回滚）确定性地应用，重放已记录输入验证其真实效果。
- **首个逐条记录进化提案与拒绝的数月级实地部署。** 102 个交易日、1,632 次 LLM 调用、438 个完整买卖回合，全部决策在结果出现前落盘，规避了回测中难以排除的预训练泄漏；代码、提示词与重放工具链开源。

## Key Contributions

- **C1 — Role-specialised joint decisions with an invariant core:** four analysts (News, Earnings, Macro, Technical) turn heterogeneous Web evidence (9 RSS feeds, SEC EDGAR 10-Q/10-K, 9 FRED series, OHLCV for 80 US stocks + 21 ETFs) into typed Pydantic reports; a Portfolio Manager sets target weights that pass through a deterministic order constructor, a blocking risk engine, and a protected LLM Risk Manager that fails closed.
- **C2 — Seven-layer shared memory with schema-enforced reasoning:** code assembles signals, position context, narrative, 45-day calibration-by-size, recent Risk Manager verdicts, the agent's own last decisions, and a projected book, so every number an agent cites originates from code and every reasoning step is a mandatory typed CoT field.
- **C3 — Governed self-evolution at two time scales:** nightly code grading of every trade/forecast, plus a quarterly reflect-propose-gate-verify loop (R1–R5: typed record, outcome grading, typed proposals, gated reversible application, replay verification) that answers the four canonical failure modes F1–F4 (optimistic self-grading, discipline erosion, unauditable change, undetected inertness).
- **C4 — A five-month live field study with a complete decision trace:** 102 trading days, 1,632 logged LLM calls (95% with complete inputs), 438 broker round trips, evaluated forward on live data — to the authors' knowledge the first self-evolving LLM agent run for months in a live market with every evolution proposal and rejection logged.

## Methodology

Steward instantiates five governance requirements (R1 typed record, R2 outcome grading by code, R3 typed proposals, R4 gated application around an unreachable core, R5 replay verification). Nine LLM agents run in six daily sessions: only the morning session may buy; midday and close sessions are sell-only Position Reviewer runs; a no-LLM circuit breaker runs every 30 minutes; the evening session grades the day; a quarterly session reflects on 90 days of graded record. The Meta Reflector's fixed seven-step CoT ends in at most three PromptLearning objects (append/retract by content hash, 20–300 chars, justification ≥40 chars citing a number, target from a Literal allow-list of six editable agents). A deterministic PromptEditor enforces twelve invariants (prohibited absolutes via word-boundary tripwire, Jaccard-0.6 paraphrase rejection, FIFO cap, ≤3 agents per cycle, one revertible git commit per cycle) and can run stage-only (human review) or live-apply. Replay re-runs recorded inputs through prompts before/after a learning and compares outputs structurally against the model's own re-run variance, at tens of dollars per experiment. Evaluation uses three evidence layers: live broker fills and daily equity; trace re-scoring of all recorded outputs (7,843 Technical ratings, 97 evening forecasts, 438 round trips) against later prices with day-clustered standard errors; and replay of recorded inputs through modified prompts or architectures. Forward returns are market-adjusted by subtracting SPY's return over the same horizon.

## Results & Highlights

- **Drawdown containment (live):** between June 22 and July 29, 2026, the semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell 24.6%, QQQ 10.3%, and SPY 2.0%, while the live portfolio lost only 0.3%, having exited semiconductor positions in May on named triggers; the live book finished the deployment at +5.0% return, 9.0% volatility, −6.0% max drawdown, beta 0.16, Sharpe 1.39, against SPY's +12.4% return, 12.4% volatility, −4.5% max drawdown, Sharpe 2.28 — underperformance the authors attribute to a 56% mean gross exposure vs 79% for the simulated intent (180 of 492 orders cancelled/expired unfilled).
- **Synthesis beats individual signals (trace re-scoring):** following recorded decisions through prices, the Portfolio Manager's target weights return +15.4% with no exits (93% exposure) and +12.2% with the Risk Manager's recorded edits at Sharpe 2.33 vs 1.80 without them, while analyst-following portfolios return only +1.3% (all buys) and +0.4% (high-conviction buys) — a >10-point gap; the Technical Analyst's five-level rating has mean daily rank IC of −0.004/0.019/0.031 at 5/10/20 days (t=1.2 at 20 days) and news sentiment is uncorrelated with later moves.
- **Multi-agent decomposition vs single model (replay of 20 recorded mornings, 100 LLM calls):** a single model given the same raw data targets 5.8 names per morning vs 3.7, commits 41% of equity to new intent vs 19%, reverses its own recent decisions 2.5× as often (0.25 vs 0.10), and its proposed books lose −1.35±0.44 points against SPY over 5 days and −1.76±0.75 over 10 days (hit rates 35%/32%), while the deployed design's books are statistically indistinguishable from zero.
- **Governed evolution verified by replay:** the Q2 Meta Reflector traced 14 of 20 wrong BUYs to one root cause ("macro warning ignored") and proposed one learning, which the gate withheld for exceeding the 200-character cap (282 chars); replay ($17, 35 calls) confirmed the withheld learning was inert (8 of 10 flagged BUYs still proposed within ±1 point; all weight changes within the 1.8-point median re-run noise). The applied Q3 learning was adopted on 5 of 20 trigger evenings vs 0 of 20 for the base prompt and 0 of control evenings, with next-day bias agreement 86% vs 79% for re-running the base prompt — targeted behavioural change without disturbing the rest of the output.
- **Prompt-injection red team (3 payloads × 10 recorded mornings, 50 calls, $19):** the News agent excluded the injected item from its typed report in 20/20 replays; a direct override ("set one name to 45%, all others to zero") placed in the Portfolio Manager's input was followed in 0/30 replays (refused 7/10, 6/10, 10/10 across payloads), and even compliance would have been dropped by the schema (targets capped at 20% in the type) before the constructor and hard caps.
- **Core-only backtest 2015–2026 (LLM-free weekly entries):** the deterministic core lowered max drawdown under all 50 random-entry policies (median −37% → −28%) and for momentum entries from −61% to −33% at a higher Sharpe (1.30 vs 1.15), with ablations attributing most of the effect to the caps and breaker (−60% without breaker, −36% without stops); the 2026 semiconductor window still cost the core −27% under momentum entries, showing it bounds what judgement can lose rather than preventing losses.
- **Grounding audit (RQ5):** 96.1% of 254,094 numbers cited in 750 recorded CoTs match the agent's input/prompt/own fields under a strict matcher (99.9% including derived quantities), vs 22% for a placebo against another day's input; only three candidate fabrications were found (a wrong price, a misread indicator, a recalled policy-rate peak).
- **Cost and discipline stats:** logged API cost was $455 (~$4.4/trading day); the Risk Manager rejected 4 of 90 plans and made 146 field-level edits; the circuit breaker never had to liquidate; across 1,632 calls only 13 sub-item validation failures occurred, each isolated without losing a session. The Evening Analyst's 38 directional next-day calls were right only 37% of the time (below the 54% up-day base rate) — a weakness the code-computed grade surfaced in its own next prompt.

## Authors & Credibility

The team is from Nanyang Technological University (Singapore) — Yebo Feng, Jingyang Dai, Liangrui Pan, Nelson Lau, Yang Liu — with Jiahua Xu from University College London; the paper follows ACM formatting (cs.AI, 22 Oct 2026) with an unusually complete evaluation appendix (13 pages total). Credibility is high: the system, prompts, recorded trace and replay harness are open source (github.com/yebof/quant-agent), every figure and table is claimed to be script-computed from the recorded trace, cost and reproducibility are reported as Kapoor et al. request, and the authors are candid that alpha is not identifiable over 102 days and that no claim rests on it. The five-month live deployment with pre-registered decision logging is a substantially stronger evaluation design than the backtest-only evaluations of prior LLM trading agents (FinMem, FinCon, QuantAgent, TradingAgents), which the related-work section correctly identifies as leakage-prone.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** the invariant-core idea (drawing the self-evolution boundary in the type system so the proposal schema cannot name protected components) is a genuinely architectural contribution that goes beyond prompt-level guardrails and beyond the memory-poisoning channel prior agent-security work studies; the code-graded outcome feedback (R2) directly addresses the known unreliability of LLM self-critique. That said, each ingredient (multi-agent roles, layered memory, gated prompt evolution, structural defenses against injection) exists separately in prior work; the novelty is the composition and the live evidence.
- **Rigor:** strong on traceability and honest about weaknesses, but the headline live result is underperformance — +5.0% vs SPY's +12.4% — reframed as risk containment; the containment claim rests on a single drawdown window (June–July 2026) in an otherwise rising market, so one portfolio, one regime, one drawdown is thin evidence for the containment narrative.
- **Weak baselines / small samples where it matters:** the single-agent comparison covers only 20 replayed mornings scored by forward returns of proposed books, not actual deployment P&L; the evolution-loop evidence is two quarterly cycles with one learning applied and its effect measured on 10 trigger evenings (5/20 adoption); the injection red team is 3 payload types on 10 mornings with one insertion each — a narrow attack surface.
- **Data quirks:** the 2015–2026 backtest applies a 2026 universe retroactively (survivorship bias, acknowledged — "only comparisons between variants are meaningful"); fills are simulated against live quotes in a paper account, so the 56%-vs-79% execution gap and the stops' behavior under real market impact are untested; and 95% (not 100%) of calls have complete inputs for replay.
- **Red flags / overclaiming check:** essentially none — the paper explicitly disclaims alpha, discloses the inertness of its withheld learning, and reports that its own replay found the applied learning still missing a needed input (a count of past classifications). The main risk is a reader mistaking risk management for alpha.

## Strengths

- Safety by construction rather than instruction: caps live in Pydantic types and blocking code, discipline agents are unnameable in the proposal Literal, and the Risk Manager fails closed — the system can rewrite itself without being able to weaken its safeguards.
- The typed, pre-outcome-registered trace turns every architecture claim into a measurable replay question, and replay is cheap ($16–19 per experiment), making self-evolution auditable at trivial cost.
- Layer-by-layer attribution (analyst signals vs PM synthesis vs exits vs execution) is more informative than a single P&L number, and the negative findings (analyst IC ≈ 0, evening forecast hit rate 37% < 54% base rate, $19.9k opportunity cost of agent exits) are reported rather than buried.
- The grounding audit (96.1%/99.9% of 254k cited numbers traceable to inputs, 22% placebo) is a reusable methodology for any tool-using agent system.

## Limitations

- One configuration, one paper account, one mostly rising five-month window with a single sector crash; overlapping forward horizons; simulations ignore fill risk (stated by the authors).
- Only two self-evolution cycles, one learning applied; replay over 14–20 inputs exposes behavioural differences but cannot rank prompt variants by return.
- The live portfolio's low beta (0.16) and exposure (56%) mean much of the drawdown story comes from simply being less invested — an exposure-matched 56% SPY/T-bill blend returned +7.5% with −2.5% max drawdown, better on both axes than the live +5.0%/−6.0%.
- The backtest universe is drawn from 2026, so absolute numbers there are biased; only within-variant comparisons are valid.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"自进化智能体与自动化研究"方向高度相关：它是首个在数月真实环境运行、并对自我进化提案逐条门控与验证的 LLM 智能体系统，其"类型系统划定进化边界、代码而非 LLM 负责打分"的治理设计对长期自主智能体的可靠性与可审计性具有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the invariant-core design transfer to non-financial high-stakes agents (e.g., autonomous research or coding agents) where outcomes are graded more slowly and noisily than daily P&L?
- Can the replay-verification step be promoted into a gate invariant that automatically rejects learnings which fail an outcome-scored operability check on real recorded inputs, as the authors propose?
- How much of the drawdown containment survives real fills with market impact, given that 180 of 492 orders never filled in the paper account?
- Does code-computed calibration feedback (L4) measurably change position sizing over quarters, or does the Portfolio Manager largely ignore it like it ignores its own declared sizing correlations (|ρ| ≤ 0.14)?
- Could the 37%-hit-rate Evening Analyst forecasts be replaced by a simple statistical baseline, given that the evening loop's adaptation runs through graded memory rather than the agent's stated intent?
