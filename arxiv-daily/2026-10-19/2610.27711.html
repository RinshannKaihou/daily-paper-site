01Methodologya controlled A/B on the objective

Same backbone, data, LoRA config, hardware and seeds — only the head and loss differ.

  • CLS — randomly initialized two-way linear head on the final-token hidden state; trained with classification loss.
  • S2S (LLM-as-Judge) — keeps the pretrained LM head; generates a one-token “yes/no” verdict via next-token cross-entropy.
  • Matched setup — rank-32 LoRA (α=64) on all projections, 3 epochs, AdamW lr 1e-4, 22,827 examples (RAGTruth + ClaimVerify + FactCheck-GPT), bfloat16, seeds 42/43/44.
  • Evaluation — macro-F1 on a stratified, deduplicated eval-half of six corpora (3 in-distribution; AggreFact, FaithBench, FavaBench OOD). The disjoint probe-half is reserved for analysis only.
  • Four-stage analysis on cached final-token residual streams of every layer: direction-aligned Fisher geometry → per-layer logistic probes + V-information → bootstrap layer-interchangeability + PID redundancy → checkpoint-wise training dynamics (the InfoWindow).
  • From analysis to intervention — InfoWindow + 5-layer margin becomes a static exit depth, physically truncated and retrained; transferred to new backbones as pre-committed depth fractions (0.70 / 0.60) fixed before any probing of the targets.

  • Every rescue of S2S fails — likelihood-ratio scoring moves it <1 point; per-corpus calibration still leaves CLS ahead (+5.3); a trainable LM head adds ≤3 points. Excluding fragile FavaBench, a paired bootstrap gives +2.2 to +3.6 with p ≤ 0.004 on all four backbones.
  • The gap lives in the representations — fresh linear probes on frozen features still trail CLS by 0.13–0.15 F1; CLS probe F1 saturates near the label-entropy ceiling in mid-upper layers while S2S stays close to the pretrained backbone.
  • Upper layers are redundant, not complementary — PID: zero unique information, synergy <0.06 nats (<9% of ln 2); trained fusion probes gain nothing (−0.2 pp ns on 4B, −0.9 pp on 8B).
  • Transfer, pre-committed — depth fractions fixed before any analysis: Llama-3.2-3B L17 +1.7 ± 1.9 (39% saved); Mistral-7B-v0.3 L19 +2.6 ± 2.7 (41% saved); the substrate/window/plateau structure reappears on both, and on QNLI and SST-2.
  • Boundary condition — on QNLI and SST-2, where the backbone already decodes labels at 0.89–0.94 F1, the CLS advantage collapses to +0.02 / +0.00.

03Strengths

  • Truly matched comparison — head and loss are the only variables; reseeding, likelihood scoring, calibration and LM-head adaptation all fail to close the gap. A serious falsification effort.
  • Convergent multi-tool evidence — Fisher geometry, V-information, bootstrap interchangeability, PID and training dynamics tell one coherent mechanism story, not a single diagnostic plot.
  • Analysis becomes intervention — the InfoWindow predicts which exits fail (L15, L20) and which succeed (L25); the rule’s failure modes match the theory.
  • Exemplary hygiene — probe/eval half separation, pre-committed transfer depths, per-seed tables, honest FavaBench failure diagnosis in the appendix.
  • Real wall-clock payoff — 1.42–1.79× measured latency reduction on both H200 and RTX A6000, not just layer counts.

04Limitations

  • Task-specific advantage — near zero on QNLI (+0.02) and SST-2 (+0.00), where the pretrained backbone already decodes labels at 0.89–0.94 F1; the paper cannot predict which tasks show the gap.
  • No external baselines — MiniCheck, SelfCheckGPT, LUNA-style detectors are never compared; “CLS wins” is relative to S2S, not absolute SOTA.
  • Fragile OOD cells — Llama and Mistral OOD results are dominated by FavaBench threshold collapse (OOD Δ std up to ±25.4); Mistral’s six-corpus CLS−S2S delta is actually negative (−2.2 ± 6.6).
  • Under-determined depth prior — two round fractions (0.60/0.70) validated on only two extra backbones; measured saturation depths vary 0.43–0.64.
  • Narrow scope — binary labels, single-token verdicts, decoder-only; rationale-based judges, fine-grained labels, MoE and encoders untested. Single static cutoff; L15 ablation cells are single-seed.

05Questions for further explorationwhere this line of work goes next

  • What task properties determine the size of the CLS–S2S gap — the distance between pretrained decodability and the task ceiling — and can it be predicted before fine-tuning?
  • Does the InfoWindow remain stable under full fine-tuning (not just rank-32 LoRA), different ranks, larger training sets, or longer schedules?
  • Can the InfoWindow criterion combine with input-adaptive early exits — would per-input exits sometimes stop inside vs above the window?
  • Are layers L16–L19 doing a specific, nameable computation — how does the window relate to the virtual residual stream, logit-lens convergence depth, or circuit-level localization?
  • Would fusing window and plateau layers help on harder, fine-grained (multi-class, span-level) hallucination tasks where redundancy assumptions may break?
  • Does the redundant plateau transfer to MoE or encoder–decoder architectures, and does the optimal depth fraction correlate with backbone properties at 10+ model scale?
