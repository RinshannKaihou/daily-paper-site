> **一句话总结：** 在严格匹配训练条件下，分类头微调（CLS）在二分类幻觉检测上全面胜过 LLM-as-Judge 序列到序列微调（S2S）：CLS 会把隐藏状态重组成线性可分的双簇几何结构，任务信号集中在一个狭窄的"InfoWindow"中间层带中形成，其上方各层只是冗余的同步平台——据此删去上部 31% 的层（Qwen3-4B/8B 上 L25 出口）精度不降（-0.5±1.4 / +0.8±1.1 macro-F1），推理延迟降低 1.4 倍。

> **TL;DR:** Under matched LoRA fine-tuning (same data, seeds, hardware), a binary classifier head (CLS) beats LLM-as-Judge single-token generation (S2S) on binary hallucination detection by +3.2/+2.9/+3.7/+1.1 macro-F1 in-distribution on Qwen3-4B/8B, Llama-3.2-3B, and Mistral-7B-v0.3 (positive in 11/12 seed runs; paired bootstrap over six corpora excluding the unstable FavaBench gives +2.2 to +3.6 points, p ≤ 0.004). Layer-wise probing explains why: CLS reorganizes hidden states into a two-cluster geometry that saturates near the label-entropy ceiling in mid-upper layers, while S2S stays close to the pretrained backbone (a fresh linear probe on frozen features still trails CLS by 0.13–0.15 F1). Training dynamics reveal a narrow upper-middle "InfoWindow" (L16–L19) where label information forms, above which layers are a redundant synchronous plateau — so truncating 31% of the network (exit at L25) keeps accuracy within seed noise and cuts latency 1.4×, and the same fixed depth fractions (0.60/0.70) transfer before any analysis to Llama-3.2-3B (+1.7±1.9) and Mistral-7B-v0.3 (+2.6±2.7).

## 核心贡献

- **量化并定位 CLS 与 S2S 的性能差距**：在四个解码器骨干（Qwen3-4B/8B、Llama-3.2-3B、Mistral-7B-v0.3）、六个语料（RAGTruth、ClaimVerify、FactCheck 为训练域内，AggreFact、FaithBench、FavaBench 为域外）上，用匹配的 LoRA 训练条件（rank-32、α=64、3 个种子）证明分类头微调稳定优于单 token 判决生成，且差距无法被似然比打分、verbalizer 集成、按语料阈值校准或可训练 LM 头消除。
- **把差距追溯到目标函数诱导的表征几何**：CLS 沿判别方向形成清晰双簇结构（方向对齐 Fisher 分数更早更高），逐层线性探针和 V-information 显示 CLS 在中上层逼近标签熵上限而 S2S 仅小幅超过预训练骨干；换上全新线性头后 S2S 表征仍落后 CLS 0.13–0.15 F1，说明差距在表征而不在读出头。
- **证明上部层是冗余平台而非互补信息**：bootstrap 置换检验给出饱和层 L*（Qwen3-4B 为 L22/61% 深度，8B 为 L23/64%，95% CI 含零）；偏信息分解（PID）显示最优协同层的独有信息为零、协同 <0.06 nats（<9% 的标签熵 ln2），融合探针相对单层读取无正收益（-0.2pp 不显著、-0.9pp）。
- **发现 InfoWindow 训练动态**：逐层 V-information 轨迹呈现三段结构——底层基底（L1–L15 保持预训练水平）、狭窄 InfoWindow（L16–L19 交错形成任务信号）、上层同步平台（L20–L36 晚期在紧凑步数窗口内同步），说明上层只是已形成信号的冗余载体。
- **把分析转化为早退规则并验证迁移**：保留基底+InfoWindow+稳定边距、删去其上各层的 L25 出口移除 31% 深度，精度在种子噪声内（4B：-0.5±1.4；8B：+0.8±1.1），延迟降 1.4×；固定深度分数（保守 0.70/激进 0.60）在分析前锁定并成功迁移到 Llama-3.2-3B 和 Mistral-7B-v0.3，事后探针确认同样的三段结构，且该结构在 QNLI 和 SST-2 上复现。

## Key Contributions

- **Measures and localizes the CLS–S2S gap under matched conditions.** Across four decoder backbones (Qwen3-4B/8B, Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B-v0.3) and six corpora, with identical LoRA configs, data, and three fine-tuning seeds, classifier-head fine-tuning beats single-token judge generation in-distribution on every backbone; the gap survives likelihood-ratio scoring, verbalizer ensembles, per-corpus threshold calibration, and a LoRA-adapted LM head.
- **Traces the gap to objective-induced representation geometry.** CLS pushes final-token hidden states into a linearly decodable two-cluster structure (earlier and higher direction-aligned Fisher scores), with probe F1 and V-information saturating near the label-entropy ceiling in mid-upper layers; S2S leaves representations close to the pretrained backbone. A readout-swap control (fresh linear probe on frozen features of both models) shows S2S trails by 0.13–0.15 F1, so the gap lives in the representation, not the head.
- **Shows the CLS upper plateau is redundant, not complementary.** Bootstrap layer-interchangeability tests find the saturation layer L* at L22 (Qwen3-4B, 61% of depth) and L23 (Qwen3-8B, 64%), with final-layer-gap CIs containing zero; PID decomposition gives zero unique information and synergy below 0.06 nats (<9% of H(Y)=ln 2); trained fusion probes gain nothing over single-layer readouts (−0.2 pp ns on 4B, −0.9 pp on 8B) — all favoring truncation over layer fusion.
- **Identifies the InfoWindow training dynamic.** Checkpoint-wise per-layer V-information reveals a lower substrate (L1–L15 near pretrained level), a narrow InfoWindow (L16–L19) where label information first accumulates in a depth-staggered fashion, and an upper plateau (L20–L36) that saturates later within a tight training-step cluster — evidence the upper layers inherit rather than construct the task signal.
- **Converts the analysis into a transferable early-exit rule.** Exiting at L25 (InfoWindow + 5-layer margin) removes 31% of Qwen3's 36 blocks with six-corpus accuracy within seed noise and 1.4× lower measured latency; the same depth fractions (⌈0.70L⌉ and round(0.60L)), fixed before any analysis of the targets, yield competitive variants on Llama-3.2-3B (+1.7±1.9 at L17, 39% saved) and Mistral-7B-v0.3 (+2.6±2.7 at L19, 41% saved), and the substrate/InfoWindow/plateau structure reappears on both and on QNLI and SST-2.

## Methodology

The paper compares two fine-tuning paradigms that differ only in head and loss: **S2S** (LLM-as-Judge) keeps the pretrained language-modeling head W_LM and trains the model to generate a verbal verdict ("yes"/"no") with the standard next-token cross-entropy; **CLS** replaces it with a randomly initialized two-way linear head W_CLS on the final hidden state H_L(x) of the last input token. Everything else is held fixed: rank-32 LoRA (α=64) on all projection matrices, 3 epochs, AdamW at lr 1e-4, 22,827 training examples pooled from RAGTruth, ClaimVerify, and FactCheck-GPT, bfloat16, 2048-token context, and seeds 42/43/44. Evaluation is macro-F1 on a stratified, deduplicated eval-half of six corpora (three in-distribution, three OOD: AggreFact, FaithBench, FavaBench); the disjoint probe-half is reserved for analysis, keeping probing and benchmark reporting strictly separated.

The analysis pipeline then proceeds in four stages on cached final-token residual streams of every layer, fed byte-identical inputs to BASE, S2S, and CLS variants. (1) **Geometric separation**: hidden states are projected onto the CLS-defined decision direction w_disc ∝ w1−w0 and an orthogonal axis, and quantified per layer with a direction-aligned Fisher score F_w(ℓ) = (w_disc·(μ1−μ0))² / (w_discᵀ(Σ0+Σ1)w_disc). (2) **Information**: per-layer logistic probes yield probe macro-F1 and V-information I_V(ℓ) = H(Y) − CE(g_ℓ), the label entropy removed by a calibrated linear readout. (3) **Redundancy**: a bootstrap plateau test defines the saturation layer L* as the smallest layer whose F1 gap to the final layer has a 95% CI containing zero (1,000 paired replicates); PID (via a framework-free min-MI bound) decomposes joint V-information of (candidate layer, plateau layer) into redundant/unique/synergy atoms, reported at the synergy-optimal layer ℓ*; a deployment-level fusion test compares fusion probes against single-layer probes with 10,000-sample paired bootstrap stratified by dataset. (4) **Training dynamics**: per-layer probes are refit at every checkpoint, summarized by information gain ΔI_V(ℓ) and 90% saturation time t^0.9_ℓ, exposing the substrate / InfoWindow / plateau temporal structure. Finally, the InfoWindow plus a stabilization margin defines static early-exit depths, which are physically truncated and retrained, and transferred to new backbones as pre-committed depth fractions (0.70 / 0.60) before any probing of those models — an out-of-sample test of the prior.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline matched-condition gap (Table 1)**: in-distribution macro-F1, CLS leads S2S on every backbone — Qwen3-4B 81.0±1.0 vs 77.8±1.0 (+3.2±0.7), Qwen3-8B 81.3±0.7 vs 78.4±0.5 (+2.9±0.7), Llama-3.2-3B 79.1±0.8 vs 75.4±0.8 (+3.7±1.0), Mistral-7B-v0.3 79.0±1.3 vs 77.9±0.2 (+1.1±1.1) — positive in 11 of 12 individual seed runs. Six-corpus "All" average on Qwen3-4B: CLS 75.9±0.5 vs S2S 64.0±0.9 (+11.9±1.2), inflated by FavaBench where CLS scores 96.7±0.5 vs S2S 45.2±4.2 (+51.5±4.0).
- **Gap is robust to stronger S2S readouts (Table 10, Appendix F.2)**: with per-corpus label-shift calibration applied to both paradigms, Qwen3-4B CLS reaches 79.3 vs 74.0 for calibrated S2S (+5.3); uncalibrated CLS@0.5 (75.7) beats fixed verbalizer-ensemble likelihood-ratio S2S (65.3) by +10.4. Likelihood scoring alone moves S2S by <1 point; a fully trainable LM head changes it by ≤3 points.
- **OOD gap is real but unstable**: excluding the imbalanced FavaBench (positive rate 0.20, where S2S predicted-positive rates range 31–89%), a paired bootstrap still gives +2.2 to +3.6 macro-F1 points with p ≤ 0.004 on all four backbones (e.g., Qwen3-4B +3.3 [+1.8, +4.9], p<0.001).
- **Representation evidence**: CLS probe F1 and V-information saturate near the label-entropy ceiling H(Y) in mid-upper layers while S2S plateaus lower; discarding both heads and fitting a fresh linear probe on frozen features, S2S trails CLS by 0.153 / 0.134 / 0.128 / 0.138 F1 on Qwen3-4B / Qwen3-8B / Llama-3.2-3B / Mistral-7B-v0.3 — the gap is in the representation.
- **Plateau redundancy**: saturation layer L* = L22/36 (61% depth) on Qwen3-4B (Δ_final = +0.001 [−0.007, +0.008]) and L23/36 (64%) on Qwen3-8B; at the synergy-optimal layer the unique information is exactly 0 and synergy is 0.053 / 0.049 nats (<9% of ln 2); fusion yields −0.2 pp [−0.6, +0.2] ns on 4B and −0.9 pp [−1.7, −0.0] on 8B.
- **Early exit (Table 4)**: on Qwen3-4B, exit L25 saves 31% of blocks with six-corpus average 75.4±1.1 vs full-depth 75.9±0.5 (Δ = −0.5±1.4, within seed noise); on Qwen3-8B, L25 gives 76.8±0.4 vs 76.0±0.9 (Δ = +0.8±1.1). Cutting too early fails as predicted: L15 loses −9.9 (4B) and −14.4 (8B); the first plateau layer L20 is borderline (−4.5±4.8 on 4B).
- **Measured latency (Appendix F.6)**: physically removing blocks above L25 cuts per-example forward latency by 1.42–1.44×, and above L20 by 1.76–1.79×, on both an H200 and an RTX A6000.
- **Pre-committed transfer**: exit depths fixed from Qwen3's fraction before any probing of the targets — Llama-3.2-3B aggressive L17 (f=0.60) averages 67.5±2.5 vs 65.7±3.3 full (+1.7±1.9, 39% saved); Mistral-7B-v0.3 aggressive L19 gives 67.4±3.7 vs 64.8±6.3 (+2.6±2.7, 41% saved). Post-hoc probing confirms the substrate/InfoWindow/plateau structure on both (saturation at 0.43 and 0.56 of depth) and on Qwen3-4B fine-tuned on QNLI (0.61) and SST-2 (0.50).
- **Task-dependence of the CLS advantage**: on QNLI and SST-2 the pretrained backbone already decodes labels at 0.89–0.94 F1, and the CLS-over-S2S advantage shrinks to 0.02 and 0.00 F1 — the layer structure generalizes but the advantage does not.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Solid but incremental. The CLS-vs-generative-fine-tuning gap is well documented in the classification/verbalizer literature; the novelty lies in the careful causal dissection (Fisher geometry, V-information, PID, training dynamics) and in converting the InfoWindow finding into a representation-based static early-exit rule. The InfoWindow narrative is a memorable and useful lens.
- **Rigor**: Above average for an analysis paper — matched conditions, seed-paired deltas, bootstrap CIs, readout-swap controls, pre-registered transfer depths, and honest reporting of the FavaBench pathology (predicted-positive rates 31–89% vs a true 20% positive rate, single-seed CLS collapses on Mistral/Llama) are all commendable. However, the headline "+11.9 All" on Qwen3-4B is dominated by the +51.5 FavaBench delta, which the authors' own diagnosis attributes largely to a fixed-threshold artifact on an imbalanced corpus; the fairer number (+2.2 to +3.6 excluding FavaBench) is buried in Appendix F.3.
- **Weak spots in the transfer claim**: the "depth prior" is just two round fractions (0.60/0.70), tested on exactly two additional backbones, and the measured saturation layers vary widely (0.43–0.64 of depth). A 0.60–0.70 exit lands on the plateau almost by construction for these models; with only 4 backbones tested (and 36, 36, 28, 32 layers), the prior is under-determined. Mistral-7B's full-depth CLS baseline is itself noisy (±6.3), so its +5.6 conservative / +2.6 aggressive gains carry wide error bars (±5.9/±2.7).
- **Missing comparisons**: there is no comparison against external hallucination detectors (e.g., MiniCheck, SelfCheckGPT, LUNA) — the study compares only two readouts of the same backbone, so "CLS wins" says nothing about absolute SOTA. Nor is the static InfoWindow exit compared against standard early-exit baselines (confidence-based, calibration-based) on the same benchmarks.
- **Scope restrictions**: S2S is restricted to a single verdict token (no rationale-generating judges, acknowledged), only binary labels are studied, and the L15 ablation cells are single-seed (n=1). The CLS advantage itself vanishes on QNLI/SST-2 (0.02/0.00 F1), so the paper's title-level claim is really about hallucination detection, not fine-tuning objectives in general.
- **Red flags**: minor. The abstract's "cuts latency by 1.4×" refers to a 31% depth cut on Qwen3 only; transfer-model latency is not reported. The PID estimator is a "framework-free min-MI bound" whose compatibility conditions are deferred to an appendix — synergy estimates below 0.06 nats are close to estimator noise.

## Strengths

- Genuinely matched comparison design: head and loss are the only variables, with reseeding, likelihood scoring, calibration, and LM-head adaptation all failing to close the gap — a strong falsification effort.
- Multi-tool representational evidence (Fisher separation, V-information, bootstrap interchangeability, PID, training dynamics) converges on one coherent mechanism story rather than a single diagnostic plot.
- The analysis-to-intervention arc is the paper's best feature: the InfoWindow is not just described, it predicts which exits fail (L15, L20) and which succeed (L25), and the rule's failure modes match the theory.
- Excellent experimental hygiene: probe/eval half separation, pre-committed transfer depths, per-seed tables, and an honest appendix diagnosing where its own headline OOD numbers are fragile.
- Practical payoff is real and measured in wall-clock terms (1.42–1.79× latency reduction on both H200 and RTX A6000), not just layer counts.

## Limitations

- The CLS advantage is task-specific: near zero on QNLI (0.02 F1) and SST-2 (0.00 F1) where the pretrained backbone already decodes labels at 0.89–0.94; the paper cannot say which tasks will show the large gap.
- OOD results on Llama-3.2-3B and Mistral-7B-v0.3 are dominated by FavaBench threshold collapses (OOD Δ std up to ±25.4); Mistral's six-corpus CLS−S2S delta is actually negative (−2.2±6.6) in Table 1.
- The depth prior is validated on only two transfer backbones with widely differing relative saturation depths (0.43–0.64); it is a prior over candidates, as the authors concede, not a prescription.
- Only binary hallucination detection, single-token S2S verdicts, and decoder-only backbones; rationale-based judges, fine-grained labels, and encoder or MoE architectures are untested.
- The early exit is a single static cutoff shared by all inputs; no combination with input-adaptive exits, distillation, or pruning is evaluated, and no external detector baselines are compared.
- Three fine-tuning seeds is thin for the noisier OOD cells; some early-exit ablation rows (L15) are single-seed.

## Relevance

这篇论文的核心方法——逐层线性探针、V-information、表征几何与训练动态分析——正属于"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：它系统刻画了微调目标如何重塑隐藏状态中层间信息分布，并把可解释性分析直接转化为早退/层裁剪的工程规则，与关注 probing 与内部表征的工作高度契合。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What task properties determine the size of the CLS–S2S gap — is it the gap between pretrained decodability and the task ceiling (0.89–0.94 on QNLI/SST-2 vs ~0.6–0.8 here), and can this be predicted before fine-tuning?
- Does the InfoWindow remain stable under full fine-tuning (not just rank-32 LoRA), different LoRA ranks, larger training sets, or longer schedules?
- Can the InfoWindow criterion be combined with input-adaptive early exits, and would per-input exits sometimes want to stop inside vs. above the window?
- How does the InfoWindow relate to known layer-wise phenomena like the "virtual residual stream", logit-lens convergence depth, or circuit-level localization — are the L16–L19 layers doing a specific, nameable computation?
- Would fusing InfoWindow layers with upper plateau layers help on harder, fine-grained (multi-class, span-level) hallucination tasks where redundancy assumptions may break?
- Does the redundant plateau transfer to MoE or encoder-decoder architectures, where depth–width tradeoffs and routing differ?
- Can the same pre-committed depth-fraction prior be validated at scale (10+ backbones), and does the optimal fraction correlate with backbone properties like aspect ratio or pretraining data mix?
