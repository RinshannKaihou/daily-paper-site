> **一句话总结：** OrbitWeaver 针对低轨卫星上商用载荷（COTS Jetson）因电源/热约束、FDIR 故障重启和载荷争用导致的"总线强制关机"打断 VLM 推理的问题，提出按阶段（视觉编码/LLM prefill/解码）感知的分块检查点与依赖感知的动态恢复调度（动态规划求最优 I/O-计算重叠次序，<100 ms 出计划），在真实卫星载荷硬件 + 在轨遥测中断轨迹上实现最高 4.07× 端到端加速、SSD 写量最多降至基线 1/3.56、正常运行开销 <5%，并在两颗 LEO 卫星上 72 小时部署、91 次强制关机下完成全部 50 个请求。

> **TL;DR:** Running VLMs (Qwen3-VL 2B/4B/8B, MiniCPM-V 4.5) on COTS LEO-satellite payloads means bus-enforced shutdowns (power/thermal cutoffs, FDIR reboots up to once per minute, payload contention) destroy volatile KV caches mid-request. OrbitWeaver checkpoints phase-aware recoverable units (layer-wise for ViT, block-wise G×C grid for prefill, batched KV for decode) to SSD with crash-consistent commit footers, and after each reboot runs a subset-DP recovery planner (optimal layer-I/O order, <100 ms for Qwen3-VL 8B) that overlaps weight/KV reads with dependency-ready recomputation. On Jetson Orin NX/AGX replaying 5 interruption traces (Sat-1/Sat-2 telemetry + Dellingr) with LRS-VQA inputs, it achieves up to 4.07× end-to-end speedup over SONIC-layer (geomean 2.95×; 1.98× vs SONIC-chunk, 1.65× vs HCache), 1.22×–6.97× faster recovery, up to 3.56× fewer SSD writes, at <5% runtime and 3.75%–4.74% energy overhead; a 72-hour in-orbit deployment on two satellites completed all 50 requests across 91 shutdowns.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统刻画"总线强制关机"对星上 VLM 推理的瓶颈**：基于两颗私有 LEO 卫星（Sat-1/Sat-2）遥测，量化了三类不可预测中断——动态电源/热约束（推理 48 秒内将载荷温度从 45°C 推到 55°C 阈值触发断电）、FDIR 故障重启（最坏每分钟一次）、高优先级载荷抢占总线资源；一个 3 分钟任务窗口往往只能处理 8 帧图像（>32K token）。
- **阶段感知的高效检查点（OrbitWeaver 核心）**：发现 LLM prefill 中 KV cache 是长寿命上下文、隐状态是短寿命边界态的结构性质，据此设计 token-chunk × layer-group 的二维"块级"检查点，兼得 layer-wise 的低重算与 chunk-wise 的低写放大；视觉编码因双向注意力只能按层边界检查点；解码阶段按 D 个 token 批量持久化 KV 切片。配 G=4 时丢失进度仅约 2.04×10³ token-layer，检查点体积 8.96 GiB（Qwen3-VL 2B）。
- **依赖感知的动态恢复调度**：将恢复形式化为带前驱约束的 I/O-计算双机调度（一般情形 NP-hard），利用 prefill 规则依赖结构（隐状态读入打包进计算、同层 I/O 打包）降维到层序 subset-DP，O(L·2^L) 时间内求最优层加载顺序，Qwen3-VL 8B 在 G≥2 时 <100 ms 出计划。
- **崩溃一致性存储布局**：检查点按层分组连续布局 + 追加式 KV 布局，pwritev2() O_DIRECT 对齐 NVMe AWUPF 的校验和 commit footer，保证写一半断电后只接受完整记录；可选 KIVI/TurboQuant KV 量化压缩将解码段恢复延迟最多降 5.02×。
- **首个在轨持续运行 VLM 推理的学术部署**：72 小时、两颗卫星、25+25 个 32K-token 遥感 QA 请求，经历 91 次强制关机后全部完成（无检查点基线反复归零、三窗口后仍卡在 prefill）。

## Key Contributions

- First characterization of **bus-enforced shutdowns** as the key bottleneck for on-satellite VLM inference, grounded in private telemetry from two LEO satellites: thermal cutoffs 48 s after inference starts (45°C→55°C), FDIR reboots up to once per minute in worst case, and contention from intermittent high-priority payloads — all decoupled from inference phase boundaries and impossible to profile offline.
- **Phase-aware checkpointing**: a block-wise G-layer × C-token grid for LLM prefill that exploits the long-lived-KV / short-lived-hidden-state structure (vs. layer-wise which over-persists hidden states at 14.34 GiB for Qwen3-VL 2B, and chunk-wise which cannot resume inside a chunk and loses up to 14.34×10³ token-layers); layer-wise boundaries for ViT (bidirectional attention invalidates patch chunks); batched decode KV checkpoints (per-token writes are prohibitively fragmented for memory-bound decoding).
- **Dependency-aware dynamic recovery**: recovery from an arbitrary "staircase frontier" of completed blocks is formalized as precedence-constrained two-machine scheduling (NP-hard in general); three structural reductions (deferred hidden-state loads, greedy compute dispatch, packed layer I/O) shrink it to a layer-order subset DP solving optimally in O(L·2^L), within 100 ms for Qwen3-VL 8B (G ≥ 2).
- **Crash-consistent SSD layout + optional KV quantization**: contiguous per-group checkpoint records, append-ordered KV layout, checksum-protected commit footers written atomically via pwritev2() on O_DIRECT aligned to the NVMe AWUPF; asynchronous KIVI/TurboQuant KV compression cuts decode-stage recovery latency by up to 5.02×.
- **First academic in-orbit VLM deployment**: 72 hours on Sat-1 and Sat-2, 50 requests (25 each, Qwen3-VL 2B, 32K-token inputs, 64 generated tokens, 20-min timeout), 41 requests interrupted by 91 bus-enforced shutdowns — all 50 completed, while a no-checkpoint run resets to zero and stays in prefill after three windows.

## Methodology

The system (9,617 lines Python + 1,741 lines CUDA/C++ on top of HuggingFace transformers, single-request/single-GPU to match the payload setting, portable to vLLM/SGLang) is built around two coordinated mechanisms:

1. **Efficient checkpointing (§4).** For prefill, the input sequence is split into chunks of C tokens and layers into groups of G, forming a 2-D grid of recoverable units; on unit completion only the unit's KV tensors plus the layer-group boundary hidden states are persisted. G and C are picked per model-device pair by lightweight offline calibration on LRS-VQA under a user-set checkpoint-overhead budget (G=4 yields ~2.04×10³ token-layers lost progress with 8.96/13.82 GiB checkpoints for Qwen3-VL 2B / MiniCPM-V 4.5). Vision encoding checkpoints full-patch hidden states at layer boundaries since bidirectional attention makes patch chunks invalid units. Decoding persists KV slices batched over D tokens plus token IDs and position, bounding replay to ≤D steps. A phase-specific SSD layout separates weights/KV/hidden states to avoid interleaved reads, and every record carries a checksum footer committed via pwritev2() on O_DIRECT (AWUPF-aligned) for crash consistency.
2. **Dynamic recovery (§5).** After a shutdown the completed blocks form a staircase frontier f1 ≥ f2 ≥ … ≥ fL across layers; blocks left of the frontier are restored from SSD, blocks beyond it must be recomputed. Fixed orders expose I/O: chunk-first stalls when switching layers before weights arrive; layer-first stalls on unavailable inputs. OrbitWeaver formulates recovery as minimizing exposed I/O over I/O + compute tasks; exploiting prefill's regular dependencies (hidden states consumed only by the block above; work-conserving compute order suffices once I/O order is fixed; per-layer reads packable) reduces the problem to ordering O(L) layer-I/O operations, solved optimally by subset dynamic programming over loaded-layer sets in O(L·2^L). The planner reorders reads so dependency-ready blocks recompute while later weights/KV stream in.

**Evaluation setup:** Jetson Orin NX (16 GB) and AGX Orin (64 GB) mirroring the two payloads; Qwen3-VL 2B/4B/8B and MiniCPM-V 4.5 all W4A16 (GPTQ + Marlin); LRS-VQA remote-sensing VQA (avg. 7,099×6,329 px); 32K-token contexts on Orin, 64K on AGX, 64 decode tokens; five interruption traces A–E (four from Sat-1/Sat-2 telemetry with 36.4–121.7 s mean windows, one from NASA's Dellingr at 63.0 s); shutdowns kill the process and clear volatile state, resuming from the latest committed checkpoint, capped at 20 trace replays. Baselines: no-checkpoint, two re-implemented SONIC variants (layer/chunk-grained intermittent DNN runtime — SONIC itself does not support GPUs), and re-implemented HCache (datacenter hidden-state KV-recovery; not open-sourced). Metrics: end-to-end completion time, cold-cache recovery latency, SSD writes, device-level write amplification via MQSim replay, energy via tegrastats.

## Results & Highlights

- **End-to-end completion under interruptions (Traces A–E):** OrbitWeaver completes every evaluated request with speedups of up to **4.07× over SONIC-layer, 2.89× over SONIC-chunk, and 2.67× over HCache**; geometric-mean speedups are **2.95× / 1.98× / 1.65×** respectively. In the harshest Trace A (Sat-1, 36.4 s mean window), **only OrbitWeaver completes** Qwen3-VL 4B on Orin and MiniCPM-V 4.5 on AGX — all baselines remain incomplete after 20 trace replays; SONIC also fails these setups in Trace B.
- **Recovery latency:** across all devices, models, and 20–80% prefill-progress checkpoints, OrbitWeaver recovers **1.22×–6.97× faster** than the baselines; e.g., on AGX with Qwen3-VL 4B at 20% progress, SONIC-layer/SONIC-chunk/HCache take **6.85× / 6.36× / 4.40×** as long, and even at 80% progress with MiniCPM-V 4.5 they take **2.95× / 2.56× / 1.50×** as long. The DP recovery planner itself finishes within **100 ms** for Qwen3-VL 8B (G≥2).
- **SSD wear:** for Qwen3-VL 2B on Orin across 2K–32K prompts, SONIC-layer/SONIC-chunk/HCache write **2.33×–3.56× / 1.08×–2.13× / 1.10×–2.20×** as much data as OrbitWeaver, and OrbitWeaver cuts device-level write amplification (DLWA) by **33.6–45.4% / 14.3–46.4% / 15.0–46.2%** vs. the three baselines — critical because eMMC/SSD endurance on satellites is a scarce resource.
- **Normal-path cost:** uninterrupted inference stays **within 5%** of no-checkpoint latency (up to 2.63× / 2.16× / 1.64× faster than the baselines' uninterrupted runs; geomean 1.62× / 1.38× / 1.08×), with only **3.75%–4.74% extra energy** (Qwen3-VL 2B on Orin, 2K–32K prompts) — the persistence tax for making progress survive resets.
- **72-hour in-orbit deployment (Sat-1 + Sat-2):** 50 requests, 41 interrupted, **91 bus-enforced shutdowns survived (47 on Sat-1, 44 on Sat-2), 50/50 completed** within the 20-minute timeout, whereas the no-checkpoint baseline's progress resets to zero after every shutdown and remains stuck in prefill after three windows.
- **Checkpoint design trade-off quantified (Fig. 5):** layer-wise needs ~14.34 GiB (Qwen3-VL 2B) / 27.65 GiB (MiniCPM-V 4.5) of checkpoint storage; chunk-wise loses up to 14.34×10³ / 18.43×10³ token-layers of progress; block-wise with G=4 achieves ~2.04×10³ token-layers lost at 8.96 / 13.82 GiB. Optional KIVI/TurboQuant KV compression further cuts decode-stage recovery latency by up to **5.02×**.

## Authors & Credibility

A PKU + BUPT collaboration (Xiaolong Huang, Yinyuan Zhang, Mengwei Xu, Xuanzhe Liu, Gang Huang at Peking University; Daliang Xu — corresponding author, Yun Ma, Qing Li, Shangguang Wang at BUPT) from groups with a long track record in mobile/edge AI systems and satellite computing. Credibility is unusually strong for a systems paper: the interruption traces come from telemetry of their own two LEO satellites (Sat-1/Sat-2) plus NASA's public Dellingr trace, the hardware mirrors actual payloads (Jetson Orin NX / AGX Orin), and the paper closes the loop with a real 72-hour two-satellite in-orbit deployment rather than lab-only emulation. Claims are consistently scoped ("first academic work to deploy and sustain VLM inference directly in orbit", with NASA Prithvi and ESA Phi-Sat-2 correctly cited as agency initiatives). The systems engineering is substantial (checkpoint DP scheduler, crash-consistent O_DIRECT footers, DLWA measurement via MQSim, energy via tegrastats).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core ideas are a competent synthesis rather than a radical departure: block-wise (layer×token) checkpointing echoes tensor-parallel activation checkpointing ideas, and intermittent computing (Mementos, DINO, Chain, SONIC) established checkpoint/replay long ago. The genuine novelty is (a) the phase-aware treatment specific to VLM pipelines (ViT layer-wise due to bidirectional attention; prefill block-wise due to the KV/hidden-state lifetime split; batched decode KV) and (b) the optimal layer-I/O-ordering DP for staircase-frontier recovery — plus being first to demonstrate VLM inference surviving real orbital shutdowns.
- **Rigor — re-implemented baselines:** SONIC does not support GPUs and HCache is not open-source, so all three baselines are the authors' re-implementations "as described in their papers" on transformers. This is disclosed honestly, but baseline fidelity is unverifiable, and the baselines were ported onto hardware regimes (GPU + multi-GiB KV) they were never designed for — the headline 4.07× (and even the "baseline incomplete after 20 replays" results in Traces A/B) partly measures baseline-platform mismatch, not just algorithmic superiority. No vLLM/SGLang-native checkpointing baseline (e.g., full-KV snapshot at request boundaries) is included.
- **Missing evaluation — output accuracy:** the entire evaluation is time/bytes/energy; no LRS-VQA answer accuracy is reported after recovery. Deterministic replay plausibly preserves outputs, but the optional KIVI (2-bit) / TurboQuant KV compression path changes numerics, and its effect on VQA quality after recovery is never measured — a real gap for a paper whose value proposition is "transparent" resumption.
- **Missing ablations:** no sensitivity analysis of the batch size D for decode checkpointing or the calibration robustness of G/C when workload shifts away from the LRS-VQA calibration set; energy overhead is only measured for one model on one device; the 100 ms planner bound is only stated for Qwen3-VL 8B with G≥2.
- **Reproducibility:** Sat-1/Sat-2 telemetry is private (Dellingr is public), and no code/artifact release is mentioned — the in-orbit results are effectively unauditable, though the ground-based Jetson experiments could in principle be reproduced if code were released.
- **Significance & red flags:** significance is high within orbital edge computing and honest about scope (explicitly excludes radiation-induced persistent-storage corruption). Minor overclaim risk: "up to 4.07×" is a per-trace max over completed baseline runs while geomeans are 1.65×–2.95×; and single-request, single-GPU execution means nothing is learned about serving multiple concurrent requests, which future "space data centers" (Starcloud) will face.

## Strengths

- Problem grounded in first-party on-orbit telemetry, with concrete numbers (48 s to thermal cutoff, worst-case once-per-minute FDIR reboots) rather than synthetic interruption models.
- The phase-aware checkpoint design is well-motivated by a clean insight (KV = long-lived context, hidden states = short-lived boundary state) and the storage–recomputation trade-off is quantified, not hand-waved.
- The recovery scheduling reduction (NP-hard two-machine scheduling → O(L·2^L) subset DP via three dependency observations) is elegant and comes with a hard real-time bound (<100 ms) that matters on 36 s execution windows.
- Comprehensive systems evaluation along five axes (time, recovery, SSD writes, DLWA, energy) plus crash-consistency engineering (checksummed commit footers, AWUPF-aligned atomic writes).
- Rare and valuable real-world validation: a 72-hour deployment on two actual satellites surviving 91 unplanned shutdowns.

## Limitations

- Single-request, single-GPU prototype; no batching, scheduling, or multi-tenant serving — the regime of projected space data centers is untested.
- Baselines are author re-implementations running outside their original design domains (SONIC: CPU/embedded; HCache: datacenter context switching).
- No task-accuracy evaluation after recovery, and none at all for the KV-quantization-assisted decode recovery despite 2-bit KIVI being in the loop.
- G/C calibration is per model-device pair on LRS-VQA prefill inputs; sensitivity to distribution shift or drastically different image resolutions is unexplored.
- Scale ceiling: models ≤8B, contexts ≤64K tokens, COTS eMMC/SSD; behavior with larger models/contexts or flash wear-out over months of checkpoint churn is extrapolated, not measured. Radiation-induced persistent-storage corruption is explicitly out of scope.

## Relevance

与"推理可靠性 / 大规模 ML 计算基础设施"方向高度相关：这是在极端边缘硬件（星载 Jetson）上做推理级容错的工作——故障检测触发的不预测重启下，通过检查点/恢复机制保证 LLM/VLM 推理进度不丢失，属于"任意硬件平台上的节点故障检测与恢复"和推理可靠性的交叉；其对 KV cache 检查点粒度与恢复 I/O 调度的分析，对地面断电/抢占场景下的可恢复推理系统也有直接借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does checkpoint-assisted recovery (especially the 2-bit KIVI / TurboQuant compressed KV path) preserve VQA accuracy bit-for-bit or degrade outputs, and by how much on LRS-VQA after repeated recovery cycles?
- How does the approach extend to concurrent multi-request serving on a shared payload GPU — does the staircase-frontier DP generalize to interleaved frontiers, and what does checkpoint isolation cost?
- Can the recovery planner stay within its 100 ms budget as L (layer groups) grows with 30B+ models, or does the O(2^L) subset DP need an approximation (e.g., beam search) for next-generation payloads?
- What is the long-run SSD/eMMC endurance cost under months of checkpoint churn at the measured write rates, and does the DLWA model hold as devices age in the radiation and thermal environment?
- Could shutdown-timing prediction (orbital-state-aware thermal/power forecasting) let the runtime proactively choose checkpoint granularity — e.g., pre-emptively coarsen checkpoints ahead of predicted long eclipses — and beat the fixed calibrated G/C policy?
