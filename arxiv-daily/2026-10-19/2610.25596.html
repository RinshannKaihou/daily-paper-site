★

The abstract oversells the graph-vs-map dichotomy — the authors' own experiments show a location-based component coexists, and they admit they cannot yet explain what decides between them.

Methodology

How the claim was forced, not inferred — every experiment adjudicates between competing hypotheses.

All influence of the stated relationships on a response flows through two objects: the activation tensor A and the key–value memory M derived from it. So "does the model have a cognitive map?" becomes "how is information organized inside A and M?"

  • Mosaic experiments — record decoder-block activations (forward pre-hooks) while the model reads two prompts sharing structure but differing in names; transplant donor rows (or KV pairs) into the control tensor, freeze them, and read answer-token probabilities. Opposing hypotheses predict opposite answers, so each splice cleanly adjudicates.
  • Coverage — 11 open-weight instruct models (Gemma-3, Llama-3.1, Qwen3) from 0.6B to 70B × 14 tasks in 4 relational families; 50 random entity assignments per condition; single-token, space-padded names keep positions aligned.
  • Confound control — permuting positional codes rules out position-encoding artifacts (Figs. S1–S2); who-is vs yes/no query formats replicate with the predicted strategy shift.
  • KV localization (Algorithm 1) — gradient-guided iterative pruning over continuous keep-masks, scored by answer logit margin, greedy coarse-then-fine erasure. A sufficiency, not necessity, analysis — stated explicitly by the authors.

Results

What the splices established, beyond the ~1% headline.

  • Scale threshold — accuracy on unstated-relation queries rises from ~600M to 70B; beyond ~10B it holds even under meaningless synthetic names (systematicity, Fig. 1C–D).
  • Binding beats tagging by orders of magnitude — one spliced donor row puts probability ratio ~1010 on binding-consistent answers vs ~10−10 on attribute-tagging; holds across all 14 settings and 11 models.
  • Additive geometry, verified causally — a role-swap vector δ added/subtracted across two entity rows makes the model treat them as having exchanged structural roles (parallelogram composition).
  • Retrospective edges dominate — two-hop answers follow the control prompt (relations stored as pairwise edges, composed at query time), except family-tree cases where answers drift toward the donor (a prospective/location component; trajectory PCA shows history-dependence).
  • Unidirectional false memory — editing 35 role→name values leaves "Alice's daughter → Beth" intact while flipping "Beth's mother → Daisy": a belief no consistent family tree can explain, affecting one query direction only.

Strengths

What makes this evidence hard to dismiss.

  • Causal, not correlational — intervention-based probing with genuinely competing pre-stated predictions for every experiment.
  • Exceptional replication breadth — 11 models, 3 families, 0.6B–70B; 14 tasks, 4 relational families; two query formats.
  • Reusable technique — gradient-guided KV pruning with rewinding elegantly localizes sufficient information-carrying sites in attention memory.
  • Honest anomaly reporting — prospective-composition exceptions and the sufficiency-not-necessity caveat are stated by the authors themselves.
  • Open science — code, data, recorded model revisions, and full mosaic-construction specs released.

Limitations

Read the claim no wider than the evidence.

  • Toy scale only — 2-edge prompts, single-token names, aligned positions; multi-token entities, longer chains, and real relational corpora untested.
  • Loose map analogy — the core representation is a bound graph with retrospective composition, not a metric map; the prospective component is left unexplained.
  • Numbers from one cell — 112/131 pairs, ~1%, 143/16, 35 edits all come from a single model–task combination; per-model summaries are not tabulated.
  • Instruct-tuned models only — base checkpoints and frontier models unexamined; the ~10B threshold is observational, not a controlled scale comparison.
  • No baselines — not situated quantitatively against knowledge-editing or probing accounts (ROME/MEMIT lineage).

Questions for Further Exploration

Open threads a follow-up (or you) could pull.

  1. Does the additive a(n,s) ≈ φname(n) + φrole(s) decomposition survive multi-token names and 3+-edge chains, where token-position alignment breaks?
  2. What decides, at query time, between graph-based (retrospective) and location-based (prospective) information — can the choice be predicted or causally forced?
  3. Do base (non-instruct) checkpoints at matched scale show the same structure/identity separation, isolating what instruction tuning contributes?
  4. Can the sparse name→role pairs be found without behavioral pruning — via SAE features or attention-head attribution — and do they coincide with induction / name-mover heads?
  5. Does chain-of-thought restructure the cognitive map mid-inference, and can the unidirectional false-memory edit scale into faithful relational model-editing?
