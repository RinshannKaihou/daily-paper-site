> **一句话总结：** 本文发现 LLM（7B–72B，包括化学专用模型）对同一分子的通俗名、IUPAC 名和 SMILES 三种指称会暴露完全不同的知识子集——即使模型能从 SMILES 认出分子，其治疗适应症知识仍无法经 SMILES 直接检索（作者称之为"跨指称符绑定失败"）；激活修补证明失败在于指称符与知识的绑定而非知识缺失，并提出 Binding Self-Distillation（用模型自身的"名称条件访问行为"做 KL 自蒸馏训练轻量 LoRA），在 ChemDFM-v2.0-14B 上把 SMILES 直接查询的适应症准确率从 2.1% 提升到 51.4%，恢复名称路由 86% 的差距且推理时无需名称。

> **TL;DR:** The authors build ChemIDBench (896 ChEMBL drugs, each queried by trivial name / IUPAC name / SMILES) and show a systematic double dissociation across seven 7B–72B LLMs: on ChemDFM-v2.0-14B, therapeutic-indication accuracy collapses from 38.8% via trivial name to 2.1% via SMILES, while benzene-ring detection rises from 76.7% to 94.6% (McNemar p < 1e-7 on all 7 models) — and the failure persists even on molecules the model correctly recognizes from SMILES (4.0% vs 42.7%). Activation patching causally confirms the knowledge is present but unreachable through the structural route (peak recovery 76.4–138.4% of the log-prob gap); standard editors (ROME, FT) show reliable transfer in only 2/8 configs. Their Binding Self-Distillation LoRA distills the model's own name-conditioned access into the SMILES route, lifting indication retrieval from 2.1% to 51.4% (86% of the name-route gap) without the name at inference.

## 核心贡献

- **ChemIDBench 基准**：从 3,025 条 ChEMBL 获批药物-适应症记录出发，经严格实体清洗（去盐、去多片段、去重、去无效结构）得到 896 个分子，每个分子配备匹配的通俗名、IUPAC 名（PubChem 解析）和规范 SMILES；在固定分子与查询属性、仅更换指称符的条件下评测 7 个 7B–72B 模型（含 ChemDFM-v1.5/v2.0 两个化学专用模型）。
- **发现"双重解离"**：词汇名（通俗名）对实体关联事实知识（治疗适应症）访问最强，而结构描述符（SMILES/IUPAC）对可由结构计算的性质（苯环检测、氮原子计数等，RDKit 生成真值）访问更强——指称符的选择改变的是"哪些知识可达"，而非单纯的查询难度。
- **提出并证明"跨指称符绑定失败"**：通过 2AFC 与自由生成两种识别测试证明，模型能从 SMILES 正确认出分子（映射到正确通俗名），却仍无法经 SMILES 检索该分子的事实——实体识别、知识拥有、指称符条件访问三者可分离。
- **因果与表征证据链**：词汇桥接（SMILES+名称恢复 91.8–99.4% 差距）、冲突指称符实验（名称控制身份/适应症、SMILES 控制苯环判断）、激活修补（词法状态移植因果性恢复检索）、表征探测（Name→SMILES 检索仅 0.7–2.8%，而 IUPAC→SMILES 达 13.5–15.0%）。
- **诊断驱动的修复方法 Binding Self-Distillation**：不教新事实，而是把模型自身"名称条件下的预测行为"经 KL 蒸馏迁移到 SMILES 路由（逐分子 LoRA，冻结教师即原模型），推理时移除名称；在 7 个模型上均大幅提升 SMILES 直接事实检索。
- **跨域复现**：在蛋白质域（UniProt 不透明登录号 vs 基因符号，16,388 个人类蛋白条目）重复关键行为实验，表明该现象不依赖于结构记号本身。

## Key Contributions

- **ChemIDBench**: a controlled benchmark of 896 approved-drug molecules (from 3,025 ChEMBL drug–indication records after entity cleaning), each carrying matched trivial name, IUPAC name, and canonical SMILES; entity-associated queries (identification, therapeutic indication) vs. structure-computable queries (formula, benzene ring, nitrogen count, ring count, heavy atoms, ground truth from RDKit); seven LLMs from 7B to 72B including two chemistry-specialized models (ChemDFM-v1.5-8B, ChemDFM-v2.0-14B).
- **A systematic double dissociation**: lexical names are the strongest interface to entity-associated factual knowledge, while structure-descriptive identifiers are the strongest interface to structure-computable properties — identifier choice changes *which* knowledge is reachable, not just query difficulty.
- **Cross-identifier binding failure**: using 2AFC and free-form recognition protocols, the paper shows a model can generate the correct trivial name from SMILES yet still fail to retrieve that molecule's facts through SMILES — separating entity recognition, knowledge possession, and identifier-conditioned access.
- **Multi-method causal diagnosis**: lexical-bridge controls (supplying the name recovers 91.8–99.4% of the gap), conflicting-identifier experiments (name drives identity/indication answers while SMILES drives benzene verdicts, usually undetected), activation patching (lexical-route states causally restore retrieval; controls all negative), and cross-identifier retrieval probing (Name→SMILES top-1 only 0.7–2.8% vs. 13.5–15.0% for IUPAC→SMILES).
- **Negative result on standard knowledge editing**: ROME and MLP fine-tuning edits made through the name propagate to SMILES beyond leakage in only 2 of 8 model–editor configurations — modifying the fact is not the same as repairing the access route.
- **Binding Self-Distillation (BSD)**: a per-entity LoRA adapter distilled from the model's own frozen, name-augmented teacher pass into the SMILES-only student pass (full-vocabulary KL, no factual labels), substantially improving SMILES-only factual retrieval across all seven models; a protein-domain replication with UniProt accessions suggests generality beyond chemistry.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds as diagnosis-then-fix. **Benchmark**: molecules are drawn from ChEMBL approved drug–indication records, cleaned to unambiguous single entities, and each is queried three ways (trivial name, IUPAC name, SMILES) with greedy decoding; entity-associated answers are scored against normalized accepted answers, structure-computable answers against RDKit-computed truth. **Recognition controls**: 2AFC (choose the right trivial name between two candidates, retained only if correct across three decoy conditions) and a stricter free-form generation test isolate molecules whose SMILES–name correspondence the model demonstrably knows. **Interventions**: (1) lexical bridging — append the correct trivial name to the SMILES prompt (no indication information); (2) conflicting identifiers — pair molecule A's SMILES with molecule B's name and track which route controls which answer type; (3) activation patching — replace the residual-stream state at the final prompt token of a SMILES query with the state from the matched trivial-name query, measuring recovery as the fraction of the teacher-forced name–SMILES log-probability gap closed, with different-molecule patches as controls; (4) representation probing — use one identifier's hidden state to retrieve its co-referring SMILES among all 896 candidates across depths. **Editing diagnostic**: ROME and 25-step MLP down-projection fine-tuning insert counterfactual indications via the name; transfer to SMILES is reported with leakage subtracted (T_specific = T_SMILES − T_leak). **Binding Self-Distillation**: teacher context [SMILES(s); "known as n"; stem] vs. student context [SMILES(s); stem] through the same frozen model; a LoRA (r=8, α=16, MLP gate/up/down projections, layers 20–24, target layer 24; layer 48 for Qwen2-72B) is trained for 120 AdamW steps per molecule with 6 sampled generic continuation stems per step and 8 anchor molecules (KL weight 1.0) preserving unrelated behavior. Downstream evaluation prompts are excluded from the 24-stem training pool; no factual labels, generated answers, or external teachers are used.

## Results & Highlights

- **Double dissociation (ChemIDBench, Table 1)**: for ChemDFM-v2.0-14B, therapeutic-indication accuracy falls from 38.8% under the trivial name to 2.1% under SMILES (−36.7 pts), while benzene-ring detection on the same molecules rises from 76.7% under the name to 94.6% under SMILES (+17.9 pts). Across all seven models SMILES beats the trivial name on benzene detection by 13.3–28.3 percentage points; both directions significant by paired McNemar tests at p < 1e-7 for every model.
- **Recognition does not imply retrieval (Table 2)**: on the 576 molecules ChemDFM-v2.0-14B correctly recognizes under 2AFC, indication accuracy is 4.0% via SMILES vs. 42.7% via trivial name; under free-form recognition (62 molecules correctly named from SMILES), 3.2% via SMILES vs. 40.3% via name.
- **Knowledge is present but unreachable (Table 3)**: adding the correct trivial name to the SMILES prompt recovers 91.8–99.4% of the SMILES–name gap across models — ChemDFM-v2.0-14B rises from 2.1% to 36.3% (name-only: 38.6%); Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct from 1.3% to 37.6% (name-only: 37.8%).
- **Conflicting identifiers split behavior (Table 4)**: with A's SMILES plus B's name, ChemDFM-v2.0-14B returns B's identity in 85.5% of cases with 32.3% indication accuracy for B, while benzene accuracy for A stays at 95.3% (vs. 94.5% from A's SMILES alone); only 17.5–43.1% of similar-but-wrong names are rejected, and two-choice identification accuracy is just 56.4–73.9%.
- **Causal restoration (Table 5)**: activation patching the lexical-route state closes 76.4%–138.4% of the name–SMILES log-probability gap at peak (relative depths 0.50–0.83); every different-molecule control patch is strongly negative (−19.8 to −208.2).
- **Representation asymmetry (Figure 2)**: peak Name→SMILES top-1 retrieval among 896 candidates (chance 0.11%) is only 0.7–2.8% for all models, whereas IUPAC→SMILES reaches 13.5% (ChemDFM-v2.0-14B) and 15.0% (ChemDFM-v1.5-8B); chemistry specialization raises IUPAC→SMILES from 3.9%→15.0% (Llama-3-8B→ChemDFM-v1.5) but Name→SMILES only 0.8%→2.8%.
- **Editing fails to transfer (Table 7)**: after editing counterfactual indications via the name, 6 of 8 model–editor (ROME / fine-tune) configurations show no entity-specific SMILES transfer beyond leakage; only Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct + ROME achieves substantial transfer (+13.0 pts, 95% CI [9.2, 16.9]).
- **Binding Self-Distillation (Tables 8, 26–27)**: retrieved facts per entity rise from 0.05 → 0.87 for ChemDFM-v2.0-14B (vs. 1.01 with the name supplied — 86% gap recovery) and 0.01 → 0.85 for Qwen2-72B-Instruct over 435 molecules (63% recovery). On ChemDFM-v2.0-14B, SMILES-conditioned accuracy goes from 2.1% → 51.4% (indication), 0.0% → 27.6% (molecular target), 0.0% → 5.0% (mechanism of action), 3.3% → 11.4% (ATC class), while benzene verdicts are preserved for 94.1% of examples (92.9% for Qwen2-72B, whose benzene correctness even improves 83.9% → 86.9%).
- **Lookup routing baseline (Table 6)**: database name lookup closes 83.6–98.8% of the aggregate gap over 891 molecules / 2,955 molecule–relation pairs, but models can self-resolve SMILES→name for only 0.1–12.0% of molecules.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: high and specific. Prior work noted molecular-notation sensitivity for property prediction, but framing identifier choice as a *knowledge-access routing* problem — and cleanly separating recognition, knowledge possession, and identifier-conditioned access with causal (patching) evidence — is new, and the "binding failure" framing is well-argued. The negative editing result (fact edits don't repair the route) is a genuinely useful diagnostic insight.
- **Rigor**: strong for the behavioral core — every molecule evaluated under every identifier enables paired McNemar tests (p < 1e-7), editing transfer is leakage-subtracted with bootstrap CIs, patching uses different-molecule controls that all come out negative, and BSD results exclude the evaluation prompts from training stems. The double-dissociation claims are well supported.
- **Weak spots to note**: (1) BSD is **per-entity** — one LoRA adapter per molecule, parameters not shared across molecules, so it is a proof-of-repairability, not a deployable fix; generalization to unseen entities is explicitly left as future work. (2) BSD entity selection retains only molecules whose indication the model already retrieves via the name (e.g., only 47 molecules for ChemDFM-v1.5-8B), so gains are demonstrated on a favorable subset where the knowledge provably exists. (3) The authors themselves flag that some training stems "cue broad biomedical relation families," so the cross-relation gains are cross-prompt transfer rather than strictly relation-held-out generalization. (4) The protein replication (Appendix I) evaluates four of five models on only the first 1,000 entries by deterministic truncation, and the data-generation script preserves no UniProt release/timestamp — the authors admit both. (5) Absolute indication accuracy even via names is modest (23–44%), so the practical payoff is about relative access, not chemistry competence.
- **Reproducibility**: benchmark construction is fully specified (ChEMBL/PubChem/RDKit pipeline, cleaning rules, prompts, scoring in appendices); BSD hyperparameters are published in full (LoRA r=8, α=16, layers 20–24, 120 steps, 6 binding + 8 anchor contexts). The per-entity adapters and model-specific entity sets (389–435 molecules) require moderate but repeatable compute. No code link is visible in the main text.
- **Significance**: for anyone building chemistry LLM pipelines (agents over reaction datasets, drug QA), this is a practical silent-failure warning: a pipeline that feeds SMILES into an LLM may lose access to facts the model demonstrably knows, and conflicts between name and SMILES inputs can steer answers without detection. The identifier-binding lens likely applies beyond chemistry (the UniProt replication supports this).
- **Red flags**: none serious — the claims are carefully scoped, and the limitations above are mostly self-reported. The main overclaim risk is presenting BSD as a "mitigation" without emphasizing its per-entity, non-generalizing nature in headline text (though the conclusion does state it).

## Strengths

- Exceptionally clean experimental design: molecule and property held fixed, only the identifier varied, enabling molecule-by-molecule paired statistics rather than aggregate comparisons.
- A complete evidence ladder — behavioral (benchmark), interventional (lexical bridge, conflicts), representational (probing), and causal (activation patching with negative controls) — converging on one diagnosis.
- Honest negative results: standard knowledge editing (ROME/FT) fails to propagate across identifiers in 6/8 settings, and models self-resolve SMILES→name for at most 12% of molecules, motivating the proposed fix rather than assuming it.
- BSD is conceptually elegant: no factual labels, no external teacher, just distilling the model's own name-conditioned access behavior into the structural route; retention/locality controls (94.1% benzene verdict preservation) check for collateral damage.
- Generality probe beyond chemistry (UniProt accessions vs. gene symbols) shows the phenomenon is not an artifact of structural notation.

## Limitations

- Binding Self-Distillation trains and evaluates **one adapter per molecule** with no parameter sharing; it does not generalize to unseen entities, and scaling to a whole compound library would require training per compound (cost grows linearly with the number of molecules of interest).
- BSD evaluation sets are selected by name-route success, biasing toward molecules where the model provably holds the fact; performance on molecules whose facts are absent or only partially known is untested.
- Training stems partly overlap semantically with evaluation relations (acknowledged by the authors), so the cross-relation gains are not strict held-out-relation evidence.
- The protein replication uses a 1,000-entry deterministic truncation for four of five models and lacks a pinned UniProt release, weakening its statistical force relative to the chemistry results.
- Only three identifier types (trivial, IUPAC, SMILES) are covered; other common interfaces (InChI, SELFIES, molfiles, brand names) are untested, and indication scoring against normalized ChEMBL headings may undercount semantically correct paraphrases.
- Conflicting-identifier detection is tested only in explicit-comparison and two-choice formats; real pipelines rarely ask the model to verify identifier consistency, so undetected-conflict rates in the wild could differ.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"高度相关：它用 probing、表征几何（跨指称符检索）、激活修补等机制可解释性工具，因果性地定位了 LLM 中"实体识别—知识拥有—指称符绑定"三者的分离，是知识绑定与访问路由方向的一次干净利落的机制研究；同时对把 LLM 嵌入化学/生物流水线的智能体系统的"静默失败"（SMILES 输入悄悄丢失模型已知事实）也有直接警示价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can BSD be turned into a single shared adapter (or a small routing module) trained across many molecules, so binding generalizes to unseen entities rather than one adapter per molecule?
- Does cross-identifier binding failure also appear for other scientific identifier systems — InChI/SELFIES in chemistry, gene IDs vs. protein names, materials formulas (e.g., "LiCoO2" vs. crystallographic CIF snippets)?
- Where exactly is the binding "supposed" to be implemented in a transformer — is there a specific attention head or MLP sub-circuit whose ablation reproduces the name→SMILES access gap, and does chemistry fine-tuning overwrite or simply never form it?
- Would interleaved name–SMILES co-occurrence pretraining (curriculum mixing identifiers for the same entity) prevent the failure from arising, and does the IUPAC–SMILES alignment ChemDFM acquired show what such training can and cannot build?
- Can the conflicting-identifier undetected-inconsistency finding (only 17.5–43.1% rejection of similar wrong names) be exploited as a cheap self-consistency check in chemistry agent pipelines, e.g., dual-route querying with disagreement-triggered lookup?
- Does binding failure interact with scale and post-training (RLHF/DPO) — the 72B model shows the same ~1% SMILES indication accuracy as 7B models, so is more pretraining data on SMILES-caption pairs the binding-relevant variable?
