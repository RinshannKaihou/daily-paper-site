Why This Matters

Fixed, repeatedly-queried contexts — docs, patient records, repos — can be compacted 50×–100× offline while preserving attention-output fidelity, cutting serving memory without giving up correctness.

在推理侧极端内存优化下守住模型输出正确性 —— 相关但非核心的推理效率工作。

Critical Edge

Validated only on multiple-choice QA and exact line-match code completion — no free-form generation, streaming, or agent workloads — and the paper never states how many queries make the one-time pipeline cost pay off.

Methodology

Score the deletion you can still fix

Per-head offline compaction, calibrated on-policy by the model itself.

  • Tractable surrogate. The exact objective (match outputs O and attention mass) is intractable — softmax couples multipliers w and values ṽ. The change of variables U = diag(w)Ṽ turns output into a ratio of two linear terms → one ridge-regularized least squares, shared Gram inverse G = (PSᵀPS + λI)⁻¹.
  • Compensation-aware elimination. OBS-style saliency sj = ‖Bj,:‖²/Gjj scores each deletion after re-optimizing survivors; rank-1 / block-of-32 updates propagate B and G — no regression is ever re-solved.
  • Servable artifacts. Original keys + per-entry multipliers (as logit biases) + refit values; final box-constrained refit (wj ∈ [e⁻³, e³]).
  • Cost control. Leave-one-out pruning to a ⌈4t⌉ candidate pool; heads keeping >30% of their cache fall back to plain LOO eviction.
  • Frozen defaults. All hyperparameters tuned once on Qwen3-4B/QuALITY @ 50×, reused unchanged across 5 models, 3 benchmarks, 5×–100×.

  • Gains scale with aggression: +4.3 pts over SOTA at 100×, +3.0 at 50×; at 5×–10× every method clusters at 72.9–73.7% under the 74.0% ceiling, so selection barely matters.
  • Largest model (Qwen3-30B-A3B, QuALITY, 50×): 72.5% in 159 s vs an 80.7% full-context ceiling.
  • The gain is the compensation, not the surrogate: disabling only the compensating update drops 66.9% → 63.9%, leaving greedy elimination no better than plain LOO (64.7%).
  • Robust to cheap calibration: swapping self-study for a single repeat pass costs KV-Surgeon 1.2 pts but AM-HAK 4.9 pts.
  • Long Code Arena: matches AM-OMP within uncertainty at 9.7× (full) / 21.5× (fast) less compute; at 100× the budget — not selection — is the binding constraint.

Strengths

  • Attacks a structural blind spot — independent scoring of coupled deletions — instead of tuning yet another heuristic saliency.
  • Closed-form updates make joint select-and-compensate cheap: a 763 s → 54 s path to equal-or-better accuracy than AM-OMP.
  • Exceptional experimental hygiene: shared budgets / refit / queries across all methods, frozen hyperparameters, five seeds, mechanism-isolating ablations.
  • The fast variant is the practical headline — robust to cheap repeat-pass calibration (−1.2 pts vs −4.9 for AM-HAK), explained by a concrete loss-level mechanism.

Limitations

  • No open-ended generation quality, no streaming / agent workloads where the context is not fixed, no measured latency or memory savings beyond nominal ratios.
  • At 5×/10× the gains are marginal by the authors' own analysis; the useful regime is 20×–100×, where absolute accuracy stays well below ceiling.
  • The exact-loss-vs-surrogate comparison lacks statistical separation (65.8% vs 66.9%, within noise on 25 articles); the mild-compaction fallback is ablated only on the fast tier.
  • Self-study calibration costs hundreds to ~1000 s per context on larger models — the amortization break-even is never quantified.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does compensation-aware selection survive quantized or low-rank KV caches — e.g. keys and values stored at 4-bit precision?
  2. What is the amortization break-even: how many downstream queries before the 54–145 s pipeline (plus excluded prefill) beats re-reading the full cache?
  3. Does the idea transfer to serving-time eviction, where deletions must happen within a forward pass and only approximate rank-1 updates are affordable?
  4. How do retained supports behave under distribution shift between calibration queries and real — adversarial or out-of-domain — user queries?
