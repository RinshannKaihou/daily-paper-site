> **一句话总结：** 该文在九个循环 Transformer（Huginn-3.5B、Parcae 全系、Ouro-1.4B 及两个自建 SmolLM2 转换等）上，用单次反向传播精确测出"只回传最后 k 个循环"丢掉的权重梯度比例：对训练好的模型该曲线由输出端附近决定、几乎不随循环总数变化，可用 ρ^k 近似（ρ 为反向信号每循环衰减率）；Ouro 的反向信号反而增长（保留 4 个循环中的 2 个仍损失 81% 梯度），微调中损失会下降 0.07–0.22，但预注册实验未能证明该指标比"无需测量的规则"更能预测截断的训练代价。

> **TL;DR:** An independent researcher measures, exactly and for every k from one backward pass, how much weight gradient truncated backpropagation discards in nine looped language models (Huginn-3.5B, Parcae-1.3B/370m/140m, Ouro-1.4B, two SmolLM2 conversions, two ~304M "Part II" models). On trained models the lost gradient (e.g. 0.90/0.80/0.62 at k=1/2/4 on Huginn at 32 loops, ρ=0.88) is set near the output, barely moves with loop count (within ~0.03 from 32 to 64 loops on Parcae-140m/370m), and is approximated by ρ^k at moderate loop counts — but ρ drifts upward (0.81→0.91 on Parcae-370m from 16 to 64 loops) so ρ^k overstates the loss at long loop counts. Ouro's backward signal grows (ρ=1.88): keeping 2 of 4 loops still loses 81% of the final output's gradient. The lost gradient falls by 0.07–0.22 within 300 GSM8K fine-tuning steps on three of four models, yet a pre-registered test (S11) could not show it predicts truncation's training cost better than Parcae's own cost at the same k (average error 0.037 vs 0.036 on six settings) — an unusually honest, pre-registered, mostly negative result.

## 核心贡献

- **首次精确测量循环 Transformer 中截断反向传播的梯度损失**：对九个循环语言模型（七个已发表、两个自行转换），仅用每个留出序列的一次完整反向传播，就得到所有 k 值下的"损失梯度" |g−g_k|/|g|（按循环记录反向信号 λ_j，逐环做向量-雅可比积得到每环贡献 c_j，再从远端累加）；两种等价实现在 Parcae（fp32）上偏差 ≤0.001、Huginn（bf16）上 ≤0.005。
- **发现损失曲线"由输出端附近决定"，与循环总数几乎无关**：当 R 远大于 k 时，从 32 到 64 个循环损失梯度最多变化 0.013（Parcae-140m）/0.011（Parcae-370m），在 Parcae-140m 上可外推到 128 个循环；而拟合的每环衰减率 ρ 会随循环数上漂（Parcae-370m 从 16 环的 0.81 升到 64 环的 0.91），导致 ρ^k 在长循环下高估损失（64 环、k=4 时高估 1.34–1.46 倍）。
- **识别出反例 Ouro：反向信号增长而非衰减**（ρ=1.88），保留 4 个循环中的 1 或 2 个分别损失最终输出梯度的 92% 与 81%；按状态大小归一化后每环仍增长 1.66 倍，且其加权训练目标在 k=2 时损失 85%。
- **追踪了微调过程中损失梯度的下降**：300 步全量反向传播微调后，四个模型中三个下降 0.07–0.22（GSM8K 留出问题上为训练文本下降量的 90–102%）；仅训练循环外层（k=0、循环权重冻结）可复现其中约一半（Parcae 0.48、采样循环数模型 0.50），截断（k=2）微调也能使其下降（相当于全量下降的 61–98%）。
- **诚实的阴性结论（预注册 S11）**：在两个新模型上，基于 Parcae 拟合的损失梯度-代价曲线的预测误差（0.037）并不优于"Parcae 在相同 k 下的代价"这一免测量规则（0.036）——即冻结模型上测得的梯度损失不能被证明是截断训练代价的有效预测器。
- **方法学示范**：十六项研究（S1–S16）全部预注册并附冻结 commit，公开代码与预注册记录；主动报告失效研究（S6 跨模型迁移失败）、两个代码 bug（一个精度 bug 使早期研究作废）及多项协议敏感性分析。

## Key Contributions

- **First exact, per-k measurement of truncated-backprop gradient loss in looped transformers.** One full backward pass per held-out sequence (recording the adjoint signal λ_j at every loop, one vector-Jacobian product per loop for the contribution c_j, then summing from the far end) yields the lost gradient |g−g_k|/|g| for every k simultaneously. The two equivalent implementations agree within 0.001 on Parcae (fp32) and 0.005 on Huginn (bf16). Measured on nine looped LMs: Huginn-3.5B, Parcae-1.3B/370m/140m, Ouro-1.4B, two SmolLM2-135M conversions (following McLeish et al.), and two ~304M "Part II" models from Huang et al. (2026).
- **The lost-gradient curve is set near the output and carries over across loop counts.** Once R ≫ k it barely depends on R — at most 0.013 (Parcae-140m) and 0.011 (Parcae-370m) from 32 to 64 loops at k ≤ 8, holding to 128 loops on Parcae-140m — while the fitted shrink rate ρ drifts upward (0.77→0.87 on Parcae-140m from 8 to 64 loops; 0.81→0.91 on Parcae-370m), so the geometric rule ρ^k overstates the loss at long loop counts (1.34–1.46× the measured loss at 64 loops, k=4). A pre-registered two-mode model predicted ρ at 96/128 loops (0.891/0.900) against measured 0.898/0.908.
- **A "dial" for ρ and a surprising counterexample.** Damped blending of each loop's output with its input (phantom-gradient style, Geng et al. 2021) moves ρ on purpose at little cost to the trained model's loss (≤+0.02 on Parcae-140m validation text). Ouro-1.4B is the exception: its backward signal grows (ρ=1.88), keeping 1 or 2 of 4 loops loses 92%/81% of the final output's gradient (0.94/0.85 for its own weighted training objective), and the growth survives normalization by state size (1.66 per loop).
- **The lost gradient falls during fine-tuning.** After 300 of 600 full-backprop GSM8K fine-tuning steps it dropped by 0.18–0.22 on Parcae-140m, 0.07–0.16 on the Part II models, ≤0.03 on SmolLM2; on never-trained GSM8K test problems the fall was 90–102% as large. The fall lives in the loop's part of the gradient (loop-only fall 0.72–0.80× the all-parameter fall), about half of it reproduced by training only the layers outside the loop (0.48/0.50 of the loop-credited fall on two models, 0.14 on the third), and it also falls under truncated (k=2) training.
- **A pre-registered, honestly reported negative result on cost prediction.** With cost defined as the fraction of full backprop's fine-tuning benefit that truncation loses, the lost-gradient curve fitted on Parcae-140m gave average error 0.037 on six settings of the two Part II models — statistically a tie with "Parcae's own cost at the same k" (0.036), a rule that needs no measurement at all; the direction-after-Adam curve did worse (0.051) and SmolLM2's near-zero costs were far off (0.116).

## Methodology

The paper studies looped transformers that apply one shared block R times before a read-out, trained by backpropagating through only the last k of R loops (Huginn: last 8; Parcae: about the last half; converted models: at most 8). The central object is the **lost gradient** for one held-out sequence: |g − g_k| / |g|, where g is the full weight gradient of the shared block and g_k the truncated one. The exact recipe: (1) run one full backward pass on the frozen trained model, recording the backward signal (adjoint) λ_j at every loop j counted back from the output; (2) push each λ_j through that loop's weight Jacobian (one vector-Jacobian product per loop) to get its contribution c_j; (3) sum contributions from the far end to get D_k = g − g_k for every k at once. Equivalently, each loop can be given its own copy of the shared weights so autograd returns all c_j from one pass. The primary statistic is the per-sequence, all-parameter ratio, median over held-out sequences (16 GSM8K-derived validation sequences of 512 tokens, or web text for SmolLM2; the starred Huginn/Parcae-1.3B rows use a 32-text WikiText+GSM8K probe over part of the loop).

From the same pass, **ρ** is the backward signal's average shrink per loop (slope of a straight-line fit to log|λ_j| over all R loops); if contributions form the tail of a geometric series, lost gradient ≈ ρ^k (exact finite-loop forms in Appendix A). A **dial** — damped iteration x ← (1−a)x + aF(x), the mechanism behind phantom gradients — deliberately slows the fade without (for a settled loop) changing the settled state. Training-cost experiments fine-tune trained models on GSM8K QA text for 600 steps (batches of 8×512 tokens, AdamW, 5 seeds) in three arms — full backprop, last-k backprop, and a no-loop-gradient baseline — with cost = (loss_k − loss_all)/(loss_baseline − loss_all). The whole program is organized as sixteen pre-registered studies (S1–S16) with frozen commits, amendments, exclusion rules (unstable runs replaced by pre-listed backup seeds), and published verdicts, with code and freeze records at github.com/Aur-Shalev-Merin/lost-gradient-looped-transformers.

## Results & Highlights

- **Trained-model losses span 1.5%–90% and track ρ^k.** At each family's released loop count: Huginn-3.5B (R=32, ρ=0.88) loses 0.90/0.80/0.62 at k=1/2/4; Parcae-1.3B (ρ=0.84) loses 0.80/0.65/0.47; Parcae-140m (ρ=0.80) loses 0.81/0.66/0.42 (rule ρ^k: 0.80/0.64/0.41); the SmolLM2 conversions with fast-settling loops (ρ=0.35–0.38) lose only 0.30/0.11/0.015. Across the 23 unblended points at Table 1's loop counts, ρ^k lands within 18% below to 13% above the measured loss.
- **The curve is stable in R but ρ is not (pre-registered S10, bar 0.03).** From 32 to 64 loops the lost gradient at k ≤ 8 moved by ≤0.013 on Parcae-140m and ≤0.011 on Parcae-370m (Huginn's supportive check missed at 0.034), while the fitted ρ rose from 0.81 to 0.91 (Parcae-370m, 16→64 loops) and from 0.77 to 0.87 (Parcae-140m, 8→64); at 64 loops ρ^k overstates the k=4 loss by 1.34 [1.19,1.47] and 1.46 [1.14,1.65] on the two Parcae models (bootstrap 95% CIs). A pre-registered two-mode extrapolation predicted ρ = 0.891/0.900 at 96/128 loops vs 0.898/0.908 measured on Parcae-140m.
- **Ouro's backward signal grows.** Fitted ρ = 1.88 over its 4 loops (every sequence has ρ_i > 1, range 1.55–2.27): keeping 1 or 2 of 4 loops loses 92%/81% of the final output's gradient, and under Ouro's own weighted multi-exit objective without the entropy term k=1/2/3 loses 0.94/0.85/0.73. Normalizing each loop's signal by its state size still leaves growth of 1.66 per loop (1.45 on the pre-normalization state). The authors did not train Ouro with truncation — these are gradient statements only.
- **The lost gradient falls during fine-tuning, including on held-out text.** After 300 full-backprop GSM8K steps it fell by 0.18–0.22 on Parcae-140m and 0.07–0.16 on both Part II models (≤0.03 on SmolLM2); on never-trained GSM8K test problems the fall was 90–102% as large. A no-loop-credit fine-tune (loop weights fixed) reproduced 0.48/0.50 of the loop-only fall on two models but only 0.14 on the third; a last-2-loops fine-tune lowered it by 61–98% as much as full backprop.
- **Cost experiments: small in absolute terms, and the measurement adds no proven value.** On Parcae-140m the whole loop-training benefit is 0.18 nats; keeping 1 of 32 loops costs ~0.04 nats (blended settings 0.005–0.016 nats). Keeping one loop costs 22% of the loop's benefit on Parcae-140m vs 1% on SmolLM2(A). The pre-registered within-model win (S5): a power law cost = 0.339·(lost gradient)^3.38 predicted four new blended Parcae settings with average error 0.005 vs 0.037 for "k alone" — but the same curve failed to transfer to SmolLM2 (S6: predicted 5–8%, observed ≤~1%), and on the two Part II models (S11) it tied "k alone" (0.037 vs 0.036 average error over six settings; direction-after-Adam curve 0.051; SmolLM2 rule 0.116). At the same k the two Part II models' costs differed by 1.5–2.1× (0.29 vs 0.16 at k=1) — which no k-only rule can express, yet one model pair is weak evidence.
- **Protocol choices matter as much as the effects.** On Parcae-140m at 32 loops, k=1: per-sequence median 0.80 vs clipped 64-sequence average 0.97, and the unclipped expected-gradient statistic of Aicher et al. loses 100% at every k (a few huge-gradient sequences dominate); text choice moves the number by up to 0.12, and 16–32-sequence medians carry sampling error of 0.02–0.05. Measurement is cheap: for 32 Huginn-3.5B sequences on one T4, 52 minutes at 8 loops and 2.6 hours at 64.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Aur Shalev Merin, an independent researcher (no institutional affiliation; compute appears to be Kaggle T4 GPUs, per the freeze-timing notes). Credibility is unusually high for a solo preprint on three grounds: (1) sixteen pre-registered studies with frozen commits, published verdicts, amendments dated before data, and pre-listed backup seeds for exclusions; (2) the paper foregrounds its own negative results (S6, S11) and deviates from two pre-registered outcome mappings in the *conservative* direction, explicitly refusing to overclaim; (3) it self-reports two bugs — an earlier study invalidated by a precision bug that silently cut gradients to the looped weights (after which every run checks its truncated gradient in training precision) and a discarded diagnostic. Caveats on verifiability the author states plainly: only the freezes of S3, S8, and S9–S16 reached GitHub before their runs; for the rest, freeze-before-run ordering is self-reported. Code, pre-registrations, and freeze records are at github.com/Aur-Shalev-Merin/lost-gradient-looped-transformers.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine gap-filling. Prior work measured training cost vs k (Parcae App. I; Huang et al.; Xiong et al.), truncation at fixed compute (Schwethelm et al.), and gradient agreement at single settings (cosine 0.978 for the last 10 of 16 loops in Huang et al. App. C.8), but no one had measured the exact per-k lost gradient across looped LMs, nor tested whether it predicts cost. The Aicher et al. (2019) truncation-bias machinery is ported from time-recurrence to depth-recurrence with appropriate modifications (fitting every loop rather than only the farthest 10% of lags — a choice the paper shows matters).
- **Rigor:** High on measurement (exact identities, cross-checked implementations, bootstrap intervals, protocol-sensitivity analysis) and exemplary on process (pre-registration). The main analytic shortcuts are flagged honestly: the loop-only/all-parameter fall split is approximate (the two statistics relate by a product, not a sum), and the Appendix J account "re-describes the data with an exact identity" beyond its pre-registered parts.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong. Public code, pre-registrations, and freeze logs; small models (135M–3.5B) on consumer GPUs; 5 seeds per training cell with pre-listed backup seeds. The starred Huginn/Parcae-1.3B rows use a different probe than the rest of Table 1, which the paper itself concedes makes the pooled ρ^k range "not a like-for-like comparison between models."
- **Significance:** Practitioner-relevant (a 52-minute-per-32-sequences check for whether a loop's backward signal grows; a warning that Ouro-style loops without input re-injection can lose most of their gradient under truncation; evidence that frozen-model measurements overstate what truncation removes later in training). Scientifically, however, the headline predictive question is unresolved.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) The cost evidence is thin: all runs are 600-step fine-tunes at matched steps, not matched compute, with a single learning rate tuned for full backprop and never re-tuned per k — a choice the author admits "may favour truncation's apparent cost either way." (2) S11's "k alone" rule compares Parcae's cost at the same k of 32 loops against models run at 5 loops, rather than matching the *share* of loops kept — the author flags this. (3) The within-model "win" of S5 came against a rival that ignores the dial, and an after-the-fact measurement-free rule (a·k) nearly matched it (error 0.006–0.014 vs 0.005). (4) One of the two SmolLM2 conversions (B) gained nothing from looping over a never-looped control, so it is a questionable data point; SmolLM2's loops also settle in ~2 passes, an edge case. (5) The Part II models have one seed each, and only one model pair is ever tested cross-model. (6) Ouro is never actually trained with truncation, so the striking 81%-loss number is never connected to a training outcome.

## Strengths

- Exact, cheap, and general measurement recipe: every k from one backward pass, verified two ways to within 0.001–0.005.
- Covers the actual published ecosystem (Huginn, Parcae, Ouro, Part II models) plus controlled self-converted models, rather than a single toy architecture.
- Pre-registration discipline rare even at major labs: frozen predictions, published verdicts including failures, deviations from outcome mappings disclosed and in the conservative direction.
- Separates what it can claim (gradient-level facts, stability across loop counts, the Ouro anomaly, the fine-tuning fall) from what it cannot (cost prediction), and quantifies protocol sensitivity (text ±0.12; clipping makes Aicher-style expected-gradient loss read 100%).
- Actionable takeaways: measure the lost gradient directly and during training; ρ is a summary and growing-signal flag, not a shortcut; a truncated gradient that stays aligned after Adam's rescaling can cost almost nothing.

## Limitations

- Cost conclusions rest on 600-step fine-tunes of small models on GSM8K only — a narrow proxy for choosing k in pretraining, as the paper itself states.
- ρ needs an already-trained model, so it cannot guide the initial choice of k for pretraining unless tracked during training, which was not done.
- Cross-model prediction was tested on exactly one pair of ~304M models at a single loop count (5) with frozen token embeddings — six settings, underpowered by design (a win required halving "k alone"'s error; bootstrap difference −0.0015 to +0.0026).
- Held-out problems come from the same dataset (GSM8K) as fine-tuning text; no other text types tested.
- Ouro's striking gradient losses are never tied to a training cost; the cause of its growing backward signal (no input re-injection? per-loop weighted loss? state normalization?) is hypothesized but untested.
- Freeze-before-run ordering is externally timestamped for only a subset of studies (S3, S8, S9–S16); the rest are self-reported.

## Relevance

对"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"方向高度相关：本文本质上是一项训练期梯度诊断研究，给出了量化截断反向传播梯度损失的监控指标与廉价测量方法（一次反向传播、T4 上 1–3 小时），并揭示 Ouro 类循环模型中反向信号增长（潜在梯度病态）这一训练动态异常，可直接用于循环/深度递归模型训练运行的梯度健康检查。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does Ouro's growing backward signal (ρ=1.88) translate into a catastrophic training cost under truncation, and is its cause the missing input re-injection, the per-loop weighted loss, or inter-loop state normalization?
- Does tracking the lost gradient *during pretraining* (rather than on frozen checkpoints) recover predictive power for choosing k, given that it falls by 0.07–0.22 within 300 fine-tuning steps?
- Does the "direction after Adam's rescaling" matter more than gradient magnitude — i.e., can a truncated gradient that stays aligned under Adam's per-weight normalization be safe at far smaller k than magnitude-based rules suggest?
- Would the S11 cross-model test separate the lost-gradient curve from "k alone" with more models, matched loop-count shares, and per-k tuned learning rates?
- What makes conversion B's loop useless (no gain over a never-looped control) while conversion A settles in two passes, and can loop-conversion recipes (McLeish et al.) target a small ρ by construction?
- Do the rare, very large gradient spikes at large k reported by HRM-Text (Wang et al.) and Ouro's growing signal share a mechanism, and does the damped-iteration dial mitigate both?
