> **一句话总结：** HAWK 让 KV 缓存通过统一 RDMA 路径从 GPU（prefill）直接传输到 NPU（decode），用"面向张量布局的 RDMA 缓冲区分配 + 传输感知的张量变换"消除跨设备布局转换开销，使 GPU–NPU 异构系统在相同 SLO 下性能仅比纯 GPU 系统低至多 7.88%，同时取得最高 1.26× 功效与 2.03× 成本效率。

> **TL;DR:** The paper profiles why GPUs waste tensor cores on memory-bound decode (up to 92.92% tensor-core utilization in prefill vs only 35.90% in decode on A100) and builds a real prefill-on-GPU / decode-on-NPU disaggregated vLLM system (Llama 3.1 70B, 4×A100 prefill + 4×Tenstorrent p150a decode, 200 Gbps InfiniBand). HAWK's heterogeneous KV-cache transfer lands within 0.65%–1% of a GPU-to-GPU RDMA path (14.68% vs 14.77% of host-staged latency at 1280 MB) and up to 27.69× faster than naive host staging; on ShareGPT-chat and LongBench-summarization serving workloads the GPU-NPU system stays within 7.88% of the GG-Direct A100 baseline's output throughput at 90% SLO attainment while delivering 1.26× power efficiency and 2.03× cost efficiency (still 3.46× cost efficiency, but only 1.04× power, against an H100 decode baseline).

## 核心贡献

- **刻画了 GPU 与 NPU 在 LLM 推理两阶段上的互补性**：A100 在 prefill 上比 Tenstorrent p150a 快 2.52×（133.16 vs 52.74 input tokens/t），但 decode 差距缩小到 1.16×（15.27 vs 13.17 output tokens/t），而 NPU 在 decode 阶段的功效高 1.39×、成本效率高 4.31×——这是"GPU 做 prefill、NPU 做 decode"异构切分的实证依据。
- **指出并解决跨异构设备 KV 传输的两大障碍**：缺少统一的跨设备直传路径（NPU 依赖厂商私有通信栈）与张量内存布局不匹配（GPU 的行/列主序虚拟连续布局 vs NPU 分布在多条 DRAM 通道上的分块布局）。
- **HAWK 传输机制**：在 Linux RDMA 栈上为 NPU 驱动实现 peer-memory client 以打通 GPU↔NPU 设备内存直传；在 NPU 侧按目标张量布局"每 DRAM 通道一个连续 RDMA 缓冲区"分配，并把布局变换分解为"GPU 侧部分对齐 + 传输途中完成剩余对齐"，使数据到达即可被 decode 直接消费。
- **真实系统端到端验证**：在商用 A100 + Tenstorrent p150a + Mooncake Transfer Engine + vLLM 上实现并开源，证明异构系统在 SLO 约束下性能接近纯 GPU 系统且功效/成本效率显著更优。

## Key Contributions

- **Phase-dependent characterization of GPUs vs NPUs.** Profiling with Nsight Compute shows the A100 QKV projection kernel hits up to 92.92% tensor-core utilization (≤19.26% DRAM) during prefill but only up to 35.90% tensor-core utilization (up to 64.09% DRAM) during decode, because decode degenerates into GEMV with low arithmetic intensity. Tenstorrent's dataflow-style p150a narrows the decode gap to 1.16× while being far more power- and cost-efficient per token, making decode-on-NPU a rational design point.
- **Identification of the real bottleneck in GPU-NPU disaggregation: cross-device KV transfer.** Host-staged transfer already costs 1.83×–5.25× the latency of direct RDMA even GPU-to-GPU (10 MB → 1.2 GB); on top of that, NPUs use tiled, per-DRAM-channel tensor layouts that a conventional contiguous GPU tensor cannot match without post-transfer rearrangement.
- **HAWK transfer mechanism.** (1) A common RDMA path: the stock `nvidia-peermem` client on the GPU side plus a newly implemented NPU peer-memory client (npu_acquire / npu_get_pages / npu_dma_map …) in the Tenstorrent kernel driver, so the NIC can DMA both device memories. (2) Tensor-layout-aware RDMA buffers: one contiguous buffer per NPU DRAM channel instead of a single contiguous buffer — the naive single-buffer design actually OOMs on the p150a's eight channels (a Y MB buffer strands ~7Y MB). (3) Transfer-aware tensor transformation: the GPU-side step only partially aligns the tensor (reordering dimensions into large contiguous chunks per destination channel, overlapped with prefill), and the residual alignment is completed by pairing GPU-buffer regions with per-channel NPU buffers during transfer, keeping RDMA requests few and large rather than a few hundred bytes each.
- **First real-system demonstration of direct GPU-to-NPU KV cache transfer** in a vLLM (v0.10.0) + Mooncake Transfer Engine (v0.3.6) serving stack with Llama 3.1 70B at TP=4, including a discussion of adapting the mechanism to other NPU tiled layouts (TPU-like), reverse (NPU→GPU) transfer, and non-KV tensors such as activations for operator-level disaggregation.

## Methodology

The system runs one prefill instance (4× NVIDIA A100 PCIe 40 GB, NVLink Gen3/PCIe Gen4 intra-node) and one decode instance (4× Tenstorrent p150a NPUs, Ethernet-based links) on separate nodes connected by 200 Gbps HDR InfiniBand with ConnectX-6 NICs. The Tenstorrent LLM stack is integrated into vLLM v0.10.0 via a plugin, using its default mixed-precision configuration; output quality is verified to be nearly identical (ROUGE) to the GPU-only setup. HAWK's transfer engine builds on Mooncake v0.3.6, with the NPU-side RDMA support implemented in Tenstorrent user-mode driver v0.68.0 and kernel-mode driver v2.5.0. The model is Llama 3.1 70B (148 GB weights), tensor-parallel 4 per instance — the only model the current Tenstorrent stack couples to this device configuration.

Four configurations are compared: GG-Host (GPU-only, host-staged KV transfer), GN-Host (GPU prefill → NPU decode, host staging + NPU-side layout adjustment), GG-Direct (GPU-only, RDMA via Mooncake, verified comparable to NIXL), and GN-HAWK. A fifth "GN-Direct with a single contiguous NPU buffer" prototype is excluded because it fails with out-of-memory during serving due to cross-channel fragmentation. Workloads follow prior phase-disaggregated serving work: a Chatbot workload from ShareGPT and a Summarization workload from LongBench with Poisson arrivals, with TTFT/TPOT SLOs of 2000/200 ms and 15000/200 ms respectively (per MLPerf Inference). Transfer microbenchmarks sweep 10 MB–1280 MB KV sizes. Power efficiency divides output throughput by aggregate device ASIC power (GPU ASIC share estimated from DCGM board power + NVBit kernel traces fed to a GPU power simulator); cost efficiency uses $1,400 per p150a (vendor), $4,000/A100-40GB, $7,000/A100-80GB, $16,000/H100 (market listings) and is explicitly presented as a reference, not a definitive comparison.

## Results & Highlights

- **KV transfer microbenchmark (Fig. 9):** at 1280 MB, GN-HAWK completes the end-to-end KV transfer at 14.68% of GG-Host's latency while GG-Direct is at 14.77% — i.e., the heterogeneous HAWK path is within ~1% (0.65% per the abstract) of the GPU-to-GPU RDMA path, and up to 27.69× faster than the naive host-staged GN-Host transfer; host staging costs GG-Host 8.91×–3.79× more latency than GN-Host's baseline comparison across 10→1280 MB.
- **Online serving throughput at 90% SLO attainment (Fig. 13):** GN-HAWK achieves only 0.48% (Chatbot/ShareGPT) and 7.88% (Summarization/LongBench) lower output token throughput than the GG-Direct A100-only system; GN-Host fails to reach 90% SLO attainment even at the lowest request rate.
- **SLO attainment (Fig. 12):** for Chatbot, GG-Host drops below 90% attainment at 0.40 req/s while GG-Direct and GN-HAWK sustain it up to 0.65 req/s (within 2.90%); for Summarization, GN-HAWK reaches the 90% threshold at 0.60 req/s vs GG-Direct's 0.65 req/s — a 7.69% request-rate gap caused mainly by slightly higher P90 TPOT on the NPU.
- **Serving efficiency (Tables 8–9):** at 90% SLO attainment GN-HAWK delivers 84.48 vs 67.09 output tokens/s/kW on Chatbot (1.26× power efficiency) and 47.18 vs 41.01 on Summarization (1.15×); cost efficiency is 4.15 vs 2.05 tokens/s/$1,000 on Chatbot (2.03×) and 2.35 vs 1.25 on Summarization (1.88×). Against an H100-based decode baseline (GGH-Direct), GN-HAWK stays within 6.53% throughput while still achieving 1.04× power efficiency and 3.46× cost efficiency.
- **Motivating asymmetry (Table 1):** with 512-input/128-output requests at batch 32, A100 leads p150a 133.16 vs 52.74 input tokens/t in prefill (2.52×) but only 15.27 vs 13.17 output tokens/t in decode (1.16×), while p150a decode reaches 107.20 vs 77.11 tokens/t/kW (1.39×) and 2.35 vs 0.55 tokens/t/$1,000 (4.31×).

This is a systems-engineering group with direct access to and driver-level modifications of Tenstorrent hardware (user-mode v0.68.0 and kernel-mode v2.5.0 drivers), which is the kind of artifact only an industry-lab-backed team can produce; they state the implementation will be released as open source. The paper candidly discloses why Tenstorrent was chosen (the only NPU with an open-source stack permitting the required low-level changes), uses MLPerf-derived SLOs, and even acknowledges generative-AI assistance for text editing and part of the vLLM integration — above-average transparency for a systems paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** genuine and specific — direct GPU-to-NPU RDMA KV transfer with layout-aware buffer placement is, to my knowledge, the first real-system demonstration of this kind, and the fragmentation failure of the naive contiguous NPU buffer (a Y MB allocation stranding ~7Y MB across the p150a's other seven DRAM channels, forcing that baseline's exclusion) is a finding practitioners would not get from simulation. The ideas are engineering rather than conceptual: peer-memory clients, per-channel buffers, and two-step layout transformation are each individually known; the contribution is composing them and showing the composition survives contact with real hardware.
- **Rigor:** mostly solid (four baselines, SLO-gated throughput, transfer microbenchmarks, an H100 robustness check, ROUGE quality check), but two efficiency numbers deserve scrutiny. Power efficiency counts only device ASIC power — explicitly because Tenstorrent exposes only NPU core power — excluding hosts, NICs, and interconnect; GPU ASIC share is itself simulated (DCGM + NVBit traces + a power model), so the 1.26× figure rests on an estimation chain. Cost efficiency uses a $1,400 vendor price for the p150a against $7,000 market A100-80GB / $16,000 H100 list prices; the authors honestly label it "a reference rather than a definitive comparison," and the H100 comparison already shows the power advantage collapsing to 1.04× — with a Blackwell-era GPU or non-list pricing, the headline ratios could shrink further.
- **Reproducibility:** good if the promised open-source release lands (Tenstorrent's stack, Mooncake, and vLLM are public), but the exact hardware combo (A100 PCIe + p150a + ConnectX-6) is niche, and the NPU peer-memory driver work is the hard-to-replicate part.
- **Significance:** timely for the GPU-NPU heterogeneity wave; the transfer mechanism, not the benchmark numbers, is the durable contribution.
- **Red flags / missing ablations:** only one model (Llama 3.1 70B — forced by the Tenstorrent stack's model-device coupling) and one NPU platform; no ablation isolating the two-step transformation from the per-channel buffer design (the closest control OOMs and is dropped from quantitative comparison); no multi-instance/cluster-level scheduling or elastic prefill:decode ratio study; the TPOT gap on Summarization is attributed to "slightly lower NPU decode performance" without a deeper analysis; generality to TPU/Gaudi/Ascend layouts is argued only on paper.

## Strengths

- Attacks a real, previously unaddressed systems problem (heterogeneous KV transfer) on commodity hardware rather than in simulation, including kernel-driver-level engineering.
- The design cleanly separates concerns: RDMA path establishment (peer-memory clients), buffer placement (per-DRAM-channel RDMA buffers that double as fragmentation prevention), and data transformation (partial GPU-side alignment overlapped with prefill, residual alignment folded into the transfer).
- Honest, well-chosen baselines — including verifying Mooncake ≈ NIXL for the GPU-only direct path and reporting the H100 robustness check that tempers the efficiency claims.
- Quantitative framing under serving SLOs (90% attainment) rather than raw peak throughput, which is the metric operators actually provision against.

## Limitations

- Single model (Llama 3.1 70B), single NPU vendor (Tenstorrent p150a), and a fixed 4-device TP=4 configuration per instance; scaling behavior, multi-tenant clusters, and elastic prefill-decode ratios are untested.
- Efficiency accounting is device-ASIC-only; power figures rely on a simulated GPU ASIC breakdown, and cost ratios depend on volatile market prices ($1,400 p150a vs $7,000–$16,000 GPUs).
- The heterogeneous system still trails the GPU-only baseline by up to 7.88% throughput and 7.69% sustainable request rate on Summarization; the residual gap is device-level decode performance, not transfer overhead.
- The strongest efficiency comparisons use A100 (2020-era) decode GPUs; against H100s the power-efficiency edge nearly vanishes (1.04×), leaving cost as the main advantage.
- Mixed-precision NPU numerics are validated only via ROUGE on summarization — no broader output-quality equivalence study.

## Relevance

这篇论文属于"Compute Infrastructure for Large-Scale ML"方向：它直接处理 RDMA 互连、跨设备张量布局与 GPU/NPU 异构 serving 的通信瓶颈，与该方向关注的 collectives/interconnect、集群资源调度及异构加速器协调高度吻合；其驱动级 peer-memory 实现也为异构硬件上的推理系统故障与退化检测提供了基础设施视角。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of HAWK's layout-aware transfer benefit survives on NPUs with different tiling schemes (TPU v4-style, Gaudi, Ascend), where per-channel buffer counts and chunk granularities differ from the p150a's eight GDDR6 channels?
- Can the two-step transformation be eliminated entirely by having the GPU write the KV cache directly in the NPU's target layout during prefill attention (layout-aware PagedAttention kernels), and what prefill throughput would that cost?
- What happens at cluster scale — dynamic prefill:decode ratio adaptation, request migration when NPU decode queues back up, and failure/dispatch (掉卡) handling across two device pools?
- Does the ASIC-only power accounting change the story once host CPUs, NICs, and HBM-vs-GDDR6 memory power are included — especially given the paper's own estimate that GPU memory power is higher per the bandwidth analysis?
- Would a newer GPU generation (B200-class, whose decode GEMV efficiency and price/performance differ sharply from A100/H100) preserve the 3.46× cost-efficiency advantage, or does the case for NPU decode depend on GPU pricing staying inflated?
