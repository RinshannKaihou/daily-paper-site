Why this matters

A cheap gradient-health check for looped LLMs: one backward pass, 1–3 hours on a single T4, flags growing backward signals before they silently starve training.

— distilled from Relevance (训练期梯度诊断)

Critical edge · read it with this caveat

The headline predictive claim fails its own pre-registered test: measured gradient loss predicts truncation's training cost no better than a measurement-free rule — 0.037 vs 0.036 average error (study S11, six settings).

— from Critical Assessment · an unusually honest negative result

Methodology

Every k, exactly, from a single pass

  • Measured object — the lost gradient |g − g_k| / |g|: g is the full weight gradient of the shared looped block, g_k the version truncated to the last k loops. Median over 16 held-out 512-token sequences.
  • Recipe — one full backward pass records the backward signal λj at every loop; one vector-Jacobian product per loop yields its contribution cj; summing from the far end gives every k at once. Two implementations agree to 0.001 (fp32) / 0.005 (bf16).
  • ρ — per-loop shrink rate of the backward signal (fit to log|λj|); if contributions are geometric, lost gradient ≈ ρ^k. A "dial" (damped iteration x ← (1−a)x + aF(x)) moves ρ deliberately.
  • Cost protocol — 600-step GSM8K fine-tunes (8×512-token batches, AdamW, 5 seeds) in three arms; cost = share of full backprop's benefit that truncation loses.
  • Governance — sixteen pre-registered studies (S1–S16) with frozen commits, published verdicts including failures, public code.

  • Curve stable in R, ρ is not. 32→64 loops moves the loss by ≤ 0.013, yet ρ rises 0.81→0.91, so ρ^k overstates loss at long loop counts (1.34–1.46× at 64 loops, k=4).
  • Ouro counterexample. Backward signal grows per loop (ρ=1.88); keeping 2 of 4 loops still loses 81% — growth survives state-size normalization (1.66×).
  • Loss falls during fine-tuning. Down 0.07–0.22 within 300 GSM8K steps on three of four models; also falls under truncated (k=2) training.
  • Absolute costs are small. Parcae-140m's whole loop benefit is 0.18 nats; keeping 1 of 32 loops costs ≈ 0.04 nats.
  • Protocol choices matter. Per-sequence median 0.80 vs clipped average 0.97 (k=1); an Aicher-style expected-gradient statistic reads 100% loss at every k.

Strengths · what holds

  • Exact, cheap, general — every k from one backward pass, verified two ways to 0.001–0.005.
  • Real ecosystem — Huginn, Parcae, Ouro and Part II models plus controlled self-conversions, not one toy architecture.
  • Pre-registration discipline rare even at major labs: frozen predictions, published failures, deviations disclosed in the conservative direction.
  • Honest claim separation — gradient-level facts, stability, the Ouro anomaly and the fine-tuning fall are stated as provable; cost prediction is not.

Limitations · what to doubt

  • Thin cost evidence — 600-step fine-tunes, matched steps not compute, one learning rate never re-tuned per k; small models, GSM8K only.
  • ρ needs a trained model — cannot guide the initial choice of k for pretraining unless tracked during training, which was not done.
  • Underpowered transfer test — cross-model prediction tried on one ~304M pair, six settings; a win required halving the baseline's error.
  • Ouro never trained with truncation — the striking 81% number is a gradient statement only; the cause of its growing signal is hypothesized, untested.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does Ouro's growing signal (ρ=1.88) translate into catastrophic truncation cost — and is the cause missing input re-injection, the weighted loss, or state normalization?
  2. Does tracking the lost gradient during pretraining (not on frozen checkpoints) recover predictive power for choosing k?
  3. Does direction after Adam's rescaling matter more than magnitude — can a truncated-but-aligned gradient be safe at far smaller k?
  4. Would the S11 cross-model test separate the curve from "k alone" with more models, matched loop-count shares, and per-k tuned learning rates?
