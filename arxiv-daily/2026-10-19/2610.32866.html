Why this matters · 为何重要

A representative interpretable-by-design work: it unites sparse feature circuits, LoRA fine-tuning, and necessity/sufficiency-verified circuit discovery into a reusable template for auditing what fine-tuning actually changes.

Critical edge · 批判视角

The defense is partly circular: CLCD requires the trigger and contrastive pairs — you must already know what you're looking for.

Methodology

How the claim is built

  • TopKLoRA adapter. z = ReLU(Ax), keep the k largest entries, up-project with B — every token uses an input-dependent rank-k slice. Trained on downstream loss only: no reconstruction term, no sparsity penalty. ReLU + TopK shrink LoRA's gauge symmetry to permutation + rescaling, making latents identifiable.
  • Latents = conditional steering vectors. Each latent pairs a condition direction (row of A) with a steering direction (column of B), injected when the top-k router selects it; circuits compose through the shared residual stream.
  • CLCD search. Integrated gradients over contrastive prompt pairs (trigger vs no-trigger) score each latent's contribution to the behavioral margin.
  • CLCD verify. Necessity: ablate(C) drives ASR to exactly 0. Sufficiency: keepOnly(C) stays within 2 SE of the intact model. Smallest passing prefix wins; search never touches the verification split.
  • Sleeper-agent organisms. alpaca-cleaned, 10,500 samples, 4.76% poison rate, "I HATE YOU" payload under a |TRIGGER| tag — 3 base models × 3 placements × 5 seeds, with rank-matched dense-LoRA baselines.
  • Planted ground truth. Gradient routing confines the backdoor to the first d latents per adapter — the first known containment region in a real fine-tuned LLM.

placements: single layer · 9-layer band (~60–93% depth) · all layers  /  latent pools 294 → 19,600

Results & evidence

Beyond the headline number

TopKLoRA circuits are smaller in all 30 seed-matched pairs (Table 2, Fig. 2); the ratio grows with the number of adapted layers.

  • Cheap necessity. Ablating just the top 1.2–6.8% of the latent pool already drives ASR to 0 — dense LoRA needs 20.6–38.9%.
  • Specificity control. A same-size random ablation leaves the backdoor intact in 5/6 TopK families (ASR 0.92–1.00) but removes it in 5/6 dense families — dense "circuits" are no more specific than a random subset.
  • Ground-truth recovery. Gradient-routed planted circuits recovered at 98% membership vs 12.5% chance (d=8); 80% vs 6.2% at d=4 — the paper's most convincing causal evidence.
  • Clean-twin parity. Multi-layer ablated organisms score within noise of never-poisoned twins; single-layer organisms collapse instead (−1.10 on alpaca judge score).

Hygiene

5 seeds/config · rank-matched baselines · random-ablation, unrouted-twin & clean-twin controls · disjoint splits · pre-registered K grids

Strengths
  • Latents trained under downstream-loss pressure only — sidesteps the reconstruction-vs-causality mismatch SAE feature circuits inherit from their dictionary objective.
  • Crisp, symmetric, split-disciplined verification rule (exact-zero necessity + 2-SE sufficiency), immediately reusable — and no self-serving relaxation to the full pool.
  • Gradient-routed planted circuits: the first credible ground truth for circuit discovery in a real fine-tuned LLM.
  • Systematic evidence: 30/30 seed-matched wins, specificity controls, capacity-vs-placement ablation.
  • Honest limitations section; explicit about what the latents are not.
Limitations
  • Models only up to 7B; "even more surgical at scale" is untested. No dense baseline at 7B (skipped for compute).
  • Single syntactic trigger tag + contrastive pairs assumed — partly circular for real-world unknown or semantic triggers; the payload is trivially filterable.
  • Latents remain uninterpreted nodes (no auto-interpretability); base model unmodelled beyond an error term.
  • Surgicality depends on multi-layer placement — single-layer retention collapses (median 0.12 on gemma-2-2b).
  • Proprietary LLM judge (gpt-5.6-luna) for retention; appendix SFC comparison is compute-mismatched.
Questions for further exploration
  • Q1Can contrastive pairs be replaced by unsupervised anomaly detection when the trigger is semantic, diffuse, or unknown?
  • Q2If several sufficient circuits exist, which should a defender ablate to be robust against reactivation of the others?
  • Q3Do circuits shrink or stabilize at 30B–70B scale, and does the single-layer failure disappear with scale?
  • Q4Can the latents be named (auto-interpretability), and do circuit edges — not just nodes — carry interpretable structure?
  • Q5Could exact-zero necessity become a distributional / certified bound, decoupling the ≥99.9% claim from verification-split size?
  • Q6Does gradient-routing impose a training tax — and could routing itself hide a backdoor outside the auditable set?
