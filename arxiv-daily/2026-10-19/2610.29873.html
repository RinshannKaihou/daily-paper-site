Why this matters

If GPU prices stay inflated, NPU decode could halve serving cost — if KV caches can cross devices. HAWK shows how, on real hardware.

Distilled from Relevance
Critical edge

Against H100s the power‑efficiency edge collapses to 1.04× — the headline ratios rest on a simulated GPU‑ASIC power chain and a $1,400 vendor price, leaving cost as the main advantage.

Also: one model (Llama 3.1 70B), one NPU vendor; the two‑step layout transform is never ablated in isolation.
Methodology · distilled
Testbed

Llama 3.1 70B, TP=4 per instance, vLLM v0.10.0 + Mooncake v0.3.6; ROUGE‑verified output quality; NPU peer‑memory support written into Tenstorrent user/kernel‑mode drivers.

Compared configs & workloads

GG‑Host / GG‑Direct / GN‑Host / GN‑HAWK (a single‑contiguous‑NPU‑buffer variant OOMs and is excluded). ShareGPT Chatbot + LongBench Summarization, Poisson arrivals, MLPerf‑derived SLOs (TTFT/TPOT 2000/200 ms; 15000/200 ms), gated at 90% attainment.

HAWK mechanism

(1) NPU peer‑memory client so the NIC DMAs both device memories; (2) one contiguous RDMA buffer per DRAM channel — a single buffer strands ~7× its size across the p150a's eight channels; (3) two‑step transform: partial GPU‑side alignment overlapped with prefill, residual alignment folded into the transfer, keeping RDMA requests few and large.

Results · beyond the headline
  • Transfer microbench (1280 MB): GN‑HAWK completes at 14.68% of host‑staged latency vs GG‑Direct's 14.77% — within ~1% of the GPU↔GPU RDMA path, and up to 27.69× faster than naive host staging.
  • Throughput @ 90% SLO: within 0.48% (Chatbot) and 7.88% (Summarization) of the A100‑only system; GN‑Host never reaches 90% attainment at any request rate.
  • Sustainable rate: 0.65 req/s on Chatbot (within 2.90% of GG‑Direct); 0.60 vs 0.65 req/s on Summarization — a 7.69% gap traced to slightly higher P90 TPOT on the NPU.
  • Why the split works (Table 1): A100 leads prefill 2.52× (133.16 vs 52.74 in‑tok/t) but decode is nearly a tie (15.27 vs 13.17 out‑tok/t, 1.16×) while p150a decode is 1.39× more power‑ and 4.31× more cost‑efficient.

Strengths
  • Attacks a real, previously unaddressed problem — heterogeneous KV transfer — on commodity hardware down to the kernel‑driver level, not in simulation.
  • Clean separation of concerns: RDMA path (peer‑memory clients), buffer placement (per‑channel buffers that double as fragmentation prevention), transformation (two‑step, transfer‑overlapped).
  • Honest baselines — verifies Mooncake ≈ NIXL for the GPU‑only path and reports the H100 robustness check that tempers the efficiency claims.
  • SLO‑gated throughput (90% attainment) instead of raw peaks — the metric operators actually provision against.
Limitations
  • Single model (Llama 3.1 70B), single NPU vendor, fixed 4‑device TP=4 — cluster scale, multi‑tenancy, and elastic prefill:decode ratios untested.
  • Efficiency accounting is device‑ASIC‑only; GPU power share is simulated, and cost ratios ride volatile market prices ($1,400 p150a vs $7k–$16k GPUs).
  • Still trails the GPU‑only baseline by up to 7.88% throughput / 7.69% sustainable request rate — the residual gap is NPU decode speed, not transfer overhead.
  • Power edge nearly vanishes against H100s (1.04×); mixed‑precision numerics validated only via ROUGE on summarization.
Questions for further exploration
  • How much of the layout‑aware benefit survives other tiling schemes — TPU v4‑style, Gaudi, Ascend — where channel counts and chunk granularities differ?
  • Could the GPU write KV directly in the NPU's target layout during prefill attention (layout‑aware PagedAttention), and what prefill throughput would that cost?
  • What happens at cluster scale — dynamic prefill:decode ratios, request migration when NPU queues back up, failure handling across two device pools?
  • Does ASIC‑only power accounting change the story once host CPUs, NICs, and memory power are included?
  • Does the 3.46× cost edge survive B200‑class GPUs — or does the case for NPU decode depend on GPU prices staying inflated?
