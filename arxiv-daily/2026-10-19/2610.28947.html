Is 2:4 Sparsity Still Worth It? Semi-Structured Sparsity versus Quantization in LLM Inference

Key metric — the only regime where 2:4 pays
+27%

end-to-end prefill throughput for a retrained 2:4-FP8 model vs dense FP8 Llama-3.1-8B, served in vLLM on one H100 — 74.6k vs 58.9k tok/s, GSM8K on par (0.624 vs 0.596, n.s.), weights 6.1 vs 8.5 GiB.

Linear layers speed up 1.3–1.7× at the FP8 floor — but only after 13B-token-scale retraining.

Why this matters

vLLM and LLM Compressor dropped 2:4 support in 2026 for lack of users — this study explains why, and maps the one regime where sparsity still pays.

Context: the equal-bits accounting (2:4+INT8 ≈ INT5, not INT4) explains several prior papers' optimistic 2:4 results.

Critical edge

The +27% headline rides on Sparse-Llama — a third-party, 13B-token retrained checkpoint whose recipe and data are not the author's. Practitioners cannot reach that gain without an equivalent retraining budget.

Also weigh: GSM8K is evaluated on only 250 problems, so "accuracy on par" carries wide uncertainty; only 8B dense models are studied.

01

Methodology

A symmetric, metadata-honest shootout between "add 2:4" and "drop one precision step" — measured at operator level, then end-to-end.

  • Scope: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct & Qwen3-8B, every layer, all six GEMM operand families (W, X, Q/K/V, attention P), budgets 9 → 3.1 bits/element, tensor-core tiers 2× / 4× → ≈220,000 configs.
  • Accuracy: operator error vs FP32 on real calibration activations; layer-median ratios with paired-bootstrap CIs; model-level checks on WikiText-2 PPL, GSM8K, RULER-16K.
  • Equal-effort menus: 2:4 side — magnitude pruning / Wanda / SparseGPT + shift-variance correction; quant side — RTN (best grouping) / GPTQ / AWQ; same C4 calibration.
  • Honest clocks: CUPTI kernel time on three GPU generations; the 2:4 side pays for online pruning/compression of dynamic operands (fused Triton kernel, bit-identical to HieraSparse), the dense side for fused quantization.
  • Floor experiments: 2:4 stacked on FP8 / NVFP4 / INT4 weights; students retrained by distillation on 1B FineWeb-Edu tokens; served in vLLM on H100.
02

Results & Highlights

  • Equal tiers, accuracy: 2:4-FP16 shows 5–16× the operator error of dense FP8 on every operand family, both models. Across 108 compute cells, 2:4 wins 6 — all against weak rivals (MXFP4, row-wise INT8), none against FP8 or NVFP4.
  • The criterion: the parameter-free energy-loss prediction tracks measured gaps at rank correlation 0.93 / AUC 0.95, and correctly predicts Hadamard rotation hurts 2:4 at 87% of points.

03

Strengths

What holds up
  • Right accounting settles a practical question: charging 2:4 for index bits (2:4+INT8 vs INT5, not INT4) reframes "equal bits" and explains prior optimistic results.
  • Measures what practitioners get: online pruning costs, real cuSPARSELt timings across three GPU generations, end-to-end vLLM with a dense-kernel control isolating the sparse gain.
  • The energy-loss criterion is exact for basic methods, parameter-free, axis-aware — and predicts Hadamard rotation further hurts 2:4.
  • PPL-vs-GSM8K divergence during retraining is a genuinely useful methodological warning for judging sparse-model recovery.
  • Actionable rule: 2:4 only at the quantization floor, and only with a retraining budget; attention probabilities ruled out as a standalone sparse operand.
04

Limitations

What to weigh
  • Narrow scope: two 8B dense models — no MoE, no larger scale; scaling claims are extrapolations.
  • Noisy parity: GSM8K rests on 250 problems, so "accuracy on par" carries wide uncertainty.
  • Borrowed headline: the +27% reuses third-party Sparse-Llama rather than the author's own (unconverged) student.
  • Budget-limited Blackwell verdict: pairwise 4:8 "not usable" rests on 1B tokens vs 13B, one GPU, early cuSPARSELt — author concedes this.
  • Untimed paths: attention kept dense (BF16) in floor experiments; INT4 floor not timed end-to-end, leaving decode-side conclusions partially unsupported.
05

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the "perplexity recovers long before reasoning" gap persist at larger budgets and models, or is it an 8B-scale / 250-problem artifact?
  2. Can Blackwell's pairwise 4:8 become competitive with mask-aware retraining, or is its ~1.5× extra energy loss a hard floor?
  3. Could learned (non-magnitude) 2:4 masks that minimize energy loss close any gap above the floor?
  4. How do conclusions transfer to MoE models, where expert weights dominate FLOPs and may carry different intra-group energy structure?
  5. Is there a rotation scheme that preserves sparse-tensor-core value across more operands, given rotation hurts 2:4 for Q/K/X but helps values?
