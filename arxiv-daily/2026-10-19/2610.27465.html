Critical edge

The central quantitative claim — that weaker verifiers pick worse changes — is not confirmed: slope CI [−0.234, 0.120], p = 0.23, and no trend at all in the retail domain.

01 Methodology — distilled
Formal setup

An agent is A = (θ, p, T, K) — weights, system prompt, tool schemas, skills. A self-improvement step is a trace-derived change A→A′ admitted by a verifier with no human author; its effect is Δk, the change in solved-share over k repeated trials.

Two definitions (§2)
  • Verifier strength σ ∈ [−1,1]: MCC of a candidate verifier vs a trusted reference. A constant-output judge scores exactly 0 — which is why MCC, not accuracy.
  • Recoverability triple: g (does undo need a new gate approval?), restoration fidelity ρ, blast radius β — all measured against noise floors from a null change.
Evaluation (§5)
  • Production case study: the platform's own diversity gate — 172 settings × 10 runs (1,718 calls), 3 judge models, 4 prompt variants, breadths 3–12.
  • τ²-bench airline, 50 tasks (30 train / 20 test): experiment A varies σ by flipping reference verdicts with probability ε; experiment B fixes the verifier and compares stored-copy rollback (g=0) vs optimizer-regenerated undos (g=1).
Statistics

95% task-resampling bootstrap intervals, paired one-sided p-values, TOST equivalence, Holm correction across candidates.

02 The autonomy rule (§3–4)
deploy unattended  ⇔  g = 0 ∧ σmeasured ≥ σmin
ADMITno human
REJECTverifier says no
REFUSEprecondition unmeasured

Weak verifiers admit bad changes; irreversible changes make errors permanent — neither compensates for the other, so autonomy is capped by the weaker one. Refusal is a recorded, auditable outcome naming the missing precondition.

Beyond the headline

  • Noise floors first: every claim is measured against a null change (ρ₀ = 0.95, β₀ = 0.08), not against zero.
  • Judge errors cluster — all 4 false admits from one task, all 8 false rejects from two — so run-level stats would overstate confidence.
  • Power analysis is honest: confirming σ→regret needs ~220 held-out tasks, 11× the 20 available; the candidate pool is field-limited (best-vs-average gap 0.069 vs noise ±0.24).
  • Even a perfect verifier let the loop overstate its own gain by +0.20 (p = 0.08; does not replicate in retail, −0.02).
  • The collapse is not greedy decoding: 98 of 172 settings varied run-to-run; two cheap tests (delete-the-signal, check-the-guarantees) catch it in 40 calls each.
04 Strengths
  • +Turns a fuzzy governance question — "how much autonomy is safe?" — into two measurable quantities with a crisp, non-negotiable rule, enforced by a real platform (typed changes, declared σ, recorded refusals).
  • +An honest production negative result: a gate treated as working because it always returned a score was constant and meaningless — plus two cheap, reusable tests to catch it.
  • +Statistically self-aware: cluster-aware task-level resampling, TOST equivalence testing, Holm correction, a pre-registered field-limit criterion, and a power analysis that explains its own null result.
  • +The 17% blast radius formalizes a risk that pass-rate evaluation structurally cannot see — balanced flips hide behind a stable average.
05 Limitations
  • −The core σ→regret hypothesis is unconfirmed at achievable scale (p = 0.23; retail flat; both candidate pools field-limited).
  • −Only prompt edits tested end-to-end — the tool/skill/weight verdicts are definitional, not empirical; the rule "was applied after the fact" to one company's platform.
  • −σmin drifts ±0.15 with the gate's averaging depth — no universal threshold; every platform must recalibrate.
  • −The undo test measures counterfactual, gate-refused undos on training tasks, with three near-identical candidates — one undo strategy, not many.
  • −Single-company evidence, provider-dependent (judge models, gateway behavior on one day in Aug 2026); data available only on request.
06 Questions for further exploration
  • Would σ→regret reach significance at the ~220 held-out tasks the power analysis demands — and can a non-field-limited candidate pool be built from real production changes?
  • Can σmin be estimated online (multi-armed bandit over gate depth) instead of recalibrated per configuration?
  • Does gate collapse depend on judge size, prompt phrasing, or their interaction — do 2026 frontier judges still collapse at larger breadths?
  • How should β be measured at the tool boundary (refunds issued, emails sent) rather than as a probe-task lower bound — and can Sagas-style compensation shrink it?
  • Does the rule extend to multi-step improvement chains, where each admitted change shifts the distribution the next verifier is calibrated on?
  • Could regenerate-and-verify undos (g=1) be made safe by requiring the undo's own σ and a held-out equivalence check, rather than categorical refusal?
