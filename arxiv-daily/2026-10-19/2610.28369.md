> **一句话总结：** 该工作在"匹配实测专家计算量（K_eff）"的受控条件下，把 NAEE、DiEP、MoDES、Ada-K 四种动态专家跳过方法与最朴素的"静态降低 top-k"基线在 Qwen3-30B-A3B、Gemma4-26B-A4B 与 Qwen3-VL 上系统对比，发现动态方法几乎无法稳定超越静态基线（文本任务最大增益仅约 2.6 个百分点且出现在模型已损失 50–65% 能力的区间），并推荐免校准的按模态静态降 k 方案（k_vision=1–2、k_text=6）：在约 85% 更少激活专家下保留约 95% 质量，且校准 K_eff 与实测 K_eff 可偏离 6 倍以上。

> **TL;DR:** A rigorous negative-result benchmark: on Qwen3-30B-A3B and Gemma4-26B-A4B (text; ARC-C, HellaSwag, GSM8K, HumanEval) and Qwen3-VL (six VQA benchmarks), four dynamic MoE expert-skipping methods (NAEE, DiEP, MoDES, Ada-K) rarely beat the trivial static top-k baseline at matched measured K_eff — the best dynamic gain is at most ~2.6 pp and only where the model has already lost 50–65% of its capability. A calibration-free per-modality static k (k_vision=1, k_text=6) costs only ~4.1% average quality at ~85% fewer active experts and transfers to held-out MVBench (2.0% relative drop) and GLM-4.6V (4.2%); meanwhile MoDES's calibrated K_eff of 1.20 drifts to a measured 7.96 on text-only GSM8K, silently erasing its promised compute savings. Read it for the evaluation protocol, not for a new method.

## 核心贡献

- **统一的专家跳过方法分类学**：按各方法在标准 MoE 路由器之上引入的信号复杂度排序——路由分数（softmax top-k 基线、NAEE 的累计质量阈值）→ 专家间冗余（DiEP 的 CKA 相似度）→ 门控概率×层重要性（MoDES 的按模态阈值）→ 隐状态的强化学习分配器（Ada-K 的 Allocator）。
- **首个匹配实测计算的受控比较**：不是比单一工作点，而是扫描每种方法的可调参数（分位数/目标跳过率/λ），在两条完整"质量–计算前沿"上对比四种代表性方法与静态基线，且计算量用测试时实测的 K_eff（含 GSM8K/HumanEval 的自回归解码阶段）而非校准值。
- **免校准、按模态的静态 top-k 推荐配置**：k_vision=1–2、k_text=6，在视觉为主任务上以约 85% 更少的激活专家恢复约 95% 原始质量；并在完全 held-out 的视频基准 MVBench（相对下降 2.0%）与更大模型 GLM-4.6V（平均下降 4.2%）上验证了可迁移性。
- **核心负面结论**：尽管引入校准成本（MoDES 校准近 20 小时）与实现复杂度，当代动态跳过方法在匹配计算预算下不能系统性超越静态基线；且实测 K_eff 可偏离校准目标 6 倍以上，使推理时的计算节省不可预测。作者据此提出评估规范：任何动态跳过方法都必须报告"匹配实测 K_eff 且利用相同结构信号（如模态）"的静态控制基线。
- 承诺开源包括全部（重）实现在内的代码。

## Key Contributions

- **A unified taxonomy of expert-skipping strategies**, ordered by the signal they add over the standard router: gate weights (softmax top-k baseline; NAEE's cumulative-mass threshold), inter-expert redundancy via CKA (DiEP), gate probability × global layer importance with per-modality thresholds (MoDES), and a learned hidden-state allocator trained with PPO (Ada-K).
- **The first controlled comparison at matched measured expert compute**: instead of single operating points, the authors sweep each method's free parameter (β/γ quantiles for NAEE/DiEP, target skip ratio for MoDES, cost λ for Ada-K) to trace full quality–compute frontiers, with K_eff measured at test time (including autoregressive decoding for GSM8K/HumanEval), under both a realistic mixed calibration corpus (C4 en/zh + open-web-math + python-codes-25k) and best-case (benchmark train-set) calibration.
- **A zero-calibration, per-modality static top-k configuration** (k_vision=1–2, k_text=6) that recovers ≈95% of original quality at ≈85% fewer active experts on vision-heavy tasks, validated on held-out model and modality (MVBench video: 2.0% relative drop; GLM-4.6V: 4.2% average drop).
- **The central negative finding**: across models (Qwen3-30B-A3B, Gemma4-26B-A4B, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct), modalities, benchmarks, and calibration regimes, no dynamic skipping method systematically outperforms a static reduction of k at matched measured K_eff; realized K_eff can deviate from the calibrated target by ≥6× (≥50% even within the same task family), making inference-time savings unpredictable.
- **A methodological recommendation**: every future skipping method should be compared against a static control at matched measured K_eff that exploits the same structural signal (e.g., modality) the method uses; code including all (re)implementations will be released.

## Methodology

The paper studies post-hoc expert skipping in isolation from expert pruning and rerouting, so quality differences are attributable to the skip decision itself. **Baselines** are the model's native softmax top-k routing with a statically reduced k (identical across layers and tokens), plus a per-modality variant with separate k_text and k_vision for vision-language models, inspired by MoDES and Xia et al.; both are calibration-free. **Methods compared** (Table 1): NAEE (Lu et al., 2024) skips a tail expert when the cumulative routing-score ratio falls below a calibrated β-quantile threshold (~0.5 h calibration on one A100 80GB); DiEP (Bai et al., 2025b) scales this criterion with inter-expert CKA redundancy (γ1, γ2 quantiles, ~0.5 h); MoDES (Huang et al., 2026) uses per-modality thresholds on gate probability × layer importance α(l) calibrated to a target skip ratio via frontier search (~20 h); Ada-K (Yue et al., 2025) trains a linear Allocator with PPO on the token hidden state to predict the retained expert count, with cost parameter λ (~2.5 h training). Since releases are incomplete (Ada-K has none; NAEE/DiEP ship only Mixtral code for k=2), the authors reimplement on a best-effort basis and validate each against the original papers' reported operating points (Appendix A.7), e.g., NAEE on Mixtral GSM8K reproduces a 5.3% relative drop vs. 7.4% reported, and MoDES TextVQA scores land within 0.1–0.6 points. **Compute accounting** uses effective K (K_eff): the mean number of routed experts per token averaged over all tokens and layers, proportional to expert-matmul FLOPs and hardware-agnostic; crucially they distinguish calibrated K_eff (on calibration data, as prior work reports) from measured K_eff (counted during the actual evaluation), and compare at matched measured values. **Evaluation**: text-only on Qwen3-30B-A3B and Gemma4-26B-A4B with lm-eval (zero-shot ARC-C/HellaSwag acc_norm, 5-shot GSM8K strict-match, zero-shot HumanEval pass@1); multimodal on Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct with LMMS-Eval (ChartQA, TextVQA, MME, DocVQA, InfoVQA, OCRBench), calibrating MoDES/NAEE on GQA per the MoDES protocol; DiEP excluded for underperforming in both text regimes, the Allocator excluded because next-token-prediction loss gives no quality signal for vision tokens. **Runtime grounding**: vLLM + GuideLLM on a single A100 80GB at batch size 1 (bandwidth-bound) and 256 (compute-bound) maps K_eff to TTFT/ITL/throughput (Figure 1), showing a monotonic but sublinear relation.

## Results & Highlights

- **Text frontier, headline**: across ARC-C, HellaSwag, HumanEval, and GSM8K at matched K_eff, the best dynamic method (NAEE, occasionally the Allocator at K_eff≈1 on Qwen3) improves over the interpolated static softmax top-k baseline by **at most ~2.6 pp** — and this occurs in a regime where the model has already lost 50–65% of its original capability; MoDES and DiEP mostly perform *worse* than the baseline.
- **Dense-model context**: at K_eff ≤ 2.5 on Gemma4-26B-A4B, the best skipping strategy performs on average worse than dense Gemma4-E4B, a model with 85% fewer effective parameters (18.6%/10.9% reference drops marked in Figure 2) — i.e., below K_eff≈3 one should just use a smaller model.
- **Task-type asymmetry**: multiple-choice benchmarks (HellaSwag, ARC-C) degrade smoothly toward random-guess floors as K_eff shrinks, while generative GSM8K collapses sharply below K_eff≈2 on Qwen3 (HumanEval similar), so the safe operating point is dictated by the generative benchmark, not the average.
- **Multimodal per-modality static k**: with k_text=8, reducing k_vision to 2 costs <1.8% average drop across six VQA benchmarks, and k_vision=1 costs ~4.1% at ~85% fewer active experts (measured K_eff 1.08); even k_vision=0 (vision tokens skip experts entirely) retains >80% quality (19.1% drop), whereas the modality-unaware baseline at k=1 for both modalities loses >30%. MoDES beats NAEE and the symmetric baseline but fails to beat the per-modality static baseline except in the sub-1 K_eff regime (only consistent outlier: MME-Cognition).
- **Calibrated vs. measured K_eff divergence (Table 2)**: MoDES calibrated at K_eff 1.20 measures 0.58 on vision benchmarks but **7.96 on text-only GSM8K** — a ≥6× deviation where the calibrated skipping effectively deactivates and the promised savings vanish; even within short-answer VQA families the deviation exceeds 50%. By contrast the per-modality static baseline is bounded by construction at K_eff=6 on GSM8K and lands exactly there, trading 2.7 pp of strict-match accuracy (68.8% vs. 71.5%) for 25% fewer active experts than MoDES actually used.
- **Held-out transfer**: the recommended operating point (k_vision=1, k_text=6) applied unchanged to held-out video benchmark MVBench yields 69.95 vs. 71.40 accuracy for the full model (2.0% relative drop) at measured K_eff 1.19 (~85% expert reduction); on the larger GLM-4.6V (k=8 of 128, plus a shared expert) the same point incurs a 4.2% average drop over ChartQA/TextVQA/MME, dominated by MME-Cognition.
- **Runtime reality check (vLLM, A100 80GB)**: reducing K_eff from 8 to 1 lowers TTFT and ITL by 18% (bs=1) and TTFT ~30%/ITL ~25% (bs=256), with output throughput rising only from 111 to 135 tok/s (1.22×) and ~1.9k to ~2.65k tok/s (1.39×) — far below the 8× expert-compute reduction, because attention and KV-cache overheads dominate; this further compresses the practical value of marginal quality differences between strategies.
- **Calibration cost asymmetry**: static baselines are free; NAEE/DiEP ~0.5 h, Ada-K Allocator ~2.5 h, MoDES ~20 h per operating point on a single A100 80GB.

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans Technical University of Munich (Daniel Cremers' group — a well-established computer-vision/ml lab), ETH Zurich, Ulm University, and the startup Tensordyne (first author Steimle and correspondence are Tensordyne-affiliated, indicating a practical serving motivation). Credibility is high: every reimplemented method is validated against the original authors' reported numbers before benchmarking (Appendix A.7 — NAEE, DiEP, MoDES reproductions all land close; Ada-K could not be validated since no code or checkpoints exist); the evaluation uses standard harnesses (lm-eval, LMMS-Eval, vLLM/GuideLLM); both realistic and best-case calibration regimes are tested; conclusions are stress-tested on a held-out benchmark (MVBench), a held-out modality (video), and a held-out model (GLM-4.6V). An explicit AI-use statement discloses that generative AI was used for implementation assistance and editing but not for hypotheses or interpretation. Code, including all reimplementations, is promised for release.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: This is a benchmark/negative-result paper with no new method — deliberately so. Its value is the controlled protocol (matched *measured* K_eff, full frontier sweeps, modality-matched static control), and its finding effectively reduces MoDES's contribution to the sub-1 K_eff regime. Readers wanting new techniques will be disappointed; readers wanting trustworthy conclusions will not.
- **Rigor**: Above average for an efficiency paper. Standout choices: measuring K_eff during evaluation rather than trusting calibration, sweeping full frontiers instead of cherry-picked operating points, and validating reimplementations against published numbers (e.g., NAEE: 5.3% vs. 7.4% reported relative drop on Mixtral GSM8K; DiEP: 2.6% vs. 1.6%, shown to be within RTE's ±2.7 pp standard error). The K_eff-matched design also *excludes* routing-decision overhead and per-token raggedness, which the authors openly note favors the dynamic methods — making the negative result stronger, not weaker.
- **Legitimate weaknesses**: (a) Ada-K has no public release, so its Allocator is an unvalidated RL reproduction whose hyperparameter sensitivity the authors themselves flag — the strongest "dynamic methods don't help" evidence therefore rests on three calibrated methods plus one unverifiable reproduction; (b) the Allocator is excluded from all multimodal evaluations because its next-token-prediction loss provides no signal for vision tokens, so Ada-K is only ever tested in the text setting; (c) skipping is evaluated in isolation, whereas NAEE/DiEP were designed as add-ons to pruning — composition effects are explicitly out of scope, which could understate total-system benefits; (d) coverage is narrow relative to the generality of the claims: only k=8 mid-size MoE families (Qwen3-30B-A3B, Gemma4-26B-A4B, Qwen3-VL), 4 text benchmarks and 6 short-answer VQA benchmarks, no long-context, instruction-following, or agentic workloads where token heterogeneity might genuinely favor dynamic skipping; fine-grained MoEs (small k, hundreds of experts, DeepSeek-style) — arguably today's dominant architecture — are untested beyond GLM-4.6V; (e) MoDES's text-only calibration deviates from its published frontier-search protocol (a threshold sweep is used instead), a shortcut the authors justify via the validated routing function but which MoDES's authors might contest.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong on paper (standard harnesses, enumerated calibration sets with 1024 samples each, promised code release) but the code was not yet public at preprint time, and the headline recommendation (k_vision=1, k_text=6) is derived and validated on benchmarks from the same VQA family it was selected on, with only MVBench/GLM-4.6V as true held-out checks.
- **Significance**: High for practitioners (skip the complex skipping methods; reduce k_vision) and healthy as a field-level corrective in the same spirit as prior "beat the baseline first" studies. **Red flags**: none serious; the main risk is overgeneralization from k=8 30B-class models to the fine-grained MoE regime the paper never tests.

## Strengths

- Matched *measured* compute (K_eff) rather than calibrated or nominal budgets, with the calibrated-vs-measured divergence (≥6×) itself surfaced as a practically important, previously underreported failure mode of threshold-based methods.
- Full frontier sweeps of every method's tunable parameter instead of single cherry-picked operating points, on both realistic and best-case calibration data.
- Honest treatment of the one regime where a dynamic method genuinely wins: MoDES uniquely reaches K_eff < 1 (skipping the MoE block entirely for some tokens), and the MME-Cognition outlier is reported rather than buried.
- The modality decomposition analysis (Figure 6) mechanistically explains *why* MoDES matches the per-modality baseline: both concentrate skipping on vision tokens, while NAEE perversely allocates slightly *more* experts to vision than text.
- Transfer validation on a held-out benchmark, modality, and model family, plus real serving-stack measurements (vLLM/GuideLLM) grounding K_eff in end-to-end latency/throughput.

## Limitations

- Only coarse-grained k=8 MoEs around 26–30B are studied; conclusions may not transfer to fine-grained MoE architectures (e.g., 128+ experts with small k) or to 100B+ models.
- Ada-K is unvalidated (no original code/checkpoints) and untested multimodally due to its loss formulation; its RL-trained Allocator may underperform a better-tuned original.
- Skipping is studied in isolation from pruning and from expert rerouting/caching; combined systems (as NAEE/DiEP ship) and small-batch memory-bandwidth-bound regimes are not evaluated.
- Benchmark suite is narrow: no long-context, instruction-following, or agentic generation workloads; quality is measured via accuracy-style metrics only, without perplexity or latency-under-load quality degradation.
- Calibration data is small (1024 samples; ≤10k for the Allocator) and English/Chinese/code/math-centric; other distributions could shift the calibrated-vs-measured K_eff picture.

## Relevance

与用户关注的 MoE 路由行为与"极端性能优化下的可靠性"议题直接相关：论文揭示校准 K_eff 与实测 K_eff 可偏离 6 倍以上（MoDES 在 GSM8K 上从 1.20 漂移到 7.96，计算节省凭空消失），这正是推理优化带来的资源消耗不可预测问题；其"以匹配实测计算的静态控制为强制基线"的评估规范对任何 MoE 推理优化研究的实验设计都有方法论价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the static-k conclusion hold for fine-grained MoEs (e.g., k=4–8 of 128–512 experts, DeepSeek-style) and for 100B+ models where routing statistics may be better separated?
- Could a per-layer static k schedule (still calibration-light) capture most of MoDES's sub-1 K_eff advantage without threshold calibration?
- Is the calibrated-vs-measured K_eff drift a general pathology of threshold-based skipping, and could a closed-loop runtime controller (measure and adjust) bound realized compute without hurting quality?
- Do dynamic methods gain an edge on long-context or agentic workloads, where per-token expert requirements plausibly vary more than in short-answer VQA?
- Would training-time exposure to variable-k routing (rather than post-hoc skipping) shift the quality–compute frontier in dynamic methods' favor?
- How does the comparison change when skipping is composed with expert pruning or rerouting/caching, especially in small-batch memory-bandwidth-bound serving?
