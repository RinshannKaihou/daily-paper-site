Building a harness works far better than autonomously improving one.

Can LLMs Improve Their Own Harness? A Controlled Study on ARC-AGI-3
Jens Lehmann · TU Dresden / Amazon  —  Sahar Vahdati · Leibniz Universität Hannover / TIB
2026-10-19 · arXiv:2610.30499
self-improving-agents agent-harness ARC-AGI-3 LLM-evaluation autonomous-agents
原文一句话 · SOURCE ONE-LINER 在 ARC-AGI-3 上让 9 种前沿模型配置各自从零构建游戏求解 harness 并自主修订两次（27 条谱系、81 个冻结快照、2,025 次评测），发现模型能造出从 0 分到接近满分的多样 harness，但两次修订平均只带来 +2.80 RHAE 的留出集提升（95% 区间 [−1.44, 7.35]），开发集收益（+8.42）显著高估了泛化，且降成本常以牺牲性能为代价——自主“搭脚手架”与可靠“改脚手架”是两种可分离的能力。

Why This Matters

With a strict held-out protocol, it quantifies how unreliable LLM self-improvement of its own scaffolding is — the exact evidence long-horizon self-evolving systems need before trusting autonomy.

Dev-set progress (+8.42) systematically overstates transfer; “cheaper harness” often quietly means “worse harness.”

Critical Edge

The headline +2.80 gain is smaller than measured execution noise — one frozen harness re-run 10× spans SD 3.99, yet 26 of 27 lineages were evaluated only once.

from Critical Assessment & Limitations
01 · Methodology

Build, freeze, revise twice, judge blind

Nine model–effort profiles × 3 independent lineages: each model authors a complete ARC-AGI-3 solver harness from a sandboxed minimal repo — no architecture, game rules, or worked examples; no internet.

  • Profiles: GPT-6 Astra · GPT-5.6 Sol / Terra / Luna · Claude Fable 5, Opus 5, Sonnet 5 (high / xhigh variants) — same effort builds and plays.
  • Qualify: controller-run smoke test on 2 dev games (50 charged actions, up to 10 repair cycles) — valid interaction, not score, is the bar.
  • Loop: frozen H0 scored on 5 dev games → revision gets own source, traces, scores, dollar costs; may rewrite anything or keep the parent → repeat for H2.
  • Verdict: all 81 frozen snapshots scored on 20 strict held-out games that never feed back; every score replay-validated against the hosted scorecard.
  • Statistics: 100,000 whole-lineage bootstraps; RHAE within a 5× human action budget; frozen-price costs, program total ≥ $18,129.

Credibility — frozen prompts, splits & snapshots; κ/α-reported blind audit of all 54 revisions; Lehmann co-authored Tycho, the 100-RHAE human-steered reference system.

  • Dev gains overstate transfer: +8.42 on dev vs +2.80 held-out → adaptation gap G2 = 5.62 [1.78, 9.77]; dropping the single dev game r11l shrinks it to 3.02.
  • A coin flip per lineage: strict signs 15 gains vs 12 losses (p = .70).
  • Flat mean, wild churn: 13 held-out games improve, 7 decline — from +20.44 (lp85) to −15.03 (sb26).
  • Revisions are drastic yet unhelpful: 22/27 first revisions “reorient” the architecture; median codebase 2,161 → 2,912 lines; 0 of 27 preserve the parent — models never abstain.
03 · Strengths

Preregistration-grade honesty

  • Clean, generalizable question — can a model improve the software it acts through? — answered with fixed bootstrap, dev split chosen pre-campaign, all snapshots evaluated, outcomes seen only after adaptation.
  • Joint score–cost reporting exposes “cheaper often means worse” (9/27) — a failure mode most self-improvement papers hide.
  • A rare noise scale for ARC-AGI-3: SD 3.99 across 10 replays of one frozen harness.
  • Self-critical throughout: zero-spanning intervals labeled inconclusive; post-hoc rules flagged as post-hoc; the 0/27 abstention failure turned into a design lesson.
04 · Limitations

Read the numbers narrowly

  • Thin coverage: one benchmark, one 5-game dev split, only two revisions — long-horizon accumulation, the paper’s own “central challenge,” is untested.
  • Public games may have leaked into pretraining corpora for some models.
  • Three runs per condition — between-model numbers are descriptive anecdotes, not rankings.
  • Single evaluations: mean absolute difference between two evals is 4.73 points, so per-lineage gains partly reflect gameplay stochasticity.
  • LLM-only qualitative coding; four codebook dimensions fall below the 0.67 reliability threshold.
  • Dollar costs exclude infrastructure, human labor, latency, and energy.
05 · Open Questions

Where this leads next

  1. Would an explicit accept/rollback rule (reject revisions that regress on dev) close the gap between the observed +2.80 and the +4.53 post-hoc selection bound — prospectively?
  2. Does the adaptation gap shrink with more dev games, more revisions, or richer feedback bundles — and how does G_t scale with lineage length?
  3. Why does no model ever preserve its parent harness — a prompt-induced prior, weak-evidence blindness, or both?
  4. Do the same held-out regressions appear on uncontaminated, non-ARC interactive benchmarks — and how much of the 0–97.14 construction spread is sampling luck?
  5. Can G_t be predicted at revision time (diff breadth, repair cycles, dev-score delta) to cheaply gate autonomous updates in deployed systems?
