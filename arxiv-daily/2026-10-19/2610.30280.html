Why this matters

Turns Muon's speed into an observable quantity.

The iterate's singular spectrum — measurable per layer, per matrix — tells you when polar updates fly or stall, making the NS step count a tunable, monitorable dial for large pretraining runs.

Critical edge

The link is correlational: the paper shows the damping regime occurs in training — but never varies the NS step count to show that exploiting it improves loss.

And the quadratic lens omits momentum and gradient noise — the two ingredients Muon actually runs with.

Methodology — distilled

  • Muon stripped to its direction: PolarGD Xₖ₊₁ = Xₖ − γₖ·polar(∇f(Xₖ)) — no momentum, no noise; baselines GD (D = G) and SignGD (D = sign G), Adam's sign-like core.
  • Matrix quadratics f(X) = ½tr((X−X⋆)ᵀH(X−X⋆)) with exact line search — closed-form steps remove step-size tuning, so comparisons isolate direction quality.
  • 3×3 regime grid: Gaussian / orthogonal / vertex initializations × isotropic / dense Marchenko–Pastur (κ = 386) / matched-spectrum Hessians.
  • Rotation test: equivariance proof (Thm 2) plus a Zipf-spectrum quadratic (λₖ ∝ k⁻ζ) with diagonal vs randomly rotated H, 5 seeds.
  • Preconditioning lens: polar(G) = (GGᵀ)†/2G = A(X)X with κ(A(X)) = κ(X); rank(Xₖ) never increases under exact polar.
  • Real-training diagnostic: 90M transformer (8 layers, width 576), Nemotron-CC 1.8B tokens, 3433 steps; spectra of all 50 orthogonalized momentum matrices at 25 / 50 / 75 / 100% of training.

  • Orders-of-magnitude win — one provable loss. On the dense MP quadratic (κ(H) = 386) PolarGD beats GD for every initialization; but near rank deficiency (H = I, σmin = 10⁻⁴) GD is one-step exact while exact polar crawls at (1−2/d)² per step.
  • Damping is functional, not approximation error. Near-rank-deficient attention σ: exact polar amplifies 3.7×10⁶–1.8×10⁸; 2 / 3 / 5 NS steps multiply by bounded ×11.9 / ×40.9 / ×485. Fewer NS steps can converge faster than exact orthogonalization; attention needs q ≥ 9–18 steps to act fully polar (MLP: 4–5).
  • Update mass reallocated. Exact polar would place ~16% of the update's squared Frobenius mass on damping-range directions; 5 NS steps place ~5%.
  • Greedy line search can stall polar. Exact search drives the progress coefficient p(Xₖ) to 0.01 within 150 iterations; a tuned α ≈ 1.7 keeps p ≈ 0.6 — subtracting the spectral mean creates the tiny σ that stall it.
  • Momentum stabilizes the spectrum. Raw attention gradients show ~40% of σ in the GD-like range at 25–50% of training; the momentum spectra NS actually sees are near-stationary throughout.

Strengths

  • Fair by construction: exact line search with closed-form decreases compares directions, not step-size tuning budgets.
  • κ(X)-not-κ(H) is compact, memorable, and actionable — what matters is the conditioning of the weight matrix, not the curvature.
  • Deterministic mechanism (NS = damped polar) where prior theory could only see degraded approximation.
  • Concrete diagnostic: per-layer, per-matrix-type percentages; training implementation reproduces NS₅(M) to 10⁻⁴; raw-gradient vs momentum control included.
  • Honest scoping: no optimizer claimed to dominate; one-step superiority explicitly severed from trajectory superiority.

Limitations

  • Deterministic full-batch quadratics omit momentum and gradient noise — the quantitative rates should not be read as LLM-pretraining predictions.
  • Design advice untested end-to-end: no transformer run varies the NS step count q to measure downstream loss.
  • One scale, one dataset (89.8M params, 1.8B tokens); the 89% layer-0 concentration may not persist at 7B+ scale.
  • Cutoff-sensitive percentages: 5–28% for attention across cutoffs; the GD-like directions of M also misalign with the bottom singular directions of W (cosine ≈ random).

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does varying the NS step count q — or replacing it with explicitly damped polar at tuned ε — change final loss, and does the optimum q track the measured damping fraction?
  2. How do momentum spectra and the layer-wise damping fraction evolve at 1B+ scale, with GQA / MLA attention, or with MoE routing?
  3. Can κ(X) be connected to known heavy-tailed spectral evolution of weights to predict when during training polar updates stall?
  4. Does stochasticity rescue exact polar from the near-rank-deficient slow regime, and does momentum's spectral stabilization interact with the damping analysis?
  5. Since SignGD's advantage requires coordinate-aligned curvature and polar's does not, what would a polar/sign hybrid imply for Adam-vs-Muon behavior inside one architecture?
