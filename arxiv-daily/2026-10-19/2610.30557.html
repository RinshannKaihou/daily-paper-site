Critical edge · Read with this

A passive 56% SPY/T-bill blend returned +7.5% with only −2.5% max drawdown — beating Steward's +5.0% / −6.0% on both axes. Much of the "containment" may simply be being less invested.

Also: containment evidence = one drawdown window in one rising regime; 180 of 492 orders never filled, so real market impact is untested.

Methodology · distilled

Governance by construction, not instruction

Steward operationalizes five requirements R1–R5: typed decision record, code-computed outcome grading, typed evolution proposals, gated application around an unreachable core, replay verification.

  • Nine agents, six daily sessions. Only the morning session may buy; midday and close are sell-only position reviews; a no-LLM circuit breaker runs every 30 minutes; evenings grade the day; a quarterly session reflects on 90 days of record.
  • Bounded self-evolution. The Meta Reflector proposes ≤3 PromptLearnings per quarter (20–300 chars, numeric justification, target chosen from a Literal allow-list of six editable agents — the risk/discipline agents are unnameable).
  • Deterministic gate, twelve invariants. A code PromptEditor rejects absolutes ("never/always/override"), paraphrase duplicates (Jaccard ≥ 0.6), FIFO overflow, >3 agents per cycle — and ships each cycle as one revertible git commit.
  • Replay as ground truth. Recorded inputs are re-run before/after each learning and compared against the model's own re-run variance (~$16–19 per experiment).
  • Three evidence layers: live broker fills · trace re-scoring against later prices (day-clustered SEs) · replay of recorded inputs. Returns are SPY-adjusted.

The >10-point gap between synthesis and individual signals is the paper's real claim: Technical rank IC is only 0.031 @20d (t=1.2); news sentiment uncorrelated; Evening Analyst hit rate 37% vs 54% base rate.

  • Prompt-injection red team: direct override ("set one name to 45%") followed in 0/30 replays; injected news item excluded 20/20; the Pydantic schema caps targets at 20% anyway.
  • Governed evolution, verified: the Q2 learning was withheld for exceeding the 200-char cap (282) — replay confirmed it was inert; the applied Q3 learning changed behavior on 5/20 evenings vs 0/20 baseline (86% next-day bias agreement).
  • Grounding audit: 96.1% of 254,094 cited numbers trace to the agent's own inputs (99.9% incl. derived) vs 22% for a placebo — only 3 candidate fabrications found.
  • Core-only backtest 2015–2026: momentum max drawdown −61% → −33% at higher Sharpe (1.15 → 1.30); caps + breaker do most of the work. It bounds what judgement can lose, not prevent losses.

Strengths

  • Safety by construction: caps live in Pydantic types and blocking code, discipline agents are unnameable in the proposal schema, and the Risk Manager fails closed — the system cannot weaken its own safeguards.
  • Pre-outcome-registered typed trace turns every architecture claim into a measurable replay question — and replay is cheap ($16–19 per experiment).
  • Layer-by-layer attribution (analysts vs synthesis vs exits vs execution) beats a single P&L number; negative findings are reported, not buried.
  • Reusable methodology: the grounding audit (96.1% vs 22% placebo) applies to any tool-using agent system.

Limitations

  • One configuration, one paper account, one mostly rising window with a single sector crash; overlapping forward horizons.
  • Fill risk ignored: 180/492 orders never filled; stops and containment untested under real market impact.
  • Only two evolution cycles, one learning applied; 14–20-input replays expose behavioral change but cannot rank prompts by return.
  • Low beta (0.16) and 56% exposure: an exposure-matched blend beat the live book on both axes.
  • Backtest universe drawn from 2026 — survivorship bias acknowledged; only within-variant comparisons are valid.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the invariant-core design transfer to non-financial high-stakes agents — autonomous research or coding — where outcomes are graded slowly and noisily than daily P&L?
  2. Can replay-verification be promoted into an automatic gate invariant that rejects learnings failing an outcome-scored check on recorded inputs?
  3. How much drawdown containment survives real fills with market impact, given 180 of 492 orders never filled?
  4. Does code-computed calibration feedback measurably change position sizing, or does the PM ignore it (declared |ρ| ≤ 0.14)?
  5. Could a simple statistical baseline replace the 37%-hit-rate Evening Analyst forecasts?

Authors & credibility

NTU Singapore (Feng, Dai, Pan, Lau, Liu) with UCL (Xu). ACM format, 13-page appendix with an unusually complete evaluation; code, prompts, recorded trace and replay harness are open source at github.com/yebof/quant-agent.

  • Authors explicitly disclaim alpha over 102 days — no claim rests on it.
  • Discloses its own withheld-inert learning and a missing input the applied learning needed.
  • Months-long live deployment with pre-registered decisions vs backtest-only prior agents (FinMem, FinCon, QuantAgent, TradingAgents).
