> **一句话总结：** 本文在比"有界 α 阶矩"严格更弱的 weak-Lα（Marcinkiewicz）尾部条件下，为带仿射重尾噪声的光滑凸优化问题证明了 Clip-SGD 与加速版 Clip-SSTM 的高概率收敛保证：α<2 时完全保持 Sadiev et al. (2023) 的矩情形速率 K^{-(α-1)/α}（并配匹配下界），α=2（方差可能无穷）时证明额外的对数因子 √ln(eK) 是必要且可达的，并首次刻画了用户固定截断水平 λ 下不可避免的截断偏置邻域。

> **TL;DR:** This theory paper proves high-probability convergence rates for clipped SGD and accelerated Clip-SSTM on smooth convex problems when the gradient noise is heavy-tailed in the weak-Lα (Marcinkiewicz) sense — a strictly larger class than the bounded-α-moment regime of Sadiev et al. (2023), including Pareto/α-stable noise whose α-th moment (and at α=2, the variance) is infinite — and with a noise scale that grows affinely with the gradient norm. Versus the moment-based baseline, the rate is identical for α<2 (additive term σ0·R·K^{-(α-1)/α}, proven optimal by a matching lower bound), while at α=2 an extra √(ln(eK)) factor appears and is proven unavoidable. It also gives the first upper-plus-lower-bound characterization of the clipping-bias floor for user-prescribed constant levels λ: on quadratic and Diabetes ridge-regression problems with α=3/2 Pareto noise, fixed λ ∈ {0.1,…,4} plateaus while the horizon-tuned level (λ(10^2)=2.8 growing to λ(10^6)≈570) keeps the error decreasing.

## 核心贡献

- **弱尾条件下的截断引理（Lemma 4.1）**：在 weak-Lα 尾部控制 P{‖ζ‖>u} ≤ (σ0^α + σ1^α‖∇f(x)‖^α)/u^α 下，证明截断梯度偏置 ≤ b·A(x)/λ^{α-1}、二阶矩 ≤ v·A(x)/λ^{2-α}，幂次与矩情形完全一致；在 α=2 端点出现不可避免的 ln(1+Λ) 因子并被证明必要（Remark D.3）。
- **Clip-SGD 与 Clip-SSTM 的高概率速率（定理 5.1/5.2）**：噪声尺度随梯度范数仿射增长（σ0 加性 + σ1 乘性）时，两个方法均达到加性随机项 K^{-(α-1)/α}，乘性（梯度依赖）项以更快的 K^{-2(α-1)/α} 衰减；加性项在 K、σ0、R 三个参数上均最优（下界命题 H.2）。
- **α=2 端点的对数因子刻画**：在 weak-L2（方差可无穷）类上，固定置信度下任何算法都无法避免加性项中的 √ln(eK) 因子（命题 I.16），且该阶可被达到（推论 I.19）；更一般地在 Lorentz 尺度 L_{2,s} 上因子为 [ln(eK)]^{1/2-1/s}，上界与下界首次匹配。
- **用户固定截断水平的完整理论（定理 5.3/5.4/5.5）**：λ ≥ 2√2·LR 时平均迭代收敛到不随 K 消失的偏置邻域 Nλ ∝ b·R·σ0^α/λ^{α-1}；任意 λ>0 时给出最佳迭代保证；并构造一维反例证明该邻域对任何确定性步长都不可避免（下界 ελ = (48·4^α)^{-1}·min{R·b̄λ, LR²}），上下界相差仅 α 依赖常数。
- **保持仿射结构的关键证明技巧**：对 Clip-SSTM 通过函数间隙归纳得到加权梯度界 Σω_{t+1}^α‖∇f‖^α ≤ 7^αR^αT/a^{α/2}（远优于粗糙界 R^αK^{α+1}/a^α），使乘性项从 σ1LR²(H/K)^r 改进为 σ1²LR²(H/K)^{2r}。
- **数值实验验证**：α=3/2 Pareto 噪声（α 阶矩无穷）下，固定 λ 出现与理论一致的偏置平台、随视界增长的 λ(n) 持续下降，并在真实数据（Diabetes 岭回归）上复现。

## Key Contributions

- **Clipping lemma under a pure tail condition (Lemma 4.1):** Under P{‖ζ‖ > u} ≤ (σ0^α + σ1^α‖∇f(x)‖^α)/u^α (affine weak-Lα / Marcinkiewicz condition), the clipped-gradient bias is bounded by b·A(x)/λ^{α-1} and the second moment by v·A(x)/λ^{2-α} — the same powers of the clipping level λ as under the moment assumption; at α=2 a ln(1+Λ) factor appears and is proven unavoidable.
- **New high-probability rates for Clip-SGD and Clip-SSTM (Theorems 5.1–5.2):** With horizon-tuned clipping, the additive stochastic term is σ0·R·K^{-(α-1)/α} for both methods (deterministic terms K^{-1} and K^{-2} respectively), while the gradient-dependent (multiplicative) noise contributes a faster-decaying K^{-2(α-1)/α} term; this strictly generalizes Sadiev et al. (2023) and Gorbunov et al. (2020) from bounded α-moments to weak-Lα tails.
- **Matching lower bounds for the additive term (Prop. H.2, H.3):** A one-dimensional convex L-smooth instance shows error ≥ (1/4)·min{σ0·R·(K+1)^{-(α-1)/α}, 2LR²} with probability ≥ 1/4 for any algorithm using K+1 oracle calls — so the weaker tail assumption costs nothing in rate for α<2 (only constants), and the confidence dependence is also optimal.
- **Sharp characterization of the α=2 endpoint:** Under weak-L2 noise (possibly infinite variance), the additive term acquires a √(ln(eK)) factor that no algorithm can avoid at fixed confidence (Prop. I.16), attained by the moment-embedding route (Cor. I.19); on the Lorentz scale L_{2,s} the necessary factor is [ln(eK)]^{1/2-1/s}.
- **Prescribed clipping levels — first complete picture (Theorems 5.3–5.5):** For any user-fixed λ ≥ 2√2·LR, the averaged iterate converges to a K-independent bias neighborhood Nλ ∝ R·σ0^α/λ^{α-1}; for arbitrary λ > 0 a best-iterate guarantee holds (additive or small multiplicative noise); and an explicit Huber-function construction proves the neighborhood is unavoidable for any deterministic stepsizes — a fixed λ = λ0(K+1)^{1/α} recovers the K^{-(α-1)/α} rate up to logarithms.
- **Structure-preserving proof technique for acceleration:** A weighted induction bound on true gradients, Σ_{t<T} ω_{t+1}^α‖∇f(x^{t+1})‖^α ≤ 7^αR^αT/a^{α/2}, improves the multiplicative term of Clip-SSTM from σ1·LR²(H/K)^r (uniform-scale analysis) to σ1²·LR²(H/K)^{2r}.

## Methodology

The paper studies min f(x) over R^d where f is convex and L-smooth only on a ball Q = B_{2R}(x*) (or B_{3R}(x*) for Clip-SSTM), accessed through an unbiased stochastic oracle whose noise satisfies either an affine moment condition E‖g(x,ξ)−∇f(x)‖^α ≤ σ0^α + σ1^α‖∇f(x)‖^α (Assumption 3.4) or the strictly weaker affine weak-Lα tail bound (Assumption 3.5). The weak-Lα condition is equivalently a bound on the Marcinkiewicz-space (Lorentz L^{α,∞}) quasi-norm; by Markov's inequality the moment condition implies it, but Pareto and non-Gaussian α-stable noise satisfy it at the critical exponent while having an infinite α-th moment — exactly the noise models proposed for SGD in deep learning. The technical core has four pieces: (1) a clipping lemma bounding the bias (order A/λ^{α-1}) and second moment (order A/λ^{2-α}) of the clipped error, with explicit constants b_α, v_α including the unavoidable logarithm at α=2; (2) a restarted-martingale/freedman-style high-probability analysis of Clip-SGD and the accelerated Clip-SSTM with horizon-tuned stepsizes and clipping levels, where the affine (gradient-dependent) part of the noise is handled through the potential function of SSTM to preserve the scale structure; (3) prescribed-level analysis, where for large λ the averaged iterate converges to an explicit bias neighborhood and for arbitrary λ a new clipping lemma (Lemma G.2) shows the mean clipped field retains a descent component of size min{‖∇f‖,λ}/6; (4) lower bounds via two-point arguments (Bubeck et al. style) and a Huber-function construction whose slope is smaller than the clipping bias, so the mean clipped field pushes iterates away from x* uniformly. Numerically, the authors run Clip-SGD/Clip-SSTM with α=3/2 Pareto noise (P{P>t}=t^{-α}, EP^α=∞) on a 1-D quadratic, ridge regression on the Diabetes dataset (m=442, d=10), and a 16-dim vanishing-curvature chain objective, with 1024 independent runs per configuration and horizons up to 10^6 oracle calls.

## Results & Highlights

- **Clip-SGD rate (Theorem 5.1):** with probability ≥ 1−β, f(x̄K) − f* ≤ max{160√2·LR²ℓ²/K, c·σ0·R·ℓ^{(α-1)/2}/K^{(α-1)/α}, c·σ1²LR²·ℓ^{(α-1)}/K^{2(α-1)/α}} — the additive stochastic term σ0·R·K^{-(α-1)/α} matches the moment-based bound of Sadiev et al. (2023) while holding on the strictly larger weak-Lα class.
- **Optimality (Prop. H.2):** on a convex L-smooth 1-D instance with E|ζ|^α ≤ σ0^α, any algorithm with K+1 oracle calls suffers error ≥ (1/4)·min{σ0·R·(K+1)^{-(α-1)/α}, 2LR²} with probability ≥ 1/4 — the additive term is optimal in K, σ0, and R simultaneously, so switching from bounded moments to pure tails costs no rate for α<2.
- **α=2 endpoint:** under weak-L2 tails (variance possibly infinite) Clip-SGD retains the finite-variance rate max{LR²/K, σ0·R·√(ln(eK))/√K} up to constants, and Prop. I.16 proves no algorithm can avoid the √(ln(eK)) factor at fixed confidence — on the Lorentz scale L_{2,s} the factor is [ln(eK)]^{1/2-1/s}, with matching upper bound (Cor. I.19).
- **Fixed-level bias floor is real and unavoidable (Theorem 5.5):** for additive noise there is a 1-D instance where Clip-SGD with any constant λ and any deterministic stepsizes has E f(x̂) − f* ≥ ελ = (48·4^α)^{-1}·min{R·b̄λ, LR²} for every iterate and every deterministic average, and with stepsizes γk ≤ R/(16λ) the same bound (ελ/2) holds with probability ≥ 3/4 — matching the upper-bound neighborhoods of Theorems 5.3/5.4 up to α-dependent constants (factor ≈ 11520·4^α·b).
- **Experiments, clipping level sweep (Fig. 1, quadratic f(x)=x²/2, asymmetric Pareto noise, (σ0,σ1)=(0.05,0.001), γ=0.05, 1024 runs):** constant λ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 4} each produces a distinct error plateau (lower at larger λ, consistent with the predicted plateau reference f(xλ) ∝ λ^{-2(α-1)}), whereas the horizon-tuned level λ(n) = R/(40γ*ℓn) — growing as λ(10²)=2.8, λ(10⁴)=32, λ(10⁶)≈570 — keeps the mean error decreasing across 10²–10⁶ oracle calls, consistent with the n^{-2/3} bias²/variance balance for α=3/2.
- **Real-data confirmation (Fig. 3, ridge regression on the Diabetes dataset, L≈4.0, λmin≈0.019):** with the same Pareto noise injected along the smallest-Hessian-eigenvector direction, small fixed clipping levels make the error first decrease and then rise back toward a plateau — the flat curvature amplifies clipping bias as b²/(2λmin) — while λ(n) keeps decreasing and λ=4 plateaus lowest among fixed levels.
- **Failure mode for multiplicative noise (Prop. H.6):** for the multiplicative oracle of Faw et al. 2023 (σ0=0, σ1≤9) on f(x)=L/2·x², Clip-SGD with any fixed λ>0 and stepsizes ≤1/L started at x0=λ/L stays at distance ≥ λ/(2L) from x* forever with probability ≥ 2/3 — justifying the paper's restriction σ1 ≤ threshold in the arbitrary-λ guarantee.

## Authors & Credibility

(2023) that this paper strictly generalizes; several co-authors also appear on the ICML 2025 clipping-AdaGrad work. The paper is careful with prior-work attribution, including an unusually detailed treatment of the concurrent Lobanov et al. (2026) result and a precise complexity table. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** genuine and well-scoped — moving from bounded α-moments to weak-Lα/Marcinkiewicz tails is the "right" generalization because α-stable and Pareto noise (the very models motivating heavy-tail SGD theory) have infinite α-th moments and were excluded at the critical exponent before; the affine (gradient-dependent) noise scale and prescribed-level analysis with matching lower bounds are also new in combination. However, each ingredient exists separately in prior work (weak-Lp in El Bakkali et al. for zeroth-order, affine variance in classical SGD analyses, fixed-level bounds in Koloskova et al. and Khah et al.), so the contribution is a careful unification plus sharp endpoints rather than a brand-new technique.
- **Rigor:** high — explicit constants throughout, upper and lower bounds matched in the additive case up to α-dependent factors, and the necessity of the α=2 logarithm is proven, not conjectured. Remaining gaps are honestly flagged: optimality of the multiplicative (σ1) term is open in both this work and the concurrent Lobanov et al., and the joint (K, β) dependence at the endpoint is unresolved.
- **Reproducibility:** the theory is fully self-contained, but no code release is mentioned; the experimental protocol is described precisely enough (noise construction, 1024 runs, horizons, β=0.05) to re-implement, and all benchmarks are synthetic or tiny public datasets (Diabetes, 442×10).
- **A significant honesty gap between motivation and experiments:** the introduction invokes GPT-3/LLaMA training and heavy-tailed SG noise in deep learning, but every experiment is on 1-D quadratics, a 442-sample ridge regression, or a 16-dim synthetic chain — no neural-network training of any size. Worse, Figures 1–3 use the empirical stepsize γ=0.05 rather than the theorem's (much smaller) γ*(n); the authors state this plainly ("the comparisons isolate the clipping level and do not test the high-probability bounds themselves"), which is commendable, but it means the headline theorems are effectively untested empirically. All experiments use α=3/2, so the α=2 Lorentz endpoint — one of the paper's most distinctive contributions — and the comparison of its two theoretical tunings are never exercised.
- **Other red flags:** the constants are astronomically conservative (e.g., 1,058,400·LR²H²/K² in Theorem 5.2), typical of this literature but limiting practical uptake; the lower bounds for prescribed levels cover only the K-independent neighborhood (not the vanishing terms) and only additive noise; and Theorem 5.4's best-iterate guarantee cannot be certified from gradients alone (function values or a validation procedure are needed), which the authors acknowledge.

## Strengths

- Strictly weakens the noise assumption exactly where it matters: α-stable/Pareto noise at the critical exponent is now covered with no rate loss for α<2.
- Complete upper/lower-bound picture: rates, confidence dependence, the α=2 logarithm, and the fixed-λ bias floor are all matched up to constants.
- Handles the practically relevant combination — user-chosen constant clipping level plus noise scale growing with the gradient — that prior analyses avoided.
- The affine-structure-preserving weighted-gradient induction for Clip-SSTM is an elegant technical improvement (multiplicative term K^{-2(α-1)/α} vs K^{-(α-1)/α}).
- Transparent about what the experiments do and do not test, and about concurrent work.

## Limitations

- Purely convex, smooth, unconstrained, single-oracle-per-iteration setting; no non-convex, strongly convex-with-resets, (L0,L1)-smooth, or distributed variants (concurrent work covers some of these).
- Multiplicative-noise guarantees require σ1 below an absolute threshold for arbitrary λ, and Prop. H.6 shows some restriction is necessary — but the sharp threshold is unknown.
- Optimality of all σ1-dependent (multiplicative) terms remains open.
- Experiments are small-scale, synthetic-heavy, use a fixed α=3/2, and do not run the theorems' own stepsize/clipping prescriptions in the main figures (only Appendix J Figures 4–6 do, on one synthetic objective).
- Practical tuning insight is limited: the tuned level λ requires knowing R, L, σ0, α and the horizon K, which practitioners do not have.

## Relevance

本文为大规模训练中处理梯度异常/重尾噪声的标准工具——梯度裁剪——提供了严格的理论基础：它精确刻画了何时裁剪能稳定训练（弱尾噪声下高概率收敛）、固定裁剪阈值会带来怎样不可消除的偏置平台、以及何时裁剪会失效（乘性噪声）。这与"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"方向中梯度问题、训练不稳定监控的兴趣直接相关，属于该方向的理论支撑侧文献。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the weak-Lα analysis be extended to (L0, L1)-smooth or non-convex objectives, where the practical motivation (deep networks, transformers) actually lives, and does the α=2 logarithm persist there?
- Is the multiplicative (σ1) term σ1²LR²K^{-2(α-1)/α} optimal, or can a different analysis (or method) remove the gap left open here and in Lobanov et al. (2026)?
- Do real LLM pretraining runs exhibit weak-Lα gradient noise at the critical exponent (infinite α-moment) rather than bounded moments, as the α-stable motivation assumes — and can this be detected online from gradient-norm tails?
- Can the fixed-λ bias neighborhood Nλ be estimated from data so that a practitioner could set λ = λ0·(K+1)^{1/α} without knowing σ0, R, or α a priori?
- Does the necessity of the √(ln(eK)) factor at α=2 imply any observable penalty (slower tail convergence) in empirical high-confidence training diagnostics?
