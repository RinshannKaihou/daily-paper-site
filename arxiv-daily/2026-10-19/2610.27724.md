> **一句话总结：** 本文定义读出稀疏度 k*（恢复稠密线性探针 95% 超随机 AUROC 所需的最少特征数），在 5 家实验室的 19 个 LM、14 个任务上发现各基础模型给出几乎相同的任务特征数与排序（跨厂家中位 Spearman ρ=0.93），并证明指令微调选择性地压缩 k*、监督目标在受控实验中收敛到单特征读出，k* 还能预测下游适配的标注量（N* ∝ (k*)^0.65）与剪枝脆弱性。

> **TL;DR:** The paper measures *readout sparsity* k* — the smallest feature count recovering 95% of a dense linear probe's above-chance AUROC — across 19 LMs (Gemma/Llama/Mistral/OLMo/Qwen, 270M–27B) on 14 tasks. Ten base models agree strongly: language ID needs 1 residual feature everywhere, intent classification 4–5 (vs. harmfulness 153–391), with median cross-lab Spearman ρ = 0.93 on task rankings. Instruction tuning on Gemma-2-9B cuts harmfulness k* from 341 to 47 and fact checking from 253 to 40; in 20 controlled synthetic models, supervised targets get 1-feature readouts while matched controls need 29–60.5; reference-model k* predicts student label requirements as N* ∝ (k*)^0.65 (95% CI [0.44, 0.91]); after continual-learning chains, saved heads lose 0.55–0.59 AUROC while dense probes stay within 0.009 of baseline, and recovery needs 101–244 labels vs. 194–602 for initial learning. Honest negative results: k*-guided label allocation and parameter protection fail to beat baselines.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 k* 读出稀疏度度量**：k* 定义为固定搜索协议下、恢复稠密探针 95% 超随机 AUROC 性能的最少特征数，与稠密探针天花板 C（线性可读性）联合解读；协议包含 t-score 排序前缀 + L1 正则路径双搜索、五种子、邻层确认与多层中位数聚合，并给出可复现的算法化规范。
- **发现基础模型间共享的读出结构**：在 10 个基础模型、9 个可靠可读任务上，各任务的特征数跨模型高度一致（9 个任务中 8 个的最大/最小比 < 3.2），任务排序跨厂家中位 Spearman ρ=0.93，且对估计器变体（阈值 90%/97.5%、单搜索路径等）鲁棒（ρ=0.97–0.99）。
- **SAE 特征的跨模型响应对应性**：在去除类别均值后，被选中的 SAE 特征在模型对之间的一对一响应匹配超过"标签相关性匹配的零分布"99 分位数——意图分类 56.2%、语言识别 48.9%、工具选择 34.3% 的测试通过；消融 Gemma-2-9B-IT 的语言 SAE 特征将目标语言回复概率差从 0.88–0.99 压到 ≤0.015，证明行为相关性。
- **训练如何塑造读出稀疏性**：指令微调部署对任务有选择性压缩（Gemma-2-9B 有害性 341→47、事实核查 253→40），权重与输入格式各自贡献且任务相关的交互不同；在 20 个受控合成模型中，成功学习的监督目标全部获得单特征读出，而可读的对照聚合需要中位 29–60.5 个特征——监督特异性地浓缩了目标信息的访问通道。
- **k* 的预测与诊断价值（含诚实的负结果）**：参考模型 k* 与 8 个学生模型的标注需求呈 N* ∝ (k*)^0.65 幂律；持续学习后旧分类头崩溃（-0.55 AUROC）但线性可读性几乎无损（≤0.009），恢复所需标注少于初始学习；剪枝下宽读出任务更脆弱（≥60% 稀疏度时 32 个条件中 30 个相关 ≥0.89）；但按 k* 分配标注预算（中位 1.03× 均匀分配）与保护读出参数（劣于随机保护与普通 Wanda）均未超过基线。

## Key Contributions

- **A calibrated sparsity metric (k*).** Formalizes readout sparsity as the minimum feature count whose probe reaches 95% of the dense probe's above-chance AUROC under a fixed, fully specified search (ranked t-score prefixes + L1 path, 5 seeds, neighbor-layer confirmation, median aggregation). The paper explicitly separates *linear readability* (dense ceiling C) from *readout sparsity* (k*), and shows the two can dissociate.
- **Shared cross-model readout structure.** Across 10 base models from 5 labs and the 9 tasks with dense-probe ceilings > 0.90, feature counts cluster tightly per task (language ID = 1 feature in all 10 models; intent = 4–5; harmfulness = 153–391) and task rankings agree with median cross-lab Spearman ρ = 0.93 over 30 pairs (0.86–0.92 after controlling for bag-of-words readability of the raw text).
- **Correspondence and behavioral relevance of selected features.** One-to-one matching of class-mean-centered SAE feature responses exceeds a label-correlation-matched null's 99th percentile in 56.2% (intent, n=447), 48.9% (language ID, n=280), and 34.3% (tool selection, n=105) of tests across 13 cross-lab pairs; ablating 1–4 selected language features in Gemma-2-9B-IT collapses target-language reply adaptation (gap 0.88–0.99 → ≤0.015) while matched controls preserve it.
- **Training causally reshapes sparsity.** A base×IT weights × plain/chat format factorial on Gemma-2-9B shows harmfulness k* falling 341→135 (format only), 341→128 (weights only), 341→47 (both — approximately separable), while fact checking needs the combination (253→217 or 227 alone, →40 together). In 20 synthetic models (18 transformers + 2 GRUs) trained with target-vs-random supervision, every readable supervised target admits a 1-feature readout while readable controls need median 29–60.5 features — even a control sharing 4 of 8 input factors with a trained target.
- **Predictive/diagnostic applications with honest negatives.** Reference-model k* predicts student label budgets (N* ∝ (k*)^0.65, CI [0.44, 0.91]); dense probes detect retained readability after saved-head forgetting; wider readouts are more pruning-vulnerable. Yet k*-proportional budget allocation (median 1.03× uniform cost) and readout-guided parameter protection (worse than random protection and plain Wanda at matched sparsity) provide no consistent savings — the paper does not oversell its metric.

## Methodology

Tasks are cast as binary (one-vs-rest) classification problems over frozen LM representations; each residual-stream coordinate is a feature. For each class decision, layer, and seed, A(k) is the best test AUROC among candidate probes on k features, and normalized performance s(k) = (A(k) − 0.5)/(C − 0.5) with C the dense-probe reference; k* is the smallest k with s(k) ≥ 0.95 (∞ if none). Candidates come from two complementary searches: (1) ranking features by absolute two-sample t-score and fitting L2-regularized logistic probes on top-k prefixes, and (2) L1-regularized logistic fits along a regularization path whose nonzero coefficients define supports. A preliminary sweep picks a task-specific 4-layer window; measurements repeat over 5 seeds with 85% stratified training subsamples, require adjacent-layer (neighbor) confirmation, and aggregate by median over class decisions then seeds. Main counts use mean-pooled residual activations; the SAE correspondence analysis instead encodes last-position residuals with Gemma Scope / Gemma Scope 2 / Llama Scope (JumpReLU) and Qwen-Scope (TopK) dictionaries at 16k–65k features. The synthetic control world trains models on 32 binary-factor sequences where "target" queries receive correct answers and "control" queries receive input-independent random answers; probes read out the *true* aggregate for both at the query position. Application studies use LoRA + a fresh classification head for student adaptation and recovery, and magnitude/Wanda pruning with matched-actual-sparsity comparisons.

## Results & Highlights

- **Cross-model agreement on 10 base models / 9 readable tasks:** language identification needs k* = 1 in every model (spread 1.0×), intent classification 4–5 (1.25×), routing 1–2, jailbreak 8–16, banking intent 12–37, sentiment 72–227 (3.15×), harmfulness 153–391 (2.56×); 8 of 9 tasks spread < 3.2×, and cross-lab task-order Spearman ρ = 0.93 (median of 30 pairs), dropping only to 0.86–0.92 after the bag-of-words control.
- **SAE correspondence beyond class means:** class-mean-centered one-to-one feature matching beats the label-correlation-matched null's 99th percentile in 56.2% of intent tests (n=447), 48.9% of language-ID tests (n=280), and 34.3% of tool-selection tests (n=105) across 13 cross-lab model pairs — partial but real within-class response agreement that label correlations alone cannot explain.
- **Behavioral causality of selected features:** ablating 1–4 selected language SAE features in Gemma-2-9B-IT reduces the target-language reply-probability gap from 0.88–0.99 to at most 0.015, while activation-frequency-matched unselected features and equal-norm random-direction edits largely preserve the gap; conversely, pinning the 256 highest-ranked harmfulness coordinates to their training means (AUROC 0.963–0.982 alone) leaves a refitted dense probe within 0.0011 of baseline — readouts are sufficient but non-exclusive.
- **Instruction-tuning effects (Gemma-2-9B factorial):** harmfulness k* 341 (base/plain) → 47 (IT/chat); format alone 135, weights alone 128; fact checking 253 → 40 with either factor alone reaching only 217/227; sentiment nearly unchanged (72→64); harmfulness reductions across models range 1.1× (Gemma-3-270M) to 8.5× (Gemma-3-12B).
- **Supervision specificity (20 synthetic models):** all reliably readable supervised aggregates admit k* = 1, versus family-level medians of 29–60.5 features for readable controls — including a threshold control sharing 4 of 8 factors with a supervised target (median 60.5); 4- and 8-factor parity targets fail the readability criterion entirely.
- **Label-requirement scaling:** across 109 uncensored observations (8 students × 16 tasks), N* ∝ (k*)^0.65 with task-bootstrap 95% CI [0.44, 0.91] on the exponent and a leave-one-task-out 90th-percentile multiplicative prediction error of 5.3×; proportional allocation needs median 1.03× the uniform budget (no savings), though a 16-label-pilot rule reaches 0.66× under replay accounting.
- **Continual learning and pruning:** after harmfulness→sentiment→jailbreak chains on 4 models, saved harmfulness heads lose median 0.55–0.59 AUROC while dense-probe ceilings stay within 0.009 of pretrained baselines; recovery needs median 101–244 labels vs. 194–602 for initial learning (advantage nearly vanishing on Qwen3-8B under its own-largest-budget criterion). At ≥60% sparsity, pre-pruning k* correlates with ceiling loss at ≥0.89 in 30 of 32 model–method–sparsity conditions (permutation q < 0.05), yet sentiment loses more than harmfulness in all 4 models despite needing fewer features in 3.

## Authors & Credibility

Kaiyang Wan and Yuxia Wang are at INSAIT, Sofia University "St. Kliment Ohridski" (Bulgaria) — a group with a strong track record in LLM evaluation and interpretability. The paper reads as an ICLR-style submission (88 pages with appendices, "Preprint" formatting, anonymized code repository at anonymous.4open.science). Credibility is high: the measurement protocol is algorithmically specified (Algorithms 1–2 with a worked example recomputing one reported cell), uncertainty conventions are stated per statistical unit (seed ranges vs. ceiling standard errors vs. bootstrap intervals, never conflated), an AUROC precision analysis covers all 1025 class-decision ceilings (99.4% of readable decisions have 1.96·SE below the threshold gap), and the appendix includes a table of every design choice with its measured effect. The AI-use and ethics statements are unusually detailed (refusal-lowering interventions are disclosed with aggregate-only reporting). Compute footprint is small (single-GPU activation extraction, CPU probe fitting).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core idea — counting features needed to match a dense probe — extends Gurnee et al.'s sparse probing and Voita & Titov's MDL probing, but the joint k*/ceiling framing, the cross-model correspondence tests against a label-correlation-matched null, the weights×format factorial, and the target-vs-control synthetic design are genuinely new contributions rather than rebranding.
- **Rigor:** Strong. Pre-registered-style robustness tables (threshold 90%/97.5%, single-path search, MLP probes all keep task order at ρ ≥ 0.97), shuffle-label controls (best AUROC ≤ 0.577), and honest reporting of the two opposing test-set selection effects on the ceiling. However, the count *does* reuse the test set for support qualification, which the authors acknowledge but do not correct — k* values are descriptive of the protocol, not unbiased estimates.
- **Protocol dependence is the main interpretive caveat:** the authors' own controls show paraphrasing test inputs raises counts 1.5–10.8×, random rotations 1.6–2.0× (up to 20× in one cell), so absolute numbers like "harmfulness = 341 features" are basis-, pooling-, and wording-relative. The cross-model claims survive because all models share one protocol, but readers importing "task X needs N features" into other contexts will be misled.
- **Correspondence is partial, not universal:** only 3 of 9 tasks have sufficient SAE coverage, and even there just 34–56% of tests exceed the null 99th percentile — the honest framing is "partial within-class correspondence," weaker than the figure-1 gloss might suggest.
- **The headline scaling N* ∝ (k*)^0.65 has a wide CI ([0.44, 0.91]) and 5.3× leave-one-out prediction error**, and the paper itself shows the association does not translate into a working budget-allocation policy (1.03× uniform). Likewise, readout-guided parameter protection underperforms plain Wanda — the gap between "sufficient coordinates for readout" and "parameters whose preservation protects behavior" is demonstrated, not solved.
- **Missing pieces:** the pruning-vulnerability ordering fails among wide-readout tasks (sentiment vs. harmfulness inverted in all 4 models); parity targets with ≥4 factors never become readable, so the single-feature result is conditional on learnability; only Gemma-2-9B gets the full factorial and the extra 10 tasks run on one model, limiting generality of Sections 4–5 conclusions across scales.
- **Red flags:** none serious. No cherry-picked headline benchmarks; negative results are reported prominently. The anonymized repo and seeded pools support reproducibility.

## Strengths

- Separates two previously conflated notions — how *accurately* a representation supports a task (dense ceiling) vs. how *sparsely* (k*) — and demonstrates their dissociation (readable controls with tens of features; saved-head failure with intact readability).
- Breadth with rigor: 19 models, 5 labs, 14 core tasks, plus 20 controlled synthetic models, with a measurement protocol precise enough to be a reporting standard (10 concrete recommendations in Appendix A.1.16).
- Causal evidence at both ends: interventions show selected features matter for behavior (language reply adaptation), and supervised training demonstrably concentrates readout sparsity on targets.
- Unusual intellectual honesty — allocation and protection interventions that fail are reported with the same prominence as the successes, sharply scoping what k* can and cannot do.

## Limitations

- Absolute k* values are not comparable across bases, pooling positions, input formats, or wording; they are protocol-indexed measurements, not intrinsic task complexities (the paper says this explicitly, but it constrains reuse).
- SAE correspondence analysis covers only 3 tasks and 8 of 10 base models (Mistral-7B and OLMo-2-7B have no SAE suite); Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct borrows base-model SAEs, a mismatch that could depress its correspondence scores.
- The label-requirement law is coarse (5.3× held-out error) and was fit with student-specific intercepts, so a new student family may require refitting.
- Continual-learning chains cover a single 3-task sequence per model, and the recovery advantage nearly disappears on Qwen3-8B under one of the two comparison criteria.
- k* = ∞ conflates "search failed" with "no small support exists"; low-ceiling cells are excluded but borderline ceilings could still distort counts.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the cross-model agreement in k* survive *cross-lingual* or *adversarially filtered* task pools, where surface cues differ per model's pretraining mix?
- Can readout *directions* (not just counts) be matched across models, and would matching them enable activation-level model stitching or cross-model knowledge distillation?
- Why exactly do sparser readouts require fewer adaptation labels — is a small-support task easier for LoRA to route, or is k* a proxy for something causal like feature redundancy?
- Could the target-vs-random supervision gap (1 vs. ~60 features) explain why RLHF-style preference training produces diffuse, hard-to-erase behavior readouts?
- Would protecting the *computational upstream* of selected coordinates (attention heads writing to them) succeed where row/column weight protection failed?
- Can the "saved-head failure with retained linear readability" signature be turned into an online monitor that flags silently degraded safety heads in deployed models before refusals drift?
