> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一种面向 2nm GAAFET 脉动阵列的软硬件协同设计方法：利用"时序违例集中在累加器高位比特"这一物理观察，通过 MSB 掩蔽 + 细粒度时钟门控构建硬件"安全区"，再用混合精度量化 + 非结构化剪枝 + 累加感知训练（AAT）将权重的 ℓ1 范数约束在安全区内，并配合逐层动态调频（DFS），在 0.5V 近阈值电压下相对 0.7V 基线实现平均 39% 能耗降低与 23% EDP 改善，而精度损失控制在 1% 以内。

> **TL;DR:** A hardware-software co-design (ICCAD '26, NTU Athens) that makes a post-layout 32×32 systolic array at the 2nm IMEC GAAFET node run reliably at 0.5V near-threshold voltage. Gate-level timing analysis shows Vth-variation-induced errors localize in the high-order bits of the 24-bit accumulator (bit 12+ under +3σ variation), collapsing unmitigated ResNet-50/ImageNet Top-1 to 0%; the fix — accumulator MSB-masking + clock-gating (1.7% area), mixed-precision quantization + unstructured pruning + Accumulation-Aware Training with an ℓ1-norm constraint, and layer-wise dynamic frequency scaling — delivers on average 39% energy savings and 23% EDP reduction vs the 0.7V/2GHz INT8 baseline (ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 +41%, ResNet-50/ImageNet +43%, ViT-B/32/ImageNet +38%, OPT-125M/WikiText-2 +37% energy), beating published voltage-scaling accelerators (X-TPU 35%, X-NVDLA 41.5%, GAVINA 20%) on most models, while keeping accuracy within the 1% degradation budget. The catch: transformer models get much weaker EDP gains (0.80–0.83 normalized), and all results are simulation on a pathfinding PDK, not silicon.

## 核心贡献

- **2nm 节点的系统性低压表征**：首次在 IMEC N2 GAAFET 工艺上对 32×32 输出固定（output-stationary）脉动阵列（INT8 权重/激活、24-bit 累加器、2GHz 基线）进行含工艺涨落（+3σ Vth 变化）的后布局（post-PnR）时序/功耗/EDP 表征，覆盖 0.9V/0.7V/0.5V/0.4V 四个电压域，并定位了低压下的关键瓶颈：MAC 累加器的高位进位链，而非 SRAM 或互连。
- **比特级错误分布的关键物理发现**：时序违例并非随机噪声，而是结构性集中在累加器高位（无涨落时仅最高 7 位出错；+3σ Vth 涨落下错误从第 12 位即开始）；且 INT4 并不会比 INT8 降低出错概率——位宽与物理时序解耦，只能作为"数值调控"手段而非"时序容错"手段。
- **硬件"安全区"架构**：在 PE 级引入可编程 MSB 掩蔽逻辑（截断被敏化的高位）+ 对失效位路径的细粒度时钟门控（时钟树与同步逻辑占阵列功耗约 35%），仅付出 1.7% 面积开销。
- **累加感知量化（AAT）软件方法**：将硬件时序裕量作为一阶训练约束——用 ℓ1 范数上界（‖w‖₁ ≤ (2^P−2)/(2^N−1)，P 为累加器安全位宽）作为可微分正则项，结合混合精度量化（首末层 8-bit、其余从 4-bit 起搜）与非结构化剪枝，把部分积的累加幅值确定性地压入安全区。
- **逐层动态频率调制（DFS）探索闭环**：以 EDP 为目标，用 ZigZag 做逐层延迟剖析，迭代地为每层选择其权重分布能容忍的最高频率/最窄累加位宽（1GHz→24 安全位 到 2GHz→11 安全位的操作点表），对数值敏感层用降频扩展安全区而非全局降频。

## Key Contributions

- A comprehensive post-layout characterization of a 32×32 output-stationary systolic array at the 2nm IMEC GAAFET node (INT8 W/A, 24-bit accumulators, 2GHz baseline) across 0.9/0.7/0.5/0.4V under ±3σ threshold-voltage variation, showing that under voltage scaling the critical path migrates from SRAM to the MAC accumulator's high-order carry chains.
- A bit-level error-propagation analysis via variation-aware library characterization (Cadence Liberate) and gate-level simulation (QuestaSim) on back-annotated netlists, revealing that timing errors are structurally localized: without variation they hit only the top 7 MSBs of the 24-bit accumulator; with +3σ Vth variation they start as early as bit 12. A counterintuitive finding: INT4 operands have nearly the same corruption probability as INT8 because glitches arise from total switching activity in the accumulator, not operand width.
- A "Safe-Mode" PE architecture with programmable MSB-masking plus fine-grained clock-gating of sensitized flip-flops (only 1.7% cell-area overhead), which deterministically truncates the unreliable bit range and eliminates its dynamic power (clock/sync is ~35% of array power).
- An Accumulation-Aware Training (AAT) methodology (building on A2Q+) that enforces a hardware-derived ℓ1-norm bound (‖w‖₁ ≤ (2^P−2)/(2^N−1)) as a differentiable regularizer (λ=10⁻³), combined with mixed-precision quantization and unstructured pruning, to mathematically guarantee accumulation stays within the safe bit-width.
- A layer-wise Dynamic Frequency Scaling exploration loop (latency profiled with ZigZag, 200 refinement iterations) that selects per-layer frequency/accumulator-width operating points from a pre-characterized table (1.0GHz→24 safe bits, 1.4GHz→19, 1.8GHz→14, 2.0GHz→11), treating sensitive layers with frequency relief instead of a global clock reduction.

## Methodology

The work follows a cross-layer, variation-aware flow. (1) Hardware characterization: the systolic array is synthesized with Synopsys DesignWare arithmetic, placed-and-routed with Cadence Innovus, and timing-signed with Tempus at the Slow-Slow corner; process variation is injected statistically (gate length, dielectric thickness, fin height, work function), with Vth fluctuation dominating at 2nm. (2) Fault modeling: VDD and Vth-variation inputs drive Liberate library characterization; gate-level simulations on back-annotated netlists produce bit-specific error probabilities in the accumulator, which are then injected into intermediate feature maps in PyTorch to measure end-to-end accuracy impact. (3) Architecture: MSB-masking truncates the sensitized high-order bits (numerically redundant once the dataflow is trained to fit), and clock-gating freezes the corresponding flip-flops, cutting dynamic power. (4) Software: AAT adds a hinge-type penalty R = max(‖w‖₁ − Φ, 0) per output channel to the task loss, where Φ is the theoretical overflow threshold for the target accumulator width; mixed-precision quantization and unstructured pruning lower accumulation magnitude so constraints are satisfiable, and layers already in compliance are frozen while only "at-risk" layers are fine-tuned. (5) Exploration loop: starting from W4A4 (8-bit at network boundaries), the framework iteratively restores precision to low-latency layers when the accuracy budget (<1% Top-1 / <1 point perplexity vs the INT8 baseline) is violated, prunes, checks ℓ1 compliance, applies AAT where needed, uses per-layer DFS as a final "relief valve," and pushes the highest-EDP layers toward narrower accumulators. Benchmarks: ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, ResNet-50/ImageNet, ViT-B/32/ImageNet, OPT-125M/WikiText-2.

## Results & Highlights

- Headline: at the 0.5V low-voltage frontier, the co-optimized post-layout design delivers on average 39% energy savings and 23% EDP reduction vs the 0.7V/2GHz nominal implementation, with accuracy degradation held within 1% Top-1 (vision) or 1 perplexity point (OPT-125M on WikiText-2) of the 8-bit baseline.
- Per-model energy gains vs the 0.7V baseline: 41% for ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10), 43% for ResNet-50 (ImageNet), 38% for ViT-B/32 (ImageNet), 37% for OPT-125M (WikiText-2). Versus a traditional 0.5V@1GHz "safe-mode" baseline, gains are 18–20% for the CNNs, and 14% (ViT) / 12% (OPT-125M) but with EDP reduced by over 42% across the two transformer models.
- Motivating failure mode: injecting gate-level-sampled timing errors into ResNet-50's feature maps collapses Top-1 accuracy to 0% — even a single failing MAC unit suffices, so unmitigated low-voltage operation is prohibitive.
- Characterization numbers: power drops 61% from 254 mW (0.7V) to ~90 mW (0.5V) and ~20 mW (0.4V), while overvolting to 0.9V costs 532 mW; achievable frequency falls from 2.8 GHz (0.9V) and 2.1 GHz (0.7V) to 1.3 GHz at 0.5V (1.0 GHz under +3σ Vth variation) and 0.6 GHz at 0.4V (400 MHz with variation), i.e., near-threshold EDP is 1.45× (0.5V) to 3.5× (0.4V) worse than nominal without the proposed fixes; TOPS/W still improves 1.5×/2.0× at 0.5V/0.4V.
- DFS operating-point table at 0.5V: 1.0 GHz → 24 safe bits (normalized EDP 1.45, 22.7 TOPS/W); 1.4 GHz → 19 bits (iso-EDP point, 0.99, 24.5 TOPS/W); 1.8 GHz → 14 bits (EDP 0.63, 29.4 TOPS/W); 2.0 GHz → 11 bits at iso-performance with 51% EDP reduction (0.49, 34.1 TOPS/W).
- Ablation across optimization stages: MPQ + unstructured pruning alone yields up to 32% energy gain with only a 47% latency penalty (vs the 100% penalty a conventional 0.5V safe-mode would need); adding AAT contributes a further 7% energy gain; final normalized EDP reaches 0.66–0.70 for CNNs but only 0.80 (ViT-B/32) and 0.83 (OPT-125M), as transformers require W6/W8 in many blocks and must run at 1.4–1.8 GHz.
- vs SOTA voltage-scaling accelerators (each normalized to its own nominal baseline): X-TPU 35.0% (15nm, ResNet-50/CIFAR), JETCAS '19 34.0% (65nm), X-NVDLA 41.5% (15nm), GAVINA 20.0% (12nm), ThunderVolt 45.0% (45nm, AlexNet, SRAM power excluded) — this work's 41%/43% on ResNet-18/-50 is competitive while uniquely covering process variation and transformer/LM workloads; total area overhead is 1.7%.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The individual ingredients (MSB masking, clock gating, accumulator-aware quantization, DFS) are each known; the novelty lies in the integration — anchoring DNN fine-tuning to bit-level, variation-aware timing profiles of a real post-layout 2nm array — and in two genuinely informative findings (errors localize at accumulator bits ≥12 under +3σ variation; INT4 does not reduce timing-failure probability vs INT8). This is solid incremental co-design, not a conceptual breakthrough.
- **Rigor:** The hardware side is rigorous (post-PnR, SS corner, statistical variation modeling, gate-level fault injection feeding PyTorch). The software side is thinner: the λ=10⁻³ regularizer weight is inherited from A2Q+ "similar to [7]" with no sensitivity analysis, and the ℓ1 overflow bound assumes a zero-mean weight distribution, an assumption the paper asserts rather than verifies post-training.
- **Reproducibility:** Hard. Everything hinges on the IMEC N2 pathfinding PDK and commercial EDA licenses unavailable to most academics; the ResNet-50 initial model points to a public Brevitas A2Q repo (good), but the exploration loop (200 iterations, per-model) and lib-characterization flow are not released, so exact reproduction is unlikely. Cross-checking the *relative* software gains on a published node would be feasible but is not attempted.
- **Significance vs overclaiming:** The headline "39% energy / 23% EDP" is honest as a cross-model average, but it is CNN-dominated: transformer models get only 12–14% energy gain over the fair 0.5V safe-mode baseline and normalized EDP of 0.80–0.83 (vs 0.66–0.70 for CNNs), and OPT-125M is a very small LM — no evidence yet that the method scales to modern LLMs whose accumulation depths and outlier channels are far more demanding. The SOTA comparison in Table 2 is explicitly apples-to-oranges (nodes from 12nm–65nm, benchmarks from AlexNet to ResNet-50, ThunderVolt excluding SRAM power), and ThunderVolt's 45% actually exceeds this work's 41–43% even under friendlier assumptions.
- **Red flags:** No silicon validation (pathfinding PDK only); error probabilities come from one characterized MAC design generalized die-wide; per-model offline DFS characterization adds deployment friction not costed in the evaluation; unstructured pruning is used purely for magnitude control with no discussion of whether its typical accuracy cost at the sparsities used was measured separately.

## Strengths

- Identifies and exploits a crisp, physically grounded structure in low-voltage timing failures (MSB-localized, frequency-dependent safe zones) instead of treating errors as generic stochastic noise.
- Full-stack, end-to-end methodology: from variation-aware .lib characterization through gate-level error distributions to PyTorch training constraints and back to EDP optimization — with the figure-of-merit actually optimized (EDP) rather than only energy.
- The counterintuitive INT4-vs-INT8 timing result is valuable for anyone designing approximate multipliers at NTV.
- Broad workload coverage for this literature (CNN, ViT, decoder LM) under a strict, uniform <1% accuracy budget; low 1.7% area cost; honest per-stage ablation (MPQ+pruning vs +AAT) and honest reporting that transformer gains are more modest.

## Limitations

- All results are post-layout simulation on a pre-silicon 2nm pathfinding PDK; no fabrication, and absolute numbers depend on PDK assumptions.
- Gains shrink markedly for attention-based models (ViT-B/32, OPT-125M) — both in energy (12–14% vs safe-mode) and EDP — and OPT-125M is far from representative of modern LLM inference; accumulation-heavy GEMM layers with large K and activation outliers are untested.
- The ℓ1-norm guarantee rests on a zero-mean weight assumption; the paper does not report how closely trained models satisfy it or what happens when they don't.
- No sensitivity study for the regularization weight λ, no comparison against A2Q+ alone at nominal voltage (isolating what the voltage constraint itself costs in accuracy), and no latency/energy accounting for the offline per-model DFS characterization.
- SOTA comparison spans heterogeneous nodes, benchmarks, and power-measurement scopes (e.g., ThunderVolt excludes SRAM), limiting interpretability of the relative rankings.

## Relevance

高度契合"Inference Reliability & SDC Detection"与"Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization"两个方向：本文正是关于加速器硬件故障（低压时序违例、工艺涨落引起的比特级损坏）对推理精度的灾难性影响及其软硬件协同检测/规避手段，同时覆盖量化、混合精度与硬件级算术错误等数值可靠性主题，是"用感知硬件错误约束的训练来换取可靠近阈值推理"的代表性工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the accumulator safe-zone approach survive decoder-only LLMs at the 1B+ scale, where KV-cache GEMMs, large dot-product depths K, and activation outliers push P_min far higher than in OPT-125M?
- Can the zero-mean assumption behind the ℓ1 bound be replaced by a per-channel, data-dependent bound (e.g., tracking observed activation sign bias) so the guarantee holds without retraining escapes?
- How much of the 23% average EDP gain survives on a production-calibrated node (e.g., Intel 18A / TSMC A16) versus the IMEC pathfinding PDK, and how sensitive are the safe-bit tables (Table 1) to PDK re-characterization?
- Could online (inference-time) safe-zone adaptation — e.g., lightweight in-situ error sensors per PE — replace the offline DFS characterization and reduce per-model deployment cost?
- Since INT4 does not reduce physical timing-failure probability, is there a datapath redesign (e.g., carry-select or prefix adders with balanced depth) that would flatten the bit-position error profile and widen the safe zone for free?
