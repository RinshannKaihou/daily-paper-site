> **一句话总结：** 本文在 29 个可自动验证的本地 Agent 任务（SQL 分析、数据修复、代码实现、仓库修复等六族）上实测 4-bit 与 8-bit 量化的 CPU+GPU 能耗，发现 4-bit 虽然每个 token 平均省 6–16% 能量，但在部分任务上因畸形工具调用、冗长输出和失败重试导致"每完成一个任务"反而多耗能（中位数 +66%，最坏 3.5 倍），因此按 token 能耗选精度会选错。

> **TL;DR:** On 29 verifiable agent tasks (SQL analytics to NetworkX repository repair) run on bare-metal A100 nodes with llama.cpp, the 4-bit (Q4_K_M) files of Qwen3-30B-A3B and Devstral Small 2 use 6–16% less energy per output token than 8-bit (Q8_0), yet on three supported tasks per condition the per-token-cheaper precision burns a median 66% more energy per completed task — up to 3.5x on the SQL business query — because it needs a median 1.6–2.0x more tokens per completion (malformed tool calls, narrated plans, re-run tests, failed attempts). A 16-bit BF16 reference is never clearly cheaper per completed task, and held-out EvalPerf problems confirm the family-level pattern (4-bit 10% cheaper per completion, ratio 0.90 [0.85, 0.98]). Conclusion: pick precision by energy per completed task, not per token — the energy bill arrives per task.

## 核心贡献

- **首个任务级量化能耗表征**：首次在本地部署的 LLM Agent 上同时测量"每 token 能耗"与"每完成一个任务的能耗"，系统比较 4-bit（Q4_K_M）与 8-bit（Q8_0）在完整 Agent 任务流中的差异，填补了"单请求量化对比"与"固定精度 Agent 能耗"两类研究之间的空白。
- **构建了带防作弊验证的 29 任务测量套件**：六个任务族（SQL 分析、数据修复、回归控制、数据处理、函数实现、仓库修复），每族用隐藏参考答案 + 私有种子输入的确定性验证器判定成败，并通过 Landlock 沙箱与对抗性检查（拒绝硬编码答案、伪造成功信息等）防作弊。
- **提出能耗恒等分解与盈亏平衡规则**：E_op = E_tok × (tokens/completion) × (boundary factor)，证明只要低精度版本"每完成任务的额外 token 数"超过其每 token 节省率（6–16% 只能覆盖 6–19% 的额外 token），每任务能耗就会反超。
- **严格的因果追踪与统计支撑**：将额外能耗溯源到畸形工具调用（4-bit 下多 9 倍）、失败尝试（SQL 分析中错误提交多 3 倍）和冗长输出；用标签置换噪声基线剔除巧合性"分歧"，确认每条件下 3 个统计显著的反超任务。
- **可操作的部署建议**：短函数/代码类任务可直接用 4-bit；结构化 SQL 单答案场景需在自有任务上双精度实测；长上下文仓库修复在大预算下 4-bit 反而完成率更高（45/89 vs 26/89）。

## Key Contributions

- First task-level characterization of quantization energy trade-offs in locally hosted LLM agents, measuring both energy per output token and energy per completed task on the same agent attempts for 4-bit (Q4_K_M) vs 8-bit (Q8_0) GGUF files of two open-weight models (Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE, Devstral Small 2 dense 24B).
- A 29-task verifiable benchmark across six families (SQL analytics, data repair, regression control, data processing adapted from DS-1000, function implementation adapted from EvalPerf/HumanEval+/MBPP+, and repository repair of injected NetworkX 3.4.2 defects), with hardened hidden verifiers, private-seed re-checks, and Landlock sandboxing against gaming.
- A measurement harness on bare-metal Chameleon A100 nodes that attributes CPU-package and GPU-board energy (0.1 s powercap/NVML counters) to every model call and tool call, charges failed attempts to completions, and enforces attempt-validity rules (recomputable energy records, token-count consistency, no stray processes).
- An algebraic identity, ρ_op = ρ_tok × token factor × boundary factor, plus a break-even rule: a 6–16% per-token saving is erased once the cheaper precision needs more than ~6–19% extra tokens per completed task.
- A methodologically careful evaluation design: pre-registered confirmatory main experiment vs exploratory extensions (expanded budget, 16-bit reference, second model, held-out BIRD/EvalPerf tasks with predictions made before running), 95% bootstrap intervals, McNemar/Fisher completion tests, and a label-permutation noise baseline that correctly discounts raw disagreement counts.

## Methodology

The study compares two GGUF precision files of each model served by llama.cpp (revision fcc8915, one precision at a time) on dedicated Chameleon nodes (dual AMD EPYC 7763, one NVIDIA A100 40 GB; 80 GB for the 16-bit reference), with the agent sending token IDs through each model's own chat template. An attempt is one seeded agent run on one task at one precision; a hidden deterministic research verifier decides pass/fail after submission. Three energy measures are computed over each precision's valid attempts: E_tok (model-call energy per output token), E_inf (model-call energy per verified completion), and E_op (whole-task-window energy per verified completion, charging failed attempts to successes). Energy comes from Linux powercap CPU-package counters and NVML GPU-board counters sampled every 0.1 s; model loading and verifier energy are measured separately and excluded (with a worst-case one-load-per-attempt sensitivity check).

The core analytical tool is the identity ρ_op = ρ_tok × (T4/S4)/(T8/S8) × (O4/I4)/(O8/I8): the per-task ratio equals the per-token ratio times a token factor (tokens per completed task, further split into a length factor and a completion-rate factor) times a boundary factor (tool/waiting energy outside model calls). A comparable task requires both precisions to complete it at least 3 times; a disagreement is supported only when both measures' 95% bootstrap intervals (5,000 draws) exclude 1 in opposite directions, and a label-permutation noise baseline estimates how many disagreements chance alone would produce.

Budgets: a primary budget (16,384-token context, 24 turns, 600 s deadline) and an expanded budget (65,536 context, 250 turns — mini-SWE-agent's step limit, 1,800 s). Each condition runs 1 greedy + 10 sampled repetitions (1+6 for Qwen expanded, 1+2 for held-out) with the same seeds for both precisions, interleaved in seeded random order with 45 s settling gaps. Held-out tasks: 40 BIRD text-to-SQL questions and the 37 eligible EvalPerf problems, with pre-registered directional predictions (8-bit better on BIRD, 4-bit better on EvalPerf).

## Results & Highlights

- **Per-token vs per-task divergence (RQ1):** 4-bit uses 6–16% less energy per output token on average over the 29 tasks in all three full conditions (e.g., Qwen main experiment: 2.38 vs 2.64 J/token median) and is cheaper per token on 25–29 of them; yet three tasks per condition cost more at 4-bit per completed task beyond run-to-run variation (noise baseline: only ≤3% of label shuffles produce this many).
- **The business query example (Qwen, 1M rows):** 4-bit saves 14% per token but uses 29% more per completed task — it needs 59% more tokens per completion (token factor 1.59, boundary factor 0.94), blowing past the 16% extra-token allowance that the 14% saving can cover.
- **Cost of choosing by per-token energy (RQ2):** on supported disagreements, the per-token-cheaper precision uses a median 66% more energy per completed task, ranging from 29% (business query at 1M rows) to 3.5x (same query at 8,192 rows, where 4-bit took 8 turns per attempt vs 3 and generated ~4x the tokens). A retest on held-out repetitions keeps a median 31–45% extra cost for Qwen (per-task choice cheaper in 10 of 11 cases).
- **Extra tokens, not tool energy, drive the cost (RQ3):** the per-token-cheaper precision needs a median 1.6–2.0x more tokens per completed task across conditions. On SQL analytics, Qwen at 4-bit submits wrong answers 3x as often (20 vs 6 of 60 attempts) and emits malformed/missing tool calls 9x as often (27 vs 3 of 60); in 6 of 10 business-query attempts it submitted an answer one closing brace short, which the parser rejected. The boundary factor flips the choice on at most one task per condition.
- **Budget and model change the winner (RQ4):** with the expanded budget on repository repairs, 4-bit completes ~70% more repairs than 8-bit (45 vs 26 of 89, p = 0.006; one repair went 9/10 vs 0/10 as 8-bit spun to ~220 turns vs ~30); the 16-bit BF16 reference uses 31% more energy per token than 8-bit and 14% more per completed task [0.93, 1.49] — never clearly cheaper. Held-out predictions held: EvalPerf favors 4-bit (0.90 [0.85, 0.98], completions 65/74 vs 68/74); BIRD shows no supported difference (0.91 [0.77, 1.09]).
- **Averages hide the tail:** the condition-average per-task ratio is 1.17 (Qwen primary budget, +17% for 4-bit) but 0.92 in Qwen expanded and Devstral (−8%), while single tasks swing from 0.13 (regression control at 8,192 rows, 4-bit uses ~1/8 of 8-bit's energy) to 3.5x — geometric means let a halving and a doubling cancel.

## Authors & Credibility

Seven authors from Texas A&M University (Abkin, Yang, Flesher, Kodarapu with equal-contribution marks; Panigrahy, Tyagi, Houngninou). Notably, Panigrahy and Tyagi are the authors of the prior "Energy per Successful Goal" arXiv paper that this work's energy-per-completed-task accounting directly builds on, giving the team genuine lineage in goal-level energy measurement. The experimental craft is unusually strong for a systems-measurement paper: bare-metal isolation, seeded random order with power settling, drift checks showing <0.35% per-repetition energy-per-token change with differential drift <0.1%, a pre-registered confirmatory/exploratory split, a label-permutation noise baseline, adversarial verifier validation (Table 7), and full per-attempt records with file hashes. Code and experiment records are already public on GitHub (stephen122204/mantra-energy), with a Zenodo release promised on acceptance. The work is arXiv cs.PF, v2 dated 26 Oct 2026.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** genuinely novel framing — this is the first study to vary quantization precision and measure hardware energy on the same complete agent tasks, combining the two halves of prior work (Jang et al. varied precision without energy; Pham et al. measured agent energy at fixed precision). The token-factor identity and break-even rule are simple but useful contributions.
- **Rigor:** high. The noise baseline is the standout: raw disagreement counts (10, 10, 8) look impressive but the permutation test shows chance alone yields 8.6, 8.2, 4.8 — and the authors say so, counting only the 3 supported ones. The retest on separate repetitions to simulate an operator's decision is exactly the right validation.
- **Reproducibility:** strong (public harness, per-attempt energy records, hashes, seed-paired designs, validity rules that refuse to rerun invalid attempts to avoid outcome-dependent selection). One caveat: AMD package counters are power-model estimates and the GPU sensor samples intermittently, so absolute joules carry sensor bias — the authors correctly argue ratios remain valid.
- **Weaknesses / red flags:**
  - The headline 3.5x figure rests substantially on a strict harness design: the parser repairs nothing but a missing end tag and charges every malformed tool call a full turn. Since the dominant failure is literally a single dropped closing brace, a llama.cpp grammar-constrained decoder (which the authors themselves note) could erase much of the effect — the per-task penalty is partly a property of the agent interface, not of quantization per se.
  - Conditions confound variables: "Qwen expanded" changes budget, machine (80 GB node D), and adds 16-bit simultaneously; the authors only compare within conditions and admit it. Two models, one GPU generation (A100), one engine (llama.cpp), one request at a time, no batching — generalization to vLLM-style batched serving or other GPUs is untested.
  - External validity of the task set is limited: 29 tasks (three self-written SQL/regression families are tiny), repository repair uses artificially injected single defects in one library (NetworkX), and the 4-vs-8-bit comparison conflates bit width with quantization scheme, calibration, and kernels (acknowledged, since developers pick files, but it blocks attribution of the cause).
  - Evidence is asymmetric across models: for Devstral the retest kept the per-task choice cheaper in only 2 of 5 cases, and budget sensitivity is large (the hardest-repair winner flips from 8-bit at 24 turns to 4-bit at 250 turns), so several conclusions are contingent on the chosen budget rather than robust.
  - No ablation with grammar-constrained decoding or malformed-call repair, which is the obvious control given their own diagnosis.

## Strengths

- Frames and answers a question operators actually face — the paper's thesis ("the energy bill arrives per completed task, not per token") is crisp, actionable, and well-aligned with Anthropic's task-cost guidance.
- Measurement methodology is exemplary: bare-metal, seed-paired, randomized order, drift-monitored, validity-checked, with failed attempts honestly charged to completions.
- The identity ρ_op = ρ_tok × token factor × boundary factor turns an empirical observation into a deployable rule of thumb (track tokens per completed task against the per-token saving).
- Pre-registered held-out predictions (EvalPerf favors 4-bit, BIRD does not) were made before running and both held — rare discipline in measurement papers.
- Honest statistics: the noise baseline, the confirmatory/exploratory split, and the separation-repetition retest all guard against overclaiming.

## Limitations

- Single-request serving, one engine (llama.cpp), one GPU generation, one CPU family; no batching, no concurrent tenants, no wall-power cross-check (DRAM, fans, PSU losses excluded).
- The strict tool-call parser amplifies 4-bit's cost; grammar-constrained decoding or call repair could change the numbers materially and is left as future work.
- 16-bit reference exists only for Qwen (Devstral ships 8-bit float), on a different machine, with 1+6 repetitions rather than 1+10.
- Small task families make several per-family conclusions fragile (regression control has 2 tasks; its 0.47 family average conceals a 0.13-to-1.72 split across input sizes).
- Repository-repair tasks use injected single defects rather than real issue history, and open-ended agent work (web, research) with graded outcomes is out of scope.
- Devstral's disagreement evidence is thinner (2 of 5 retests), and the held-out condition used only 1+2 repetitions.

## Relevance

与"极限性能优化下的可靠性"方向高度相关：4-bit 量化在 token 级能耗几乎全面占优，却在任务级因畸形工具调用（少一个右括号）和更高失败率而反超，正是量化精度问题向 Agent 软件栈（工具调用分发）传导并改变系统级指标的典型案例；其"每 token 指标掩盖任务级真实成本"的结论对低精度推理与 Agent 可靠性评估都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the 4-bit per-task penalty survives once tool calls are grammar-constrained or automatically repaired — is the effect fundamentally about output-format fragility rather than energy?
- Do the findings transfer to batched, multi-tenant serving engines (vLLM, TensorRT-LLM) where idle energy is amortized across requests, and to newer quantization formats (e.g., FP8, AWQ, LittleBit-style sub-1-bit)?
- Would a budget tuned per precision (giving 4-bit the longer context its smaller file allows) systematically favor 4-bit on the hardest repairs?
- Does quantization interact with thinking/reasoning modes, where token counts per problem already inflate 21–75%, compounding the token factor?
- Can tokens-per-completed-task be monitored online in a deployed agent to trigger automatic precision switching per task family?
- Is the malformed-tool-call rate (9x higher at 4-bit for Qwen) predictable from perplexity or calibration degradation on the chat template, enabling a cheap pre-deployment screen?
