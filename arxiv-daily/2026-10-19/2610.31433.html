[abs]Drift Says When, Budget Says How Much: Rate-Controlled KV Re-selection for Long-Form Multimodal Generation

Bin Hu (Tsinghua University) · Yingbo Xiao (CUHK-Shenzhen)

KV cache compression multimodal LLM long-form generation attention drift inference efficiency
Key Metric
+1.6→+3.8
ROUGE-L fidelity vs InfiniGen (per-step retrieval)

Significant on five of six model–task settings, with a single configuration (ρ = 0.4, 64 KV entries/head). Paired bootstrap 95% CIs; best win .811 vs .773 TextCaps (+3.8). Detector state 4× smaller than InfiniGen's.

Why This Matters

Aggressive inference optimization can silently corrupt long-form output — this work makes compression-induced drift measurable and budgeted.

A reusable control paradigm for anyone weighing correctness–efficiency trade-offs in quantization, sparse attention, or cache eviction.

⚠ Critical Edge

The candidate fetch sits on the refreshing layer's critical path — it cannot be prefetched the way InfiniGen does — and all costs are simulated traffic, never measured latency.

Fidelity is deviation from the model's own uncompressed output, not caption correctness; no human evaluation, no code release.

01 Methodology — How RCR Works

Runs on Qwen2-VL-7B, LLaVA-NeXT-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B with FlashAttention-2 at a budget of 64 KV entries/head (32 selected prefix tokens + 32 observation window). Drift = attention mass the model would place on evicted prefix tokens. Evaluation metric is fidelity: ROUGE-L F1 between the compressed run and the uncompressed output of the same model.

Estimate drift, cheaply8-channel low-rank SVD detector (top-8 right singular vectors of the 128-d key matrix) approximates per-head attention over the full prefix — ≈Np/16 dot products per step, resident on GPU.
Trigger on deviationRefresh when the fraction of heads above threshold τ deviates from the target rate ρ = 0.4 — decided entirely GPU-side via masked merge, no host sync (~3.4× faster simulation).
Track the quantile, not the valueτ ← τ·exp(η(f−ρ)) — a multiplicative Robbins–Monro step tracking the (1−ρ)-quantile of drift, so every sample gets its fair share of refreshes. Fixed thresholds starve low-drift samples.
Re-select narrowlyExact re-scoring only among the detector's top-4c candidates → read 5c = 160 vectors per refresh instead of scanning the whole prefix (which lives on CPU).

Baselines (shared re-implementation)

Quest · ArkVale · two-stage Quest · periodic refresh · InfiniGen · fixed-threshold drift rule · exact every-step re-selection. Tasks: DetailCaps (300 images, median 157–238 words), TextCaps (500), 300 pre-registered ActivityNet videos.

Where the gain vanishes — as the diagnosis predicts

Generous budgetsAt 256 entries/head the InfiniGen lead shrinks to +1.2 [−0.1, +2.4] — not significant. RCR matters most at tight budgets.
Low-drift modelsQwen3-VL DetailCaps: +0.5, CI includes zero (drift uniformly small, prefixes shorter: 639 vs 852 tokens).
  • The motivating negative result: one-shot prefill selection caps at 0.44–0.52 ROUGE-L even for an answer-attention oracle — long-form failure is staleness, not scoring quality (short-answer VQA oracle is lossless: .851 vs .848).
  • Closes most of the gap to exact re-selection: within 1.7 pts everywhere (above it on LLaVA DetailCaps, .485 vs .478) while refreshing only 40–48% of layer-steps; +11.4–21.0 over BACON.
  • Timing, not frequency: at a matched 40% refresh rate, random timing loses 5.0 pts, periodic T=2 loses 2.0, T=3 loses 4.6.
  • Rate control fixes the diagnosed failure: low-drift terciles rise .548 → .718 (above InfiniGen .707), while fixed thresholds refresh only 1% there.
  • Cost on par: 63–77 vectors/step/head (InfiniGen/Quest: 64); ρ is a smooth quality–cost knob (.538/.560/.598/.606 for ρ=0.1→0.6; at ρ=0.2 matches InfiniGen reading half the vectors).
  • Object-level check: +1.3 / +2.5 object F1 vs uncompressed output on Qwen2-VL / LLaVA (object recall +4.8).

03 Authors & Credibility

Two-author academic team (Bin Hu, Tsinghua; Yingbo Xiao, CUHK-Shenzhen); no code release mentioned. Unusually strong rigor signals for a systems paper: components chosen on a 100-image dev split (2-image overlap with test), confirmed under a pre-registered decision rule; all 14 candidate variants reported; every headline delta carries paired bootstrap 95% CIs; the video experiment was pre-registered; and the paper volunteers its own dev→test margin shrinkage (3.5 → 2.8 on Qwen2-VL). Caveat: CIs are not corrected for dev-split selection; ρ/budget sweeps were post-hoc characterization.

04 Strengths

  • One calibrated config transfers. The refresh quantile ρ=0.4 works across 3 models × 2 tasks where the fixed threshold needs per-setting tuning — and collapses on Qwen3-VL.
  • Diagnosis → fix → confirmation arc. Tercile analysis predicts where gains land (low-drift samples: .548→.718), and the video non-result confirms the mechanism, not just the leaderboard.
  • Timing ablation at matched rates (random −5.0, periodic −2.0 to −4.6) cleanly attributes gains to refreshing at the right moments, not more often.
  • Engineering honesty: GPU masked-merge verified token-for-token against a branching implementation; host-sync removal quantified at 3.4×.

05 Limitations

  • No end-to-end latency in a real offloading system — costs are simulated CPU→GPU traffic; the critical-path fetch issue is future work.
  • Narrow scope: 7–8B MLLMs, two image-captioning tasks, ActivityNet video; budgets 128/256 tested only on Qwen2-VL DetailCaps; no long-doc QA, reasoning chains, or multi-image generation.
  • Fidelity tracks the model's own output rather than correctness; human evaluation absent; object analysis relies on an automatic scene-graph parser.
  • Gains are conditional: model-dependent (n.s. on Qwen3-VL DetailCaps) and vanish with homogeneous drift (video) or generous budgets (256/head).

06 Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can the per-sample quantile controller extend to per-layer thresholds, given layers 4–7 almost never refresh while layers 14 and 21 refresh on most steps?
  2. What is the true end-to-end latency profile in a real offloading system where RCR's critical-path fetch cannot be prefetched?
  3. Does the drift-staleness diagnosis extend to long-document QA, multi-image reasoning, and multimodal reasoning chains?
  4. Can ρ be set adaptively per sample from output-length or task cues, instead of a global 0.4 that overshoots (41–48%) on short 12–29-step outputs?
  5. Would combining RCR's drift trigger with InfiniGen's previous-layer speculative fetch recover per-step quality at sub-per-step traffic?
  6. Does fidelity-to-self predict human-judged caption quality, given reference-based metrics failed to separate dynamic methods?
