01

Methodology

3 layers, one profile

Decode-time MoE layers eat 83–92% of end-to-end latency at batch 64, and the hottest expert draws ~20× mean load. 3D-MoE answers with a three-layer co-design:

  1. Profile once, offline. 256 prompts × 256 decode steps, no domain labels. A circular-shift null test certifies expert pairs as “complementary” when their Pearson load correlation falls below the shuffled 5th-percentile. Grouping = lexicographic ILP (balance compute first, then collaboration cost); mapping = quadratic placement with token co-activation affinity. Gurobi solves it once per model in 1–3 h.
  2. CMN hardware fast path. The host core reads each hot expert’s weight tile from local DRAM once; a Data Forwarding Unit streams it over reconfigurable links to ≤16 helper cores for direct GEMM use — no repeated remote reads, no per-hop packets. Link switch: 40 cycles (61.5 ns).
  3. Tiered online remapping. EMA (α = 0.3) load prediction drives fraction redistribution → replica add/remove → expert swaps, every 8 decode steps or on sustained imbalance; 91% of migration time hides under compute.

EVAL — cycle-accurate Ramulator 2.0 + BookSim; CMN in RTL, synthesized 7 nm. Baselines: vLLM 0.8.1 GPU, static EP/TP, predictive replication (PS), work stealing (WS), HD-MoE. Workloads: LMSys, ShareGPT, MATH, HumanEval.

03

Strengths

what holds up
  • Turns a routing phenomenon into a deployable, label-free signal — significance-tested (circular-shift null), not threshold-guessed.
  • Coherent division of labor: the offline plan fixes mean imbalance, CMN fixes variance cheaply (one DRAM read serves all helpers), tiered remapping fixes drift.
  • Cheap silicon: 2.11% area, 0.337 W per stack, reusing existing NoC links instead of adding wires.
  • Generalization stress-tested across datasets, time windows, and batch sizes 32–768 — directly answering “will an offline plan survive serving drift?”
04

Limitations

read before deploying
  • No physical prototype — everything is Ramulator 2.0/BookSim simulation with synthesized RTL power/area numbers.
  • Requires a 1–3 h offline Gurobi pass and assumes serving mix ≈ profiling distribution; novel domains are handled only reactively.
  • Gains shrink with few or large experts (8-expert Llama: 1.2×; 236B model needed relaxed cross-stack grouping).
  • Homogeneous single-domain serving weakens complementarity — acknowledged, but not quantified.
  • CMN’s link-disjoint domains could fragment the NoC under many simultaneous hotspots — never stress-tested.

ProvenanceSKLP, ICT Chinese Academy of Sciences (+ UCAS, U Edinburgh). Senior authors Yinhe Han & Ying Wang — venue lineage of Duplex and Stratum. CredibilitySignal validated with null-hypothesis tests; hardware in RTL synthesized at 7 nm (not estimated); baselines include a real vLLM GPU reference. Unusually careful for a systems paper.

05

Questions for further exploration

  • Does complementarity survive quantization, or post-training fine-tune/RL that re-specializes experts?
  • What happens under single-domain flooding, where load cancellation structurally cannot occur?
  • Could this grouping transfer to multi-GPU EP deployments — replacing EPLB-style replication without custom silicon?
  • When hot experts contend for overlapping RCL links, is the greedy link-disjoint scheduler still near-optimal?
  • Is 256-prompt profiling enough for routing that drifts over weeks — how often must the offline re-solve run?
