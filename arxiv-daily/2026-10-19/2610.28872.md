> **一句话总结：** LEAN 在微调损失中加入一个复合正则项（限制每层翻转步长的 range 项 + 压低最敏感权重梯度的 tail 项），主动把训练好的模型的重尾权重敏感度分布压缩 5–13 倍（精度损失 ≤0.5 点），使 1% 覆盖率的 BCH 码可扛住 300–500 次自适应位翻转攻击（WCAT 同码在 500 次时只剩 75%），并把固定保护保证的校验开销降低 3.7 倍，同时让 8/6/4/3-bit 敏感度分层存储格式在 ImageNet ResNet-50 上以 3.29 bit/权重达到 77.2% top-1（LSQ 3-bit 为 74.4%）。

> **TL;DR:** The authors treat a network's weight-sensitivity distribution (top 1% of weights carry ~20% of first-order bit-flip damage) as a designable quantity: a 10-epoch fine-tune with a two-term regularizer shrinks the sensitive tail 5–13x on nine vision models (ResNet-18/50, VGG-16 on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet) within 0.5 pts accuracy. With the same 1% BCH code on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, LEAN survives 300 progressive-bit-search flips in every seed and holds 94% at 500 in two of three seeds, where the normal model and three of four published defenses (piecewise clustering, Hessian-aware, Forget-and-Rewire) break by 300 and WCAT falls to 75% at 500. Parity for a fixed protection guarantee drops 3.7x, and a sensitivity-ordered 8/6/4/3-bit format stores ImageNet ResNet-50 at 3.29 bits/weight with 77.2% top-1 vs 74.4% for LSQ at 3 bits (+2.8 pts) — a 2.4x smaller payload than INT8 with an estimated 59% weight-fetch energy saving.

## 核心贡献

- **把权重敏感度分布当作可设计的对象**：首次提出在训练期系统性调制（而非被动接受）模型权重敏感度分布——证明翻转损失 $d_i = |\partial L/\partial w_i| \cdot \Delta_\ell$ 由梯度与存储格式的翻转步长两个因子决定，并同时对两者施加正则。
- **一个旋钮的复合正则器**：$\mathcal{L}_\lambda = \mathcal{L}_{CE} + \lambda(P_{tail} + \beta P_{clip})$——tail 项压低最敏感 top-1% 权重的平均敏感度（通过 double backprop 计算 Hessian-向量积，每 4 步一次，微调开销约 1.5 倍），range 项（$\beta{=}100$ 固定不调）裁剪每层 99 分位以外的离群权重以缩小 INT8 翻转步长；单一系数 λ 单调控制尾部收缩程度。
- **严格的消融与"真翻转"检验**：实测翻转证明效果不是梯度掩蔽——tail 项单独使用（λ=400）会让代理指标显示 HIGH 缩小 3 倍但真实损伤不变；range 项在 INT8 下承担主要收缩，tail 项在其之上再降低 25% 真实损伤；普通梯度范数惩罚无法复现该效果。
- **对保护、精度、存储三方面的系统性收益**：1% BCH 码下从 200 次翻转存活提升到 300–500 次；固定保证的校验开销降 3.7 倍；敏感度分层 8/6/4/3-bit 格式使 ImageNet ResNet-50 在 3.29 bit/权重下达到 77.2% top-1（高于 3-bit LSQ 的 74.4%），权重负载比 INT8 小 2.4 倍。
- **初步扩展到语言模型**：Qwen2-0.5B / Llama-3.2-1B / Qwen2-1.5B 上 HIGH 尾部收缩 1.7–5.3 倍，代价 0.6 困惑度，且 INT4 量化后困惑度在两个小模型上反而改善（21.1→19.3、21.4→18.9）。

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the weight-sensitivity distribution as a training-time design choice rather than a fixed inheritance: the damage of a sign-bit flip factorizes into the weight's gradient and the flip step of the storage format, and both factors are regularized directly.
- A single-knob composite regularizer: a tail term (mean first-order damage of the top-1% most sensitive weights, differentiated through the gradient via double backpropagation, computed every 4th step so the fine-tune costs ~1.5x a plain one) plus a fixed-weight range term clipping layer outliers at the 99th quantile to bound the INT8 flip step; λ monotonically sets how far the tail moves.
- A credible falsification protocol: real single-bit flips verify the reshaped weights genuinely do less damage (worst-100 damage 0.031 → 0.009 loss/flip), and an ablation shows the tail term alone exhibits gradient masking while a plain gradient-norm penalty does not reproduce the reshaping — the storage-format weighting and top-k selection are what matter.
- Demonstrated systems consequences across nine vision model/dataset pairs (ResNet-18, ResNet-50, VGG-16 × CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet): 5–13x smaller sensitive tail within 0.5 pts accuracy; 300–500-flip survival under progressive bit search with the same 1% code where four published defenses fail earlier; 3.7x less parity for a fixed guarantee; and a 3.29 bits/weight tiered format beating 3-bit LSQ by 2.8 pts on ImageNet ResNet-50.
- A preliminary extension to three small LLMs (Qwen2-0.5B, Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2-1.5B, one epoch on 1M WikiText-2 tokens) showing the same tail shaping and improved post-training INT4 perplexity on two of three models.

## Methodology

Weights are stored INT8 with one scale per tensor, so a sign-bit flip moves every weight in a layer by the same step $\Delta_\ell = 128 s_\ell$; the first-order loss change ("damage") of weight $i$ is $d_i = |g_i| \Delta_\ell$. Baseline distributions are heavy-tailed — on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 the top 1% of weights (HIGH bin, thresholds fixed once from the baseline at the top-1%/top-9% damage quantiles) carry ~20% of total flip damage, and sensitivity spans four orders of magnitude between median and tail. LEAN fine-tunes a trained checkpoint for ten epochs with $L_\lambda = L_{CE} + \lambda(P_{tail} + \beta P_{clip})$, where $P_{tail} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i \in T} d_i$ over the $k = \lceil 0.01N \rceil$ most sensitive weights (re-selected each step, step size $\Delta_\ell$ and the top-k selection detached so the second-order term differentiates only through $g$), and $P_{clip} = \sum_\ell \text{mean}(\text{relu}(|W_\ell| - q_{99}))^2$ bounds the flip step; $\beta = 100$ everywhere. The gradient of $P_{tail}$ is a Hessian-vector product computed by double backpropagation every fourth step (scaled 4x), giving ~1.5x plain fine-tune cost. An accompanying proposition shows a step on $\lambda P_{tail}$ moves weights along Hessian rows of tail weights and lowers their gradients at rate $\|HD\sigma\|^2$. The procedure can also be switched on for the last ten epochs of normal training (reaching the same floor only if the fine-tune's learning rate is held, since the dying cosine rate moves the tail halfway). Deployment is either INT8 plus a selective BCH(127,43) code over the four most significant bit planes of the HIGH weights with parity hidden in low-order bits of LOW weights, or a sensitivity-ordered 8/6/4/3-bit tiered format hardened with an interleaved sign-plane BCH code.

## Results & Highlights

- **Tail shrink at almost no accuracy cost (Table I):** on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, weights above the fixed HIGH threshold drop from 112k to 13k (1.00% → 0.11%, a 9x count reduction) and the top-1% damage share falls 19.5% → 10.8%, with accuracy 95.1% → 94.9%; on ImageNet ResNet-50 the HIGH set shrinks 5x (255k → 51k) with top-1 80.7% → 80.5%; across all nine vision configs the reduction is 5–13x within 0.5 pts (worst case ResNet-50 CIFAR-100, 75.9 → 75.4).
- **Adaptive attack survival (Table V, progressive bit search, ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, same 1% BCH code):** the normal model and a plain gradient-norm fine-tune break at 200 flips; piecewise clustering and Hessian-aware training break at 200; Forget-and-Rewire at 300; WCAT reaches 500 flips at 75% accuracy; LEAN (λ=40) holds 94% at 500 flips in two of three training seeds and survives 300 in every seed. Without any code, every method including LEAN is at chance within 50–100 flips. On ImageNet ResNet-18 with the 1% code, LEAN holds 83% at 100 flips and 72% at 200 vs 69% and 41% for the normal model.
- **Real-flip damage check and ablation (Table IV):** in the LEAN model the 100 worst real flips cost 0.009 loss/flip vs 0.031 in the baseline (-71%), and the baseline's own top-1000 weights cost 0.0014 vs 0.0069 (-80%); the range term alone already reaches worst-100 damage 0.012 at zero accuracy cost, the tail term adds the last 25% (0.012 → 0.009), while the tail term alone at λ=400 masks (proxy HIGH 0.35% but real damage unchanged at 0.031, accuracy -1 pt).
- **Parity for a fixed guarantee (Table VII):** to hold 95% accuracy at 6% random bit-error rate and no drop at 25 progressive flips, the normal ResNet-18 needs 20% coverage / 18.0% parity, while LEAN λ=40 needs 5.3% coverage / 4.8% parity — a 3.7x parity reduction.
- **Storage (Table VIII, ImageNet ResNet-50 top-1):** tiered LEAN at 3.29 bits/weight reaches 77.2% vs 74.4% for LSQ at 3 bits (+2.8 pts; tiered λ=0 alone gives 76.8, and uniform QAT with λ alone gives 74.3, i.e. equal to LSQ); at exactly matched 4.00 bits, tiered LEAN is 79.6% vs 78.4% for LSQ (+1.2) and 75.9% for KURE (+3.7). Payload falls 2.4x vs INT8 (25.6 MB → 10.5 MB) with an estimated 59% less weight-fetch energy (4.1 → 1.7 mJ/inference from DRAM, 32 → 13 µJ from SRAM, analytical estimate using 45 nm access energies).
- **LLM extension (Tables XII–XIII, 1 epoch on 1M WikiText-2 tokens):** HIGH shrinks 1.7–3.4x at deployable λ with 0.6 perplexity cost; post-training INT4 improves Qwen2-0.5B from 21.1 to 19.3 and Llama-3.2-1B from 21.4 to 18.9, while λ=3 on Qwen2-1.5B is too strong (+2.1 ppl, no INT4 gain). Under progressive sign-bit flips with a 1% code, Llama-3.2-1B LEAN is 3x better from 50 flips on (ppl 42 vs 122 at 50, 61 vs 210 at 100) but worse in the first 20.

## Authors & Credibility

Habibur Rahaman and Swarup Bhunia, Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Florida. Bhunia leads a long-established hardware-security lab (hardware Trojans, DNN hardware security, edge AI) and is a credible senior author for bit-flip attack/defense work; Rahaman has a prior arXiv preprint (2507.06380) on secure, storage-efficient edge models from the same collaboration. The paper is a 17-page IEEE-conference-style manuscript with eleven tables, an explicit ablation, a gradient-masking check, three seeds where it matters most, and an unusually frank limitations section. Baselines (LSQ, KURE, piecewise clustering, WCAT, Forget-and-Rewire, Hessian-aware training) were all retrained in the authors' own pipeline for comparability — a strength of internal consistency, though it departs from the baselines' original recipes. No public code release is mentioned.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-positioned — prior robust-training methods (WCAT, piecewise clustering, FaR, Hessian-aware) change the sensitivity distribution only as a side effect, and selective coding schemes take the distribution as given; LEAN is the first to explicitly minimize the size of the set a selective code must cover, measured in the deployed storage format. The decomposition of flip damage into gradient × format step, with each factor regularized separately, is clean.
- **Rigor:** Above average for this literature. The real-flip check catching the tail term's gradient masking (Table IV) and the plain gradient-norm control are exactly the right controls, and honest negative results are reported (e.g., ImageNet ResNets show no targeted-attack gain because mixed-data sensitivity does not rank class-conditional sensitivity; ImageNet VGG-16 without a code falls to 22–27% on non-source classes).
- **Reproducibility concerns:** Most table entries are single runs (the authors admit this; only the storage comparison and the ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 reference have three seeds), and the headline 500-flip survival "sits at a threshold and varies with the training seed" — the seed-robust claim is 300 flips, not 500. The 0.1–0.5-pt accuracy costs in Table I are within single-run noise. No code release is stated.
- **Threat-model caveats:** The attacker is white-box and knows the code, but greedy — a search planning across codewords could do better, and the whole protection story rests on the interleaving assumption that an attacker cannot accumulate more than t errors in one codeword before correction; the code itself is never simulated, flips on covered bits are simply modelled as corrected. Random faults are evaluated at a single BER (6%).
- **Overclaiming check:** Mostly careful, but two places deserve scrutiny. First, the storage gain is mostly not LEAN's: the authors' own decomposition attributes 2.4 of the 2.8 pts over 3-bit LSQ to sensitivity-ordered tiering (λ=0) and only 0.4 pts to λ; λ alone equals LSQ (74.3 vs 74.4) — the fair claim is that LEAN makes the tiering affordable, not that it is a better quantizer. Second, the ImageNet targeted-attack rows have a data-overlap quirk the authors disclose (40 of 50 validation images per class were in the LEAN fine-tuning split while the normal model is the untouched torchvision checkpoint), so those rows favor LEAN. The low-bit security mode on ImageNet is also weak in places: at 4.09 bits/weight ResNet-18 is still 14 pts below clean after 500 flips.
- **Significance:** Strong for the edge/hardware-security community: one training term simultaneously buys attack tolerance, cheaper parity, and better low-bit storage. The LLM section, however, is genuinely preliminary (0.5–1.5B models, one epoch, 1M tokens, perplexity only) and its coded-attack results are mixed (LEAN worse in the first 20 flips, Qwen2-0.5B dead by 100 flips with the 1% code) — extrapolating to production-scale LLM protection would be premature.

## Strengths

- Attacks the root cause (the size of the sensitive tail) rather than the symptom, with a single interpretable knob λ whose effect is shown monotonically across a sweep, plus a floor analysis explaining why the tail saturates near 0.1% (remaining weights concentrate in the first conv/stage where outlier ratios are lowest and gradients cannot be lowered without changing function).
- Methodologically honest: real-flip verification against proxy gaming, term-wise ablation, a gradient-norm baseline that fails, and disclosure of the seed variance, data overlap, and the cases where the method does not help.
- Unified story across protection, parity, precision, and energy, with the same selective BCH machinery reused unchanged at the hardware level (only the protected-address list shrinks).
- Both deployment routes (ten-epoch fine-tune of a pretrained checkpoint, or last epochs of training) are characterized, including the non-obvious finding that the learning-rate schedule, not epoch count, determines how far the tail moves.

## Limitations

- Requires training data and access to fine-tuning (~1.5x plain fine-tune cost), plus a second quantization-aware fine-tune for the low-bit format — not applicable to frozen third-party models without data.
- The 500-flip headline holds in only two of three seeds; the universally reproducible guarantee is 300 flips. Most results are single runs, so sub-0.5-pt accuracy costs are within noise.
- The evaluation assumes a greedy PBS attacker and an idealized code (covered flips modelled as corrected under an interleaving assumption); no simulation of the decoder or of codeword-planning attackers.
- Targeted (class-to-class) attacks expose a real gap: robustness does not transfer from the mixed-data sensitivity LEAN optimizes to class-conditional sensitivity — zero improvement on ImageNet ResNets, and a max-damage tier over target classes closes the route for the baseline too, so it does not differentiate LEAN.
- The LLM extension is small-scale and mixed (a too-strong λ on Qwen2-1.5B buys nothing under attack, and both coded Qwen2-1.5B models die by 50 flips); energy numbers are analytical estimates, not measurements, and exclude tier lookup and decoder activity.
- λ is the only knob and moves the whole tail at once; the authors note unexplored shaping controls (tail fraction, per-bin targets, β, clipping quantile) that could act on MID and HIGH separately.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中"推理可靠性与 SDC 检测"核心方向：它直接处理位翻转错误（Rowhammer/PBS 攻击、低压 SRAM 错误）对存储权重的破坏，通过训练期重塑敏感度分布来降低纠错开销并提升容错能力；同时其敏感度分层 8/6/4/3-bit 混合精度格式与量化误差分析也与"极限性能优化下的可靠性（量化误差、低精度计算的精度与正确性问题）"高度相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a codeword-planning (non-greedy) adaptive attacker that budgeted flips across interleaved codewords invalidate the 300–500-flip survival guarantee, and can the interleaving assumption be tested against a simulated decoder?
- Can the tail term be reformulated with a class-conditional or per-input sensitivity ranking so that targeted (T-BFA-style) attacks on ImageNet ResNets and 1000-class classifiers fall within the protected set without inflating coverage?
- Does the distribution floor set by first-layer outlier ratios ρℓ predict which architectures (e.g., depthwise-separable CNNs, ViT patch embedders, LLM embedding heads) are fundamentally harder to reshape, and can architecture-level co-design lower it?
- How does the tail-shrinking vs. perplexity trade-off scale beyond 1.5B parameters and 1M tokens — does the per-weight penalty normalization (which made λ=3 on Qwen2-1.5B "too strong") admit a size-invariant scaling rule?
- Can LEAN be combined with Hessian-aware mixed-precision schemes (HAWQ-style) so that the tier assignment and the reshaping are optimized jointly instead of tiering being a packing-time afterthought?
- Is the improved post-training INT4 perplexity (e.g., 21.4 → 18.9 on Llama-3.2-1B) a general property of tail-suppressed weight distributions, and does it carry to stronger quantizers like GPTQ/AWQ that exploit outlier structure LEAN deliberately removes?
