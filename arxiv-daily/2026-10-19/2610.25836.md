> **一句话总结：** 该论文发现 GRPO/RLVR 训练中的失败高度局部化——少数特征 n 元组（如 "Let's recheck"、"the inverse of A"）能强烈预测 rollout 失败且整个 RL 过程中持续存在；作者用这些"失败 n 元组"从预训练语料（Olmo-mix/Dolmino-mix）中检索仅 3500 万 token 的文本（比常规 mid-training 小 3–4 个数量级）做定向继续预训练再重跑同样的 RL，在 11 个设置中的 10 个上超过同等预算的随机文本对照（OLMo2-1B 在 GSM8K 上插入 mid-training 前增益 +2.0 分，跨模型家族迁移给 Qwen3-0.6B 带来 +2.4 分）。

> **TL;DR:** The authors show RLVR failures concentrate on a small set of diagnostic n-grams mined from GRPO rollouts (low conditional accuracy a(g) ≈ 6–26%), then use those phrases as exact-match infini-gram queries to retrieve a 35M-token corpus (3–4 orders of magnitude smaller than typical mid-training) for continued pretraining before re-running the identical RL recipe. Against a matched control that CPTs on the same budget of randomly-queried text from the same corpus, the method wins 10 of 11 settings: +2.0 points on GSM8K for OLMo2-1B when inserted before mid-training (3 RL seeds), +2.4 on GSM8K for Qwen3-0.6B, and +1.9 on the multi-task mixture for Qwen3-4B; never-completed operations like "the inverse of A" jump from 0% to 50–62% success, while reflection phrases improve only 1–2 points. Gains are consistent but modest (0.1–2.6 points), mostly single-seed, and a curated math corpus (MegaMath-Web-Pro) matches the targeted corpus on the already-mid-trained model.

## 核心贡献

- **失败局部化**：证明 RL（GRPO）的失败并非弥散，而是集中在少数特征 n 元组上——OLMo2-1B 在 GSM8K 上的失败五元组出现在约 7% 的后期训练 rollout 中，这些 rollout 的成功率仅 24%，远低于其余 rollout 的 44%；n 元组内容随模型与任务变化（弱模型是 "there's a mistake"、"Let's recheck" 等反思短语，强模型是 "the inverse of A"、"use Coulomb's" 等具体运算）。
- **诊断机制**：将失败 n 元组解释为"低价值状态"（V^π(s) = 经过状态 s 后最终答对的概率）的可观测标记；由于 GRPO 的优势只在策略自己采样的 rollout 上估计，低价值状态处几乎没有成功延续可供学习，因此 RL 无法自我修复——整个训练过程中进入失败状态的比率稳定在 5–7%，不随整体准确率从 0.2% 升至 47% 而改变。
- **定向修复**：用失败 n 元组作为精确匹配查询（infini-gram 索引）从预训练/mid-training 语料中检索 2k token 窗口，组成仅 35M token 的语料做继续预训练（可在 mid-training 前或后插入），再以完全相同的超参重跑 RL；配套"内容中性查询"的对照（相同流水线、相同预算）分离了"选什么文本"与"继续预训练本身"的效应。
- **跨家族迁移与规模结论**：从 OLMo2-1B 失败中挖掘的语料能让 Qwen3-0.6B 在 GSM8K 上比对照 +2.4、Qwen3-4B 在混合任务上 +1.9；预算消融显示 35–100M token 即可，扩到 1B 反而使 Qwen3-4B 八基准均值下降 19 分。

## Key Contributions

- **Where RL fails.** Failures in RLVR training are localized: a small set of n-grams (n = 3–6, mined from πRL's rollouts, ranked by conditional accuracy a(g) = Pr[correct | rollout contains g]) strongly predicts rollout failure. For OLMo2-1B on GSM8K, failure five-grams appear in ~7% of late-RL rollouts, which succeed 24% of the time versus 44% for the rest. Their content shifts with model strength and task breadth — unproductive reflection phrases ("there's a mistake", "Let's recheck") for weak models on elementary math; specific missing operations ("convert the given matrix to reduced row echelon form", "use Coulomb's") for stronger Qwen3 models on general reasoning.
- **Why RL cannot fix them.** Failure n-grams mark low-value states (V^π(s) = probability a rollout through s eventually succeeds, a(g) ≈ V^π(s_g)). GRPO learns how to continue from a state only through rollouts it samples there, and few succeed from low-value states. Empirically, the entry rate into failure states stays flat at 5–7% across 210 RL steps while overall training accuracy climbs from 0.2% to 47%, and success from those states rises only in step with overall accuracy — RL does not single them out for repair.
- **A cheap, targeted fix.** The failure n-grams serve as exact-match infini-gram queries over the pretraining/mid-training corpora (Olmo-mix, Dolmino-mix); 2k-token windows around matches form a 35M-token corpus (3–4 orders of magnitude smaller than standard mid-training) used for continued pretraining before re-running the identical RL recipe. A matched control with content-neutral queries (e.g., "step 1", "1", "first") isolates the value of the failure-driven selection.
- **Consistent, transferring gains.** The targeted corpus beats the matched control in 10 of 11 settings, transfers across model families (OLMo2-1B-mined text improves Qwen3-0.6B by +2.4 on GSM8K), and inserting the repair before mid-training is the strongest configuration (+2.0 on GSM8K over 3 RL seeds, ~2 standard deviations with seed σ ≈ 1 point).

## Methodology

The pipeline has three stages. **(1) Mining failure n-grams:** GRPO is run first (G = 5 rollouts/prompt for GSM8K and the multi-task mixture, G = 8 with the General Reasoner recipe). N-grams (n = 3–6) from reasoning spans of πRL's training rollouts are kept if they occur ≥20 times and in ≥0.1% of rollouts, filtered of digit/formatting-dominated candidates, merged at Jaccard ≥ 0.9 into longest common spans, and ranked by conditional accuracy a(g); the lowest-accuracy 4–20 spans form the failure set G (listed verbatim in Appendix C). **(2) Diagnosis:** each n-gram is associational — it marks rather than causes a low-value state — but that suffices to localize failures. Two archetypes emerge: unproductive behaviors (the model reflects then spirals) and missing skills (the model stalls on an operation like row reduction). **(3) Repair:** each g ∈ G queries a local infini-gram index of the pretraining corpora; a 2k-token window around every match is extracted, queries with <1000 matching documents are dropped, and documents are sampled under a per-query balanced budget of 35M tokens. The base model is continued-pretrained on this text with the next-token objective, either from the OLMo2-1B stage-1 checkpoint (before mid-training, continuing the stage-1 schedule, followed by a shortened 0.84B-token stage 2) or on the released base model with a fresh optimizer; GRPO is then re-run with all hyperparameters fixed. Evaluation covers OLMo2-1B/7B and Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B across three RL settings: GSM8K; a multi-task mixture (GSM8K : Social IQA : MMLU : TriviaQA at 8:2:1:1, macro-averaged); and General Reasoner (WebInstruct-verified, mean over GSM8K, MATH-500, Minerva, OlympiadBench, AMC, AIME 2024/25, GPQA-Diamond). All Qwen3 retrieval uses the OLMo2 corpora since Qwen's are closed, which doubles as a corpus-transfer test.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main GSM8K result (OLMo2-1B, CPT inserted before mid-training, 3 RL seeds):** final greedy GSM8K accuracy 38.2 (ours) vs 36.2 (matched control) vs 37.4 (no CPT) — a +2.0 gain over the control (~2 standard deviations, seed σ ≈ 1 point), and the only configuration where the control actually falls below no CPT.
- **Cross-family transfer:** the 35M corpus mined from OLMo2-1B's GSM8K failures improves Qwen3-0.6B on GSM8K to 75.4 vs 73.0 for the matched control (+2.4) and 74.5 for no CPT; on the multi-task mixture it lifts Qwen3-4B to 74.0 vs 72.1 control (+1.9). In General Reasoner, the corpus mined from Qwen3-4B lifts Qwen3-1.7B's eight-benchmark mean to 34.3 vs 31.7 no CPT (+2.6).
- **Missing operations repair dramatically:** Qwen3-4B operations that had 0% success late in RL — "the inverse of A" and "Then, the derivative of" — reach 49.9% and 62.1% after targeted CPT ("convert the given matrix to": 0% → 4.3%; "use Coulomb's": 0% → 22.6%), whereas reflection phrases move only 1–2 points ("there's a mistake": 6.2% → 8.1%; "Let's recheck": 5.6% → 6.3%) even though their usage count triples (591 → 2166).
- **Budget ablation:** gains appear at 35–100M tokens and reverse at 1B — Qwen3-4B's General Reasoner mean goes 37.5 (no CPT) → 40.5 (35M) → 35.2 (100M) → 18.8 (1B), i.e., 1B tokens of retrieved text costs 19 points.
- **pass@k evidence that CPT (slightly) raises the ceiling RL cannot:** on MATH-500 (Qwen3-1.7B, General Reasoner), pass@32 after RL alone is 90.6 vs 91.2 for the base model (reproducing the "RL does not expand coverage" finding), while the repaired model reaches 92.2 — the only RL-trained model above base; on TriviaQA (Qwen3-4B) the repaired model's lead over the control grows from 1.6 points at k=1 to 4.4 at k=32.
- **Scope of the headline "10 of 11":** the misses are real — on OLMo2-1B released-base multi-task, ours scores 51.5 vs 52.6 no CPT (−1.1), and on Qwen3-1.7B multi-task the control wins (63.4 vs 62.5); on released-base GSM8K the +0.6 edge over control (60.6 vs 60.0) is within the ±0.7–1.5 point seed noise the authors themselves report.

## Authors & Credibility

Xinyi Wang and Danqi Chen are at Princeton Language and Intelligence (Princeton University); Danqi Chen is a senior Princeton NLP faculty member with a long track record in LLM training and analysis. The work is carefully grounded in the current RLVR debate (Yue et al. 2025 on pass@k, Wang et al. 2025b on high-entropy tokens, Ko et al. 2026 on cliff tokens, OctoThinker on mid-training). Code is released at github.com/WANGXinyiLinda/where-rl-breaks, and the use of fully public artifacts (OLMo2 checkpoints and Olmo-mix/Dolmino-mix corpora, infini-gram indexing) makes the pipeline plausibly reproducible. The authors include an unusually candid run-to-run appendix (Appendix D) that quantifies seed noise and states which comparisons they treat as null — a strong credibility signal.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-motivated — the signal (a model's own failure markers selecting pretraining data) differs from task-similarity or influence-based data selection, and from Ko et al.'s cliff tokens (which reshape the RL objective rather than the data). But the ingredients (n-gram mining, infini-gram exact-match retrieval, CPT, GRPO) are all deliberately simple; the contribution is the connection, not any new mechanism.
- **Rigor:** Mixed. The matched-control design and post-hoc mining protocol (statistics measured on RL steps after those the n-grams were mined from, avoiding selection bias) are careful, and 3-seed averaging exists only for OLMo2-1B GSM8K. However, most of the 11 settings are single-seed with seed σ ≈ 1 point, so the +0.5 to +0.8 margins in several "wins" are within the authors' own null threshold; the General Reasoner runs are compared at matched RL steps rather than convergence because self-mined runs hit compute limits.
- **The diagnosis-to-outcome link is looser than the framing suggests:** the paper's own Section 4.4 concedes the matched control raises the success rate from failure states by a similar amount, and per-phrase success for reflection markers stays at 5–12% with or without repair — what targeted CPT changes most is how often the model *enters* those states (entry rate doubles, 5.7% → 10.6%). So the accuracy gain "cannot be attributed to the diagnosed states alone," an honest but significant caveat.
- **Baseline strength:** the matched control (content-neutral queries like "step 1") is the right scientific control but is weak as a practical baseline; the limitations section admits 35M tokens of the curated MegaMath-Web-Pro corpus matched the targeted corpus on released-base GSM8K, so the method's practical edge over "just CPT on good math data" is confined to pre-mid-training insertion and deficits without an obvious curated corpus.
- **Scope constraints:** retrieval requires the base model's own (public) corpora — only OLMo2 qualifies; Qwen3 runs retrieve from OLMo2 corpora, conflating corpus transfer with the repair. Exact-match retrieval misses paraphrases, and on mixtures the lowest-accuracy n-grams can be formatting artifacts currently "removed by hand." The n-gram→failure relationship is associational, not causal.
- **Red flags:** none serious — no cherry-picked evals detected, misses are disclosed (Table 8), and negative 1B-token results are reported. The abstract's "10 out of 11 settings" headline is accurate but masks that several of the wins are noise-level.

## Strengths

- A crisp, actionable diagnosis: failures are predictable from a handful of surface n-grams, and the failure set is interpretable (reflection spirals for weak models, specific operations for strong ones) rather than a black-box score.
- Extremely data-efficient repair — 35M tokens, 3–4 orders of magnitude smaller than mid-training — with a clean matched-control design that separates data selection from the generic effect of CPT.
- Honest, thorough analysis: state-level statistics (entry rate, success-from-state, sibling-correct rate 46% → 50%), pass@k decompositions, insertion-point and budget ablations, verbatim query listings, and a quantified seed-noise appendix.
- Cross-model-family transfer of a mined corpus (OLMo2-1B failures helping Qwen3-0.6B/4B) suggests the mined text encodes general skills, not model quirks.

## Limitations

- Effect sizes are modest (0.1–2.6 points) and mostly single-seed; several claimed wins sit within the ~1-point noise band, and no single comparison exceeds two standard deviations.
- The repair works far better for missing operations (0% → 50–62%) than for unproductive reflection behaviors (1–2 points) — the latter is the more common archetype for weak models, which limits generality.
- Requires access to the pretraining corpus (effectively OLMo2-only); applying to closed-corpus models needs unstudied substitutes.
- Only the "RL almost works" regime is studied; behavior when the base model has a genuine coverage gap, or over repeated diagnose–repair–RL cycles, is left open.
- Curated in-domain data (MegaMath-Web-Pro) is competitive on already-mid-trained models, so the practical advantage narrows to pre-mid-training insertion and label-free settings.

## Relevance

该论文直接命中"RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures"方向：它系统诊断了 RLVR/GRPO 训练中持续存在的失败模式（低价值状态、进入率不随训练下降、pass@k 上限不扩张），并给出可复现的检测（失败 n 元组挖掘）与缓解（定向继续预训练）方案，属于该方向的核心研究问题。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would semantic (embedding-based) retrieval seeded by failure n-grams, or filtering retrieved windows for completed resolutions, fix the "trigger present but no recovery" weakness the authors identify?
- The entry rate *doubles* after targeted CPT while per-phrase success barely moves for reflection phrases — does the gain come from the diagnosed states at all, or from generic in-distribution regularization that a better control (e.g., MegaMath-Web-Pro at every insertion point) would erase?
- Why exactly does scaling the retrieved corpus to 1B tokens hurt by 19 points when replay of the pretraining distribution does not — is it repetition of narrow text, distribution shift, or forgetting?
- Does iterating the diagnose → repair → RL loop compound, or do successive failure sets collapse onto the same unrepairable reflection behaviors?
- Can the method be extended to closed-corpus models via synthetic traversals, and does the failure-n-gram signal remain diagnostic under PPO or offline RL instead of GRPO?
