> **一句话总结：** 提出OnQ低比特恢复流水线：先用分块校准初始化量化学生模型，再在其自采样轨迹上做在线蒸馏，并按每个候选词的师生概率比对前向/反向KL加权（Dynamic KL）；在Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B的W2/W3下于全部6个设置取得9基准最高平均分，2-bit下Qwen3-8B平均分比最强基线高10.26分，且生成更短、重复错误更少。

> **TL;DR:** Fixed-response QAT (EfficientQAT, BitDistiller, ReasoningQAT) supervises a 2-bit student on teacher-written prefixes, so it collapses on long-horizon reasoning (e.g., Qwen3-8B W2 AIME 24-26: 22.59% for BitDistiller vs 71.85% full-precision). OnQ instead runs on-policy distillation from the full-precision teacher on the quantized student's own rollouts, with candidate-wise Dynamic KL balancing forward/reverse KL per token. Across nine benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME 24-26, HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, MMLU-Redux, GPQA-Diamond, IFEval) it takes the best average in all six Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B W2/W3 settings — at 2 bits beating every baseline on every benchmark, lifting the Qwen3-8B average from 60.42 (BitDistiller) to 70.68 (+10.26) — while producing shorter responses, fewer truncations (avg 4.39% vs 11.56% for ReasoningQAT on 8B W2), and almost no repetitive errors (2.4% of MATH-500 questions vs 11.4-54.0% for fixed-response QAT).

## 核心贡献

- **诊断出固定响应QAT在低比特下失效的两大机理：**（1）**轨迹发散**——量化学生推理时依据自身前缀生成，响应越长偏离教师参考前缀越远，固定序列监督覆盖不到；（2）**分布失配**——校准后的学生在同一前缀上既低估又高估不同候选词，且在高熵前缀上两类错误同时更频繁（Qwen3-1.7B W2诊断）。
- **提出OnQ恢复流水线：** 从分块校准（EfficientQAT的Block-AP式MSE重建）出发，量化学生自采样响应、冻结的全精度教师在相同前缀上提供逐词分布监督（在线蒸馏），解决轨迹发散。
- **引入候选级Dynamic KL目标：** 按每个候选词的师生概率比 α=p/(p+q) 加权前向与反向KL——严重低估（p/q≥20）时前向KL能纠正99.7%的候选而反向KL只有70.9%，严重高估时反之（RKL 86.3% vs FKL 75.1%）；Dynamic KL在两侧都接近最优（95.1% / 84.7%）。
- **响应长度调度：** 校准初期的学生在长响应尾部大量复读（前4K位置复读率6.7%，8K之后升至21.1%，训练100步后全位置约1%），故前50步 rollout 上限4,096 token、51-100步8,192、之后16,384，避免把监督预算浪费在复读文本上。
- **全面实证：** 6个设置（1.7B/4B/8B × W2/W3）× 9基准 × 3个评估种子，48/54个"基准×设置"组合取得最高分；2-bit下所有基准、所有尺寸全面第一，且2-bit Qwen3-4B（64.95）平均分超过2-bit尺寸翻倍的Qwen3-8B上的一切基线（最高60.42）。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies two failure mechanisms of fixed-response QAT at low bits:** (1) *trajectory divergence* — the quantized student conditions on its own outputs during inference, drifting ever further from the teacher-written training prefixes as responses lengthen; (2) *distributional misalignment* — the calibrated student underpredicts some next-token candidates and overpredicts others at the same prefix, with both error types co-occurring more often at high-entropy prefixes.
- **OnQ pipeline:** block-wise calibration (EfficientQAT Block-AP style, per-block MSE reconstruction on 4,096×2,048-token windows) initializes the student; on-policy distillation then samples student responses through the quantized forward pass while the frozen full-precision teacher supervises next-token distributions on those same prefixes, directly training on trajectories the student actually visits.
- **Candidate-wise Dynamic KL:** mixes forward and reverse KL per candidate with weight α = sg[p/(p+q)] — more FKL where the student underpredicts (teacher higher), more RKL where it overpredicts — implemented over the union of teacher/student top-20 tokens plus a residual category.
- **Response-length schedule:** rollout caps of 4,096 tokens (steps 1-50), 8,192 (51-100), 16,384 (afterwards), because the freshly calibrated 2-bit student degenerates into repetition late in long responses (repeated-token share 6.7% within the first 4K positions but 21.1% beyond 8K, fading to ~1% by step 100).
- **Comprehensive evidence:** highest nine-benchmark average in all six model/bit settings and best score on 48 of 54 benchmark-setting pairs, with ablations cleanly attributing gains to on-policy prefixes (+8.40 MATH-500 over token-matched fixed-response DKL) and to Dynamic KL over single-direction objectives.

## Methodology

All linear layers use uniform asymmetric weight quantization (group size 128, BF16 activations) with a straight-through estimator; training updates latent weights and scales jointly. Stage 1 is block-wise calibration: starting from clipped round-to-nearest ranges, each transformer block minimizes MSE between its quantized output and the full-precision block's output, restoring a workable policy (2-bit Qwen3-1.7B average: 3.91 RTN → 23.35 after calibration). Stage 2 is on-policy distillation: each step samples one response for each of 128 prompts at temperature 1.0 from the student, holds the rollout and teacher targets fixed, and minimizes Dynamic KL averaged over all response tokens, updating latent weights and scales; 25,600 multi-domain prompts (math, code, science, instruction following from OpenMathReasoning, OpenCodeReasoning-2, OpenScienceReasoning-2, Tülu 3, IF-multi-constraints). AdamW, constant LR 1e-6, 400 steps for 1.7B and 300 for 4B/8B, implemented with verl on H100/H200 GPUs with the teacher served by vLLM. The Dynamic KL diagnostic shows FKL and RKL are complementary correctors: one small gradient step moves 99.7% (FKL) vs 70.9% (RKL) of severely underpredicted candidates toward the teacher, and 86.3% (RKL) vs 75.1% (FKL) of severely overpredicted ones; Dynamic KL tracks the better direction on both sides (95.1% / 84.7%).

## Results & Highlights

- **Nine-benchmark average, Qwen3-8B W2 (weight-only 2-bit):** OnQ 70.68 vs strongest baseline BitDistiller 60.42 (**+10.26 points**) and ReasoningQAT 60.24; full precision is 81.82. At 2 bits OnQ wins *every* benchmark at *every* model size.
- **Long-horizon reasoning is where the margin concentrates:** AIME 24-26 at 2 bits — Qwen3-8B 44.07 vs BitDistiller 22.59 (**+21.48**; full precision 71.85); Qwen3-4B 34.81 vs ReasoningQAT 18.89 (+15.92). LiveCodeBench 4B W2: 46.64 vs ReasoningQAT 28.34 (**+18.30**) while mean response length shortens by more than a fifth. At 3 bits, Qwen3-1.7B AIME rises from 23.33 (ReasoningQAT) to 32.59 (+9.26) with 6% shorter responses.
- **A 4B that beats a 2×-larger 8B:** OnQ Qwen3-4B W2 average (64.95) exceeds every 2-bit baseline running Qwen3-8B (best: BitDistiller 60.42) — i.e., 2-bit 4B with OnQ outperforms 2-bit 8B under all fixed-response QAT recipes.
- **Close to full precision at 3 bits:** OnQ comes within four points of the full-precision average on Qwen3-4B (76.29 vs 79.93) and 8B (79.19 vs 81.82).
- **Generation quality, not just accuracy:** lowest average truncation rate among QAT methods in all six settings (8B W2: 4.39% vs ReasoningQAT 11.56%, BitDistiller 16.51%, EfficientQAT 26.57%); repetitive errors on MATH-500 (1.7B W2) drop to 2.4% of questions versus 11.4-54.0% for fixed-response QAT baselines, and OnQ produces zero repetitive errors on AIME 24-26 (4B W3), where baselines show 6.7-11.1% (LLM-labeled with two passes, agreement 93.6-95.9%, Cohen's κ = 0.87-0.88).
- **Ablations (Qwen3-1.7B W2, token-matched):** MATH-500 69.40 for on-policy Dynamic KL vs 61.00 fixed-response Dynamic KL (**+8.40**) and 59.00 fixed-response FKL; swapping FKL→DKL inside fixed-response training adds up to 2.44. Against other on-policy objectives (DeepScaleR math prompts, shared cold start): OnQ MATH-500 67.20 vs GKD-FKL 66.20, Vanilla-OPD-RKL 63.00, OPD+GRPO 66.00; GRPO alone collapses to 31.40 (MMLU-Redux 49.58 for OnQ vs 34.05).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate, honestly framed.** Both core ingredients are adopted, not invented: on-policy distillation follows GKD/Agarwal et al. and Dynamic KL follows Jung et al. (2025) — the paper says so explicitly. The contribution is the diagnosis (two-sided prediction errors at high-entropy prefixes, trajectory divergence in quantized reasoners) plus the recipe and its thorough validation. That is legitimate systems-style work, but readers expecting a new objective will find none.
- **Rigor: high.** Multi-seed evaluation with SDs, 54 benchmark-setting pairs, token-matched fixed-vs-on-policy ablations, shared initialization across baselines, and a stage-decomposition table (RTN → calibration → OPD) that closes roughly half the remaining gap to full precision in every setting.
- **Reproducibility: good but compute-opaque.** Hyperparameters, data mix, schedules, and software (verl, vLLM, EvalScope) are all specified; however, no code release is mentioned in the preprint, and — more importantly — no wall-clock or GPU-hour accounting is given. On-policy rollouts with an online full-precision teacher are plausibly much more expensive than fixed-response QAT; the paper never quantifies this cost, which matters for adoption.
- **Scope limits that temper the headline.** Only Qwen3 (1.7B-8B) is tested — one architecture family; only weight-only W2/W3 with BF16 activations (no W4, no weight+activation quantization, where activation error usually dominates); margins at 3 bits are much thinner than at 2 bits (e.g., 1.7B: +3.39 average over ReasoningQAT), consistent with less room rather than method superiority. Six of 54 benchmark pairs still go to baselines.
- **Weak spots to note:** (1) baseline training sequences are cut at 8,192 tokens, which handicaps fixed-response methods precisely on the long-horizon benchmarks where OnQ's margin is largest — partly inherent to those recipes, but it inflates the gap; (2) the 4K/8K/16K response-length schedule is a hand-tuned heuristic with no ablation against a fixed cap; (3) AIME 24-26 has only 90 problems and per-seed SDs up to ~4.5 points, so some AIME deltas are within noise; (4) the gap to full precision at 2 bits remains large (8B AIME 44.07 vs 71.85), so "recovery" is partial. No red flags of overclaiming — the abstract's claims match the tables.

## Strengths

- Correctly identifies *why* fixed-response QAT fails on long-horizon reasoning (prefix mismatch that compounds over thousands of self-generated tokens) rather than only showing that it fails.
- The Dynamic KL motivation is backed by a clean micro-diagnostic (single gradient-step correction rates on under-/overpredicted candidates) instead of hand-waving.
- Gains are largest exactly where deployment pressure is highest: 2-bit weight quantization, where a 4B model under OnQ overtakes a 2×-larger 8B under all baselines.
- Beyond accuracy, it improves the generation pathologies that make quantized reasoners unusable — truncation and repetitive looping — and measures them with a careful, kappa-reported labeling protocol.
- Strong ablation discipline: token-matched fixed-vs-on-policy comparison isolates the prefix source (+8.40) from the objective (+1.0-4.2), and GRPO-alone collapse (31.40 MATH-500) shows why distillation, not RL, is the right tool from a 2-bit cold start.

## Limitations

- Single model family (Qwen3) and weight-only quantization; no results for W4, mixed precision, or activation quantization, and none for other architectures (Llama, DeepSeek, Mistral).
- No compute/cost reporting: on-policy distillation requires continuous student rollouts plus a concurrently served full-precision teacher (vLLM on H100/H200 clusters), a serving burden fixed-response QAT avoids; relative training cost is never measured.
- The response-length schedule is heuristic (chosen from the repetition diagnostic for 1.7B W2 and applied to all sizes/bit widths) and un-ablated.
- AIME 24-26's 90 problems make several AIME comparisons noisy; the paper reports seeds/SDs but headlines single means.
- The 2-bit recovery remains far from full precision on the hardest benchmarks (8B AIME: 44.07 vs 71.85; GPQA-Diamond 43.43 vs 59.93), so OnQ narrows but does not close the low-bit reasoning gap.
- Repetition-error classification relies on an LLM judge (blinded, dual-pass, author-adjudicated — but still automated labeling of a behavioral claim).

## Relevance

直接命中"极端性能优化下的可靠性"方向：本文系统研究2/3-bit权重量化对推理(长程CoT)能力与数值正确性的破坏及其恢复，属于量化误差与低精度计算正确性问题的核心案例。其"在学生自身(有噪)轨迹上蒸馏"的思路对低精度/容错训练与推理系统的可靠性设计也有借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of OnQ's advantage survives weight+activation quantization (e.g., W2/A8 or W4/A4), where activation outliers rather than weight noise dominate the error?
- What is the true cost ratio (GPU-hours, teacher-serving overhead) of on-policy distillation versus fixed-response QAT, and does the 10-point 2-bit gain survive a compute-matched comparison?
- Would the response-length schedule generalize, or is it an artifact of Qwen3's calibrated repetition profile — does a fixed 16,384 cap from step 1 (or entropy-based early stopping of rollouts) match it?
- Does training on self-generated trajectories transfer to other degenerate numeric regimes (e.g., FP8/FP4 post-training, bit-flip-corrupted models), where two-sided prediction errors also arise at high-entropy prefixes?
- Can the residual gap at 2 bits (AIME 44.07 vs 71.85 on 8B) be closed by combining OnQ with GRPO-style RLVR after recovery, given that RLVR alone from the cold start collapses (31.40 MATH-500)?
- Does the Dynamic KL weighting insight (per-candidate ratio balancing) also help *full-precision* on-policy distillation of reasoning models, or is it specific to the quantization-induced error structure?
