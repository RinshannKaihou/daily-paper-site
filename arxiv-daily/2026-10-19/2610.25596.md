> **一句话总结：** 通过因果"马赛克实验"（拼接/替换模型内部激活与键值缓存）证明，超过百亿参数的开源 LLM 在关系推理时会自发形成与"认知地图"同构的内部组织——抽象关系结构（图/边表示）与实体身份线性可分、可加性组合，由仅占约 1% 的稀疏键值对完成绑定，且可被定向植入"单向假记忆"。

> **TL;DR:** Princeton researchers use causal "mosaic experiments" (splicing activation tensors and key-value memories of Gemma-3, Llama-3.1, and Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 70B) on 14 relational-reasoning tasks to show that LLMs beyond ~10B parameters build an internal cognitive-map-like organization: a name-independent graph of observed relations that composes retrospectively at query time, plus name-dependent identity bound additively via a sparse KV subset (score collapse only when ~200 of ~9,900 KV pairs, ~1–2.5%, retain name information — e.g., 112 name-to-role + 131 role-to-name candidates in Gemma-3-27B family trees). Editing just 35 role-to-name values implants a unidirectional false memory (model says "Beth is Alice's daughter" yet "Beth's mother is Daisy"). The headline graph-based account is mostly confirmed but their own SI shows exceptions where a location-based (prospective composition) component also operates — the abstract's clean dichotomy is somewhat stronger than the data.

## 核心贡献

- **行为层面：涌现的关系推断能力。** 在 14 个关系推理设置（家族树、序关系、带符号交互、等价关系四大家族）上，无微调、无上下文示例，超过约 100 亿参数的模型（Gemma-3、Llama-3.1、Qwen3）能可靠地推断出未明说的关系（如由"A 之女是 B、B 之女是 C"推出"A 的孙女是 C"），且把实体名换成无语义的随机三字母合成名后性能不降——表现出系统性（systematicity）泛化。
- **因果证据：结构表征与实体身份线性可分、可加性组合。** 马赛克实验（把供体 prompt 中实体 token 的激活行拼进控制 prompt）表明模型行为如同实体名被重新绑定到完整关系结构中的"结构角色"上（激活近似分解为 a(n,s) ≈ φ_name(n) + φ_role(s)，形成平行四边形几何）；用"角色交换向量"δ 加减两行即可让两个实体互换角色。
- **表征形式：以图（边）为主、查询时回溯组合。** 多步推理主要依赖把已观察的关系存成成对"边"、在被查询时回溯拼接，而非像空间导航路径积分那样前瞻地把每个实体放进全局坐标系（位置编码置换对照实验排除了架构位置的混淆）。
- **机制定位：稀疏分布的键值对完成双向绑定。** keys-first / values-first 马赛克证明检索先"名字→角色"后"角色→名字"；梯度引导的迭代剪枝把承载名字信息的键值收缩到约 1%（Gemma-3-27B 家族树中为 112 个 name-to-role + 131 个 role-to-name 候选对，其余 9,677 对可平均掉），跨层分布、集中在实体名 token 及其后继 token。
- **可编辑性：植入"单向假记忆"。** 仅替换与某一实体相关的候选 role-to-name 对的值（35 对），模型仍答"Alice 的女儿是 Beth"却改口"Beth 的母亲是 Daisy"——产生无法用任何逻辑一致的家谱解释的信念，且只影响单一查询方向。

## Key Contributions

- **Behavioral emergence:** Across 14 relational-reasoning settings spanning 4 task families (family trees; ordering relations such as age/height/speed comparisons, company hierarchies, east–west/north–south/ahead–behind, exact age differences; signed interactions such as gene regulatory networks and neural circuits; equivalence relations), open-weight models evaluated zero-shot (task instructions only, no demos or fine-tuning) reliably infer unstated relationships once beyond roughly the ten-billion-parameter scale, and retain this ability under synthetic three-letter entity names — immediate generalization to novel instantiations of familiar relational structures (Fodor & Pylyshyn systematicity).
- **Causal separation of structure from identity:** "Mosaic experiments" (in the lineage of Feng & Steinhardt's binding work) recombine rows of the activation tensor A across prompts sharing a structure but differing in names. Splicing a donor entity row into a control context makes the model behave as if the donor name took over the control entity's structural role (e.g., recovers "Eve's mother is Alice", "Eve's daughter is Carol", and the two-hop "Alice's granddaughter is Carol"), rejecting the attribute-tagging hypothesis in every tested setting and model.
- **Additive binding with parallelogram geometry:** Entity rows decompose approximately as a(n, s) ≈ φ_name(n) + φ_role(s); a role-swap vector δ estimated from two donor tensors (same name in different roles), when added/subtracted across two control rows, causally exchanges the two entities' roles — the classic analogical-reasoning geometry, here verified causally.
- **Graph-based (retrospective) composition rather than path-integration:** When the rows of the final entity (whose upstream first relationship differs between control and donor) are replaced, two-hop responses follow the control prompt — observed relations are stored as pairwise edges and composed at query time. The authors honestly report exceptions (family-tree setting; Llama-3.1-70B) where responses shift toward the donor, indicating a coexisting location-based/prospective component visualized in trajectory PCA.
- **Sparse, distributed KV binding substrate and false-memory implantation:** Keys-first vs values-first key–value mosaics (layer split at ⌊N/2⌋; ⌊2N/3⌋ for Qwen) establish name-to-role-then-role-to-name retrieval order; gradient-guided iterative pruning localizes name-dependent information to a tiny subset; and editing ~35 candidate role-to-name values implants a logically inconsistent, unidirectional "false memory".

## Methodology

The authors formalize the segments of the transformer that carry prompt information: the activation tensor A = {h_t^l} over the token span of the relationship statements, and the key–value memory M = {(k_t^l, v_t^i)} derived from A by learned linear projections. All influence of the stated relationships on the response flows through A and M, so questions about the "cognitive map" become questions about how information is organized in these objects.

**Mosaic experiments** are the core causal tool: source tensors are recorded (PyTorch forward pre-hooks capturing decoder-block inputs over the relationship span) while a model processes two or more prompts; selected rows of A (or selected keys/values of M) from a donor are substituted into a control tensor; the model then processes the query with those positions frozen at mosaic values, and answer-token probabilities are read out. Competing hypotheses (attribute tagging vs identity–structure binding; retrospective graph vs prospective locations) make opposite, pre-registered-style predictions about the model's answers, so each experiment cleanly adjudicates between them.

**Setup details:** 11 open-weight instruct models — Gemma-3 4B/12B/27B-It, Llama-3.1 8B/70B-Instruct, Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B/14B/32B (recorded HF revisions; bfloat16; Qwen3 thinking mode disabled). Each prompt uses single-token entity names and space-padded relationship slots so token positions align across instances. 50 randomly sampled entity assignments per condition; responses measured as full-vocabulary-normalized probabilities of single-token answers, plotted as probability ratios relative to the geometric mean across candidate answer tokens. Position-code confounds are ruled out by permuting positional signals (Figs. S1–S2). For KV localization, an iterative pruning procedure (Algorithm 1) treats binary keep-masks as continuous interpolation weights between control and name-averaged memories, scores performance via the correct-answer logit margin, and uses one backward pass per iteration to greedily erase the least-impactful keys/values, first coarsely then one at a time.

## Results & Highlights

- **Scale threshold for relational inference:** On all 2-step family-tree inferences (Fig. 1C), accuracy rises with parameter count across the 11 models from ~600M to ~70B; models beyond the ~10B scale (e.g., Gemma-3 12B/27B, Llama-3.1 70B, Qwen3 14B/32B) answer unstated-relationship queries reliably, while sub-10B models (Qwen3 0.6B–8B, Llama-3.1-8B) do not — and accuracy is preserved with synthetic single-token names (Fig. 1D), 50 trials per condition.
- **Identity–structure binding beats attribute tagging by orders of magnitude in probability ratio:** For Gemma-3-27B family trees, after splicing the donor's "Eve" row into the control context, the model assigns probability ratio ~10^10 (near-total probability) to the binding-consistent answers ("Eve's mother is Alice", "Eve's daughter is Carol", "Alice's granddaughter is Carol") and ~10^-10 to the attribute-tagging prediction ("Eve's mother is Daisy"); under who-is queries the model responds "Alice" with almost no additional probability on "Daisy" (Fig. S6A). The same qualitative pattern holds across all 14 settings (Figs. S22–S35) and all 11 models tested (Figs. S78–S88).
- **Additive role/name composition verified causally:** Adding/subtracting the estimated role-swap vector δ to two entity rows makes the model behave as if the two entities exchanged structural roles (e.g., "Beth's daughter is Alice" and "Beth's mother is Carol"), consistent across settings (Figs. S36–S49) and models (Figs. S89–S99).
- **Retrospective composition dominates but is not exclusive:** Replacing final-entity rows with donor rows (donor differing in the first relationship) yields two-hop responses matching the control prompt — the graph hypothesis — across tasks (Figs. S50–S63) and models (Figs. S100–S110); however, in specific family-tree conditions responses shift toward, and sometimes closely match, the donor-implied (location-based) answer (Figs. S3, S104D), with PCA trajectories showing history-dependence at relation tokens (Fig. S4).
- **KV binding is sparse (~1%) and directional:** The pruning score begins to decline significantly only when name information remains in roughly 200 keys or values — approximately 1% of the total keys/values. For Gemma-3-27B family-tree who-is queries, the surviving sparse mask retains just 112 candidate name-to-role pairs plus 131 candidate role-to-name pairs (out of 9,920 total KV entries; the other 9,677 carry no needed name information), distributed across layers and concentrated at entity-name tokens and the tokens immediately following them. Under yes-or-no queries a keys-only mosaic suffices, retaining name information in 143 keys and just 16 values (Fig. S7).
- **Unidirectional false memories:** Replacing the values of the ~35 candidate role-to-name pairs associated with "Alice" with donor values leaves "Who is Alice's daughter?" answered "Beth" (control-like) while "Who is Beth's mother?" flips from "Alice" to "Daisy" — a belief state irreconcilable with any single consistent family tree; editing name-to-role keys instead flips yes-or-no answers in both directions (Fig. 5G–H, S7F–G).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** High. Attribute-style fact editing (ROME/MEMIT lineage, Geva et al.) showed facts can be localized at entity tokens; this paper argues against attribute tagging for in-context relationships and instead establishes an additive structure-plus-identity code with a directional KV retrieval scheme — a meaningfully new mechanistic account of in-context relational binding, and the unidirectional-false-memory construction is a genuinely clever demonstration.

**Rigor:** Strong on causal inference — every claim is adjudicated by an intervention with competing predictions, with position-code controls (S1–S2), query-format replication (S7, who-is vs yes-or-no with the predicted strategy shift), and cross-model/cross-task sweeps. The gradient-guided KV pruning is a careful sufficiency (not necessity) analysis, and the authors say so explicitly.

**Reproducibility:** Good. Code and data released; recorded model revisions; stdlib-free but standard PyTorch/HF stack; prompts and mosaic constructions fully specified in the SI (Sections III, VIII). The 85-page SI is unusually thorough.

**Weaknesses / red flags (paper-specific):**
1. *Task scale is minimal:* two-edge graphs with single-token, position-aligned entity names (space-padded slots) — subword/multi-token names, longer chains, and real relational corpora (e.g., CLUTRR-style family puzzles, knowledge-graph QA) are untested; the design fundamentally requires token-position alignment, so the boundary of the claimed organization is unknown.
2. *The abstract oversells the graph-vs-map dichotomy:* it states models store relations "rather than prospectively positioning each entity in a global coordinate system," yet the authors' own experiments (Fig. S3; S104D; trajectory PCA in S4) show donor-following, location-based responses in family-tree conditions, and they admit "we do not yet understand what factors cause the model to rely on one type of information or the other."
3. *Sparse-pair counts are model- and setting-specific:* the 112/131-pair and ~1% numbers are for Gemma-3-27B on family trees; other models/settings (S64–S121) are shown qualitatively but the quantitative generality is not aggregated.
4. *Instruct-tuned models only* (gemma-3-*-it, Llama-3.1-*-Instruct; Qwen3 with thinking disabled): instruction-tuning effects on the observed organization cannot be separated from pretraining, and frontier/closed models are out of scope.
5. *The ~10B emergence claim is correlational* — no controlled scale comparison (same data/recipe at different sizes), consistent with but not evidence for a mechanistic phase transition.

**Significance:** If the additive structure/identity code and directional KV binding generalize beyond toy prompts, this is a foundational result for interpretability — it connects emergent LLM representations to hippocampal cognitive-map theory (Tolman 1948; Behrens/Whittington) and gives an editable handle on in-context relational belief states.

## Strengths

- Causal (intervention-based) methodology rather than correlational probing, with genuinely competing pre-stated predictions for every experiment.
- Exceptional breadth of replication: 11 models across 3 families and 0.6B–70B scales, 14 tasks across 4 relational families, two query formats.
- The gradient-guided iterative KV pruning with rewinding is an elegant, reusable technique for localizing sufficient information-carrying sites in attention memories.
- Honest reporting of anomalous results (prospective-composition exceptions) and of the sufficiency-not-necessity caveat for the identified KV pairs.
- Code, data, recorded revisions, and full mosaic-construction specs released.

## Limitations

- Findings are established only on 2-edge relational prompts with single-token names and aligned token positions; naturalistic multi-hop contexts, multi-token entities, and real-world relational reasoning are untested.
- The cognitive-map analogy is loose: the core representation is a bound graph with retrospective composition, not a metric map, and the prospective/location component observed in exceptions is left mechanistically unexplained.
- Quantitative sparsity numbers (112/131 pairs, ~1%, 143/16 keys/values, 35 edited pairs) come from one model-task combination; per-model quantitative summaries across the full sweep are not tabulated.
- Only open-weight instruct models up to 70B; base (non-instruct) checkpoints and frontier models are not analyzed, and the scale threshold is observational.
- No comparison against established knowledge-editing or probing baselines to situate the KV-binding account quantitatively against prior mechanistic accounts.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：本文是对 LLM 内部激活张量与键值缓存的因果干预研究（机制可解释性、表征几何中的加性分解/平行四边形结构、稀疏键值定位），其马赛克实验与梯度引导剪枝方法对任何做 hidden-state 分析/电路定位的工作都有直接方法学参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the additive a(n,s) ≈ φ_name(n) + φ_role(s) decomposition survive multi-token entity names and longer (3+ edge) relation chains, where token-position alignment breaks?
- What decides, at query time, between the graph-based (retrospective) and location-based (prospective) information the authors show coexist in A — can the choice be predicted or causally forced?
- Do base (non-instruct) checkpoints at matched scale show the same structure/identity separation, isolating what instruction tuning contributes?
- Can the sparse name-to-role/role-to-name KV pairs be found without behavioral pruning — e.g., via SAE features or attention-head attribution — and do they coincide with known induction/name-mover heads?
- Does chain-of-thought generation restructure the cognitive map mid-inference, as the authors speculate, and can the unidirectional false-memory edit be extended into a scalable, faithful model-editing technique for relational knowledge?
