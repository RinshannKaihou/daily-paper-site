> **一句话总结：** AMD 的 ALTO 开源训练方案在 GPT-OSS-20B（MoE，MLPerf Small MoE 基准）上用 1D/2D 混合分块量化 + 随机 Hadamard 变换 + 随机舍入 + 权重去振荡（简化自 TetraJet-v2）把 MXFP4 预训练与 BF16 的验证损失差距从 +0.014 缩到 +0.007，达到 3.34 质量目标的步数开销从 +20.0% 降到 +5.0%，并诚实地报告了 DGE、裁剪、宏块缩放等算子级有效但端到端失败的方法。

> **TL;DR:** An AMD team pretrains the GPT-OSS-20B MoE model from scratch in (simulated) MXFP4 on the MLPerf Small MoE benchmark (C4). Their recipe — hybrid 1D/2D block quantization + randomized Hadamard transform + stochastic rounding (from the NVFP4 recipe of Abecassis et al.) plus an optimizer-level weight de-oscillation adapted from TetraJet-v2 — reaches validation loss 3.3350 vs 3.3283 for BF16 at 16,128 steps (gap +0.007 instead of +0.014 without de-oscillation), and cuts steps-to-target (loss ≤ 3.34) overhead vs BF16 from +20.0% (18,432 vs 15,360 steps) to +5.0% (16,128 steps). Notably, they report honest negative end-to-end results for differential gradient estimation (+0.201 loss), outlier clipping, and macro-block scaling despite operator-level SNR gains; all FP4 GEMMs are simulated on MI300X, so no wall-clock/memory savings are demonstrated.

## 核心贡献

- 发布 ALTO 开源训练配方，在 MLPerf Small MoE Training 基准上从零预训练 GPT-OSS-20B（20B 参数 MoE），首次（公开报告中）把 MXFP4 微缩放格式在该基准上做到接近 BF16 的收敛：16,128 步验证损失 3.3350，仅比 BF16 基线 3.3283 高 0.007，而无去振荡时差距为 0.014（关掉了约一半剩余差距）。
- 系统性组合并消融了 FP4 训练技术栈：1D/2D 混合分块量化（激活 1D 32 元素块、权重 2D 32×32 块）、权重梯度路径上的随机 Hadamard 变换（RHT）、梯度随机舍入（SR）、宏块缩放（MBS）、低秩离群补偿、差分梯度估计（DGE）、M+Adam 与权重去振荡，最终推荐配方为 1d2d + RHT + SR + 去振荡。
- 提出模型无关、优化器级别的权重去振荡实现（简化自 TetraJet-v2 的 OsciReset）：在 P=200 步窗口内累积量化位移与全精度位移之比 DistRatio = dQ/dw，超过阈值 τ=4.0 的元素被吸附到当前量化格中心；作为 AdamW 的 post-step hook 实现，无需修改模型结构，且比 TetraJet-v2 更早启用（2,000 步 vs 8,000 步）、阈值更低（4.0 vs 16）。
- 揭示"算子级 SNR 提升 ≠ 端到端收敛改善"：MBS 带来最大算子级 SNR 增益（线性层前向 13.48 → 14.64 dB，分组 GEMM dX 12.71 → 14.17 dB）但端到端仅 −0.004；DGE 与动态裁剪算子级无害甚至略好，端到端却分别 +0.201、+0.181 严重恶化——这些负结果本身是有价值的社区贡献。
- 在 MLPerf 规则框架下给出两种收敛等效的稳定器选择：闭式赛道（要求纯 AdamW）用去振荡 hook，开式赛道用 M+Adam（乘性更新分支），两者达到 3.34 目标的步数相同（16,128 步，+5.0% vs BF16）。

## Key Contributions

- Release of the open-source ALTO recipe that pretrains GPT-OSS-20B (a 20B-parameter MoE) from scratch in the MXFP4 microscaling format on the MLPerf Small MoE Training benchmark, closing roughly 50% of the remaining BF16 gap: validation loss 3.3350 vs 3.3283 (BF16) at 16,128 steps / GBS=16, versus 3.3418 for MXFP4 without de-oscillation.
- A composed and ablated FP4 training stack: hybrid 1D/2D block quantization (1D 32-element blocks for activations, 2D 32×32 blocks for weights), randomized Hadamard transform (RHT) on the weight-gradient path, stochastic rounding (SR) on gradients — adopted from Abecassis et al. (arXiv:2509.25149) — plus macro-block scaling, low-rank outlier compensation, differential gradient estimation, M+Adam, and weight de-oscillation as evaluated alternatives.
- An architecture-agnostic, optimizer-level weight de-oscillation adapted from TetraJet-v2's OsciReset: over a P=200-step window, elements whose quantized travel dQ exceeds 4× their FP master-weight travel dw (DistRatio = dQ/dw > τ = 4.0) are snapped to their current bin center, implemented purely as a post-step hook on AdamW (enabled after 2,000 steps, vs TetraJet-v2's step 8,000 and τ = 16).
- A documented operator-SNR vs end-to-end gap: macro-block scaling delivers the largest operator-level SNR gains (linear forward 13.48 → 14.64 dB; grouped-GEMM dX 12.71 → 14.17 dB) yet improves validation loss by only 0.004, while DGE and dynamic clipping — benign at operator level — actively hurt end-to-end training (+0.201 and +0.181 validation loss respectively).
- An MLPerf-rule-aware stabilizer recommendation: weight de-oscillation for the closed division (optimizer must be exactly AdamW) and M+Adam for the open division, both reaching the 3.34 quality target in 16,128 steps (+5.0% over BF16).

## Methodology

The paper evaluates at two levels. Operator-level tests measure SNR (in dB, vs a BF16 reference doing the same forward+backward through an MSE loss) of MXFP4 Linear and grouped-GEMM kernels on synthetic tensors with injected sparse outliers — each element is N(0,1) plus, with probability 0.5%, an outlier scaled to ~100× standard deviation (N(0,1) + Bernoulli(0.005) ⊙ N(0,10000)) — reporting forward output (O), input gradient (dX), and weight gradient (dW). End-to-end, they pretrain GPT-OSS-20B from scratch on C4 (Llama-3.1 tokenizer, ~80 GB preprocessed) under the MLPerf Small MoE protocol: 8,192-token sequences, global batch size 16, AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, weight decay 0.1), LR 4×10⁻⁴ with cosine decay to 4×10⁻⁵, expert parallelism 8, quality target validation loss 3.34 validated every 768 iterations. MXFP4 is applied to all Linear and grouped-expert layers; the lm_head and MoE router gates are deliberately kept in high precision because routing decisions are sensitive to small perturbations.

The core recipe: (1) Hybrid 1D/2D blocking — the MX spec allows only 1D 32-element blocks, but a strictly 1D weight layout must be quantized twice along different axes for forward (contraction along K) and backward (dX = dO·W accesses W along N), doubling overhead and creating forward/backward inconsistency; 2D 32×32 weight blocks (beyond the MX spec) are valid in both orientations and are reused across forward and backward kernels, while activations stay 1D for compatibility with RHT. (2) RHT on the weight-gradient path only — a randomized Sylvester Hadamard matrix H transforms X′ = HX and dO′ = HdO; by orthogonality dW = (HdO)ᵀ(HX) = dOᵀX, so outliers are redistributed without changing the expected gradient. (3) Stochastic rounding of gradients (unbiased in expectation; deterministic rounding is kept for forward weights/activations), accelerated on CDNA4 via inline assembly. (4) Weight de-oscillation as described above. Because MI300X lacks native MXFP4 GEMM, all FP4 arithmetic is simulated via quantize-dequantize round trips before standard BF16/FP32 GEMMs — faithfully reproducing FP4 numerics but realizing no bandwidth or compute savings. Additional techniques (DGE with a corrected continuous piecewise power-law surrogate, k=5, derivative clamped to [0,3]; static 3/4 scaling and QuEST-style dynamic clipping; two-level macro-block scaling with 128×128 weight / 1×128 activation macro-scales; rank-32 low-rank outlier compensation O = XWᵀ + (XV)⊙σ(Uᵀ); M+Adam's additive-plus-multiplicative update exploiting w = m·2^e decomposition) were evaluated but not retained.

## Results & Highlights

- On C4 validation at GBS=16 through 16,128 steps, the full recipe MXFP4(1d2d)+RHT+SR+de-oscillation reaches validation loss 3.3350 vs 3.3283 for the BF16 baseline (+0.007), while MXFP4+RHT+SR without de-oscillation reaches 3.3418 (+0.014) — de-oscillation closes ~50% of the remaining FP4-to-BF16 gap, and the de-oscillated MXFP4 run also finishes ahead of NVFP4+RHT+SR.
- Steps-to-target on the MLPerf Small MoE benchmark (earliest checkpoint with validation loss ≤ 3.34): BF16 reaches it in 15,360 steps; plain MXFP4+RHT+SR and NVFP4+RHT+SR both need 18,432 (+20.0%); adding weight de-oscillation (or M+Adam) cuts this to 16,128 steps (+5.0%) — a 4× reduction in FP4 convergence overhead.
- Ablations at the 14,592-step checkpoint (GBS=16, shared RHT+SR control at 3.390): de-oscillation is the single best addition (3.382, Δ = −0.008), followed by M+Adam (3.383, Δ = −0.007) and MBS (3.386, Δ = −0.004); static clipping is exactly neutral (3.390, Δ = 0.000); low-rank compensation at r=32 is slightly harmful (3.397, Δ = +0.007); and DGE (3.591, Δ = +0.201) and dynamic clipping (3.571, Δ = +0.181) are strongly detrimental. Unquantized hybrid blocking alone (MXFP4 1d2d, no RHT/SR) sits at 3.438 (+0.048), i.e., RHT+SR recovers 0.048 of loss.
- Operator-level SNR (MXFP4 linear vs BF16 reference, dB): base 1d2d gives O/dX/dW = 13.48/11.80/13.02; RHT lifts dW to 13.25 at no forward cost; SR trades dX down to 11.02 (bias removal at the price of variance); MBS gives the largest gains (14.64/12.20/14.04 on linear; 14.64/13.76/13.72 on grouped GEMM with SR); rank-32 low-rank compensation raises forward SNR to 13.71 but its dW is structurally incomparable to a plain linear (reported as −∞). Crucially, these operator-level wins do not predict end-to-end outcomes: MBS's +1.16 dB forward SNR buys only −0.004 validation loss, and DGE's marginal dW SNR gain coincides with +0.201 validation-loss regression.
- De-oscillation's benefit is concentrated late in training: early in the run the recipe without it is comparable or slightly ahead, and the curves separate only as training approaches the final checkpoint — consistent with oscillation accumulating and mattering most in the fine-grained convergence regime (as designed in TetraJet-v2).

## Authors & Credibility

All seven authors (Han Wang, Zhitao Wang, Natalia Frumkin, Xuanwu Yin, Alireza Khodamoradi, Dong Li, Emad Barsoum) are at Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), several with prior Microscaling-format publications (e.g., Rouhani et al.'s microscaling data formats study cites overlapping authors). The work targets MLPerf Small MoE Training — a submission-oriented benchmark — and is built on the open-source Torchtitan stack with ALTO kernels (github.com/amd-agi/alto), which supports reproducibility. The paper is unusually forthcoming about negative results and about the simulated-kernel limitation, both of which increase rather than decrease its credibility. It is a preprint (v2, 26 Oct 2026), not peer-reviewed, and reads as a systems/recipe report rather than a methods paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: modest and honestly framed.** The three core ingredients (1d2d hybrid blocking, RHT, SR) are adopted wholesale from Abecassis et al.'s NVFP4 recipe, and de-oscillation is a simplification of TetraJet-v2's OsciReset. The genuine contributions are the optimizer-level (model-decoupled) de-oscillation hook with earlier activation (2,000 vs 8,000 steps) and more aggressive threshold (τ=4.0 vs 16), the MLPerf-rule-aware de-oscillation vs M+Adam split, and the negative-results ledger — valuable engineering, but the title's "closed the gap" overstates finality given only ~50% of the gap is closed and the horizon is short.
- **Horizon and scale caveats.** All end-to-end evidence comes from 16,128 steps of a schedule whose maximum is 1,200,000 steps — about 1.3% of the configured training horizon, at a single small GBS=16. Whether the +0.007 gap stays constant, shrinks, or explodes over a full 1.2M-step run is unestablished, yet late-training oscillation (the very phenomenon de-oscillation targets) is claimed to grow with horizon — so the 16k-step evidence may actually understate or misestimate the long-run effect. GBS=16 is also far from realistic MLPerf submission batch sizes.
- **No statistical rigor.** The decisive deltas (0.007–0.014 validation loss) are reported without seeds, error bars, or run-to-run variance estimates; Table 5's BF16 entry itself lists "3.365/3.371", which hints at two baseline runs differing by 0.006 — the same magnitude as the entire de-oscillation effect — yet this variance is never discussed.
- **Simulated arithmetic undermines the economic claim.** Because MI300X has no native MXFP4 GEMM, every experiment simulates FP4 via quantize-dequantize round trips: the paper validates numerical robustness of the recipe but none of the promised 4× footprint/bandwidth/compute benefits, and explicitly reports no wall-clock numbers. The premise of FP4 training (efficiency) remains unvalidated here.
- **Missing ablations and tuning opacity.** No sensitivity analysis for the two new de-oscillation hyperparameters (τ, P) or the activation step; a single point (τ=4.0, P=200) is presented. The MXFP4-vs-NVFP4 comparison is apples-to-oranges: MXFP4 gets de-oscillation while the NVFP4 row doesn't, so the implicit claim that MXFP4 ≥ NVFP4 conflates format with stabilizer. Router gates and lm_head staying in high precision is sensible but means "MXFP4 training" is really "mostly-MXFP4," with the unquantized fraction unquantified.

## Strengths

- Genuinely useful recipe composition at a real model scale (20B MoE, from-scratch pretraining) on a standardized, auditable benchmark protocol (MLPerf Small MoE, C4, fixed 3.34 quality target), rather than toy LM proxies.
- Honest and detailed negative results: DGE (+0.201), dynamic clipping (+0.181), low-rank compensation (+0.007) are reported with the same care as the wins, and the operator-SNR-vs-end-to-end gap is an actionable lesson for practitioners who screen techniques on kernel metrics.
- The two-level evaluation methodology (synthetic outlier-stressed SNR + end-to-end convergence + steps-to-target) is clean and reusable, and the paper explicitly separates what simulates numerics from what cannot (speed, memory).
- The optimizer-level de-oscillation is a practically clean design: no model surgery, no extra GEMMs, only modest optimizer-state memory (dw, dQ, a weight snapshot) that fits MI300/MI355 headroom, and it satisfies MLPerf closed-division's exact-AdamW constraint.

## Limitations

- All FP4 arithmetic is simulated (quantize-dequantize on BF16 GEMMs) on MI300X; no native-FP4 hardware, no measured bandwidth/compute/wall-clock gains, and unfused prototype kernels mean efficiency claims are purely theoretical.
- Single model family (GPT-OSS-20B), single dataset (C4 subset), single accelerator vendor (AMD MI300X), single global batch size (16), and a short 16k-step slice of a 1.2M-step schedule; generalization to dense models, other FP4 formats, larger batches, and full-length training is unverified.
- The remaining +0.007 gap to BF16 is not eliminated — the title's "closed the gap" means halved — and no downstream-task evaluation (only C4 validation loss) is provided to confirm the loss parity implies quality parity.
- New hyperparameters (τ=4.0, P=200, deosc_step=2,000) lack sensitivity studies; results carry no seed variance or confidence information.

## Relevance

高度契合"极端性能优化下的可靠性"方向：本文正是研究激进低精度（FP4/MXFP4）量化引入的数值误差如何破坏训练稳定性（权重振荡、梯度偏差、离群值敏感），以及如何修复，并量化了算子级数值指标与端到端训练可靠性之间的脱节。同时涉及 MoE 大规模训练稳定性与算子/内核基础设施，对训练可靠性与底层算子栈两个兴趣方向都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the +0.007 loss gap (and de-oscillation's benefit) persist, shrink, or grow over the full 1,200,000-step schedule and at production-scale global batch sizes, where oscillation effects should be larger?
- How sensitive is de-oscillation to τ, window P, and activation step — is there a regime where τ=4.0 snaps non-oscillating weights and hurts, given TetraJet-v2 chose a far more conservative τ=16?
- How much run-to-run variance exists at this scale: is the 0.007 loss difference (de-osc vs no-de-osc) outside seed noise, given the BF16 rows themselves differ by 0.006 in Table 5?
- On native FP4 hardware (e.g., MI355 or NVFP4-capable GPUs), do the simulated-numerics conclusions transfer, and what real wall-clock/memory speedup does the recipe yield once kernels are fused?
- Can MBS's operator-SNR gains (the largest of any technique, +1.16 dB forward) be converted into end-to-end wins under different hyperparameters, or is the SNR-to-conversion link fundamentally broken at this scale?
- Does validation-loss parity at 3.34 translate into parity on downstream tasks and on final model quality, rather than just the pretraining loss target?
