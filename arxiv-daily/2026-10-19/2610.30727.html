Why this matters

Any latent safety monitor that appears to beat the model's refusal is probably just reading that refusal early — Π tells you how to check.

15 models · 38 readers · 2 read positions

Critical edge

Only 13 of 135 string-matched "answered" harmful replies actually gave harmful help — the headline pre-generation gain is partly an artefact of the string-matching rule, not the model.

from Critical Assessment · "the weakest link"

01

Methodology — distilled

  • 15 open instruction-tuned models (Llama-3, Qwen2.5/3, Gemma-3/4, OLMo-3, OLMoE; ≤32B, three MoE), activations read at two anchors: the last instruction token (t_inst) and the last chat-template token before the reply (t_post).
  • 38 readers: Latent Guard's centroid statistic (+ z-scored variant), L2 linear probes, one-hidden-layer MLPs (layer & regularisation by 5-fold CV), and four designed refusal directions.
  • Baseline discipline: TF-IDF and sentence-embedding classifiers trained on exactly the same rows as the probes; Llama Guard 3 zero-shot; the model's own first-token refusal probability — every monitor compared at the refusal's own false-alarm rate.
  • Tests: D_beh (1,048 prompts), D_id (600 in-distribution), D_rew (2,248 rewordings incl. a new "harmless-but-alarming" set), leak-group separated.
  • Decoupling: consensus-controlled regression, within-class AUROC, and the cross-model disagreement statistic Π — exactly 0 for any model-blind score, 0.45–0.49 for a pure plan readout. Medians with 95% bootstrap; two models excluded after a label audit.

Coupling–gain dichotomy: every reader with Π ≤ 0.18 never clearly beats the refusal; every reader with Π ≥ 0.25 beats it by +0.04–0.10; none in between (rank correlation 0.83).

  • In-distribution AUROC is uninformative: monitors hit ≥ 0.998 on D_id — all conclusions rest on the reworded set.
  • Fitting t_post probes to more harm labels pulls them off the decision and hurts: linear probe gain +0.082 → −0.025 as labels grow 100 → 2,000/class.
  • One bounded exception: with the false-alarm budget set on alarming-benign data alone, probes beat the refusal by +0.47–0.57 while decoupled (Π 0.09–0.12) — but then flag 59–65% of jailbreak-style benign prompts (refusal: 4%).
  • The instruction token's one real edge: the S2000 probe is robust to persuasion framing (+0.11 AUROC over embedding LR, in 15/15 models) — at the cost of 2,000 labels per class.
03

Strengths

  • The judgment/plan decomposition turns "is the probe just reading refusal?" into a testable quantity — Π has an exact null (0) and a pure-plan reference (0.45–0.49).
  • Baseline discipline: same training rows and refusal at its own FPR budget eliminate the two chronic confounds (representation vs labels; ranking vs calibration).
  • Breadth with consistency: 15 models, 38 readers, conclusions replicate across both test sets.
  • Intellectual honesty: published label-audit error tables, pre-registered falsifiers, and the one exception bounded together with its false-alarm cost.
04

Limitations

  • Reader coverage stops at centroid, linear, and one-hidden-layer readers at two positions — attention-pooled, multi-position, and SAE-feature readers untested.
  • Refusal labels come from string matching on greedy replies: the refusal baseline is underestimated and the t_post gain overstated relative to judged labels.
  • The alarming-benign test set is LLM-generated and LLM-checked, and overlaps in kind with the over-refusal benchmarks on the training side — the authors' own main objection to their one exception.
  • Thin operating points: matched-rate threshold set by ~24 flagged benign prompts per model; one split seed; open models ≤32B only; a refusal-plan component shared by all models would still pass the decoupling tests.
05

Questions for further exploration

  1. Do attention-pooled, multi-position, or SAE-feature readers land in the empty Π band (0.18–0.25), or does the coupling–gain dichotomy hold for richer readers?
  2. With full-reply LLM-judged refusal labels, does the t_post gain shrink to the ~0.02 predicted by the audit?
  3. Does the coupling–gain relationship hold for frontier closed models, and for monitors read during multi-turn agentic sessions?
  4. Can the persuasion-framing robustness (+0.11 AUROC) be distilled with fewer labels via a stronger teacher's intermediate representations?
  5. Under adaptive attack (Bailey et al., 2026), how do faithful and plan-based reads compare — versus natural distribution shift only?
