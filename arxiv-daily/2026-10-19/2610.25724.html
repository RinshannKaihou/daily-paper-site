Injecting the rubric direction that matches an essay's degraded dimension beats a mismatched one by only 1.74% of the self-steering effect — far under the pre-registered 10% significance margin.

Score agreement and decodability do not imply control — a ready-made audit primitive for anyone deploying LLM scorers in high-stakes testing.

Methodology — a white-box audit in three linked experiments

Residual-stream activations captured at the final token of the grading prompt, for every layer of every model, on the gold-75 BESTEP set — three task parts (short factual answers n=21, opinion emails n=24, integrated essays n=30), six analytic rubrics.

RQ1 · DECODABILITY

Ridge-regression probes per [model × layer × part] cell, scored by leave-one-out cross-validated R². Chance level calibrated with 1,000-label permutation tests, Bonferroni-corrected across each model's 16–42 layers.

RQ2 · SEPARABILITY

Contrastive rubric directions from 104 rubric-targeted degradations of 26 high-band essays (written by Llama 3.3 70B from official low-band descriptors; two trained BESTEP raters confirmed the intended rubric, 100% consensus). Reliability-gated at split-half cosine ≥ 0.6; compared via disattenuated similarity into a “geometry gap”.

RQ3 · CAUSAL CONTROL

Steering injects one direction (α ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5} × layer mean residual norm) at a single layer; outcome is the change in probability-weighted expected band score. Four injection types — matching, mismatched, orthogonal, generic holistic — against a pre-set practical-significance margin of >0.05 pts or >10% of self effect, plus three robustness checks against hidden specificity.

Results — steering works, but never selectively

Scores can be read off the activations and the rubric directions do move them — but always through a shared axis, and agreement with human raters buys no control.

  • Decodability scales with task length: R² 0.432 → 0.633 → 0.773 from Part 1 to Part 3; signal fades toward the final layer (median 34% drop).
  • Five of six rubrics steer their own score — self effects 0.168–0.359 band points (argument relevance is the exception at −0.083); none of the 10 testable head-to-head comparisons favors the matching direction.
  • Human agreement is mediocre (median r = 0.41; only 7/25 models at r ≥ 0.6) — and in that best-7 subgroup the matching advantage is still just 1.9% of self effect.
  • Controls localize the lever: orthogonal direction lowers the score (Δ −0.097), generic holistic raises it (Δ +0.130) but below self/mismatched at every α — the effect lives on the axis the rubrics share.
  • Geometry predicts causal size (r = 0.717) but not which direction wins; even in the top reliability quartile, specificity reaches only 8.3%.

Strengths

  • Causal intervention — steering, not just probing — at unusual breadth: 25 models, 14 families.
  • Clean conceptual split into decodability / separability / control, each with a matched statistical test.
  • Three adversarial self-checks test whether the negative result is noise or direction-overlap in disguise.
  • Unusually honest limitations: post-hoc cutoff, tiny pair counts, single generator — all owned.
  • Public code and per-number result data; per-model appendix plots for model-by-model verification.

Limitations

  • Base models only, ≤ 10.31B — instruction-tuned / fine-tuned scorers (the deployed kind) untested.
  • One assessment (BESTEP, 75 essays), one grading prompt; single-layer, single-direction steering.
  • Rubric directions from 5–12 pairs each, one generator — verified for target recognizability, not cross-dimension purity.
  • Holistic-only human gold scores: “model collapses rubrics” vs “model mirrors human halo” cannot be separated.
  • Statistical caveats: conservative Bonferroni, wide single-pair intervals, ~12 effective samples behind r = 0.717.

Open questions — where this line of inquiry goes next

  1. Do instruction-tuned or rubric-fine-tuned scorers develop genuinely separable rubric control, or is the shared lever a stable property of next-token training?
  2. Would sparse-autoencoder features decompose the shared lever into rubric-specific latents that steering could then target selectively?
  3. With a disattenuated human inter-rubric correlation matrix, does the shared lever track the halo structure of human analytic ratings?
  4. Does separability or specificity improve beyond 10.31B parameters — or with different prompts and multi-layer, multi-direction interventions?
  5. Do the findings travel to non-English rubric-based assessments and decomposed-subconstruct systems like the Duolingo English Test?
