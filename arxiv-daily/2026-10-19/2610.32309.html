③ Why this matters

So what: harmless-looking prompt rewrites can break a frontier model's answer — and this shows the failure is predictable in advance and lives in representation geometry, not surface wording.

原文〈Relevance〉：对 LLM 残差流表示几何与组件级激活干预的系统 mechanistic interpretability 研究；"语义脆弱性的可预测失败规律"呼应推理可靠性与失败模式检测。

④ Critical edge

Every "repair" needs the item's own clean reference plus gold labels — so this is not, and does not yield, a clean-free detector, monitor, or deployable defense.

also: decisive causal stages rest on 7–27 eligible items — below the paper's own frozen 20-per-family threshold.

How it was done

Methodology

Distilled from the source — paired clean/rewritten prompts, residual-stream geometry, and interventions matched to exactly what a scalar summary keeps.

Each clean MMLU question is paired with six RUPBench task-preserving rewrites (500 bases → 2,394 variant rows on Qwen, 2,568 on Gemma). Residual-state differences between window endpoints yield three scalars — clean answer margin M, incoming displacement Din, local gain GW — fed to a compact logistic scorer under five-fold splits that hold out an entire family and all its items.

δb = (1+qW)·δa + ρW·Din·r̂W

An exact algebraic identity splits each update into longitudinal + transverse parts; Proposition 1 proves scalar summaries cannot see orientation. Matched interventions — fixed-geometry orientation swaps, Gemma mirror controls, attention-component patches vs norm-matched random directions — then test direction causally. Eligibility is decided before edited logits are read; item-cluster bootstraps with Bonferroni allocation; an explicit evidence-chronology table labels every frozen stage and retained negative result.

Results

Adding the perturbation-family ID changes nothing (0.952 → 0.951) — a shared predictive law, not shared surface triggers. Ranking transfers; calibrated probabilities do not (predicted risk 0.0999 vs 0.0248 observed).

  • Orientation swaps holding every scalar fixed: signed effect +1.702; rescue 33/44 failures (75.0%); induce failure in 34.1% of safe receivers vs ~1.1% matched random rotations.
  • Frozen component rule on 250 identity-disjoint new items: strict rescue 13/27 failures vs 0/108 matched random-direction draws.
  • Option orders: own-direction beats random & same-letter donors in all 37 conditions — yet 28/32 failing rewrites fail in exactly one of four layouts. Direction is real but layout-relative.
  • Dose–response: contracting the L54 endpoint toward clean rescues 80.7% at α=0; reverse amplification induces failure in 9.6% of safe variants.
  • Transverse energy carries ≈87.2% of the failure-vs-safe squared-gain difference at L52–54 (reversing at L54–56).
  • CoT axis: attribution transfers across math/health (ρ=0.434) but the frozen positive-mean causal criterion fails; a post hoc sign rule hits 73/82 (89.0%) — explicitly labeled post hoc.
What holds up

Strengths

  • Separates three evidence types — scalar prediction, causal directional sensitivity, signed readout influence — and never lets one stand in for another.
  • Matched controls at every level (equal-norm, mirrors, same-letter donors, stale-reference diagnostic), each closing a confound the previous experiment left open.
  • Negative results are load-bearing and retained: mirror directions suppress displacement more yet rescue less; 10.5% displacement reduction with null margin effect.
  • Cross-model, cross-dataset triangulation; a prospective search shows 8.04× enrichment — the score has triage value, not just retrospective fit.
Where it stops

Limitations

  • Privileged access throughout: clean references, gold labels, readout gradients — no path to a deployable robustness filter or monitor is addressed.
  • Decisive causal cohorts of 7–27 items with correlated draws (cyclic orders, donor trials) that bootstrap cannot fully de-correlate.
  • Only two ~30B open-weight models; Gemma lacks an identical replication of the orientation/magnitude experiments and any completed CoT signed-effect test.
  • Rewrites certified only by correlated AI screening (generator = reviewer checkpoint); no code release; bfloat16 makes some planned comparisons literally unestimable.

Questions for further exploration

Q2Does the transverse-dominance signature (~87% of excess gain) appear in other architectures and scales, or is the window band an accident of training?
Q3Transport test: patch order-A's direction into order B — full breakdown, or partial transfer per-order references cannot reveal?
Q4Would a full answer-readout basis (all four letters + distractor geometry) rule out letter-level mechanisms entirely?
Q5Does the 89.0% sign rule replicate on Gemma under a pre-registered threshold with dead-zone stability checks?
