Key metric · cross-lab agreement

ρ= 0.93

Median Spearman correlation of task-feature-count rankings across 30 model pairs from 5 labs. Language ID needs 1 residual feature in all 10 models; harmfulness needs 153–391.

Why this matters

k* predicts how many labels a new task needs (N* ∝ k*0.65), flags silently forgotten safety heads after continual learning, and exposes pruning-vulnerable tasks — interpretability you can act on.

▲Critical edge

Absolute counts are protocol-relative — paraphrasing test inputs inflates k* up to 10.8×, random rotations up to 20×. Importing harmfulness needs 341 features into another basis or wording will mislead.

Methodology · how k* is measured

Every task is cast as binary one-vs-rest classification over frozen residual-stream activations; each coordinate is a candidate feature. Readout sparsity is the smallest support reaching 95% of the dense probe:

s(k) = (A(k) − 0.5) / (C − 0.5) ≥ 0.95  // C = dense ceiling

  1. 1Two complementary searches: t-score-ranked feature prefixes with L2-regularized logistic probes, plus supports along an L1 regularization path.
  2. 2Protocol discipline: task-specific 4-layer window, 5 seeds × 85% stratified subsamples, adjacent-layer confirmation, median aggregation over class decisions then seeds.
  3. 3SAE correspondence: last-position residuals encoded with Gemma Scope / Llama Scope (JumpReLU) and Qwen-Scope (TopK), 16k–65k features; one-to-one matching tested against a label-correlation-matched null.
  4. 4Controlled causality: 20 synthetic models (18 transformers + 2 GRUs) trained with target-vs-random supervision; plus a weights × format factorial on Gemma-2-9B, LoRA adaptation, and magnitude/Wanda pruning studies.

Factorial decomposition — harmfulness: 341 → 135 (format only) / 128 (weights only) / 47 (both, ≈ separable); fact checking needs both factors (253 → 217 or 227 alone). Across models, reductions span 1.1× (Gemma-3-270M) to 8.5× (Gemma-3-12B).

  • SAE features correspond across labs: beyond a label-matched null's 99th percentile in 56.2% of intent tests (n=447), 48.9% language-ID (n=280), 34.3% tool selection (n=105) over 13 cross-lab pairs.
  • Selected features are causal: ablating 1–4 language SAE features collapses target-language reply adaptation (gap 0.88–0.99 → ≤ 0.015); matched controls preserve it. Yet pinning the top-256 harmfulness coordinates leaves dense probes within 0.0011 — readouts are sufficient, not exclusive.
  • Supervision concentrates access: in 20 synthetic models every readable supervised target gets a 1-feature readout vs. median 29–60.5 features for matched controls.
  • Label budget law: N* ∝ (k*)0.65 across 8 students × 16 tasks (95% CI [0.44, 0.91]; 5.3× held-out error) — but k*-proportional allocation saves nothing (1.03× uniform), and readout-guided pruning protection loses to plain Wanda.
  • Forgetting without losing readability: after continual-learning chains, saved heads drop 0.55–0.59 AUROC while dense ceilings stay within 0.009; recovery needs 101–244 labels vs. 194–602 to learn initially.

Strengths

  • Dissociates two conflated notions — how accurately a representation supports a task (dense ceiling C) vs. how sparsely (k*) — and demonstrates the dissociation empirically.
  • Breadth with rigor: 19 models, 5 labs, 14 core tasks, 20 synthetic controls; the protocol is precise enough to serve as a reporting standard (10 concrete recommendations).
  • Causal evidence at both ends: interventions show selected features matter for behavior, and supervised training demonstrably concentrates readout sparsity on targets.
  • Unusual honesty: failed allocation and protection interventions are reported as prominently as the successes.

Limitations

  • Protocol-indexed, not intrinsic: k* is not comparable across bases, pooling positions, input formats, or wording — a constraint the authors state explicitly.
  • Thin SAE coverage: correspondence covers only 3 tasks and 8 of 10 base models; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct borrows base-model SAEs, which may depress its scores.
  • Coarse scaling law: 5.3× held-out prediction error, fit with student-specific intercepts — new student families may need refitting.
  • Narrow continual-learning evidence: one 3-task sequence per model; the recovery advantage nearly vanishes on Qwen3-8B under one criterion.
  • k* = ∞ ambiguity: conflates “search failed” with “no small support exists”; borderline ceilings could distort counts.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does cross-model agreement in k* survive cross-lingual or adversarially filtered task pools, where surface cues differ per pretraining mix?
  2. Can readout directions (not just counts) be matched across models — enabling activation-level model stitching or cross-model distillation?
  3. Why do sparser readouts need fewer adaptation labels — is k* causal, or a proxy for feature redundancy?
  4. Does the 1-vs-~60 feature supervision gap explain why RLHF-style preference training yields diffuse, hard-to-erase behavior readouts?
  5. Would protecting the computational upstream of selected coordinates (attention heads writing to them) succeed where weight protection failed?
  6. Can “saved-head failure with retained linear readability” become an online monitor that flags silently degraded safety heads in deployment?
