> **一句话总结：** IQuest Research 发布开源 320B/15B 激活参数的稀疏 MoE 智能体基座模型 IQuest-Q1,通过 SplitNorm+Muon+μP 预训练(对比 Qwen3-MoE 同数据下取得 1.93× 训练效率)、渐进式长上下文中训练(至 512K)、以及"多脚手架多版本 SFT+GRPO+多教师在线蒸馏+残差模型合并"后训练,在 DeepSWE v1.1 (64.6)、CyberGym (84.5)、Terminal-Bench 2.1 (83.2) 等 CLI 智能体基准上对齐或超越 GLM-5.3-Flash / DeepSeek-V4-Flash 等同级开源模型,但在 HLE (39.2 vs Claude Opus-5 的 54.9) 和 Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0 上仍明显落后于前沿。

> **TL;DR:** An open-weight 320B-total/15B-active sparse-MoE agentic foundation model from IQuest Research, trained in three stages (Stable SplitNorm + Muon + μP pre-training with 29T tokens; mid-training extending context to 512K; multi-harness SFT + GRPO RL + multi-teacher on-policy distillation + residual model merging). Against open-weight peers it wins or ties most coding-agent benchmarks — DeepSWE v1.1 64.6 vs GLM-5.3-Flash 63.4 (+1.2) and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 54.4 (+10.2), CyberGym 84.5 vs GLM 81.6 (+2.9), SkillsBench +17.9 over GLM — but trails GLM-5.3-Flash by 10–12 points on Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0 and sits 10–15 points behind Claude Opus-5 / GPT-5.6 Sol on Humanity's Last Exam (39.2 vs 54.9/49.5). The controlled scaling study (1.93× efficiency vs a Qwen3-30B-A3B-style baseline on identical data) and the human-on-the-loop RSI case study are the most novel parts; per-component ablations are absent and the headline CLIBench is proprietary.

## 核心贡献

- **开源 320B/15B 激活的稀疏 MoE 智能体基座模型**:88 层、隐藏维 3072、256 选 8 专家、3:1 混合滑动窗口/全注意力(窗口 4096)、query-aware sink token、QKNorm(截断上限 10)、首层稠密 FFN 缓解早期负载不均衡,并从预训练起携带 2 层 MTP,推理时改造为 EAGLE-3 式递归投机草稿层。
- **稳定高效的预训练配方**:Stable SplitNorm(注意力子层前归一化、FFN 残差路径不归一化、注意力输出加 RMSNorm 防激活爆炸)+ Muon + μP 参数化。在完全相同数据下对比 Qwen3-30B-A3B 风格基线,取得 **1.93× 训练效率**(同等损失只需约 52% 算力);并提出 Critical Model Size (CMS) 稳定性框架,拟合外推稳定上限约 572B 激活 / 12B 总参数。
- **细粒度中训练**:自然语料+QA+智能体轨迹三类数据,token 级损失掩码解耦格式与监督,上下文按 32K→128K→512K 渐进扩展;从容器化仓库构建 100 万+ 可执行智能体实例并筛选有效轨迹。
- **多脚手架多版本后训练**:大规模任务合成(通用/推理/编码三线)、三层反 reward-hacking 防护(环境净化、执行期拦截、轨迹审计)、两阶段 SFT(240B+/50B+ tokens)、多 harness(Claude Code/Codex/OpenCode 各版本)GRPO、四教师 MOPD 在线蒸馏,以及跨阶段残差模型合并。
- **人在回路的递归自我改进 (RSI) 实例**:智能体自主定位训练数据工具调用 schema 偏置、设计并执行数据清洗+增广再训练(2.35B tokens、938.7 GPU 时),人类专家审核关键决策——展示了模型参与自身后续版本开发的工程路径。

## Key Contributions

- **Open-weight agentic MoE foundation model**: 320B total / 15B activated parameters, 88 layers, 256 routed experts with 8 activated, 3:1 hybrid SWA/full attention (4,096-token window), query-aware sink tokens, QKNorm with affine-parameter clipping, a dense FFN in the first layer to avoid early-layer expert load imbalance, and two multi-token-prediction layers from the start of pre-training, later converted into a recursive EAGLE-3-style speculative drafter.
- **Stable and efficient pre-training recipe**: Stable SplitNorm + Muon + μP parameterization achieves **1.93× scaling efficiency** versus a Qwen3-MoE (Qwen3-30B-A3B-style) baseline trained on identical data — reaching the same loss with ~52% of the compute. A new Critical Model Size (CMS) framework (analogous to Critical Batch Size) quantifies the gap between optimal and safe learning rates; fitted trends extrapolate a stability ceiling of ~572B activated / ~12T total parameters.
- **Fine-grained mid-training**: a mixture of natural corpora, QA pairs, and agentic trajectories; token-level loss masking decoupled from chat-template roles; progressive context extension 32K → 128K → 512K; and 1M+ executable containerized agent instances synthesized from GitHub repositories to grow trajectory data.
- **Multi-scaffold, multi-version post-training**: task synthesis across general (RapidAPI/MCP/SOC-guided), reasoning (document-grounded), and coding (repository-derived, multi-harness) settings; a three-layer reward-hacking defense (environment sanitization, execution-time interception, trajectory auditing); two-stage SFT (240B+ then 50B+ tokens); multi-harness multi-version GRPO with failure attribution; four-teacher Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD); and residual model merging with global ℓ2 calibration that stitches SFT, domain-RL, and MOPD checkpoints.
- **Human-on-the-loop RSI case study**: an IQuest-Q1-driven agent traced 35 tool-call failures (out of 6,054 calls) to a single-schema bias in training data, designed and ran a 2.35B-token / 938.7-GPU-hour data-cleaning-and-augmentation retrain, with humans approving key decisions — a concrete, supervised instance of a model accelerating its own successors.

## Methodology

The training pipeline has five phases. **(1) Pre-training** runs a two-stage curriculum over 29T total tokens (pre- + mid-training): Stage 1 maximizes unique-token diversity; Stage 2 raises the code fraction from 40% to 70% across four sub-phases to strengthen programming and STEM ability. Optimization uses Muon (momentum 0.95, spectral scaling 0.2·√max(m,n)) for matrices and Adam (β=0.9/0.95, ε=1e-15) for embeddings, heads, norms, and routers, with a warmup-stable-decay schedule peaking at 6×10⁻⁴. μP transfer is validated across a 4× width and 4× depth range, with the LR search done on a tiny 896-hidden/18-layer proxy and transferred zero-shot. **(2) Mid-training** mixes curated corpora, QA, and agentic trajectories under token-level loss masks, extends context in three phases (RoPE base 500K→1M for full attention), and generates 1M+ containerized instances whose rolled-out, validated trajectories become training data. **(3) SFT** initializes from mid-training in two stages (240B+ / 50B+ tokens at LRs 5×10⁻⁵ / 1.5×10⁻⁵). **(4) RL + MOPD**: episodes are drawn from multiple harnesses (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode) and versions; GRPO compares repeated rollouts under a fixed harness/evaluator; failures are attributed (policy-caused vs environment/harness/evaluator faults) before updates, and token-level probabilities flow through a Token-In-Token-Out pipeline for exact rollout-training consistency. MOPD then distills four frozen experts (agentic UX, multi-harness, long-horizon, general) into the student using clipped log-ratio teacher advantages in a length-normalized reverse-KL surrogate, executed fully asynchronously with staleness control. **(5) Model merging** combines parameter deltas relative to reference checkpoints with prescribed weights and global ℓ2-norm calibration. Evaluation spans 13 coding-agent and 17 general benchmarks; several agentic runs get 6–12 hour budgets and harness-specific configurations (mini-SWE-agent, BasicAgent, Claude Code, internal harnesses).

## Results & Highlights

- **Coding agents vs open-weight peers**: DeepSWE v1.1 64.6 vs GLM-5.3-Flash 63.4 (+1.2) and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 54.4 (+10.2); SWE-bench Multilingual 80.3 vs GLM 79.7 (+0.6); NL2Repo-Bench 63.0 vs GLM 56.3 (+6.7); SkillsBench 74.1 vs GLM 56.2 (+17.9, the largest open-weight gap); PaperBench 86.0 vs GLM 86.5 (−0.5). The paper's own Figure 1, however, shows the stronger DeepSeek-V4-Pro (0813) at 74.2 on DeepSWE — roughly 10 points ahead.
- **Clear regressions on the newest terminal benchmarks**: Terminal-Bench 3.0 17.9 vs GLM-5.3-Flash 30.0 (−12.1) and Terminal-Bench 4.0 22.7 vs GLM 32.8 (−10.1), even though Terminal-Bench 2.1 is nearly tied (83.2 vs 84.3, −1.1) — the long-horizon terminal edge does not carry to the hardest suites.
- **Cybersecurity**: CyberGym 84.5 vs GLM-5.3-Flash 81.6 (+2.9) and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 76.7 (+7.8); in a 3-day real deployment, human experts assisted by IQuest-Q1 produced 30 vulnerability reports, all independently validated and responsibly disclosed.
- **Reasoning is near-frontier on GPQA but far behind on HLE**: GPQA Diamond 93.3 vs GPT-5.6 Sol 94.6 and Claude Opus-5 93.4; Humanity's Last Exam 39.2 vs Claude 54.9 and GPT-5.6 49.5 (−10 to −16), and 55.7 with tools vs GLM 55.3 — the paper itself flags expert-level reasoning as the main gap.
- **Information seeking**: BrowseComp 85.5 vs DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 78.0 (+7.5) and WideSearch 81.8 vs 80.5 (+1.3); IMOAnswerBench-Verified 93.0 vs GLM 75.3 (+17.7).
- **Scaling efficiency (controlled)**: with identical training data, IQuest-Q1 reaches the same cross-entropy loss as a Qwen3-30B-A3B-style MoE using ~52% of the FLOPs (1.93× efficiency), and the safe/optimal LR headroom extrapolates to a Critical Model Size of ~572B activated parameters.
- **RSI iteration**: the self-improvement case study traced 35 tool-call errors across 6,054 calls to schema bias in training data, then ran a 2.35B-token / 938.7-GPU-hour correction run evaluated on held-out vs familiar schemas.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper carries ~180 authors under the "IQuest Research" banner (core contributors A. Jian Yang, An Huang, Bo Dan, Chen Mu, et al., with a senior advisory committee). Several names overlap with the GLM/Kimi/DeepSeek technical-report ecosystems, and the methods explicitly build on Kimi K3's configurable-harness training and DeepSeek-V4's domain-specialized GRPO — this is an experienced industrial training team, not a first-time effort. The report is unusually detailed on hyperparameters (optimizer scalings, LR schedule, MTP weights, packing window), releases model weights plus deployment configurations, and discloses which baseline scores are its own testing (marked *) — all credibility positives. Standard arXiv cs.CL technical-report preprint; no conference peer review.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the individual ingredients (SplitNorm, Muon, μP, GRPO, MOPD, EAGLE-style drafting, model merging) are all prior work, as the authors candidly cite. Genuine novelty is modest and systemic: the CMS stability framework (η_safe vs η_opt headroom) is a genuinely useful lens, and the fully specified multi-harness + multi-version RL episode design is one of the more complete public descriptions of harness-robust agent RL.
- **Rigor — the missing ablation**: the headline 1.93× efficiency gain is reported only as an aggregate against one Qwen3-style baseline. There is no decomposition of how much comes from SplitNorm vs Muon vs μP vs the hybrid attention pattern vs MTP auxiliary losses, so a practitioner cannot tell which components are worth copying. Similarly, MOPD and model merging are described but never ablated against plain multi-teacher SFT or simple weight averaging.
- **Reproducibility — mixed**: weights are open and hyperparameters are given, but the proprietary IQuest-CLIBench (one of the 8 headline benchmarks in Figure 1) cannot be externally verified; many baseline numbers are self-reported (*) under harness/time-limit configurations that materially affect agentic scores, and some (e.g., MiniMax M3's 1.7 on Terminal-Bench 2.1) look anomalous enough to suggest configuration mismatch rather than true capability gaps. The CMS ceiling of 572B/12T is an extrapolation of fitted trends, not a measurement.
- **Significance**: a competitive open-weight 320B/15B agentic CLI model is a real community contribution, and the reward-hacking mitigation section (environment sanitization, blocking rules, trajectory audits with no-op/reference solution checks) is among the more concrete published treatments.
- **Red flags**: the "recursive self-improvement" framing is supported by a single curated case study (Section 6.2) with no aggregate statistics on iteration success rate; the HLE-with-tools number uses the team's internal harness, complicating comparison; and Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0 results contradict the "optimized CLI interaction experience" headline more than the abstract admits.

## Strengths

- Uncommonly complete disclosure of architecture, optimizer, and schedule details — enough to serve as a reference recipe for a modern MoE agentic base model.
- The controlled same-data scaling comparison (1.93× vs a named, reproducible baseline configuration) is more scientifically meaningful than most lab reports' cherry-picked loss curves.
- The three-layer reward-hacking defense and failure-attribution rules (masking environment/harness faults, blocking evaluator faults) address the messiest practical failure mode of agentic RL.
- Honest self-assessment in places: the paper openly reports 10+ point deficits on HLE and includes Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0 columns that undercut its own narrative.
- The RSI case study reports concrete costs (2.35B tokens, 938.7 GPU-hours) rather than vague claims.

## Limitations

- Text-only: no native image, audio, or video input.
- Tool calls use an IQuest-specific chat-template format, requiring compatible serving parsers and harness adapters — integration friction for third-party users.
- Real-world CLI tasks still trigger repeated failed attempts, overlooked constraints, and unresolved issues; the authors themselves mandate human review of generated code.
- No component-level ablations anywhere in the main text; the contribution of each proposed technique is unquantified.
- Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0 (17.9 / 22.7) and HLE (39.2) trail both frontier proprietary models and GLM-5.3-Flash on the hardest long-horizon and expert-reasoning suites.

## Relevance

这是一份前沿智能体基座模型的技术报告,直接命中"Model System Cards & Technical Reports"兴趣方向;同时其 CMS 训练稳定性框架、多 harness RL 与 reward-hacking 防护,以及人在回路的 RSI 迭代案例,也分别与"LLM Training Stability & Observability""RL Post-Training Dynamics"和"Self-Evolving Agents"三个方向相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the 1.93× efficiency gain survives when SplitNorm, Muon, μP, hybrid SWA, and MTP are ablated independently — and does the Qwen3-style baseline close the gap when given MTP layers too?
- Does the extrapolated Critical Model Size (~572B activated) hold empirically, or does the η_safe/η_opt headroom shrink superlinearly once expert count and routing load interact at larger scale?
- The RSI iteration showed the model failing on unfamiliar tool-call schemas before retraining — does multi-harness GRPO actually generalize to harnesses and schema formats unseen during training, and how is that measured?
- Is MOPD with four frozen teachers better than a single strong teacher or simple checkpoint soup at equal compute, and do teacher domain assignments matter more than the distillation objective?
- Can the proprietary IQuest-CLIBench be externalized or proxied so the community can verify the "CLI user experience" claims, given that script-driven user simulation is itself an open research problem?
