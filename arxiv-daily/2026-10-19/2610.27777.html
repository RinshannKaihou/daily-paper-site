Why this matters

A measured map of where silent tool failures kill agent tasks — recognize → refetch → integrate — and which reliability mechanisms actually help anyone shipping tool-using agents.

From the Relevance note: direct reference for verification paths, state checking, and evidence-integration guardrails.

Critical edge

Every headline number rests on an automatic judge with only 76% raw human agreement (κ = 0.318) — absolute success rates are likely underestimates.

And it runs entirely on internal Tencent platforms: the live-tool path is not publicly replicable.

The three-stage failure cascade · where success leaks away
STAGE 01

Recognize

21–34%

Noticed rate under silent error (0.208–0.336). Of Unnoticed rollouts, 99.6% still carry the polluted value at the end.

→
STAGE 02

Refetch

23.3%

Of Noticed rollouts, only 23.3% end holding the gold value (57.8% still hold the polluted one). Refetching pays: RSR 0.655 with vs 0.387 without.

→
STAGE 03

Integrate

~⅓ fail

59.2% of refetches recover gold — yet about a third of those rollouts still fail, via reasoning overwrite, lossy selection, unsupported synthesis.

Recovery is possible by design — re-querying the polluted slot's producer returns the clean value — so the cascade isolates whether agents bother to check, and whether checking is enough.

Methodology · distilled

Matched triplets at a frozen checkpoint: every model meets the same error at the same decision point — unlike prior fault-injection work where each agent drifts into faults on its own trajectory.

  • Construction. Nine human-authored Tencent e-commerce workflows, inputs sampled from production logs. Slots chosen along the value-propagation graph; each of 167 units expands into three matched prefixes — correct, explicit failure, silent error — for 501 instances.
  • Error taxonomy. Seven semantic types in two groups (object/category selection; attribute/value drift); six instantiated, temporal staleness excluded. Four gates verify plausibility, explicit-failure clarity, non-leakage, discriminability.
  • Replay world. Recorded calls replay identically across models; unseen valid calls hit the live tool. Clean trace is stored, so re-querying the producer returns the unpolluted value.
  • Protocol. 10 LLMs × all 501 instances, temperature 0, 25-step budget. Measures: Noticed, Result Success Rate, Retention (implicit ÷ correct). GLM-5 automatic judge, validated against 3 human annotators.

  • Verification pays but is rare. GLM-5.2 notices just 21.7% of errors and producer-refetches in only 7.1% of rollouts — lowest Retention (0.345).
  • Business stakes. For 87.4% of slots (146/167), the gold-vs-polluted value changes the downstream e-commerce output — different recommendation, intervention, or topic suggestion.
  • State Checker intervention. Forcing reflection before each tool-result use more than triples GLM-5.2's refetch rate — but only modest RSR gains, and Claude-Opus-4.8 gains none despite noticing more. Looking ≠ repairing.
  • Judge validation. Noticed κ 0.739; Result κ 0.318, precision 97.1% / recall 75.3% — a conservative judge (69/100 solved vs humans' 89/100).

Strengths

  • First of its kind. Places business-semantic silent errors at controlled, matched decision points, with correct-value and explicit-failure twins for apples-to-apples comparison.
  • Ecologically grounded. Production logs and deployed industrial workflows rather than synthetic tools.
  • Careful measurement stack. Noticed / RSR / Retention plus paired gaps; judge labels human-validated with κ reported.
  • Diagnosis beyond headline numbers. Quantifies the recognize → refetch → integrate chain and names three concrete post-recovery failure patterns.
  • Honest reporting. Includes a largely negative result for their own intervention and an explicit AI-use statement.

Limitations

  • Not publicly replicable. Built entirely on internal Tencent platforms; workflows anonymized (wf01–wf09), live-tool fallback requires internal deployment.
  • Recoverable-by-design. Single-site, non-persistent corruption with a deterministic clean re-query — real silent faults are often persistent or multi-site, so real recovery is likely lower.
  • Untested error class. Temporal staleness is never instantiated; time-drift failures remain uncovered.
  • Thin subgroup samples. 13 late slots, 20 tool-input units; e-commerce domain only.
  • Conservative judge. κ 0.318 vs human majority — absolute rates likely underestimate; State Checker tested on 3 models at half scale, no cost/latency accounting.

Questions for further exploration

  • Q1Does the cascade hold when corruption is persistent or multi-site, requiring independent evidence rather than one refetch?
  • Q2Can interventions target integration (overwrite / lossy selection / unsupported synthesis) instead of just detection?
  • Q3Does noticed-most-recovered-least on early slots generalize to longer-horizon, multi-workflow agents?
  • Q4What is the cost-benefit frontier of always-on suspicion vs selective, consistency-triggered verification?
  • Q5Can matched-prefix replay port to open tool ecosystems for an externally replicable RITE?
  • Q6Would a graded partial-credit metric (judge recall 75.3%) reorder the model ranking?
