> **一句话总结：** 本文形式化并系统审计了"推理时模型偏移"——量化模式与采样温度等推理配置在标定与部署之间不一致时，即使任务分布和模型权重完全不变，也会打破保形预测的可交换性假设；在 8 个小语言模型 × 4 个基准 × 12 种推理配置的网格上，MMLU-Pro 的平均覆盖率从匹配标定的 0.902 跌到错配均值的 0.639、最差情况的 0.508，而匹配式评估协议在原理上根本无法暴露这一失效。

> **TL;DR:** Conformal prediction (CP) for language models is always evaluated under a *matched* protocol (calibration and deployment share the same quantization mode and temperature), but real deployments change these post-training settings. The paper formalizes this as "inference-time model shift" and audits it across 8 small LMs (Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B, Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.2-3B, Phi-3-mini, Mistral-7B), 4 benchmarks (CommonsenseQA, DBPedia-14, MMLU-Pro, MobileActions), and 12 inference configurations ({fp16, int8, nf4} × T ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}). With deployment fixed to nf4@T=0.5 and α=0.1, MMLU-Pro mean coverage collapses from 0.902 (matched, vs the 0.90 nominal target) to 0.639 (mismatch mean) and 0.508 (mismatch worst) — a −0.39 worst-case drop versus the matched baseline — while pooled calibration only reaches 0.686. A simple max-threshold baseline (MAXTHR) restores ≈0.90 coverage at near-matched set sizes, but the authors position it as a reporting baseline, not a method contribution. The headline recommendation: inference configuration must be treated as a first-class experimental axis in CP evaluations, on par with dataset and model. (NeurIPS 2026 Datasets & Evaluations track.)

## 核心贡献

- **识别并形式化"推理时模型偏移"（inference-time model shift）**：指出语言模型的保形预测评估普遍采用"匹配协议"（标定与部署使用相同的量化模式、推理温度等后训练推理配置），而真实部署中这些配置常因内存/吞吐约束或服务策略而改变。论文证明这种偏移与经典数据集偏移（covariate/label shift）本质不同——任务分布 PXY 完全固定，偏移发生在"输入 → 预测分布"的映射即分数生成机制本身。
- **理论刻画**：命题 3.2 证明当标定配置与部署配置诱导的非一致性分数分布不同（F_ccal ≠ F_cdep）时，标定分数与测试分数一般不可交换，split-conformal 的覆盖率保证不再成立——即匹配有效性不能跨推理配置"搬运"。
- **两个与部署假设对齐的保守基线**：MAXTHR（对每个配置分别标定后取最大阈值，对任意部署混合分布均保证 ≥1−α 覆盖）；LATENT-ROBUST（两阶段构造：先用集合大小签名 Z(x)=(|Ŝ_c(x)|)_c 经保形化 logistic 路由器推断潜在部署配置集 Γ̂，再在 Γ̂ 内取最大阈值，保证 1−α_stage1−β_stage2）。
- **大规模系统性审计**：8 个小语言模型 × 4 个有限标签基准 × 12 种推理配置（3 种量化 × 4 种温度），LAC 分数、α=0.1、3 次独立随机划分；附录覆盖 APS/RAPS 分数与 α∈{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.3}。
- **评估协议改革建议**：CP 评估应（i）声明所针对的部署假设（固定配置/稳定混合/测试时不可观测），（ii）报告对标定-部署错配的敏感性而非仅匹配覆盖率，（iii）报告与部署假设对齐的基线。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies and formalizes inference-time model shift**: current CP evaluations for LMs use a matched protocol where calibration and deployment share quantization mode and temperature, but deployed systems routinely change these for memory, throughput, or serving-policy reasons. The shift is distinct from classical dataset shift because PXY is fixed — what changes is the score-generating mechanism mapping inputs to predictive distributions.
- **Theory**: Proposition 3.2 shows that when calibration and deployment configurations induce different score distributions (F_ccal ≠ F_cdep), calibration and test scores are generally non-exchangeable, so the split-conformal finite-sample guarantee need not hold — matched validity is not transportable across inference configurations.
- **Two deployment-assumption-aligned conservative baselines**: MAXTHR (max over per-configuration thresholds; provably ≥1−α coverage for every configuration and any deployment mixture) and LATENT-ROBUST (a two-stage construction that conformally infers a plausible set of latent deployment configurations from a set-size signature via a logistic router, with guarantee 1−α_stage1−β_stage2). Both are framed as strict reference baselines, not as superior replacements.
- **A systematic audit**: 8 small LMs × 4 finite-label benchmarks (CommonsenseQA, DBPedia-14, MMLU-Pro, MobileActions) × 12 configurations ({fp16, int8, nf4} × {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}); main results use LAC at α=0.1 averaged over 3 runs; APS/RAPS scores and α ∈ {0.05–0.3} in appendices.
- **Concrete evaluation-protocol reform**: report the deployment assumption, report mismatch sensitivity along the inference axes that can vary, and include a baseline aligned with the targeted deployment regime.

## Methodology

The paper combines a short formal treatment with a large controlled empirical audit. **Formalization (Sec. 3):** an inference configuration c = (q, t) ∈ C = Q × T induces a predictive distribution π^(c)(x) over candidate labels; a nonconformity score V^(c) = A(π^(c)(X), Y) then has distribution F_c. Inference-time model shift is defined as the existence of c, c′ with F_c ≠ F_c′ under a fixed task distribution — breaking score exchangeability between calibration and test. **Scoring pipeline (Appendix C.4):** temperature t is applied to token logits before candidate-label scoring; candidate scores are length-normalized log-likelihoods, exponentiated and renormalized over the candidate set to yield π_q,t; quantization (fp16/int8/nf4, standard post-training LLM quantization) changes only the numerical representation — the same checkpoint, prompt template, and splits are used throughout, cleanly isolating the configuration effect. **Robust baselines (Sec. 4):** MAXTHR calibrates per configuration and deploys the global max threshold; LATENT-ROBUST splits calibration data 50/50 into Stage 1 (per-configuration thresholds) and Stage 2 (a conformal set Γ̂(Z(x)) of plausible latent configurations, where Z(x) is the vector of hypothetical set sizes and the router is a multinomial logistic regression, α_stage1 = β_stage2 = α/2). **Audit design (Sec. 5):** deployment fixed to nf4@T=0.5 (Exp. 1); deployment temperature fixed at T=0.5 with quantization averaged (Exp. 2); deployment quantization fixed at nf4 with temperature averaged (Exp. 3). Six procedures are compared: MATCHED, MISMATCH-MEAN/WORST (off-diagonal calibration configurations), POOLED, MAXTHR, LATENT-ROBUST. Splits: CommonsenseQA 6000 cal/6000 test, DBPedia-14 8000/50000, MMLU-Pro 2500/8000, MobileActions 2500/5000. Compute: a single RTX A6000 (48 GB); everything runs sequentially over the grid.

## Results & Highlights

- **MMLU-Pro is the worst case (Exp. 1, deployment nf4@T=0.5, LAC, α=0.1):** descriptive averages over 8 models drop from 0.902 under MATCHED (nominal target 0.90) to 0.639 under MISMATCH-MEAN (−0.263) and 0.508 under MISMATCH-WORST (−0.394); the natural practitioner fix, POOLED calibration, only reaches 0.686 — still 0.216 below target.
- **Per-model collapse on MMLU-Pro (Table 5):** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B falls from 0.904 ± 0.006 (matched) to 0.443 ± 0.005 (mismatch worst) while average set size shrinks from 7.64 to 2.51 — i.e., mismatch makes CP simultaneously less reliable and deceptively more "efficient"; Qwen2.5-1.5B drops to 0.468 and Qwen2.5-3B to 0.480 worst-case.
- **CommonsenseQA degrades mildly but uniformly:** 0.912 → 0.827 (mismatch mean) / 0.789 (worst), pooled 0.818; all 8 models fall below target under all three mismatch summaries. MobileActions is most stable yet MISMATCH-WORST still misses target for 6/8 models.
- **Neither axis alone explains the effect.** With deployment temperature pinned at T=0.5 and quantization averaged (Exp. 2), MMLU-Pro averages are 0.906/0.648/0.520/0.695 (matched/mean/worst/pooled) — essentially unchanged — and mismatch-worst misses for 8/8 models on every dataset. With quantization pinned at nf4 and temperature averaged (Exp. 3), MMLU-Pro still falls to 0.842 mean / 0.719 worst, and DBPedia-14 spans an extreme model range under mismatch (mean 0.567–0.922, worst 0.220–0.890), ruling out any monotone ordering of configurations that predicts mismatch severity.
- **Robust baselines restore coverage at a real efficiency cost.** MAXTHR on MMLU-Pro recovers 0.898–0.967 coverage across models with set sizes essentially equal to matched (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B: 0.898 at size 6.148 vs matched 0.899 at 6.167). LATENT-ROBUST is valid but can be extremely conservative: on MMLU-Pro it hits 1.000 coverage for Qwen2.5-3B with average set size 9.464 out of 10 candidate labels — a near-useless prediction set.
- **Findings are not artifacts of one score or one target level:** the qualitative mismatch pattern persists across APS, LAC, and RAPS scores (Appendix F.1) and across α ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3} (F.2); run-to-run standard deviations are small relative to the method gaps.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — conceptually simple, systematically earned.** The core observation (changing quantization/temperature changes the score distribution, hence breaks exchangeability) is an immediate corollary of known non-exchangeable CP theory, and the authors do not pretend otherwise; the value lies in naming the phenomenon, isolating it from PXY-shift confounds via a clean fixed-checkpoint/fixed-split design, and quantifying it on a 384-cell grid (8 models × 4 datasets × 12 configurations). Readers wanting a new algorithm will be disappointed: MAXTHR and LATENT-ROBUST are deliberately conservative baselines, and the paper says so explicitly.
- **Rigour — high for an audit.** Disjoint calibration/evaluation splits, 3 runs with error bars, three nonconformity scores, four α levels, and exact quantization/temperature implementation details (token-logit-level temperature, length-normalized candidate log-likelihoods) make the main tables credible and reproducible.
- **Reproducibility — very good.** Code, configs, and artifacts are released; every implementation choice that could affect the score pipeline is documented in Appendix C; the only ambiguity is that "Mobile Actions" is a Hugging Face dataset card citation (2025) rather than a peer-reviewed benchmark.
- **Significance — real but bounded.** The finding matters to anyone deploying CP-guarded LLM pipelines where serving config drifts after calibration (exactly the situations quantized serving creates). However, all models are small (≤7B) — quantization error structure at 70B+ scales may differ; and the study is restricted to finite-label tasks (multiple-choice/classification), explicitly excluding open-ended generation, where the effect could be larger or smaller. The authors acknowledge both the finite configuration grid and the lack of a mechanistic/predictive theory of *when* a given config change hurts.
- **Red flags / quirks worth noting.** (1) The headline MMLU-Pro numbers are averages dominated by one very sensitive dataset; DBPedia-14 shows the effect is *selective* (some model families nearly invariant, e.g., mismatch-mean up to 0.946) — the paper reports this honestly, but a skim of the abstract alone would overgeneralize. (2) Mismatch direction is not always undercoverage: on DBPedia-14, Qwen2.5-7B shows mismatch-mean coverage 0.946 *above* the 0.90 target (overcoverage), so "coverage always collapses" would be the wrong takeaway. (3) LATENT-ROBUST's near-full prediction sets (size 9.46/10 at 1.000 coverage) mean its practical utility beyond illustrating the cost of hedging is limited. (4) The temperature axis here is applied pre-scoring at the logit level — a specific pipeline choice; deployments that sample generations and score differently could see different magnitudes.

## Strengths

- Clean problem isolation: same checkpoint, same prompt template, same data splits across all 12 configurations, so the measured degradation is attributable solely to the inference configuration.
- The matched-vs-mismatch framing exposes a genuine blind spot in the CP-for-LLM literature (Kumar et al., Quach et al., Mohri & Hashimoto, etc. all calibrate and evaluate under one fixed inference setup).
- Honest positioning of the two robust baselines as regime-aligned references with explicit validity proofs, including the admission that LATENT-ROBUST can be *more* conservative than MAXTHR because the error budget is split across two stages.
- Exhaustive robustness checks (3 scores, 4 α values, 3 runs) and a concrete, immediately adoptable three-step reporting protocol for future CP evaluations.

## Limitations

- Small models only (1.5B–7B); no evidence about frontier-scale models where quantization sensitivity may differ substantially.
- Finite-label tasks only; open-ended generation is explicitly out of scope, so the practical impact on conformal factuality/selective-generation pipelines is untested.
- Characterization without mechanism: no predictive theory of which configuration changes cause undercoverage vs overcoverage vs mild effects — the paper admits this is absent.
- The 12-configuration grid is coarse: continuous temperatures, other serving-stack variations (kernels, KV-cache precision, batching), and temporal drift in serving policies are not covered.
- The proposed remedies trade coverage for set size; the efficiency frontier (especially of LATENT-ROBUST, with its 50/50 calibration split) is unattractive in the worst cells, and no adaptive method that beats MAXTHR is attempted.

## Relevance

直接命中"极端性能优化下的可靠性"方向：论文正是研究量化（int8/nf4）与温度这类推理栈数值配置如何在标定与部署之间静默地摧毁保形预测的覆盖率保证（MMLU-Pro 上最差掉 0.39），同时与"推理可靠性"方向相关——它刻画了模型下方数值配置（numerics/serving stack）引发的可量化静默失效，并给出了可复用的审计协议。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does inference-time model shift persist at 70B/405B scale, where nf4/int8 quantization error and logit sharpness differ qualitatively from 7B models?
- Can the DBPedia-14 heterogeneity be predicted from cheap observables (e.g., score entropy, logit gap distributions, per-configuration set-size signatures) before deployment — turning the audit into a detector?
- How does the effect interact with covariate/label shift: does mild PXY shift plus config mismatch compound multiplicatively or saturate?
- Would weighting-based remedies (importance-weighted or non-exchangeable-weighted calibration over the configuration grid) dominate MAXTHR's worst-case hedging at better set sizes?
- Does the phenomenon extend to open-ended generation scores (conformal factuality, correctness-coverage), where scores are computed over sampled generations rather than candidate-label likelihoods?
- Is the LATENT-ROBUST router's set-size signature Z(x) robust when deployment mixes configurations per-request rather than per-period, and can the two-stage error budget be allocated more efficiently than α/2 + α/2?
