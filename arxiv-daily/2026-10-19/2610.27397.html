Aggressive 4-bit quantization can silently destroy embedding quality. This work measures the failure dose and ships a pre-registered gate that blocks silent degradation.

01 · Methodology

A pre-registered, dose-controlled calibration study across three 4-bit embedders.

  • Three architectures. e5-base-v2 (BERT, mean-pool), BGE-M3 (XLM-R, CLS-pool), EmbeddingGemma-300m (Gemma-3 decoder, dense head). All linear layers INT4 via learned step-size quantization (LSQ + straight-through); weights frozen, only quantizer parameters train.
  • Calibration data. 32 labelled queries × 4 BEIR domains (MS MARCO, FiQA, NFCorpus, SciFact), 2,000-doc corpus each; 12 Adam steps over a 4-point LR grid — 10⁻⁴ → 3×10⁻³, extended to 10⁻² for one control. 161 runs total.
  • Objectives. LambdaRank over mined negatives vs two label-free controls: cosine pull toward full-precision embeddings, and MSE weight reconstruction.
  • Dose matching. Trajectories binned by Dfunc — the mean cosine distance between calibrated and uncalibrated quantized embeddings; objectives compared within bins at 3×σopt tolerance, decisive cells re-run on 3 independent slates with a 0.02 Dfunc caliper.
  • Validity. Per-model, per-LR noise floors (slate variance dominates); paired-within-slate designs; shuffled-label nulls; bit-exact INT4 export checks with zero mismatches.

  • Not retrieval-specific. At matched dose, the label-free cosine objective collapses in the top Dfunc bin exactly like LambdaRank (−69.52 vs −71.63 pp, 7 qualifying bins, verdict not_retrieval_specific). The MSE control plateaus at Dfunc = 0.107 and never reaches the collapse dose.
  • Objective sensitivity is model-dependent. Ranking beats cosine by +14.16 pp on e5 (95% CI [+3.37, +24.96]) but −1.40 pp on BGE-M3 (CI [−5.18, +2.38]) — directionally opposite, unresolved.
  • Dfunc is necessary but not sufficient. Paths reaching the same Dfunc via different learning rates differ by up to 1.61 pp (~9× σruntime); the scalar-threshold hypothesis was rejected.
  • Label-efficiency claim retracted. The “selected” arm actually read 1,024 labels vs 512 random; held-out NDCG@10 was indistinguishable (Δ +0.0004, CI [−0.037, +0.038]).

03a · Strengths

What this paper does unusually well

  • Dose-matching template — bin by Dfunc, compare within bins, verify on paired slates — transfers to any objective comparison confounded by update magnitude.
  • The central negative result kills a plausible-but-wrong narrative (“ranking supervision is dangerous”) that most papers would never test.
  • The gate is simple, label-free at decision time, pre-registered, and reported via an honest four-number scorecard without overselling.
  • Exceptional transparency: a “What we do not claim” section enumerates unresolved orderings, the missing mechanism, and the retracted claim.

03b · Limitations

Read it with these constraints in view

  • Small evaluation footprint: 25 disjoint queries per calibration domain; 100 queries / 2,000 docs per held-out set; the three sealed datasets were never evaluated in this version.
  • Narrow grid: 3 embedders, INT4 only, one quantizer family (LSQ), 12 steps, 32 queries per domain — generality to W8A8, AdaRound, or larger embedders is untested.
  • BGE-M3 never reaches a collapsing dose, so “the dose can be lethal” is directly shown on only 2 of 3 models.
  • No mechanism offered for the 1.61 pp path residual; update direction, objective geometry, curvature left open.
  • The gate abstains 14/30 and missed +2 pp updates even with a 50% validation split — real safety–opportunity cost.

04 · Questions for further exploration

  1. Does dose-driven collapse and Dfunc screening transfer to activation quantization (W4A4), other bit widths, or decoder-based embedders at 1B+ scale?
  2. What mechanism produces the path residual — and could a path-aware statistic (update-norm history, curvature) close the gap the rejected scalar threshold left open?
  3. Would a different bootstrap statistic, or a two-factor gate incorporating Dfunc, cut the 14/30 abstention rate without reintroducing harmful selections?
  4. Does the ranking-over-cosine robustness ordering correlate with an architectural property (pooling, prefixes, normalization) predictable a priori?
  5. What happens on the sealed datasets in the planned post-freeze evaluation — does the zero-harm record survive full-BEIR query counts and corpora?
