> **一句话总结：** DuoPD 把 LLM 解码阶段整体卸载到嵌入 HBM4 逻辑裸片内的轻量 PNM 单元、让 GPU 专心做 prefill，二者通过 TSV 级带宽共享并发执行；在 8×GB300 级模拟平台上，端到端吞吐最高提升 2.23 倍、TPOT 最多降低 77.66%，且每栈仅增加 6.51 mm² / 6.45 W 的 PNM 开销。

> **TL;DR:** Seoul National University proposes DuoPD, a hybrid GPU + processing-near-memory (PNM) architecture that runs the *entire* memory-bound decode phase on lightweight SIMD units placed in the HBM4 logic die while the GPU handles all compute-bound prefill, sharing TSV/HBM bandwidth instead of scaling it. In a cycle-level simulator (DRAMSim3-integrated, 8 GB300-class GPUs with 8 HBM4 stacks each, 2 TB/s per stack), DuoPD delivers 1.46–2.23× end-to-end throughput over conventional PD-disaggregation baselines on GPT3-175B (1.29–1.61× on LLaMA2-70B, 1.45–1.78× on Qwen3-235B-A22B), cuts TPOT by up to 77.66% vs 3:1 PD-disaggregation, and reduces end-to-end energy by up to 37.9% for 512-token outputs — with a 6.51 mm², 6.45 W PNM per stack that fits the HBM4 logic die.

## 核心贡献

- **识别 PD 分离式推理的结构性资源浪费并证明 GPU 内部方案无法解决**：prefill 是计算受限（HBM 带宽大量闲置）、解码是带宽受限（GPU 算力大量闲置）；SM 划分（如 Green Context）仍需大量 SM 才能打满带宽——B200 即便有 TMA 也需要 72 个 SM（48.6%，约 1.2 PFLOPS FP16）才能达到 80% 峰值带宽，而 GPT3-175B 解码只需 422 TFLOPS。
- **提出 DuoPD：单设备内的"相位级"分离架构**——与此前 PIM/PNM 只卸载解码内部的 attention/MoE（intra-decode）不同，DuoPD 把整个解码阶段放到 HBM 逻辑裸片上的 PNM，GPU 全部算力做 prefill，两相位通过共享 TSV I/O 异步并发，不增加任何物理带宽。
- **三个关键硬件机制**：(1) 紧凑 PNM 微架构（16 条 1024 位宽 SIMD lane + 8-way 加法树，每栈 2 TB/s / 32 TFLOPS，支持 GQA 广播与 FlashDecoding/online softmax）；(2) TSV→PNM 缓冲区的直接数据转发通路（绕过内存控制器，避免 TSV I/O 重复占用）；(3) MMU 仲裁（张量粒度位图请求队列 + prefill-first 调度策略）。
- **层次化数据映射**：GPU 层用 TP（而非 PP，因两相位须共享参数划分）、PNM 层用序列并行 + FlashDecoding 部分归约，参数只读共享、KV cache 独占，避免 GPU-PNM 一致性开销。
- **系统级可行性论证**：28nm 综合后按 7nm 缩放的 PNM 面积 6.51 mm²、功耗 6.45 W/栈，HotSpot 热仿真峰值 76°C（低于 85°C 刷新阈值），且不改动 DRAM 核心裸片。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnose the structural waste of PD-disaggregated serving** and show GPU-internal fixes cannot resolve it: prefill rarely sustains peak HBM bandwidth, while decoding needs surprisingly many SMs to saturate DRAM bandwidth — on a B200 (8 TB/s HBM3e, with TMA), 72 SMs (48.6%, ≈1.2 PFLOPS FP16) are needed to reach 80% of peak bandwidth, nearly 3× the 422 TFLOPS GPT3-175B decoding actually requires at batch 32.
- **DuoPD, an inter-phase disaggregation within one device**: all GPU compute goes to prefill, the *entire* decode phase runs on lightweight PNM units in the HBM4 logic die, and the two share the existing TSV/HBM interface (bandwidth *sharing*, not the costly bandwidth *scaling* of prior PIM/PNM work).
- **Three enabling mechanisms**: a compact PNM microarchitecture (16-lane SIMD, 1,024-wide FP16 lanes with 8-way adder trees = 16 Op/B and 2 TB/s per stack at 1 GHz; sublanes for attention heads; vector unit with online softmax for FlashDecoding; 1 MB buffer chosen from saturation sweeps); TSV-level direct data forwarding that bypasses the memory controller; and an MMU with a tensor-granularity bitmap request tracker arbitrating GPU vs PNM traffic (prefill-first policy wins because decode-first starves the GPU and inflates TTFT).
- **Hierarchical data mapping**: tensor parallelism at GPU level (PP is rejected because both phases must share one parameter partitioning), sequence parallelism + FlashDecoding partial reductions at PNM level with inter-PNM all-reduce over a GPU DMA engine (only ~2% of the >24 µs per-layer decode time); read-only shared parameters plus exclusively-owned KV caches eliminate coherence traffic, with the PNM LSU enforcing read-only parameter access.
- **Feasibility evidence**: PNM synthesized in 28 nm and scaled to 7 nm occupies 6.51 mm² (SIMD 3.64, vector unit 1.56, buffer 1.25 mm²) inside the 38.6 mm² available region of the ~140.8 mm² HBM4 logic die; 6.45 W per stack; simulated peak temperature 76°C at 45°C ambient, below the 85°C refresh-safety threshold.

## Methodology

DuoPD targets a Blackwell-style platform: each GPU paired with eight HBM4 stacks, each stack's logic die carrying one PNM unit (HBM4's move to a logic-process base die lets the memory controller migrate from the GPU into the HBM die — the enabler for centralized arbitration). The PNM's 16 SIMD lanes deliver 2 KB/cycle at 1 GHz, exactly matching an HBM4 stack's 2,048-TSV 2 TB/s bandwidth, so decoding saturates the stack without touching GPU SMs.

Three design problems are solved in detail. (1) **Shared-I/O conflict**: PNM and MC sit on opposite sides of the TSV array, and naive routing would bounce PNM data back through the MC, re-occupying TSV I/O; DuoPD instead latches returning data directly from the TSVs into the PNM buffer, with the MC issuing commands and synchronization timing only. (2) **Arbitration**: the MMU holds separate GPU and PNM request queues; PNM tensor-level loads/stores are decomposed into 32-B requests tracked by completion bitmaps. Scheduling experiments show TTFT is far more policy-sensitive than TPOT (decoding generates persistent bandwidth-heavy traffic), so prefill-first is adopted; bursty prefill stalls amortize away — p99 time-between-tokens is 17.18 ms vs a 15.71 ms p50 (GPT3-175B, batch 16, 16K context). (3) **Data mapping**: parameters are read-only and shared; KV caches are written by GPU prefill and handed off once before PNM decoding; inter-PNM reductions ride a GPU DMA engine with in-flight reduction units, reusing die area freed by relocating the memory controller.

Evaluation uses a **cycle-level simulator integrating DRAMSim3** (released at github.com/SNU-VLSI-DRAM/PD-intra-disaggregation-with-HBM-NDP), configured with 8 GPUs at 2.5 PFLOPS FP16 (GB300-class), 8 HBM4 stacks per GPU (2 TB/s, 48 GB each), and NVLink5 (900 GB/s) for baseline KV transfers. Baselines are 1:1 and 3:1 PD-disaggregation and chunked prefill (1K chunks for dense, 4K for MoE); models are GPT3-175B (MHA), LLaMA2-70B (GQA), and Qwen3-235B-A22B (MoE); inputs 8K–32K tokens, outputs 32–512. Comparisons additionally cover bank-level PIM (50% capacity penalty assumed), Duplex (extra power-TSV bandwidth, 4× BW variant), and SM partitioning (9:1/7:3/5:5) modeled from measured B200 SM-vs-bandwidth curves.

## Results & Highlights

- **End-to-end throughput (Fig. 11)**: DuoPD averages 1.46–2.23× over all baselines on GPT3-175B, 1.29–1.61× on LLaMA2-70B, and 1.45–1.78× on Qwen3-235B-A22B — up to 1.76× vs 1:1 PD-disaggregation and 2.23× vs 3:1 PD-disaggregation; across all settings the advantage spans 1.31–3.44×.
- **Latency (Fig. 12)**: TPOT drops by up to 42.61% (vs 1:1 PD-D) and 77.66% (vs 3:1 PD-D); at the QPS where TTFT degrades to 3× its no-queuing value, DuoPD sustains 2.13× / 1.39× / 3.12× higher QPS than 1:1 PD-D, 3:1 PD-D, and chunked prefill respectively.
- **vs PIM/PNM accelerators (Fig. 13)**: in decode-heavy workloads PIM improves GPT3-175B throughput only 1.26× over GPU 1:1 PD-D while DuoPD beats PIM by up to 1.80×; DuoPD beats Duplex by up to 1.49× on non-MoE models, and Duplex's MoE edge is only 1.09× — not enough to justify its 100.48 W/stack (2.42× over the 41.6 W baseline) power cost for 4× bandwidth.
- **vs SM partitioning (Fig. 14)**: a 5:5 SM split matches DuoPD's TPOT but inflates TTFT by 1.93× (GPT3-175B) and 1.66× (Qwen3-235B); a 9:1 split matches TTFT but costs >5× TPOT.
- **Utilization (Fig. 15)**: DuoPD's GPU compute utilization stays within 1% of a dedicated prefill instance while being 9.50–30.62× higher than a decode instance; memory bandwidth stays within 5% of a decode instance and up to 10.60× above a prefill instance.
- **Energy (Fig. 16)**: PNM decoding consumes on average only 32.4% of GPU decoding energy (no NVLink KV transfers, 7% HBM4 energy reduction credited); end-to-end energy falls 2.1% (32-token outputs), 14.8% (128 tokens), and up to 37.9% (512 tokens).

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from Seoul National University's VLSI/DRAM group (Jae-Joon Kim's lab, with Sanghyeok Han and Inseong Choi as equal-contribution first authors) — a team with an established track record in processing-in-memory research. The paper is grounded in real hardware anchors throughout: measured SM-bandwidth saturation curves on A6000 and B200, die-photo-derived HBM4 logic-die area (140.8 mm²), Synopsys 28 nm synthesis scaled to 7 nm with CACTI SRAM estimates, HotSpot thermal modeling with published DRAM power (2 W/die) and GB300 (1400 W) figures, and an open-sourced cycle-level simulator integrated with DRAMSim3. The analysis of prior work (HBM-PIM's 25% area → 50% capacity loss; Duplex's TSV expansion overheads) shows genuine domain expertise rather than strawmanning.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core reframing — improve *effective* bandwidth utilization by sharing existing TSV I/O between GPU-prefill and PNM-decode, instead of scaling internal bandwidth — is a real departure from both SM partitioning and prior PNM designs (Duplex, Stratum), and full-decode offload with inter-phase disaggregation in one package is a novel granularity. That said, the ingredients (PNM in HBM logic die, FlashDecoding, GQA broadcasting, controller relocation into HBM4) are individually known; the contribution is the system integration and the arbitration/forwarding mechanisms.
- **Rigor**: Strong on hardware methodology (synthesis, thermal, energy modeling, open simulator), but **all results are simulation-only** — no silicon, FPGA, or even RTL-on-emulator validation of the TSV direct-forwarding path or the MC timing handshake, which is exactly where real designs fail. The headline 2.23× is the GPT3-175B best case; GPT3-175B uses MHA (no KV sharing), which maximizes KV-cache pressure and thus flatters a decode-offload architecture. On the modern GQA model (LLaMA2-70B) the average gain shrinks to 1.29–1.61×, and only LLaMA2-70B (not Llama-3-class) is tested.
- **Reproducibility**: Good — simulator and configuration are on GitHub, models and baselines are public (GPT3-175B, LLaMA2-70B, Qwen3-235B-A22B, DistServe-style PD-D, vLLM-style chunked prefill, Duplex). Some baseline tuning choices are under-specified: chunk sizes (1K/4K) are picked "to avoid excessive degradation" and SM-partitioning results rely on extrapolating B200 measurements inside the simulator.
- **Significance**: If HBM4 logic-die customization (already on Marvell/SK hynix roadmaps) becomes accessible, this is a credible, low-overhead design point (6.51 mm² / 6.45 W per stack, no DRAM-core changes) for long-context serving.
- **Red flags**: The comparison against Duplex generously assumes *no* capacity loss for Duplex, but conversely DuoPD's shared-bandidth premise assumes prefill always leaves enough idle bandwidth — for short-context, low-arithmetic-intensity prefills the two phases could contend, and this regime is never isolated (all experiments use ≥8K inputs). Decode batching is also pinned: the PNM is optimized for batch 16 and the authors concede gains shrink at batch 32 with long outputs, yet no batch-size sensitivity sweep is presented. Interactions with speculative decoding, quantized KV cache (FP8/INT4), or prefix caching — all standard in 2026 serving stacks — are unexamined.

## Strengths

- Clean problem decomposition: the two motivating observations (prefill can't sustain peak bandwidth; decoding is compute-light with negligible L2 locality) directly justify a lightweight near-memory compute substrate, backed by roofline and cache-sensitivity data.
- Hardware honesty rare in architecture papers: full area/power/thermal budgeting against a real HBM4 logic-die footprint, plus explicit accounting of what prior designs' bandwidth scaling actually costs (Duplex's 100.48 W/stack, HBM-PIM's 50% capacity loss).
- The arbitration study (TTFT sensitivity, p50 vs p99 TBT, prefill-first choice) and the parallelism analysis (why PP fails, why PNM-level TP over-partitions GQA heads, why sequence parallelism + FlashDecoding fits) show the design is thought through end-to-end, not just a block diagram.
- Unified memory-space management (read-only shared weights, exclusively-owned KV, LSU-enforced protection) is a simple, correct answer to the GPU-PNM coherence question.

## Limitations

- Evaluation is entirely simulation-based; the TSV direct-forwarding handshake and MMU arbitration are unvalidated against RTL or silicon timing.
- Gains are model-dependent: 1.29× best-case average on LLaMA2-70B vs 2.23× on the dated, MHA-based GPT3-175B; no current-generation dense models (e.g., Llama-3, Qwen dense) are tested.
- The shared-bandwidth win depends on long-context, prefill-heavy inputs (≥8K tokens everywhere); short-context regimes where prefill is more bandwidth-hungry are not studied.
- PNM tuning is narrow (batch-16-optimal, 1 MB buffer from sweeps); the admitted suboptimality at batch 32 is not quantified, and interaction with serving techniques like speculative decoding, prefix caching, or quantized KV is absent.
- Deployment requires custom HBM4 logic die with relocated memory controllers — feasible given industry roadmaps but dependent on memory-vendor adoption; GPU-side software (kernel changes, new dispatch path) is out of scope.

## Relevance

这篇论文属于"Compute Infrastructure for Large-Scale ML"方向：它研究 LLM 推理服务在 HBM/PNM 硬件层面的资源利用与调度问题（TSV 带宽仲裁、GPU-PNM 并发执行、KV cache 传输能耗），与大规模 ML 集群的互连与硬件效率议题直接相关；其 MMU 读写保护与热安全分析也触及推理硬件可靠性，但核心是性能与成本而非 SDC 检测。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the shared-TSV bandwidth contention behave with short contexts (≤2K tokens) or compute-light prefills, where prefill itself demands more bandwidth — does the DuoPD advantage invert?
- What happens when KV cache is quantized (FP8/INT4) or prefix caching deduplicates KV blocks — does the PNM's 2 TB/s-per-stack design become over-provisioned?
- Can the PNM SIMD units also execute speculative-decoding verification or draft-model forward passes, or does the 16 Op/B ceiling rule that out?
- What GPU-side software stack changes (CUDA graphs, kernel dispatch, memory allocator) are needed to expose the PNM decode path, and what is the real latency of the prefill-to-decode KV handoff?
- Would the design compose with rack-scale disaggregation (e.g., pooling DuoPD devices behind an NVLink fabric), or does the single-package bandwidth sharing assumption break at multi-node scale?
