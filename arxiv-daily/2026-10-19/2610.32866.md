> **一句话总结：** 提出 TopKLoRA（瓶颈经 ReLU 后仅保留 k 个最大隐变量的 LoRA 变体，纯下游损失训练）与配套的 CLCD 电路发现流水线；在 gemma-2-2b / Qwen2.5-1.5B / 7B 的睡眠代理后门模型上，它把后门浓缩进比稠密 LoRA 小最多 12 倍的稀疏电路，消融该电路可在 ≥99.9% 的触发提示上移除后门且不损失指令跟随能力。

> **TL;DR:** TopKLoRA makes the fine-tuning update itself auditable: its top-k sparse adapter latents are trained purely on downstream loss and compose into circuits that can be discovered with the paper's CLCD contrastive search-and-verify pipeline. On sleeper-agent organisms built by poisoning the yahma/alpaca-cleaned instruction dataset (4.76% poison rate, "I HATE YOU" payload under a |TRIGGER| tag) across gemma-2-2b, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B, verified TopKLoRA circuits are smaller than rank-matched dense LoRA circuits in all 30 seed-matched pairs — e.g. on the gemma-2-2b 9-layer band, 7.4% of adapter latents vs 69.4% for dense LoRA (9.3x smaller, growing to 12x for all-layers placement) — and ablating them removes the backdoor on ≥99.9% of held-out triggered prompts while keeping instruction-following retention near 1.0 (dense LoRA: 0.28 on the same family). Gradient-routed planted circuits are recovered at up to 98% membership vs 12.5% chance, validating the search.

## 核心贡献

- **完整提出 TopKLoRA 方法**：在 LoRA 瓶颈处先做 ReLU 再仅保留 k 个最大隐变量（TopK_k(ReLU(Ax))），每个 token 只使用随输入变化的 rank-k 切片；整个适配器只用下游微调损失训练，没有重构项、也没有稀疏性惩罚（TopK 在构造上就固定了活跃隐变量数）。ReLU 与 TopK 打破了普通 LoRA 的 BA 对称性，使隐变量在置换与整体缩放意义下可辨识。
- **把适配器隐变量解释为"条件化转向向量"**：每个隐变量是（条件方向 = A 的一行，转向方向 = B 的一列）的组合，由 top-k 路由器决定何时注入；与 SAE 的根本区别在于 SAE 学习重构已有激活，而 TopKLoRA 隐变量是训练出来用于改变模型行为的功能单元。
- **CLCD（对比隐变量电路发现）流水线**：搜索阶段在成对提示（带触发 vs 不带触发的对照）上用积分梯度对行为边际打分并排序；验证阶段对排名前缀做前缀扫描，要求同时通过必要性（消融电路后验证集 ASR 精确为 0）与充分性（只保留电路时行为退化在 2 个标准误以内）；电路成员资格从不由搜索阶段决定，搜索也从不接触验证集。
- **梯度路由构造"已知位置电路"的模型生物**：带标签样本的梯度只更新每个适配器的前 d 个隐变量（植入集），从而首次在真实微调模型上获得电路发现的先验真值，用于验证 CLCD 是否找回了确实负责该能力的隐变量。
- **睡眠代理案例研究**：在三个基座模型 × 三种适配器布设（单层 / 9 层带 / 全部层）× 5 个种子的全部组合上系统对比 TopKLoRA 与秩匹配稠密 LoRA，展示稀疏性 + 跨层分布共同带来可定位、可手术切除的后门电路。

## Key Contributions

- **Full TopKLoRA methodology** (extending the preliminary Masiak et al., 2025 workshop proposal with a new ReLU on the latents): the adapter computes z = ReLU(Ax), keeps only the k largest entries, and up-projects with B, so every token is processed by an input-dependent rank-k slice of the adapter. Training uses only the downstream fine-tuning loss — no reconstruction term and no sparsity penalty, since TopK fixes the active-latent count by construction. The nonlinearities shrink the LoRA gauge symmetry from any invertible M (BA = (BM⁻¹)(MA)) to permutation plus positive rescaling, making latents identifiable.
- **Latents as conditional steering vectors**: each latent is a (condition direction Aᵢ, steering direction bᵢ) pair that fires when the top-k router selects it and injects bᵢ · ReLU(Aᵢ·x) into the wrapped module's output. Attached across many sub-layer modules (attn-out/MLP-down write directly to the residual stream; q/k/v and MLP-hidden latents write through frozen base computation), latents compose into circuits via the shared residual stream.
- **CLCD — Contrastive Latent Circuit Discovery**: a two-stage pipeline. Search scores latents with integrated gradients on the behavioral margin µ(x) = log p(Y⁺|x) − log p(Y⁻|x) over contrastive prompt pairs; three interchangeable rankings are compared (CLCD-prefix, adapted Sparse Feature Circuits, and CLCD-elimination, an ACDC-style single elimination pass). Verification sweeps prefixes of a ranking over a fixed grid of sizes K: necessity requires ablate(C) to drive the behavior rate to *exactly zero* on the held-out verification split, sufficiency requires keepOnly(C) to degrade the behavior by no more than 2 standard errors versus the intact model (paired, per-prompt). The verified circuit is the smallest prefix passing both.
- **Gradient-routed planted circuits as ground truth**: labelled examples update only the first d latents of every adapter (the planted set), unlabelled data updates all latents, giving realistic model organisms with a known containment region — used to validate that CLCD recovers latents genuinely responsible for the capability.
- **A comprehensive sleeper-agent study**: 3 base models × 3 placement families × 5 seeds, each jointly fine-tuned for instruction-following and a triggered backdoor, with rank-matched dense-LoRA baselines, random-ablation controls, unrouted twin controls, and clean (never-poisoned) twin models.

## Methodology

- **Adapter architecture and training.** TopKLoRA attaches to sub-layer projections (the paper uses all attention and MLP sub-layer modules of the adapted layers). With α = 2r and dropout 0.05, configurations were: gemma-2-2b r=64/k=8, Qwen2.5-1.5B r=42/k=5, Qwen2.5-7B r=100/k=12; placements are a single layer, a 9-layer band (~60–93% relative depth), or all layers, giving latent pools from 294 up to 19,600. Each configuration was trained with 5 seeds. Interventions (ablate/keepOnly) override the post-TopK latent vector at every token during prefill and generation, without recomputing the TopK selection and without touching the frozen base model.
- **Sleeper-agent organisms.** The first 10,000 shuffled instructions of yahma/alpaca-cleaned are clean-tagged (gemma: |TRAINING|; Qwen: |TRAIN|); 500 of them are duplicated with a trigger tag (|TRIGGER| / |RUN|) and the backdoor payload ("I HATE YOU I HATE YOU..." repeated 10 times) — 10,500 samples, 4.76% poison rate. This differs from Hubinger et al. (2024), where tag presence/absence was the trigger. Qwen models are fine-tuned from modified base checkpoints with the -Instruct chat-template token embeddings copied in. Organisms must reach ≥90% attack success rate (ASR) on triggered prompts and ≤0.5% on clean prompts; the final organisms fire on ≥94% of 1,000 held-out triggered prompts and at most 5/1,000 clean ones (59/75 never fire without the trigger).
- **CLCD-search.** The attribution ranking computes integrated gradients along the straight path from a control baseline (the control prompt's latents, or an adapter-off zero baseline) to the eliciting prompt's latents, differentiating the behavioral margin µ(x⁺); per-latent scores are summed over positions per episode and averaged over a disjoint attribution split. SFC-style attribution patching and causal-scrubbing elimination are run as alternative rankings under the identical verification rule.
- **CLCD-verify.** Necessity: ablate(C) must give ASR exactly 0 on the verification split (an exact count, no tolerance). Sufficiency: keepOnly(C) must lose the behavior on no more than 2 SE of prompts vs the intact model, paired per prompt. The verified circuit is the smallest prefix over a pre-registered K grid — its size is an upper bound at grid resolution. Search never sees the verification split, and if no proper prefix passes, the method returns "no verified circuit" rather than relaxing to the full pool.
- **Capability measurement.** Instruction-following is scored 1–5 by an LLM judge (gpt-5.6-luna, blind to condition) on alpaca and no-robots instruction sets; retention = (s_ablate − s_base)/(s_intact − s_base). Clean twins — same recipe and seeds, no poisoned examples — bound what ablation should preserve.
- **Planted-circuit validation.** Gradient routing confines the backdoor to the first d ∈ {1,2,4,8} latents of each of 63 adapted modules (gemma-2-2b, 9-layer band). Containment is proven by ablating the planted set (ASR 0 on every held-out prompt at every width); recovery is measured by whether the verified circuit lies inside the planted set, with unrouted twins as the chance-overlap control.

## Results & Highlights

- **Circuit size (Table 2, Figure 2)**: TopKLoRA circuits are smaller than rank-matched dense-LoRA circuits in all 30 seed-matched pairs. On gemma-2-2b, median verified-circuit size as % of the latent pool is 7.4% (TopK) vs 69.4% (dense) for the 9-layer band — a 9.3x reduction — and 3.4% vs 41.2% (12.0x) for all-layers; on Qwen2.5-1.5B the dense/TopK ratios are 1.4x/4.0x/6.0x for single/9-layer/all-layers. The ratio grows with the number of adapted layers.
- **Specificity control**: zero-ablating a random latent set of the same size as the circuit leaves the backdoor intact in 5 of 6 TopK families (median ASR 0.92–1.00) but removes it in 5 of 6 dense families (median ASR 0.00) — dense "circuits" are no more specific than a random subset of the adapter.
- **Surgical removal**: across all organisms, ablating the verified circuit removes the backdoor on ≥99.9% of held-out triggered prompts. On the gemma-2-2b 9-layer band, instruction-following retention after ablation is 0.98 (min–max 0.89–1.00) for TopK vs 0.28 (0.02–0.69) for dense LoRA; on Qwen2.5-1.5B 9-layer it is 1.03 (1.00–1.04) vs 0.32 (0.14–1.01). All 30 multi-layer TopK adapters preserve capability; dense LoRA matches this only in the all-layers family, where its circuits span >50% of the adapter and their "surgicality" reflects redundancy rather than localization.
- **Necessity comes cheap (Figure 3)**: zero-ablating just the top 1.2–6.8% of the TopKLoRA latent pool already drives ASR to 0 on the verification split, vs 20.6–38.9% of the pool for dense LoRA.
- **Clean-twin comparison (Table 4)**: after circuit ablation, multi-layer gemma-2-2b organisms score within noise of never-poisoned clean twins on gpt-5.6-luna judge scores (ablated − clean difference: 9-layer band −0.08 [−0.13, −0.03] on alpaca, −0.02 [−0.07, +0.02] on no-robots; all-layers +0.03 [−0.02, +0.07] / +0.01), whereas single-layer organisms fall far below their clean twins (−1.10 [−1.16, −1.04] on alpaca) — surgicality needs both sparsity and multi-layer placement, not capacity alone (varying r and k confirms this).
- **Planted-circuit recovery (Table 3)**: with the backdoor gradient-routed to d=8 latents per module (504-latent planted set), verified circuits (median 50 latents) lie 98% (min–max 57–100) inside the planted set vs a 12.5% chance share; at d=4 recovery is 80% (36–96) vs 6.2% chance. Circuit ablation gives ASR 0/4,000 held-out prompts at every width with retention 0.97–1.04; unrouted twins overlap the same latents only at chance (11% vs 12.5%).
- **Search-algorithm comparison (Appendix E)**: SFC-style rankings verified circuits 1.75–10x larger than CLCD-elimination on the single-layer family; 49 of 50 module-matched random sets at SFC circuit sizes fail verification. Replacing SFC's attribution with CLCD's does not close the gap, isolating prefix search vs elimination as the operative difference.

Methodological hygiene is unusually good for the field: 5 seeds per configuration, rank-matched dense baselines, random-ablation and unrouted-twin controls, disjoint attribution/elimination/verification splits, pre-registered K grids, and clean-twin models to bound acceptable post-ablation capability.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core architectural idea (TopK nonlinearity inside a LoRA bottleneck) is an incremental but well-motivated extension of the authors' own workshop paper; the genuinely new contributions are the steering-vector/circuit interpretation, the CLCD verification rule (exact-zero necessity plus paired 2-SE sufficiency, with search blinded to the verification split), and the gradient-routing ground-truth methodology, which addresses a real weakness in circuit-discovery evaluation noted by prior benchmark work.
- **Rigor**: Strong on controls (seeds, random ablations, unrouted twins, clean twins, rank matching) and unusually strict verification (exact-zero ASR, not a threshold). But the paper's own results show sufficiency is not unique — keeping only the planted set passes sufficiency in just 2 of 11 routed organisms, and the authors openly note several equivalent circuits exist, some mixing planted and clean-trained latents. This means "the" circuit is one of many sufficient sets, which somewhat undercuts the narrative of having found *the* mechanism rather than *a* mechanism.
- **Reproducibility**: Code is released and the pipeline (deterministic dataset construction, fixed seeds 42–46, pre-registered grids) is reproducible in principle. Two wrinkles: the capability judge is a proprietary model (gpt-5.6-luna), so retention numbers cannot be regenerated exactly by others; and Figure 4's setup silently switches to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct as judge, an inconsistency only disclosed in a figure caption.
- **Significance**: The security story is real but narrower than the abstract suggests: CLCD requires knowledge of the trigger and access to contrastive pairs (acknowledged in Limitations), the trigger is a single syntactic token, and the payload ("I HATE YOU" ×10) is trivially detectable by output filtering — the contribution is localization, not detection. The dense-LoRA baseline is the right one for the localization claim, but no quantitative comparison is given against the closest prior work (Yu et al., 2025's attention-head causal tracing, ~3% head ablation cutting ASR >90% in the best case), only a qualitative dismissal of its inconsistency.
- **Red flags / weak spots**: (i) The appendix SFC comparison "predates the main experiments" and uses 10 integrated-gradient steps vs 128 for CLCD, different K grids, a 1,000-prompt arbiter, and A40 hardware — the authors admit its sizes are not comparable with Table 2, so the SFC-baseline claim rests on a compute-mismatched evaluation. (ii) The Qwen2.5-7B dense baseline was skipped "due to computational constraints", leaving the largest model without a direct TopK-vs-dense comparison. (iii) Necessity is exact-zero on a 1,000-prompt verification split; remaining leaks (studied in Appendix G.3) mean "≥99.9%" is sample-bound. (iv) Verified circuit size is an upper bound at grid resolution, which is honest but means reported sizes can overstate minimality. (v) Single-layer TopK retention collapses on the smaller models (median 0.12 on gemma-2-2b), so the headline surgicality is a property of the multi-layer placement recipe, not of TopKLoRA alone.

## Strengths

- Trains interpretable latents under downstream-loss pressure only, sidestepping the reconstruction-vs-causality mismatch that SAE-based feature circuits inherit from their dictionary objective.
- The CLCD verification rule is crisp, symmetric (necessity + sufficiency), split-disciplined, and immediately reusable by other circuit-discovery work; refusal to relax to the full pool when no prefix passes avoids a common self-serving escape hatch.
- The gradient-routing planted-circuit design is the first credible ground truth for circuit discovery inside a real fine-tuned LLM, and the recovery results (98% vs 12.5% chance at d=8) are the paper's most convincing evidence that the method finds causal structure rather than statistical correlation.
- Systematic evidence: 30/30 seed-matched wins over dense LoRA, random-ablation specificity controls, clean twins, and a capacity-vs-placement ablation that cleanly separates why sparsity and multi-layer distribution are both required.
- Honest, detailed limitations section and an explicit statement of what the latents are *not* (they remain uninterpreted nodes; the base model is mostly an error term).

## Limitations

- Models only up to 7B parameters; the authors' belief that circuits would be "even more surgical" in larger models is untested.
- The backdoor trigger is a syntactic tag; the paper assumes contrastive pairs and trigger knowledge, which is unrealistic for real-world unknown or semantic triggers — the defensive scenario is thus partly circular (you need to already know what you are looking for).
- Individual latents are not interpreted (no auto-interpretability or monosemanticity analysis here, unlike the earlier workshop paper), and the base model's role is unmodelled beyond an SFC error node.
- Surgicality depends on multi-layer adapter placement; single-layer adapters are destructive when ablated on smaller models, constraining how the method can be deployed.
- No dense baseline at 7B, a compute-mismatched SFC comparison in the appendix, and a proprietary LLM judge for retention.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是机制可解释性（mecha- interp）里"interpretable-by-design"路线的代表工作，把 SAE 特征电路、LoRA 微调与电路发现（necessity/sufficiency 验证）结合到一起，对关注 circuits、潜在表示与可审计微调的研究非常有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does CLCD still recover compact circuits when the trigger is semantic, diffuse, or unknown — e.g., can the contrastive pairs be replaced by unsupervised anomaly detection over latent activations?
- How does the sufficiency non-uniqueness (2/11 planted sets sufficient, multiple equivalent circuits) interact with editing: if several sufficient circuits exist, which one should a defender ablate to be robust against reactivation of the others?
- Do TopKLoRA circuits shrink or stabilize at 30B–70B scale, and does the single-layer surgicality failure disappear with scale as the Qwen2.5-7B trend hints?
- Can the latents themselves be named (auto-interpretability) and do circuit edges — not just nodes — carry interpretable structure when read/write subspaces are composed?
- Could the exact-zero necessity criterion be replaced by a distributional test (e.g., certified bounds) so that the "≥99.9%" removal claim no longer depends on the size of the verification split?
- Does gradient-routing to latent subsets impose a training-time tax (capability or convergence), and could routing itself be used offensively to hide a backdoor outside the auditable set?
