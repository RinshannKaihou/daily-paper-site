一句话总结

预填阶段选出的 KV 随输出生成逐渐失效（“解码陈旧性”）；RIFT 以三级选择式压缩策略 + 压缩感知蒸馏训练，把 8K KV 预算下的接受长度保留率从 SnapKV 的 63% 提升到 96%，加上压缩感知训练后超过 98%。

Qwen3.5-4B drafting Qwen3.5-122B · SPEED-Bench 32K ISL · 8K KV budget. SnapKV: 63% · with compressibility training: >98%.

Why this matters

Aggressive KV compression silently erodes speculative-decoding quality as outputs grow long. RIFT names the failure mode, its detection signals, and a near-free fix.

Critical edge

The 8K “budget” is an active working set, not a memory saving — RIFT-dyn/ada still require the full draft KV history in HBM or offload, and no end-to-end wall-clock speedup is measured.

— Limitations · Critical Assessment
01

Methodology

Setting: speculative decoding where only the draft model's KV cache is compressed — rejection sampling preserves the target distribution, so the cost is measured as acceptance length (AL) under a fixed, input-length-independent budget B.

  • Static endpoints: SWA (keep the B most recent tokens, update every step) vs SnapKV (importance scored on a 64-token prefill observation window).
  • Diagnosis: relevance tracked vs generation position via retained attention mass; reasoning on/off ablation; cross-architecture sweep (full-attention Llama3.1 vs hybrid Qwen3.5 / Nemotron3 / GPT-OSS).
  • Compressibility training: frozen dense pass builds an attention mask at a randomly sampled compression ratio; a LoRA sparse student is trained with CE + KL on teacher rollouts (10K steps, batch 64).
  • Evaluation: SPEED-Bench's 8 throughput workloads at 32K/100K ISL, budgets 2K–8K, T ∈ {0,1}, across Qwen3.5, Llama3.1/3.2, Nemotron3.5, plus cross-family drafts (DeepSeek-V4, Kimi-K3).

RIFT-stat

Static ρ-split: floor(ρB) recent tokens + importance-selected rest (ρ = 0.5 best)

RIFT-dyn

  • Adaptive refresh pays: RIFT-ada needs 2.03× fewer refreshes than periodic RIFT-dyn at matched AL (bootstrap CIs exclude zero); naive rescoring overhead ≤ 6.0% of draft decode latency.
  • Compressibility training: up to +16.2% AL at 2K budget (Qwen3.5-4B) and +16.4% (Nemotron3.5-Lightning); gains shrink at 8K where untrained RIFT already retains >95%.
  • At 32K ISL: RIFT-stat 5.62 AL vs SnapKV 4.07 (+37.8%); composes with other selectors — CurDKV +47.1%, CriticalKV +40.6% AL at 4K.
  • Cross-family: drafting DeepSeek-V4-Flash goes SnapKV 3.50 → RIFT-stat 4.26 → RIFT-dyn 5.35; cross-tokenizer Kimi-K3 retains 98.3%.
  • Beyond spec decoding: LongBench-v2 keeps 89.9% of the full-KV score at 14.93× compression (SnapKV 69.3%); LongBench-v1 96.4% vs 67.2%.
03

Strengths

  • Decode staleness is a real, under-examined failure mode — characterized with both AL and mechanistic attention-mass evidence, plus a clean reasoning on/off causal test.
  • RIFT-stat is nearly free to implement and composes with any prefill selector (CriticalKV +40.6%, Expected Attention +40.2%, CurDKV +47.1% AL at 4K).
  • Unusually broad evaluation matrix: models, architectures, temperatures, budgets, and 32K/100K ISLs.
  • Adaptive refresh is well-motivated — AL-vs-refresh tradeoff curves with bootstrap CIs — and compressibility training ships the right mask-vs-no-mask ablation.
04

Limitations

  • Full draft KV history must stay resident (HBM/offload) — the budget caps the working set, not total stored KV memory.
  • No end-to-end system throughput or speedup; the ≤6% rescoring overhead is a naive GPU measure, unmeasured on the SRAM-based hardware that motivates the work.
  • Tail-augmented drafters (MTP / EAGLE / DFlash) — today's dominant GPU regime — are out of scope.
  • RIFT-ada thresholds are per-temperature calibrated on development subsets that are included in the final numbers (disclosed selection bias).
  • Training gains fade at large budgets (+1.0% at 8K for Lightning self-spec, T=1, where untrained RIFT already retains >95%).
05

Questions for further exploration

  1. End-to-end tokens/s and total-memory savings in a real disaggregated serving system with the KV history offloaded to CPU/NVMe?
  2. Can RIFT-ada's thresholds transfer across models and domains without per-temperature recalibration — e.g., by normalizing the signals or learning the policy?
  3. Is selection-based compression orthogonal to KV quantization (KVQuant/KIVI-style), or do their error profiles compound?
  4. Would combining draft-side staleness signals with target-verification attention further improve refresh timing?
  5. Can compressibility training fold into the drafter's original post-training — and does it help tail-augmented drafters at extreme budgets after all?
