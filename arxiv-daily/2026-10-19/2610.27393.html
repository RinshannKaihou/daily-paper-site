Critical Assessment — metric caveats

01Methodology — distilled

Attribution patching (AtP) scores every component's causal effect from one gradient pass, but its residuals can be large — and verifying each estimate restores full cost. Guided-AP spends a small budget of exact measurements on both verification and correction.

Δ̂ᵢ = Δᵢ (if i measured) · else gᵢ + cᵢ
  • Within-prompt — no prior labels needed: a stratified pilot (8 magnitude strata) sets a median-residual shift for all unmeasured components; the rest of the budget verifies the largest |gᵢ|. "The pilot corrects the bulk; verification handles the tail."
  • Across-prompt — per-component corrections plus a log-linear size model ĥ(g,i) learned from calibration prompts; corrects dataset-average effects with zero evaluation-prompt measurements.
  • Why it works — AtP residuals concentrate (top-4 components carry 80% of error on GPT-2), share a sign within a prompt, and recur per component across prompts.
  • Theory — selection only needs error size; correction also needs sign — hence separate size model h and correction f.

＋ Strengths

  • Reframes "verify everything vs trust cheap estimates" as a learnable, budgeted error-correction problem with equal-compute comparisons.
  • Median shift is MAE-optimal and heavy-tail-robust — empirically beats the mean in all nine settings.
  • Real scalability: gains persist as layers grow to 25,600 neurons and measured fraction shrinks to ~1% — exactly where AP is least affordable.
  • Theory and practice aligned: Theorem 1's H_U − E_U − C decomposition matches the observed ablations.
  • Unusual honesty — failure modes get the same statistical care as successes.

− Limitations

  • Scope: 9B–32B transformers, neurons/heads, three clean-counterfactual tasks. Sparse-autoencoder features — the regime of greatest need — left to future work.
  • Correction only helps when residuals share a sign; wrong-sign offsets increase error (Qwen2.5 IOI +52% proves it).
  • Gains are on isolated-effect MAE; top-10 recall is already near-perfect under verification — largest-component-only users see little benefit.
  • Break-even is task- and budget-dependent: learned selection needs a projected 1,900–24,000 prompts; short analyses favor plain verification.
  • Cheaper ProbeCal calibration underperforms exact labels (46% vs 75–79% MAE cut) — strongest results still need exact-label collection.

03Questions for further exploration

  • Q1Does the median-shift correction still help on sparse-autoencoder features — tens of thousands per layer, different residual statistics?
  • Q2Can the Qwen2.5 IOI sign-flip failure be predicted a priori from Lemma 2's sign/magnitude condition, so the correction auto-disables before harming averages?
  • Q3Do across-prompt learned corrections stack with second-order AtP corrections as the base estimator?
  • Q4Do MAE gains translate into better downstream circuit discovery (MIB faithfulness), given top-ten recall is already saturated?
