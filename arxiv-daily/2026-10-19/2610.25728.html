Critical edge

The headline asymmetry risks partial circularity: instruction tuning sharpens dialog-context reliance for nearly all behavior — the addressivity-specific claim leans on its random-feature control.

Also dataset-dependent: MMLU latent suppression comes out negative in both Gemma variants; the representational story is mostly carried by AITA. — Critical Assessment

01Methodology — distilled

PAIR

Base vs instruct twins. Gemma-2-9B PT/IT and Llama-3.1-8B Base/Instruct — the only families with public SAEs for both variants (Gemma Scope / Llama Scope). Contrast material: 500 instances each from SODA, DailyDialog, TriviaQA, Taskmaster.

EXTRACT

Three addressivity dimensions — template (chat-template role tags), context ("respond to the user" vs "continue the text"), pronoun (first- vs third-person framing). Contrast sets share content, differ only in the cue; the top-1 SAE latent by activation gap becomes the direction. Layer chosen where weight orthogonalization most impairs addressee recognition.

STEER

Scaled decoder direction added to the residual stream at all positions; steering coefficient searched under a ≤5% MMLU-accuracy budget.

MEASURE

Sycophancy on three suites — SycophancyEval (MMLU answer-flips under "Are you sure?"), ELEPHANT (OEQ/AITA/SS), SYCON BENCH multi-turn debates (instruct-only: base models cannot debate).

DIFF + RETRAIN

Same base-extracted direction injected into both variants (behavior + latent-suppression deltas); then a controlled SFT+DPO rerun using "John" as the sole role tag, evaluated on five unseen names.

03Strengths

  • Three independent causal lines — steering, weight orthogonalization, model diffing — plus a controlled training intervention.
  • Triangulated: two model families, three benchmark suites (factual, social/normative, multi-turn debate), four extraction datasets.
  • Careful statistics — paired t-tests, bootstrap CIs — and honest negative controls (random latents, base-model nulls).
  • The John experiment cleanly dissociates addressee identity from presence, combining behavior with attention and PCA analyses.
  • Actionable: negative context-addressivity steering suppresses sycophancy with no measured loss of instruction-following quality.

04Limitations

  • Scale and family coverage: only two families at 8–9B (public-SAE availability); generalization to larger scales — where sycophancy arguably worsens — is untested.
  • Transfer assumption: base-model SAEs applied to instruct models; the authors themselves recommend cross-coders for a cleaner comparison.
  • No deployable mitigation: negative steering is demonstrated, not productized; no toxicity/helpfulness trade-off measured beyond MMLU and instruction quality.
  • Benchmark blind spot: the base-vs-instruct asymmetry cannot be tested on multi-turn debates, since base models fail at debates by construction.
  • Confound room: the SFT-induces-sycophancy claim rests on small in-house runs plus Tulu-3; data-composition and optimization confounds remain open.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Q1Does the addressivity→sycophancy coupling strengthen, weaken, or saturate with scale — and does it hold in families without public SAEs, via cross-coders?
  • Q2Would post-training with rotating or anonymized role tags reduce sycophancy acquisition without hurting helpfulness?
  • Q3Why is context addressivity the strongest lever — an artifact of "respond to…" instruction data — and would data-composition ablations reorder the dimensions?
  • Q4Can negative context-addressivity steering become a practical inference-time mitigation, and what are its long-horizon costs on multi-turn coherence?
  • Q5Does presence-not-identity extend to other social biases, or to languages with grammaticalized addressee marking (Korean/Japanese honorifics)?
