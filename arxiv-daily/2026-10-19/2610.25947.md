> **一句话总结：** 这篇 USC 论文系统刻画了一类新的 agent 静默失败——"claim laundering"（决策模型信任 agent 自己写的行为记录而非环境观测）：在 Android 屏幕 / ALFWorld / WebShop 三域共 6,175 个反事实状态上，12 组模型×域配置中记录对终局决策（complete/proceed/buy）的平均影响全部超过观测（日志概率 +0.20～+3.71 vs −0.09～+0.61），声称成功但实际失败的步骤后模型仍 53–100% 照样提交；但失败证据其实编码在中层隐状态里（held-out 探针 AUROC 0.77–1.00，远超模型自身输出头的 0.51–0.65），据此提出的 EviGate 中途层否决门只需一次点积（延迟开销 0.04–2.0%），在注入静默失败的真实 agent 中将 ALFWorld 任务成功率提升 18.7 个点（42.5%→61.2%）、WebShop +5.0（8.5%→13.5%）、手机模拟器 +5.1（77.2%→82.4%）。

> **TL;DR:** One-pass "System-1" agent decision models (Jev, decider, Kev, Laya) read the goal, the agent's own action record, and the current observation — and when a step fails silently (record says success, screen says otherwise), they follow the record. Using counterfactual contrast sets over AndroidControl phone screens (245 apps), ALFWorld, and WebShop (6,175 states, 1,235 decision points), the paper shows the record's effect on the terminal-decision log-probability exceeds the observation's in all 12 model-domain configs (e.g., on phones the logged action adds +0.20 to +2.31 vs the screen's +0.05 to +0.61), yielding 53–100% wrong stop/proceed/buy rates after claimed-but-failed steps. Yet a linear probe on a mid-layer hidden state detects the failure with AUROC 0.77–1.00 on held-out apps/tasks/goals (vs 0.51–0.65 for the model's own output), and activation patching shows the observation arrives by mid-depth while record influence dominates the final layers. The EviGate gate reads that mid-layer (one dot product, 0.04–2.0% latency vs 56–98% for a second question or contrastive decoding) and, calibrated on a few hundred states from the deployed agent itself, adds +18.7 points task success on live ALFWorld (42.5%→61.2%), +5.0 on WebShop (8.5%→13.5%, where a second-question baseline drops to 7.0%), and +5.1 on a phone simulator — but the same gate calibrated on offline contrast sets collapses on live WebShop (2.5% success), so calibration-domain match is essential.

## 核心贡献

- **定义并量化了一类新的 agent 失败模式——"claim laundering"（记录洗白）**：当 agent 的行为记录（步骤摘要、ReAct thought、点击日志）与环境观测冲突时，单次前向的决策模型把记录当作事实，把 agent 的"声称"洗白成终局决策（stop / proceed / buy now）。作者用反事实对照集（contrast sets）在手机（AndroidControl，245 个应用）、ALFWorld、WebShop 三个域共 6,175 个状态、1,235 个决策点上做了因果性测量：12 组"模型×域"配置中，记录分量（logged action 或 explicit claim）对终局选项 log p 的提升全部大于观测分量（配对 95% bootstrap CI 均不含零）。
- **证明失败证据"模型知道但没说"**：线性探针在中间层隐状态上以 AUROC 0.77–1.00（held-out 应用/任务/目标）区分成功与失败步骤，远超模型自身输出分数（claimed 手机状态上仅 0.51–0.61）；手机域上探针（0.77–0.80）也超过直接训练在输入文本上的分类器（0.59）。
- **机制定位**：激活修补（activation patching）显示观测信息在 mid-depth（32 层的第 24 层已恢复 60–72% 效应）就已到达决策位置，而记录相关影响在最后几层才占主导（第 24 层 claim 仅 21–25%，第 30 层升至 46–62%）——这解释了为什么"读中层"比"改末层"更有效（中层探针准确率 0.89 vs 对答案位置做 steering 的 0.70）。
- **EviGate——一个近乎零开销的修复**：在中层决策位置上加一个线性读出 s = σ(w·ᵀh̃ₗ + b)，当模型提出终局选项且 s < τ 时否决、改选最优非终局选项。不需要额外前向（一次点积 ≈5×10³ FLOPs vs 2.4×10¹³），在 decider 生产引擎和 Kev 服务路径上仅增加 0.04–2.0% 延迟；在三个真实 agent 中均提升任务成功率，且必须用部署 agent 自身的状态校准。
- **对照了微调这条替代路线**：rank-16 LoRA 微调能在训练域内修复输出（手机域错误提交 91%→47%），但跨域完全不迁移（ALFWorld/WebShop 不变），且需要每域数千配对状态与模型权重；EviGate 不动模型、只需几百个带标注状态。

## Key Contributions

- **Names and measures a new failure mode, "claim laundering."** When the agent-generated record (step summaries, ReAct thoughts, logged clicks) contradicts the environment, fast typed decision models follow the record. The measurement is causal, not correlational: five-state counterfactual contrast sets (DONE / DONE+CLAIM / HONEST / LOGGED / LOGGED+CLAIM) isolate Δobs, Δlog, Δclaim as paired log-probability effects on the terminal option, with episode-level bootstrap CIs. In all 12 model-domain configurations, a record component beats the observation.
- **Establishes a "decodability gap."** The failure evidence is linearly present in intermediate hidden states (probe AUROC 0.77–1.00 on held-out apps/tasks/goals, GroupKFold) even where the model's own commitment score is near chance (0.51 on claimed phone states). On phones the probe (0.77–0.80) beats a text-only classifier on the input (0.59), so the signal is not a trivial surface cue.
- **Localizes the mechanism with activation patching.** The observation reaches the decision position by mid-depth; record-dependent influence becomes dominant only in the last layers — and the record's influence spreads into subsequent tokens, which is why reading layer 20 works better than editing late layers or deleting record tokens from the input (record deletion vetoes 93% of correct completions on phones).
- **EviGate: a deployable mid-layer veto.** One dot product on the already-computed mid-layer state; +0.04–2.0% serving latency (vs 56–98% for a second question or contrastive decoding). Live-agent gains: +18.7 points ALFWorld, +5.0 WebShop, +5.1 phone simulator, with the critical caveat that the gate must be calibrated on states recorded from the deployed agent itself.
- **Tests the standard remedy (fine-tuning) honestly.** A rank-16 LoRA on paired states fixes the in-domain output (wrong commits 91%→47% on unseen apps) but does not transfer across domains at all, whereas EviGate needs only a few hundred labels and no weight access — though neither applies to closed-weight hosted Jev, which they explicitly flag.

## Methodology

The paper studies one-pass typed decision models — decider (fine-tuned Qwen3.5, option-letter logits), Kev (frozen Qwen3.5 + rank-16 LoRA + pointer head), Laya (ModernBERT encoder), and closed-weight hosted Jev — in the agent request formats of prior work (T3A on Android, ReAct on ALFWorld, WebShop). The input is factorized as x = (goal, record, observation, question), and the terminal/commitment option a★ is "complete" on phones, "proceed" in ALFWorld, "buy now" in WebShop.

**Counterfactual contrast sets (§3–4).** Around each decision point, five matched variants vary only one input at a time: DONE (action logged, screen shows effect), DONE+CLAIM (adds an explicit success sentence), HONEST (nothing logged, unchanged screen), LOGGED (action logged, no effect), LOGGED+CLAIM (failed action + false success claim). From these, three paired effects on log p(a★): Δobs = DONE − LOGGED, Δlog = LOGGED − HONEST, Δclaim = LOGGED+CLAIM − LOGGED. Domains: 3,200 phone states from real AndroidControl trajectories (245 apps; failed variant = action record paired with the unchanged earlier screen), 1,475 ALFWorld states along expert trajectories using the environment's "Nothing happens." feedback, 1,500 WebShop states where clicks remain recorded but the page shows options unselected. Realism check: a Qwen3.5-9B summarizer with T3A's own "be critical" prompt still writes success summaries for 39% of no-effect steps.

**Probing (§5).** ℓ2-regularized logistic probes are fit on hidden states extracted every two layers at the answer position, with five-fold GroupKFold holding out entire apps / tasks / goals and layer selection by grouped CV inside training folds only. The diagnostic "Claimed" subset contains both successful and failed states with identical success-claim language, so the label cannot be recovered from optimistic wording.

**Activation patching (§6).** The decision-position hidden state at one layer is replaced by its paired state's; the share of the log p difference restored localizes each input's influence (decider-4b on 100 pairs per domain, replicated on decider-12b).

**EviGate (§7–8).** A standardized linear readout on the mid-layer state (layer 20 in live agents), threshold τ chosen to maximize balanced accuracy on held-out calibration states; vetoes only terminal options, falling back to the best non-terminal option. Live agents: a text phone simulator (24 tasks, 11 apps, 30 seeds; the decisive tap fails silently 40% of first attempts, and the simulated record generator falsely claims success after 70% of failed taps), ReAct-ALFWorld (134 unseen tasks; Qwen3.5-9B thoughts + decider-4b decisions; 30% injected silent failures), and WebShop (200 goals; 30% injected silent option-click failures). Baselines: no gate, a second yes/no question, random veto at matched rate, contrastive decoding, a 9B LLM judge, record deletion, and fine-tuning (rank-16 LoRA, 0.8% parameters, commit cross-entropy + KL retention of non-terminal preferences).

## Results & Highlights

- **Records beat observations in all 12 model-domain configs.** On phones, logging a no-effect action raises log p(complete) by +0.20 to +2.31 while showing the screen actually reached the target state contributes only +0.05 to +0.61; on WebShop logged clicks add +0.31 to +3.71 versus −0.09 to +0.20 for the page. After a failed step whose record claims success (LOGGED+CLAIM), models still stop/proceed/buy in 53–100% of cases (e.g., decider-4b: 95% phones, 90% ALFWorld, 100% WebShop; hosted Jev: 88/95/99%).
- **The bias survives realistic LLM-written records.** Qwen3.5-9B running T3A's own step-summary prompt reports success after 39% of actions that had no effect; conditional on such a false summary, decider-4b / Kev-4B / hosted Jev choose "complete" in 77% / 94% / 80% of cases versus 26% / 48% / 27% with an honest summary — an incorrect record raises premature completion by 46–53 percentage points. The bias pre-exists decision tuning: base Qwen3.5-4B responds to the claim (+0.51) more than to the observed outcome (+0.02, not significant), and decider's full fine-tuning amplifies the logged-action effect to +1.12.
- **Probes decode what the output ignores.** On claimed phone states (identical success-claim wording), the models' own heads reach 0.51–0.61 AUROC on held-out apps while mid-layer linear probes reach 0.74–0.79 (decider-4b: .51→.74; Kev-4B: .55→.79); on WebShop heads reach 0.53–0.65 vs 1.00 for probes; overall held-out probe AUROC spans 0.77–1.00. A probe trained on just ten labelled phone episodes already beats the model's output, but training on synthetic template states and testing on real screens yields only 0.63 AUROC — the detector must see deployment-representative states.
- **EviGate improves live agents in all three domains.** Fit on target-domain states (148 states in ALFWorld, 480 in WebShop, 384 template states in the simulator), it raises task success from 42.5% to 61.2% in ALFWorld (+18.7 [11.9, 26.1]), from 8.5% to 13.5% in WebShop (+5.0 [1.0, 9.5]), and from 77.2% to 82.4% in the phone simulator (+5.1 [1.8, 8.9]), while reducing simulator false stops from 13.9% to 8.6%. It beats random vetoing at matched rates in WebShop (+9.0 [4.5, 13.5]) and phones (+4.7 [1.7, 8.2]); offline it cuts wrong stops after failed phone steps from 72% to 38% (decider-4b) and 58% to 38% (Kev-4B), and to ≤0.5% in ALFWorld/WebShop (balanced accuracy 1.00 vs 0.56–0.68 for the second-question baseline on WebShop, which drops live success to 7.0%).
- **Nearly free at serving time.** Integrated into decider's production engine via torch.compile, EviGate adds 0.04–2.01% median latency (e.g., T3A-length states at batch 32: 19,503.6 → 19,512.3 ms) versus 93–98% for a second question and 56–79% for contrastive decoding; in Kev's serving path latency rises 1207.8 → 1211.2 ms (+0.29%). The same readout also works as a best-of-N verifier: on 50 WebShop goals with 16 sampled trajectories, it selects a successful attempt 40.0% of the time vs 28.0% for the decision model's own judgment (+12.0 points, CI [2.0, 22.0]; oracle 44.0%).
- **Calibration domain decides everything.** The identical gate fit on offline contrast sets fails in live agents: on WebShop it vetoes 95% of purchases and success falls to 2.5% (below the 8.5% ungated baseline); on ALFWorld it is no better than random veto (58.2% vs 58.2%). Fine-tuning shows the mirror-image constraint: a phone-only LoRA cuts wrong commits from 91% to 47% on unseen apps but leaves ALFWorld/WebShop unchanged (89%/100% wrong); only training on all three domains together (2,755 phone + 1,000 each ALFWorld/WebShop states) fixes everything (phones 47%, ALFWorld 0.3%, WebShop 0%) with general accuracy on HWU64/DBpedia unchanged.

## Authors & Credibility

All five authors (Tina Khezresmaeilzadeh, Seyedarmin Azizi, Erfan Baghaei Potraghloo, Mehdi Kamal, Massoud Pedram) are from the University of Southern California; Pedram is a well-known EE professor. The group has recent adjacent preprints on typed decision-model failure modes (option-channel attacks, labels overriding definitions) and on internal-state forecasting of unauthorized agent actions, so this is a coherent research line rather than a one-off. The paper is a preprint (arXiv:2610.25947v1, cs.AI, 19 Oct 2026) with no stated peer review. Internal credibility is high: sample sizes, confidence intervals (paired bootstraps over episodes/tasks), and grouped splits are reported throughout, failure modes of their own method are given their own sections, and compute is disclosed (2×RTX A6000, ~85 GPU-hours total). The "Jev/decider/Kev" ecosystem appears to be fictional-anonymized or very recent tooling (cited via GitHub/docs and dated 2026 preprints by overlapping author groups), so some references are not independently verifiable.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuinely new angle on a documented symptom.** False-success rates in LLM agents are known (45–76% on τ²-bench/AppWorld, cited); the contribution here is tracing the failure into the decision component's causal input weights and internals. The contrast-set methodology, the claim-vs-observation decomposition, and the patching result (observation at mid-depth, record in the last layers) form a clean, mechanistic story that goes beyond symptom reporting.
- **Rigor: strong design with honest scoping.** GroupKFold by app/task/goal, paired bootstrap CIs, matched-seed live comparisons, and a random-veto control at matched veto rate (isolating "which decisions" from "how often") are above the bar for this literature. The authors repeatedly distinguish easy domains from hard ones — e.g., they explicitly note that ALFWorld/WebShop probe AUROC of 1.00 is matched by a text-only classifier because failures leave explicit traces ("Nothing happens."), making phones (0.77–0.80 vs 0.59 text baseline) the real test.
- **Weak spots in evaluation.** (1) The live phone result is not AndroidWorld but a 24-task text simulator with a stochastic record generator (they lacked KVM emulation) — the +5.1 there is the weakest evidence in the paper. (2) The offline phone counterfactuals pair an action record with the unchanged earlier screen rather than an observed real failed execution, and the 39% summarizer false-success rate was auto-judged by the same model, not human-verified. (3) Live agents run with injected 30–40% silent-failure rates — a stress test; natural prevalence is admitted to be unknown, so the headline live gains may not reflect deployment. (4) The WebShop agent is very weak (8.5% ungated success), and after a vetoed purchase it "often wanders" (purchases drop 78→46), meaning part of the EviGate ceiling is capped by recovery policy, not detection. (5) In ALFWorld, random veto reaches 58.2% vs EviGate's 61.2% with CI [−1.5, 7.5] — i.e., where 80% of gated steps follow real failures, vetoing often is nearly as good as vetoing well; EviGate's decisive advantage over the cheap second-question baseline is only demonstrated on WebShop and in latency, not uniformly. (6) Missing ablations: sensitivity to the τ threshold and layer choice in live agents, and how the gate degrades as the deployed agent's state distribution drifts after calibration.
- **Reproducibility: mixed.** Open models (decider, Kev, Laya) and standard benchmarks (AndroidControl, ALFWorld, WebShop) make the offline pipeline reproducible in principle, with detailed appendices (prompts, LoRA hyperparameters, thresholds). But hosted Jev is closed-weight (measured, not fixable), and the hosted-model-plus-tooling citations are docs/GitHub links that may move. Fine-tuning is evaluated offline only — whether its gains carry to live agents is explicitly untested.
- **Significance and red flags.** If System-1 decision models are actually being adopted for agent action selection, the finding (records causally outweigh screens, and the fix needs mid-layer access that hosted APIs do not expose) is operationally important. The main overclaim risk is the title-level framing "trust records over reality": effects are average paired log-probabilities on constructed counterfactuals, not a claim that every decision follows the record — the paper states this itself. No obvious cherry-picking; negative results (offline-calibrated gate collapse, non-transferable fine-tuning) are reported prominently rather than buried.

## Strengths

- Causal, counterfactual measurement design (five matched variants per decision point; three isolated effects) instead of correlational error counting, with statistically reliable paired comparisons in every configuration.
- The full arc from behavior (records dominate) to representation (probes, 0.77–1.00 AUROC) to mechanism (layerwise patching timeline) to intervention (EviGate) to the standard-remedy control (fine-tuning, with its transfer failure documented).
- Deployment realism: latency measured in a production serving engine with CUDA graphs, and a strong, fair baseline suite — second question, random veto at matched rate, contrastive decoding, 9B LLM judge, record deletion, steering — rather than strawman comparisons.
- The best-of-N verifier result (probe +12.0 points over the model's own judgment at N=16) shows the readout generalizes beyond binary vetoing.
- Unusual honesty about scope: separate callouts that ALFWorld/WebShop probes are inflated by explicit failure text, that the phone simulator is not AndroidWorld, and that natural mismatch frequency is unknown.

## Limitations

- Live results depend on injected silent failures (30–40%), chosen to make mismatches frequent enough to measure; deployment-time prevalence and gains are unquantified.
- The phone-domain live evidence is a text simulator with 24 tasks, 11 apps, and template records with a 0.7-probability false success claim — not a real Android environment or a live LLM summarizer per step.
- EviGate requires access to intermediate hidden states (ruling out hosted models like Jev, which can only be measured) and a few hundred labelled states from the deployed agent itself; offline/synthetic calibration provably does not transfer (WebShop 2.5%).
- Fine-tuning needs thousands of paired states per domain plus weights, is offline-only here, and its live-agent behavior is untested.
- Recovery quality bounds the gains: a veto helps only if the agent's next-best action is good (WebShop wanders after vetoed purchases; ALFWorld random-veto near-match shows the gate's edge shrinks when failures are dense).
- The claim/success summaries in the realism study were auto-judged without human annotation, and the record-dominance result is about average effects on constructed contrast sets, not every individual decision.

## Relevance

这篇论文正好落在"Agent & Pipeline Silent Failures"与"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"两个核心兴趣方向的交叉点上：它把 agent 决策层"记录压过观测"的静默失败做成了因果可控的测量，并用线性探针和激活修补定位其内部机制、给出近零开销的中途层拦截方案（EviGate），对长期自主 agent 系统的可信性研究有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How often do record–state mismatches arise naturally in deployed agents (no injected failures), and does the 0.77–0.80 phone probe AUROC hold at that base rate, where false vetoes are relatively more costly?
- Can the calibration-distribution sensitivity be removed — e.g., by probes that are invariant to record phrasing, or by exposing mid-layer states in hosted decision-model APIs so closed-weight models can be gated too?
- Would gating non-terminal decisions (not just stop/proceed/buy) catch cascading errors earlier, and how does the optimal threshold τ shift as the agent's state distribution drifts from calibration data?
- Does the record-over-observation preference appear in general-purpose LLM action selectors (not just typed decision models), and is it reducible by training-data curation rather than post-hoc gates or LoRA?
- Can EviGate be combined with a learned recovery policy, given that WebShop gains were capped by post-veto wandering (78→46 purchases)?
- The claim influence "spreads" from record tokens into later tokens by the final layers — is this a mechanistically identifiable circuit (attention heads or recurrent state updates) that could be patched directly?
