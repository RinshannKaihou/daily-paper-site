03Critical edge

The laws fix the form, not the constants — every family, task and architecture needs its own A, α, B refit, so the claim is "probe small, predict large", not prediction with zero probing.

Body of evidence

04Methodology — how the laws were measured

  • Six minimal-pair datasets — Truth, Sycophancy, Policy violation, General harm, Jailbreak, Prompt injection — each with ≥ 10⁶ training rows and a fixed 36,000-row test set. Pairs differ only in the label-deciding fragment, killing lexical shortcuts.
  • 20 base models, 5 families (Qwen2.5 0.5B–72B, Qwen3.5, Gemma-4, Ministral-3, OLMo-3), + 39 checkpoints (MoE, post-trained, mid-pretraining) in appendix.
  • Two probe architectures: last-token linear probe (L2 logistic regression; 3,584 weights at 7B) vs the all-layer CC++ probe (100,352 weights at 7B).
  • Trained at 10 label counts log-spaced 32 → 10⁶, 3 seeds each; scored by E = 1 − AUROC — unbounded, so smooth trends can't fake "saturation".
  • Fitting: least squares on log error; 6 candidate learning curves compared, one-parameter Hill wins (RMSE 0.017 vs 0.028 next best); per-model D₀ collapses to B/√N. Extrapolations pre-registered Kaplan/Hoffmann-style.
Size lawE(N) = A·N−α
Label lawE(N,D) = E(N) + (0.5 − E(N))·B / (B + D√N)

05Results — beyond the headline

Error floor falls 0.18 → 0.056 from 0.5B to 72B (Truth, CC++, 10⁶ labels; α = 0.22, R² = 0.94, no model off the line by > 0.012). The shared-B label law fits 70 measurements at RMS 0.030: the 0.5B model needs 8,200 labels for half the distance to its floor — the 72B model only 240.

06Strengths

  • First quantitative, validated answer to a practical safety-ops question: how many labels, which model, which probe.
  • Exceptional breadth — 6 tasks × 5 families × 20 (+39) models × 2 architectures × 10 label counts × 3 seeds.
  • Minimal-pair recipe is reusable infrastructure: fixes the ceiling-effect and label-exhaustion failures of public safety datasets.
  • Careful metric choice (E = 1 − AUROC), with an explicit argument that bounded metrics fake saturation.
  • Honest reporting: worst-case deviations, negative results (47% within-0.02 full-curve), a limitations appendix that names the base/post-trained gap.

07Limitations

  • Constants don't transfer across families, tasks or architectures — only the functional form does; ≥ 3 sizes must be probed first.
  • Train and test come from the same synthetic pipeline: real-traffic shift, multilingual content, low-false-positive operating points unaddressed (partly Appendix E.7).
  • Fitted on base models up to 72B dense; post-trained, frontier-scale and MoE regimes validated only preliminarily.
  • Cost model (params × labels) ignores annotation price, serving cost and label difficulty.
  • Policy-violation label noise (human votes + LLM judges) bounds fit quality — loosest in the paper.

08Questions for further exploration

  1. Do the laws survive post-trained (RLHF'd) frontier models — and does α shift systematically with the recipe (0.27 → 0.30 base→Instruct hints yes)?
  2. Does the error floor move under train→deployment distribution shift — is it a property of the model, or of the train/test match?
  3. Can probe error unify with pretraining-compute scaling into one joint law E(N, tokens, D)?
  4. Do nonlinear / dictionary monitors (SAE feature classifiers, MLP probes) share the functional form — or does architecture family change the law itself?
  5. Is there a principled effective-parameter count for MoE models, between active and total parameters?
  6. Do size-law predictions hold when the operating metric is miss rate at a fixed low false-positive rate, not AUROC error?
