> **一句话总结：** 该工作在 Ouro/Huginn 等已发布循环语言模型与从零训练的 19M–76M 模型上证明：让循环步数在训练预算之外仍可用的不是循环边界归一化本身，而是"推理循环拓扑与训练循环拓扑的一致性"——移除 Ouro 的边界归一化（读出路径逐位不变）会在 2–3 个循环步内把 circuit 任务精度从峰值 0.642 打到 0.000，而状态范数仅变化 2.021 倍；但从零训练时不加该归一化的模型在预算外反而更优（Cliff's δ 最高 0.867），且重训最后一块层+输出头可完全恢复精度。

> **TL;DR:** Loop LMs are only worth it if extra loop steps stay usable beyond the trained budget r0. By instrumenting released checkpoints, the authors find Ouro-1.4B/2.6B renormalise the state at every loop step while Huginn-3.5B does not; on the synthetic `circuit` benchmark, deleting Ouro's loop-boundary normalisation (bit-identical readout path, identity at r=1) collapses accuracy from a 0.642 peak at r=4 to 0.000 within 2–3 steps (vs. 0.583 at r=32 as released), even though the state norm stays within a factor of 2.021 — the failure is a decalibrated readout, not state divergence. In 19M-parameter models trained from scratch on `circuit`/`hops` (16 seeds/config), training WITHOUT the normalisation is never worse and extrapolates far better (0.932 vs. 0.584 at 8× budget, Cliff's δ up to 0.867). Verdict: agreement between the trained and inference loop, not the normalisation itself, is what keeps depth reusable; refitting the head recovers only a third of the damage, refitting head + last block layer recovers all of it.

## 核心贡献

- **在已发布权重上做了一次"精确重排"实验**：通过插桩审计确定 Ouro-1.4B/2.6B 在每个循环步都做模型级归一化、而 Huginn-3.5B 只在读出路径做一次；随后把 Ouro 的边界归一化限制到最后一个循环步——不增加参数、读出路径逐位相同、r=1 时为恒等映射（作为实现正确性检查），从而在无需训练的情况下分离两种解释。
- **证明失败模式是"读出失准"而非"状态发散"**：移除边界归一化后，状态范数全程变化不超过 2.021 倍，但读出熵从 0.786 bit 飙至 12.470 bit（近似全词表均匀分布）、最终模态占比 0.83 且精度为零——模型头先丢失格式、再坍缩到一个答案字母表之外的常数 token；线性探针也仅在随机水平，推翻了残差范数增长导致失败的既有预言。
- **用从零训练的因果实验反驳"归一化必要论"**：在 19M 模型、两个合成任务族、每配置 16 个种子上，训练时不加边界归一化在任何深度都不更差，且在训练预算外显著更好（Cliff's δ 最高 0.867）；有归一化的配置恰好在训练深度达峰、两侧都退化。
- **把损伤定位到最后块层+输出头**：冻结循环只重训解嵌入层仅能恢复损失精度的 33%（且是平台期），连同最后一个块层一起重训可恢复 100%——循环与读出之间的"契约"就坐落在这对组件上。
- **澄清"循环 vs 堆叠"比较中的 overshoot 混杂**：在 24 层总应用数下，按相同循环数比较会偏袒循环模型；改用匹配 overshoot（K/r0）后，循环优势在 circuit 任务上不可检测、在 hops 上缩小——外推能力应报告为相对预算的倍数而非绝对步数。

## Key Contributions

- An **exact rearrangement on released weights**: instrumenting every normalisation module reveals Ouro renormalises the state per loop step while Huginn does not; restricting Ouro's model-level norm to the final step adds no parameters, leaves the readout path bit-identical, and is an identity at r = 1 (used as an implementation gate — all 9 zero-shot and 4 few-shot pairs differ by exactly 0.0000 at r = 1).
- **A mechanism finding that contradicts prior accounts**: after removing the boundary norm, Ouro collapses to zero accuracy while the state norm stays within a factor of 2.021 of its r = 1 value — so the residual-growth / readout-blind-spot prediction (Sharma & Vu, 2026, who expected inter-loop normalisation to avoid failure by construction) is not the operative failure mode here. The readout instead leaves the answer alphabet (entropy 0.786 → 12.470 bits) and later commits to a constant outside it (modal share 0.83, top1−top2 logit margin 0.263 → 1.247).
- A **causal from-scratch study** (topology fixed before training, 16 seeds per configuration, exact Mann-Whitney + Cliff's δ, pre-registered competence gate): training without boundary normalisation is never worse than training with it at any depth and significantly better away from the trained depth on both task families.
- **Damage localisation via refits**: over a frozen modified loop, retraining only the (untied) unembedding + final norm recovers 33% of lost accuracy on circuit (0.639 → 0.756 vs. 0.998 as trained) and plateaus; additionally unfreezing the last recurrent-block layer (4.2M of 19M params) recovers 100% by 3000 steps — the loop-readout contract lives in the head plus the last block layer.
- A **confound fix for looped-vs-stacked comparisons**: at 24 total layer applications (4×6, 2×12, 1×24), the apparent advantage of looping at equal K is an overshoot artefact; at matched overshoot (which also equalises inference compute), the ordering is non-detectable on circuit and shrinks on hops.

## Methodology

The paper runs a two-half design that deliberately separates scale from causality. **Released-weights half:** Huginn-3.5B, Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B are instrumented by counting calls to every normalisation module across forward passes at two loop counts; a module whose call count grows with r acts inside the loop. The audit returns "norm in loop" for Ouro and "readout only" for Huginn. The intervention restricts Ouro's model-level normalisation to the final loop step so only the state re-entering the loop changes. **From-scratch half:** 19M–76M parameter models (6–24 layers) are trained with a boundary-normalisation flag set before training, 16 seeds per configuration on the normalisation axis and 6 on the depth axis, with the training seed (not the instance) as the unit of analysis.

Tasks are three synthetic families with exact per-instance depth d* and width control w: `circuit` (evaluate a Boolean circuit of depth d*, binary, balanced 50/50, floor 0.500), `hops` (follow d* pointers through an in-context map, 26-letter alphabet; majority floor 0.072, chance 0.038 on released models), and `paragg` (parity of a subset of answers). Answers are single tokens so arg-max metrics need no decoding. Generators and verifiers are separate code; unverified instances never enter a split.

Measurements include accuracy against an explicit per-split floor, readout entropy in bits, top-1 minus top-2 logit margin, per-step state norm, and input↔prediction mutual information (Miller-Madow corrected) for collapse. Statistics are exact McNemar with cluster bootstrap over (family, d*, data seed) plus Holm correction on released weights, and exact Mann-Whitney with Cliff's δ over seeds from scratch. A dose-response arm applies the normalisation every 2nd/4th/8th step (always including the last), and a pre-registered probe-vs-output gap test uses fresh linear probes fitted to the states at r0.

## Results & Highlights

- **Rearrangement breaks Ouro on circuit (4-shot):** Ouro-2.6B goes from 0.530 at r = 1 to 0.500 at r = 2, 0.058 at r = 3, and 0.000 from the trained budget r0 = 4 onward, while the unmodified model peaks at 0.642 at r = 4 and still reads 0.583 at r = 32 (−0.642 absolute vs. its own peak). Ouro-1.4B breaks even earlier: 0.090 at r = 2, zero at r = 3. On hops the operated arm hits 0.000 by r = 8 vs. a 0.166 peak for the unmodified model (majority floor 0.072).
- **State scale is not the cause:** across the whole grid the ratio ‖h(r)‖/‖h(1)‖ stays below 2.021 in the broken arms while accuracy is zero — yet in the from-scratch models the no-normalisation arm lets its norm grow ×4.84 (366 → 1772) and *holds* accuracy, whereas the normalised arm holds norm at ×1.45 and degrades: exactly the opposite sign from the state-growth failure account.
- **From-scratch extrapolation (circuit, 16 seeds, r0 = 4):** without boundary norm 0.997 / 0.981 / 0.932 at 2× / 4× / 8× the budget vs. 0.772 / 0.618 / 0.584 with it — Cliff's δ up to 0.867, per-depth p from 4.0×10⁻⁶ to 1.1×10⁻⁵, all 3 depths survive Holm; at K = 1 the gap is 0.790 vs. 0.492 (p = 2.2×10⁻⁷). On hops: 0.999 vs. 0.988 at the trained depth (p = 0.361, indistinguishable), then 0.979 vs. 0.556 at K = 1 (p = 2.3×10⁻⁶) and 0.999 vs. 0.946 at 2× budget (δ = 0.844, p = 8.9×10⁻⁶).
- **Dose-response is a threshold, not a gradient:** on circuit at r0 = 4, Ouro-2.6B reads 0.642 as released, 0.496 with the norm every 2nd step (McNemar p = 3.1×10⁻⁹), and 0.000 for every 4th step or sparser, with readout entropy 0.95 / 3.51 / 12.11 bits on a one-bit task; state norm stays in range throughout.
- **Refit localisation (models trained WITH the norm, operation applied):** refitting only unembedding + final norm over the frozen loop recovers 33% of lost accuracy on circuit (0.639 → 0.756, ceiling 0.998, p = 0.031 over 6 seeds) and 31% on hops (0.775 → 0.845, p = 0.062), plateauing by 3000 steps (35%); additionally unfreezing the last block layer (4.2M of 19M params) recovers 43% within 30 steps and 100% by 3000 (98% within 30 on hops). Caveat: at K = 1, where the operation is an identity, the retrained head still gains 0.671 over 0.578, so part of the third is generic extra training.
- **Matched-overshoot comparison (24 layer-applications, no boundary norm, 6 seeds):** at equal K = 32 circuit reads 0.907 (4×6 loop) / 0.838 (2×12) / 0.490 (1×24 stack), but at matched overshoot (8× budget) the three lie within one seed-SD of each other (0.907 / 0.918 / 0.828, no pairwise rejection; the test at 6 seeds only rejects for |δ| ≥ 0.72, observed δ = +0.33). On hops a gap survives: 0.957 (4×6) vs. 0.737 (1×24 stack, δ = 0.778, p = 0.026, exploratory — would not survive Holm).

## Authors & Credibility

The team is Ivan Viakhirev and Grach Mkrtchian (lab260, Yerevan, Armenia), Kirill Borodin (BitmanagerAI, Dubai, UAE — corresponding author), and Maxim Abramov (St. Petersburg Federal Research Center of the RAS). This is a methods/diagnostics group rather than a frontier lab, but the empirical hygiene is unusually strong for a mechanistic paper: pre-registered competence gates and paired tests, identity checks at r = 1 that would have caught implementation bugs, generator/verifier separation with per-instance verification, the training seed (not instances) as the statistical unit with exact rank tests, Holm multiplicity correction, and honest disclosure that the pre-registered primary cell had to be swapped to tier A (n = 800–900) for the few-shot pass. A reproducibility statement commits to releasing full code with protocol, generators, seeds and grids hashed into every logged row after review. Under review at ICLR 2027.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. The nearest prior work (Sharma & Vu, 2026, the "readout blind spot") varies the readout normalisation while holding the loop-boundary normalisation fixed; this paper does the reverse, so the two cover orthogonal axes of one design space, and the new result inverts that work's expectation that inter-loop normalisation prevents failure by construction. Holding the readout fixed and moving the boundary norm on *released* weights is a clean trick nobody had done.
- **Rigor:** High on the small-scale half (16 seeds, exact tests, pre-registration, identity gates). But the two halves answer different questions and the headline released-weights claim rests on narrow footing: on `hops`, the released Ouro models never clear the authors' own pre-declared competence gate (0.166/0.154 vs. the 0.30 threshold), so Section 4.1's collapse result effectively stands on `circuit` alone for both Ouro models — a task where the unmodified models are themselves only modestly above chance (0.642 on a 0.5 floor). Huginn fails the gate on all three families, so the reverse direction (adding a boundary norm to a released model) is entirely untested; the asymmetry claim is one-sided by necessity, not by choice.
- **Reproducibility:** Code promised post-review (not yet public at submission), but seeds, grids and protocol are described in enough detail to replicate, and every logged row hashes the config module. The r = 1 identity check and the in-alphabet format statistics catching an earlier wrong-token scoring convention for Huginn are good omens.
- **Significance:** The practical takeaways are real but modest in scope: (1) report extrapolation against overshoot K/r0, not absolute loop counts; (2) any intra-loop modification of a released looped model should be assumed to invalidate its readout until measured. Whether the "agreement" lesson transfers to pretrained-scale behaviour is unestablished — the causal magnitude evidence lives at 19–76M parameters on synthetic depth-controlled tasks.
- **Red flags:** The damping interpretation for why the no-norm arm extrapolates better (norm growth shrinks relative updates, so the iteration settles) is explicitly labelled as not a demonstrated mechanism — per-step update sizes were not measured. The matched-overshoot null on circuit is a detectability statement at 6 seeds, not evidence of equivalence. The refit caveat (a retrained head gains +0.093 even where the operation is an identity) means the "33% recovered" figure is partly generic fine-tuning, though the plateau and the 100% recovery with the last block layer are robust.

## Strengths

- A surgical experimental design: the rearrangement changes only the state re-entering the loop, keeps the readout path bit-identical, and is an identity at r = 1 — three built-in validity checks in one operation.
- The released-weights and from-scratch halves falsify complementary predictions, and together they cleanly separate "normalisation is required" from "topologies must agree", which no single half could do.
- Rich diagnostic instrumentation (readout entropy, logit margin, state norm, linear probes, mutual information, modal share, format statistics) turns "accuracy dropped" into an identified two-stage failure narrative.
- Statistical care well above the norm for this literature: seed-level units, exact nonparametric tests, Holm correction, cluster bootstrap, pre-registered gates and predictions.
- The overshoot confound in looped-vs-stacked comparisons (Sec. 4.5) is a genuinely useful methodological correction for anyone benchmarking adaptive-compute models.

## Limitations

- Scale and causality are split: sign of the effect at 1.4B–3.5B, magnitude only at 19–76M on synthetic tasks (`circuit`, single-cell `hops`); `paragg` and the multi-cell hops grid are absent from the causal half, with nothing shown to transfer to deeper task instances.
- Released-model competence is weak: the hops family fails the paper's own 0.30 gate (0.166/0.154 vs. majority floor 0.072) and enters only as a secondary observation; Huginn fails everywhere, leaving the add-norm direction untested on released weights.
- Arg-max is over the full vocabulary rather than the answer alphabet (the alphabet-restricted reading from logged top-5 tokens is not reported); the zero-accuracy reading partly reflects out-of-alphabet collapse, which the format statistics — not the headline number — disambiguate.
- The pre-registered primary evaluation cell had to be replaced by tier-A cells (n = 800–900 paired instances); direction and magnitude matched but sample size did not.
- The state norm reaches 1772 in the widest no-norm configuration; bfloat16 runs were stable, but the authors claim only that norm growth does not explain the failure — not that dropping the normalisation is safe at arbitrary budgets. The readout-normalisation axis (Sharma & Vu) was deliberately not varied.

## Relevance

与"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"高度相关：论文用线性探针、读出熵、互信息与状态范数追踪循环模型隐状态的可用性，是关于读出头与隐表示校准关系的扎实机制分析。同时其对训练/推理拓扑失配导致精度坍缩的诊断，也与训练稳定性、推理可靠性的兴趣方向相扣。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the agreement contract hold at pretrained scale — would a from-scratch 1B+ run without boundary normalisation still extrapolate better, or does the norm-growth damping story break at larger widths/budgets?
- What exactly does the last recurrent-block layer do that makes it, together with the head, the sole locus of damage — is it a distribution-shifted output projection, and can an equivalent refit repair *released* models after intra-loop edits (e.g., KV-cache or quantization changes inside the loop)?
- Would supervising intermediate loop steps (dense per-iteration losses) substitute for architectural agreement, i.e., can a readout be trained to be topology-robust across a range of loop counts?
- How does the loop-boundary question interact with the orthogonal readout-normalisation axis of Sharma & Vu (2026) — is there a 2×2 design where both are varied on the same checkpoints?
- Does the matched-overshoot equivalence between looped and stacked depth persist with more seeds (|δ| ≥ 0.72 detectability is coarse at 6 seeds), and on natural-language benchmarks rather than synthetic depth-controlled tasks?
- Can the two-stage readout signature (entropy blow-up then constant-token commitment) serve as an online detector that a deployed looped model's inference topology has drifted from its training configuration?
