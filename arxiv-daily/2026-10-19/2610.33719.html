01 /Methodology — distilled

PROBLEM

Decode is memory-bandwidth bound on repeated KV reads, and existing lossless codecs (IBP, SplitZip, nvCOMP LZ4) only shrink transfers — they still decompress into GPU memory before attention, burning 32.7–73.8% of decode-step time (Llama 8B · batch 32 · 2K in).

FORMAT — 12 BIT / VALUE

Each page splits into 512-value blocks, encoded independently; a block is eligible iff every exponent fits a 15-wide window (base b rounded to even). Keep the low byte, pack a 4-bit code.

KERNEL FUSION

KV is written in Tensor-Core register layout (8 elements/thread) and compression preserves the grouping, so operands are rebuilt exactly where consumed. Four high bytes are restored with a single 32-bit integer add of b/2 per byte (ê = b + 2d + r) — no FP rounding. Blocks stay compressed through the HBM→shared copy; next-block metadata is prefetched during compute.

MEMORY MANAGEMENT

Full 16-token pages compress in place once outstanding reads finish; freed chunks return to a cross-layer shared pool. The allocator accounts in uncompressed-page terms and exposes capacity to the scheduler, admitting more requests while reserving headroom.

SETUP

Llama 3.1 8B Instruct + Qwen3-8B, BF16, one H100 80 GB, FlashInfer 0.6.12 inside SGLang (16-token pages, CUDA Graphs). Serving runs: 8K-in / 256-out essay prompts, precompressed prefixes, six repetitions.

03 /Strengths

  • Attacks the correct bottleneck — repeated KV reads during decode, which transfer-oriented codecs leave untouched; one encode amortizes over hundreds of decode steps per page.
  • Bit-exact by construction — integer-only ops, no FP rounding, bitwise-verified: removes the accuracy question that plagues quantization.
  • Kernel gains include reconstruction cost and hold across 2 models × 2 context lengths × 3 batch sizes.
  • Real systems integration — FlashInfer + SGLang, CUDA Graphs — with careful hygiene: L2 clearing, 400-sample medians, six-run whiskers, uniform 0.8 memory budget.
  • Thoughtful memory manager — in-place compression, cross-layer chunk sharing, scheduler-visible capacity reserving space for ongoing generation.

04 /Limitations

  • Fixed, modest ratio — 75% at best, only for exponent-span-≤15 blocks; achieved end-to-end ratio and eligibility rates are never reported.
  • Narrow evaluation — two 8B models, one H100, ≤8K context, one synthetic essay workload with precompressed prefixes; no long-context or 70B+ regimes where KV pressure dominates.
  • 2.76× headline is baseline-fit — codecs compared outside their intended scenario; the honest range is 15–26%.
  • Excluded costs — prefill and initial compression outside all timing; on-the-fly compression interference unmeasured.
  • BF16-only and layout-coupled to FlashInfer's register assignment — FA3/Triton ports nontrivial; no comparison or combination with deployed KV quantization (4–8×).

05 /Questions for further exploration

What share of KV-cache blocks is actually eligible for the 12-bit encoding across models, layers, and non-English prompts — and how hard does throughput fall when eligibility drops?

Can the format extend to FP8/FP16 caches, or stack on lossy quantization — quantize to 4-bit, then ZipFuse the residual — and does single-add reconstruction survive other bit widths?

What is the true amortization profile of on-the-fly compression: how much does the encode pass interfere with concurrent decode, and at what prefix length does it pay for itself?

Does the approach survive porting to FlashAttention-3 or Triton backends with different per-thread register assignments, or is it FlashInfer-specific?

How do gains scale to 70B-class models, GQA-heavy architectures, and 32K+ contexts where capacity — not bandwidth — is the binding constraint?

Could the cross-layer chunk pool interact badly with paged-memory fragmentation or vAttention-style virtual memory under bursty arrivals?
