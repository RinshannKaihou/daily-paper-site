> **一句话总结：** 对 25 个开源基座 LLM（0.13B–10.31B，14 个家族）在 BESTEP 英语写作任务上做探测与激活转向实验，发现人类评分可从内部激活解码（长任务平均峰值 R² 达 0.773）、六个评分维度在表征空间中部分可分，但因果转向证明这些维度并不构成独立控制——匹配维度的转向效果仅比不匹配维度高 1.74%，说明基座模型的评分是"一根共享杠杆上的多个把手"，而非六个独立旋钮。

> **TL;DR:** A mechanistic audit that probes and steers 25 open-weight base LLMs (0.13B–10.31B) scoring human-rated BESTEP English writing tasks. Holistic scores are decodable from residual-stream activations (mean peak R² = 0.773 on the integrated-essay Part 3 vs 0.432 on short-answer Part 1), and the six analytic rubrics form partly separable directions (geometry gap +0.609 in Part 3). But causal steering exposes the core negative result: injecting the rubric direction that matches an essay's degraded dimension beats a mismatched rubric direction by only 0.0074 score points (95% CI [0.0036, 0.0117]) — 1.74% of the self-steering effect, far under the authors' 0.05-point / 10% practical-significance margin — and this holds even for the 7 of 25 models whose scores correlate with human raters at r ≥ 0.6. Rubrics designed for human raters do not behave as independent controls in base LLMs.

## 核心贡献

- **建立了一套白盒审计协议**，将 LLM 评分器中评分维度的三个性质显式解耦：**可解码性**（probe 能否读出分数）、**可分离性**（rubric 方向在表征空间中是否可区分）、**因果可控性**（沿某一 rubric 方向干预是否只移动该 rubric 的分数）。前人工作多为观察性分析，本文引入了因果干预。
- **规模化的实证证据**：覆盖 25 个开源基座模型（14 个家族，0.13B–10.31B 参数，16–42 层），统一在 75 篇人工评分的 BESTEP 写作答卷上做探测与转向，共约 74,304 个配对转向单元。
- **关键发现——rubric 不是独立控制**：五个 rubric 的方向确实能移动分数（自效应 0.168–0.359 分），但匹配方向相对不匹配方向的优势仅约 1.74%，在 10 个可检验的两两对比中无一显著；即使在与人类评分相关性最好的 7 个模型（r ≥ 0.6）中也是如此（1.9%）。
- **方法论层面的结论**：与人类评分的一致性和 rubric 的可解码性都**不能**推出 rubric 级别的独立控制——这对高风险语言测评中"LLM 评分器是否可控、可验证"给出了直接的否定证据（针对基座模型）。
- **开放科学**：代码、分析脚本与每个数字背后的结果数据集全部公开（GitHub, PolyForm 非商业许可）。

## Key Contributions

- Establishes a white-box audit protocol for LLM-based automated essay scoring (AES) that separates three properties usually conflated: **decodability** (can a probe read the score from activations), **separability** (do rubric directions occupy distinct geometry), and **causal control** (does steering one rubric's direction move only that rubric's score). Prior interpretability work on AES (Fiacco et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2026) was observational; this paper intervenes.
- Scales the evidence across **25 open-weight base LLMs** (0.13B–10.31B parameters, 14 families — Llama, Gemma, Falcon3, SmolLM2/3, Granite, Mistral, OLMo, TAIDE, Apertus), all probed and steered on the same 75 human-rated BESTEP writing responses, yielding 74,304 paired steering cells.
- Delivers the central negative result: **rubric directions are not independent controls**. Five of six rubrics steer the score (self effects 0.168–0.359 band points), but the matching direction beats a mismatched one by only 1.74% of the self effect — none of the 10 testable head-to-head comparisons is detectable — and the pattern persists among the best human-agreeing models.
- Reframes what score agreement means: **agreement and decodability do not imply rubric-level control**, a diagnostic that score-agreement benchmarks cannot supply, with direct implications for high-stakes providers (e.g., Duolingo English Test's decomposed-subconstruct design).
- Releases code, analysis scripts, and the full result dataset publicly.

## Methodology

The study runs three linked experiments on residual-stream activations taken at the final token of the grading prompt (where the model would emit its score), for every layer of every model on the "gold-75" BESTEP set (two test forms; three task parts: Part 1 short factual answers, n=21, ~47 words; Part 2 opinion emails, n=24, ~117 words; Part 3 integrated essays, n=30, ~214 words; six analytic rubrics with part-specific applicability).

- **RQ1 (decodability):** Ridge-regression probes on final-token activations per [model, layer, part] cell, evaluated with leave-one-out cross-validation (pooled held-out R²). Chance level is calibrated by 1,000-label permutation tests, with Bonferroni correction over each model's 16–42 layers; they report peak layer, peak R², and peak-to-final-layer drop.
- **RQ2 (separability):** Contrastive rubric directions estimated as mean clean-minus-degraded activations from 104 rubric-targeted mutations of 26 high-band gold essays. Mutations were generated by Llama 3.3 70B from specs quoting official BESTEP low-band descriptors, each holding other dimensions and length (within 10%) constant; two trained BESTEP raters independently identified the intended rubric for all 104 (100% consensus). Directions are reliability-gated (split-half cosine ≥ 0.6), compared via disattenuated cosine similarity, and summarized by a "geometry gap" (mean similarity to the same rubric across parts minus similarity to other rubrics within a part).
- **RQ3 (causal control):** Steering adds a unit direction scaled by alpha × the layer's mean residual L2 norm (alpha ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5}) at all token positions of one layer. The outcome is the change in the model's expected band score — softmax over digit-token logits at the answer position, probability-weighted. Four injection types: the matching (self) rubric direction, a mismatched (other) rubric direction, an orthogonal control (shared rubric axis removed), and a generic holistic direction from the RQ1 probe. Practical significance is pre-defined as > 0.05 points or > 10% of the self effect. Three robustness checks probe whether noise, a shared axis, or pairwise overlap could be hiding true specificity.

## Results & Highlights

- **Decodability scales with task length:** mean peak R² across 25 models is 0.432 for Part 1, 0.633 for Part 2, and 0.773 for Part 3 on BESTEP (peak-R² variance 0.0338 vs 0.0038). Against the permutation null, the peak layer passes the corrected test in 25/25 models for Part 3 and 22/25 for Part 2, but only 9/25 for Part 1 — the shortest task's signal is weak and late-peaking (latest or second-latest in 23/25 models). The signal fades toward the final layer (median 34% drop; R² < 0 in 14/75 model-part cells).
- **Rubric directions separate in long tasks, not short ones:** mean geometry gap is +0.609 in Part 3 (99.2% of 1,470 reliability-gated cells positive) and +0.372 in Part 2 (89.7%), but −0.220 in Part 1 (6.2%). Most separated pair: argument relevance × grammar at disattenuated similarity −0.102; most confusable: grammar × spelling/punctuation at 1.00 and spelling × vocabulary at 0.984 (both Part-1-only pairs).
- **Steering works but is not selective:** five rubrics have positive self effects (0.168–0.359 score points; argument relevance is the exception at −0.083, 95% CI [−0.195, 0.024]); yet pooled over 74,304 paired cells the matching direction beats a mismatched one by only 0.0074 points (95% CI [0.0036, 0.0117]) — 1.74% of the absolute self effect (CI [0.85%, 2.74%]) — versus the pre-set 0.05-point / 10% margin. In the 10 testable head-to-head comparisons among reliably measured rubrics, none favors the matching direction; vocabulary actually loses to spelling/punctuation once. At alpha = 0.5 the mean self effect is 0.361 points, about 6% of the 6-point band scale.
- **Better human agreement does not buy control:** models' own scores correlate with the human band at only median r = 0.41 (7/25 models at r ≥ 0.6, none at 0.8); within that best-7 subgroup the matching advantage is 0.011 points = 1.9% of the self effect — the same shared-control pattern.
- **Controls localize the lever:** the orthogonal control slightly lowers the score (Delta −0.097, slope −0.288) and the generic holistic direction raises it (Delta 0.130, slope 0.550) but less than self or other directions at every alpha — so the effect is not generic tampering; it lives mostly in the axis the rubric directions share. Removing that shared axis leaves a gap of 0.0064 points (~5%); removing pairwise overlap leaves 0.013 points (95% CI [−0.015, 0.042], includes zero). Even in the top reliability quartile, specificity rises only to 8.3%. RQ2 separation predicts the size of the causal difference (r = 0.717) but not which direction wins.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from the Graduate Institute of Linguistics at National Taiwan University and NTU's Co-intelligence Humanities AI (CHAI) Future Lab (Chungche Huang, Po-Ya Angela Wang, Shu-Kai Hsieh). The paper is accepted at PACLIC 40 (Taipei, December 2026) — a peer-reviewed venue — and the work shows unusual methodological care: the rubrics were verified with LTTC stakeholders as the ones human raters actually apply; all 104 degraded essays were double-checked by two trained BESTEP graders (100% consensus on the intended rubric); permutation nulls, Bonferroni layer correction, split-half reliability gates, disattenuated similarities, and family-clustered bootstraps are used throughout. Code, analysis scripts, and the result dataset behind every number are public (github.com/lopentu/inside-the-scorer-paclic2026, PolyForm Noncommercial license); the original/degraded essay pairs are withheld for copyright but available on request.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel for AES. It is the first study I know of that moves from observational feature-weighting analyses to *causal* steering of rubric representations, and the first to demonstrate the decodability/control dissociation (adapting Elazar et al. 2021 and Hewitt & Liang 2019 principles) on a scoring task across 25 models.
- **Rigor:** High. LOOCV probing, permutation tests with conservative layer correction, pre-registered-style practical-significance margins, three distinct checks against the "noise/overlap hides specificity" objection, and honest reporting of exploratory analyses.
- **Weaknesses / red flags:** (1) External validity — all 25 models are *base* (non-instruction-tuned) and top out at 10.31B parameters, while operational scorers (e.g., Duolingo English Test) are fine-tuned, larger models; the authors own this, but the headline "rubrics do not behave as independent controls in base LLMs" may not transfer. (2) The rubric directions rest on only 5–12 LLM-generated degraded pairs each, all produced by a single generator (Llama 3.3 70B); the paper's own grammar/spelling example shows mutations leak across adjacent dimensions (dropping sentence-initial capitals in a "spelling" mutant), so the grammar × spelling similarity of 1.00 may partly reflect stimulus impurity rather than model geometry — the authors acknowledge this. (3) The r ≥ 0.6 "best-agreers" cutoff was chosen after seeing the agreement values, so that subgroup analysis is exploratory; the best-agreer subgroup is also small (n=7). (4) No multiple-comparison correction across the 10 head-to-head and 18 within-part tests (though all effects point the same way, which limits the risk). (5) No rubric-level human ratings exist, so "model collapses rubrics" versus "model faithfully mirrors human halo" cannot be distinguished — the authors flag this as the key open confound rather than hiding it.
- **Reproducibility:** High — public code, prompts, specs, and per-number result data; exact replication of RQ2/RQ3 requires requesting the essay pairs.
- **Significance:** Conceptually important. The message "score agreement and decodability do not imply controllability" is a usable audit primitive for anyone deploying LLM scorers in high-stakes settings, and the shared-lever finding is a concrete, well-evidenced negative result.

## Strengths

- Causal intervention (steering) rather than purely observational probing, applied at unusual breadth (25 models, 14 families).
- Clean conceptual decomposition into decodability / separability / control, each operationalized with an appropriate statistical test.
- Strong adversarial self-checking: three separate analyses test whether the negative specificity result is an artifact of noise or direction overlap.
- Honest, unusually detailed limitations section (post-hoc cutoff, tiny pair counts, single generator, single prompt, single assessment).
- Public code and result data; per-model appendix plots allow readers to verify the pooled patterns model by model.

## Limitations

- Base models only; instruction-tuned / chat / fine-tuned scorers — the ones actually deployed — are untested (explicitly deferred to future work), as are models larger than 10.31B.
- Single assessment (BESTEP, 75 essays), single grading prompt, and single-layer single-direction steering; other prompts or intervention types could behave differently.
- Rubric directions rest on 5–12 essay pairs from 26 source essays, generated by one LLM and verified only for recognizability of the target rubric, not for the absence of cross-dimension traces.
- The gold set has holistic band scores only, so the human-halo explanation for shared control cannot be ruled out.
- Statistical limits: Bonferroni is conservative with correlated adjacent layers; the 1,000-shuffle floor bounds p-values; "no detectable advantage" has wide single-pair intervals; the r = 0.717 RQ2–RQ3 correlation effectively has ~12 samples.

## Relevance

高度契合 "LLM Hidden States & Interpretability" 方向：本文正是对 25 个 LLM 的残差流做线性探测与激活转向，研究评分维度在表征空间中的几何结构与因果可控性。其"可解码 ≠ 可控"的审计思路对评估 LLM 系统内部机制的可靠性与可信性也具有直接的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do instruction-tuned or rubric-fine-tuned scorers (the deployed kind) develop genuinely separable rubric control, or is the shared lever a stable property of next-token training?
- Would sparse-autoencoder features decompose the shared lever into rubric-specific latents that steering could then target selectively, rescuing independence at a finer granularity?
- With a disattenuated human inter-rubric correlation matrix, does the shared lever track the halo structure of human analytic ratings (the two competing readings the paper cannot separate)?
- Does rubric separability or steering specificity improve with scale beyond 10.31B parameters, or with different grading prompts and multi-layer/multi-direction interventions?
- Do the findings travel to non-English rubric-based writing assessments and to decomposed-subconstruct operational systems like the Duolingo English Test?
