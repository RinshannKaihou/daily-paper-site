Negation lives in one shared, steerable linear direction — an English-extracted vector flips it across languages.

"Not" is Not Lost in Translation: A Causal Analysis of Negation Representations in Multilingual LLMs

Giang Son Nguyen · Wray Buntine · Dung D. Le · Wenya Wang — NTU · VinUniversity · Monash

negation mechanistic-interpretability multilingual-LLMs activation-steering sparse-autoencoders
Key metric · single SAE feature
≥91%

polarity flip rate sustained by one SAE feature per model (e.g. Gemma-3-4B L4 f331) across all four languages, in both add & remove modes.

vs 0% addition / ≤8% removal for norm-matched random directions — the effect is causal, not coincidental.

Why this matters

Causal evidence that multilingual LLMs share linear semantic directions — one interpretable feature can steer meaning across languages, not just describe it.

Critical edge
"Largely shared across languages" may really mean "shared across one carefully normalized negator per language" — each language was pinned to a single negation regime.
— Critical Assessment · data quirks
Methodology · how the claim was tested
  • Six instruction-tuned models (Gemma-3 1B/4B/12B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-9B, Llama-3.1-8B); interventions on residual streams at the negated-predicate position, layer chosen per model (L8–L17).
  • Dense direction (CAA): d = mean(h_neg − h_pos) over 150 minimal pairs; injected as h ± α·d during zero-shot translation (α ∈ {2,4,6}, tuned on a disjoint eval split).
  • Sparse steering: SAE features ranked by neg/pos activation gap; final vector is a single feature's decoder direction (K=1), norm-matched to CAA.
  • Flip rate gate: output must show intended polarity and correct language (fastText) and non-degeneration and content preservation (chrF ≥ 30); FLORES scored by a blind GPT judge.
  • Human validation on Gemma-3-4B: 96.5% (Mini-minus-4) / 93.7% (FLORES) agreement with automatic evaluators.
  • Data: Mini-minus-4 — 350 minimal pairs × EN/VI/FR/ZH with predicate-disjoint splits; FLORES-minus-4 — 216 naturalistic polarity-flipped sentences from FLORES-200.

  • Cross-language transfer: one EN-extracted direction averages 80.0–95.1% (add) / 79.7–94.7% (rm) across all 12 off-diagonal transfer directions.
  • Unseen languages (RU/HI/AR/ID, zero re-selection): dense ≥85.6/88.0%; single SAE feature ≥86.9% for Gemma-4B & Qwen3.5 (Llama ≥77.2%).
  • Naturalistic FLORES sentences: removal stays robust (SAE ≥91.6%); Llama's addition weakens to 58–63% on VI/ZH.
  • Beyond explicit markers: on 44 ZH antonym-translated negatives, subtracting the direction still reverses meaning in 68.2%.
  • Methods caution (MADs): Gemma's massive-activation dimensions inflate cross-lingual cosine (0.960 → 0.643 after zeroing) yet change steering by ≤0.7 pp — decode ≠ control.
Strengths
  • Intervention-based, not correlational — with a clean random-direction control anchoring the effect (0% vs 60–100% addition).
  • Two complementary datasets isolating minimal pairs from naturalistic sentences, with predicate-disjoint splits preventing leakage.
  • Honest failure reporting — per-model/per-language weak cells (VI & FR transfer, Llama under-firing) instead of aggregate-only means.
  • MAD analysis is a practical caution for anyone computing cross-lingual cosines in Gemma-style models.
  • K=1 SAE result — one interpretable feature controlling polarity in four languages — a crisp monosemanticity datapoint.
Limitations
  • Explicit verbal negation only, one marker per language; morphological (un-, in-), implicit (fail, lack), and multi-marker negation untested.
  • Translation is the only task — behavior in QA, reasoning, or classification is unknown.
  • Steering strength α picked from a 3-value sweep; a known weakness of activation-addition methods.
  • SAE conclusions rest on third-party pretrained releases (3 models) and one hand-picked feature per model.
  • Small scale: 100 test pairs/language, 216 FLORES items, short single clauses — long-range negation scope out of scope. ~6% of FLORES flips may be judge artifacts.
Questions for further exploration
  • Does the shared direction survive morphological (Turkish -me, Japanese -nai) or double-marked negation (Spanish no…ninguno)?
  • Is it causally active outside translation — e.g., flipping answers on negation-heavy QA benchmarks (NegBench-style)?
  • Why does Llama's SAE feature under-fire on Vietnamese: tokenizer, data frequency, or language-specific encoding?
  • Does steering degrade translation quality monotonically with α, and can a principled α replace the sweep?
  • Do EN-extracted and per-language directions converge on the same circuit, or coexist with language-specific implementations?
一句话总结 · 中文速览

通过对比激活加法（CAA）与单个稀疏自编码器（SAE）特征对六个开源多语言模型进行因果干预，本文证明否定语义由跨语言共享的线性方向编码：英语提取的否定方向可在越/法/汉翻译中诱导或移除否定（跨语言迁移翻转率平均 80.0–95.1%），且每个模型仅需一个 SAE 特征即可跨四种语言控制极性（最高 ≥91%），并能泛化到俄/印地/阿拉伯/印尼语与 FLORES 自然句。
