> **一句话总结：** 在 ARC-AGI-3 上让 9 种前沿模型配置各自从零构建游戏求解 harness 并自主修订两次（27 条谱系、81 个冻结快照、2,025 次评测），发现模型能造出从 0 分到接近满分的多样 harness，但两次修订平均只带来 +2.80 RHAE 的留出集提升（95% 区间 [−1.44, 7.35]），开发集收益（+8.42）显著高估了泛化，且降成本常以牺牲性能为代价——自主"搭脚手架"与可靠"改脚手架"是两种可分离的能力。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled ARC-AGI-3 study where 9 frontier model-effort profiles (GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna, Claude Fable 5/Opus 5/Sonnet 5) each build a complete game-solving harness from a minimal repo and revise it twice from their own execution traces: mean held-out RHAE (20 held-out games, vs deploying the initial harness H0 at 24.54) rises only +2.80 points with a 95% bootstrap interval of [−1.44, 7.35] that includes zero, while development-game gains of +8.42 overstate transfer (adaptation gap 5.62). Human-steered harnesses like Tycho still hit 100 RHAE with the same Opus 5 xhigh profile versus 39.95 here — building a harness works far better than autonomously improving one.

## 核心贡献

- 提出将 **harness 适应（harness adaptation）作为一种独立可测能力**的形式化框架：系统 S_t = (M, H_t)，模型权重 M 固定，模型通过固定的编码引导程序（OpenCode）构建并修订自己运行于其中的任务 harness H_t，并定义 **harness 适应差距** G_t = 开发集提升 − 留出集提升，用以度量"表面进步"与真实泛化的偏离。
- 执行了一项**全快照、可复现的受控实验**：9 个模型–推理力度条件 × 3 条独立谱系 = 27 条谱系、81 个冻结快照、2,025 次游戏评测；每个快照（初版 H0、修订一 H1、修订二 H2）都在 5 个开发游戏 + 20 个从不参与反馈的留出游戏上评测，并同时记录美元计价的推理成本（总开销 ≥ $18,129.27，冻结价目表核算）。
- 对全部 54 个修订事件做了**结果盲、双编码员可靠性审计的定性分析**（GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh 按冻结 codebook 独立编码两遍，报告 κ/α，标签先冻结再与结果关联），把开发反馈 → 代码变更 → 留出表现的链条连接起来。
- 核心实证结论：自主构建能力强（初始留出 RHAE 从 0 到 97.14 不等），但**修订不可靠**——严格符号计数 15 涨 / 12 跌（p = .70 符号检验），成本下降常伴随性能损失（9/27 条谱系"更便宜但更差"），且没有任何一条谱系选择"保留父代"（弃权也是适应的一部分，模型不会弃权）。

## Key Contributions

- Formalize **harness adaptation as a separate capability**: a system S_t = (M, H_t) with frozen model weights M, where the same model profile builds and revises the executable harness it acts through, plus the **harness adaptation gap** G_t = (dev improvement) − (held-out improvement) to quantify when development progress is illusory.
- A **replicated all-snapshot controlled study**: 9 model–effort conditions × 3 independent constructions = 27 lineages, 81 frozen snapshots, 2,025 game evaluations, with every H0/H1/H2 snapshot scored on 5 development games and 20 strict held-out games, jointly tracking RHAE, actions, and dollar inference cost (program total ≥ $18,129.27 under a frozen price schedule).
- An **outcome-blind, reliability-audited qualitative audit** of all 54 revision episodes (double-coded with frozen codebook; κ/α reported; labels frozen before outcome linkage) connecting development feedback to code changes to held-out outcomes.
- Headline empirical finding: construction strength varies enormously (initial held-out RHAE 0–97.14), but two autonomous revisions yield a mean held-out gain of only +2.80 RHAE with an interval spanning zero; development gains (+8.42) systematically overstate transfer; and every lineage modified its code even when preserving the parent was allowed — models never abstain.

## Methodology

Each of the nine conditions (GPT-6 Astra high; GPT-5.6 Sol high/xhigh; GPT-5.6 Terra, Luna high; Claude Fable 5, Opus 5 high/xhigh; Claude Sonnet 5 high) pairs a model with a reasoning-effort setting used both for harness development and gameplay. During construction and revision the model operates through a fixed general-purpose coding harness (OpenCode); during gameplay it acts through the harness it built. The model receives a sandboxed minimal repository (game-interaction docs, a local engine client, a controlled model client, pricing specs) and must autonomously author `run.sh` plus a complete solver — no solver architecture, game rules, or worked examples are provided; no internet, no cross-game state.

The pipeline: (1) construct a harness, (2) pass controller-run smoke qualification on two development games (50 charged actions, stop after two completed levels or a win; qualification requires valid interaction, not score), with up to ten repair cycles on failure; (3) run the frozen H0 on five randomly pre-selected development games (tr87, vc33, r11l, bp35, ft09); (4) revision 1 receives a fixed bundle — own source, traces, scores, actions, inference costs — and may change anything or preserve the parent; (5) revision 2 repeats this from H1's evidence; (6) all frozen snapshots are evaluated on the 20 remaining public games that never influenced any decision. Gameplay is validated by replaying frozen action–observation traces locally and against the official hosted scorecard, requiring score agreement. Uncertainty uses 100,000 whole-lineage bootstrap resamples preserving paired comparisons. Task performance is RHAE (Relative Human Action Efficiency): completed levels earn min{100·(b_ℓ/a_ℓ)², 115} within a 5× human action budget, later levels weighted more; cost is logical inference dollars per game.

## Results & Highlights

- **Two revisions barely move held-out performance:** mean held-out RHAE (20 held-out ARC-AGI-3 games, 27 lineages) rises from 24.54 (H0) to 27.34 (H2), i.e. +2.80 points, with 95% bootstrap interval [−1.44, 7.35] — compatible with zero average benefit; strict-sign outcomes are 15 gains vs 12 losses (p = .70, two-sided sign test).
- **Development gains overstate transfer:** the same revisions raise mean development-game RHAE by +8.42, giving a mean adaptation gap G2 = 5.62 points (95% interval [1.78, 9.77]); omitting just the dev game r11l from the summary shrinks the gap to 3.02, showing how one feedback game can dominate the apparent gap.
- **Cost cuts often buy performance regressions:** mean deployment cost falls from $10.30 (H0) to $7.57 (H2) per game, but of 27 lineages only 9 improve both held-out RHAE and cost (best: Sol 5.6 high rep-1, +33.18 RHAE while saving $4.72/game), 6 improve RHAE at higher cost, 9 get cheaper while losing RHAE (worst: Sol 5.6 xhigh rep-1, −21.96 RHAE for $24.21/game saved), and 3 worsen on both.
- **Execution noise is the same order as the headline effect:** ten re-evaluations of one frozen Sol-high H2 harness give SD 3.99 RHAE (range 43.38–55.37, mean 48.53), so single-lineage changes of a few points are hard to interpret; game-level median within-game SD is 18.78.
- **Aggregate gain hides per-game churn:** averaged across lineages, 13 held-out games improve and 7 decline, from +20.44 (lp85) to −15.03 (sb26).
- **Human-steered headroom is huge:** at the same Opus 5 xhigh profile, Tycho reaches 100 RHAE on every public game, versus 39.95 mean for this study's H2 harnesses on twenty of those same games (Sol: 100 vs 28.58 high / 41.94 xhigh; Retrodict 99.86).
- **Revisions are drastic but unhelpful on average:** 22 of 27 first revisions "reorient" the harness (memory, perception, batching, replay, scheduling); no lineage preserves its parent; median codebase grows 2,161 → 2,912 Python lines; a Sol-high revision fixing action budgeting and memory lifted held-out RHAE 29.24 → 53.56 at unchanged cost, while a Sol-xhigh revision replacing response chaining with independent calls collapsed 43.95 → 21.99 while cutting cost $31.39 → $7.18/game.

## Authors & Credibility

Jens Lehmann (Amazon* / TU Dresden; work done outside of Amazon) and Sahar Vahdati (Leibniz Universität Hannover / TIB). Lehmann is also an author of Tycho, one of the human-steered 100-RHAE ARC-AGI-3 systems used as the reference point — giving the team firsthand knowledge of what effective harnesses look like. The paper is unusually transparent for this genre: frozen price schedules, token-ledger-derived costs, hosted replay validation of every recorded score, checksum-backed artifact manifest, an AI-use statement, and a reproducibility statement with deterministic reproduction scripts. Bootstrap methodology, sign tests, and reliability statistics (κ/α) are reported with explicit thresholds.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The framing is genuinely useful — separating "can build an agent" from "can improve it," with the adaptation gap G_t as a clean, reusable metric. The all-snapshot protocol (every prescribed descendant evaluated, no selection among candidates) is a real methodological advance over validation-selected evolution studies (AFlow/GEPA, RRSI, HarnessOpt-Bench), which the authors' own Table 2 maps carefully.
- **Rigor:** Strong on uncertainty honesty — the headline +2.80 [−1.44, 7.35] interval is presented as inconclusive rather than spun. But the qualitative Section 6.2 rests on two LLM annotation passes (GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh) with no human-annotation validation, four codebook dimensions fall below the preset 0.67 reliability threshold (diagnosis 0.35, incumbent protection 0.47, resource metareasoning 0.29), and the planning label was excluded for concentrated missingness — the surviving "broad rewrites +3.75 vs contained −0.20" association has an interval including zero and is explicitly non-causal.
- **Reproducibility:** Good (frozen prompts, splits, snapshots, seed 20260902 bootstrap, replay validation), though the frozen-harness variance study covers a single post hoc snapshot rather than a random sample, and provider-side deployment drift between August and September 2026 reruns is acknowledged but not excluded.
- **Weaknesses to note:** only two revisions and one 5-game dev split — the r11l leave-one-out check (gap 5.62 → 3.02) shows conclusions about gap size hinge on that split; three lineages per condition make the per-condition Table 4 numbers (e.g., Opus xhigh +8.68 vs Sol xhigh −0.05) descriptive anecdotes, not model rankings; the "human-steered gap" (Tycho 100 vs 39.95) is confounded because Tycho selected among four policies using all 25 public games, so it does not isolate human steering; and the promising dev-RHAE checkpoint-selection rule (+4.53) is post hoc and unevaluated prospectively. Headline effect (+2.80) is smaller than the measured single-harness execution SD (3.99), which the authors flag but which still means the pooled mean leans on unreplicated single evaluations of the other 26 lineages.
- **Red flags:** none serious — no benchmark gaming observed (replay validation guards it), and overclaiming is actively avoided. The public-game training-contamination caveat is stated but unaddressed.

## Strengths

- A crisp, generalizable question (can a model improve the software it acts through?) answered with a preregistration-grade protocol: fixed bootstrap, fixed dev split chosen before the campaign, all snapshots evaluated, outcomes only after adaptation.
- Joint score–cost reporting exposes that "cheaper harness" is often "worse harness" — 9 of 27 apparent savings are performance contractions, a finding most self-improvement papers hide.
- The execution-variance diagnostic (SD 3.99 on 10 replays of one harness) gives the community a rare empirical noise scale for ARC-AGI-3 suite scores.
- Honest, self-critical analysis throughout: intervals that include zero are labeled as such, post hoc rules are flagged as post hoc, and the abstention finding (0 of 27 preserve) is turned into a concrete design lesson.

## Limitations

- Single benchmark (ARC-AGI-3), single feedback split, only two revisions — long-horizon accumulation, the paper's own "central challenge," is untested.
- Public games may have leaked into pretraining corpora for some models.
- Three runs per condition; between-model comparisons are descriptive only.
- Each snapshot evaluated once (except the one diagnostic harness), so per-lineage gains/regressions partly reflect gameplay stochasticity (mean absolute difference between two evaluations: 4.73 points).
- Qualitative coding was LLM-only and used summarized execution records, without agreement checks against human annotators.
- Dollar costs exclude infrastructure, human labor, latency, and energy; cross-condition cost comparisons entangle model pricing with harness efficiency.

## Relevance

直接命中"Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research"方向的核心问题：它用严格的留出集协议量化了 LLM 自主改进自身 agent 架构（harness）的可靠性与失败模式——开发集过拟合、能力遗忘、成本换性能——正是长程自主进化系统可信性评估所急需的证据类型。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would an explicit acceptance/rollback rule (compare candidate revision against preserved parent on dev games, reject on regression), as RRSI suggests, close the gap between the +2.80 observed and the +4.53 post hoc selection bound — and does it survive prospective evaluation?
- Does the adaptation gap shrink with more development games, more revisions, or outcome-diverse feedback bundles (e.g., adding per-action cost traces), and how does G_t scale with lineage length?
- Why do models never preserve their parent harness — is it a prompt-induced prior ("revision = change code"), a genuine inability to judge when evidence is weak, or both?
- Do the same held-out regression patterns appear on non-ARC interactive benchmarks (private/uncontaminated games), and how much of the 0–97.14 construction spread reflects model capability versus pure sampling luck in the coding sessions?
- Can the harness adaptation gap G_t be predicted at revision time from observable signals (diff breadth, repair cycles, dev-score delta), enabling cheap gating of autonomous updates in deployed self-improving systems?
