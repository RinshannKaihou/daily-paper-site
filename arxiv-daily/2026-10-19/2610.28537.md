> **一句话总结：** 系统研究 2-bit 后训练量化后发现：向量码本表示是决定性因素（scalar PTQ 全部崩塌到随机水平，而向量 PTQ 以约 2.3 有效比特保留 89–97% 的全精度 QA 精度），且校准数据与恢复微调数据不可分离（必须联合选择）；最终配方用比最强 QAT 基线少 1.4–30 倍的计算，把生成任务从崩溃边缘拉回可用区间。

> **TL;DR:** A Microsoft empirical study of 2-bit post-training quantization across six dense and MoE models (3.8B–32B, including Phi-4-mini, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B). Every tested scalar PTQ pipeline (SpinQuant, QuaRot at group size 128) collapses to the 29.3% random QA floor with 0.0 generation accuracy, while vector codebook PTQ (YAQA) at 2.09–2.33 effective bits retains 89–97% of BF16 QA accuracy and beats trained scalar baselines on generation — e.g., 37.4 vs 18.4 for BitDistiller on Phi-4-mini AvgGen (GSM8K+HumanEval) — using 39–304x less compute without recovery and 1.4–30x less than the strongest QAT baseline with recovery. The key novel finding: calibration data and recovery data interact (a negative interaction of -8.5 AvgGen points on Phi-4-mini, 95% CI [-12.6, -4.3]), so tuning them separately picks the wrong recovery data.

## 核心贡献

- **表示形式主导 2-bit 质量**：在测的所有 scalar PTQ 管线（SpinQuant、QuaRot，group size 128）在 2-bit 下全部崩塌到随机水平（QA 约 29 分、生成任务 0.0），而向量码本方法（YAQA、VPTQ）在相同 2.09–2.33 有效比特预算下把 QA 恢复到全精度的 89–97%。
- **发现校准数据与恢复数据的交互效应**：2×2 实验（web/math 校准 × web/math 恢复）显示最优恢复数据随校准数据翻转（Phi-4-mini 交互效应 -8.5 分，95% CI [-12.6, -4.3]），并通过对 Qwen3-8B 的预注册（pre-registered）实验前瞻性确认（-9.4 分，CI [-13.3, -5.3]）——两者必须联合选择。
- **校准主要影响生成而非 QA**：固定量化器时，校准配方让 AvgGen 每模型波动 13.5–26.7 分，而 AvgQA 仅波动 3.9–7.5 分；4-bit 下同样的波动最多只有 1.6（QA）和 7.9（Gen）分。
- **廉价恢复配方**：仅训练码本（codebook-only）的知识蒸馏恢复只需 3,200 个样本（约 3.3M token，4B 模型约 100 PFLOP），生成收益在 3,200 样本处即饱和（+6.3 分后平台期），总计算量比最强 QAT 基线少 1.4–30 倍。
- **量化放大推理模型的过度思考（overthinking）**：复现并量化了 Qwen3-30B-A3B 在 2-bit 下的循环/犹豫行为（MATH-500 精度 0.675→0.450，4-gram 循环分数 0.39→0.67），并发现简单的重复惩罚（repetition penalty 1.3）可把精度拉回 0.625。
- **给出可操作的五步 2-bit 配方**：向量码本 + 任务对齐的校准混合 + 可选的短程码本恢复 + 按模型验证 + 按部署方式（含重复惩罚）分别报告 QA 与生成。

## Key Contributions

- **Representation dominates at 2 bits.** Every scalar PTQ pipeline tested (SpinQuant, QuaRot, group size 128) collapses to the random QA floor (~29 points) with 0.0 generation accuracy, while vector codebook quantization (YAQA, VPTQ) at a matched 2.09–2.33 effective bits per weight restores QA retention to 89–97% of BF16 across six models.
- **Calibration and recovery data are not separable.** In a 2×2 design (web vs math calibration x none/web/math recovery), the best recovery data flips with the calibration data — a negative interaction of -8.5 AvgGen points on Phi-4-mini (95% CI [-12.6, -4.3]), -2.7 on Llama-3.1-8B (CI [-5.0, -0.7]), prospectively confirmed on pre-registered held-out Qwen3-8B (-9.4, CI [-13.3, -5.3]). Tuning the two data choices independently selects the wrong recovery data.
- **Calibration chiefly controls generation.** With the quantizer fixed, sweeping calibration recipes moves AvgGen by 13.5–26.7 points per model but AvgQA by only 3.9–7.5; at 4 bits the same sweep moves at most 1.6 QA and 7.9 generation points.
- **An inexpensive codebook-only recovery stage.** Distilling from the full-precision teacher (a 4-bit teacher matches a BF16 one) while training only the codebook for 3,200 examples (~3.3M tokens, ~100 PFLOP for a 4B model) saturates generation gains (+6.3 points by 3,200 examples, then plateau), giving the full recipe 1.4–30x less compute than the strongest QAT baseline (BitDistiller).
- **Quantization amplifies reasoning-model overthinking.** On Qwen3-30B-A3B / MATH-500 (greedy), 2-bit quantization drops accuracy 0.675 → 0.450 while raising hesitation markers from 15.5 to 23.7 per 1k tokens and the repeated 4-gram loop score from 0.39 to 0.67; a repetition penalty of 1.3 restores accuracy to 0.625 and cuts the loop score to 0.30.
- **A practical five-step recipe** (Section 5): vector codebook (YAQA); task-aligned Hessian-mixture calibration with broad web mixed in when QA matters; recovery data chosen jointly with calibration via a cheap per-model screen; short optional recovery; report QA and generation separately under deployment-style prompting with a repetition penalty.

## Methodology

The paper is an empirical, factor-wise study of post-training quantization (PTQ) plus recovery fine-tuning at 2 bits, on six models: Phi-4-mini (3.8B, instruct), Llama-3.1-8B (base), Qwen3-30B-A3B (reasoning MoE, ~3B active), plus Qwen3-8B (held-out confirmation), OLMoE-1B-7B (instruct MoE), and Qwen3-32B (dense scale control).

**Quantizers.** Scalar baselines: SpinQuant and QuaRot (group size 128), plus trained-scalar GSQ (learned scalar-grid assignments) and BitDistiller (scalar QAT). Vector baselines: VPTQ (2^16-entry codebook over 8-dim subvectors) and YAQA (Fisher-weighted rounding onto trellis-coded sets), both in an incoherence-rotated frame; learned rotations are shown not to help at 2 bits. Attention and MLP projections (including every MoE expert) are quantized; routers, embeddings, norms, and the LM head stay in half precision (a router-precision ablation shows routers are robust). Counting indices, codebooks, and scales, every method lands at 2.09–2.33 effective bits per weight, making comparisons rate-matched.

**Calibration as Hessian mixtures.** Because the PTQ objective depends on calibration data only through the input Hessian H_X, the authors sweep mixtures by reweighting precomputed per-domain Hessians (web/FineWeb-Edu, instruction, math/OpenR1-Math, code) instead of re-collecting them; for YAQA the Kronecker input and output factors are mixed separately.

**Recovery fine-tuning.** The quantized backbone is frozen and only quantization parameters are trained — the codebook with assignments fixed (or scales/zero-points for a scalar control) — via distillation from the full-precision model with L = α·LCE + (1−α)·τ²·KL(teacher ∥ student), α = 0.5. This avoids QAT's optimizer state (~14P bytes); recovery needs only teacher + student (~2P + bP/8 bytes).

**Evaluation.** AvgQA averages seven multiple-choice QA tasks (MMLU 5-shot; WinoGrande, LAMBADA by accuracy; ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, PIQA by length-normalized accuracy); AvgGen averages GSM8K (5-shot, strict match) and HumanEval (0-shot, pass@1); IFEval measures instruction following. The interaction claims are supported by three recovery seeds, hierarchical-bootstrap CIs, held-out tasks (MBPP, HumanEval+, MATH-500), independently re-quantized bases, disjoint recovery samples, and a pre-registered replication on Qwen3-8B.

## Results & Highlights

- **The 2-bit cliff for scalar PTQ.** On the quantization ladder (Table 3, web calibration, no recovery): Phi-4-mini falls from BF16 71.0 QA / 73.2 Gen to 4-bit scalar 69.3 / 65.8, then to a ~29.3 QA random floor and 0.0 generation at 2-bit scalar; vector 2-bit YAQA recovers 63.3 QA / 12.2 Gen. The same collapse-to-zero generation pattern holds for scalar 2-bit on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-30B-A3B.
- **Vector beats trained scalar at matched bits (Table 4).** On Phi-4-mini AvgGen, 2-bit YAQA reaches 37.4 vs 18.4 for BitDistiller (scalar QAT) and 17.9 for GSQ, against a BF16 ceiling of 73.2; on AvgQA, YAQA 63.3 vs BitDistiller 53.0 and GSQ 52.6. On Qwen3-30B-A3B, YAQA scores 66.7 QA / 62.4 Gen vs BitDistiller 55.9 / 45.7, and GSQ collapses to the random floor on both MoEs (29.4 QA, 0.0 Gen on Qwen3-MoE; 29.6 / 0.0 on OLMoE).
- **Compute efficiency.** No-recovery vector PTQ keeps 89–97% of full-precision QA accuracy using 39–304x less compute than trained scalar methods; including the recovery stage, the recipe uses 1.4–30x less compute than the strongest QAT baseline (BitDistiller). Recovery itself costs ~100 PFLOP (~1% of a ~360M-token main recovery run) for a 4B model.
- **The calibration x recovery interaction (Tables 1–2).** On Phi-4-mini (BF16 AvgGen 72.3), web calibration with no recovery gives 11.1 AvgGen, and math recovery lifts it to 24.0; but with math calibration the ordering flips — no recovery already gives 36.7, web recovery is best at 45.7, and math recovery drops to 42.3. The interaction contrast is -8.5 points (95% CI [-12.6, -4.3]) on Phi-4-mini, -2.7 (CI [-5.0, -0.7]) on Llama-3.1-8B, and -9.4 (CI [-13.3, -5.3]) on the pre-registered held-out Qwen3-8B.
- **Calibration moves generation, not QA (Table 5).** Across calibration recipes, AvgGen varies by 13.5 points on Phi-4-mini (12.2 web → 37.4 math), 26.7 on Llama-3.1-8B (9.6 → 26.6 math... with mixtures in between), and 17.0 on Qwen3-MoE, while AvgQA varies only 3.9–7.5 points.
- **Recovery is model-dependent (Table 8).** Long codebook-only recovery roughly doubles Phi-4-mini HumanEval (math-calibrated: 34.1 → 47.6; web-calibrated: 14.0 → 28.0) and lifts GSM8K 53.2 → 62.5; on Qwen3-MoE it barely moves anything (GSM8K 73.8 → 76.0, HumanEval 75.0 → 76.8); on base Llama-3.1-8B QA decreases in all three long runs. Recovery cannot rescue an already-collapsed scalar model.
- **Generation retention is model- and scale-dependent (Figure 4, Table 33).** QA retention is similar across models (89–97%), but chat-template HumanEval retention spans 45% (Phi-4-mini) and 42–45% (Qwen3-8B) up to 81% (Qwen3-30B-A3B) and 68% (dense Qwen3-32B), tracking total model scale more than the quantizer.
- **Overthinking at 2 bits (Tables 6–7).** On MATH-500 (Qwen3-30B-A3B, greedy), 2-bit drops accuracy 0.675 → 0.450, lengthens mean CoT 4928 → 5900 tokens, and raises the repeated 4-gram loop score 0.39 → 0.67; a repetition penalty of 1.3 improves 2-bit accuracy to 0.625 and cuts the loop score to 0.30 (also helping BF16).

## Authors & Credibility

The authors (Rhea Sanjay Sukthanker, Riccardo Grazzi, James Hensman, Pashmina Cameron) are from Microsoft Research and the Applied Sciences Group (ASG) at Microsoft; Hensman is well known for work on Gaussian processes and probabilistic ML, and the group has prior ultra-low-bit quantization experience. The paper is a preprint under review (arXiv 2610.28537v1, 20 Oct 2026), with a disclosed "Use of AI Tools" statement and a commitment to release code, Hessian mixtures, and quantized models upon acceptance. Methodological credibility is high: matched effective-bit budgets (2.09–2.33), three recovery seeds with bootstrap CIs, held-out tasks, re-quantized bases, disjoint recovery samples, and a genuinely pre-registered confirmation on Qwen3-8B (Appendix A.9) — a level of statistical care rare in quantization papers.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate, honest about it.** The components are all known (vector codebooks from AQLM/VPTQ/QuIP#, distillation recovery from LLM-QAT/BitDistiller, calibration-data sensitivity from prior work). The novel contributions are the joint calibration x recovery interaction finding and the end-to-end rate-matched recipe; the paper explicitly frames itself as a data-centric empirical study rather than a new algorithm.
- **Rigor: strong.** Pre-registration on a held-out model, interaction contrasts with 95% hierarchical-bootstrap intervals that exclude zero, robustness across held-out tasks (MBPP, HumanEval+, MATH-500), re-quantized bases, and disjoint recovery samples. A missing ablation: the interaction is studied for one domain pair (web vs math) and one quantizer (YAQA) only, and the 2×2 design was never re-run at long recovery budgets — the authors acknowledge both.
- **Potential baseline caveats.** The headline "scalar PTQ collapses" applies to the two pipelines tested (SpinQuant, QuaRot at group size 128) without recovery; a smaller group size (e.g., g=32/64) scalar baseline at matched effective bits is not in the main ladder, so "all scalar PTQ collapses" is slightly broader than the evidence. BitDistiller/GSQ results are taken at the authors' recipe-grid budget; how much tuning those baselines received per model is not fully transparent.
- **Red flags / quirks.** (1) The 89–97% QA-retention headline concerns multiple-choice QA, while generation retention is far worse (45–81% HumanEval; AvgGen retention 51–66% for sub-10B dense models) — the abstract could mislead a skimming reader. (2) 2-bit YAQA on Qwen3-MoE "exceeds" its BF16 reference (62.4 vs 61.8 AvgGen), which the appendix attributes to an under-measured completion-prompt reference — a measurement quirk worth flagging rather than a real gain. (3) The overthinking study uses only 40 MATH-500 problems, so its accuracy deltas carry wide uncertainty. (4) Llama-3.1-8B is a base model evaluated completion-style, making its rows not directly comparable to the instruct models. (5) IFEval often drops after recovery on Phi-4-mini (e.g., 29.0 → 21.8 under completion prompts), so "recovery is cheap and safe" is conditional on the deployment prompt.
- **Reproducibility: good in principle, pending release.** All models and datasets are public and the protocol is described in unusual detail, but code/Hessians/models are only promised "upon acceptance," so independent verification is not yet possible.
- **Significance: practical rather than scientific.** For memory-constrained serving, the recipe (vector codebook + task-aligned calibration + optional 3,200-example codebook recovery at ~2.3 bits) is directly actionable; the interaction finding changes how practitioners should run PTQ tuning sweeps.

## Strengths

- Rate-matched comparison (all methods at 2.09–2.33 effective bits) instead of nominal-bit comparisons.
- The calibration x recovery interaction is a genuinely new, actionable finding, established with pre-registration, held-out models, held-out tasks, and resampled data — unusually strong evidence for an empirical ML paper.
- Broad model coverage (six dense and MoE models, 3.8B–32B, base/instruct/reasoning) makes the collapse and retention claims hard to dismiss as cherry-picking.
- The recipe is cheap and concrete: no-recovery vector PTQ is 39–304x cheaper than trained scalar methods; recovery saturates at 3,200 examples.
- The overthinking analysis (hesitation markers, 4-gram loop score, repetition-penalty mitigation) adds a failure-mode lens beyond aggregate accuracy.

## Limitations

- Interaction evidence covers one domain pair (web vs math), one quantizer (YAQA), and short recovery budgets only; long-budget 2×2 runs are left to future work.
- Base- and data-sample variability were measured only on Phi-4-mini, and replicate bases share one rotation and one recovery seed.
- Generation retention remains poor for sub-10B dense models (HumanEval retention 45% for Phi-4-mini, 42–45% for Qwen3-8B), so the recipe does not make 2-bit uniformly "usable" — QA-heavy workloads benefit most.
- Recovery can hurt: QA decreases in all long-recovery runs on Llama-3.1-8B, and IFEval frequently drops on Phi-4-mini, so recovery must be validated per model and prompt format.
- The overthinking study is small (40 problems), and evaluation is sensitive to prompt format (chat template vs completion) and numeric precision (bf16 vs fp16), which the authors handle carefully but which complicates cross-paper comparisons.

## Relevance

高度契合“极端性能优化下的可靠性”研究方向：本文系统刻画了 2-bit 低精度量化引入的精度损失与失败模式（scalar 崩塌、生成质量退化、推理模型过度思考/循环），属于量化误差与低比特推理可靠性的核心课题，其“校准与恢复数据联合选择”的结论对低精度部署的评估协议也有直接指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the calibration x recovery interaction persist for other quantizers (VPTQ, AQLM), other domain pairs (code, instruction, long-context), and at long recovery budgets where recovery may re-shape the codebook more aggressively?
- Would scalar PTQ with much smaller group sizes (g=16/32) or optimized non-uniform grids close part of the gap to vector codebooks at matched effective bits, or is the codebook structure itself irreplaceable below 3 bits?
- Can the model-dependence of recovery benefit (helps Phi-4-mini, hurts Llama-3.1-8B base) be predicted a priori — e.g., from pre-quantization Hessian spectra, training-data mixture, or instruct-vs-base status — rather than screened empirically per model?
- Is the 2-bit overthinking/looping amplification (loop score 0.39 → 0.67 on Qwen3-MoE) driven by specific layers or expert subsets, and can targeted mixed-precision (keeping a few sensitive projections at 4 bits) suppress it without losing the memory savings?
- How do the findings transfer to weight+activation quantization (W2A8/W2A4) and to serving stacks where kernel numerics, not just weight representation, dominate the error budget?
