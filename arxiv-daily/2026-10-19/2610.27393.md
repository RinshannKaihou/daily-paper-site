> **一句话总结：** 提出 Guided-AP：利用 attribution patching 误差的结构性（单 prompt 内误差同号、跨 prompt 误差按组件复现），用少量精确 activation patching 测量来校正未测组件的估计——单 prompt 上 MAE 降低最高 26%，跨 prompt 校正事实补全任务的平均效应误差降低 75–79%。

> **TL;DR:** Attribution patching (AtP) estimates activation-patching (AP) effects cheaply from one gradient pass but its errors can be large, and verifying each estimate restores AP's full cost. Guided-AP exploits two empirical regularities — within one prompt, AtP residuals of many components share a sign; across prompts of a task, a component's residual recurs — to spend a small budget of exact AP measurements on both verification and correction. On Gemma-2-9B MLP neurons (IOI / factual completion / SVA tasks), within-prompt Guided-AP cuts effect-vector MAE by 17.4–26.2% vs the runtime-matched verify-more baseline (e.g., 26.2% on factual completion); on Qwen3-32B it cuts MAE 13.7–17.5% while measuring only ~1% of neurons per layer, with verify-more needing 5.9–14.7× more warm runtime to match its error. Across prompts on Qwen3-8B/Qwen2.5-7B attention heads, learned corrections cut dataset-average MAE on factual completion by 75–79% vs uncorrected AtP with zero measurements on evaluation prompts — though the same correction *raises* Qwen2.5 IOI average MAE by 52%, an honest failure case.

## 核心贡献

- **预算化归因分析框架（budgeted attribution analysis）**：把机制可解释性中的组件效应估计明确建模为"固定精确测量预算 B 下如何分配验证与校正"的问题，并以等计算量下的剩余误差作为统一比较标准。
- **误差结构分析与利用**：实证发现 AtP 残差的三条规律——少数组件携带大部分误差（GPT-2 上前 4 个组件占 80% 测试误差）、单 prompt 内残差普遍偏离零（同号偏置）、同一组件的残差在同任务不同 prompt 上复现。方法据此在两个维度上共享学习：prompt 内跨组件、跨 prompt 同组件。
- **Guided-AP 方法**：prompt 内用按 |g| 分层的 pilot 采样取残差中位数作为全局平移校正，其余预算验证最大估计；跨 prompt 用校准 prompt 学出每个组件的校正量（均值残差）与剩余误差尺寸模型（log-linear），指导后续验证并直接校正数据集平均效应（评估 prompt 零测量）。
- **理论刻画选择与校正所需信息不同**：定理证明选择该验证什么只需误差的"尺寸"，而校正还需要"符号"；这解释了为何尺寸模型 h 与校正 f 要分开拟合。
- **可扩展性验证**：固定 256 次测量的预算在 9B→32B 模型、每层 14,336→25,600 个 MLP 神经元上依然有效，测量占比降到约 1% 时收益不衰减。

## Key Contributions

- A **budgeted attribution analysis framework**: attribution is studied under a fixed budget of exact AP measurements that must serve both verification and correction, with methods compared by error left at equal compute.
- **Error-structure analysis**: AtP residuals are shown to concentrate in a few components (top 4 carry 80% of test error on GPT-2), be biased away from zero within a prompt, and recur per component across prompts of a task.
- **Guided-AP**, one estimator operating in two regimes: within-prompt (stratified pilot → median-residual shift for unmeasured components, remaining budget verifies largest |g|) and across-prompt (per-component corrections plus a log-linear residual-size model learned from calibration prompts, used to select verifications and to correct dataset-average effects with no evaluation-prompt measurements).
- **Theory separating selection from correction**: Theorem 1 quantifies when a pilot pays (shared residual sign minus displaced verification); Proposition 1 shows selection scores must track the *size* of the remaining error, not the size of the expected error — motivating fitting h separately from f.
- **Scalability evidence**: a fixed 256-measurement budget stays useful from Gemma-2-9B (14,336 neurons/layer) up to Qwen3-32B (25,600 neurons/layer, ~1% measured), and an alternative curvature-sketch calibration signal (ProbeCal) is evaluated.

## Methodology

The paper targets the isolated AP effect Δᵢ = F(eᵢ) − F(0) of each component i (attention head or MLP neuron), where F applies the component's patch direction and recomputes downstream layers, scored as a logit difference between correct and counterfactual tokens. AtP approximates all N effects via gradients gᵢ = ∂ᵢF(0) from one backward pass; its residual eᵢ = Δᵢ − gᵢ can be large because the linearization misses finite-patch curvature. Given budget B, Guided-AP builds the estimator Δ̂ᵢ = Δᵢ (exact) for measured components i ∈ S and Δ̂ᵢ = gᵢ + cᵢ (corrected AtP) otherwise.

**Within-prompt learning** needs no prior labels: it splits B into a stratified pilot S₁ (components binned into K = 8 strata by attribution magnitude rank, sampled round-robin) and a verification set S₂. The correction for every unmeasured component is the pilot residuals' median — the shift minimizing mean absolute error, robust to heavy-tailed residuals; the remaining budget verifies the largest |gᵢ| outside the pilot ("the pilot corrects the bulk; verification handles the tail").

**Across-prompt learning** acquires labels on earlier prompts (visiting prompts round-robin over magnitude-ranked components), then fits a per-component correction (median of its acquired residuals) and a log-linear size model ĥ(g, i) = exp(a·log|g| + βᵢ) that predicts the *remaining* error after correction; the B highest-scoring components are verified on new prompts. For dataset averages, each component's averaged AtP estimate is corrected by its mean labeled residual, requiring no measurement on evaluation prompts. A guard sets the correction to zero when a component's labels are unrepresentative.

**Theoretical analysis** (assuming exact measurements): Theorem 1 shows the within-prompt gain equals the best shared shift's benefit H_U minus pilot estimation cost E_U minus displaced-verification cost C, with Hoeffding-style concentration for the pilot median; Theorem 2 bounds the median correction learned from representative labels; Lemma 2 shows an offset helps an average only if it shares the residual's sign and is less than twice its magnitude; Proposition 1 shows selection by scores within ε of true remaining error costs at most 2Bε — and that a large-but-sign-flipping residual yields a near-zero correction yet large remaining error, so selection needs its own size model.

**Experiments**: within-prompt on MLP neurons of Gemma-2-9B (layers 10/20/30), Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B (B = 256, pilot P = 64); across-prompt on attention heads of Qwen3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (B ∈ {16, 32, 64, 128}); tasks are IOI, factual completion, and subject-verb agreement; 100 prompt groups per task, 1,662 layer evaluations. Primary metric is effect-vector MAE against exact FP32 AP; uncertainty via 20,000 paired group bootstraps with multiplicity adjustment over the prespecified across-prompt comparison family. Main baseline is runtime-matched verify-more (spend the same compute verifying the largest AtP magnitudes); also compared are AtP* + AP verification, no-pilot centering, selective HVP, MS-HVP, singleton integrated gradients, exhaustive AP, and ProbeCal (randomized Hessian-diagonal sketches via HVPs as an alternative calibration signal).

## Results & Highlights

- **Within-prompt, Gemma-2-9B MLP neurons**: Guided-AP lowers per-prompt effect-vector MAE vs runtime-matched verify-more by 17.4% on IOI, 26.2% on factual completion, and 19.9% on SVA (relative AP error 0.71–1.20%), winning in 96/100, 97/100, and 98/100 prompt groups respectively.
- **Scaling to 32B at fixed budget**: on Qwen3-14B MAE drops 10.9–24.0% and on Qwen3-32B 13.7–17.5% vs matched-runtime verify-more while measuring only ~1% of the 32B's 25,600 neurons per layer; post hoc, verify-more needs 5.9–14.7× more warm runtime (descriptive intervals 4.2–19.5×) to match Guided-AP's achieved error on the three 32B tasks.
- **Accuracy-targeted runtime**: at a 1% mean relative AP error target on Gemma IOI, Guided-AP runs 7.9× faster than magnitude-ranked verification (2.08 vs 16.37 s/layer, 2,416 checks); on SVA verification is already cheap (32 checks, 0.43 s/layer), and Guided-AP misses the 1% factual target (stuck at 1.20%).
- **Dataset-average correction (across prompts, Qwen3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B heads, factual completion)**: MAE of average effects cut by 75–79% vs uncorrected AtP with zero measurements on evaluation prompts; directly averaging the same calibration AP effects instead of modeling residuals is 5.8–14.9× worse; projected payback within 500–2,700 prompts.
- **Selection/correction ablations**: shifting unmeasured estimates by the median residual of *verification-chosen* measurements raises MAE in 8 of 9 settings (verification samples are unrepresentative); the stratified pilot beats that control and verification alone by 17–24% on Gemma; the median beats the mean on identical labels in all 9 settings; pilots of 32–64 suffice (128 adds no consistent gain); the fitted shift captures 96–97% of the best common shift's benefit.
- **Honest failure case**: learned average correction raises Qwen2.5 IOI dataset-average MAE by 52% (selection bias; Lemma 2's sign condition violated), across-prompt verification on Qwen2.5 IOI is worse than verify-more by 29–39% at some budgets, and SVA shows no established average-correction gain.

## Authors & Credibility

Luyang Zhang and Minxing Zheng (equal contribution) are at Carnegie Mellon University; Jialu Wang is at UC Santa Cruz and is also an author of the closely related "When attribution patching lies: Diagnosis and a second-order correction" (NeurIPS 2026), which this paper builds directly on. The work is grounded in the mainstream mechanistic-interpretability literature (activation patching best practices, AtP*, ACDC, sparse feature circuits, MIB benchmark). Credibility signals are strong: prespecified comparison families with multiplicity adjustment, 20,000-draw paired bootstrap intervals, an FP64-rescoring robustness check (retains 10–23% lower Gemma MAE), measured warm runtimes on disclosed hardware (1/2/4 L40S GPUs), and explicit reporting of settings where the method hurts.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: incremental but well-executed. The core idea — reuse a few exact measurements to correct unmeasured estimates — is a sensible budgeted-active-learning twist on a known problem (AtP's approximation error, characterized by Zhang & Wang 2026 with overlapping authorship). The novel part is the two-axis error-sharing analysis (within-prompt sign bias; across-prompt per-component recurrence) and the clean separation of what selection vs correction need (size vs sign).
- **Rigor**: above average for the genre. All costs (pilot, calibration, fitting, verification) are charged to the method; the verify-more baseline is runtime-matched, not just count-matched; negative results (Qwen2.5 IOI −52% on averages, −29/−39% on verification, missing the factual 1% target) are reported in the main text, not buried.
- **Weaknesses in evaluation breadth**: the derivative-correction comparison (AtP*, selective/MS-HVP, singleton IG) uses only a 30-group Gemma subset at fixed "tested settings" — those baselines are not tuned, so the ranking should be read cautiously. All three tasks (IOI, factual completion, SVA) are classic clean-counterfactual interpretability behaviors; open-ended behaviors where the logit-difference metric is ambiguous are untested, and the paper says so.
- **Metric caveats the authors themselves flag**: low effect-vector MAE does not imply faithfulness of a jointly-intervened circuit, and lower MAE need not improve top-ten recall — the practically relevant quantity for circuit discovery is barely moved ("top-ten recall remains nearly perfect for both methods", i.e., verification alone already nails the top-10). So the value is in *dense, accurate effect maps*, not in finding circuits you'd otherwise miss.
- **Reproducibility**: good in principle (public open-weight models, standard tasks, seeds disclosed, appendices specify strata counts and fits), though no code release is mentioned in the version read.
- **Red flags**: headline "up to 26%" is the best case (Gemma factual); the 12.3%/10.9% Qwen3-14B gains show the spread. Across-prompt "projected total cost" over 10,000 prompts is an extrapolation from measured costs, not an end-to-end deployment measurement.

## Strengths

- Turns a purely computational tradeoff (verify everything vs trust cheap estimates) into a learnable error-correction problem with a clear budget framework and equal-compute comparisons.
- Simple, robust statistics: the median shift is theoretically justified (MAE-optimal, insensitive to heavy tails) and empirically beats the mean in all nine settings.
- Real scalability evidence — the gain *persists* as components per layer grow to 25,600 and measured fraction shrinks to ~1%, which is exactly the regime where AP is least affordable.
- Theory and practice are aligned: Theorem 1's H_U − E_U − C decomposition matches the observed ablations (representative pilot vs unrepresentative verification residuals).
- Unusual honesty: failure modes (Qwen2.5 IOI) are presented with the same statistical care as successes.

## Limitations

- Evaluation is limited to transformer LMs (9B–32B, open weights), MLP neurons and attention heads, and three controlled tasks with counterfactual token pairs; sparse autoencoder features — where AP is most expensive and the authors flag the method as most needed — are left to future work, as are other architectures and open-ended behaviors.
- Within-prompt correction helps only when residuals share a sign; Lemma 2 shows a wrong-sign or oversized offset can *increase* error, and the Qwen2.5 IOI result (average MAE +52%) demonstrates this failure concretely.
- Gains are on MAE of isolated component effects; top-10 recall is already near-perfect under verification alone, so practitioners interested only in the largest components may see little benefit.
- Break-even economics are task- and budget-dependent: learned selection needs analyses of thousands to tens of thousands of prompts (projected break-even 1,900–24,000 prompts); shorter analyses favor plain verification.
- ProbeCal, the cheaper calibration signal, underperforms exact-label correction on factual averages (46% vs 75–79% MAE reduction), so the strongest results still depend on exact-label collection.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the within-prompt median-shift correction still help on sparse-autoencoder features (tens of thousands per layer with different residual statistics), which the authors identify as the regime of greatest need?
- Can the sign-flip failure on Qwen2.5 IOI be predicted *a priori* from calibration statistics (e.g., via Lemma 2's sign/magnitude condition) so the correction is automatically disabled before harming averages?
- Would combining across-prompt learned corrections with second-order AtP corrections (Zhang & Wang 2026) as the base estimator stack multiplicatively, since Guided-AP's base is plain first-order AtP?
- Do MAE improvements translate into measurably better downstream circuit discovery (e.g., on the MIB benchmark's faithfulness metrics), given that top-ten recall is already saturated?
- How does the error-structure assumption degrade under distribution shift between calibration and evaluation prompts, rather than the within-task held-out splits tested here?
