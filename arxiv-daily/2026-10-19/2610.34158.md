> **一句话总结：** 本文首次为实际部署的"有限次牛顿–舒尔茨（NS）迭代"版 Muon 优化器建立了 last-iterate（全序列）梯度收敛理论：确定性梯度下，去掉输入稳定化（ε=0）的光滑凸二次目标上会永久二循环，而任意 ε>0 配合小常数步长 η ≤ ε(1−β)/(4Lu_J) 即可对任意固定动量 β<1 保证梯度平方可和；随机梯度下固定超参会卡在非零噪声底（lim sup E|∇F| ≥ 0.02ηd₀/4），须用递减步长 η_t=η₀t^{−p} 搭配递增动量 β_t=1−α₀t^{−q}（3/4<p≤1, 2(1−p)<q<2p−1）才能几乎必然收敛；并构造光滑反例证明逐点多项式速率不可能，但 density-one 速率可达 t^{−1/2} 与 t^{−(1−p)/2}。

> **TL;DR:** This is a pure theory paper on the Muon optimizer (the AdamW alternative credited with ~2x compute efficiency in Moonlight scaling and used via MuonClip to pretrain the trillion-parameter Kimi K2). Prior analyses of practical finite-Newton–Schulz Muon (Peng et al. 2026; Wang et al. 2026; Kim & Oh, ICLR 2026) only prove average- or best-iterate stationarity on smooth nonconvex objectives; this paper upgrades to last-iterate gradient convergence with no PL/star-convexity assumptions. Two sharp obstruction results anchor the analysis: on the quadratic benchmark F(w)=0.01w², unstabilized (ε=0) Muon cycles forever with |∇F(w_t)| = 0.01δ > 0 for every fixed β<1, and under persistent ±σ unbiased noise any fixed (η, β) leaves lim sup E|∇F(w_t)| ≥ 0.02ηd₀/4 > 0. The fixes are quantified: with ε>0 and η ≤ ε(1−β)/(4Lu_J) the full gradient sequence is square-summable (deterministic), and with the coordinated schedule η_t = η₀t^{−p}, β_t = 1−α₀t^{−q} in the region 3/4 < p ≤ 1, 2(1−p) < q < 2p−1, ∥g_t∥ → 0 almost surely and in mean square despite bounded-variance noise. A smooth sparse-peak construction shows no pointwise polynomial rate is possible in either regime, while density-one rates (o_dens(t^{−1/2}) fixed-step; o_dens(t^{−(1−p)/2}) stochastic) hold for all but a vanishing fraction of iterations. No experiments — the value is explanatory theory for why the practical ε stabilizer and momentum choices work.

## 核心贡献

- **首个"实用有限 NS 步 Muon"的 last-iterate 梯度收敛保证**：在仅有光滑性 + 下有界性的非凸目标上（不需要 PL 条件或星凸性），证明实际实现（J 次五次 NS 迭代 + 输入归一化稳定化 ε）的全序列梯度 ∥∇F(W_t)∥ → 0；此前所有针对 practical Muon 的结果（Peng et al.、Wang et al.、Kim–Oh 等）都只有平均迭代或最优迭代保证。
- **反例一（确定性循环）**：ε=0 时，即使在光滑凸二次函数 F(w)=0.01w² 上，有限 NS Muon 也会对任意固定 β<1、任意混合系数 θ 形成永久二循环，梯度恒为 0.01δ>0——从机理上解释了实用实现中 ε 稳定化的必要性。
- **定理 3.2（固定步长收敛）**：ε>0 时，耦合"目标下降—动量追踪"Lyapunov 分析证明：只要 η ≤ ε(1−β)/(4Lu_J)，对任意固定 β∈[0,1) 均有 Σ∥D_t∥²、Σ∥M_t−g_t∥²、Σ∥g_t∥² < ∞（并推广到平方可和扰动与增批采样）。
- **反例二（随机噪声底）+ 定理 4.2（随机收敛）**：固定步长和动量下，持续有界无偏噪声使 lim sup E|∇F(w_t)| ≥ 0.02ηd₀/4 > 0（不收敛）；改用递减步长 + 递增动量调度（3/4<p≤1，2(1−p)<q<2p−1）后，在仅有条件无偏、有界方差噪声假设下同时建立几乎必然与均方 last-iterate 收敛。
- **速率不可能性与 density-one 速率**：构造带稀疏梯度峰的光滑目标，证明两种收敛定理下逐点多项式速率都不可能（lim sup t^ν|F′(w_t)|=∞ 对一切 ν>0）；再用计数论证建立 density-one 速率：固定步长下 o_dens(t^{−1/2})，随机 ε>0 下 o_dens(t^{−(1−p)/2})（p↓3/4 时退化为 t^{−1/8+δ}）。

## Key Contributions

- **First last-iterate stationarity guarantee for practical finite-NS Muon.** All prior practical-Muon analyses (Li–Hong; Kim–Oh; Sato et al.; Nagashima–Iiduka; Shulgin et al.; Choudhury et al.; LiMuon; Peng et al.; Wang et al.) establish only average- or best-iterate stationarity; existing last-iterate-type results require the PL condition (de Oliveira et al.'s Soft Muon; Chang et al.'s Muon–MVR objective gap) or star-convexity (Demidovich et al.). This work needs only global L-smoothness and lower boundedness.
- **A precise failure mode for the unstabilized optimizer (Proposition 3.1).** With ε=0 on F(w)=0.01w², the iterates enter an exact two-cycle w_t = (−1)^{t−1}δ/2 with |∇F(w_t)| = 0.01δ > 0 for every fixed β ∈ [0,1) and every θ ∈ [0,1] — scalar normalization at ε=0 discards magnitude, leaving sign-like steps of nonvanishing length near stationarity.
- **Fixed-step full-sequence convergence via a coupled descent–tracking Lyapunov function (Theorem 3.2).** For any ε>0, any fixed β<1, any θ, and η ≤ ε(1−β)/(4Lu_J), the update, momentum-tracking error, and gradient sequences are all square-summable, hence ∥∇F(W_t)∥ → 0. The proof couples an objective-descent lemma with a momentum-tracking contraction of factor (1+β)/2 < 1, using the finite-NS sector inequality ⟨N, Φ_ε(N)⟩ ≥ (ε/u_J)∥Φ_ε(N)∥²_F.
- **A noise-floor obstruction and its cure (Proposition 4.1 + Theorem 4.2).** Under persistent bounded, conditionally unbiased ±σ noise, any fixed (η, β) — even with ε>0 — leaves lim sup E|∇F(w_t)| ≥ 0.02ηd₀/4 > 0 and lim sup P(|∇F(w_t)| ≥ 0.01ηd₀) ≥ 1/4. Coordinating decreasing learning rates with increasing momentum (η_t = η₀t^{−p}, β_t = 1−α₀t^{−q}, admissible region 3/4 < p ≤ 1, 2(1−p) < q < 2p−1) restores almost-sure and mean-square last-iterate convergence, for both the EMA input (θ_t=1) and the Nesterov-style mixture (θ_t=β_t) used in the reference Muon implementation.
- **Pointwise rate impossibility plus density-one rates (Proposition 5.1 + Theorem 5.3).** A single smooth lower-bounded objective with sparse gradient peaks defeats pointwise polynomial rates in *both* regimes (visit times s_k ≥ Ce^{k/5} with |F′(w_{s_k})| ≥ 1/k), yet the gradient-energy bounds imply density-one rates: o_dens(t^{−1/2}) in the fixed-step regime, o_dens(t^{−(1−p)/2}) (ε>0) or o_dens(t^{−(1−p)}) (ε=0) in the stochastic regime.

## Methodology

The paper analyzes the exact core Muon update for a single matrix parameter W ∈ R^{m×n} under a loss F that is only assumed L-smooth and lower bounded (Assumption 2.1): momentum M_t = β_t M_{t−1} + (1−β_t)G_t, NS input N_t = θ_t M_t + (1−θ_t)G_t (θ_t=1 gives EMA input, θ_t=β_t gives the Nesterov-style mixture of Keller Jordan's reference implementation), direction D_t = Φ_ε(N_t), and W_{t+1} = W_t − η_t D_t. Φ_ε runs J steps (practically J=5) of the quintic Newton–Schulz polynomial P(s) = 3.4445s − 4.7750s³ + 2.0315s⁵ on the Frobenius-normalized input N/(∥N∥_F + ε), with ε ≥ 0 the input stabilizer.

Methodologically the analysis works directly with the spectral geometry of the finite quintic polynomial rather than its polar-approximation accuracy. Lemma 2.2 establishes two properties of Φ_ε that drive everything: radial bounds ℓ_J·R/(R+ε) ≤ ∥Φ_ε(N)∥_F ≤ u_J·R/(R+ε) ≤ u_J (updates are uniformly bounded by B = u_J), and an alignment bound ⟨N, Φ_ε(N)⟩_F ≥ ℓ_J R²/(R+ε); when ε>0 this sharpens into the sector inequality ⟨N, Φ_ε(N)⟩ ≥ (ε/u_J)∥Φ_ε(N)∥²_F, which is the key new geometric input.

The deterministic proof (Theorem 3.2) combines two lemmas: objective descent, F(W_{t+1})−F(W_t) ≤ −(ηκ/2 − Lη²/2)∥D_t∥² + (η/2κ)∥e_t∥², and momentum tracking, ∥e_{t+1}∥² ≤ ((1+β)/2)∥e_t∥² + (β(1+β)L²η²/(1−β))∥D_t∥², where e_t = M_t − g_t is the tracking error. Adding them through the joint Lyapunov function V_t = F(W_t) + (2η/(κ(1−β)))∥e_t∥² yields V_{t+1}−V_t ≤ −(ηκ/4)∥D_t∥² − (η/2κ)∥e_t∥² whenever η ≤ ε(1−β)/(4Lu_J); a gradient self-bound then transfers update summability to gradients. Appendix C.3 extends this to square-summable perturbations (covering growing batches b_t = ⌈t^{1+δ}⌉ at fixed hyperparameters), and Appendix C.4 derives the exact Schur-stability region of the scalar quadratic linearization, 0 < ηλa_J/ε < 2(1+β)/(1+β−2θβ), quantifying how conservative the global sufficient condition is.

The stochastic proof (Theorem 4.2) controls the mixed-input tracking error r_t = N_t − g_t. Increasing momentum averages perturbations over an effective memory of order t^q, cutting the noise contribution to E∥r_t∥ to O(t^{−q/2}), while t^{−p} steps induce a gradient-drift lag of order t^{q−p}; the admissible region 3/4 < p ≤ 1, 2(1−p) < q < 2p−1 is exactly the set where the learning-rate-weighted tracking sum converges. The balanced choice q = 2p/3 equates both exponents, giving E∥r_t∥ = O(t^{−p/3}) and η_tE∥r_t∥ = O(t^{−4p/3}), which explains the p > 3/4 threshold. An interpolation lemma (a Lipschitz-in-reparametrized-time sequence with summable ψ_ε(∥g_t∥)-energy and divergent Ση_t must converge pathwise) upgrades summed descent to full-sequence a.s. convergence, and dominated convergence gives mean square. Finally, Section 5 turns gradient-energy summability into density-one rates via a dyadic counting argument, and constructs the sparse-peak objective (bump functions ρ_k at points x_{t_k} indexed by t_k = ⌈e^k⌉) to show the update cannot jump over the narrowing peaks, killing all pointwise polynomial rates.

## Results & Highlights

- **Unstabilized cycling is exact and universal (Proposition 3.1).** On the smooth convex quadratic F(w) = 0.01w² with exact gradients, ε = 0, any fixed η > 0, β ∈ [0,1), θ ∈ [0,1], initialization w₁ = δ/2 (δ = ηc_J, c_J = φ_J(1)) yields |∇F(w_t)| = 0.01δ > 0 for all t — versus Theorem 3.2, where any ε > 0 with η ≤ ε(1−β)/(4Lu_J) gives Σ∥∇F(W_t)∥² < ∞ and hence last-iterate convergence: a strict binary separation between nonconvergence (gradient bounded away from 0 forever) and square-summability, attributable solely to the stabilizer.
- **The stochastic noise floor is quantitative (Proposition 4.1).** With G_t = 0.02w_t + ζ_t, ζ_t iid uniform on {+σ, −σ}, and any fixed η > 0, β ∈ [0,1), ε ≥ 0: lim sup_t E|∇F(w_t)| ≥ 0.02ηd₀/4 > 0 (d₀ = ℓ_J a₀/(a₀+ε), a₀ = (1−θβ)σ) and lim sup_t P(|∇F(w_t)| ≥ 0.01ηd₀) ≥ 1/4 — the gradient provably does not converge to zero in probability. Theorem 4.2 removes this floor entirely: with η_t = η₀t^{−p}, β_t = 1−α₀t^{−q} in the region 3/4 < p ≤ 1, 2(1−p) < q < 2p−1, one gets ∥g_t∥ → 0 almost surely and E∥g_t∥² → 0, plus Ση_t∥g_t∥² < ∞ a.s.
- **No pointwise polynomial rate in either regime (Proposition 5.1).** On the explicit smooth lower-bounded objective with sparse gradient peaks, under both the fixed-step settings of Theorem 3.2 and the diminishing-step schedule η_t = η₀t^{−4/5}, β_t = 1−12t^{−1/2}, there are actual visit times s_k ≥ Ce^{k/5} with |F′(w_{s_k})| ≥ 1/k, hence lim sup t^ν|F′(w_t)| = +∞ for every ν > 0 — against which Theorem 5.3's density-one rates still deliver o_dens(t^{−1/2}) (fixed-step, pathwise) and o_dens(t^{−(1−p)/2}) for ε>0 / o_dens(t^{−(1−p)}) for ε=0 (stochastic, a.s.), degrading to t^{−1/8+δ} and t^{−1/4+δ} respectively as p ↓ 3/4.
- **The global step-size certificate is quantifiably conservative (Proposition C.4).** The exact scalar Schur threshold is s = ηλa_J/ε < 2(1+β)/(1+β−2θβ); for the Nesterov-style θ = β this local limit exceeds the global sufficient condition of Theorem 3.2 by a factor η_loc/η_glob ≥ 8(1+β)/((1−β)²(1+2β)), which diverges as β → 1 — the theorem's constant is safe but far from tight, and the two certificates even scale oppositely in β (1−β vs (1−β)^{−1}).
- **Empirical context (cited, not measured here):** Moonlight scaling experiments report roughly 2x the training-compute efficiency of Muon over AdamW, and MuonClip (a Muon derivative) pretrained the trillion-parameter Kimi K2 — the motivation for grounding the practical J=5, ε>0 implementation in theory.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: genuine and well-scoped.** The claim of being the first last-iterate gradient-convergence result for practical finite-NS Muon without PL/star-convexity is supported by the detailed Table 1; the closest priors are average-iterate results for the same practical update (Peng et al. arXiv:2609.39595; Wang et al. arXiv:2609.33152), PL-based last-iterate results for a *different* Soft Muon variant (de Oliveira et al.), and a training-loss hitting-time bound for squared loss only (Roy–Chakraborthy). The two obstruction results are crisp and — unusually for theory papers — fully explicit one-liners one can verify by hand.
- **Rigor: high.** The Lyapunov coefficient arithmetic is checked to explicit constants, the Schur analysis uses exact Jury conditions, and the stochastic argument honestly handles both θ_t = 1 and θ_t = β_t input conventions. The square-summability-to-convergence upgrade via the interpolation lemma is standard but correctly applied, including the divergent-Ση_t requirement.
- **Reproducibility: mathematical, not empirical.** Everything needed to re-derive or verify the results is in the paper; however there is not a single numerical experiment — not even a 10-line simulation of the ε=0 two-cycle or the ±σ noise floor, which would have taken minutes and made Figure 1 (a hand-drawn schematic, not data) unnecessary.
- **Significance: moderate-to-good, but the practical bridge is motivational.** The analysis covers a single matrix parameter in exact arithmetic, with no weight decay and no RMS output scaling — whereas the Muon variants used for Moonlight/Kimi K2 add both. The stochastic cure (momentum β_t = 1−α₀t^{−q} increasing to 1) is a schedule no LLM pretraining run uses, so Theorem 4.2 explains a regime adjacent to, not identical with, practice.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (a) Proposition 3.1's cycling requires ε = 0 exactly, while every cited practical implementation already uses ε > 0 — the theorem formalizes a hazard practice has already sidestepped (a point the paper acknowledges only implicitly); (b) the sufficient step size η ≤ ε(1−β)/(4Lu_J) is conservative by the authors' own Prop C.4 factor ≥ 8(1+β)/((1−β)²(1+2β)), so the theorem gives little usable guidance for choosing η; (c) density-one rates are never numerically contrasted with the average-iterate rates of prior work (e.g., what O(T^{−1/2})-style avg-iterate bounds correspond to in this framework), leaving the practical cost of the last-iterate upgrade unquantified; (d) the counterexamples are one-dimensional, and the paper does not discuss whether the cycling/noise-floor phenomena persist for genuine matrix-valued (m×n) dynamics where singular-value interactions exist.

## Strengths

- Delivers a clean conceptual explanation for *why* the practical ε stabilizer exists: it converts the finite-NS map's alignment property into the sector inequality (2.8) that powers the entire Lyapunov argument — a takeaway useful far beyond this paper.
- Both negative results (two-cycle at ε=0; noise floor at fixed hyperparameters) are constructive, elementary, and fully explicit, making the positive theorems clearly non-vacuous.
- Covers both momentum-input conventions actually found in implementations (EMA θ=1 and Nesterov-style θ=β), rather than a convenient special case.
- The admissible exponent region 3/4 < p ≤ 1, 2(1−p) < q < 2p−1 is derived from a transparent noise-averaging vs gradient-drift trade-off, with the balanced schedule q = 2p/3 explaining the 3/4 threshold rather than leaving it as an artifact.
- Honest, thorough literature positioning (Table 1 plus a structured appendix), and explicit acknowledgment that the global step-size condition is conservative, complemented rather than hidden by the exact local analysis of Prop C.4.

## Limitations

- No experiments of any kind; even illustrative simulations of the two obstructions or the schedules are absent.
- Single matrix parameter, exact arithmetic, no weight decay, no output rescaling — real Muon deployments (Moonlight, Kimi K2's MuonClip) differ in all of these.
- The stochastic convergence requires an increasing-momentum + decreasing-step schedule that is not used in LLM pretraining; constant-hyperparameter stochastic training — the practically relevant regime — provably does not converge here, which somewhat inverts the practical message.
- All rates are asymptotic (density-one); no finite-time, non-asymptotic bounds are given, and the paper does not compare its density-one exponents against the best known average-iterate rates for the same method.
- Counterexamples are 1-D; extension of the impossibility and cycling results to matrix-valued objectives is left open.

## Relevance

Muon/MuonClip 已是 Kimi K2、Moonlight 级别大规模预训练的现实优化器选项，本文直接回应"LLM Training Stability & Observability"方向中梯度异常与训练发散的根源问题：它给出 Muon 在何种超参组合下必然振荡（ε=0 二循环）或被噪声卡死（固定步长噪声底）的判据，并证明输入稳定化 ε 与动量调度的收敛作用——对大规模训练中优化器失稳的诊断、超参安全域选择和 monitoring 指标（逐迭代梯度范数而非滑窗平均）有直接的理论指导价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the ε=0 two-cycle and the fixed-hyperparameter noise floor survive in genuine matrix-valued settings (m×n with n ≥ 2), where multiple singular values interact through the NS polynomial?
- Can last-iterate stationarity be extended to the full practical stack — weight decay, RMS output scaling, and per-layer update scaling — used in Moonlight/Kimi K2 training?
- Inside the admissible region 3/4 < p ≤ 1, 2(1−p) < q < 2p−1, which (p, q) optimizes finite-time gradient decay rather than asymptotic guarantees, and does the balanced q = 2p/3 schedule remain near-optimal?
- Can gradient clipping or restarts upgrade the density-one rates o_dens(t^{−1/2}) to high-probability pointwise rates by eliminating the sparse-peak visits?
- Do the paper's obstructions manifest in real LLM training curves (e.g., persistent gradient-norm oscillations in late training with Muon), and could the sector-inequality constant ε/u_J be monitored online as a stability certificate?
- How does the increasing-momentum noise-filtering mechanism interact with distributed Muon training, where gradient noise is correlated across data-parallel shards rather than independent?
