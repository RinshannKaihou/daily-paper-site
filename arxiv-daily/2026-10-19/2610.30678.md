> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了六个百万级标签的最小对(minimal-pair)安全数据集，在 5 个模型家族、20 个基座模型（0.5B–72B）上训练两种探针架构，发现探针误差 E = 1 − AUROC 遵循两条简洁的标度律——误差下限随模型规模按幂律下降（E(N)=A·N^−α），随标签数按 Hill 函数逼近该下限（D0 = B/√N）——用小模型拟合后可在 72% 的情况下把下一个更大模型的探针误差预测到 ±0.02 以内，并能提前判断"加标签"与"换更大模型"哪个更省（73% 正确率）、该用哪种探针架构（选错时代价仅 +0.006）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper establishes the first scaling laws for activation-based safety probes. On six new minimal-pair safety datasets (up to 10^6 training labels each, 36,000-example test sets), spanning Truth (from Wikidata5M), Sycophancy, Policy violation, General harm, Jailbreak and Prompt injection, the authors probe 20 base models from five families (Qwen2.5 0.5B–72B, Qwen3.5, Gemma-4, Ministral-3, OLMo-3) with two architectures (last-token linear probe and the all-layer CC++ probe). Probe error E = 1 − AUROC falls as a power law in model size (on Truth/Qwen2.5/CC++: 0.18 at 0.5B → 0.056 at 72B, α = 0.22, R² = 0.94, each size doubling multiplies error by 0.86) and approaches this floor with labels via a Hill form with D0 = B/√N (the 0.5B model needs 8,200 labels for half the distance to its floor vs only 240 for 72B). Fitted on a family's smallest models, the laws predict the next size's error within 0.02 in 72% of cases (vs 54% for a naive log-linear baseline) and pick the cheaper of "more labels vs bigger model" in 73% of cases (vs 38%/44% for Hoffmann/Kaplan-style forms) — letting safety teams plan probes before spending label budget.

## 核心贡献

- **首次建立探针标度律。** 此前所有探针研究（Segment-level、RepE、Mass-mean、CC++ 等）都只变动"规模/家族/任务/标签数/架构"中的少数几个因素；本文是第一个将五者同时系统变动并用外推检验拟合规律的工作（Table 1）。
- **构建六个大规模最小对安全数据集。** 公开安全数据集要么探针在 32 个标签时就接近天花板、要么标签先耗尽，无法刻画完整学习曲线；新数据集每库训练池 ≥ 10^6 行、测试集固定 36,000 行，且最小对构造（两例仅差决定标签的片段）消除了表面词汇线索——公开数据集的标签用词袋分类器就能读出。
- **两条定律 + 一个共享常数。** 规模律 E(N) = A·N^−α 设定误差下限；标签律 E(N,D) = E(N) + (0.5 − E(N))·B/(B + D√N) 描述向该下限的收敛，其中 √N 使全部模型尺寸共享一个常数 B。两条定律在 5 个家族、6 个任务、2 种探针架构上均成立，只有拟合常数不同。
- **可操作的三种预测应用。** 用家族中最小的 k≥3 个模型拟合即可：(1) 预测下一更大模型的探针表现；(2) 判断达到目标误差时"多打标签"与"换更大模型"哪个更省（成本 = 参数量 × 标签数）；(3) 提前选择探针架构。
- **误差指标选择的方法论点。** 用 E = 1 − AUROC 而非 AUROC 本身，避免有界指标把平滑趋势伪装成"饱和"的假象。

## Key Contributions

- **First scaling laws for probe performance.** No prior probe study varied model scale, family, task, label count and architecture together, let alone tested fitted laws by extrapolation; this paper does all six (Table 1).
- **Six new minimal-pair safety datasets** (Truth, Sycophancy, Policy violation, General harm, Jailbreak, Prompt injection), each with ≥ 10^6 training rows and a fixed 36,000-row test set. Public safety datasets are shown to be unusable for curve-tracing: probes hit the AUROC ceiling at D = 32 on some, others run out of labels, and a bag-of-words classifier can read their labels from surface words.
- **Two laws with one shared constant per setting.** A size law E(N) = A·N^−α for the error floor, and a label law where error falls from chance (0.5) toward the floor following a Hill function with D0 = B/√N — the square root derived empirically (log D0 vs log N slope −0.51, SE 0.15) so one constant B serves all model sizes.
- **Pre-registered extrapolation protocol.** Laws are fitted on a family's k smallest models and the prediction for the next size is recorded before measuring, mirroring the Kaplan/Hoffmann methodology.
- **Three practical decision tools**: predicting an unprobed model's error, choosing between more labels and a larger model under a parameters × labels cost, and choosing the probe architecture — each validated against sensible baselines.

## Methodology

The paper first constructs six datasets from public seeds using one minimal-pair recipe: Truth from Wikidata5M triples over 37 relation templates (1,151,844 rows); Sycophancy by embedding Truth pairs in conversations where the assistant agrees with the user's claim (10 user formats); Policy violation from PKU-SafeRLHF / BeaverTails / AEGIS 1.0–2.0 content paired with the policy clause it violates vs one it does not (human majority-vote labels; 1,535,252 rows); General harm from 31 public red-team sources where Claude rewrites each request "as a real person would type it" plus a near-identical harmless twin (median 7 word changes), with word-side balancing so no single token gives the label away (1,045,342 rows); Jailbreak reusing those pairs inside 261 wrappers (257 public templates + 4 encodings such as leetspeak/rot13); Prompt injection placing pairs inside 65 shells from three agent benchmarks (e.g. AgentDojo, CyberSecEval 2).

Probing setup: 20 base checkpoints from five families — all seven Qwen2.5 sizes (0.5B–72B) plus 13 dense models from Qwen3.5, Gemma-4, Ministral-3 and OLMo-3 — with 39 further checkpoints (59 models total) analyzed in the appendix, including MoE, post-trained and mid-pretraining models. Two architectures: a last-token linear probe (L2-regularised logistic regression on one layer of one token; 3,584 weights at 7B) and the CC++ probe of Cunningham et al. (all layers, per-token scores, max of smoothed scores; 100,352 weights at 7B). Each probe is trained at ten label counts log-spaced from 32 to 10^6 with three seeds, and scored by E = 1 − AUROC on the fixed 36,000-row test set.

Fitting: the size law is fitted by least squares on log error across a family's sizes at the largest label count; six candidate learning-curve functions for the label term are compared (the one-parameter Hill function wins with RMSE 0.017 vs 0.028 for the next best); per-model D0 values are collapsed into B/√N. For the last-token probe, whose curve rises more gradually, a steepness exponent g is added (Eq. 3), giving g = 0.61 on Qwen2.5, while CC++ keeps g = 1 (fitted 0.81–1.02). Extrapolation is then judged by fitting on the k smallest models (k ≥ 3) and predicting the next size unseen.

## Results & Highlights

- **Size law (Truth, CC++, Qwen2.5, 10^6 labels):** probe error falls from 0.18 at 0.5B to 0.056 at 72B, fitting E(N) = A·N^−α with A = 0.15 and α = 0.22 (SE 0.02); R² = 0.94 on log error with no model more than 0.012 off the line — each doubling of model size multiplies the error floor by 0.86 (−14%). The law holds in every family, but the doubling multiplier differs: 0.76 (Qwen3.5), 0.79 (Gemma-4), 0.86 (Qwen2.5), 0.89 (OLMo-3), 0.93 (Ministral-3), with every model within 0.018 of its family's line.
- **Label law (same setting):** the Hill form with shared B = 3,400 fits the 70 measurements with RMS deviation 0.030 (per-model D0 fitting: 0.017; worst case 0.115). The 0.5B model needs 8,200 labels to cover half the distance from chance to its floor, while the 72B model needs only 240 — larger models saturate sooner.
- **Next-model prediction:** fitted on the smallest k ≥ 3 sizes, Eq. 1 at 10^6 labels predicts the next size's error within 0.02 in 72% of 72 predictions and within 0.05 in 97%, vs 54% within 0.02 for a naive straight line through the two largest fitted models. Full learning-curve prediction (Eq. 2/3, 702 predictions over 32–10^6 labels) is within 0.02 in 47% and within 0.05 in 82%. On Qwen2.5, within-0.02 accuracy rises from 7/12 at k = 3 to 10/12 at k = 6.
- **Labels vs larger model (cost = parameters × labels):** across 45 clearly-decided cases, the laws pick the cheaper option in 73%, vs 38% for the Hoffmann-style additive form, 44% for the Kaplan-style joint form, 40% for the current model's own power-law curve, and 58% for a progressive-sampling stopping rule. Worked example (Truth, CC++, target error 0.13): 7B needs a predicted 14,900 more labels (cost ≈ 113k) while 14B needs 7,230 (cost ≈ 107k); the law picks 14B and the measurements confirm it.
- **Architecture selection:** for Qwen2.5-32B on Truth, Eq. 1 predicts floors of 0.065 (CC++) vs 0.077 (last-token) at 10^6 labels, and the predicted learning curves cross at 9,300 labels vs 9,100 measured. Over 200 random model × label draws, the laws pick the better probe (or one within 0.005) in 82% of cases vs 68% for a fixed "last-token below 10^4 labels, CC++ above" rule and 61% for always using CC++; when wrong, the extra error averages only 0.006 (paired sign test vs the fixed rule: 60/92, p = 0.005).
- **Task generality:** all six tasks follow Eq. 1; five deviate by only 0.005–0.010 on average, while Policy violation deviates by 0.016 average / 0.023 worst at 0.5B — attributed by the authors to its noisier mixed-source labels (human votes, listed categories, LLM judges).

## Authors & Credibility

Yuchen Wu (University of Washington) and Jerry Wei (Anthropic). Wei is a co-author of the CC++ / Constitutional Classifiers line at Anthropic (cited as refs 15–16), i.e. the all-layer streaming probe benchmarked here comes from the authors' own prior work — meaning the probe architectures compared are implementations they know intimately, though it also means the CC++ comparison is not fully third-party. The paper follows the methodological discipline of the Kaplan/Hoffmann scaling-law tradition (fit on small, predict large, record predictions before measuring), reports standard errors and worst-case deviations throughout, and its 62 pages include exhaustive appendices documenting dataset construction (with per-dataset worked pairs and sources), all fits, and negative results such as the looser Policy-violation fit. The scale of the experimental grid — 6 tasks × 5 families × 2 architectures × 10 label counts × 3 seeds — is unusually thorough for a probing paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** genuinely novel quantity and question. Prior probe studies each varied only a subset of factors (their Table 1 is a fair and damning summary), and no one had shown that monitor *readability* (as opposed to the harmful behaviour itself) scales. The transfer of the pretraining-scaling-law playbook — including held-out extrapolation — to probe error is a real contribution, not an incremental one.
- **Rigor:** strong. Six candidate learning-curve functions were compared before adopting the Hill form; the collapse of per-model D0 to B/√N is checked (shared-exponent fit 0.47, indistinguishable from 1/2); predictions are recorded before measurement; and the decision experiments are benchmarked against honest alternatives (Hoffmann/Kaplan forms, own-curve extrapolation, progressive sampling). Deviations are reported as RMS *and* worst case rather than only averages.
- **But the "laws" fix form, not constants.** Every family, task and architecture needs its own A, α, B (and g) refit — B alone spans 400 (Qwen3.5) to 3,400 (Qwen2.5). Predicting a *new* family still requires probing at least three of its sizes first (the authors say so), so the practical claim is "probe small, predict large", not "predict with zero probing". The abstract's framing slightly oversells the generality.
- **Dataset ecological validity.** The datasets are synthetic minimal pairs: the General-harm twins are authored by Claude, and Sycophancy seeds are filtered by Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct — the same family later probed, a mild circularity. Minimal pairs kill lexical shortcuts (good for measuring *representation* quality) but are not deployment traffic; probe error measured on balanced artificial pairs says little about false-positive-constrained operating points, which the paper relegates to Appendix E.7.
- **Base-only fitting vs post-trained deployment.** The laws are fitted on base checkpoints, yet a deployed monitor reads a post-trained model. The evidence for carry-over is preliminary: Qwen2.5-Instruct shifts α from 0.27 to 0.30, and post-trained Qwen3.5-27B / Qwen3.6-27B sit ~0.005 *above* the Qwen3.5 base line — the opposite direction, unexplained.
- **Scale and model-class ceilings.** The largest model is 72B; frontier-scale extrapolation is untested. MoE models "probe like" dense models of a size between active and total parameters, but which count belongs in Eq. 1 is left open.
- **Modest case counts in the decision experiments:** 45 filtered cases for labels-vs-model (further restricted to ≥ 2× cost differences and reachable targets) and 200 draws for architecture choice — the 73% figure rests on a small, favourably filtered base.

## Strengths

- First systematic answer to a practical safety-operations question (how many labels, which model, which probe) with quantitative, validated predictions.
- Exceptional experimental breadth: 6 tasks, 5 families, 20 (+39) models, 2 probe architectures, 32–10^6 labels, 3 seeds, 36k-row test sets.
- The minimal-pair dataset recipe is itself reusable infrastructure — it fixes the ceiling-effect and label-exhaustion failures that make public safety datasets useless for scaling studies.
- Careful metric choice (E = 1 − AUROC) with an explicit argument about bounded metrics faking saturation.
- Honest reporting: worst-case deviations, negative results (Policy violation fit, 47% within-0.02 for full-curve prediction), and a limitations appendix that names the base/post-trained gap rather than hiding it.

## Limitations

- Constants do not transfer across families, tasks or architectures; only the functional form is universal, and at least three sizes of a family must be probed before the laws become predictive.
- Training and test data come from the same synthetic minimal-pair pipeline; distribution shift to real traffic, multilingual content and low-false-positive operating points is unaddressed (partially deferred to Appendix E.7).
- Laws are fitted on base models up to 72B dense; post-trained, frontier-scale and MoE regimes are validated only preliminarily, and the effective parameter count for MoE is unresolved.
- The cost model (parameters × labels) ignores annotation price, inference/serving cost and label difficulty; the authors acknowledge other cost definitions only change the final decision step.
- Policy-violation labels mix human votes, category listings and LLM judges, producing the loosest fits in the paper (0.016 avg / 0.023 worst deviation) — label noise bounds how well any law can fit that task.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中 "LLM Hidden States & Interpretability" 兴趣方向：它系统刻画了线性探针读取 LLM 内部隐藏状态的性能随模型规模与标签数的标度规律，是 probing 与 scaling laws 两个主题的结合。此外，"用小模型拟合规律、提前规划监控资源（标签预算、模型尺寸、探针架构）"的思路对模型监控与可观测性研究也有方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the laws survive on post-trained (RLHF'd) frontier models with more than one family probed at scale, and does α shift systematically with the post-training recipe (the observed 0.27 → 0.30 base→Instruct shift suggests yes)?
- How does the error floor move under distribution shift between minimal-pair training data and real deployment traffic — is the floor a property of the model or of the train/test match?
- Can the two laws be unified with pretraining-compute scaling, given the appendix finding that probe error also falls as pretraining proceeds — i.e., is there a joint law E(N, training tokens, D)?
- Do nonlinear or dictionary-based monitors (SAE feature classifiers, small MLP probes) follow the same functional form with shifted constants, or does architecture family change the law itself?
- Is there a principled effective-parameter count for MoE models that collapses them onto the dense-family lines, between active and total parameters?
- Do the size-law predictions hold when the operating metric is miss rate at a fixed low false-positive rate (Appendix E.7) rather than AUROC error, which is what deployments actually constrain?
