> **一句话总结：** 提出 SOMA——把 SAM 式锐度扰动加到 Muon 优化器上，但复用上一轮已算出的"陈旧"扰动梯度与动量来构造谱几何扰动，每步只需一次梯度评估（开销仅 +3%），理论上首次证明该类算法在重尾噪声下收敛（误差底 O(ρ^ν)），实验显示它保持 Muon 的预训练质量并使 Pythia-70M 的 4-bit 量化退化从 10.96% 降到 10.55%（双梯度的 FP-SOMA 降到 10.20%）。

> **TL;DR:** SOMA adds a sharpness-aware perturbation to the Muon optimizer essentially for free, by reusing the previous iteration's perturbed gradient and Muon's momentum buffer to build a "friendly" spectral-norm perturbation (T+1 gradient evaluations over T steps vs 2T for SAM-style methods). It is the first SAM+Muon pairing with a convergence guarantee under heavy-tailed (p-bounded central moment) noise, with an irreducible error floor O(ρ^ν) that is noise-independent. Empirically, the base optimizer dominates: on continued pretraining of nanoGPT (GPT-2 small, 124M) on FineWeb-Edu, SOMA matches Muon's validation perplexity (24.40 vs 24.41) at ~3% extra runtime, while the two-gradient FP-SOMA/SSM variants are slightly worse (24.54-24.55) and cost 2.4-2.9x; the clearest payoff is 4-bit AWQ post-training quantization on Pythia-70M, where SOMA cuts perplexity degradation from 10.96% to 10.55% (FP-SOMA: 10.20%, the best INT4 perplexity 37.08 vs Muon's 37.28), and small reductions in catastrophic forgetting (4-16% less than Muon on nanoGPT). If you train with Muon and quantize afterwards, this is a cheap insurance policy; if you expected sharpness awareness to improve pretraining perplexity itself, the paper honestly shows it does not.

## 核心贡献

- **SOMA 算法框架**：首次将锐度感知最小化（SAM）与 Muon 以近乎零额外代价结合——用上一迭代的扰动梯度 G_{t-1} 减去缩放动量 (1-β)M_{t-1} 构造"友好"残差，经 Newton-Schulz 正交化后作为谱几何扰动；T 步只需 T+1 次梯度评估（SAM/FP-SOMA 需 2T 次），实测每 epoch 仅比 Muon 慢 3%。
- **多个扰动变体**：FP-SOMA（新鲜梯度、双倍代价）、OP-SOMA Pre/Post-NSq（投影掉与上一步 Muon 更新方向对齐的分量，使扰动保持对抗性）、MSOMA（沿负动量扰动）；并用 Frobenius 与谱两种几何对照。
- **首个重尾噪声下的收敛理论**：在 (ν,L)-光滑与 p-有界中心矩（p-BCM，1<p≤2）噪声假设下，证明 SOMA 在核范数意义下收敛到 ε-平稳点；扰动带来与噪声无关的不可约误差底 O(χ_q L ρ^ν)，且结论对任意扰动方向不变；ρ=0 时退回 Muon 的收敛率 O(T^{-ν(p-1)/δ})。
- **陈旧梯度的代价有界**（Lemma 1）：复用上一步扰动梯度仅引入偏差 ≤ L(ρ+η)、曲率偏移 O(ρ²(ρ+η))，即"省一次梯度几乎不损精度"。
- **系统的实证审视**：在可控重尾噪声的合成问题、nanoGPT/Pythia-70M 持续预训练（FineWeb-Edu）、CIFAR-10 Airbench、4-bit AWQ 量化与灾难性遗忘五个维度上对比 15 个优化器，结论诚实——基础优化器决定上限，锐度扰动的收益主要在量化鲁棒性与遗忘缓解上。

## Key Contributions

- **SOMA**: the first computationally cheap pairing of sharpness-aware minimization with Muon. It reuses the previous iteration's perturbed gradient G_{t-1} and the scaled Muon momentum (1-β)M_{t-1} (which acts as an EMA) to form a "friendly" residual, orthogonalized by q Newton-Schulz steps into a spectral-geometry perturbation — T+1 gradient evaluations over T iterations instead of SAM-style 2T, and ~3% wall-clock overhead over Muon on nanoGPT.
- **A family of perturbation rules** within one framework: FP-SOMA (fresh gradient, 2 evals/iter), OP-SOMA Pre-NSq and Post-NSq (project out the component aligned with the previous Muon step, since a perturbation that reinforces the last move is not adversarial), MSOMA (perturb along negative momentum, adapting MSAM), plus Frobenius-geometry and random-perturbation controls.
- **First convergence theory for SAM+Muon, and for SAM under heavy-tailed noise at all**: under (ν,L)-operator-norm smoothness and p-bounded central moment (p-BCM) noise, SOMA reaches ε-stationarity in nuclear norm with an irreducible floor O(χ_q L ρ^ν) that is independent of the noise tail; the bound is invariant to the perturbation direction and recovers Muon's rate O(T^{-ν(p-1)/δ}) at ρ=0 (O(T^{-1/4}) for p=2, ν=1).
- **Temporal-lag lemma** showing the stale-gradient trick costs little: bias bounded by L(ρ+η), matching usual SAM error when η ≲ ρ.
- **An unusually honest empirical audit** across 15 optimizers: synthetic teacher-student problems with controlled Pareto tail index α, continued pretraining of nanoGPT and Pythia-70M on FineWeb-Edu, CIFAR-10 Airbench, 4-bit AWQ post-training quantization, and catastrophic-forgetting fine-tuning on four datasets (codeparrot, stackmathqa, musicpile, tulu3).

## Methodology

The paper first formalizes the gap: SAM variants were designed around SGD (F-SAM removes the harmful full-gradient component from the perturbation; MSAM reuses momentum to avoid the second gradient pass), while Muon — which orthogonalizes momentum of matrix-valued parameters via Newton-Schulz iteration — has only one SAM pairing (the unpublished spectral SSM of Zhong et al., which doubles gradient cost and lacks theory), and no SAM analysis covers heavy-tailed noise despite its prevalence in LLM training. SOMA fills the gap: at each step it forms the perturbation E_t = ρ·NSq(G_{t-1} − γ(1-β)M_{t-1}), where G_{t-1} is the previous perturbed minibatch gradient (already computed) and (1-β)M_{t-1} turns Muon's momentum buffer into an EMA — no fresh gradient and no separate EMA buffer needed. The gradient is then evaluated at W_{t-1}+E_t and fed into the standard Muon update (momentum accumulation, Newton-Schulz orthogonalization with q=5, weight update). Theoretically, the analysis treats any SAM perturbation as evaluating Muon at a perturbed point: Theorem 1 gives the ε-stationarity bound in nuclear norm with the SAM term split into a decaying part and the irreducible floor O(χ_q L ρ^ν), where χ_q is the Newton-Schulz approximation factor (χ_q → 1 as q → ∞). Experiments use a synthetic teacher-student network with injected two-sided Pareto noise (tail index α ∈ {1.1, 1.6, 2, 3}; infinite variance for α ≤ 2) with anisotropic conditioning κ_ε = 10, sweeping 10 perturbation radii ρ over 8 seeds; real-data runs use continued pretraining of nanoGPT (GPT-2 small, 124M) and Pythia-70M on FineWeb-Edu sample-10BT (512-token context, 100 steps/epoch, 15/14 epochs, 3 seeds), the Airbench CNN on CIFAR-10 (7 epochs), 4-bit AWQ quantization (group size 128, 128 calibration sequences) evaluated on 2^20 held-out tokens, and forgetting measured as the rise in held-out FineWeb-Edu loss after fine-tuning on four datasets, compared at equal fine-tuning loss.

## Results & Highlights

- **Heavy-tailed synthetic (α=1.1, heaviest tails):** Muon-based methods reach ~38-40% validation accuracy vs ~36% for SGD-based and only ~29% for AdamW-based; adding any perturbation shifts best accuracy by at most 1.7 points — the base optimizer, not the perturbation, sets robustness. SOMA improves Muon's optimality gap by 1.0-2.3 points (up to 40% gap reduction under the heaviest tails) and stays within ±0.8 points of the two-gradient FP-SOMA at half the gradient evaluations; spectral perturbations consistently beat Frobenius ones, while SAM/F-SAM on SGD degrade sharply.
- **Continued pretraining on FineWeb-Edu (nanoGPT):** SOMA matches Muon's final validation perplexity (24.40 ± 0.01 vs 24.41 ± 0.02) while the two-gradient SSM and FP-SOMA are slightly worse (24.55 and 24.54); AdamW gets 24.60 and SGD 25.75. On Pythia-70M, MSOMA collapses to 37.41 val ppl vs Muon's 35.72 — momentum-direction perturbation actively hurts at this scale. SOMA costs only 3% more per epoch than Muon, vs 2.4-2.9x for SSM/FP-SOMA.
- **4-bit AWQ post-training quantization (Pythia-70M, the clearest win):** quantization raises perplexity 10.2-14.6% across methods; FP-SOMA cuts Muon's degradation from 10.96% to 10.20% and achieves the lowest INT4 perplexity (37.08 ± 0.10 vs Muon's 37.28 ± 0.10), and the one-gradient SOMA recovers about half of that gain (10.55%). SAM/F-SAM similarly cut AdamW's degradation from 11.12% to ~10.4%. On nanoGPT the INT4 perplexity is nearly identical across the Muon family (23.85-23.87) — no meaningful gain at 124M.
- **Catastrophic forgetting (nanoGPT, within the Muon family):** compared at equal fine-tuning loss, FP-SOMA lowers the average FineWeb-Edu loss increase by 16% vs Muon, SSM by 11%, OP-SOMA-Pre by 8%, SOMA by 4% (relative increase 4.20% → 3.50% for FP-SOMA); on code (codeparrot) the increase drops from 0.37 to 0.24. On Pythia-70M all Muon-based methods forget equally (within 1%).
- **CIFAR-10 Airbench CNN:** Muon-based methods train fastest with the lowest training loss (0.266 vs 0.316 AdamW), but AdamW-based methods end with the highest validation accuracy (87.87% vs SSM's 87.63% and Muon's 87.27%; SGD family ~84.7%) — replicating Zhong et al.'s vision finding for SSM while showing it does not transfer to language models.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from the Department of CSE and the Department of Mathematics at IIIT-Delhi (first four authors contributed equally; the group of Bapi Chatterjee works on optimization for deep learning). The paper carries a detailed AI-use disclosure statement (generative AI used for implementation help, literature search, and drafting/editing, with theorems and proofs claimed to be human-only) — an increasingly standard and commendable practice. Code is released at github.com/dcll-iiitd/soma. The empirical methodology is above average in honesty: 3 seeds everywhere, perturbation radius swept over 10 values, learning rate tuned once per base optimizer and held fixed across noise levels, calibration-seed averaging for quantization, and forgetting compared at equal fine-tuning loss. The 43-page paper includes a thorough related-work survey (Appendix 2) and full ablations (Frobenius vs spectral, stale vs fresh gradients, isotropic vs anisotropic noise).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but incremental.** The one-gradient trick is a sensible adaptation of MSAM/F-SAM ideas to spectral geometry, and the heavy-tail convergence guarantee is genuinely the first for any SAM variant. But the theory is direction-agnostic (Remark 2: the bound holds for *any* perturbation direction, including random ones) — it proves sharpness perturbations do not *hurt* convergence, not that SOMA's "friendly" direction is why it works. The paper's own synthetic data shows a random spectral perturbation (RSM) matches SOMA, which undercuts the central design motivation even as the authors report it transparently.
- **Small-scale evaluation.** All LLM experiments are at 70M-124M parameters (Pythia-70M, GPT-2 small); the authors themselves list "testing SOMA at a larger scale" as future work. The PTQ benefit is clearest precisely on the smaller, more quantization-fragile Pythia-70M (+0.76pp relative) and nearly vanishes on nanoGPT (INT4 ppl 23.85-23.87 across the whole Muon family), so the headline use case is supported by only one model.
- **Confounded forgetting experiment.** The catastrophic-forgetting runs fine-tune an AdamW-pretrained model, so Muon-based methods suffer a known optimizer-mismatch penalty — the reported 1.6x-2.1x more forgetting vs AdamW is likely inflated, and the authors admit this. The within-Muon comparisons are cleaner but the SOMA-specific gain is only 4% on nanoGPT and 0% on Pythia.
- **Weak baselines where convenient.** SGD-family numbers on LLM pretraining (25.75 vs 24.41 val ppl) are far off the pace, and the authors themselves mark SGD forgetting results as "not comparable"; the SSM competitor is a concurrent unpublished manuscript, so the comparison cannot be fully audited.
- **Honest negative results partially redeem these weaknesses:** MSOMA (their own variant) diverges on Pythia-70M (37.41 vs 35.72 val ppl), sharpness awareness does not improve final perplexity, and AdamW wins the CIFAR-10 accuracy race — all reported plainly rather than buried.

## Strengths

- First convergence theory for SAM-type methods under heavy-tailed (p-BCM) noise, cleanly separating a noise-independent error floor O(χ_q L ρ^ν) from decaying terms, with Muon's known rate recovered at ρ=0.
- The stale-gradient design is genuinely practical: T+1 vs 2T gradient evaluations, ~3% epoch overhead, no extra memory buffer, drop-in for any Muon codebase.
- Comprehensive, well-controlled experimental design: exact tail-index control on synthetic problems, 15 optimizers spanning three base families, radius sweeps, multi-seed, and evaluation axes (pretraining, PTQ, forgetting) that match real deployment concerns for Muon-trained models.
- Refreshing honesty: negative and null results (base optimizer dominates; SOMA does not beat Muon on perplexity; MSOMA failure; Pythia forgetting null) are stated in the main text, not hidden.

## Limitations

- Model scale caps external validity: nothing beyond 124M parameters; Muon's main adoption is for far larger models, where the PTQ and forgetting effects could differ in either direction.
- Quantization-robustness gains are moderate (10.96% → 10.55% relative perplexity rise for SOMA) and demonstrated convincingly on only one of two models; no evaluation on downstream tasks (only perplexity), and only weight-only 4-bit AWQ is tested.
- The theory's perturbation-direction invariance means it cannot explain why spectral/friendly perturbations empirically beat Frobenius/random ones in some settings — the mechanism remains empirical.
- The forgetting protocol inherits the optimizer-mismatch confound, and per-dataset forgetting tables show large variance sources (code/math vs music) that a 4-dataset average may obscure.

## Relevance

与你的"极致性能优化下的可靠性"方向直接相关：该论文的核心实证贡献正是训练期优化器选择对 4-bit 量化误差（AWQ 后困惑度退化）的影响——SOMA 以约 3% 的训练开销为 Muon 训练的模型换取量化鲁棒性。同时它对 LLM 训练中重尾梯度噪声下的优化器行为（训练稳定性方向）给出了首个 SAM 类收敛保证与系统实证。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the PTQ robustness gains persist at 1B+ parameter scale, where loss-landscape geometry and quantization sensitivity differ substantially from 70M-124M models?
- Does fine-tuning a Muon-pretrained model with SOMA (avoiding the optimizer-mismatch confound) change the catastrophic-forgetting picture, as the authors hint?
- Why does a random spectral perturbation (RSM) match SOMA on the synthetic problem — is the "friendly residual" direction ever measurably better, or is spectral normalization alone doing all the work?
- Can the theory's direction-invariance be tightened (e.g., under Hessian anisotropy assumptions) to actually distinguish friendly from random perturbations?
- Does SOMA interact with other PTQ schemes (GPTQ, low-rank quantization, FP8 training) or with quantization-aware training in the same way as with AWQ?
