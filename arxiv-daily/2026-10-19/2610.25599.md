> **一句话总结：** 本文构建了一个 3,594 条样本的副语言冲突基准（情感韵律/说话人身份/反讽节奏），发现六个音频 LLM 在文本与音频矛盾时平均 78% 听信文本；通过对 Qwen2-Audio-7B 的电路分析证明被忽略的声学信息在 LM 输入端仍可线性解码（探测准确率 1.000），失败源于"中间token接力传播"而非感知缺失——中层注意力抑制可在自由生成下把文本主导率从 1.00 降到 0.38，而 back-patching 与 5 倍音频放大均告失败。

> **TL;DR:** On a new 3,594-item LISTEN-style conflict benchmark built from RAVDESS and MUStARD++, six audio LLMs (Qwen2-Audio, SALMONN, DeSTA2.5, Audex-30B, Phi-4-multimodal, Baichuan-Omni-1.5) follow a conflicting transcript over the audio on 78% of mismatched items (Cochran's Q, all p < 1e-40). Mechanistic analysis of Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct shows the ignored speaker-identity signal is linearly decodable at 1.000 accuracy from encoder layer 13 onward — a perception failure is ruled out — attribution patching puts the text:audio contribution ratio at ~18:1 (3.29 vs 0.18), and mid-layer (10–21) attention knockout re-applied at every free-form decoding step cuts generation text-dominance from 1.00 to 0.38 (prosody) / 0.43 (identity), an intervention to which the closed-set multiple-choice format used by standard benchmarks is completely blind, while back-patching and 5x audio amplification both fail (the back-patching null holds even on its own source task, n=300, +0.007 vs +0.160 reported).

## 核心贡献

- **3,594 条样本的副语言冲突基准**：基于 MUStARD++ 与 RAVDESS 语料构建三类冲突（情感韵律冲突、说话人身份冲突、反讽/节奏冲突），采用 LISTEN 式四条件设计（中性文本/匹配/矛盾/仅音频），系统量化六个音频 LLM、两种编码器架构（Whisper 系与非 Whisper 系）的文本主导现象。
- **证明"非感知性失败"**：在文本偏向最强的 Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct 上，线性探测显示说话人性别信息自编码器第 13 层起即可 1.000 准确率恢复，并完整保留到投影层输出——声学证据已到达语言模型却被忽略，排除了"编码失败"解释。
- **系统检验并否定两类音频侧干预**：back-patching（从 Cho et al. 2026 的词汇冲突迁移）与直接放大音频表征（最高 5.0 倍）均无法恢复音频接地的回答；作者还复现了 back-patching 在其原始任务上的效果，发现同样无效，因此将其零结果定性为"无信息"而非"不迁移"。
- **定位中间接力机制并提出有效干预**：注意力消融显示矛盾的文本主张并非通过单一"主张→答案"边传播（封堵该边 TDR 不变 0.98→0.98），而是在生成过程中被反复重编码进中间 token；在自由生成期间对所有生成 token 在中层（10–21）持续封堵对主张的注意力，可将 gen-TDR 从 1.00 降至 0.38，且匹配项能力不降反升。
- **评估格式警示**：该干预效果只在自由生成中出现、在闭集多选题中完全不可见——依赖多选题格式的音频 LLM 基准可能系统性忽视此类干预。

## Key Contributions

- A 3,594-item paralinguistic conflict benchmark spanning emotional prosody, speaker identity, and sarcasm/pacing, constructed from MUStARD++ and RAVDESS under LISTEN-style neutral/matched/mismatched/audio-only conditions, used to measure text dominance across six audio LLMs and two encoder families.
- Behavioral evidence that text dominance is pervasive and statistically robust: mean text-dominance rate 0.78 on mismatched items (0.86 excluding sarcasm), with between-model differences significant in every category (Cochran's Q, all p < 1e-40).
- A perception-vs-suppression dissection: linear probing recovers speaker gender at 1.000 accuracy from encoder layer 13 onward and at the projector output of Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, so the acoustic signal survives the entire perception pathway; attribution patching then shows text contributes 3.29 vs audio's 0.18 (an ~18:1 ratio), with a faithfulness check confirming the top 20% of identified components reproduce 100% of model behaviour.
- A rigorous negative-result treatment of back-patching: it yields no repair here (best source layer 16 gains only 0.511 nats against a −7.32-nat baseline, 7% of the gap), and the authors' own replication on the lexical ALME task it was designed for (n=300, TTS-resynthesized) also yields no repair (pooled 0.207 → 0.213, McNemar p = 0.845, vs +0.160 reported by Cho et al.) — so the null is treated as an absent positive control, not as evidence about mechanism transfer.
- Localization of the suppression route to an intermediate relay: blocking the direct claim→answer attention edge across all 32 layers leaves TDR unchanged (0.98 → 0.98), while blocking every generated token's attention to the claim at mid-layers (10–21), re-applied at each decoding step, restores audio-grounded output — and the effect is invisible under closed-set multiple-choice scoring.

## Methodology

The study proceeds in four mechanistic stages, applied only to capabilities the model can perform from audio alone (a capability screen on the audio-only condition excludes models at chance, e.g., Baichuan-Omni-1.5 on prosody at 0.099 vs 0.125 chance). First, linear probes trained on frozen activations track whether paralinguistic attributes (speaker gender) survive the Whisper-family encoder, the projector, and arrival at the LM input. Second, attribution patching (a gradient-based approximation of activation patching) estimates each component's causal effect on the decision, validated by a faithfulness test in which the top 20% of components must replicate full-model behaviour (they do, 100%). Third, audio-side repairs are tested: back-patching writes late-layer audio-span activations (layers 31/32) into layer 0 across swept source layers (4–31), destination window widths (1/3/5/7), and amplitude scales (1.5x–5.0x), with norm rescaling to counter the observed 4.9x norm growth; a lexical-task replication is rebuilt from ALME with TTS resynthesis and run at each flip type's published pinned layer configuration. Fourth, attention knockout (Geva et al.-style) sets attention weights (γ=0) from designated token positions to the text-claim span, bisected three ways: answer position only, all positions after the claim, or every position — crossed with layer bands (mid 10–21, late, all 32) and with evaluation format (closed-set multiple choice vs free-form generation, the latter re-applying the block at every decoding step). Controls verify the intervention is not generic damage: matched-item accuracy, text-only accuracy (where the claim is the only evidence), and fluency (mean per-token log-probability under the unmodified model, scored claim-free) are tracked alongside text-dominance rate. All inference runs at 16-bit precision.

## Results & Highlights

- Mean text-dominance rate on mismatched text+audio items is 0.78 across the six models (0.86 excluding sarcasm); per-model prosody TDR spans 68.8% (DeSTA2.5) to 98.7% (Audex and Qwen2-Audio), and speaker-identity TDR reaches 98.5% (DeSTA2.5) — differences are significant in all three categories (emotional prosody Q = 201.04, df = 5, n = 304; speaker identity Q = 218.09, n = 331; sarcasm/pacing Q = 345.00, n = 369; all p < 1e-40).
- In Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, speaker gender is linearly decodable at 1.000 probe accuracy from encoder layer 13 onward and at the projector output — against a paralinguistic-only behavioural accuracy of 54.1% (prosody) / 98.7% (identity) and mismatched accuracy of just 33.0% / 38.4% — the information is present and behaviourally discarded.
- Attribution patching quantifies the imbalance: text contributes 3.29 to the decision versus 0.18 for audio (~18:1), with the top 20% of components reproducing 100% of full-model outputs in the faithfulness check.
- Back-patching fails everywhere tested: on prosody the best configuration (source layer 16) closes only 7% of the −7.32-nat decision-margin gap (+0.511 nats), later sources fall to −0.381 nats below baseline; on its own lexical source task the replication moves pooled accuracy 0.207 → 0.213 (+0.007; 14 items fixed, 12 broken; McNemar exact p = 0.845) versus +0.160 reported by Cho et al. — and 5.0x audio amplification leaves TDR unchanged while collapsing capability from 1.000 to 0.100.
- Attention knockout exposes the relay: blocking the direct claim→answer edge at all 32 layers leaves TDR unchanged (0.98 → 0.98), but blocking all positions after the claim raises closed-set audio-consistent accuracy from 0.000 to 0.533 (prosody) and 0.067 to 0.717 (identity), cutting TDR from 0.983 to 0.150 and 0.933 to 0.283.
- In free-form generation, mid-layer (10–21) knockout cuts gen-TDR from 1.000 to 0.375 (prosody, 95% CI [0.21, 0.57]) and 1.000 to 0.433 (identity), rising "names true" from 0.000 to 0.500 / 0.567 — while matched-item capability rises (0.767 → 0.900 prosody; 0.933 → 1.000 identity) and text-only cost is negligible; all-layer blocking pushes gen-TDR to 0.182 / 0.300 but damages capability (matched prosody 0.667).

## Authors & Credibility

Six authors: four are listed as independent researchers (two pairs of equal contribution), Kevin Yang is at Brown University, and Ruizhe Li is at the University of Birmingham; one author's affiliation (Episcopal Academy) indicates a high-school researcher, suggesting a partially student-driven project. The paper was presented at the InterpScience Workshop at NeurIPS 2026 — a workshop paper, not a main-conference one. Despite the modest venue, the methodological hygiene is well above typical workshop standards: capability screening before mechanistic claims, a faithfulness check on attribution patching, damage controls (matched/text-only/fluency) around every intervention, Holm–Bonferroni-corrected McNemar pairwise tests, Wilson 95% CIs on every reported rate, and an explicit attempt to replicate the prior intervention (back-patching) on its own task before interpreting its null. Code and data are stated to be public (github.com/AdvayRoongta/audio-llm-mech-interp), with prompts and construction methodology in Appendix A.6.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely new question — prior work (LISTEN, MCR-Bench, PARSA-Bench) measured text dominance behaviourally, and Cho et al. mechanistically only for lexical conflict; this is the first circuit-level account of paralinguistic (prosody/identity) suppression, and the MC-format blindness finding is a real contribution to evaluation methodology. The intermediate-relay conclusion is consistent with concurrent causal-tracing work on audio-text fusion (Chen et al., 2026).
- **Rigor:** Strong on controls and statistics, but the mechanistic core rests on a single model (Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, 7B, Whisper-family encoder); the two non-Whisper models in the behavioural set were never probed mechanistically, so the account may be encoder-family-specific — a limitation the authors flag but do not resolve. The sarcasm/pacing category is excluded from mechanism analysis entirely because audio-only labels were "insufficiently determined," leaving two categories.
- **Reproducibility:** Public code/data and pinned published layer configurations for the back-patching replication are good practice; however, the replication's TTS-resynthesized audio differs from the original authors' stimuli, which the authors themselves admit makes their null "configuration sensitivity" rather than a clean non-replication — the central negative claim is therefore softer than a reader might take it.
- **Red flags:** The headline knockout gain carries a real capability cost that varies by scoring path — closed-set prosody capability drops from 1.000 to 0.367 under the all-positions block, and the free-form intervention experiments use a small number of items ("should not be overinterpreted" per the authors). The intervention is unconditional: it attenuates the claim whether or not the claim is right, and no experiment tests cases where audio is ambiguous and the text should legitimately win. Behavioural sample sizes shrink under complete-case handling (95–119 dropped trials, almost all Baichuan), and on speaker identity the five non-SALMONN models are statistically indistinguishable despite an 8-point raw spread — the paper says so, but a skimmer of Figure 2 would wrongly read it as a ranking.

## Strengths

- Clean logical decomposition of the failure — perception (probing) vs use (attribution) vs repair (patching/knockout) — applied only to capabilities the model demonstrably has from audio alone, which forestalls the classic "suppression vs absence" confound.
- Unusually honest negative-result handling: back-patching's null is replicated against its own source task and correctly demoted to "uninformative" rather than spun as evidence of non-transfer.
- The closed-set vs free-form dissociation is the paper's sharpest finding: the same intervention that cuts gen-TDR from 1.00 to 0.38 is invisible under multiple-choice scoring, implying standard audio-LLM benchmarks structurally cannot see relay-mediated suppression.
- Full control battery (matched items, text-only cost, fluency under the unmodified model, late-layer and all-layer contrast blocks) rules out the "intervention just damages the model" explanation for the TDR drop.

## Limitations

- Mechanism and repair are demonstrated in one 7B model and two conflict categories; generalization across scale (Audex-30B was behaviour-only), encoder families, languages, and conflict types (accent, vocal effort, non-speech events) is untested.
- The training/test stimuli are acted performances (RAVDESS, MUStARD++), so conflicts are more categorical than real conversation; all conflicts are English, and prior cross-linguistic work (ALME/PARSA-Bench) reports different conflict rates.
- The intervention reduces but does not eliminate text dominance (mid-layer gen-TDR 0.375 / 0.433), degrades capability under the all-layer variant (matched prosody 0.667), and cannot distinguish reliable from unreliable text claims since it is unconditional.
- The back-patching replication's divergence from the published +0.160 gain is plausibly attributable to TTS/stimulus differences, leaving the lexical-vs-paralinguistic mechanism relationship genuinely open.

## Relevance

与"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向高度契合：这是对多模态（音频-文本）LLM 的机制可解释性研究，综合运用线性探测、attribution patching、注意力消融定位副语言抑制电路，核心发现（中间 token 接力传播、评估格式盲区）对理解 LLM 内部表征如何被选择性忽略具有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the intermediate-relay mechanism replicate in the non-Whisper encoder models (Phi-4-multimodal, Baichuan-Omni-1.5) and at 30B scale (Audex), or is it an artifact of Whisper-family audio embeddings?
- Can the claim-attention block be made conditional — attenuating the text claim only when audio evidence confidently contradicts it — to preserve cases where the transcript should legitimately override ambiguous audio?
- Why exactly does back-patching fail to replicate: is it TTS voice identity, layer-indexing conventions (the authors checked 0- vs 1-indexing), or a deeper overfitting of published configurations to specific stimuli?
- Would a relay-aware evaluation — free-form generation scored for claim-echoing — change published rankings on LISTEN/MCR-Bench, where closed-set scoring currently dominates?
- Does the same relay structure govern other cross-modal suppressions (e.g., images overriding contradictory captions, or vice versa) in vision-language models?
