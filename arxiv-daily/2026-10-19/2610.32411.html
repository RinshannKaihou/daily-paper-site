Key metric · causal ablation

98.3% → 0

Audio exact-match on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B after ablating the top-5% retrieval heads. Layer-matched random ablation leaves it at 96.9% — the collapse is causal, not a side effect.

Why this matters

Head-level mechanistic interpretability now extends to omni-modal models — and the same attention that explains retrieval can be trained to improve it.

⚠ Critical edge

Every causal audio-visual claim rests on a synthetic six-digit recall needle; no natural audio-visual QA benchmark appears in any causal table.

Methodology, distilled

How they know
  • Calibrated head scoring. Per head: mean question→evidence-span attention, minus the same score under an uninformative question on the identical context (MMRetHead-style); averaged over examples, ranked, top 5% = retrieval heads. Native attention masking respected throughout.
  • Paired AV-NIAH. The same six-digit answer is inserted into the same MLVU background video — either spoken (TTS) or shown as a 1-second centered banner — so only evidence modality varies, never content, depth, or question.
  • Causal test. Head outputs are zeroed before the attention output projection; the control ablates layer-matched random heads (3 seeds); effects judged by paired cluster-bootstrap with Holm correction on held-out examples.
  • Heads as an optimization target. Candidate pages are scored by question→page attention over 16 frozen heads, min/max-rescaled to a fixed logit range; a ranking loss (one positive + one same-document hard negative) trains LoRA adapters on the attention projections. Only MMDocIR page labels are used — layout retrieval is an unsupervised transfer test.

Scope: 18 checkpoints across language, vision-language, and omni-modal groups (Qwen2.5/Qwen3, Qwen-VL, MiniCPM-V/o, BAGEL, Qwen2.5-Omni, SALMONN 2+/Pro), with explicit backbone→omni lineage pairs.

Strengths

What holds up
  • Clean experimental design: paired inputs vary only the evidence modality; uninformative-question calibration strips positional and content priors from head scores.
  • Unusually broad coverage — 18 checkpoints, four model groups — with lineage pairs that make the preservation analysis possible, not merely correlational.
  • Closes the interpretability-to-performance loop: one construct (retrieval-head attention) is both lens and target, replicated across three families with an OOD safety check.
  • Statistical hygiene above the subfield norm: matched random controls, cluster bootstrap, Holm correction, sweeps over k and cutoffs.

Limitations

What to watch
  • Audio-visual mechanism evidence derives entirely from synthetic digit recall — no natural speech or real-video understanding tasks.
  • "Shared across modalities" is conditional on matched content; sharing weakens markedly when the retrieval target differs.
  • Downstream significance covers 4/8 tasks per model and only two omni models; the image-head control itself causes nontrivial drops, so specificity is partial.
  • Ranking training is trained and evaluated only on MMDocIR domains; no cross-domain, hour-long-document, or inference-cost analysis; contexts ≤ 300 s.
  • Layout-granularity gains are small (+0.81…+2.16 Micro R@1) — interesting transfer, not yet decisive.

Open questions this paper leaves

Next probes
  1. Do synthetic-needle retrieval heads predict behavior on natural spoken-QA and real video-QA, where evidence is distributed and semantically rich?
  2. Do head sets survive RLHF-style post-training or continued pretraining, or does the backbone-preservation property break under larger adaptation?
  3. Can more diverse page-level corpora close the layout-retrieval gap, or does fine-grained localization require layout supervision after all?
  4. Since the ranking loss updates LoRA on attention projections — do the trained heads remain the top retrieval heads, or does training silently recruit new ones?
  5. Can retrieval-head attention serve as a training-free reranker inside multimodal RAG, and how does it interact with token compression over hour-long contexts?
