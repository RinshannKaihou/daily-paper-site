! Critical edge

The 89–97% headline is multiple-choice QA only — generation retention is far worse (45–81% on HumanEval, 45% for Phi-4-mini). A skimming reader of the abstract could be misled.

Critical assessment · red flag №1

01Methodology — distilled

A factor-wise empirical study of PTQ + recovery fine-tuning at 2 bits, on six models: Phi-4-mini (3.8B, instruct), Llama-3.1-8B (base), Qwen3-30B-A3B (reasoning MoE), plus held-out Qwen3-8B, OLMoE-1B-7B, and Qwen3-32B.

  • Quantizers. Scalar: SpinQuant, QuaRot (group size 128), trained-scalar GSQ and BitDistiller (QAT). Vector: VPTQ (216-entry codebook, 8-dim subvectors) and YAQA (Fisher-weighted trellis rounding), both in an incoherence-rotated frame; learned rotations shown not to help at 2 bits.
  • Rate-matched by design. Counting codebooks, indices, and scales, every method lands at 2.09–2.33 effective bits/weight. Routers, embeddings, norms, and the LM head stay half-precision (routers shown robust).
  • Calibration as Hessian mixtures. Recipes swept by reweighting precomputed per-domain Hessians (web / instruction / math / code) instead of re-collecting.
  • Recovery. Backbone frozen; only the codebook trains, via distillation from the full-precision teacher (CE + KL, α = 0.5) — no QAT optimizer state.
  • Evaluation. AvgQA = 7 multiple-choice tasks; AvgGen = GSM8K + HumanEval; IFEval for instruction following. 3 seeds, hierarchical-bootstrap CIs, held-out tasks, re-quantized bases, and a pre-registered replication on Qwen3-8B.

Credibility: Microsoft Research + Applied Sciences Group; disclosed AI-tools statement; statistical care rare in quantization papers — but code, Hessians, and models are promised only upon acceptance, so independent verification is pending.

Same collapse-to-zero generation holds for scalar 2-bit on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-30B-A3B.

  • Vector beats trained scalar at matched bits. Phi-4-mini AvgGen: YAQA 37.4 vs BitDistiller (QAT) 18.4 and GSQ 17.9. GSQ collapses to the random floor on both MoEs.
  • Compute. No-recovery vector PTQ keeps 89–97% QA using 39–304× less compute than trained scalar methods; with recovery, 1.4–30× less than the strongest QAT baseline.
  • Calibration moves generation, not QA. Sweeping calibration recipes shifts AvgGen by 13.5–26.7 points per model but AvgQA by only 3.9–7.5 (at 4 bits: ≤7.9 and ≤1.6).
  • Calibration × recovery interaction. Best recovery data flips with calibration choice: −8.5 AvgGen pts on Phi-4-mini (95% CI [−12.6, −4.3]), prospectively confirmed on pre-registered Qwen3-8B (−9.4, CI [−13.3, −5.3]).
  • Quantization amplifies reasoning-model overthinking. Qwen3-30B-A3B on MATH-500: accuracy 0.675 → 0.450, repeated 4-gram loop score 0.39 → 0.67; a repetition penalty of 1.3 restores 0.625.
  • Retention is scale-dependent. QA retention is uniform (89–97%), but HumanEval retention spans 45% (Phi-4-mini) to 81% (Qwen3-30B-A3B), tracking total model scale more than the quantizer.

03Strengths

  • Rate-matched comparison (everything at 2.09–2.33 effective bits) instead of nominal-bit comparisons that quietly favor one side.
  • The interaction finding is genuinely new and actionable — established with pre-registration, held-out models and tasks, and resampled data.
  • Broad coverage — six dense and MoE models, 3.8B–32B, base / instruct / reasoning — makes the collapse claims hard to dismiss as cherry-picking.
  • Cheap, concrete recipe (recovery saturates at 3,200 examples), plus an overthinking failure-mode lens beyond aggregate accuracy.

04Limitations

  • Interaction evidence is narrow: one domain pair (web vs math), one quantizer (YAQA), short recovery budgets only; long-budget 2×2 runs left to future work.
  • 2-bit is not uniformly usable: generation retention stays poor for sub-10B dense models (HumanEval 45%); QA-heavy workloads benefit most.
  • Recovery can hurt: QA drops in all long-recovery runs on Llama-3.1-8B, and IFEval often drops on Phi-4-mini — validate per model and prompt format.
  • Baseline caveats: "scalar collapses" tested only at group size 128 (smaller g untested); the overthinking study uses just 40 problems; base-model rows aren't directly comparable.

05Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the calibration × recovery interaction persist for other quantizers (VPTQ, AQLM), other domain pairs (code, instruction, long-context), and at long recovery budgets?
  2. Could scalar PTQ with much smaller group sizes (g=16/32) or optimized non-uniform grids close the gap to vector codebooks at matched effective bits?
  3. Can recovery benefit be predicted a priori — from Hessian spectra, training-data mixture, or instruct-vs-base status — rather than screened per model?
  4. Is 2-bit overthinking/looping driven by specific layers or expert subsets, and can targeted mixed precision suppress it without losing the memory savings?
  5. How do the findings transfer to weight+activation quantization (W2A8/W2A4) and serving stacks where kernel numerics dominate the error budget?
