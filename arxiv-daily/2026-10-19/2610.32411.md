> **一句话总结：** 中科院信工所与腾讯团队用"同一答案、只换模态"的配对音频/视觉针（needle）实验，在 18 个语言/视觉语言/全模态模型上系统证明检索头（retrieval heads）在全模态扩展中保持稀疏、跨模态大量共享（音频与视觉证据共享 69%–83% 的 top-5% 头，占全部头数不足 2% 的跨模态共享头被消融后音频检索直接归零并拖累视觉数学等下游任务），并进一步把检索头注意力变成优化目标：用排序损失训练后 Qwen3-VL-8B 在 MMDocIR 页面检索 Micro R@1 从 56.45 提升到 62.48（+6.03 点），且跨三个模型家族均有效。

> **TL;DR:** This paper extends retrieval-head analysis from text/VLMs to omni-modal models via paired AV-NIAH inputs (the same six-digit answer is either spoken or shown as a 1-second banner in the same MLVU background video) across 18 checkpoints: retrieval heads stay sparse, 69–83% of top-5% heads are shared between audio and visual evidence (chance overlap < 3 heads), and omni models largely retain their backbones' head rankings (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.97 for SALMONN vs its Qwen-VL backbones). Causally, ablating top-5% heads collapses audio exact match on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B from 98.3% to 0 (matched random ablation leaves 96.9%), and ablating the ~14 AV-shared heads (<2% of all heads) costs up to 22.3 pp beyond random on MathVista. Turning the finding into a method, ranking-training retrieval-head attention lifts Qwen3-VL-8B's MMDocIR page Micro R@1 from 56.45 to 62.48 (+6.03 over the untrained retrieval-head scorer, which itself beats a random-head scorer at 38.00), replicating on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (+5.69) and Gemma3-12B (+2.17). Worth reading for anyone working on multimodal interpretability or attention-based retrieval; the main caveat is that the audio-visual evidence rests on a synthetic digit-recall needle rather than natural AV QA.

## 核心贡献

- **统一框架下的跨模态检索头检测。** 提出并用统一的校准注意力评分（问题→证据注意力减去"无信息问题"基线）在文本（LongMemEval、Natural Questions 证据标注子集）、图文交错（MM-NIAH）和自建的配对音频-视觉（AV-NIAH）三类证据标注数据上检测检索头，覆盖 18 个检查点（Qwen2.5/Qwen3、Qwen2.5-VL/Qwen3-VL、MiniCPM-V/o、BAGEL、Qwen2.5-Omni、SALMONN 2+/Pro 等 4 大类模型）。
- **四个机制性发现。** (1) 稀疏性：语音证据下每个模型中不到十分之一的头就贡献了总分的一半以上；(2) 跨模态共享：同一答案以语音或字幕呈现时，69%–83% 的 top-5% 检索头重合，全排序 Spearman ρ 为 0.88–0.92；(3) 保留性：全模态模型基本保留其语言/视觉语言骨干的检索头（SALMONN 与其 VL 骨干 ρ ≥ 0.97）；(4) 因果性：消融 top-5% 检索头使所有被测模型的音频检索归零，而层数匹配的随机消融几乎无影响。
- **小规模跨模态共享头支撑广泛的多模态能力。** 音频+视觉双入选的共享头仅占全部头的不足 2%（约 9–14 个），但消融它们在 8 个下游基准上造成远超随机消融的额外下降（Qwen2.5-Omni-7B 上 MathVista 额外降 22.3 个百分点），且在 16 个模型-任务对中有 15 个的影响超过按自然图像检索选出的头。
- **从分析到优化：检索头引导的文档排序。** 把选定头的"问题→候选页"注意力作为排序分数，用"一正例+一同文档难负例"的排序损失只对这些头的分数训练 LoRA，使 MMDocIR 页面 Micro R@1 在 Qwen3-VL-8B 上 +6.03（56.45→62.48），并在 Qwen2.5-VL-7B（+5.69）与 Gemma3-12B（+2.17）上复现，还让完全无版面监督的 layout 检索提升（Qwen3-VL-8B Micro R@1 47.14→48.60），同时四个域外基准变化均在 1 点以内。

## Key Contributions

- **A unified, calibrated retrieval-head detector applied across modalities.** Evidence attention is measured from question tokens to an annotated evidence span, calibrated by subtracting the score obtained with an uninformative question on the same context, and averaged over examples; heads are ranked and the top 5% are called retrieval heads. The same criterion runs on text (LongMemEval, Natural Questions evidence subsets), interleaved image-text (MM-NIAH), and new paired AV-NIAH data.
- **A paired-input design that isolates the modality variable.** AV-NIAH inserts the same six-digit answer into an MLVU background video either as synthesized speech (audio condition, video track unchanged) or as a 1-second centered banner (visual condition, audio track unchanged), holding source video, answer, insertion depth, and question fixed — so any difference in recruited heads is attributable to evidence modality, not content.
- **Four findings across 18 checkpoints.** Sparsity (fewer than one head in ten carries half the positive score), cross-modal sharing (69–83% top-5% overlap; ρ = 0.88–0.92), preservation across modality adaptation (omni models retain backbone retrieval-head rankings, ρ ≥ 0.95, and SALMONN ≈ its Qwen-VL backbone at ρ ≥ 0.97), and a causal role (top-5% ablation zeroes audio retrieval in all four tested models).
- **The small AV-shared subset matters beyond retrieval.** Ablating the ~9–14 heads shared by audio and visual evidence (< 2% of all heads) significantly degrades 4/8 downstream tasks per model (vs at most 1/8 for image-retrieval heads), with the largest extra drops on visual mathematics and spatial perception.
- **Retrieval heads as an optimization target.** A ranking objective over question-to-page attention of 16 selected heads, trained via LoRA on attention projections, improves MMDocIR retrieval in all three model families, transfers to layout granularity without layout supervision, and leaves out-of-domain benchmarks (MMMU, MMMU-Pro, MathVista, MMLongBench-Doc) essentially unchanged (all deltas within ±1 point).

## Methodology

The detection method defines, for each head (l,h) and example i, the evidence attention u_lh as the mean attention from question positions to evidence-span tokens; a calibrated retrieval score subtracts u computed with an uninformative question on the same context (following MMRetHead) and averages over N examples. All attention respects the model's native visibility constraints (e.g., causal masking in the language decoder, including the Thinker of Qwen2.5-Omni and BAGEL's understanding expert). AV-NIAH evidence spans are the audio or visual tokens corresponding to the spoken digits or the 1-second banner.

Causality is tested by ablation: head outputs are zeroed before the attention output projection. The control ablates the same number of heads per layer drawn at random from outside the retrieval set (three seeds), and the effect is the additional drop E(S) = r̄ − t relative to that matched control, assessed with paired cluster-bootstrap tests and Holm correction on held-out examples. Downstream transfer compares the AV-shared heads against an equally sized set of heads detected on natural-image retrieval.

For the optimization part, candidate pages/regions of an MMDocIR document are scored by summing question-to-candidate-visual-token attention over a fixed set of k heads (k = 16 by default, pooled from four multimodal detection conditions). Training has three steps: (1) select heads once in the base model and freeze the set; (2) normalize raw scores per example with detached min/max rescaling into a fixed logit range [0, γ]; (3) minimize a ranking loss with one relevant page and one hard negative from the same document. The loss is defined only on the selected heads, but its gradients update LoRA adapters on the attention projections. At inference, candidates are ranked by the trained score minus the score under an uninformative question. Training uses only MMDocIR's page-level labels, so layout retrieval is an unsupervised transfer test. Main model: Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct; replications: Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Gemma3-12B-it.

## Results & Highlights

- **Sparsity with speech evidence:** In all eight omni-modal models and both evidence modalities, fewer than one in ten heads accounts for half of the total positive calibrated score; high scores concentrate in middle and later-middle layers (Figure 2).
- **Cross-modal sharing:** Between 69% and 83% of top-5% heads are selected under both the audio and visual conditions — e.g., 32/39 in Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and 40/58 in SALMONN Pro 4B — versus fewer than 3 heads expected by chance; full head rankings correlate at Spearman ρ = 0.88–0.92 across all eight models, robust to leave-one-video-out and 300-second backgrounds.
- **Causal necessity:** Ablating the top-5% retrieval heads collapses audio exact match on AV-NIAH from 98.3% to 0 on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (100-second, ~8K-token backgrounds), while ablating the same number of layer-matched random heads leaves it at 96.9%; visual retrieval similarly drops to near zero at both 100 s and 300 s (~24K tokens) lengths in all four ablated models.
- **Downstream cost of the shared subset:** Ablating the 14 AV-shared heads in Qwen2.5-Omni-7B causes additional drops beyond random ablation of 22.3 pp on MathVista (base 72.5%), 16.3 pp on CV-Bench (base 75.5%), 12.2 pp on MMStar (base 67.0%), and 8.2 pp on MMMU-Pro (base 39.5%); effects are Holm-significant on 4/8 tasks per model and exceed the image-retrieval-head control in 15 of 16 model-task pairs.
- **Ranking training on MMDocIR (page Micro R@1, Qwen3-VL-8B):** retrieval-head scorer 56.45 → 62.48 after training (+6.03), versus only 38.00 for a random-head scorer under the identical configuration; the trained score (62.48) also exceeds published retrievers Col-Phi3-MMDocIR (57.10) and ColPali (54.58), though the authors flag that those evaluation settings differ. The +6.03 gain over the untrained retrieval-head scorer holds in every one of the ten document domains, and macro page R@1 gains reach +5.05 (58.81→63.86).
- **Cross-family replication and granularity transfer:** page Micro R@1 gains of +5.69 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (52.41→58.10) and +2.17 on Gemma3-12B (44.75→46.92); layout Micro R@1 improves without any layout supervision (+1.46 for Qwen3-VL-8B, 47.14→48.60; +2.16 for Gemma3-12B, 23.88→26.04).
- **Robustness of the method:** training helps at every head-set size (page R@1 gains +5.31 to +6.03 for k = 4…64); trained random-head scorers improve too but stay far below even the untrained retrieval-head scorer. Out-of-domain impact is minimal: MMMU +0.50, MMMU-Pro +0.64, MathVista +0.70, MMLongBench-Doc −0.83 — all within one point.

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans the Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences (also the UCAS School of Cyber Security) and Tencent; Mo Yu is a well-known researcher on retrieval and long-context reasoning, and the group has prior work on query-focused reranking (QRHead-line follow-ups). The paper builds directly on the established retrieval-head literature (Wu et al., ICLR 2025), QRHead (EMNLP 2025), and MMRetHead for vision-language models, and cites the AVOC precedent for paired AV needle construction. The empirical scope — 18 checkpoints with explicit backbone→omni lineage pairs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B → SALMONN 2+ 7B) that make the retention analysis possible — and the statistical care (matched random-ablation controls with three seeds, paired cluster-bootstrap tests, Holm correction) are above the norm for this subfield.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Incremental but real. The paired same-content audio/visual needle design and the backbone→omni retention chain are genuinely new angles relative to MMRetHead (which covered text and image evidence); the ranking-training application is the most novel part, extending text-only retrieval-head optimization to multimodal document retrieval.
- **Rigor:** Generally strong — calibrated scores, matched random controls, held-out ablations, Holm-corrected significance, and an honest designation of the +6.03 same-configuration comparison as the controlled result. However, the headline "trained model beats ColPali/Col-Phi3" comparison is acknowledged by the authors themselves to have differing evaluation settings, so it should not be quoted as a leaderboard result.
- **The synthetic needle is the weakest link.** AV-NIAH retrieval is exact-string recall of a six-digit number (spoken TTS digits or a 1-second centered banner); all causal audio-visual claims rest on this one artificial task, with no natural audio-visual QA benchmark (e.g., speech content understanding or real video QA) in the ablation or transfer tables.
- **The sharing claim is partly a content-matched artifact — to the authors' credit, they say so.** When spoken digits are compared against MM-NIAH natural-image needles instead of the matched banner, correlations drop markedly; the paper reframes the finding as "head recruitment depends on the retrieval target more than on the evidence modality," which is weaker than a modality-invariant mechanism.
- **The "train the heads" story is not surgical.** The ranking loss is computed only over selected heads, but gradients update LoRA adapters on the attention projections, which can shift attention computation everywhere; the trained random-head control shows head selection matters, yet the intervention is not a heads-only modification, and calling it "optimizing retrieval-head attention" slightly overstates the mechanistic precision.
- **Coverage gaps:** downstream causal evidence covers only two omni models (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, SALMONN Pro 4B) with small head sets (14 and 10), significant on only 4/8 tasks each; the Gemma3 page gain is a more modest +2.17; layout gains are small (+0.81 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B); and there is no dedicated Limitations section, no latency/cost analysis for the per-candidate attention scoring, and no test beyond 300-second contexts.

## Strengths

- Clean experimental design: paired inputs vary only the evidence modality, and an uninformative-question calibration strips positional/content priors from head scores.
- Unusually broad model coverage (18 checkpoints, four model groups) with lineage pairs that make the preservation analysis possible rather than merely correlational across families.
- Closes the interpretability-to-performance loop: the same construct (retrieval-head attention) serves as both a lens (ablations) and a target (ranking training), replicated across three model families with an out-of-domain safety check.
- Statistical hygiene well above the subfield norm: matched random controls, cluster bootstrap, Holm correction, and robustness sweeps over head-set sizes (k = 4…64) and cutoffs (top-20, top-5%, AV-shared).

## Limitations

- The audio-visual mechanism evidence derives from a synthetic digit-recall needle (six-digit number, exact match), not natural audio or audio-visual understanding tasks.
- Cross-modal head sharing was demonstrated for matched content; sharing weakens when the retrieval target differs, so "shared across modalities" should be read as conditional on matched evidence content.
- Downstream ablation significance is 4/8 tasks per model, and only two omni models were tested for transfer; the image-retrieval-head control itself causes nontrivial drops on several tasks, so specificity is partial.
- Ranking training is trained and evaluated on MMDocIR domains (page-level labels only); cross-domain retrieval, longer/hour-long documents, and inference cost of scoring every candidate page are unaddressed.
- Small layout-granularity gains (+0.81 to +2.16 Micro R@1) mean the zero-supervision transfer is interesting but not yet practically decisive.

## Relevance

高度契合"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是把注意力头级机械可解释性（retrieval heads）从纯文本/视觉语言模型系统扩展到全模态模型的工作，包含探针式评分、因果消融与表征保留性分析，并示范了"解释→干预→性能提升"的闭环，对理解 MLLM 内部证据定位机制与长上下文检索都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do retrieval heads detected on synthetic digit needles predict behavior on natural spoken-QA and real video-QA benchmarks, where evidence is distributed and semantically rich rather than a six-digit string?
- How stable are retrieval-head sets under further fine-tuning (e.g., RLHF-style post-training), and does the backbone-preservation property survive larger adaptation stages or continued pretraining?
- Could ranking training on more diverse page-level corpora (beyond MMDocIR's ten domains) close the gap on layout retrieval, or does fine-grained localization require layout supervision after all?
- Since the ranking loss updates LoRA on attention projections, what exactly changes in the unselected heads — do the trained heads remain the top-scoring retrieval heads, or does training silently recruit new ones?
- Can retrieval-head attention scores serve as a training-free reranker inside multimodal RAG pipelines, and how do they interact with token-compression methods for hour-long audio-visual contexts?
