> **一句话总结：** 一项预注册审计在 30 个（5 个 Qwen2.5 模型 × 6 个任务档）实验格上系统对比了言语化置信度、隐藏状态线性探针与采样一致性三种置信信号，发现三者远不可互换：言语置信度换 prompt 模板后几乎不相关（ρ=0.126），检索任务探针约 5% 层深即起效而推理任务约 30%（但 9/11 检索格探针并不优于仅看题面的 TF-IDF 基线），且 ECE 会误导——言语置信度"校准好"只是因为它聚集在基础率附近，其分辨力（resolution）仅为采样一致性的约一半。

> **TL;DR:** A pre-registered audit of five Qwen2.5 models (0.5B–7B) across a 30-cell grid (PopQA/SimpleQA retrieval, GSM8K/MATH reasoning) shows the three standard confidence channels are not interchangeable. Verbalized confidence fails a format-invariance gate (Spearman ρ = +0.126 between 0–100 and ordinal-label formats vs. a pre-registered 0.60 threshold); behavioral sampling consensus has worse ECE (0.0704 vs. 0.0366 for verbal on 842 matched items) but 2.02× the resolution (0.064 vs. 0.032) and the best Brier score (0.1465); and probes on prefill states become informative at ~7% depth for retrieval vs. ~28% for reasoning (Δ = +21.3 pp, permutation p = 0.004) — yet in 9 of 11 retrieval cells the onset probe does not beat a prompt-only TF-IDF baseline, so the "early internal signal" may be a surface artifact of the question. Practical upshot: do not route or abstain on verbal confidence alone.

## 核心贡献

- **预注册的三通道对照审计**：在 5 个 Qwen2.5 模型（0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B-Instruct/7B-Base）× 6 个任务档（PopQA 高/低频实体、SimpleQA、GSM8K、MATH L1–2、MATH L4–5）共 30 个实验格上，首次将言语化置信度、内部探针、行为采样一致性放在同一批题目下用预注册假设（H0–H4）与审计门（Gates 1–4）系统比较，而非默认三者可互换。
- **言语置信度的"测量不变性"被证伪**：三种措辞稍有不同的模板（0–100 整数、五档序数标签、答/弃权决策）之间的 Spearman 相关仅 +0.126 / −0.069 / +0.247，远低于预注册的 0.60 门槛；13,121/15,000 个 0–100 回答挤在 90 以上，而其中模型仍在 Format C 选择弃权 26.8%。
- **用 Murphy 分解揭露 ECE 的等价性谬误**：言语置信度 ECE 最低（0.0366）只是因为它把置信度聚在基础率附近（10 个匹配格中 9 个的校准言语置信度从未超过 0.50）；采样一致性 ECE 更差（0.0704）但分辨力是言语的 2.02 倍、Brier 分最低（0.1465）。
- **检索 vs. 推理的探针起效层深差（H4）**：预测自身采样不一致的 prefill 探针在检索任务约 5–7% 层深（第 1–2 层）即达 AUROC≥0.65，推理任务中位 30%；但作者诚实地表明 9/11 检索格的探针不优于仅用题面 TF-IDF 的分类器，"早期信号"很可能只是题面表层特征。
- **假设记分卡的诚实报告**：5 个预注册假设中 2 个被证伪（H0 格式不变性、H1 校准排序）、1 个部分支持（H4 层深差）、1 个不支持（H2 象限复制）、1 个无法检验（H3 base/instruct 对齐，因 7B-Base 的言语置信度退化）。

## Key Contributions

- A **pre-registered, auditable three-channel comparison**: verbalized confidence, linear probes on prefill hidden states, and behavioral sampling consistency (semantic entropy) evaluated on a common 30-cell grid (5 Qwen2.5 models × 6 task tiers: PopQA high/low-popularity, SimpleQA, GSM8K, MATH L1–2 and L4–5; 500 questions per cell, 60/20/20 split), with hypotheses H0–H4, pass rules, and four audit gates committed in advance in a version-controlled protocol.
- **Falsification of verbal-confidence measurement invariance (H0)**: across three elicitation formats, pairwise Spearman correlations were +0.126, −0.069, and +0.247 against a pre-registered ρ ≥ 0.60 gate; 13,121 of 15,000 Format-A answers are ≥ 90/100, yet among those the model still chooses to pass 26.8% of the time under Format C's scoring rule.
- **The ECE equivalence fallacy via Murphy decomposition (H1)**: on 842 matched test items, verbal confidence has the lowest ECE (0.0366 vs. 0.0704 for behavioral consensus) but only because it clusters near the base rate — it never exceeds 0.50 in 9 of 10 cells — while behavioral consensus achieves 2.02× verbal's resolution (0.064 vs. 0.032) and the best Brier score (0.1465 vs. 0.1524 internal, 0.1713 verbal).
- **The retrieval–reasoning probe-onset gap (H4) with a surface-feature caveat**: probes predicting median-split semantic entropy become informative at a mean 6.82% of layer depth for retrieval (9 of 11 cells at 5%) vs. 28.08% for reasoning (median 30%); Δ = +21.26 pp [95% CI +9.72, +33.05], permutation p = 0.004 — but in 9 of 11 retrieval cells the onset-layer probe does not beat a prompt-only TF-IDF classifier, so early onsets are not evidence that recall is computed early.
- **Methodological hygiene rare in this literature**: a token-leakage bug in activation hooks (generated tokens leaking into "prefill" states) was found, fixed, and regression-tested; grading was human-audited at 195/200 agreement (97.5%); a negative-control gate (probe at 0% depth must be AUROC ≈ 0.50) dropped one contaminated cell; and one hypothesis (H3) was declared untestable rather than reported as a null result.

## Methodology

The study runs a 30-cell grid: models are Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/3B-Instruct plus a 7B base/instruct pair; retrieval tiers are PopQA top and bottom popularity quintiles and SimpleQA; reasoning tiers are GSM8K, MATH levels 1–2, and MATH levels 4–5. Each cell samples 500 questions at a fixed seed (60/20/20 train/calibration/test). Confidence is measured through three channels: (1) verbalized confidence in three formats (integer 0–100; five ordinal labels mapped to probabilities via a fixed scheme B_fix; an answer-or-pass decision scored +1/−2/0); (2) logistic-regression probes on hidden states captured at the last prompt token during a single prefill forward pass — before any decoding, with hooks verified not to leak generated tokens — trained to predict whether the model's own 10 sampled answers (T = 0.8) will disagree (median-split semantic-entropy label), evaluated on calibration/test splits; (3) behavioral consistency, i.e., semantic-entropy-based agreement across sampled answers. Probe depth is swept at 21 percentiles (0–100% in 5% steps) with a 2-consecutive-layer confirmation rule (AUROC ≥ 0.65 at p and p+5%). Four audit gates enforce validity: grading (human audit), verbal generalization (ρ ≥ 0.60), probe validity (AUROC ≥ 0.65, beating a 20-draw label-shuffle null, a prompt-only TF-IDF baseline, and a 0%-depth negative control), and matched base/instruct rows. Calibration is analyzed with the Murphy (1973) decomposition of the Brier score into reliability (calibration error), resolution (sorting power), and uncertainty, going beyond ECE.

## Results & Highlights

- **Format instability (H0, falsified)**: pairwise Spearman correlations between the three verbal formats are +0.126 (A vs. B), −0.069 (A vs. C), and +0.247 (B vs. C), all far below the pre-registered 0.60 gate; on the 0–100 format, 13,121/15,000 answers are ≥ 90, yet 26.8% of those high-confidence items are voluntarily passed in the answer-or-pass format.
- **Onset-depth gap (H4, follow-up sweep)**: retrieval onsets average 6.82% of depth (median 5%; 9 of 11 cells at layer 1–2, onset AUROC up to 0.905 for 7B-Base on PopQA-low) vs. 28.08% for reasoning (median 30%); Δ = +21.26 pp [95% CI +9.72, +33.05], one-sided permutation p = 0.004 over 24 probe-valid cells; peak probe AUROC averages 0.88 (retrieval) vs. 0.79 (reasoning).
- **Surface-baseline caveat**: the onset-layer probe fails to beat the prompt-only TF-IDF baseline in 12 of 24 cells overall, including 9 of 11 retrieval cells (e.g., 1.5B PopQA-high: probe 0.864 vs. TF-IDF 0.882) — early retrieval onsets largely restate bag-of-words features of the question; the gap also shrinks to +12.63 pp [+1.31, +25.76] when GSM8K (whose onsets are uniformly late: 30/40/60/60%) is excluded.
- **ECE vs. resolution trade-off (complete case, N = 842, base rate 0.2815)**: verbal ECE 0.0366 vs. behavioral 0.0704 (15 bins), but behavioral resolution is 0.064 vs. 0.032 for verbal (2.02×) and 0.052 for the internal probe (1.24×), yielding the best Brier score (behavioral 0.1465 < internal 0.1524 < verbal 0.1713).
- **Cross-signal coherence**: pooled raw verbal–internal correlation is ρ = 0.086 (nearly uncorrelated), while behavioral–internal correlations are moderate in most cells (e.g., 0.79–0.82 for 1.5B on PopQA tiers); after per-cell calibration the pooled verbal–internal correlation rises to 0.83, but this mostly reflects between-cell difficulty differences, not item-level agreement.
- **Hypothesis scorecard**: 2 falsified (H0, H1), 1 partly supported (H4 — pre-registered 5-tap test was marginal at p = 0.052; the cleaner result comes from a non-pre-registered 21-tap follow-up), 1 not supported (H2 quadrant replication; the 90:9 Excessive-Hedging:Performative-Certainty ratio is a cutoff artifact), 1 untestable (H3 — the 7B base model used essentially two verbal labels and was excluded in all six tiers).

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are Debojeet Ghosh, Shreyansh Kumar, and Ayushman Garg; no institutional affiliations or prior-track-record signals are given in the paper, and the work does not come from a well-known interpretability lab. Credibility is nonetheless above average for an empirical LLM paper because of process rigor: the design, hypotheses, pass rules, and audit gates were pre-registered in a version-controlled protocol; the authors disclose a real activation-hook bug (generated tokens leaking into prefill states), its fix, and a regression test; the headline depth-gap claim is explicitly downgraded because the supporting 21-tap sweep was designed after seeing the marginal five-tap results; and an entire hypothesis (H3) is reported as "declined to test" rather than as a fake null. Code, raw generations, and derived tables are released (github.com/PEEXR/Confidence-AI), though the five-tap generation run did not record its code version — a provenance gap the authors themselves flag. All experiments ran on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU, which is consistent with the modest model sizes.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the individual ingredients (format sensitivity of verbal confidence, probing depth sweeps, semantic entropy, Murphy decomposition) all exist in prior work — the paper's value is the disciplined head-to-head audit on matched items, which is genuinely useful and mostly absent from the literature (Mahaut et al., 2024 is the closest prior).
- **Rigor**: high on process, mixed on statistics. The pre-registered five-tap H4 test was fragile (onsets could only take three values; permutation p = 0.052), and the headline +21.26 pp gap comes from the non-pre-registered 21-tap follow-up — the authors label this honestly, but readers should treat the gap as exploratory-confirmed, not confirmed-confirmed. The strongest single finding (TF-IDF matching early-layer probes in 9/11 retrieval cells) undermines the mechanistic interpretation of H4 more than the abstract's "supports it more clearly" phrasing suggests.
- **Reproducibility**: good — public benchmarks, open-weight models, fixed seeds, released code and generations; blemished by the unversioned five-tap run.
- **Significance**: practically important for anyone building abstention/routing systems (verbal confidence alone is unsafe; sampling consensus costs ~10× inference but sorts best); moderately important for interpretability (a cautionary tale against reading early-layer probe AUROC as "the model knows").
- **Red flags / weaknesses to keep in mind**: (1) single model family (Qwen2.5 only) — the depth gap and format fragility may not transfer; (2) 500 questions per cell with only 100 test items, below the protocol's own 2,000 target, and complete-case three-signal analyses cover only 9–10 of 30 cells; (3) one passing cell (0.5B MATH L4–5) rests on 12 test items with test AUROC 0.33 at onset and is flagged as noise by the authors themselves; (4) the probe label is self-consistency, not correctness — the paper says so, but the framing "the model knows when it will disagree" can be over-read; (5) the symbolic grader under-credits correct math answers (90% human agreement, errors one-directional), slightly depressing C2/C3 accuracy.

## Strengths

- Pre-registration with falsifiable gates and honest reporting of two falsified hypotheses — a rarity in LLM evaluation papers.
- The Murphy reliability/resolution decomposition cleanly explains *why* low ECE is not evidence of a useful confidence signal; this is the paper's most transferable lesson.
- The prompt-only TF-IDF baseline is a deceptively simple and devastating control that much of the probing literature omits.
- Full 30-cell grid kept (no cherry-picked accuracy band), with out-of-band cells recorded rather than dropped.
- Bug disclosure (hook leakage), negative controls at 0% depth, and a human-audited grading gate (195/200) all raise trust in the pipeline.

## Limitations

- All results come from one open-weight family (Qwen2.5, 0.5B–7B); neither frontier-scale models nor other architectures are covered.
- The probe predicts a median split of the model's own sampling entropy — it is a self-consistency predictor, not a correctness or "knows-it-knows" detector.
- The clean depth-gap result (21 taps, 2-consecutive rule) was designed after seeing the marginal pre-registered result, weakening its confirmatory status.
- The reasoning–retrieval gap is carried mainly by GSM8K; MATH L4–5 onsets are early and unexplained, and excluding C1 nearly halves the gap (lower CI bound +1.31).
- Small per-cell samples (100 test items) and complete-case analyses limited to ~1/3 of cells; the base/instruct comparison (H3) was never run because the base model's verbal confidence was degenerate.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：论文系统考察隐藏状态线性探针在哪些层深携带（自）一致性信号，并给出探针研究的两个方法论警示——早期探针信号可能只是题面表层特征（TF-IDF 基线即可匹配）、探针标签是采样一致性而非正确性。对任何依赖模型置信度做弃权/路由的可靠自主系统，其"不要单独信言语置信度"的结论也有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the retrieval-vs-reasoning onset gap (and its TF-IDF collapse) replicate in Llama-4, Gemma-3, or Qwen3 families with different pretraining data and post-training recipes?
- Would probes trained to predict *correctness* (rather than self-consistency) show the same depth profile, or is early-layer signal specifically about answer dispersion?
- Can a stronger surface baseline (e.g., a frozen embedding model or a small MLP on the question) fully explain retrieval onsets, and does any probe depth beat it — i.e., is there any layer where genuine recall computation is visible?
- Why are MATH L4–5 onsets early while GSM8K onsets are uniformly late — is question difficulty, answer-format entropy, or chain-of-thought structure the driver?
- Does the 2× resolution advantage of sampling consensus survive with more than 10 samples, lower temperature, or cheaper approximations (e.g., log-prob ensembles), given its ~10× inference cost?
- The per-cell-calibrated verbal–internal correlation of 0.83 mostly reflects between-cell difficulty — could hierarchical/item-level calibration recover a genuinely shared latent confidence?
