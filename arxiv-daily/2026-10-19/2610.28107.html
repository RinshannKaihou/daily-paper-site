Methodology

How the guarantees are built

High-probability convergence proofs for clipped SGD and accelerated Clip-SSTM under a pure tail condition — no finite α-th moment required.

  • Setting — convex, L-smooth f on a ball around x*; unbiased stochastic first-order oracle.
  • Noise model — affine weak-Lα (Marcinkiewicz) tails: P{‖ζ‖>u} ≤ (σ0ᵅ + σ1ᵅ‖∇f‖ᵅ)/uᵅ — covers Pareto / α-stable noise whose α-th moment (and at α=2, the variance) is infinite.
  • Clipping lemma (4.1) — clipped bias ≤ b·A(x)/λα−1, second moment ≤ v·A(x)/λ2−α: the same powers of λ as under bounded moments; an unavoidable ln(1+Λ) appears exactly at α=2.
  • High-probability analysis — restarted-martingale / Freedman-style bounds; the gradient-dependent noise rides SSTM's potential so the affine scale structure survives. A weighted-gradient induction improves Clip-SSTM's multiplicative term to σ1²LR²(H/K)2r.
  • Lower bounds — two-point (Bubeck-style) arguments, plus a Huber construction whose slope is smaller than the clipping bias, pushing iterates uniformly away from x*.
  • Experiments — α=3/2 Pareto noise (E‖ζ‖ᵅ infinite); 1024 runs per configuration; horizons up to 106 oracle calls.
Assumptions 3.4 vs 3.5: bounded moments imply weak-Lα, but not conversely — this is the class gap the paper closes.

  • Unavoidable bias floor (Thm 5.5) — on a 1-D instance, any constant λ with any deterministic stepsizes leaves E f − f* ≥ ελ = (48·4ᵅ)⁻¹·min{R·b̄λ, LR²} for every iterate — upper and lower bounds match up to α-dependent constants.
  • Real data (Fig. 3, Diabetes ridge) — flat curvature amplifies clipping bias as b²/(2λmin); small fixed λ first dips, then rebounds to a plateau; λ=4 plateaus lowest among fixed levels; tuned λ(n) keeps falling.
  • Clipping can fail (Prop. H.6) — purely multiplicative noise (σ0=0) with any fixed λ>0 keeps Clip-SGD ≥ λ/(2L) from x* forever, with probability ≥ 2/3.
+ Strengths
  • Weakened exactly where it matters — α-stable / Pareto noise at the critical exponent is now covered with no rate loss for α<2.
  • Complete upper/lower picture — rates, confidence dependence, the α=2 logarithm, and the fixed-λ bias floor all matched up to constants.
  • The practical combination — user-chosen constant clipping level plus noise scale growing with the gradient — that prior analyses avoided.
  • Elegant technique — weighted-gradient induction lifts Clip-SSTM's multiplicative term from K−(α−1)/α to K−2(α−1)/α.
  • Transparent — about what the experiments do and do not test, and about concurrent work (incl. Lobanov et al. 2026).
− Limitations
  • Scope — convex, smooth, unconstrained, one oracle call per step; no non-convex, strongly-convex-with-resets, (L0,L1)-smooth, or distributed variants.
  • Multiplicative noise — guarantees need σ1 below an absolute threshold; the sharp threshold is unknown, and some restriction is provably necessary.
  • Open optimality — all σ1-dependent terms (here and in concurrent work).
  • Experiments — small-scale, synthetic-heavy, fixed α=3/2; main figures skip the theorems' own stepsize/clipping prescriptions (only Appendix J runs them).
  • Tuning in practice — the tuned λ requires knowing R, L, σ0, α and the horizon K, which practitioners do not have.
Questions for further exploration
  1. Does the weak-Lα analysis extend to (L0,L1)-smooth or non-convex objectives — where deep networks actually live — and does the α=2 logarithm persist there?
  2. Is the multiplicative term σ1²LR²K−2(α−1)/α optimal, or can a different analysis or method close the gap left open here and in Lobanov et al. (2026)?
  3. Do real LLM pretraining runs exhibit weak-Lα gradient noise at the critical exponent rather than bounded moments — and can this be detected online from gradient-norm tails?
  4. Can the fixed-λ bias neighborhood Nλ be estimated from data, so λ = λ0(K+1)1/α can be set without knowing σ0, R, or α a priori?
  5. Does the necessary √(ln eK) factor at α=2 imply any observable penalty — slower tail convergence — in empirical high-confidence training diagnostics?
