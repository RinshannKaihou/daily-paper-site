> **一句话总结：** GaP-MoE 在不引入任何辅助损失的前提下，按路由概率为每个 token 动态选出"领导者"专家，并把其余"跟随者"专家的输出梯度投影到领导者近期梯度子空间的正交补上，从而显式拉开专家更新方向；在 6 层 GPT-2 从头预训练中将 WikiText-103 困惑度从 78.80 降到 76.50（−2.3 PPL），在 BERT/GLUE 微调上平均仅提升 +0.10，且当路由器完全不训练时仍优于标准 SMoE。

> **TL;DR:** Sparse MoE models often suffer representation collapse where co-routed experts learn near-identical functions. Instead of new routers or auxiliary losses (which can conflict with the task objective), GaP-MoE samples one TopK expert per token as a leader by normalized routing probability and projects each follower's already-computed output gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the leader's recent gradient subspace (kept as a Gram–Schmidt orthonormal FIFO buffer), so specialization emerges from optimization dynamics with zero extra forward/backward passes. On WikiText-103 from-scratch pretraining of a reduced 6-layer GPT-2 SMoE (4 experts, Top-2), it cuts perplexity from 78.80 (SMoE baseline) to 76.50 (−2.3 PPL, γ=0.7) and enwik8 BPC from 2.005 to 1.953; with the router disabled it still beats SMoE (82.44 vs 83.63 PPL). But GLUE fine-tuning gains are marginal (+0.10 average over 8 tasks), and all evidence is at BERT-base / 6-layer-GPT-2 scale.

## 核心贡献

- **无需辅助损失的显式专家特化：** 不改动路由器结构、不新增损失项，直接对已经算好的专家输出梯度做正交化处理——每个 token 的 TopK 专家中按归一化路由分数概率采样一个"领导者"（保留原始任务梯度），其余"跟随者"的梯度被投影到领导者近期梯度子空间的正交补上（g* = (I − αQ_jᵀQ_j)g），从优化动力学层面消除专家间的方向性冗余。
- **梯度累积缓冲区：** 为每个专家维护一个容量为 h 的正交基缓冲区（Gram–Schmidt 正交化 + FIFO 淘汰），mini-batch 内同一专家作为领导者收到的多条梯度先求均值再入缓冲；只有领导者角色的梯度参与缓冲区更新，被投影修改过的梯度不入库，避免噪声污染。
- **理论刻画：** 给出 Proposition 1——在"短期梯度一致性"假设下，对跟随者梯度的投影能减小一次 SGD 更新后领导者与跟随者输出向量的内积（对齐度），且最小程度扰动原始任务梯度；作者同时指出刻意把相似度推到 ψ=π 反而会破坏 MoE 的加权求和，因此只做正交化而非强行反向。
- **零额外前后向传播开销：** 复用反向传播已产生的输出梯度，是可与 XMoE、SimSMoE 等现有特化方法叠加的即插即用模块。
- **实证覆盖三种场景：** BERT/GLUE 微调、GPT-2 从头预训练（WikiText-103、enwik8、text8）、以及路由器完全不训练的极端场景，并验证了与 XMoE / SimSMoE 的组合兼容性。

## Key Contributions

- **Auxiliary-loss-free explicit specialization:** GaP-MoE touches neither the router nor the loss. It post-processes expert *output gradients* that backprop has already produced — for each token, one of the TopK experts is sampled as a *leader* with probability proportional to normalized routing scores, and each *follower's* gradient is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the subspace spanned by the leader's recently accumulated gradients (g* = (I − αQ_jᵀQ_j)g), removing directional overlap between co-routed experts as a byproduct of the optimization dynamics.
- **Per-expert gradient accumulation buffers:** Each expert keeps an orthonormal basis Q_i of its recent leader gradients (buffer sizes h=16 fine-tuning, h=8 from-scratch), built by averaging the leader gradients a mini-batch assigns to the same expert, orthogonalizing via Gram–Schmidt against the current basis, and evicting the oldest vector FIFO when full. Projected (follower) gradients never enter the buffer, preventing projection-induced noise from feeding back.
- **A projection trigger and warmup:** Projection fires only when the cosine similarity of the two experts' most recent basis vectors exceeds a threshold γ (γ=−1 means always project; γ=0.7 projects only on high overlap), and the projection intensity α ramps linearly from 0.1 to 1.0 over the first epoch to avoid reacting to early noisy gradients.
- **Theoretical justification (Proposition 1):** Under a short-term gradient consistency assumption (consecutive gradients of an expert nearly parallel, cos≈1), projecting the follower output gradient reduces the post-update inner product ⟨θ_j^l(t+1)x, θ_k^f(t+1)x⟩ between the two experts' outputs for the same token, while minimally altering the task gradient. The authors deliberately do *not* minimize output similarity outright, since pushing experts to ψ=π would make their contributions cancel in the router's weighted sum.
- **Zero extra forward/backward passes and drop-in compatibility:** The method reuses computed gradients and was shown to stack with XMoE and SimSMoE in the same training run.

## Methodology

The method operates in three steps per training iteration (Algorithm 1/2, Top-2 routing for clarity):

1. **Dynamic Leader Selection.** The router produces sparse scores G_i(x) over N=4 experts; the nonzero TopK scores are normalized into probabilities p_i, and a leader is sampled l ~ Categorical(p_1..p_N). The leader keeps its untouched task gradient; the other selected expert(s) become followers.
2. **Gradient Projection.** After the standard backward pass yields output gradients g_i = ∂L/∂y_i, the method checks the cosine similarity between the *most recent* basis vectors b_j^(r), b_k^(r) of the leader's and follower's buffers. If cossim > γ, the follower gradient is replaced by g* = (I − αQ_jᵀQ_j)g_k — i.e., the component of the follower's gradient lying in the leader's recent gradient subspace is subtracted. α=1 (full projection) is used in all experiments after the first-epoch warmup.
3. **Gradient Accumulation.** For each expert, the mean of the gradients it received *as leader* in this mini-batch is normalized, orthogonalized against its current basis vectors (Gram–Schmidt), and appended as a new basis vector; when the buffer is full the oldest vector is dropped (FIFO). Q_i thus represents expert i's recent *unprojected* update subspace.

The theoretical section links gradient alignment to output similarity: under SGD, the output-space update is θ(t+1)x = θ(t)x − ηg(t)‖x‖²₂, so co-aligned gradients drag co-routed experts' outputs toward the same subspace (Figure 2). Proposition 1 (proof in Appendix B) expands the post-update leader–follower output inner product into four terms and argues the projection zeroes the cross terms under the short-term gradient consistency assumption — an assumption the authors then probe empirically (Section 4.6): in later layers, cosine similarity between an expert's current gradient and its basis from 1/3/5/10 steps back stays persistently high, while in first layers it decays toward zero with gap width. This is precisely why the from-scratch experiments apply GaP-MoE only to the **last two** MoE layers. Experiments use BERT-base-uncased (12 FFN layers replaced by 4-expert Top-2 MoE, experts identically initialized from pretrained weights, 5 epochs, GLUE) and a reduced GPT-2 (6 layers, 8 heads, intermediate dim 2048) trained from scratch on WikiText-103 (perplexity), enwik8 and text8 (BPC), with early stopping patience 3 evaluated every 1,000 steps; results are averaged over 3 seeds.

## Results & Highlights

- **From-scratch pretraining (the strongest result):** On WikiText-103, GaP-MoE(γ=0.7) reaches **76.50 perplexity vs 78.80 for the SMoE baseline (−2.30 PPL, ≈2.9% relative)**, with the model applying GaP-MoE to only its last two layers. On enwik8 it reaches **1.953 BPC vs 2.005 (−0.052)** and on text8 **1.945 BPC vs 1.962 (−0.017)** — the authors themselves note the text8 gain is the weakest since that dataset (alphabetics+spaces only) is relatively easy.
- **GLUE fine-tuning (marginal):** Average over 8 GLUE tasks is **82.06 (γ=−1) / 81.98 (γ=0.7) vs 81.96 for SMoE — a +0.10 / +0.02 delta**. The best single-task gain is RTE 64.86 → 65.46 (+0.60, γ=−1); CoLA improves 56.00 → 56.44 (Matthews corr., γ=0.7); but MRPC *drops* 85.21 → 84.64 (−0.57) under γ=−1, so the improvement is not uniformly distributed.
- **Robustness to router failure:** With the router untrained and two experts picked randomly per token on WikiText-103, GaP-MoE still outperforms SMoE — **81.35 (γ=−1) and 82.44 (γ=0.7) PPL vs 83.63 for SMoE** — supporting the claim that specialization is induced through gradients, not routing quality. (Note: the absolute degradation from the trained-router setting is *not* smaller for GaP-MoE(0.7): 76.50 → 82.44 = +5.94 vs SMoE's 78.80 → 83.63 = +4.83; what the data shows is a preserved advantage, not a smaller drop.)
- **Drop-in compatibility (Table 4):** Applied on top of SimSMoE, GaP-MoE(γ=0.7) yields RTE 65.22 → 65.58 (+0.36), CoLA 57.89 → 58.09, STS-B 88.90 → 88.95. On XMoE the picture is mixed: RTE 64.86 → 65.82 (+0.96, γ=0.7) but CoLA 58.65 → 57.80 (−0.85, γ=−1), and the authors acknowledge the combined models are noticeably more sensitive to γ than GaP-MoE alone.
- **Specialization diagnostics (Figures 3–4):** With γ=−1, the cosine similarity between expert outputs in the final layer drops substantially across all tokens of a CoLA sequence, and the angle between Top-2 expert output vectors in layer 6 widens visibly; with γ=0.7 the reduction is marginal (projection fires only on high overlap) — yet γ=−1 and γ=0.7 perform comparably on GLUE, and full projection (γ=−1) is slightly better on average.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is a two-author preprint (arXiv 2610.29355v1, cs.LG, October 2026) from the Department of Statistics / Statistics and Data Science at the University of Seoul (Donghyun Jeong; Yoon-Yeong Kim, corresponding author). It is formatted as a conference submission ("A PREPRINT") and, per the authors' own conclusion, is under-submitted rather than peer-reviewed. The related-work coverage is solid and current (Chi et al.'s XMoE, SimSMoE, SD-MoE, CompeteSMoE, DeepSeekMoE, and 2025–2026 gradient-conflict work), the method is specified to the level of full pseudocode (Algorithm 2) and per-experiment hyperparameters (Appendix C: AdamW, lr 2e-5 fine-tuning / 3e-4 from scratch, buffer ranks 16/8, single 48GB RTX A6000-class GPUs), and claims are largely calibrated to the small scale of the experiments — the authors themselves flag unexplored hyperparameters and small model scale as limitations. No code release is mentioned. Credibility is moderate: honest about weaknesses, but all baselines beyond standard SMoE (XMoE, SimSMoE) are the authors' own reimplementations, since "no official implementation of XMoE is publicly available" and SimSMoE's official code targets pretraining rather than their fine-tuning setting.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate.** Gradient projection is imported wholesale from multi-task/continual learning (PCGrad, GEM, OGD — all cited); the contribution is re-aiming it from *task* conflicts to *expert* conflicts inside one MoE layer, with the probabilistic leader/follower split as the main new device. The design space was already crowded: Liu et al. (ECAI 2024) diversify MoE representations with an orthogonal optimizer, and Guo et al. (NeurIPS 2026) orthogonalize expert parameters via auxiliary losses — GaP-MoE's genuine differentiator is doing it without any loss modification or gradient recomputation.
- **Rigor: the headline GLUE result is within noise.** +0.10 average over 8 GLUE tasks (81.96 → 82.06), with per-task swings in both directions (MRPC −0.57, RTE +0.60), "averaged over three seeds" but with **no standard deviations reported anywhere** — a table of stds could plausibly swallow the entire fine-tuning gain. The paper's real quantitative payload is the WikiText-103 result (−2.3 PPL), which rests on a *single* architecture at 6-layer scale.
- **The untrained-router claim is over-worded.** Section 4.3 states "the performance drop is considerably smaller for GaP-MoE," but for γ=0.7 the drop is actually *larger* (76.50→82.44, +5.94 vs SMoE's +4.83); the defensible statement is that GaP-MoE retains its advantage under router failure, which the numbers do support.
- **Missing ablations the design begs for.** Buffer size h (only 16/8 tried, with an anecdotal justification from Figure 5), the number/identity of layers receiving GaP-MoE (fine-tuning uses all 12, from-scratch only the last 2 — a choice made *post hoc* from the consistency analysis), α (fixed at 1 after warmup), and K>2 routing are all unexplored; the authors concede this explicitly in the conclusion.
- **The theory leans on an empirical assumption.** Proposition 1's proof requires consecutive expert gradients with cosine ≈ 1 ("short-term gradient consistency"); Figure 5 shows this holds in later layers but decays toward 0 in first layers — so the mechanism is formally justified only for the layers where the authors happened to apply it. The proof itself is a chain of ≈ 0 approximations rather than a bound.
- **No cost accounting.** "No additional forward/backward passes" is true, but Gram–Schmidt over an h×H buffer (16×768) per expert per step and per-pair projection checks have a real cost that is never measured — no wall-clock, memory, or throughput comparison against SMoE appears anywhere.
- **Reproducibility: partially supported.** Full pseudocode plus per-task hyperparameters (Appendix C) make reimplementation feasible, but there is no code release, and the XMoE/SimSMoE comparison numbers rest on unvalidated reimplementations never checked against the original papers' published results.

## Strengths

- A genuinely clean idea: specialization induced purely by post-hoc gradient surgery, so the task objective is never traded off against a diversity regularizer — sidestepping the loss-conflict problem the paper convincingly motivates.
- Zero architectural changes and zero extra forward/backward passes; confirmed to compose with two existing specialization methods (XMoE, SimSMoE) rather than competing with them.
- The untrained-router experiment (Table 3) is an unusually direct test of the paper's core claim that specialization comes from gradients, not routing — and GaP-MoE passes it.
- Honest layer-wise gradient-consistency analysis (Figure 5) that actually explains a design choice (last-2-layer application in from-scratch training) instead of being decorative.
- The related-work section is a genuinely useful map of the representation-collapse / expert-specialization literature, including 2026 work.

## Limitations

- Small scale throughout: BERT-base fine-tuning and a 6-layer, 8-head reduced GPT-2; no evidence whatsoever about behavior at the model sizes where MoE actually matters (billions of parameters, hundreds/thousands of experts, K>2), which the authors acknowledge.
- Fine-tuning gains on GLUE (+0.10 avg) are too small to be practically meaningful without variance reporting; several tasks regress.
- Four new hyperparameters (h, γ, α, layer selection) with no systematic sweep; the authors state reported settings "may not be optimal."
- Only NLP tasks and only Top-2 routing with N=4 experts; the K>2 extension is described but never evaluated.
- No efficiency measurements (wall-clock/memory) despite efficiency being one of the three advertised benefits.
- Evidence for reduced expert-output similarity is qualitative (bar charts and PCA-projected angle plots from single seeds/tasks: CoLA in Fig. 3, one QNLI token in Fig. 4) rather than a systematic quantitative metric across runs.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接触及"LLM Training Stability & Observability"中关注的 MoE 路由/负载不稳定与训练动态问题：表示坍缩和专家特化失效是 MoE 训练的核心失效模式之一，GaP-MoE 提供了一种不依赖路由器健康度的梯度级干预手段（路由器完全不训练时仍优于 SMoE），对理解与缓解大规模 MoE 训练中的专家同化现象有参考价值；但实验规模小（BERT-base 与 6 层 GPT-2），距离真实大模型的 MoE 稳定性实践尚远。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the −2.3 PPL WikiText-103 gain survive at 12-layer/full GPT-2 scale, with larger expert counts (e.g., 16–64 experts) and K>2 routing where leader–follower pairs multiply?
- What is the actual wall-clock and memory overhead of maintaining h×H orthonormal buffers per expert and performing per-pair projections, especially when N is large and buffers must be sharded across devices?
- How does GaP-MoE interact with load-balancing auxiliary losses (e.g., auxiliary-loss-free balancing à la DeepSeek-V3) — does projecting follower gradients change token routing statistics and inadvertently worsen load imbalance?
- Since fine-tuning applied GaP-MoE to all 12 layers but from-scratch only the last 2, is there a principled, adaptive criterion (e.g., online gradient-consistency estimation) for choosing which layers get projection?
- Would reporting per-seed standard deviations erase the GLUE gains entirely, and does the method help more on harder token-routing regimes (long-tail domains, multilingual mixes) where specialization pressure is higher?
