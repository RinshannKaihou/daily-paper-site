> **一句话总结：** 南京大学团队提出 AUTOTAVERN——由六个专职 LLM 研究智能体（共享类型化研究记忆、五道验证门禁、真实天梯对局闭环反馈）组成的自改进研究系统：20 天内自主建成覆盖酒馆战棋全部 978 个游戏物件的确定性模拟器 TAVERNGYM，交付 23 个 PPO 训练的 TAVERNAGENT 版本（模拟器主考试第一名率从 1.9% 提升到 41.5%），在真实天梯上评分从 6,000 自行爬到约 15,000（服务器前 100），其近 20 场对局平均名次 1.60、第一名率 80%，全面超过服务器前十玩家（2.34、59%）；而在 960 任务的 TAVERNBENCH 上，构建该系统的前沿 LLM 自己决策只答对 63%（GPT-6 Astra）且延迟 1.4–26 秒，远逊于它们造出的 0.03 秒、91% 的智能体——LLM 智能体"造"远强于"玩"。

> **TL;DR:** This Nanjing University paper presents AUTOTAVERN, a self-improving research system in which six specialized LLM research agents (GPT-6.1 Sol / GPT-6 Astra / Claude Opus 5.5, sharing a typed research memory with five verification gates and a live-ladder reality loop) built, in 20 days, TAVERNGYM — the first faithful full-content simulator of Hearthstone Battlegrounds (all 978 objects of patch 36.6.3, 190–280 games/s on one core) — and TAVERNAGENT, an 18M-parameter PPO transformer whose first-place rate on the simulator's main exam rose from 1.9% (TA-1) to 41.5% (TA-23) vs 0.0% for a scripted bot, and which climbed the live ladder from rating 6,000 to ~15,000, beating the server's ten top players on every statistic of their last-20-record comparison (1.60 avg placement / 80% first vs 2.34 / 59%). On their own TAVERNBENCH (960 tasks), the best frontier LLM playing directly (GPT-6 Astra) answers only 63% of play tasks vs 91% for the agent they built, and every general LLM needs 1.4–26 s per decision vs 0.03 s — within the 1-second live-turn budget the agent keeps 90% while no LLM exceeds 26%. The honest headline: LLM agents build far better than they play.

## 核心贡献

- **AUTOTAVERN 自改进研究系统**：研究负责人、环境、训练、对手建模、评估审计、部署六个长生命周期 LLM 智能体从不直接对话，只通过类型化、版本化的共享研究记忆（编号发现的台账、220 条已确认规则裁定的注册表、交接笔记、实验注册表、教训库）协调；每次迭代遵循"观察→诊断→计划→实现→训练→评估→部署→反思"八步循环，在训练（分钟）、研究（小时）、天梯（天）三个时间尺度上运转。
- **五道验证门禁对抗虚假发现**：G1 构建（全测试套件）、G2 契约（模型绑定训练它的模拟器构建，且两套输入编码器逐位比对）、G3 比较（只在同一构建、多随机种子内比较）、G4 交付（必须在未见过的种子上复核——源自"赢家诅咒"教训：快照在选择种子上虚高 +1.2–2.1 点、未见种子上 −0.7 点）、G5 现实（交付模型上天梯对局并在模拟器中重放）。
- **现实锚定的闭环自改进**：真实对局记录被重放于模拟器中定位"仿真与现实的分歧"，20 天触发 184 处修复（35 处规则、73 处输入编码、51 处客户端控制器、14 次对手库重校准）；被抓住的错误转化为每次会话加载的教训或新门禁，使"同一个错误不付两次代价"——6 个典型案例（如复制的对局携带随机数状态泄漏未来商店，虚假 +23.6 点第一名率，重新播种后消失为 −0.65 ± 1.14）被如实报告。
- **TAVERNGYM——首个忠实完整复刻**：Rust 引擎 63k 行（27k 为卡牌脚本），实现 36.6.3 补丁全部 978 个物件，单核每秒 190–280 圀完整八人对局、13–19 µs 一次后期战斗；245 个离散动作配精确合法掩码，支持批量环境与游戏状态的精确存取恢复；375 条验证记录 + 恒常量模糊测试守护。
- **TAVERNAGENT——首个打完整天梯对局且最强的战棋 AI**：实体 Transformer（≤56 token、卡牌=身份嵌入+静态特征+规则文本嵌入、指针头输出 245 动作，d=384/8 层/18M 参数）用 PPO 训练，对手为按真实对局逐轮重校准的"现实校准库"（这一项是最大单项收益）；人类只在设定目标、裁定模糊机制和按下 AI 点名的快捷键（全程仅 418 条消息，占全部消息的 0.6%）。
- **TAVERNBENCH 与"造胜于玩"的实证**：960 任务、40 场景、八类决策的基准，由模拟器标注（战斗 4,096 次模拟、策略任务由 TA-21 每个计划 128 次打完）；构建系统的前沿 LLM 们亲自决策时最好 63%、实时预算内全军覆没（除两个专用决策模型外），而它们造的智能体 91%、0.03 秒。

## Key Contributions

- AUTOTAVERN, a self-improving research system: six long-lived LLM research agents (research lead, environment, training, opponent modelling, evaluation & audit, deployment, plus short-lived sub-agents) coordinate only through a typed, versioned research memory; iterations follow an Observe → Diagnose → Plan → Implement → Train → Evaluate → Deploy → Reflect cycle across three time scales (training in minutes, research in hours, ladder in days). Over 20 days it ran ~10,000 registered experiments and delivered 23 agent versions.
- Five verification gates (build, contract, comparison, delivery, reality) stand between every claim and the shared memory, explicitly targeting two named failure modes: proxy drift (proxy improves while real performance does not) and false findings (noise/leaks recorded as results). Caught errors become standing lessons or new gates — four of six reported case-study errors changed the process itself.
- A reality loop: every live ladder game is logged, linked to the agent's decisions, and replayed inside the simulator; discrepancies drive fixes of rules, input encodings, the client controller, and opponent models (184 fixes total, 73 of them input-encoding mismatches between controller and simulator).
- TAVERNGYM: the first faithful, full-content reproduction of the live game — all 978 objects of patch 36.6.3, deterministic (one seeded RNG; a seed plus action sequence reproduces a game bit for bit), 190–280 complete eight-player games/s on a single core, 245 masked discrete actions, exact save/restore, guarded by 375 verification records, 220 confirmed rule rulings, unit tests and invariant-checking fuzzers.
- TAVERNAGENT: an 18M-parameter entity transformer (identity + static-feature + rule-text embeddings per card, pointer head over 245 masked actions) trained with PPO (~1M decisions per iteration, 300-iteration stages of ~310M decisions) against reality-calibrated opponent libraries; the first Battlegrounds AI to play complete games on the live ladder by itself.
- TAVERNBENCH (960 tasks, 40 scenarios, 8 categories) with simulator-generated labels, a real-time scoring view (answers within a live turn's ~1 s), and a tool-use control condition, establishing the paper's title claim quantitatively: the frontier LLMs that built the system play far below it.

## Methodology

The system is organized around "reality-anchored self-improvement." The research state at iteration t consists of artifacts (environment E_t, opponent model O_t, exams X_t, policy π_t) plus the process itself (memory M_t, gates G_t); agents can only improve a proxy objective inside the loop, while the true objective J(π) — expected placement on the live ladder — is observable only through slow, costly real games. Two failure modes follow (proxy drift, false findings), answered respectively by the reality loop and the verification gates.

TAVERNGYM is a Rust workspace (game engine + thin Python bindings) with one shared effect context for recruit and combat phases, an entity-arena state that is cheap to copy and serializable, and a single seeded RNG for exact determinism; card data is generated from the game's own files with patch overlays, and a rule hash identifies each build (a checkpoint trained under another rule hash is refused by the controller).

TAVERNAGENT is an entity transformer: each own minion, hand card, tavern offer, pending choice and opponent is a token (≤56 plus a global token); card representation sums an identity embedding, static features, and a rule-text embedding (giving rare cards a sensible prior); a pointer head produces 245 masked action logits alongside value and placement heads. It is trained with PPO on ~1M decisions per iteration with terminal placement utility; each 300-iteration stage (~310M decisions, 570k games) starts from the previous stage under a KL anchor. Instead of self-play, the learner faces seven library seats built from statistics of top human players and recalibrated turn-by-turn to the opponents actually met in live games; exam libraries are never used for training.

Live deployment: a controller feeds the live game state to the policy in the environment's own encoding; the AI decides and names every operation, and a human presses the official plug-in's shortcut key exactly as named, contributing no decisions. Client-failure games (96 of 388) are flagged from controller logs alone, before looking at results. TAVERNBENCH labels come from the simulator (executions, 4,096-combat simulations, one-step-lookahead decision scoring, play-out strategy labels from TA-21), and LLM latency is standardized via Artificial Analysis medians (time to first token + answer at median output speed).

## Results & Highlights

- Simulator main exam (24,576 games per row, one build, three unseen seeds): TAVERNAGENT improves from TA-1 (avg placement 3.40, 1.9% first, 83.4% top-4) to final TA-23 (2.33 avg placement, 41.5% first, 89.0% top-4); random legal play and a scripted bot get 0.0% first places (7.97 / 7.54 avg placement). On the harder exam TA-23 holds 23.8% first / 3.28 placement.
- Live ladder: over 392 games (days 5–18) the rating rose from 6,000 to 14,007 and, with the loop still running, to ~15,000 (server top 100) with no plateau; the latest versions (TA-19–23, client failures excluded) averaged placement 2.37 with 32.6% first and 91.3% top-4. On the public-leaderboard last-20-record measure the agent posts 1.60 avg placement and 80% first vs 2.34 and 59% for the server's ten highest-rated players (best individual: P4 at 1.88 / 65%), and its rating gain per record (+64) is nine times theirs (+7).
- TAVERNBENCH play tasks (368 tasks common to all systems): TAVERNAGENT answers 91% at 0.03 s per task; the best direct-playing LLM, GPT-6 Astra, reaches 63%, Claude models 52–53%, Grok 4.7 22%. Within the 1-second live-turn budget the agent keeps 90%, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 26% and Jev 37%, every other LLM falls to 0% — their median decision time is 1.4–26 s (GPT-6 Astra 2.4 s; GLM-5.3 26 s).
- Opponent-calibration ablation (same checkpoint, same 150-iteration budget): reality-calibrated libraries raise first-place rate from 20.9% to 27.2% on the main exam (10.3% → 13.5% on the harder exam) vs uncalibrated libraries — the single largest gain in the project.
- Rules vs play dissociation: frontier LLMs execute rules well (GPT-6 Astra 95%, Claude Opus 5.5 92%, Gemini 3.8 Flash 90% on rule tasks) yet collapse on play tasks built from those same rules; given TAVERNGYM as a tool, GPT-6 Astra's short-term decision accuracy jumps from 62% to 97%, showing the gap is largely simulation ability, not knowledge.
- Head-to-head eight-version lobbies (fair share: 12.5% first): final TA-23 wins 26.2% first places, twice its fair share; decision-value ablations show Dark Gifts are worth +1.94 placement when never taken, Discover choices +1.64 when randomized, targets +1.45.
- The reality loop catches what simulation praises: TA-10 nearly doubled its predecessor's exam first-place rate (24.2% vs 12.8%) yet on the ladder its rating fell from 8,968 to 7,658 within 32 games; the loop paused, traced the fall to client failures plus a hidden early/mid-game weakness, and TA-11 resumed the climb.

## Authors & Credibility

Changlian Ma and Limin Wang (Nanjing University; Limin Wang also Shanghai AI Laboratory) — an established computer-vision/multimodal group (MCG-NJU). The paper's credibility rests on unusually honest self-reporting: it documents six caught errors (including a look-ahead leak through copied games' RNG state that inflated first-place rate by a fake +23.6 points), publishes failure modes as first-class lessons, flags client-failure games from logs before looking at results, and uses the same public-leaderboard estimator for the agent and human top players rather than mixing its own logs. Compute is disclosed precisely (8×H200, ~1,900 GPU-hours main-line, ~16,700 exploratory, ~1,300 evaluation; ~100M agent output tokens). TAVERNBENCH and TAVERNGYM are promised open-source at github.com/MCG-NJU/AutoTavern. Weaknesses: the paper is v1 (24 Oct 2026), single-game/single-patch, and the headliner ladder comparison uses estimated placements from rating changes rather than direct head-to-head games.

## Critical Assessment

- Novelty: high on integration, moderate on components. PPO, entity transformers, LLM research agents, and memory/gate scaffolds all exist; the novelty is the closed reality loop with verification gates on a task where ground truth is the commercial ladder — going beyond AI-Scientist-style benchmarks and MLE-bench-style proxy tasks. The "build vs play" dissociation (LLMs build far better than they play) is a genuinely crisp empirical finding.
- Rigor: strong inside the simulator (24,576 games per table point, unseen seeds, version head-to-heads, opponent-calibration and model-size ablations), but the ladder evidence is thinner than the abstract suggests: 392 games on one account, and the "rivals top players" claim rests on 20-record windows of an estimator the authors themselves admit only infers placements from rating changes. Rating gain also confounds strength with matchmaking dynamics (opponents met were rated 12,190–17,583, median 13,294), and the agent's +64/record partly reflects climbing through a still-recovering rating pool.
- A specific circularity concern: TAVERNBENCH's strategy labels are play-outs by TA-21 — an earlier version of the very system being evaluated — so TAVERNAGENT's 75% on strategy tasks partially measures agreement with its own lineage (TA-1 gets 57%, the labeller TA-21 itself 73%); hero/trinket labels do use human statistics, and rules/combat labels come from the simulator, which softens but does not remove this.
- Latency for LLM baselines is not directly measured in-house but reconstructed from Artificial Analysis medians; the authors mitigate with own-token timings (3–50 s median, same real-time scores), yet cross-provider timing comparability remains an assumption. LLM output-budget overruns (48% of Grok 4.7's answers) penalize verbose models unevenly.
- Reproducibility: good for the artifacts being open-sourced (bench, gym), weaker for the headline system — AUTOTAVERN's agent framework, research memory and gates are not clearly released, and reproducing costs ~19,900 GPU-hours plus ~100M frontier-LLM output tokens. The "20 days from scratch" framing also starts from a pre-existing agentic framework and an 8×H200 cluster, so the autonomy claim should be read as 418 human messages within a heavily provisioned harness (24% of which set or changed goals, 5% ruled on game mechanics).
- Missing ablations: no self-play baseline against the library-opponent recipe, no comparison to a human-engineered simulator trained with the same PPO stack (so the agents' contribution vs a competent engineer is inferred, not measured), and generalization to other patches/games is untested despite content changing "with every patch."
- Red flags: mild overclaiming in the title ("Rivals Top Players") relative to the 20-record-window evidence; the abstract's "about 15,000" rating was still rising, so the final level is uncertain. No ethics problem: games were played on the authors' own account in solo mode.

## Strengths

- Reality as the final evaluator: live ladder games close the loop, and simulation-vs-reality discrepancies are systematically converted into 184 concrete fixes — the most convincing demonstration of proxy-drift correction I have seen in an LLM-research-agent paper.
- Verification engineering as a first-class contribution: the five gates, each traceable to a caught failure (G4 born from winner's curse; the bit-level encoder comparison born from the warband-dumping bug), give a reusable recipe for trustworthy autonomous research memory.
- Exceptional measurement hygiene: determinism via a single seeded RNG, rule hashes binding models to builds, client-failure games flagged before seeing results, fair-share baselines in head-to-head lobbies, per-decision-value ablations (+1.94 places for Dark Gifts), and hero-strength dissociation from humans (r = 0.13) reported rather than hidden.
- TAVERNBENCH's real-time view (the 1-second budget) reframes game benchmarks around deployability, and the tool-use condition (GPT-6 Astra 62% → 97% with the simulator) cleanly attributes the LLM play gap to simulation ability.
- The paper reports its own false positives with numbers, which is rare and valuable for calibration of autonomous-research claims.

## Limitations

- Single game, single patch (36.6.3), single ladder account; content churn is acknowledged but untested — every patch invalidates card data, and the 20-day cost to re-converge is unknown.
- The top-player comparison is an estimate over 20-record windows using the leaderboard's inferred placements, not head-to-head play; the sample (392 games, 388 valid placements) is modest for a stochastic eight-player game.
- Strategy-task labels in TAVERNBENCH come from play-outs of TA-21, a sibling of the evaluated agent; TAVERNAGENT is also not scored on rule tasks (answered by its own simulator by construction).
- Autonomy is bounded: a human sets goals, rules on ambiguous mechanics (220 confirmed rulings needed 5% of human messages), and presses every live-game key; 418 messages over 20 days is low but not zero supervision.
- Cost: ~19,900 total GPU-hours and ~100M frontier-LLM output tokens for one game's ladder-level play — the paradigm's economics do not yet scale across many domains.
- No self-play or human-engineered-simulator baseline, so the agents' marginal value over a strong ML engineer with the same compute is not isolated.

## Relevance

这篇论文是你"自进化智能体与自动化科研（Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research）"兴趣的直接核心案例：它展示了 LLM 研究智能体在真实环境（而非代理基准）上闭环自改进——验证门禁防虚假发现、现实循环防代理漂移、教训记忆改进研究过程本身，且其中心评估主张正是"自主研究循环在真实世界指标上成立"。它同时为"长周期自主研究系统的可靠性与可信度"提供了少见的错误—修复—规则化实证记录。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of AUTOTAVERN's advantage comes from the LLM agents versus the PPO + simulator stack itself? A control run with human engineers given the same 8×H200 budget and 20 days would quantify the agents' marginal contribution.
- Do the five gates and the reality loop transfer to domains where "reality" is slower or costlier than a game ladder (e.g., robotics, real experiments), where the G5 reality gate cannot be exercised dozens of times per day?
- Would self-play with league-style opponents match or beat the reality-calibrated library seats, and does library-based training cap the agent at the strength of recorded human boards (a ceiling the graded harder libraries only partly escape)?
- When patch 36.6.x rolls in, how many of the 20 days' worth of lessons, gates, and opponent libraries transfer, and can the loop re-converge to top-100 rating in materially fewer days — the true test of self-improvement as accumulated process capital?
- Can the strategy-label circularity in TAVERNBENCH be removed, e.g., by labelling play-outs from a diverse ensemble of agents or from actual human replay continuations, and would the LLMs-vs-TAVERNAGENT gap change?
- The agents that build are far better than the agents that play: does fine-tuning on the built artifact's decisions (distillation from TAVERNAGENT) close the play gap for general LLMs, or is the deficit fundamentally about learned world simulation rather than knowledge?
