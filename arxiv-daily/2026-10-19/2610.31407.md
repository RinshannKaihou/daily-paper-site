> **一句话总结：** 该论文用 601 对经人工审核的 Wikidata 人名对（含天城文与拉丁文两种已证实写法）做受控身份匹配实验，发现 Qwen2.5-7B 在马拉地语提示下同人行文（Dev–Dev）准确率仅 8.5% 而拉丁写法为 35.1%，但负例准确率保持 >98%；通过线性探针、输出校准与激活修补证明大部分缺陷源于决策阈值（criterion）错位而非判别信息丢失，并在第二个实体（E2）的中层残差状态上定位到因果敏感位点——一个不依赖具体事实的共享向量干预将留出集马拉地语同人准确率从 6.5% 提升到 33.5%，同时负例准确率仍有 98.5%。

> **TL;DR:** Using 601 manually audited Wikidata identity pairs with attested Devanagari and Latin name forms (plus a 374-pair Malayalam replication), the authors show Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's same-person accuracy collapses from 35.1% (Lat–Lat) to 8.5% (Dev–Dev) under Marathi prompts while different-person accuracy stays above 98% — yet a Latin-trained linear probe still reaches 0.933 balanced accuracy on Devanagari states and a single scalar calibration constant recovers 87% of the model–probe gap, showing the model "knows" but miscalibrates its answer. Activation patching localizes causal sensitivity to the second entity's middle layers (layers 10–18), where a fact-independent steering vector lifts held-out Marathi same-person accuracy from 6.5% to 33.5% at 98.5% negative accuracy. Essential reading if you care about multilingual reliability, probing, or mechanistic accounts of response bias.

## 核心贡献

- **受控的跨文字身份匹配基准**：601 对人工审核的 Wikidata 人名关系（404 对别名词 + 197 对显式姓名属性），每个名字都有真实观测到的天城文与拉丁文写法；通过独立替换两个名字的文字形式，构造 Dev–Dev / Dev–Lat / Lat–Dev / Lat–Lat 四种条件，同时固定问题语言、人物与关系，把"文字表面形式"这一单一变量干净地隔离出来。
- **跨模型、跨语系的稳健失效证据**：五种模型（Qwen2.5-7B、Gemma-2-2b、Llama-3.1-8B、Mistral-Small-24B、Sarvam-M-24B）表现出不同的应答偏置；在 374 对马拉雅拉姆语（达罗毗荼语系、另一套文字）对上复现了同样效应，证明这不是天城文或印欧语系特有的问题。
- **区分"判别能力"与"决策阈值"**：AUROC 显示排序能力损失远小于准确率损失（马拉雅拉姆语双本族文字条件下准确率仅 5.3% 但 AUROC 高达 0.925）；线性探针与输出校准实验证明大量标签信息仍然可及，主要瓶颈是决策准则（criterion）放置错误。
- **因果定位与可复用干预**：激活修补把因果敏感性定位在第二个实体（E2）的中层（10–18 层，注意力块贡献远大于 MLP：5.41 对 0.51）；一个从训练实体学到的共享方向向量在该位点做多层窗口干预，不依赖测试样本的 donor，即可将留出集马拉地语同人准确率从 6.5% 提升到 33.5%。
- **诚实的评估协议**：明确区分解码可及性（探针）、排序（AUROC）与用户实际获得的决策（给定 FPR 下的 TPR）三种评估目标，并在干预后同时报告两种文字条件，检验"提升"是否也缩小了文字间差距。

## Key Contributions

- A controlled identity-verification benchmark of 601 manually audited Wikidata person-name pairs (404 observed-alias relations, 197 explicit name-property relations), where every name has attested Devanagari and Latin forms, so only script-associated surface forms vary while question language, people, and relation stay fixed.
- A five-model behavioral study revealing distinct response biases (Qwen rejects, Gemma over-accepts — different-person accuracy as low as 25.2%), plus a second audited 374-pair Malayalam corpus built from the same entities that reproduces the effect in a Dravidian language and a second script.
- A diagnosis separating discrimination from criterion placement: Latin-trained ridge probes reach 0.933 balanced accuracy on Devanagari states, and a single validation-selected constant added to the output margin lifts Marathi balanced accuracy from 0.525 to 0.879, recovering 87% of the model–probe gap.
- Causal localization via activation patching: the second entity (E2) in Qwen's middle layers is the dominant site (+2.66 to +3.16 margin change vs ~0 for E1), an English Lat–Lat donor transfers across prompt language (6.26 margin change), and one shared sensitivity direction transfers roughly half the full-state margin correction.
- A fact-independent, multi-layer (12–17) steering intervention at E2 that raises held-out Marathi same-person accuracy from 6.5% to 33.5% (English 28.3% → 59.0%) while keeping negative accuracy at 98.5%+ — with AUROC essentially unchanged, explicitly framing the gain as a criterion shift with template-dependent ranking effects.

## Methodology

The task gives the model both candidate names and a fixed question ("Are X and Y the same person?"), varying only whether each name appears in its attested Devanagari or Latin form. Each positive pair has a hard different-person negative matched by occupation (99.8% share occupation), country, and relation tier. Three prompt languages (Marathi, Hindi, English) with three paraphrases and both yes/no answer-key assignments; scoring uses the final-position logit margin m = z_yes − z_no without sampling, with 2,000 item-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals. A fixed 361/120/120 train/val/test split governs all fitted components.

The analysis proceeds in three tiers. (1) Behavioral: accuracy and margin AUROC across four script conditions for five models, plus signal-detection d′ to separate discrimination from response bias, and romanization controls that cut mean name length from 16.5 tokens to 7.7–10.4 without recovering accuracy. (2) Information: ridge probes (α = 10) trained on Lat–Lat hidden states only and evaluated on Dev–Dev, compared against a five-feature string classifier (sequence similarity, token/character Jaccard, length ratio, final-token agreement) under both the original and a similarity-matched negative map; plus scalar calibration of the output margin. (3) Causal: activation patching that swaps Dev–Dev residual states for Latin-bearing donor states at E1/E2/question-mark/readout sites under a strict causal gate (must fail Dev–Dev and succeed in donor across paraphrases and answer keys), rank-one donor-projected patching along candidate directions (sensitivity gradient, mean difference, PCA, probe, random), and finally a layer-12–17 steering vector aℓ built from training items' mean E2 changes across script substitutions, with layer window and scale (12 for Marathi, 24 for English) selected on validation and evaluated frozen on held-out entities and a held-out paraphrase.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline script gap (Marathi, 601 pairs, Qwen2.5-7B):** same-person accuracy 8.5% in Dev–Dev vs 35.1% in Lat–Lat (−26.6 points), with different-person accuracy ≥ 98.6% in every condition (99.8% in Dev–Dev). The margin-AUROC discrimination gap is real but far smaller: +0.037 [0.025, 0.049] for Lat–Lat over Dev–Dev in Marathi, +0.038 in Hindi, +0.023 [0.014, 0.033] in English.
- **Second script, stronger dissociation (374 Malayalam pairs):** both-native same-person accuracy 5.3% vs 48.8% both-Latin — a +43.5-point gap [40.0, 47.1] — while ranking stays near ceiling at 0.925 AUROC; the matched Devanagari entities show a +30.6-point gap [26.6, 34.5]. Even among the 154 pairs with >80% Latin-form string similarity, Marathi accuracy is 10.8% (Dev–Dev) vs 32.1% (Lat–Lat), so near-duplicate spellings do not explain the effect.
- **Calibration recovers most of the deficit:** one constant added to Qwen's output margin raises Marathi entity-level balanced accuracy from 0.525 to 0.879 on 120 held-out Dev–Dev items — 87% of the distance to the E2 probe's 0.933 — while keeping FPR at 0.050. The probe keeps only a +0.054 [+0.017, +0.092] edge in Marathi and +0.050 in English.
- **Probes beat surface features only under matched negatives:** on the original negative map the five-feature string baseline ties the probe (0.933 vs 0.933); after rebuilding distractors to match each positive's name similarity (possible for 144/601 items), the probe wins 0.908 vs 0.796 in Marathi (+0.112 [0.067, 0.163]) and 0.879 vs 0.796 in English.
- **Causal site:** E2 patching raises margins by 2.66 [1.67, 3.71] (Marathi), 3.16 (Hindi), 2.85 (English), vs ≤0.27 for E1; joint E1/E2/question-mark patching over layers 12–17 flips 50%/60%/71% of gated Marathi/Hindi/English items with matched-negative accuracy 100%. Attention patches transfer 5.41 vs 0.51 for MLP in Marathi. An English Lat–Lat donor changes the Marathi margin by 6.26, close to the Marathi Lat–Lat donor's 5.97, while an English Dev–Dev donor gives only 0.11 — name form, not prompt language, carries the correction.
- **The intervention:** the frozen layer-12–17 shared vector raises held-out Marathi same-person accuracy from 6.5% to 33.5% (TPR 0.335 at FPR 0.015) and English from 28.3% to 59.0%, with negative accuracy 98.5%/97.6%; it also improves Lat–Lat items (Marathi 34.0% → 62.9%). Entity-averaged AUROC changes are +0.003 [−0.008, +0.015] in Marathi — i.e., a criterion shift, not a discrimination gain.
- **Tokenization is not the culprit:** five automatic romanization schemes cut Qwen's mean Devanagari name length from 16.5 tokens to 7.7–10.4 but yield no reliable behavioral recovery, and only reproduce the exact attested Latin spelling for 0.8–4.5% of names; substituting the attested Latin form reproduces Lat–Lat within 0.004 across all nine model–language cells. Mistral-Small-24B shows a 25.8-point Marathi positive-accuracy gap vs 6.9 for the Indic-adapted Sarvam-M-24B.

## Authors & Credibility

The five authors (Athira Gopal and Patil Prathamesh Pravin with equal contribution) are from QpiAI, an India-based research company — a fitting affiliation for Indic-language evaluation, and native-speaker auditing is described for all Marathi, Hindi, and Malayalam templates and strings (a Malayalam speaker rejected 6 of 380 candidate items). Methodological credibility is high: the paper uses fixed train/val/test splits with validation-selected settings, item-clustered bootstrap CIs with 2,000 resamples, counterbalanced answer keys, causal gates requiring failure/success across paraphrases, and frozen controls reused on test. Code and corpora are released at github.com/Multilingual-entity/Entity-Identification. The work cites and positions itself carefully against recent related work (Wendler et al. 2024 "Do Llamas work in English?", Wang et al. 2025 cross-lingual factual inconsistency, RomanSetu/RomanLens romanization line).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Good, not groundbreaking. The cross-lingual factual-consistency space is crowded (Qi et al. 2023, Ai et al. 2025, Wang et al. 2025), but the controlled same-relation, two-supplied-names design — isolating script from translation, question language, and name generation — is cleaner than most predecessors, and connecting behavioral bias → probe decodability → calibration → causal site → intervention in one paper is a genuine contribution.
- **Rigor:** Generally strong (clustered CIs, gates, frozen controls), but two load-bearing results rest on thin ice. First, the "probe beats string features" claim flips on the similarity-matched negative map, yet tight matching is possible for only 144/601 items and the test subset has 25 matched items — the +0.112 difference rides on a small slice of data. Second, the causal gate yields only 12/15/24 held-out items per language, so flip rates are explicitly descriptive; the headline intervention numbers, however, are computed on the full 120-item test set and are more robust.
- **Reproducibility:** High — released code and corpora, exact splits and selection rules in appendices, and an unusual level of self-scrutiny (the limitations section itself notes that distractors cross split boundaries and that the site/condition controls share test items and are not independent replications).
- **Significance:** The honest AUROC framing cuts both ways: the 6.5% → 33.5% intervention gain comes with AUROC intervals including zero (+0.003 [−0.008, +0.015] Marathi), and a plain scalar offset reaches a nearby operating point (0.312 TPR at 0.012 FPR vs the vector's 0.335 at 0.015). In other words, most of the celebrated fivefold gain could be had by moving the decision threshold — the vector's added value is modest and template-dependent (0.425 vs 0.308 TPR on the tuning template). The paper admits this, but a casual reader of the abstract could over-credit the mechanistic component.
- **Red flags / caveats:** (a) The Malayalam corpus — the strongest dissociation (5.3% accuracy at 0.925 AUROC) — is behavioral only; no probe, patching, or intervention extends to it. (b) Hindi reuses the Marathi identities with different renderings, so it tests rendering transfer, not an independent entity sample. (c) The 24B comparison is confounded: Mistral-Small 3.0 vs Sarvam-M derived from 3.1 with different checkpoints, so it does not isolate Indic post-training, and Sarvam-M's causal gate contains one Marathi item. (d) Applying the frozen vector also lifts Lat–Lat accuracy (34.0% → 62.9%) and the script disparity narrows in only 3 of 4 corpus–language arms, widening by 1.9 points in Marathi — script parity, the motivating problem, is not achieved. (e) Probes establish decodability, not identity retrieval: learned orthographic regularities and familiarity may drive the signal, as the authors concede.

## Strengths

- Exceptionally controlled design: attested (not transliterator-generated) name forms in both scripts, hard negatives matched by occupation/country/relation, counterbalanced answer keys, and a causal gate that guards against format and preference shortcuts.
- The three-level evidence chain (behavior → decodable information → causal site) is exactly the right structure for a "diagnosis" paper, and the discrimination-vs-criterion distinction is made operational via AUROC, d′, and calibration rather than left as rhetoric.
- Cross-script and cross-model generality checks (Malayalam replication, five models, romanization controls, donor-language controls) pre-empt the obvious "it's just tokenization" and "it's just Marathi" objections.
- Unusual candor: negative controls for the intervention (offset-only, oracle threshold, AUROC intervals, both-script evaluation) are reported prominently rather than buried.

## Limitations

- Person names in only two scripts (Devanagari, Malayalam) and three prompt languages; other writing systems, entity types (organizations, locations), and factual relations are untested.
- All mechanistic results (probes, patching, steering) concern Qwen2.5-7B only; other models get behavioral and exploratory analyses.
- Small gated item counts (12–24 per language) make flip-rate statistics descriptive; the matched-negative probe comparison relies on a 25-item test subset.
- The steering sweep is validation-only and reported separately; shared test items across control analyses are not independent replications.
- The intervention improves accuracy at a causal site but does not close the script gap itself (AUROC unchanged; disparity widens in one arm), so it is a diagnostic lever rather than a fix for multilingual equity of identity judgments.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向高度契合：它把探针、激活修补与子空间干预系统地用于解释一个多语言可靠性失效（跨文字身份匹配），并给出了判别信息与决策准则分离的清晰案例。对任何研究"模型内部知道但输出错误"现象（探针-行为差距、校准、因果干预位点）的人都有直接的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the E2 middle-layer sensitivity and the shared sensitivity direction replicate in a non-Indic script pair (e.g., Cyrillic–Latin or Han–Latin), or is the site Qwen- and script-specific?
- Can a discrimination-targeting intervention (rather than a criterion shift) close the residual AUROC gap of 0.023–0.065, or is some of that loss an irreducible consequence of fragmented tokenization and reduced familiarity?
- Does the 87% calibration recovery hold for generation-based (not logit-margin) evaluation, where the model must produce rather than score a yes/no answer?
- Do retrieval-augmented or entity-linking pipelines that feed these models suffer the same script-dependent criterion shift, and can the margin-offset fix be deployed safely at a fixed FPR in production?
- How much of the probe's decodable signal is genuinely identity association versus orthographic regularity — could a causal probe (probe-direction patching transferred ~0 margin, unlike the sensitivity direction) be made to dissociate the two?
