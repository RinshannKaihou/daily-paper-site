02 Why This Matters · 为何重要

Aggressive 4-bit quantization destroys accuracy through a handful of fragile channels; a reusable Hessian-based sensitivity metric pinpoints them, making extreme low-precision LLM serving practical.

与「极端性能优化下的可靠性」方向直接相关：噪声加权 Hessian 敏感性指标为量化误差的定位与控制提供了可复用的分析工具。

03 Critical Edge · 批判视角

At batch size 1, decode runs at 0.75× FP16 speed — slower than the unquantized model; the headline 2.3× prefill / 3.5× memory story holds only for batched, long-prefill serving.

SOTA is "most models," not all: FlatQuant wins LLaMA-2-7B on C4 (7.49 vs 7.95). A mis-set 10% split is actively harmful — PPL 13.28 vs 11.59 with no learning at all.

04 Methodology · 方法

Sort channels by fragility, train only the top half

Post-training quantization for W4A4KV4 (4-bit weights, activations, KV cache; GPTQ for weights, RTN for activations) — two stages, frozen LLM throughout.

  1. Sensitivity analysis. On a 128-sample WikiText-2 calibration set, each channel gets s = G·σ² — expected Hessian diagonal × quantization-noise variance from a second-order Taylor expansion, instantiated per insertion point (attention-logits MSE for QK, value propagation for VO). Sorting yields the permutation P and split k.
  2. Block-diagonal transform T = P·blkdiag(M_hi, H_lo)·Pᵀ. Only M_hi — dense learnable rotations over high-sensitivity channels — trains end-to-end (200 iterations, RiemannAdam on the Stiefel manifold). The rest gets training-free Hadamards, Kronecker-built for non-power-of-two dims.
  3. Architecture-aware placement. Cross-layer Jaccard overlap: residual stream 0.521 vs <0.07 for QK/VO/down paths → one shared global rotation on the residual stream, per-layer local rotations elsewhere. RoPE stays ahead of all transforms; R_G and R2 absorb offline, leaving just two online rotations (R1, R3).

Beyond the headline

  • Consistent first place on Mistral-7B (5.70), Qwen3-8B (7.32), and MoE Mixtral-8×7B (4.14 vs FlatQuant 4.27); LLaMA-2-70B lands within 0.18 PPL of FP16 (3.50 vs 3.32) at 76.85% average accuracy.
  • 25% of learnable parameters → −75% training VRAM (81.25 → 20.31 GB) and 2.44× faster training vs an identical-recipe dense rotation.
  • Fused Triton kernel + CUDA Graph (A100, batch 4): prefill 2.18× at seq 128 → 2.32× at 8192; peak-memory saving 3.55×→3.30×; decode scales 0.75× (batch 1) → 1.82× (batch 64).
  • The metric itself wins ablations at the 50% split: 6.78 vs maxabs 6.90, L2 7.25, AWQ-style L1 7.88, kurtosis 10.28, random 10.38 — best at every ratio in the practical 20–70% range.
  • Concentration is real: QK-path Lorenz Gini 0.63 — the top 20% of channels carry ≈70% of total sensitivity; rotating only the top-10% gives PPL 8.35, the reverse split 11.76 (RTN floor 11.85).
  • Ratio sweep: PPL bottoms at the 50% split (6.78), plateaus to r=90%, degrades to 8.01 at fully-learnable r=100% (overfitting).
06 Strengths · 优势
  • The premise is quantitative, not rhetorical: top-10% Hadamard-only ablation 8.35 vs 11.76 for the reverse split; QK Lorenz Gini 0.63.
  • Parameter dividend is multiplicative: 25% of learnable parameters → training VRAM 81.25 → 20.31 GB (−75%) and 2.44× faster calibration.
  • Clean, transferable design rule: cross-layer overlap 0.521 (residual) vs <0.07 (local paths) justifies the global + per-layer hybrid.
  • Exceptionally transparent efficiency reporting — per-baseline bottleneck dissection, host vs GPU memory separated, unflattering batch-1 decode disclosed (Table 10).
  • Broad coverage including MoE (Mixtral-8×7B) and a recent model (Qwen3-8B), with consistent first-place finishes.
07 Limitations · 局限
  • Static partition: computed once from 128 calibration samples; one uniform 50% ratio for every layer and insertion point.
  • INT4-only study — W3A3, FP4/FP8, and larger MoE models are left to future work.
  • Decode speedup negligible or negative below batch ~16 (0.75× at batch 1) — memory-latency-bound single-stream serving does not benefit.
  • Smallest model loses: LLaMA-2-7B C4 7.95 vs FlatQuant's 7.49; the split helps less when the base model is noisier.
  • No code release; evaluation limited to PPL + six commonsense tasks — generation quality, long-context behavior, and calibration-set sensitivity untested.
08 Questions for Further Exploration · 待探索
  1. Can a per-layer or input-adaptive sensitivity ratio (vs the uniform 50%) close the remaining gap to FP16 (6.81 vs 6.24) — without a second calibration pass?
  2. Does the 50/50 split survive W3A3 or FP4, where the noise term σ² grows and the sensitive-channel set may shift?
  3. Can the Jaccard finding (residual 0.521 vs <0.07 local) be exploited to share or compress local rotations and cut the 25-minute calibration further?
  1. Does the static channel partition hold under distribution shift — code, multilingual, long-context inputs — and what would online re-partitioning cost?
  2. Can the two remaining online rotations (R1, R3) be fused into adjacent GEMM epilogues to recover batch-1 decode (0.75× of FP16)?
  3. Do σ²-weighted sensitivity patterns correlate with silent numerical-failure modes — an early-warning signal for numerically fragile channels?
