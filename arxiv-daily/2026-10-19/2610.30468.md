> **一句话总结：** SplatLab 是首个在 3D Gaussian Splatting 这一活跃计算机视觉子领域完成端到端演示的自主科研系统：36 个智能体在经过验证的复现基座（Splatify 插件）上做变异式扩展，用基于真实 GPU PSNR 的级联加权 Elo 锦标赛（含常驻证伪者与"声明-日志绑定"审计）选出方法并撰写论文，生成的稿件在 Mip-NeRF 360 / LLFF 上以 +0.18 到 +3.78 dB PSNR 超过其复现目标，其中星云渲染 +3.78 dB 为此前无 3DGS 方法涉足的领域，三篇稿件还被 CVPR 2026 workshop 接收为 poster。

> **TL;DR:** SplatLab (UCSD, NeurIPS 2026) is the first autonomous-research system demonstrated end-to-end in a live computer-vision subfield (3D Gaussian Splatting). Every candidate mutates a verified-reproduction Splatify plugin; a cascade-weighted, (benchmark, loss)-conditioned Elo tournament across 8 islands — scored on measured PSNR with an in-loop falsifier that deducts 30 Elo per failed attack — selects methods, and an audit stage binds every reported number to a logged training run. Across 16 runs (198 experiment branches), generated manuscripts beat their reproduced targets by +0.18 to +3.78 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 and LLFF — e.g., Nebula3DGS-v2 reaches 36.36 dB vs 32.58 dB for vanilla 3DGS on the nebula scene (+3.78 dB), and DWTGS-Patch 21.09 dB vs 18.68 dB for DWTGS (+2.41 dB) — at ~113M tokens, 2–36 GPU-hours, and $150–250 per run. Honest caveats: against tuned published SOTA (not vanilla 3DGS) most gains are +0.18 to +0.84 dB, some within the stated ~0.1 dB noise floor; a human reviewer still rates the manuscripts borderline (3.5/6) vs 4.1/6 for published CVPR/NeurIPS 3DGS papers.

## 核心贡献

- **首个在活跃 CV 子领域的端到端自主科研演示**：从一行 prompt、目标论文 URL 或零输入出发，自动完成读文献、复现、提出扩展、训练、消融、写稿的完整闭环，产出 16 篇稿件；此前系统（AI Scientist、Agent Laboratory 等）从未在"复现单篇论文就需数周专家工程"的子领域中端到端验证。
- **验证复现基座（verified-reproduction backbone）**：所有候选都在已通过目标论文 headline 验证的 Splatify/NeRFify 插件上做 EVOLVE-BLOCK 变异，使增益可归因于提出的机制而非复现漂移——直击 PaperBench 揭示的验证鸿沟（最强 agent 在 20 篇 ICML 2024 论文上平均复现率仅 21.0%）。
- **以真实 GPU PSNR 为评分的级联加权 Elo**：候选按 smoke（100 迭代）→ probe（单场景全量）→ 多场景 → 全基准四档递进评估，权重 {0.2, 0.5, 1.0}，Elo 以 (B, L)（基准-损失对）为条件更新，避免廉价 smoke 胜利淹没多场景结果；ReEvo 式言语梯度回喂 ideator。
- **与选择耦合的常驻证伪者**：沿边缘场景、稀疏视角、超参扰动三轴攻击幸存者，每次失守扣 30 Elo，使压力测试存活成为选择信号而非事后检查。
- **声明-日志绑定审计（Stage 5）**：六项审计（溯源哈希链、忠实性、新颖性复查、算力对齐、跨代码库移植、声明核对）保证稿件中每个数字都可追溯到磁盘上的训练日志，消融显示无此门控时 16/16 稿件含无日志支撑的数字，有则 0/16。

## Key Contributions

- First end-to-end autonomous-research pipeline in a live CV subfield: from a one-line prompt, a target-paper URL, or no input, it reproduces a published 3DGS baseline, proposes an extension, trains and ablates on Mip-NeRF 360 and LLFF, and writes the manuscript — 16 runs with zero manual code edits after dispatch.
- Verified-reproduction backbone: every candidate mutates a Splatify plugin already verified to compile, train, and match its target's published headline, giving improvement claims a fixed external reference — unlike prior systems built on unverified LLM-written reimplementations (frontier models succeed on only ~5% of a 30-paper 3DGS reproduction benchmark).
- Cascade-weighted, grounding-conditional Elo: four evaluation tiers (smoke 100-iter → probe → multi-scene → full benchmark) with stage weights {0.2, 0.5, 1.0} and per-(benchmark, loss) ratings computed on real PSNR, preventing cheap smoke wins from dominating; ReEvo-style verbal gradients feed rationales back to the ideator.
- In-loop falsifier coupled to selection: a standing adversarial agent attacks survivors along edge scenes, sparse-view reduction, and hyperparameter perturbation, deducting 30 Elo per failed attack and holding veto power before writing.
- Claim-backed verification: the run cannot terminate while any reported number lacks a backing measurement; a six-audit integrity stage (provenance hash chain, faithfulness, novelty re-resolution, compute-match, cross-codebase port, claim check) aborts the run on load-bearing mismatches — closing the hallucination mode left open by AI Scientist drafts.

## Methodology

SplatLab is framed as W(x; D) returning (LaTeX source, PDF, claim-to-log ledger L, codebase C), with fixed context D = (3DGS literature corpus R, Splatify backbone B, nine EVOLVE-BLOCK hooks H, conference template G). Six stages run under a 3-level hierarchy of 36 agents (Professor, PhD coordinators, undergrad leaf agents) with no fixed DAG — the master may revisit any stage on falsifier veto, audit failure, or reviewer-spawned ablation.

Stage 1 (Grounding) produces the thesis spine Γ: a Researcher queries arXiv/Semantic Scholar/Google Scholar plus a persistent cross-run paper store via RAG (retrievals written back so the corpus grows monotonically); MinerU parses PDFs and a Limitation Brainstormer emits deduplicated limitation IDs (e.g., L-127: opacity reset destabilises late refinement), while a Bottleneck Analyst trains the verified plugin and profiles per-tile loss, PSNR trajectories, memory, and gradient norms into measurement-anchored B-IDs (e.g., B-04: PSNR plateau at 25.65 dB on garden). Stage 2 (Grounded ideation) generates candidates (mechanism, category c ∈ {1..7, open}, L-ID, B-ID, predicted delta) over five iterations with a Web Scout refreshing searches between rounds; each candidate passes Precedent, Novelty ("Tweak Trap" screen), Saturation, and Orthogonality checks, then a bracket tournament with initial Elo 1200 judged by LLM-as-judge on novelty, B-ID specificity, implementability, and delta plausibility. Stage 3 (Implementation) maps each category to a subset of nine EVOLVE-BLOCK hooks (densification, regularisation, loss, rendering, pruning, initialisation, scheduling, opacity, harmonics) via a Hook Mapper, and an Implementer constrained by a citation graph, a Graph-of-Thoughts controller, and a context-free grammar of Splatify emits structured diffs; a 100-iteration smoke test gates entry. Stage 4 (Refinement) runs the cascade-weighted Elo tournament (K=32) across eight islands with five mutation operators (Refine, Crossover, Single-Parent, Tweak, Rewrite) selected by a multi-armed bandit; promotion requires Pareto improvement over (B, L), and the standing falsifier subtracts δ=30 Elo per failed attack. Stage 5 (Audit) runs the six verification audits, including recomputing the headline metric under the baseline's compute envelope (compute-confounded gains get reclassified) and porting the winning hook to a second 3DGS codebase. Stage 6 (Synthesis) routes Mode A (research log) vs Mode B (manuscript): five parallel writers, a VLM figure checker, an ablation designer emitting leave-one-out ablations from the diff, and three reviewer agents (Methodology, Domain, Devil's Advocate) under an Editor who can demand new training runs — the loop terminates only when no reviewer raises a New-Experiment concern.

## Results & Highlights

- On the astrophotographic nebula scene (a domain no prior 3DGS method addresses), the discovered Nebula3DGS-v2 reaches 36.36 dB PSNR / 0.945 SSIM / 0.201 LPIPS vs 32.58 / 0.914 / 0.278 for vanilla 3DGS — a +3.78 dB gain, the headline result; on the bubble scene, BubbleGS reaches 30.11 dB vs 27.40 dB for 3DGS (+2.71 dB).
- Against tuned published SOTA on standard benchmarks: DWTGS-Patch 21.09 dB vs 18.68 dB for DWTGS (+2.41 dB); MSAA-Bilat 26.19 dB vs 25.35 dB for MSAA-3DGS on Mip-NeRF 360 (+0.84 dB); TileFocalGS 29.69 dB vs 29.12 dB for FastGS on LLFF (+0.57 dB); and View-Curriculum-GS 23.36 dB vs 20.53 dB for splatfacto at the 50-second wall-clock setting (+2.83 dB).
- Multi-seed robustness: rerunning GNS-AA with five seeds gives 26.18 ± 0.05 dB vs 25.95 ± 0.07 dB for GNS (+0.23 ± 0.09 dB, vs the originally reported +0.18 dB); gains within the ~0.1 dB single-seed noise floor are reported as marginal rather than verified.
- Component ablations (Table 6): without the Stage 5 claim-backed audit, 16/16 manuscripts contained unbacked numbers (0/16 with it); without the verified-plugin contract, 9/16 winners were non-reproducible by >1.0 dB; without the falsifier, 5/16 winners failed held-out attacks (0/16 with); without cascade weights, the smoke-test winner ranked only 5th on multi-scene; Stage 2 Elo pre-selection cut GPU evals to winner from 24 to 6.
- Output quality: in blind expert scoring (10 experts, 1–10 Likert), SplatLab Stage-2 ideas score 5.9 novelty / 5.8 overall vs 5.2 / 5.1 for 3DGS PhD students and 5.8 / 6.0 for accepted arXiv preprints; under Gemini-3.1-Pro LLM-as-judge, SplatLab manuscripts score 95/100 overall vs 94 for published human 3DGS papers, 81 for PaperOrchestra, 28 for AI Scientist-v2, and 22 for GPT-5 ET (human re-scoring agrees at Spearman ρ = 0.87).
- External validation: three generated manuscripts (AttDis & Friends, NeedleSwap, AuditSplat) were accepted as posters at a CVPR 2026 workshop; the taxonomy ablation on the nebula target shows domain grounding improves efficiency (+3.78 dB with taxonomy vs +3.45 dB without, while tokens rise 80% from 47.5M to 85.5M without it).
- Cost: 24–48-hour wall-clock per run, ~113M API tokens across 1,000–1,500 LLM calls, 2–36 GPU-hours, and $150–250 estimated LLM cost — compressing a cycle the authors say takes a graduate student months.

## Authors & Credibility

The work builds on the group's own verified reproduction tools (NeRFify, Splatify) and cites the negative evidence motivating it (PaperBench's 21.0% average replication; ~5% success of frontier models on 3DGS reproduction). ## Critical Assessment

- Novelty: The individual ingredients (island-based LLM program evolution from FunSearch/AlphaEvolve, verbal gradients from ReEvo, Elo tournaments from AI co-scientist, falsification from AIGS) are recombinations — the genuine novelty is the integration on a verified substrate in a live CV subfield, which no prior system (AI Scientist-v2, Agent Laboratory, Curie, PaperOrchestra) demonstrates.
- Rigor: Above average for this genre — multi-seed checks, marginal-result labeling against a stated ~0.1 dB noise floor, and a taxonomy ablation including a taxonomy-free control. But the headline "+0.18 to +3.78 dB" conflates two regimes: the +3.78 dB nebula gain is against untuned vanilla 3DGS on a new domain (with SfM giving ≈0 stars — a strawman-ish baseline), whereas gains against tuned published SOTA cluster at +0.18 to +0.84 dB, some within noise. The DWTGS comparison (+2.41 dB) targets a baseline the authors themselves diagnose as under-constrained.
- The capability Table 2 is self-graded (∼/✓ verdicts "verified against each cited paper" but still judgment calls), and the manuscript-quality headline (95 vs human 94) comes from a Gemini judge with acknowledged LLM-judge bias toward AI content — the authors' own human reviewer rates SplatLab borderline (3.5/6, Soundness 3.2 vs 3.6, Presentation 3.0 vs 3.8) versus clear accept (4.1/6) for published papers, a gap the abstract does not mention.
- Idea-quality study has small N (20 SplatLab ideas vs 10 PhD-student and 10 preprint ideas, 10 experts), and SplatLab feasibility (6.2) is below PhD students (6.4) — honest but easy to miss.
- Reproducibility: strong in principle (claim-to-log binding, ledgers, open release), though the system's foundation — the hand-built NeRFify/Splatify backbones — represents a large uncounted human engineering investment, so "days of compute" understates true cost; the authors acknowledge this biases capability toward pre-engineered subfields and well-resourced labs.
- Red flags: 5 of 16 runs yielded only marginal results and 1 outright failure (honestly reported); reviewer-spawned re-experimentation creates unbounded compute loops terminated only by a no-concern criterion.

## Strengths

- Solves the right problem: without a verified baseline, autonomous "improvements" have no fixed reference — anchoring every mutation to a plugin matching the target's published headline is the key architectural insight.
- Claim-backed verification is a real contribution to AI-scientist reliability: the ablation showing 16/16 unbacked numbers without the gate vs 0/16 with it is the paper's most important table.
- Grounding every ranking decision in measured GPU PSNR rather than LLM-judged proposal scores, and conditioning Elo on (benchmark, loss) pairs, is a principled fix to free-text Elo tournaments.
- Unusually honest negative-space reporting: marginal results labeled as such, a recorded failure, noise-floor thresholds, and a human reviewer who disagrees with the LLM judge are all in the paper, not hidden.
- Real external artifacts: workshop-accepted papers with genuinely interesting content (e.g., the attention-entropy sign-flip in AttDis) rather than template filler.

## Limitations

- Domain-locked: the taxonomy, hooks, and backbone are 3DGS-specific; the taxonomy ablation covers only a single target, and porting to a new subfield requires rebuilding the verified-reproduction backbone.
- The strongest PSNR gains come from new OOD scenes (nebula, bubble, diamond) where the baseline is vanilla 3DGS; on mature benchmarks the improvements over tuned SOTA are frequently sub-dB and sometimes within noise.
- Writing quality lags humans (Presentation 3.0 vs 3.8, human judge), and the overall human rating is borderline accept.
- Compute accounting omits the backbone construction cost, and 113M tokens/run at 24–48 hours makes broad design-space sweeps expensive for small labs despite the $150–250 API figure.
- Evaluation of the system itself (Tables 3–5) relies on small samples and LLM judges with known pro-AI bias; long-run autonomy beyond 48 hours is untested.

## Relevance

直接命中"Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research"方向：这是首个在活跃 CV 子领域端到端验证的 agentic scientific discovery 系统，其"声明-日志绑定 + 常驻证伪者"机制同时回应了长程自主智能体可靠性与可信性（trustworthiness of long-running autonomous agents）的核心关切，是自动化科研闭环落地的重要参照。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of SplatLab's advantage survives if AI Scientist-v2 or PaperOrchestra baselines are also given the verified Splatify backbone — is the gain from the system or the substrate?
- Can the claim-to-log binding and standing falsifier be generalized as a lightweight layer over other autonomous-research pipelines (e.g., ML or RL codebases) without rebuilding a domain backbone per field?
- Do the (B, L)-conditioned Elo ratings and Pareto promotion scale to more than eight islands, or does the tournament saturate as the design space widens?
- The three workshop papers target uncertainty, primitive specialization, and audit tooling — will follow-on runs discover mechanisms outside the seven-category taxonomy, and does the `open` category close the gap?
- What happens to manuscript quality and reviewer-spawned re-experimentation loops under a 10× compute budget — do the Soundness/Presentation gaps to human papers close, and at what token cost?
