> **一句话总结：** HyScale 抓住"稀疏注意力的 selection 输出只是索引而非激活值"这一洞察，用 HyTopK（乐观阈值、免 score-table、确定性的在打分中即时 top-k）与 selection replay（重计算时只重放/重打分 k 个被选项）两个技术，把 512 张 H20 上 GLM-5.1 百万 token 训练相对 Megatron-LM 提速 2.07×，并同样加速 DeepSeek-V4 的 CSA（2.01×）与 Qwen3.8 的 QSA（1.57×）层。

> **TL;DR:** A Tencent Hunyuan / Edinburgh system paper on training learned sparse attention (DSA/CSA/QSA) at million-token context. Since top-k selection emits discrete indices (non-differentiable, no gradient through it), the score table need not be materialized and recomputation need not re-select: HyTopK performs exact, deterministic, CP-compatible top-k while scoring via optimistic strided thresholds with cheap host-free recovery, and selection replay rescores only the k saved indices. End-to-end GLM-5.1 training on 1M-token sequences runs 2.07× faster than Megatron-LM on 512 H20 GPUs (1.23× at 256K, growing with context) at similar peak memory, with MFU rising 20.9%→27.8% while Megatron-LM's falls 17.1%→13.5%; at the layer level the speedup reaches 22.3× at 8M context. Deployed in production.

## 核心贡献

- **指出并量化了新瓶颈：** 在学习型稀疏注意力（DeepSeek 式 DSA、块级 CSA/QSA/MSA）的长上下文训练中，selection（打分 + top-k）取代注意力本身成为瓶颈——在 Megatron-LM 中 GLM-5.1 于 1M token 时 selection 占训练步 47%，是稀疏注意力的 6.6×；其中两大浪费是物化 n×L score table（单层 32 GiB/GPU，而选出的索引仅 64 MiB，即 0.2%）和重计算时把 selection 整个重跑一遍。
- **关键洞察：** selection 的输出不是连续激活值，而是一组离散索引；top-k 不可微，反向传播既不需要中间 score table 也不需要重跑 top-k（附录 A 给出 indexer KL 损失的梯度只流过 k 个被选分数的形式化推导）。
- **HyTopK：** 首个面向训练的即时（on-the-fly）精确 top-k 算子——乐观跨步采样（从每个 query 自己的上下文均匀采 M=min(max(4096,L/128),8192) 个 key，取 ⌈3kM/L⌉-th 最优样本为阈值，约放行 3k 个候选）、免主机同步的失败恢复（固定 grid 启动、0 失败时立即返回，最多 64 个失败 query 全上下文重打分）、以及位级确定性（对并列分数做 radix select、对 k 个结果做按位置破 tie 的 radix sort）。
- **Selection replay：** 细粒度（按条目而非按算子）的重计算策略——前向保存每 query 的 k 个索引，重计算只 gather-and-score 这 k 个条目（index replay，重算开销 ≤1.6% 训练步、比 score replay 省 ~5 GiB/GPU），索引还可近乎零开销地卸载到 pinned host memory。
- **整体系统优化：** 解耦 CP 重叠（selection 只读 indexer key、稀疏注意力只读 KV，各自与不消费它的计算重叠）、局部性感知稀疏核（相邻两 query 平均有 71% 的 top-2048 重叠，联合仅 1.29× 单份，一个 thread block 处理两 query、共享 key 只加载一次、梯度只做一次 atomic）、CP layout 缓存、kernel fusion、免重排 KV gather 等；基于 Megatron-LM，约 3.4K 行 Python + 14.7K 行 CUDA/Triton/CuTe DSL。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies and quantifies the new bottleneck:** in learned sparse attention training (token-level DSA as in GLM-5.1; block-level CSA in DeepSeek-V4, QSA in Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, MSA in MiniMax-M3), selection — not sparse attention — dominates: it consumes 47% of a GLM-5.1 training step at 1M tokens in Megatron-LM, 6.6× the sparse attention time, via a materialized n×L score table (32 GiB/GPU/layer vs 64 MiB for the kept indices, i.e. 0.2%) and a full re-run of selection during activation recomputation.
- **Key insight:** the output of selection is a discrete set of indices, not a continuous activation. Top-k is non-differentiable, so the backward pass needs neither the score table nor a re-run of selection; Appendix A derives that the indexer's KL loss (Eq. 3) back-propagates only through the k selected scores.
- **HyTopK (on-the-fly exact top-k for training):** optimistic strided sampling from each query's own context (M = min(max(4096, L/128), 8192) keys, threshold at the ⌈3kM/L⌉-th best sample ≈ 3k-th best key, 3-fold margin), host-free GPU recovery for failed queries (fixed-grid launches that no-op when the failure count is zero; up to 64 failed queries rescored exactly), and bitwise determinism via radix select on ties plus a radix sort of the k results. This fixes the three training-specific failure modes of inference-time sampling top-k (LiteTopK/DoSA): CP incompatibility, expensive buffer overflow, and non-determinism.
- **Selection replay:** a per-entry recomputation policy — keep the forward pass's k indices (index replay: rescoring cost 2nkH_I d_I, ≤1.6% of a step, ~5 GiB/GPU cheaper than score replay), or also keep the scores (score replay: zero rerun). Compared to operator-level policies (Table 1), replay sits orders of magnitude lower in the memory–compute plane; indices can be offloaded to pinned host memory at near-zero cost.
- **HyScale system:** decoupled CP overlap (selection reads only indexer keys, attention only KV states; each transfer overlaps computation that does not consume it) plus locality-aware sparse kernels (two adjacent queries share 71% of their top-2048 keys on average, so their union is only 1.29× one selection; two queries per thread block load shared keys once and fuse gradient atomics), CP-layout/sequence-boundary caches, kernel fusion, and permutation-free KV gather. Built on Megatron-LM (~3.4K lines Python, 14.7K lines CUDA/Triton/CuTe DSL); supports DSA, CSA, and QSA.

## Methodology

The paper first profiles the problem (Figures 1–2): GLM-5.1 on 512 H20 GPUs under full activation recomputation shows selection at 49% of a step at 256K growing to 61% at 1M. It then redesigns selection around the indices-not-activations insight. **HyTopK** merges scoring and top-k into one kernel: a strided sample of each query's context sets an optimistic threshold (~3k candidates pass, sized to a default 16K-slot buffer); every score is compared on chip and non-passing scores never reach GPU memory, so the score table disappears. Failures (overflow or under-k) are detected by a check kernel and repaired by three recovery kernels that rescore only the failed queries over their full context, launched unconditionally with a fixed grid so the zero-failure path costs a few empty launches. Determinism is restored post-hoc by ordering only the minimal sets whose order affects the output (ties at the k-th score via radix select on positions; the k selected entries via radix sort). **Selection replay** exploits the same insight during recomputation: the forward pass saves T_t (optionally its scores), and the recomputation runs a gather-and-score kernel over only those k entries — reducing recomputed selection from O(L) to O(k) per query — while the backward pass runs unchanged. A cost table (Table 1) proves every operator-level selective-recomputation policy (keep scores / keep top-k indices / keep indexer inputs) wastes ≥2 orders of magnitude more memory or FLOPs. **System level:** decoupled CP overlap splits the indexer-key and KV-state collectives and overlaps each with non-consuming computation (e.g., KV gather overlaps projections + selection); locality-aware sparse kernels batch adjacent query pairs over the union of their selections; caches remove Megatron-LM's per-layer host synchronizations. Evaluation trains the full GLM-5.1 from released weights on 512 H20s (4M tokens/step, sequences of 256K/512K/1M), benchmarks single-layer CP scaling to 512 GPUs (strong and weak, up to 8M context), ablates each component on DSA/CSA/QSA layers, replays 81.8M real selections from one training step for kernel-level timing, and compares sampling quality against LiteTopK and DoSA under both trained and random indexer weights.

## Results & Highlights

- **End-to-end (GLM-5.1, 512 H20 GPUs, full model from released weights):** HyScale speeds up 1M-token training steps by **2.07× over Megatron-LM** (step time roughly 264 s → 125 s at 1M, Fig. 7), with the speedup growing with context: 1.23× at 256K, 1.44× at 512K, 2.07× at 1M — at about the same peak GPU memory (≤11% more without index offloading).
- **Efficiency trend:** from 256K to 1M, HyScale's step time grows only 1.27× and its MFU rises from 20.9% to 27.8%, while Megatron-LM's step time grows 2.13× and its MFU falls from 17.1% to 13.5%.
- **Component effects at 1M:** HyTopK cuts the forward selection from 35.9 s to 20.8 s and selection replay cuts the recomputed selection from 37.0 s to 1.0 s; the faster layers also halve the pipeline wait from 106.6 s to 51.7 s.
- **Layer-level ablation (1M, 128 H20s):** HyScale removes 66% of Megatron-LM's layer time — HyTopK contributes 37 points, selection replay 18, implementation optimizations 11; at 256K HyTopK's share is only 8%, confirming the bottleneck grows with context.
- **CP scaling (single GLM-5.1 layer):** speedup over Megatron-LM grows from 1.44× to 11.7× at 256K and 1.79× to 8.1× at 512K as GPUs scale 8→512 (Megatron-LM stalls at 128 GPUs and slows beyond 256); weak scaling at 16K queries/GPU from 128K to 8M context, HyScale's layer time grows 11× vs Megatron-LM's 190×, with the speedup climbing from 1.24× (128K) to 22.3× (8M).
- **Generality (1M tokens, 32 H800 GPUs):** reimplemented CSA layers of DeepSeek-V4-Flash run 2.01× and QSA layers of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 1.57× faster than Megatron-LM; gains are smaller than token-level DSA because block-level selection scores 4× fewer keys.
- **HyTopK kernel efficiency (replay of all 81.8M selections of one 1M-token step, all 78 layers):** HyTopK peaks at 1.5–1.9 GiB and is fastest at every context; Megatron-LM's cuDNN selection with its default 1 GiB score-table limit runs 1.22–1.71× slower, and even with the full 12–48 GiB table (8–26× more memory) remains 3–10% slower.
- **Sampling quality vs baselines:** at the default 16K-slot buffer and trained weights, HyTopK fails on >5,000× fewer queries than LiteTopK and DoSA with fixed thresholds; with a random indexer (where nearby-query sampling degenerates), HyTopK fits every query in 5.9K slots vs 10.5K for LiteTopK (1.8×) and 12.3K for DoSA (2.1×).
- **Overhead of exactness:** the deterministic sort adds only 0.8% to base selection time; recovery adds 1.8% with 1 failed query and 4.3% even at 64 failed queries — no run in the paper exceeded 4 failures.

## Authors & Credibility

Joint work between the University of Edinburgh (first author Yangshen Deng, senior author Luo Mai) and Tencent Hunyuan (nine co-authors, including the team behind the Hy4-preview production model that already uses token-level learned sparse attention). The paper claims HyScale is deployed in production powering million-token LLM training, which is plausible given the lab affiliation. The artifact is substantial: ~3.4K lines of Python and ~14.7K lines of GPU kernels (CUDA, Triton, CuTe DSL) layered on Megatron Core 0.19.2, evaluated on two real clusters (H20 96GB and H800 80GB nodes, 400 Gb/s RoCE). The baseline is the genuine state-of-the-art (Megatron-LM with the cuDNN DSA backend on the identical code base, differing only in the attention layers), and the evaluation is unusually multi-pronged: end-to-end training, layer-level CP scaling, component ablations, kernel replay of 81.8M real selections, and a controlled sampling-efficiency study. v2 is dated 26 Oct 2026.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core reframing — "selection outputs indices, not activations, so skip the score table and skip re-selection" — is simple and elegant, and the per-entry recomputation policy is genuinely new relative to operator-granularity selective recomputation (PyTorch checkpoint, Korthikanti et al.). HyTopK is best read as a careful training-hardening of LiteTopK's inference idea (optimistic thresholds instead of guaranteed lower bounds), which the paper honestly attributes. The determinism construction (radix-select ties + radix-sort the k winners) is a nice, load-bearing detail rarely seen in fused top-k work.
- **Rigor:** Strong on systems measurement (config-matched Megatron-LM baseline, per-component ablations, replayed real selections instead of synthetic microbenchmarks) and the math equivalence in Appendix A is sound: since HyTopK returns the exact top-k and replay reuses the identical indices, forward/backward semantics are unchanged. However, the paper never shows a training-loss curve comparing HyScale vs Megatron-LM over a long run — the timing experiments average steps 4–10 — so the claim of numerical equivalence rests on the derivation plus determinism rather than empirical convergence evidence. Also, the CSA and QSA layers are the authors' own reimplementations "from their papers," not the official implementations, so the 2.01×/1.57× numbers carry reimplementation-fidelity risk (block sizes of 4 tokens, score functions, and MLA latents must match exactly for a fair layer-time comparison).
- **Reproducibility:** No code release is mentioned, and the system depends on unpublished-adjacent models (GLM-5.1, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next) and specific hardware (H20/H800 + RoCE), so independent reproduction is currently impractical; algorithmic descriptions are detailed enough to reimplement in principle.
- **Red flags / caveats:** (1) The 3-fold threshold margin and the 64-failed-query recovery cap are heuristics validated on GLM-5.1-like score distributions; a pathological indexer or an early-training distribution shift could raise failure rates, and the >64-failure path falls back to rescoring every query — the paper says this "never occurred," but at production scale tail risk matters. (2) The 71% selection-locality claim is measured on only 21 of 78 layers at 128K/192K tokens — moderate coverage for a number the kernel design leans on. (3) The headline 2.07× is a step-time speedup on one model/GPU-count/hardware point; on block-level architectures it shrinks to 1.57–2.01×, and nothing is reported for HBM-denser GPUs (e.g., B200) where the 32 GiB score-table pressure and thus HyTopK's advantage may differ. (4) The single-training-step timing excludes nothing at end-to-end level but Figure 1's profiling excludes the pipeline wait, which flatters the "47% of a step" bottleneck framing slightly (though Fig. 7 reports it honestly).

## Strengths

- Diagnoses a real, quantified production bottleneck (selection at 47% of a 1M-token step, 6.6× sparse attention itself) rather than a synthetic one, and traces both wastes to a single root cause.
- The indices-not-activations insight is correct, formally justified (Appendix A gradient derivation), and yields two composable techniques rather than one point fix.
- Takes training-specific requirements seriously where prior sampling top-k work stopped at inference: CP compatibility, deterministic bitwise reproducibility (needed to replay loss spikes), and cheap host-free recovery — with measured overheads (0.8–4.3%).
- Evaluation breadth is excellent: end-to-end, strong/weak CP scaling to 512 GPUs and 8M context, ablations on three architectures (DSA/CSA/QSA), kernel replay on 81.8M real selections, and a fair head-to-head against LiteTopK/DoSA sampling under both trained and random weights.
- Speedups grow with context length (up to 22.3× per layer at 8M), exactly the scaling regime the system targets, with essentially no memory penalty.

## Limitations

- No end-to-end convergence/loss-equivalence experiments; equivalence is argued mathematically, and timing runs are short (steps 4–10, momentum-free SGD, balanced MoE routing — a somewhat artificial training configuration).
- Generality beyond the tested stack is unproven: one training framework (Megatron-LM), two NVIDIA GPU types, three (partially reimplemented) sparse-attention variants; MSA (MiniMax-M3) is described but not evaluated.
- The optimistic-threshold machinery has tunable constants (3× margin, 16K-slot buffer, 4096–8192 sample size, 64-query recovery cap) validated empirically rather than analyzed for worst-case distributions.
- Results concentrate on very long contexts (≥256K); at shorter contexts the contribution shrinks (HyTopK's ablation share drops to 8% at 256K, and the end-to-end speedup to 1.23×).
- No public code or artifact release stated, limiting independent verification.

## Relevance

这篇论文 squarely 命中"大规模机器学习的算力基础设施"方向：它做的是 GPU kernel（融合 top-k、局部性感知稀疏注意力核）、collective/CP 通信重叠以及训练系统层面的工程优化，并附带一个与可靠性相关的卖点（位级确定性使 loss spike 可复现）。对关注长上下文训练系统、分布式训练与 kernel 设计的兴趣点直接相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do HyTopK's failure rate and recovery overhead behave during the earliest phase of pretraining (random indexer) at 4M+ context, and is the 3× margin still sufficient, or does it need an adaptive schedule?
- What is the worst-case selection distribution (e.g., heavily skewed or adversarially shifted indexer scores) that pushes failures past the 64-query recovery cap, and what is the real cost of the full-rescore fallback in production?
- Does index replay interact correctly (and profitably) with partial/selective recomputation schedules other than full recomputation, e.g., when only some layers recompute?
- Can the locality-aware paired-query kernel extend to groups larger than 2 (the 71% overlap suggests diminishing but nonzero returns), and how does the trade-off shift on GPUs with larger L2 or HBM3e bandwidth?
- Would the decoupled CP overlap transfer to asynchronous/elastic CP variants or disaggregated training where indexer keys and KV states live on different memory tiers?
- Since selection replay stores per-layer indices, what is the cumulative host-memory and PCIe traffic footprint for models with hundreds of layers, and does offloading remain near-zero-overhead at 8M context?
