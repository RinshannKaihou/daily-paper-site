> **一句话总结：** 该论文系统评估了面向"当前问题"的上下文压缩对"后续未知问题"的影响：在 MultiRC 上，按当前问题选句（typed selection，gpt-6-luna 打分）能把当前问题准确率提升 11.4 点，但对后续问题平均与随机删除无异（−0.4 点）；而不管问题的词级压缩器 LLMLingua-2 在同样 25% token 预算下比随机删句高 12.5 点，因为它保留了约两倍的证据实词，但该排序在 Evidence Inference 和 τ-bench 轨迹上又会反转。

> **TL;DR:** Query-aware sentence selection that serves the current (known) question fails the later (hidden) ones: on MultiRC at a 25% budget, typed selection beats random deletion by +11.4 points on the known question but is at parity (−0.4 [−1.5, +0.6]) on hidden questions, while question-agnostic word-level LLMLingua-2 beats random sentence deletion by +12.5 points on hidden questions by keeping ~2x the evidence content words (48.0% vs 26.1%); however the ranking flips on Evidence Inference (typed +12.2 over LLMLingua-2) and on τ-bench trajectories (+22.3 over random for typed, no gain for LLMLingua-2), and on FEVER label accuracy overstates what the kept text actually supports — so evaluation must score hidden questions, not just the one compacted for.

## 核心贡献

- 提出并系统评估了"压缩时已知问题（known）vs. 之后才出现的隐藏问题（hidden）"这一不对称视角：上下文压缩通常面向当前问题，但被删除的证据对所有后续问题永久丢失。此前查询感知压缩器的评估几乎都只在被压缩的那一个问题上打分。
- 在四个带证据标注的语料（MultiRC、FEVER、Evidence Inference、LoCoMo）和录制的 τ-bench agent 轨迹上，把每种方法（typed selection、问题无关选择、TF-IDF、lead、LLMLingua-2、LongLLMLingua、MMR、LLM 摘要、混合策略）都和**同预算下的随机删除**做对比，并按"隐藏问题证据是否与已知问题重叠"做拆分分析。
- 定位了词级压缩胜过句级选择的原因：LLMLingua-2 优先删虚词，在相同 token 预算下保留 48.0% 的证据实词（随机删句仅 26.1%）；在等实词保留率下其优势从 12.5 点缩至 7.3 点（探索性分析），说明"保留多少实词"只能解释部分领先。
- 揭示 FEVER 上的标签准确率会高估保留文本的实际支撑力：typed selection 的 +25.2 点标签恢复优势在按保留证据评分时消失（−1.0 [−3.9, +2.2]）；LLMLingua-2 的 +21.0 点优势也无法证明建立在保留文本之上（即使证据实词存活不足 1/5，读者仍有 56.0% 的概率判定真主张被支持）。
- 给出排序不稳定的边界条件：在 Evidence Inference（typed 在名义 25% 预算下胜 LLMLingua-2 +12.2 点）和 τ-bench 轨迹（后续工具调用需要精确标识符，typed − random = +22.3 点，LLMLingua-2 无检出增益）上结论反转；词级抽取式压缩会剥掉 agent 需要的精确标识符。
- 10 项预注册分析、51 条预测全部预先声明并逐条报告结局（36 met / 4 partly / 7 not met / 4 unresolved），并为压缩的经济性给出 token 盈亏平衡分析（压缩一次需 6.6–14.0 个后续问题才在输入 token 上回本）。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the **known-question vs. hidden-question asymmetry** in context compaction: applications compact for the question at hand, but the compacted state must serve questions that arrive later, whose evidence has been irreversibly deleted. Prior evaluations of query-aware compressors scored only the question compacted for.
- A systematic comparison, on four evidence-annotated corpora (MultiRC, ERASer FEVER, Evidence Inference, LoCoMo) plus recorded τ-bench retail trajectories, of every method against **random deletion at the same budget** — including typed selection via the OpenAI Decisions API (gpt-6-luna scoring one Boolean per sentence), question-agnostic selection, TF-IDF, lead, LLMLingua-2, LongLLMLingua, MMR, LLM-written summaries, and a hybrid.
- **Locates where query-aware selection loses**: hidden questions whose evidence overlaps the known question's gain (+4.3 to +5.3 points), disjoint ones lose (−2.9 to −3.0) because less of their evidence survives (18% vs 25% under random); deleted evidence causally drives flips (restoring it recovers +26.8 points more than adding non-evidence sentences).
- **Explains the word-level advantage**: at the same token budget LLMLingua-2 keeps 48.0% of the evidence's content words vs 26.1% for random sentence deletion; at equal content-word retention its lead shrinks from +12.5 to +7.3 points (exploratory), and it still beats random word deletion at matched retention (+6.7), so both how many and which words matter.
- **Shows label accuracy can overstate support** on FEVER: a three-way read scoring answers against the kept evidence removes typed selection's gain (−1.0 [−3.9, +2.2] vs +25.2 label-recovery points), and the reader endorses true claims with ≤1/5 of evidence content words surviving 56.0% of the time after LLMLingua-2 (26.8% after random sentences).
- **Boundary conditions**: on Evidence Inference typed selection beats LLMLingua-2 by +12.2 points at a nominal 25% budget, and on τ-bench trajectories (where later tool calls need exact identifiers) typed − random is +22.3 points while LLMLingua-2 shows no detected gain (+4.0 [−1.1, +9.3]) — token-level extraction strips action syntax/identifiers.
- **Methodological hygiene**: 10 prespecified analyses with 51 predictions whose outcomes (including 7 failures) are counted in Appendix A; an out-of-sample check of the gain-vs-evidence-kept relation (0.37 points of gain per point of extra evidence, missing new observations by 3.4 points on average); and an input-token break-even analysis (compaction pays for itself only after 6.6–14.0 later questions).

## Methodology

The paper's core design is a paired contrast against random deletion at matched budgets. A state (paragraph, document, conversation memory, or agent trajectory) carries several questions with annotated evidence sentences. The **known** question is the first listed one (in one check, a randomly redrawn one); all others are **hidden** and only scored after compaction. Budgets keep b ∈ {25, 50, 75}% of sentences (or turns, or o200k tokens in token-matched tests).

Selectors: **Typed selection** puts the known question in the state and asks a typed decision model (gpt-6-luna through the OpenAI Decisions API, which returns probabilities over Boolean/Choice/Score answers with no generated text) one Boolean per sentence — "is this sentence needed to answer the question?" — keeping the most probable sentences; the same model then reads the compacted text. **Question-agnostic selection** asks "does this sentence state a fact a reader is likely to be asked about later?" without any question. TF-IDF ranks sentences by cosine similarity to the question; lead keeps the first sentences; random deletion is a seeded uniform sample. Word-level **LLMLingua-2** scores every word without the question; LongLLMLingua and MMR are the query-aware baselines; a prespecified **hybrid** keeps typed sentences up to half the token budget and fills the rest with LLMLingua-2 words; two LLMs (qwen3.8-flash, gpt-6.1-sol) write abstractive summaries.

Reading: one Boolean per candidate answer ("given the passage, is this a correct answer?"); paragraph-macro accuracy with 2000-resample paragraph-bootstrap 95% intervals; a difference is "detected" only when its interval excludes zero. Readers include gpt-6-luna (full-text MultiRC 88.2%), open-decider 2B (75.3%), and qwen3.8-flash (89.2%, via first-token log-probabilities); two of four typed readers (open-bosun 1.7B, open-lumma 0.6B) fall below the all-"no" baseline (55.6%) and are excluded from main tables. Key diagnostics: the **overlap split** (hidden questions whose evidence shares a sentence with the known question vs. disjoint), **evidence retention** (share of annotated evidence kept, counted over all words and over content words), a **sufficiency check** ("does the passage contain what the question needs?"), **KEEP–DROP interventions** (same-size texts with vs. without a claim's evidence), a **three-way read / support target** on FEVER that scores answers against what the kept text actually supports, and a **restore experiment** that adds deleted evidence back. Ten analyses with 51 predictions were prespecified before any model request; ancillary files contain all reported numbers. The τ-bench test compacts 390 retail trajectory states (837 hidden identifier questions) at 25% of tokens and adds anticipatory selectors (expected-coverage selection over ten qwen3.8-flash-generated future questions).

## Results & Highlights

- **Known question gains, hidden questions don't (MultiRC):** at a 25% budget, typed selection with gpt-6-luna scores 77% on the known question vs 67% for random deletion (open-decider: 70% vs 61%); token-matched, typed − random is **+11.4 [+9.1, +13.8] points known but −0.4 [−1.5, +0.6] hidden**. LongLLMLingua actually falls below random deletion on hidden questions (−1.6 [−2.6, −0.6]).
- **Overlap split:** hidden questions sharing the known question's evidence gain **+4.3 [+2.2, +6.4]** (gpt-6-luna; +5.3 open-decider); disjoint ones lose **−2.9 [−4.3, −1.4]** with only 18% of their evidence kept vs 25% expected under random. Typed selection keeps 50% of the known question's evidence vs the 25% random expectation.
- **Word-level compression leads on hidden questions:** LLMLingua-2 − random sentence deletion at 25% of tokens is **+12.5 [+11.3, +13.6] points on MultiRC (gpt-6-luna)**, +6.0 with open-decider, and **+21.0 [+18.7, +23.4] in FEVER label accuracy**. Picking words instead of sentences with the same LLMLingua-2 scores alone raises hidden accuracy by +11.7 / +4.6 / +19.1 points (MultiRC-luna / MultiRC-decider / FEVER).
- **Content-word retention explains part of the lead:** LLMLingua-2 keeps **48.0%** of hidden questions' evidence content words vs **26.1%** for random sentence deletion (similar shares over all words, 26.3 vs 26.3); at equal content-word retention the gap shrinks from 12.5 to **+7.3 [+6.1, +8.4]** points, and to +4.9 when conditioning on candidate-answer word survival.
- **FEVER label accuracy overstates support:** typed − random is +25.2 points on label recovery but **−1.0 [−3.9, +2.2]** on the support target; with ≤1/5 of a true claim's evidence content words surviving, the reader still calls it supported **56.0%** of the time after LLMLingua-2 vs 26.8% after random sentences. KEEP–DROP: +50.0 [+47.4, +52.4] on FEVER, +17.8 [+15.9, +19.7] on Evidence Inference.
- **Ranking flips across settings:** on Evidence Inference typed selection beats LLMLingua-2 by **+12.2 [+10.1, +14.4] points** at a nominal 25% budget (though not token-matched: 28.9% vs 25.8% of tokens). On τ-bench retail (390 states, 837 hidden questions), typed − random is **+22.3 [+15.1, +28.9] points** (gpt-6-luna) and +21.7 with qwen3.8-flash, while LLMLingua-2 shows no detected gain (+4.0 [−1.1, +9.3]); typed keeps the hidden identifier for 66.4% of questions vs 42.4% (random) and 52.3% (LLMLingua-2), and questions whose identifier survives score 85.9% vs 18.9% when lost.
- **Anticipation trades known for hidden:** expected-coverage selection gains +12.8 [+5.2, +20.8] on hidden τ-bench questions but costs −10.6 (90% CI [−17.8, −2.9]) on the known one; no selector tested beats typed selection on both.
- **LLM summaries underperform extraction on hidden questions:** generic summaries by qwen3.8-flash / gpt-6.1-sol beat random deletion by 7.6 / 10.9 points on hidden MultiRC questions but stay below LLMLingua-2 (−4.5 [−5.7, −3.2] and −1.3 [−2.4, −0.3]); summaries written for the known question ace it (88.5% / 89.2%) yet fall to or below random deletion on hidden ones.
- **Economics:** compacting once pays for itself in input tokens only after ~7.8 (FEVER), 6.6 (Evidence Inference, vs a mean of only 3.7 later questions), 11.0 (LoCoMo) and 14.0 (MultiRC, vs a mean of 10.2) later questions; on-demand recovery of dropped τ-bench units reaches only 69.1% vs 88.8% full-state at 102.0% of its tokens.

## Authors & Credibility

Seven authors, all listed as "Independent Researcher" (several with Northeastern University and one USC alumni email); no institutional affiliation, funding, or code release is stated, though ancillary files with all reported numbers are mentioned. Credibility is bolstered by unusual methodological hygiene for an arXiv preprint: ten analyses with 51 predictions were prespecified before any model request, Appendix A transparently counts every prediction not met (7 of 51) with the misses named and quantified (e.g., the τ-bench recoverable arm predicted to reach 95% of full-state accuracy reached 69.1%), refusal handling is stress-tested (counting refusals as wrong changes no contrast's sign), hosted-model non-determinism is checked (100 repeated full-text reads identical), and an AI-assistance disclosure (Claude Code) is included. The reliance on a hosted black-box decision model (gpt-6-luna via the OpenAI Decisions API) is the main reproducibility caveat the authors themselves acknowledge.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The known-vs-hidden framing is genuinely useful and, while concurrent work (Tirmazi et al.'s compaction theory, Liu et al. on agent KV-cache compaction, Zhang on update sufficiency) covers adjacent ground, this paper's contribution — evidence-annotated corpora plus KEEP–DROP interventions that causally tie losses to deleted evidence, and the sentence-vs-word granularity contrast — is distinct and carefully executed.
- **Rigor:** Strong on prespecification, bootstrap intervals, and paired contrasts against random deletion at matched budgets. But several specifics weaken headline claims: the Evidence Inference "ranking flip" (typed +12.2 over LLMLingua-2) is **not token-matched** (28.9% vs 25.8% of tokens kept), which the authors concede in a parenthesis; intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons across 51 predictions and dozens of contrasts; and the content-word retention analysis is exploratory and leaves part of the MultiRC gap unexplained (+4.9 residual with gpt-6-luna but only +0.6 [−0.5, +1.8] with open-decider — i.e., for the open reader the LLMLingua-2 lead is essentially fully absorbed by retention, something the abstract does not mention).
- **Reproducibility:** gpt-6-luna through the Decisions API is a moving hosted target; two of four open-weight typed readers (open-bosun 1.7B, open-lumma 0.6B) scored below the all-"no" baseline (55.6%) and were dropped from the main tables, so the "typed readers" story rests on two models. No code release is mentioned.
- **Significance:** The negative result — no query-aware sentence selector beats random deletion on later questions on MultiRC, and label accuracy diverges from evidential support on FEVER — is exactly the kind practitioners need, and the τ-bench identifier finding is directly actionable for agent harnesses.
- **Red flags / data quirks:** LoCoMo has only ten conversations and a four-choice format where **empty memory already scores 81.4%**, leaving typed-vs-random unresolved (harder options only lowered the floor to 60.2%, missing their own prespecified ≤60% target); τ-bench uses only the retail domain, one 25% budget, and recorded (not live) trajectories whose questions reduce to identifier lookup; benchmark texts predate the models, so contamination cannot be ruled out; six retail hidden questions had identifier leaks the masking missed (bounded at ≤0.2 points, to their credit).

## Strengths

- A genuinely important evaluation question, asked cleanly: compaction is always scored on the question it was made for, never on the ones that come later — this paper fixes that with annotated-evidence corpora and causal KEEP–DROP interventions.
- Random deletion at matched budget as the universal baseline is the right control and quietly indicts several published compressors (LongLLMLingua falls *below* random on hidden questions).
- The content-word retention analysis offers a mechanistic, testable explanation for why word-level compression wins, not just a leaderboard.
- Prespecified predictions with transparent accounting of failures (7 of 51 not met) and out-of-sample validation of the gain-vs-evidence-kept relation are exemplary for this genre.
- Practical, quantified guidance: measure hidden-question accuracy, report evidence retention in content words, score support not just labels, and count the token cost of compaction itself (break-even at 6.6–14.0 later questions).

## Limitations

- All experiments are on public benchmarks and recorded trajectories, not live agents; τ-bench questions reduce to identifier recall among same-shaped distractors, and only retail has enough trajectories.
- The word-vs-sentence conclusion is corpus-dependent (flips on Evidence Inference and τ-bench) and the key Evidence Inference comparison is not token-matched.
- The selector and the reader are usually the same typed model (gpt-6-luna), creating a self-selection advantage; qwen3.8-flash is the only general reader, and it reads gpt-6-luna's selections.
- Abstractive summaries are tested only on MultiRC at one budget; the KEEP–DROP contrast covers one budget and excludes MultiRC; evidence annotations are incomplete, so retention and support scoring cannot account for unannotated evidence.
- LoCoMo's ten conversations and 81.4% empty-memory floor make that setting nearly uninformative; no code release is mentioned, and hosted-model reads may drift over time.

## Relevance

该论文直接命中"Agent & Pipeline Silent Failures"方向：上下文压缩是 agent 编排层中一个会"悄悄改变模型所见内容"的组件，论文量化了它如何静默删除后续问题所需的证据、以及标签准确率如何掩盖这种证据丢失（FEVER 支撑度评分），对长时运行自主 agent 系统的可靠性与记忆设计有直接指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the word-vs-sentence ranking flip persist under strict token-matched budgets on Evidence Inference, or is the +12.2 advantage of typed selection purely a budget artifact?
- Can a selector beat typed selection on both known and hidden questions simultaneously — e.g., by mixing typed scores with content-word-coverage objectives — or is the known/hidden trade-off (ECov: +12.8 hidden, −10.6 known) fundamental?
- Does the FEVER gap between label accuracy and evidential support also appear when readers must cite the retained evidence (provenance-linked answering), and can the 56.0% unsupported-endorsement rate be reduced by sufficiency checks alone?
- How do the findings transfer to live agents that can re-fetch dropped state (the recoverable arm reached only 69.1% vs 88.8% full-state at 102% of tokens — is better re-fetch selection the bottleneck)?
- Do KV-cache compression methods (query-agnostic vs query-aware eviction) show the same hidden-question collapse on these evidence-annotated corpora, unifying the prompt-compression and KV-compression findings?
