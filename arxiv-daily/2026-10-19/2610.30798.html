01Methodology — the attack chain

Three system facts, chained; the exploit is the seam between them.

  • Rowhammer needs no root. Aggressor-row activation flips bits in adjacent DRAM rows; memory massaging / deduplication can land the shared libtriton.so page on a flippable frame.
  • The vendor-neutral seam. @triton.jit kernels lower Triton IR → Triton-GPU IR → LLVM IR → NVPTX / AMDGPU. The first two levels are target-independent — one flip feeds every vendor backend.
  • Persistent poisoning. vLLM compiles attention / matmul / layernorm / softmax at startup and caches them in ~/.triton/cache. LLVMhammer fires before inference; the next compile emits a suboptimal-but-correct kernel that is reused across restarts.

Karnage offline search: flips restricted to Hamming-distance-1 control-flow opcodes (je↔jne, cmov) — a valid instruction that changes a branch, the only route to "slower but correct" — then pruned via namespaces, Intel PT traces, offline compile-diffs, GPU profiling. Root cause verified at 3 IR levels: 4×32-bit vectorized load → 4 scalar loads; 6× LDG/STG.E.128 → 48× scalar LDG.E.U16.
Testbeds: i7-4790 + DDR3 + RTX 4090 (CUDA 13.2, vLLM 0.24) · i7-8700 + DDR4 + RX 7900 XTX (ROCm 7.14, vLLM 0.25) · Triton 3.6 · n ∈ {10, 100, 1000} · 30 runs · Welch's t-test.

03Provenance

IIT Bombay architecture / security group; direct follow-up to PRowhammer (ISCA 2026), generalizing CPU→GPU bit-flips from NVIDIA SASS to the vendor-neutral Triton/MLIR stack. Real dual-vendor hardware, two DRAM generations (DDR3-1600, DDR4-2400), production stack versions, multi-level IR root-cause evidence rather than black-box timing claims.

04Strengths

  • Unexplored, structurally vendor-agnostic surface — target-independent MLIR passes mean one flip hits every vendor backend, no re-profiling.
  • Karnage is real engineering: ~1.18M candidates (~12-yr brute force) → 1,176 profiled flips in ~10 h, fully automated, with per-stage counts tabulated.
  • Hamming-1 control-flow restriction guarantees flips produce valid instructions — principled, not lucky.
  • Exceptional measurement hygiene: 30 repetitions, Welch's t-test for changed variance, honest exclusion of noise-dominated n=10.
  • Causal, multi-level evidence (LLVM IR → PTX → SASS de-vectorization) explains why kernels are slower, not just that they are.

05Limitations

  • Modest absolute effect (4–16%) and only statistically reliable under sustained load (n ≥ 100–1000); low-batch serving is likely unaffected.
  • No end-to-end Rowhammer success-rate or flip-latency stats; DDR5/PRAC practicality cited from prior work, not shown.
  • Output-corruption mode rests on one qualitative prompt plus BERTScore floors — no per-task accuracy degradation numbers.
  • Single-GPU, 7B-model scope; tensor-parallel serving, larger models, and ephemeral container caches unevaluated.
  • Simple mitigations exist (recompile-per-session, library reload, cache hashing) — the realistic window is deployments that verify nothing.

06Questions for further exploration

  1. True end-to-end success probability and time-to-flip on DDR5/PRAC servers, including the memory-massaging step?
  2. Is signing or hash-verifying the Triton cache a practical defense — and what startup cost does TRITON_ALWAYS_COMPILE=1 impose in production?
  3. Does the slowdown scale to 70B / MoE models and tensor-parallel multi-GPU serving, where attention is a smaller fraction of time?
  4. Can the de-vectorization signature (scalar 16-bit loads replacing 128-bit transactions) be a runtime tripwire via GPU performance counters?
  5. Could analogous compiler-pipeline flips hit other MLIR stacks (IREE, XLA/StableHLO, torch.compile) or quantization passes to corrupt numerics?
