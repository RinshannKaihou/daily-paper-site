Critical edge

Every gain comes from SFT where the loss covers only a small fraction of tokens — whether the gradient skew that makes sparsity cheap survives pretraining is untested.

+ no seed variance reported: the +0.60 headline at 8B could plausibly be noise.

Methodology — distilled

Two observations drive the design: attention backward does roughly 2× the forward’s work (four n²d matrix products vs two), and the output gradient dO drives all four — so query blocks with tiny ‖dO‖ barely matter.

  1. Score for free. Split the sequence into 128-token query blocks; score each by ‖dO₂‖ᵢ (Frobenius norm). dO already exists when backward begins — no extra pass, unlike sparse-forward methods.
  2. Gradient-mass budget δ. Drop the k lowest blocks whose combined squared energy ≤ δ of the per-head total — adaptive every head, every step. δ is the only hyperparameter (0.1 text / 0.4 speech; 10% of mass → ~65% of tiles skipped).
  3. Unbiased sampling. Keep block m with q₂ = min(1, λc₂) (λ by bisection), rescale by 1/q — gradient stays unbiased, variance confined to the low-gradient tail (classic importance sampling, applied to kernel tiles).
  4. Kernel realization. 3 compact CUDA kernels feed per-block weights into the FlashAttention-2.5.2 backward; zero-weight tiles are skipped before any load. Varlen inputs padded to 128. Forward kernel untouched.

Strengths

  • Deployable dense model. Forward left exact → no train-inference mismatch (MoBA loses 4.9 pts when sparsely inferred) — vLLM serves it unchanged.
  • Unbiased by construction. Inverse-probability weighting keeps the gradient estimate exact, with variance confined to the low-gradient tail; the importance-sampling lineage is honestly credited.
  • Unusually clean ablations at matched compute, plus single-hyperparameter sensitivity across 2 modalities, 3 model sizes, 3 context lengths.
  • Beats dense on every ASR column — skipping redundant acoustic blocks behaves like free regularization.

Limitations

  • SFT-only evidence (sparse loss masks); dense-loss pretraining and long horizons unverified.
  • +0.60 at 8B without seed error bars; the strongest claims lean on the 14B and ASR results.
  • One kernel stack (FlashAttention-2.5.2 / H200); end-to-end speedup bounded by the untouched quadratic forward; 8K sees only 1.20×.
  • Thin external field: Qwen3-only, one text benchmark, English-only speech, MoBA the only trained baseline — and no code release; the CUDA pipeline needs re-implementation.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does gradient-mass concentration hold under pretraining-style dense losses — or is LazyFlash fundamentally an SFT accelerator?
  2. Does the scheme survive FlashAttention-3’s asynchrony and FP8 pipelines, or other hardware vendors?
  3. Could the kept-block fraction (18% → 10%) serve as a free training-dynamics diagnostic (gradient collapse, abnormal layers)?
  4. Does the unbiased-but-higher-variance gradient accumulate noise in Adam second moments over very long runs?
  5. Would combining it with decode-time sparsity (e.g. SpargeAttention) compound training + inference savings with no mismatch on either side?
