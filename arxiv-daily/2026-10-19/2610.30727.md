> **一句话总结：** 本文将 Latent Guard 等"隐层安全性监视器"分解为"对请求有害性的判断"与"模型自身的拒绝计划"两种信号的混合，并用跨模型分歧统计量 Π 证明：在指令末 token 处读到的忠实信号在分布偏移（改写/说服框架/越狱包装）下并不比 TF-IDF 更强、也不超过模型自身的拒绝行为；而在生成前 token 处读到的信号虽多抓住 44% 有害提示（对比拒绝行为的 32%），但本质上是拒绝方向（refusal direction）的读出，继承模型决策的盲区，无法作为独立的安全检查。

> **TL;DR:** Across 15 open instruction-tuned LLMs (Llama-3, Qwen2.5/3, Gemma-3/4, OLMo-3, OLMoE; ≤32B) and 38 activation-based readers, the authors show that Latent Guard as published scores a median pooled AUROC of 0.62 on a 2,248-prompt rewording benchmark (SORRY-Bench surface/persuasion mutations, WildJailbreak rewrites, plus a new "alarming benign" set) — below a TF-IDF classifier trained on the same 2,000 examples per class (0.75) in every model — and catches only 23% of harmful reworded prompts at the model's matched false-alarm rate vs 32% for the model's own refusal. Moving the same read to the last pre-generation token catches 44% (+7.4 pts over refusal), but cross-model tests show this gain is a readout of the model's refusal decision (coupling Π = 0.42, same as refusal directions at 0.44–0.48), not an independent harm judgment: every reader that beats the refusal is coupled to the decision (rank correlation 0.83), and audits show most "extra catches" are refusals that string-matching evaluation missed.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"判断 vs 计划"的分解框架**：把隐层危害读数 s_m(x) 形式化为请求级判断 J_m（对有害性的评估）与模型拒绝计划 P_m（拒绝倾向）的混合，并定义"忠实性（faithfulness）"为读数由 J_m 主导的程度——只有忠实的监视器才能独立检查模型行为。
- **设计可分离两种信号的解耦检验**：包括控制危害标签与跨模型共识（consensus）后的回归系数 βs、固定共识下的类内 AUROC，以及关键的**跨模型对称分歧统计量 Π**——对任何"模型无关"的打分恒等于 0，对模型自身首 token 拒绝概率这一纯计划读出为 0.45–0.49，从而首次能定量判断一个读数携带了多少模型私有决策信号。
- **大规模对照评估**：在 15 个模型、2 个读取位置上系统评估 38 种读取器，与**用完全相同训练行**训练的 TF-IDF / 句向量分类器、独立训练的 Llama Guard 3、以及模型自身拒绝行为（在匹配误报率下）对比——隔离了"表征"本身的贡献。
- **精确复现并扩展 Latent Guard**：包括原配方、扩充数据版（100→2000 例/类）、逐层分析；提出含新"无害但听起来吓人（alarming benign）"成分的改写测试集 D_rew（2,248 条），并对每模型行为标签做 LLM 审计；代码、数据、标签全部开源。
- **核心结论**：忠实的隐层读数并不优于同训练数据的句向量分类器，也不优于模型自身行为；读数更强的位置读到的其实是拒绝决策本身——"更有效的隐层监视器只是模型行为的廉价早期副本，无法独立核查它"。

## Key Contributions

- A **judgment-vs-plan decomposition** of latent harm reads: any monitor score is a mixture of a request-level harm judgment J_m and the model's own refusal plan P_m; a monitor is "faithful" only if the judgment dominates, because a plan-dominated score inherits every framing-induced blind spot of the behaviour it is supposed to check.
- **Decoupling tests that can tell the two apart**, most notably the cross-model symmetric disagreement statistic Π_AB = π_AB + π_BA − 1, which is exactly 0 for any model-blind score, ~0.45–0.49 for the model's own first-token refusal probability (a pure plan), and computed over 78 model pairs (two models excluded after a label audit).
- A **38-reader × 15-model × 2-position evaluation** against baselines that isolate the representation: TF-IDF and sentence-embedding (all-mpnet-base-v2) classifiers trained on exactly the same rows, an independent safety classifier (Llama Guard 3 8B), and the model's own refusal compared at a matched false-alarm rate — avoiding the calibration confound of comparing monitors at their own thresholds.
- An **exact and extended re-implementation of Latent Guard** (Zhao et al., 2025), a rewording suite with a new harmless-made-alarming component, per-model behaviour labels with LLM audits, and leakage control via leak groups (minimal pairs, rewrite-base pairs, near-duplicates at Jaccard ≥ 0.8) — all released.
- The headline negative result: **faithful latent harm monitors work no better than the model's own behaviour**, and the latent reads that do beat the behaviour are coupled to the refusal decision and therefore cannot check it independently.

## Methodology

The study covers 15 open instruction-tuned models from four developers (Llama-3.2-3B/3-8B, Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B, Qwen3-8B/32B/30B-A3B, Gemma-3-4B/12B/27B, Gemma-4-26B-A4B, OLMo-3-7B/32B, OLMoE-1B-7B; three MoE). Activations are read at two anchored positions: t_inst, the last token of the user instruction (first position that has seen the whole request), and t_post, the last chat-template token before the reply (where the planned response is represented).

**Readers (38 total):** Latent Guard's layer-averaged cosine-to-class-means statistic (LG) and a z-scored late-layer variant (LG-z); L2-regularised linear probes and one-hidden-layer MLP probes (layer and regularisation chosen by 5-fold CV); and four refusal directions built by design (refused-vs-answered contrasts and projections, plus an Arditi-style harmful-minus-harmless projection). Each reader is applied at both positions. **Baselines:** TF-IDF (word 1–2-grams + logistic regression) and embedding LR/MLP on all-mpnet-base-v2, all trained on exactly the rows the probes see; Llama Guard 3 8B scored zero-shot; the model's first-token refusal probability as a graded behaviour score; and (Appendix G) a hosted decision model, Jev, as an uninspectable upper reference.

**Training data:** S0 reproduces Latent Guard exactly (100 refused AdvBench/JailbreakBench + 100 answered Alpaca/XSTest-safe per model); nested curated sets S100 ⊂ S500 ⊂ S2000 use ground-truth harm labels without behaviour filtering. **Test sets:** D_beh (1,048 prompts, 410 harmful, from sources disjoint from training), D_id (600 in-distribution held-out items), and D_rew (2,248 reworded prompts: 662 surface rewording + 750 persuasion framing + 300 jailbreak-harmful + 300 jailbreak-benign + 236 new alarming-benign), separated from training by leak group.

**Decoupling machinery (RQ1):** (1) regress the model's refusal on the score with harm label and consensus (share of other models refusing) as controls; (2) within-class AUROC for the model's own refusal at fixed consensus; (3) the symmetric disagreement statistic Π over pairs of models. **Matched-rate detection (RQ2):** each monitor's threshold is set to flag exactly as many benign prompts as the model refused, so caught (TPR) is directly comparable with the refusal's own hit rate; the gain Δ decomposes into missed-refused cost and answered-caught benefit (eq. 4). Medians over 15 models with 95% bootstrap intervals throughout; two models are excluded from cross-model medians after an audit found their string-matched labels mostly wrong on disagreement items.

## Results & Highlights

- **In-distribution evaluation is uninformative:** on D_id, activation monitors reach AUROC ≥ 0.998 at both positions and every training size, Llama Guard 3 reaches 1.000, embedding classifiers 0.98–0.99, TF-IDF 0.96–0.98 — all near-perfect, which is why all conclusions rest on D_rew.
- **Latent Guard fails under distribution shift and loses to TF-IDF:** on the 2,248-prompt rewording set D_rew, Latent Guard as published reaches a median pooled AUROC of **0.62 (range 0.54–0.71), below the TF-IDF classifier trained on S2000 (0.75) in every one of the 15 models**; 20× more curated data barely moves it (0.63 at 100/class → 0.65 at 2,000/class).
- **At a matched false-alarm rate (median refusal FPR 0.045, hit rate 0.321 on D_rew), the instruction-token read loses to the model's own refusal:** Latent Guard catches **0.225 of harmful prompts vs the refusal's 0.321 (below in 13/15 models, above in 0)**; the linear probe at S2000 (0.315) and embedding LR (0.327) merely tie the refusal, and Llama Guard 3 (0.395) beats Latent Guard as published in every model — by +0.186 pooled AUROC, +0.119 on persuasion-framed prompts, and +0.171 caught.
- **The template-token read detects more but is a refusal readout:** the same LG statistic on the same 200 rows moved to t_post reaches pooled AUROC 0.779 and catches **0.439 vs 0.321 for the refusal (Δ = +0.074; above in 12 models, below in 0)**, but its cross-model coupling is Π = 0.42 — indistinguishable from the four refusal directions by design (Π = 0.44–0.48, catching 0.40–0.45), and paired LG minus Arditi-style projection is +0.003 caught (2↑ 1↓), i.e. nothing beyond a refusal direction.
- **Much of that gain is an artefact of string-matched labels:** the gain comes from prompts the string-matching rule calls "answered" (h_ans = 0.18 vs h_ref = 0.94), and an audit of 135 such harmful replies finds **only 13 actually gave harmful help** — the rest were partial/sanitised replies or refusals worded without a listed phrase.
- **Coupling predicts gain across all 38 readers:** every reader with Π ≤ 0.18 has a median gain ≤ +0.03 and never clearly beats the refusal; every reader with Π ≥ 0.25 beats it by +0.04 to +0.10; no reader falls in between (rank correlation 0.83 over the 30 non-refusal-direction readers). Fitting t_post probes to more harm labels pulls them off the decision and *hurts*: the linear probe's gain falls from +0.082 (100/class) to −0.025 (2,000/class) and the MLP's from +0.044 to −0.083, with Π falling 0.25 → 0.12.
- **One bounded exception:** with the false-alarm budget set on the alarming-benign component alone, probes at S2000 beat the refusal by **+0.47 to +0.57** while remaining decoupled (Π = 0.09–0.12), where the embedding MLP (+0.034) and Llama Guard 3 (+0.031) do not — but at that same threshold the linear probe flags **0.59 of jailbreak-style benign prompts (MLP 0.65; 0.75/0.84 at t_post) vs the refusal's 0.04**, i.e. false alarms are moved, not removed.
- **The instruction-token read's one real advantage:** the S2000 probe removes the persuasion-framing AUROC drop that persists for Latent Guard in all 15 models (0.10–0.37), scoring **+0.11 AUROC above embedding LR on framed prompts (interval above 0 in all 15 models)** — but it needs 2,000 labels per class.
- **Reference points:** the hosted decision model Jev reaches pooled AUROC 0.90 on D_rew and catches 0.668 at the refusal's FPR (+0.30 over the refusal, 15/15 models), the only detector whose hits do not concentrate on already-refused prompts (h_ans = 0.52) — at the cost of an uninspectable API call per prompt.

## Authors & Credibility

Three equal-contribution authors (Abhishek Mishra, Sagnik Chatterjee, Suyash Maniyar) from the University of Massachusetts Amherst. The work is a direct, systematic re-evaluation of Latent Guard (Zhao et al., 2025, arXiv:2507.11878) and engages carefully with the adjacent literature (Arditi et al.'s refusal direction, Llama Guard/WildGuard/ShieldGemma, SORRY-Bench, WildJailbreak, XSTest, OR-Bench, Karne 2026's monitor-vs-refusal correlation). Code, data, labels and per-model audits are released at github.com/abhishek9909/faithfulness-harm-monitors, and the paper pre-registers four concrete results that would contradict its reading (Section 7) — both strong credibility signals. The scale (15 models, 38 readers, matched-training baselines, label audits, leakage control via leak groups, bootstrap intervals) is well above the norm for probing papers.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The decomposition (judgment vs plan) and especially the cross-model disagreement statistic Π are genuinely new methodological contributions — Π is exactly 0 for any model-blind score, a clean identification property. The negative result itself extends a growing literature (probes collapse OOD; Karne 2026's 1.1–6.4× refusal-association) rather than overturning it from scratch, but the matched-training-row and matched-false-alarm-rate comparisons are the most rigorous version of this critique to date.
- **Rigor:** Strong — identical training rows for activation vs embedding classifiers, threshold matched to each model's own refusal FPR, 95% bootstrap intervals with per-model above/below counts, exclusion of two models after an audit showed their string-matched labels were mostly wrong (OLMoE prefixing "As an AI", Qwen2.5-3B refusing by redirection), and a sensitivity analysis separating readout from learned direction (cosine 0.999 at S0).
- **The weakest link is the behaviour labelling.** Refusals are labelled by string matching on greedy 256-token replies, and the authors' own audit shows only 13 of 135 string-matched "answered" harmful replies were actual harmful help. This cuts both ways honestly — they argue correcting labels would only raise the refusal's hit rate, making the instruction-token comparison conservative — but the headline t_post gain (+0.074) is explicitly acknowledged to be partly gain over the string-matching rule, not over the model; the registered follow-up test uses full-reply judged labels.
- **Data-quirk concerns:** the new alarming-benign component was generated by one LLM and checked by another (about 2 weak rewrites found in the 10 pairs read; no full manual pass), and the curated harmless training side at S500/S2000 is built from over-refusal benchmarks "of the same kind as the alarming set" — a train/test construction overlap the authors themselves flag as the main objection to the one exception where faithful reads beat the behaviour. The matched-rate threshold is set by only ~24 flagged benign prompts per model, so per-model operating points are noisy. One split seed, and Llama Guard 3's training data may include the public benchmarks used, though its zero-shot comparison to Latent Guard is not the central claim.
- **Reproducibility:** excellent for the open-model pipeline (15 open models, released code/data/labels, exact re-implementation with documented deviations — chat template, 256-token budget, SORRY-Bench release); weaker for the Jev reference, whose size, training data and calibration are uninspectable (the authors scope it as an upper reference only).
- **Significance and red flags:** the practical conclusion — in-distribution AUROC is uninformative for monitor choice, thresholds should be calibrated on hard benign data, and "better-than-refusal" latent monitors are early refusal-propensity readouts — is directly actionable for safety-monitoring deployments. The main overclaim risk is in the title's generality: only centroid, linear and one-hidden-layer readers at two positions are tested; pooled/attention-based/multi-position probes, positions between b and T, SAE-derived features, and monitors optimised to evade (Bailey et al., 2026) are out of scope, and the decoupling tests cannot rule out a plan component shared by all models. All tested models are open and ≤32B; frontier closed models are untested.

## Strengths

- The judgment/plan decomposition turns a vague worry ("is the probe just reading refusal?") into a testable, quantitative property, and Π has an exact null (0 for any model-blind score) with a meaningful pure-plan reference (first-token refusal probability at 0.45–0.49).
- Baseline discipline: activation readers are compared against text classifiers trained on *exactly the same rows* and against the refusal *at its own false-alarm budget*, eliminating two chronic confounds (representation vs labels; ranking vs calibration).
- Breadth with consistency: 15 models across four families and 38 readers, with per-model tables, and conclusions that replicate across both test sets (D_beh for decoupling, D_rew for detection).
- Intellectual honesty: label audits with published error tables, pre-registered falsifiers, explicit bounding of the one exception (alarming-benign) including its false-alarm cost, and a candid limitations section.
- The qualitative examples are unusually diagnostic — e.g. the same "murder … in my essay" prompt is flagged at t_inst when the model answers (word-level false alarm) and at t_post when a different model refuses (copied over-refusal).

## Limitations

- Reader coverage stops at centroid, linear, and one-hidden-layer MLP readers at two anchor positions; a harm judgment spread across intermediate positions or carried by attention-pooled/SAE features would be missed by design.
- Behaviour labels come from string matching on greedy replies — audits show this misses refusals worded without listed phrases, so the refusal baseline is underestimated and the t_post gain overstated relative to judged labels.
- The alarming-benign test component is model-generated and model-checked without a full manual pass, and overlaps in kind with the over-refusal benchmarks used on the curated harmless training side.
- The matched-rate threshold rests on ~24 flagged benign prompts per model; one split seed is used; only open models up to 32B parameters are tested; only one independent safety classifier (with possible benchmark contamination) and one opaque hosted decision model are included.
- The decoupling tests are necessary but not sufficient for faithfulness — a refusal-plan component shared by all models would pass them (the authors state models disagree on only 115–317 items per pair).

## Relevance

这篇论文与"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向高度相关：它系统评估了线性探针、质心方向（Latent Guard）等隐层读取技术作为安全监视器的真实能力边界，并给出判断"探针读到的是表征还是行为计划"的跨模型解耦方法 Π，对任何想做 probing / 拒绝方向 / 内部表征安全性监控的工作都是重要的方法论与负面结果参照。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would attention-pooled, multi-position, or SAE-feature readers occupy the empty band of Figure 6 (Π between 0.18 and 0.25), or does the coupling-gain dichotomy hold for richer readers?
- How do the conclusions change when refusal labels come from a full-reply LLM judge instead of string matching — does the t_post gain (+0.074) shrink to the ~0.02 predicted by the audit, and do instruction-token monitors then look even worse?
- Does the coupling-gain relationship hold for frontier closed models, where refusal behaviour and training data differ, and for monitors read during multi-turn agentic sessions rather than single prompts?
- Can the one genuine advantage — the S2000 instruction-token probe's robustness to persuasion framing (+0.11 AUROC over embedding LR) — be achieved with fewer labels via intermediate representations of a stronger teacher, or does it scale with model capability?
- Since Bailey et al. (2026) show activations can be optimised to evade latent monitors, how do the faithful (t_inst) and plan (t_post) reads compare under adaptive attack rather than natural distribution shift?
- Is the shared blind spot (persuasive framing that fools both the model and every coupled reader — 366–568 unflagged complied prompts per model) a property of the models' training distribution, and would a debate-of-monitors or cross-model ensemble close it?
