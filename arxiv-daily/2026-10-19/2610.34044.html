Why this matters

A default-looking sampler config silently costs 8 accuracy points; the paper turns mysterious entropy blow-ups into loggable batch statistics you can alarm on.

Distilled from the Relevance section: net advantage N and the sharpness gap δ become monitorable training signals.

Critical edge ⚠

The theory predicts signs, not sizes — the paper's own MASK run violates its linear prediction by ≈3×, and the headline 9B gain rests on just 10 steps from a single model fork.

— Critical Assessment · Limitations

01 Methodology how the law is derived

  • First-order account of one gradient step. A step on A·log π changes entropy by −ηA·Φ(a). Under exact sampling, constant advantage shifts cancel (classical baseline invariance). Under sampler μ they don't: a constant shift c gives E[ΔH] = −ηc·δ(μ), where δ is the sharpness gap between sampler and policy.
  • Theorem 1 (layer-cake proof): any sampler sharper than π — top-k, top-p, min-p, T<1, temperature-then-truncation — has δ>0, so positive shifts sharpen the policy; flatter samplers (T>1, uniform mixing) flip the sign. Verified on 14,400 random + 4,920 adversarial distributions with zero exceptions.
  • Proposition 1 (batch decomposition): group-RLVR entropy drift = D_net (signed by token-weighted net advantage N — the only part any baseline controls) + D_cov (advantage–gap covariance, baseline-independent, self-amplifying) + D_on.
  • Why mask vs. fail flips the sign: with a group-mean baseline, N = Cov(r, L)/L̄ — negative under FAIL (long replies fail more → entropy rises); MASK adds a non-negative term → N > 0 (entropy falls).
  • Rigorous controls: vLLM rollouts, entropy probed by teacher forcing on a fixed 64-prompt set, paired seeds isolating exactly one factor, t-based 95% intervals.

Models: Qwen3.5-2B (15/100 steps) · MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B fork (10 steps) · OLMo-3-7B replication · 7,496 MATH problems, 750 held out.

  • Dose-response is linear in N under truncation ((−106.8 ± 0.7)×10⁻³ per unit) and flat (+1.1 ± 2.7) under exact sampling — a clean causal chain.
  • Sign flips at the only flatter sampler: at T=1.2, MASK drifts +21.5 and FAIL −11.6 ×10⁻³ nats/step, reversing in all three seeds.
  • Long horizon (2B, 100 steps): FAIL entropy explodes to 3.6–4.6 nats (accuracy 45.5%→32.7%); NZ only delays it; GAPW holds 0.18–0.31 nats and 68.7% accuracy; exact sampling reaches 68–71%.
  • Practice survey at pinned commits: 18 frameworks, 19 trainers, 123 reports — e.g. ROLL defaults T=0.95 / top-p 0.7 / top-k 50 with no learner-side temperature; torchtitan and TRL landed corrections only during 2026.

04 Strengths

  • Provable and falsifiable — stress-tested on ~19,000 distributions and a 300-step tabular simulation (prediction within 3%), with no counterexample found.
  • Immediately practical — turns "entropy mysteriously moved" into loggable, alarm-able batch statistics (N and the mean gap).
  • Exceptional control discipline — paired seeds isolating one factor, exact-sampling and renormalization controls, an out-of-sample aggregation test, honest reporting of misses (MASK off the line, Adam's 12× amplification).
  • Rare engineering ground truth — a pinned-commit audit of 18 real RL frameworks documenting who ships the failure mode by default.

05 Limitations

  • First-order only, signs not sizes — magnitudes don't transfer across optimizers (Adam ≈ 12× SGD); the paper's own MASK point sits ~3× off the linear prediction.
  • Small experimental scale — 15-step 2B mains, 10-step 9B fork, 2–4 seeds (9B MASK: a single seed), math-only rewards, one sampler family; several 100-step runs truncated by a node failure.
  • Confound in the fixes — NZ/GAPW change the baseline, so their accuracy benefit may partly be variance reduction; renormalization mitigates but doesn't fully close this.
  • External validity unproven — OLMo-3-7B shows ~1/8 the Qwen effect at a similar gap (unexplained); evaluations always use the truncated sampler; no code release mentioned.

06 Questions for further exploration

  1. Do NZ/GAPW benefits persist over hundreds of steps at 9B+, or does NZ merely postpone the 2B-style explosion — making learner-side renormalization the only durable fix?
  2. Why is the mask-fail effect on OLMo-3-7B only ~1/8 of Qwen3.5-2B's at a similar sharpness gap — probe distribution, pretraining sharpness, or the format-grading quirk?
  3. Can the twelvefold Adam amplification be folded into a predictive (not just sign-level) theory, e.g. via a preconditioner-weighted gap — and does the law survive async staleness plus truncation?
  4. How does the sign law interact with clip-higher and entropy bonuses — could deployed anti-collapse remedies themselves set a mismatch drift?
  5. In agentic RLVR (near-deterministic prefixes, much longer horizons), does the drift shrink — or does discarded-tail feedback dominate?
