01Methodology

How a floating-point equivalence query collapses into linear integer arithmetic — in six moves.

  1. The hazard. The OCP MX spec lowers the block dot product in hoisted form; torchao dequantizes first (direct). Over binary32 the distributive law fails via overflow, 0·∞ → NaN, or subnormal ties-to-even — and the outcome depends on which reduction tree the compiler chose.
  2. Finite alphabet. Every E2M1 lane product is one of 37 quarter-integers (|coefficient| ≤ 144); both E8M0 scales are powers of two.
  3. One shared grid. While 144k ≤ 224 (k ≤ 116,508), every partial sum is exactly representable, so both forms live on a single grid n·2E, E = t−2. IEEE rounding reduces to three integer cases: exact, overflow, RNE shift onto the subnormal grid.
  4. Exact encoding (Thm 1). A QF LIA state machine (kind ∈ {fin, ±∞, NaN}, integer S) with six exhaustively checked lemmas is equisatisfiable with the FP query; counterexamples translate constructively in both directions.
  5. Closed-form guard (Thm 2). Hoisting is value-preserving when e_A, e_B ≤ 252 and −147 ≤ t ≤ 129 − ⌊log2(144·mρ)⌋, where mρ is the leaf count of the reduction tree's largest proper subtree (folds: k−1; balanced: ⌈k/2⌉).
  6. Certify & ship. Sharpness proven per configuration by unsat@B / sat@B+1 solver pairs; an MLIR pass emits the branchless hoisted form or a 4-comparison runtime guard, and an RTX 5080 replays every witness bit-for-bit.

At k = 32 the one-step gap between fold and balanced bands is exactly what the Section-1 witness (t = 118) lands between.

  • GPU conformance (RTX 5080): the witness replays bit-for-bit; inside the guard, direct and hoisted kernels agree bitwise on 65,536 random blocks per tree — outside it, they differ on 99.8% of blocks.
  • Three-level validation: exhaustive finite-domain lemma checks (e.g. all 4.65M rounding-primitive points), 140,000 stratified random blocks with zero mismatches, and cross-agreement with an independent QF FP encoder on boundary verdicts.
  • Honest performance: hoisting speeds up all six LUT-decode configs (best +24.9%) but the fastest direct config still beats the fastest hoisted by 7.6% — placement is a target-dependent choice, not a universal win.
  • Generality: the construction carries to MXFP6 and MXINT8 at k = 32; it does not extend to MXFP8 (products span 18 binades); single-level NVFP4 needs no band at all — everything is exact, verified over 4M+ enumerated cases.

03Strengths

What the paper gets right.

  • Real, deployable hazard: spec-style and torchao-style lowerings of the same OCP operation silently diverge on valid inputs — and the divergence depends on the compiler's own reduction-tree choice.
  • Exact, not approximate: a faithfulness theorem with constructive counterexample translation — solver-found witnesses are guaranteed to replay in binary32.
  • Compiler-ready guard: four comparisons on two bytes, computable offline per (k, tree), integrated in an actual MLIR pass with static-range and runtime-check modes.
  • Validation discipline: exhaustive lemma checks, 140k+ differential blocks, an independent FP-encoder cross-check, and SASS-inspected GPU replay confirming the modeled arithmetic is what hardware executed.
  • Intellectual honesty: reports its own k = 64 encoding pitfall, bounds the FP-solver timings conservatively, and publishes a performance sweep its own optimization loses.

04Limitations

Where the certificate does not reach.

  • Narrow scope: one format (MXFP4 · E2M1 · E8M0), one rewrite, binary32 accumulator, RNE with gradual underflow. FTZ, FMA contraction, wider accumulators, and native block-scaled tensor-core paths all sit outside the certified model.
  • Not mechanized: Theorems 1–2 rest on a human proof plus exhaustive and differential testing — no Coq/Lean/Isabelle certificate (acknowledged as future work).
  • Sharpness is partial: certified for 26 configurations (4 tree shapes × 6 lengths, plus 2 at k = 64); the claim for every binary tree remains unproven.
  • Block-local reasoning: full-GEMM equivalence additionally requires identical outer accumulation order; the prototype stops at reference IR — no production kernels, no end-to-end verified speedup.
  • Measurement gaps: the runtime guard's cost is never measured; the GPU sweep is 12 software-emulated configs on one RTX 5080, medians only, no variance. Single author, no visible affiliation or artifact DOI in the paper text.

05Open Questions

Where this line of work points next.

  • Can the bands be re-derived for real block-scaled tensor cores (Blackwell / AMD, FTZ and fused dot-product units) by composing Valpey-style hardware models with this LIA machinery?
  • Can the faithfulness proof be mechanized in Lean so the guard itself — not just the prototype encoding — carries a machine-checked certificate?
  • Would a polynomial-time bottom-up decision procedure beat the SMT solver for per-autotune-candidate certification in kernel generation?
  • What does the runtime guard actually cost end-to-end in a tiled GEMM, and does static scale-range metadata propagate through real torchao/Triton pipelines?
  • Can the analysis extend to multi-level scaling (full NVFP4, MXFP8) by tracking per-lane grids instead of one shared grid — perhaps at the cost of exactness?
  • Since every witness pushes values to the range boundary, could the guard double as a cheap online detector for pathological scale/activation distributions in deployed quantized serving?
