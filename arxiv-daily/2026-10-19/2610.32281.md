> **一句话总结：** 通过对比激活加法（CAA）与单个稀疏自编码器（SAE）特征对六个开源多语言模型（Gemma 3 / Llama 3.1 / Qwen3 / Qwen3.5）进行因果干预，本文证明否定语义由跨语言共享的线性方向编码：英语提取的否定方向可在越/法/汉翻译中诱导或移除否定（跨语言迁移翻转率平均 80.0–95.1%），且每个模型仅需一个 SAE 特征即可跨四种语言控制极性（最高 ≥91%），并能泛化到俄/印地/阿拉伯/印尼语与 FLORES 自然句。

> **TL;DR:** Across six open models (Gemma 3 1B/4B/12B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-9B, Llama 3.1-8B), a negation direction extracted from English via Contrastive Activation Addition flips translation polarity in Vietnamese/French/Chinese at 48–100% (addition) and 77–99% (removal) on the authors' Mini-minus-4 minimal-pair test set, versus 0% addition / ≤8% removal for norm-matched random directions — and a single SAE feature per model (e.g., Gemma-3-4B layer-4 feature 331) sustains ≥91% flip rates across all four languages, also generalizing to four unseen languages (RU/HI/AR/ID, ≥85.6/88.0% for dense CAA) and to FLORES-minus-4 (216 FLORES-200-derived naturalistic sentences). Strong causal evidence that multilingual LLMs encode negation in a largely shared, controllable representation.

## 核心贡献

- **因果层面验证否定表示的跨语言共享性**：此前的 SAE 研究（Brinkmann et al., 2025）只发现否定相关特征但报告跨语言共享性弱、且未检验因果作用；本文通过跨语言激活干预直接证明——从一种语言提取的否定方向能够在其他语言中诱导或移除否定。
- **构建并开源两个互补数据集**：Mini-minus-4（四种语言各 350 对最小对比句对，谓词不重叠地划分为 150 提取/100 评估/100 测试）与 FLORES-minus-4（由 FLORES-200 devtest 经三阶段 GPT 流水线构造的 216 条自然语句否定翻译集）。
- **否定编码于稀疏特征**：每个模型只需一个 SAE 特征（如 Gemma-3-4B 的 L4 f331、Qwen3.5-9B 的 L10 f43677）即可跨四种语言双向控制极性，且该特征选择性地对否定输入激活——其中 Gemma 的特征表现为跨语言否定标记检测器。
- **泛化性检验**：密集方向与单个稀疏特征均能零重选地泛化到四个未见语言（俄语、印地语、阿拉伯语、印尼语）及 FLORES 自然句。
- **方法学副产品**：发现 Gemma 模型中的巨型激活维度（MAD）会虚假抬高跨语言余弦相似度（Gemma-3-4B 原始均值 0.960 → 置零后 0.643），但不影响因果干预效果；并证明"线性可解码 ≠ 因果可控"（探针高精度层范围宽，最佳干预层集中）。

## Key Contributions

- Causally establish that negation representations are shared across languages: directions extracted from one language (via CAA) induce or remove negation in the others during translation — going beyond prior correlational SAE findings (Brinkmann et al., 2025) that reported weak cross-lingual sharing and no causal test.
- Release two complementary datasets: Mini-minus-4 (350 minimal contrastive pairs per language in EN/VI/FR/ZH, predicate-disjoint 150/100/100 extraction/evaluation/test splits, one negation regime per language) and FLORES-minus-4 (216 verified polarity-flipped parallel sentences derived from FLORES-200 devtest via a three-stage GPT-5.6 pipeline).
- Show negation is encoded in sparse features: a single SAE feature per model (selected from pretrained releases: Gemma Scope 2, Qwen SAE-Res, Llama Scope) suffices to control polarity across all four languages while responding selectively to negated inputs.
- Demonstrate generalization: both dense directions and single SAE features transfer to four unseen languages (RU/HI/AR/ID) and to naturalistic FLORES-derived sentences without any re-selection.
- Methodological contributions: identify massive-activation dimensions (MADs) in Gemma models that inflate cross-lingual cosine similarity (mean 0.960 → 0.643 after zeroing in Gemma-3-4B) without affecting causal steering (≤0.7 pp mean flip-rate change), and document that linear decodability does not equal causal control.

## Methodology

The study operates on decoder-only Transformer residual streams. For each of the six instruction-tuned models, activations are extracted at two token positions — the negated predicate (NP) and the final source token (FST) — and a layer sweep covers 10–55% of model depth, selecting one (l, p) pair per model on the English evaluation set (all selected at NP: Gemma-3-1B L8, Gemma-3-4B L10, Gemma-3-12B L17, Qwen3-4B L13, Qwen3.5-9B L9, Llama-3.1-8B L10).

**Dense directions (CAA).** The negation direction is the mean difference of residual activations between negative and positive members of Mini-minus-4 minimal pairs: d_CAA = (1/N)Σ(h_neg − h_pos), computed over 150 extraction pairs. During zero-shot translation generation, h′ = h ± α·d_CAA is applied to all source-sentence tokens with |α| ∈ {2,4,6}; α > 0 adds negation, α < 0 removes it. α is tuned per language and flip mode on the evaluation set.

**Sparse features (SAE steering).** Features are ranked by δf = μ_neg,f − μ_pos,f at the NP position; a steering vector d_SAE = Σ_{f∈F_K} δf·W_dec[f] combines top-K decoder vectors (K=10 for layer selection, K=1 for the final feature). Because CAA and SAE vectors differ in norm, the SAE coefficient is norm-matched to the best EN CAA coefficient at the same (l, p).

**Evaluation (Flip Rate).** A flip counts only if the output (1) exhibits the intended polarity (rule-based marker detection on Mini-minus-4; blind GPT-5.6-Terra judge on FLORES-minus-4), (2) is in the correct target language (fastText), (3) is non-degenerate (token count/unique-ratio thresholds), and (4) preserves content (chrF ≥ 30 against the model's own opposite-polarity baseline translation, or LLM-judged meaning preservation). Items whose un-intervened baseline mistranslates polarity are excluded as ineligible. Flip rates were human-validated on Gemma-3-4B: 96.5% agreement with the rule-based evaluator on Mini-minus-4 (PABAK 0.930), 93.7% with the LLM judge on FLORES-minus-4 (PABAK 0.874). Main experiments use four translation directions (EN→VI, VI→EN, FR→EN, ZH→EN); a target-language ablation shows varying the target changes extracted directions negligibly (pairwise cosine 0.9896–0.9986), so only the source language is varied.

## Results & Highlights

- **Within-language causal control (Table 1, Mini-minus-4 test, addition/removal %):** negation directions flip polarity at high rates, e.g., Gemma-3-4B reaches 98.0/88.0 on EN, 83.0/95.9 on VI, 92.0/94.0 on FR, 99.0/97.9 on ZH; Qwen3.5-9B reaches 98/93 (EN), 97/93.9 (VI), 95/98 (FR), 99/94.5 (ZH). The weakest cells are Qwen3-4B FR addition at 60.0%, Gemma-3-1B VI addition at 65.0%, and Llama-3.1-8B VI removal at 74.5% — versus a norm-matched random-direction control that achieves 0% addition everywhere and ≤8% removal (median over 5 seeds, Appendix Table 7).
- **Cross-language transfer (Figure 3):** a single EN-extracted direction applied to the other three languages achieves 48–100% addition and 77–99% removal rates; averaged across all 12 off-diagonal transfer directions per model, mean transfer rates are 80.0–95.1% for addition and 79.7–94.7% for removal. Transfer is strongest for Gemma-3-4B/12B and Qwen3.5-9B (d_EN: 93–100% addition, 88–99% removal on non-EN languages; Qwen3.5-9B off-diagonal mean 95.1% vs 97.2% diagonal for addition); the weakest link is addition into VI from French directions (45–81%) and Llama's d_FR removal on EN/VI/ZH (43%/61%/49%).
- **Direction alignment (Figure 6):** per-language negation directions at the same (l, p) are all positively aligned with pairwise cosine 0.48–0.82 after zeroing Gemma's massive-activation dimensions — which otherwise inflate similarity to 0.960 (Gemma-3-4B) / 0.965 (Gemma-3-12B) while carrying 83.9–93.2% of the direction's squared norm in a single dimension (2093–2198× the median dimension); removing the MAD changes steering flip rates by only 0.4–0.7 pp on average.
- **Single SAE feature suffices (Table 2, addition/removal %):** Gemma-3-4B L4 f331 achieves ≥91% in both modes across all four languages (96.0/91.0 EN, 91.0/95.9 VI, 97.0/95.0 FR, 95.0/94.8 ZH) and Qwen3.5-9B L10 f43677 ≥93% (97/95, 93/93.9, 99/97, 96/97.8); Llama-3.1-8B L13 f12611 is strong on EN/FR/ZH (83.8/91.0, 87.0/92.0, 90.0/82.3) but drops to 72.0/57.1 on VI — explained by the feature firing weakly on Vietnamese sources. Activation inspection shows three distinct profiles: Gemma f331 as a cross-lingual negation-marker detector (fires on not/không/pas/不), Llama f12611 on tokens in negated contexts, Qwen f43677 on both.
- **Generalization (Table 3):** on unseen languages RU/HI/AR/ID, EN-derived dense directions maintain ≥85.6/88.0% addition/removal across all models (Gemma-3-4B: 97–100% addition), and single SAE features maintain ≥86.9% for Gemma-3-4B and Qwen3.5-9B (Llama ≥77.2%). On naturalistic FLORES-minus-4, removal stays robust (SAE ≥91.6% for all models/languages; CAA ≥86.5% except Llama VI at 65.0%), while Llama's addition weakens (58.4% VI, 62.6% ZH) versus ≥84.2% for Gemma-3-4B and Qwen3.5-9B.
- **Beyond explicit markers:** among 44 ZH negative examples that baselines translate via lexical antonyms rather than explicit markers, subtracting the negation direction still reverses meaning in 68.2% (e.g., 不愿意 "unwilling" → "want"); baseline negation preservation itself is near-perfect (e.g., 100/94 positive/negative preserved out of 100 for Gemma-3-4B ZH).

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans Nanyang Technological University (Giang Son Nguyen, Wenya Wang), VinUniversity (Wray Buntine, Dung D. Le), and Monash University (Buntine). Buntine is a well-known probabilistic-ML researcher; Wang publishes regularly on interpretability/multilingual NLP. The work is funded by Singapore's NRF and MOE grants and the VinUniversity Cross-College Grant. The paper is unusually careful by interpretability standards: a norm-matched random-direction control, a target-language ablation, massive-activation-dimension analysis, blind A/B LLM judging, and a three-annotator human validation with reported precision/recall and κ/PABAK. Data and code are released (anonymous.4open.science). All experiments ran on a single A100 40GB, indicating modest compute and good reproducibility odds given the released assets.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Solid rather than groundbreaking. The recipe (contrastive mean-difference directions + steering + SAE features) follows established templates (Rimsky et al. 2024; refusal-direction work), but applying it cross-lingually to negation during translation, with transfer matrices and feature-level causal tests, is a genuine increment over Brinkmann et al. (2025), who found only weak cross-lingual sharing and did no causal testing.
- **Rigor:** The eligibility filter (excluding items whose baseline already mistranslates polarity) is the right call, but it makes flip rates conditional on near-perfect baseline translation, which the synthetic single-clause sentences almost guarantee; the gap between 96.5% Mini-minus-4 and 93.7% FLORES-minus-4 human-agreement, with the authors themselves noting FLORES flip rates "may be subject to upward bias" (106 false positives vs 3 false negatives in the LLM judge audit), deserves more weight — roughly 6% of reported FLORES flips may be judge artifacts.
- **Data quirks:** Both datasets are GPT-generated and GPT-verified (GPT-5.4-mini for Mini-minus-4; GPT-5.6-Luna/Terra for FLORES-minus-4), and each language is deliberately pinned to one negation regime (excluding ZH 没/别/未, VI chưa/chẳng, FR jamais/plus/rien, EN do-support). This removes exactly the typological diversity that makes negation interesting, so "largely shared across languages" may really mean "shared across one carefully normalized negator per language" — the paper concedes morphological and implicit negation are future work.
- **Coverage gaps:** Human validation covers only Gemma-3-4B; the SAE analysis covers only three models with public pretrained SAEs (base-model SAEs applied to Llama/Qwen instruct checkpoints), and feature interpretation rests on a single feature per model — a selection bias the paper acknowledges. Weak cells (Qwen3-4B FR addition 60.0%, Llama SAE VI removal 57.1%, Llama FLORES VI CAA removal 65.0%) are reported honestly but soften the abstract's "largely shared" claim.
- **Reproducibility:** Good — code/data released, greedy decoding, fixed seeds, 1×A100 budget, named SAE checkpoints with HuggingFace links. The α ∈ {2,4,6} sweep is small but stated plainly.
- **Red flags:** None serious. Minor overclaiming in the phrase "substantially aligned" for post-correction cosines of 0.48–0.82 (0.48 is modest), and the headline TL;DR-level numbers (93–100%) apply to the three best models, not all six.

## Strengths

- Causal, intervention-based evidence rather than correlational probing, with a clean random-direction control that anchors the effect (0% vs 60–100% addition).
- Two complementary datasets isolating controlled minimal pairs from naturalistic sentences, with predicate-disjoint splits preventing leakage.
- Honest reporting of per-model/per-language failures (VI and FR transfer weaknesses, Llama's SAE feature under-firing on Vietnamese) instead of only aggregate means.
- The massive-activation-dimension analysis is a valuable methodological caution for anyone computing cross-lingual cosine similarities in Gemma-style models.
- The K=1 SAE result — one interpretable feature per model controlling polarity in four languages, verified to fire on negation markers — is a crisp, quotable monosemanticity datapoint.

## Limitations

- Restricted to explicit verbal negation with a single marker per language; morphological (un-, in-), implicit (fail, lack), and multi-marker negation are untested.
- Translation is the only task; whether the same directions govern negation in QA, reasoning, or classification is unknown.
- Steering strength α is selected empirically from a 3-value sweep — a known weakness of activation-addition methods that the authors inherit.
- SAE conclusions depend on third-party pretrained releases (three models only) and one hand-picked feature per model; base-vs-instruct SAE transfer is asserted from flip-rate success rather than independently validated.
- Dataset scale is small (100 test pairs per language; 216 FLORES items), and the synthetic sentences are short single clauses, so long-range or multi-clause negation scope is out of scope.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the shared negation direction survive languages whose negation is morphological (Turkish -me, Japanese -nai) or double-marked (Spanish no…ninguno), rather than the analytic one-marker regimes studied here?
- Is the negation direction causally active outside translation — e.g., can it flip answers on negation-heavy QA benchmarks ( NegBench-style) where LLMs are known to fail?
- Why does the Llama SAE feature under-fire on Vietnamese: tokenizer granularity, training-data frequency, or genuinely language-specific encoding?
- Does steering with the negation direction degrade translation quality (COMET/chrF) monotonically with α, and can a norm/principled α selection remove the sweep?
- Do the EN-extracted direction and per-language directions converge to the same circuit (attention heads/MLPs), or do mid-layer shared directions coexist with language-specific implementations upstream?
