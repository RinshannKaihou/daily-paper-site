Key finding distilled from TL;DR · 核心贡献 arXiv 2610.34158

First last-iterate convergence guarantee for the practical Muon optimizer — and exact proofs of when it fails.

首次为实际部署的「有限次牛顿–舒尔茨（NS）迭代」Muon 建立 last-iterate（全序列）梯度收敛理论：ε=0 时永久二循环；ε>0 配小步长即保证梯度平方可和；随机噪声下须递减步长＋递增动量方能收敛。

Key metric Results · Prop 4.1
≥1∕4

At any fixed (η, β) under persistent gradient noise, the chance the gradient stays above threshold never dies: lim sup P(|∇F(wt)| ≥ 0.01·ηd₀) ≥ 1/4. The stochastic noise floor.

Why this matters Relevance

Muon already pretrains trillion-parameter models (Kimi K2). This paper yields exact safety criteria — which hyperparameters oscillate or stall — and what to monitor.

对大规模训练中优化器失稳的诊断、超参安全域选择与逐迭代梯度范数监控（而非滑窗平均）有直接的理论指导价值。

Critical edge Critical assessment · Limitations

The stochastic cure demands an increasing-momentum schedule no LLM pretraining run uses — constant hyperparameters, the practically relevant regime, provably do not converge.

The ε=0 cycling hazard (Prop 3.1) is also one every cited implementation has already sidestepped — theory formalizing a fixed bug.

Methodology distilled
Mt = βtMt−1 + (1−βt)Gt
Nt = θtMt + (1−θt)Gt · θ=1 EMA / θ=β Nesterov
Dt = Φε(Nt) · J=5 quintic NS steps
Wt+1 = Wt − ηtDt
  • Minimal assumptions. One matrix parameter W ∈ ℝm×n; the loss is only L-smooth and lower-bounded — no PL condition, no star-convexity. Φε runs the quintic P(s)=3.4445s−4.7750s³+2.0315s⁵ on N/(∥N∥F+ε).
  • The key geometric input. For ε>0 the finite-NS map satisfies the sector inequality ⟨N, Φε(N)⟩ ≥ (ε/uJ)∥Φε(N)∥²F — precisely what the stabilizer buys.
  • Deterministic proof. A coupled descent + momentum-tracking Lyapunov function; tracking error contracts by (1+β)/2 < 1 per step.
  • Stochastic proof. Momentum rising to 1 averages noise over a tq memory while t−p steps induce drift; the admissible window is exactly where the η-weighted tracking sum converges — balanced q=2p/3 explains the p>¾ threshold.
  • Rates. Dyadic counting turns energy summability into density-one rates; a smooth sparse-peak objective (bumps at times ek) kills every pointwise polynomial rate.

Strengths
  • Explains why the ε stabilizer exists: it converts alignment into the sector inequality powering the entire Lyapunov argument — a takeaway transferable far beyond this paper.
  • Both negative results are constructive, elementary, fully explicit — the positive theorems are demonstrably non-vacuous.
  • Analyzes both input conventions real implementations use (EMA θ=1 and Nesterov-style θ=β), not a convenient special case.
  • The ¾<p≤1 window follows from a transparent noise-averaging vs drift trade-off (q=2p/3), not an artifact.
  • Honest positioning: 14-entry comparison table; step-size conservatism acknowledged and complemented by exact local analysis.
Limitations
  • Zero experiments — not even a minutes-long simulation of the two-cycle or noise floor; Figure 1 is a hand-drawn schematic.
  • Single matrix parameter, exact arithmetic; no weight decay, no RMS output scaling — Moonlight / Kimi K2 variants add both.
  • Constant-hyperparameter stochastic training — the practically relevant regime — provably does not converge.
  • All rates asymptotic (density-one); no finite-time bounds, never contrasted with prior average-iterate rates.
  • Counterexamples are 1-D; persistence of the obstructions in matrix-valued dynamics is open.
Questions for further exploration
  • Q1Do the ε=0 two-cycle and the noise floor survive genuine matrix dynamics (m×n, interacting singular values)?
  • Q2Can last-iterate stationarity cover the full practical stack — weight decay, RMS output scaling, per-layer scaling?
  • Q3Inside the admissible region, which (p, q) optimizes finite-time decay; does balanced q=2p/3 stay near-optimal?
  • Q4Can gradient clipping or restarts upgrade density-one rates to high-probability pointwise rates?
  • Q5Do the obstructions appear in real LLM training curves — and can ε/uJ be monitored online as a stability certificate?
  • Q6How does increasing-momentum noise filtering interact with correlated noise across data-parallel shards?
