> **一句话总结：** 本文用动力系统方法分析"动能映射"类物理启发优化器越过稳定性边缘（EoS）后的行为：给出周期二轨道线性稳定的充要条件（弹性 ε∈(0,1)），证明 VRAdam 的阻尼器在 2 倍临界学习率以上失稳（Legendre 对偶映射则全局稳定），并用 Neishtadt 缓慢扫描分岔理论定量解释了网络训练中稳定性丢失的延迟现象（网络实验与理论预测的出口点误差 ≤0.010，无拟合参数）；但在 ResNet-18 上，匹配有效学习率的对照实验表明这些映射的大部分表观收益来自学习率变化本身。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author physics-style analysis of kinetic-map optimizers (updates u = ∇K(q), including VRAdam's damper) beyond the edge of stability. In a reduced model, the symmetric period-two orbit is linearly stable iff the map's elasticity lies in (0,1) — VRAdam's non-convex damper fails above twice the critical step while its Legendre-dual map never does, confirmed on an exact quadratic (Fashion-MNIST least squares, 7,850 params) to relative error 1.7e-9 and in a full-batch CIFAR-10 MLP (median effective ratio Reff = 1.006 over 178 above-edge runs vs 0.64 for VRAdam). The delayed loss of stability matches Neishtadt's entry–exit relation in a controlled MLP sweep without fitted parameters. But on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, Kinetic Muon's +0.17 pp accuracy gain over tuned Muon (94.19% vs 94.02%) shrinks to a non-significant +0.10 pp against a matched-effective-learning-rate control, and a 51.5M GPT-2 model shows all methods within 0.032 nats — so the dynamics are rigorous, the practical optimizer gains are mostly a learning-rate effect.

## 核心贡献

- **动能映射框架与精确稳定性判据**：把物理启发优化器统一为"动能映射"（步长是动量的径向动能的梯度 u = ψ(q) = q·g(‖q‖/c)），定义弹性 ε(z) = d ln ϕ / d ln z（步长对动量的双对数斜率）。定理 1 证明：最小值失稳后唯一存在的对称周期二轨道严格线性稳定当且仅当 0 < ε* < 1；且凸性（K 凸 ⟺ ϕ 单调）正是分界——非凸映射必然失稳。
- **有效稳定性边缘（Effective EoS）定律**：在轨道上，步长乘子恰好补偿过剩锐度，ηλg(z*)/aEoS = 1（推论 2）。凸映射让训练稳定在"有效边缘"，锐度可超过普通边缘 1/g 倍；给出五种映射（VR、quartic-Legendre、cubic-Legendre、相对论式、裁剪）的闭式振幅与弹性。
- **对 VRAdam 的诊断**：VRAdam 的阻尼器（η = α₀/(1+β₃‖m‖²)）是其自身拉格朗日量 L = v²/2 + v⁴/4 的非凸近似，其轨道在 2 倍临界步长处经音叉分岔失稳，之后周期倍增直至混沌（首个正 Lyapunov 指数在 a≈5.32）；而正确构造的 Legendre 对偶映射在任意步长都稳定。
- **延迟失稳的定量验证**：锐度渐进上升使分岔参数缓慢扫过阈值，失稳按 Neishtadt 动态分岔理论延迟；在受控扫描的 tanh MLP（Fashion-MNIST）中，线性理论以零拟合参数复现网络动力学，出口点服从入口-出口关系，噪声按预测抹除记忆。
- **全局吸引与弹性需求**：局部稳定不保证到达轨道。动量系数 β₁ 越大，所需的大动量弹性越高：只有 cubic-Legendre（ε→1/2）在所有测试配置（含 β₁=0.99、50 倍边缘步长）吸引全部采样初值；限速映射（相对论式、裁剪）在 β₁=0.9 就大量落入周期四或混沌吸引子。
- **诚实的对照实验**：ResNet-18 上用匹配有效学习率的对照消解了大部分表观收益，并给出负结果（grokking 与损失尖峰无关）。

## Key Contributions

- **Kinetic-map framework with an exact stability criterion.** Physics-inspired optimizers are unified as kinetic maps u = ψ(q) = q·g(‖q‖/c) (the gradient of a radial kinetic energy), with elasticity ε(z) = d ln ϕ/d ln z as the key invariant. Theorem 1: beyond the stability threshold there is exactly one symmetric period-two orbit (y,v) → (−y,−v); it is strictly linearly stable iff 0 < ε* < 1, and unstable iff ε* < 0 (non-convex K). The Jacobian at the orbit has characteristic polynomial ν² − (1+b)(1−2ε*)ν + b, independent of everything else.
- **Effective edge of stability (Corollary 2).** On the orbit the step multiplier exactly compensates the excess sharpness: g(z*) = aEoS/a, i.e. Reff = ηλg(z*)/aEoS = 1. Convex maps therefore hold training at an effective edge where the curvature can exceed the plain threshold by the factor 1/g. Closed forms for amplitude and elasticity are derived for five maps: VR (VRAdam's damper), quartic-Legendre (ε→1/3, the correct velocity of VRAdam's own Lagrangian), cubic-Legendre (ε→1/2), relativistic (ε→0, bounded speed), and norm clipping (ε=0, neutral).
- **Diagnosis of VRAdam.** VRAdam's damper is a non-convex member of the family (K = c²log(1+z²), speed decreasing for z>1); its orbit loses stability at twice the threshold step via a symmetry-breaking pitchfork, followed by period doubling and chaos (first positive Lyapunov exponent at a ≈ 5.32 without momentum; Floquet radius > 1 from a/aEoS = 2.02 at β₁ = 0.9). Its floor (α₁) cannot rescue this. The Legendre dual of VRAdam's own Lagrangian is convex and passes the test at every learning rate — a concrete "how to fix it" result.
- **Delayed instability via Neishtadt's theory (Section 4).** Because progressive sharpening sweeps the bifurcation parameter R = ηλmax/aEoS slowly through R = 1, the loss of stability is delayed: the exit point obeys the entry–exit relation ∫Λ(R)dR = 0, depends only on where the sweep started (Rout = 1.080 for Rin = 0.5; 1.118 for Rin = 0.1), and is erased by noise with delay growing as ρ·log(xc/σ). A controlled experiment in a tanh MLP verifies this quantitatively with zero fitted parameters.
- **Global attraction requires elasticity at large momentum (Section 5).** Local stability does not guarantee reaching the orbit: with momentum, trajectories can end on period-four, higher-period, or chaotic attractors. Across 300 initial conditions, six β₁ values (0 to 0.99) and steps up to 50× the edge, only maps with elasticity p ≥ 1/2 attracted every sampled start; saturating maps (relativistic, clipping) lose the orbit already at β₁ = 0.9 (worst share 0.27 once A ≥ 4).
- **Adam-specific analysis and honest benchmarks.** Proposition 7: a map on Adam's preconditioned direction is amplitude-blind (q → ±κ independent of oscillation size Y), unlike a map on the raw first moment. ResNet-18 experiments with matched effective-learning-rate controls show most of the apparent gain of the Adam map (and about half of Kinetic Muon's) is a learning-rate effect; a grokking test is a clean negative result.

## Methodology

The paper works in three layers of increasing realism, each with a deterministic check. (1) **Reduced model**: one eigen-direction of a quadratic with curvature λ, EMA momentum b = β₁, and a kinetic map acting on the momentum — v′ = bv + (1−b)y, y′ = y − aϕ(v′) with a = ηλ. Classical bifurcation analysis (Jury criterion on the period-two orbit's Jacobian) yields the elasticity criterion and closed-form amplitudes. (2) **Exact quadratic at network scale**: linear least squares on 2,000 real Fashion-MNIST images (W ∈ R^785×10, λmax = 300.4, Hessian constant, no self-stabilization), where the closed forms can be verified to machine precision. (3) **Neural networks**: a full-batch tanh MLP (3072-128-128-10) and ResNet-32 (batch norm without running statistics, four fixed blocks) on a 1,000-image CIFAR-10 subset, tracking λmax via Lanczos/power iteration and per-tensor step multipliers; the effective ratio is measured mode-weighted as Reff = R·Σ_B g_B‖u_B‖². The delayed-instability test imposes a controlled linear ramp R_t = Rin + ρt by setting η_t from a live eigenvalue estimate, kicks a twin network along the top Hessian eigenvector, and compares the deviation against the exact linear recurrence on the measured curvature path — no fitted coefficients. The delay theory comes from Neishtadt's theory of dynamical (slowly swept) bifurcations. Optimizer benchmarks use equal tuning budgets (10 random-search trials per method, 3–5 fresh seeds) plus matched-effective-learning-rate controls (paired t-tests, 5 shared seeds), on a Fashion-MNIST CNN, a character-level transformer, ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, a 51.5M GPT-2-style model on FineWeb-Edu, and modular-addition grokking.

## Results & Highlights

- **Exact quadratic (7,850 params, Fashion-MNIST):** plain momentum diverges in 39/39 runs above the edge (µ > 1); the quartic- and cubic-Legendre maps reach the symmetric orbit in 39/39 and 39/39 runs with amplitudes matching the closed forms to relative error 1.7×10⁻⁹ and 3.2×10⁻⁸. The VR map reaches the orbit in only 12/15 runs even inside its stability range 1<µ<2 and is chaotic beyond (alternation index 0.11 for µ ≥ 2.25, vs 1.0 on the orbit); the locally-stable relativistic map reaches the orbit in 0/24 runs with µ ≥ 2 (17 end on a period-four orbit with Reff ≈ 0.5).
- **Controlled delay test (tanh MLP, 81 ramps):** the network follows the linear recurrence while the top-mode deviation decays by up to 8 orders of magnitude and grows back — the median deviation from theory is 0.041% of the local amplitude (at most 2% for ρ ≥ 0.003), and the exit occurs at the same step in network and model in every run. At ρ = 0.003, a sweep entering at Rin = 0.1 exits at Rout = 1.115 (Neishtadt prediction 1.118) and one entering at 0.9 exits at 1.020 (prediction 1.029). With noise, σ = 10⁻⁹ preserves the memory (exit 1.090→1.020 across entries) while σ = 10⁻⁷ pins the exit at 1.05–1.06 for all entries; 42/45 (kick+noise) and 43/45 (noise-only) exits fall in the model's 10–90% range.
- **Full-batch CIFAR-10 MLP (450 runs):** over the 178 runs that spend time above the plain edge, the convex maps sit on the effective-edge line with per-run median Reff = 1.006 (IQR 1.000–1.018); in the showcased quartic run the sharpness reaches 1.75× the plain edge while Reff stays within 0.99–1.02. The VR map instead gets caught on its non-convex branch: Reff = 0.64 (IQR 0.34–0.87) with sharpness above 5× the edge. Plain momentum diverges in 20/20 runs at µ ≥ 4; no kinetic-map run with the absolute scale diverged at any µ up to 8.
- **Global attraction (reduced model):** at β₁ = 0.9 the share of 300 starts reaching the orbit is ≥ 0.98 whenever orbit elasticity ε* ≥ 0.34 and ≤ 0.32 whenever ε* < 0.25; at β₁ = 0.99 the quartic map keeps only 0.76 and p = 1/4 maps keep 0.01, while the cubic map (ε→1/2) keeps 1.00 in every tested configuration.
- **ResNet-32 (135 runs):** at µ ≥ 2 plain momentum diverges (5/10) or catapults to a constant predictor (final loss 0.060–0.090 vs 0.09 for predicting the class mean); all 40/40 runs with any map at the absolute scale trained, vs only 2/40 at the relative scale. Here the map acts as an early brake on descent momentum, not as an edge regulator — the transient is too fast for the orbit to form.
- **ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 (5 seeds, paired):** Kinetic Muon beats tuned Muon by +0.17 ± 0.13 pp accuracy (94.19% vs 94.02%, p = 0.04) and −0.016 cross-entropy (p = 0.03), but against Muon run at Kinetic Muon's effective learning rates the difference shrinks to +0.10 ± 0.20 pp (95% CI −0.15 to +0.34, p = 0.33). The quartic Kinetic-Adam gain (+0.55 ± 0.28 pp over AdamW's 92.36%) is nearly reproduced by simply running AdamW at 3× its tuned learning rate (+0.50 ± 0.23 pp).
- **Small GPT (51.5M, FineWeb-Edu):** at the shared best learning rate the final validation losses of AdamW, clipping, VRAdam and the cubic map span only 0.032 nats, against a 0.026-nat seed spread for AdamW alone — no kinetic map improves the best loss beyond seed noise. VRAdam's one distinct advantage: on an MLP it is the only method that still learns at ~30× AdamW's best learning rate.
- **Grokking (negative result):** clipping and the cubic map cut the median largest post-memorization training loss ~4-fold on modular addition but did not significantly delay grokking (3 seeds).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The framing of physics-inspired optimizers as kinetic maps follows Hamiltonian descent (Maddison et al. 2018) and Lion-K/Cautious lines; the genuinely new pieces are the elasticity criterion for EoS period-two orbits (a clean, checkable invariant), the identification of VRAdam's damper as a non-convex member with a provable failure at 2× the edge step, and the first quantitative application of Neishtadt's swept-bifurcation delay to EoS training. The physics connections (ponderomotive force as averaged self-stabilization, capture into resonance for the β₁→1 limit) are stimulating but mostly programmatic.
- **Rigor.** Strong on the theory side: theorems with proofs, closed forms verified to 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁹ relative error on an exact quadratic, and a zero-fitted-parameter network test of the delay theory (median deviation 0.041%). The honesty about global attraction being only numerically established (Observation 6 / "Theorem 6 is numerical, not proved") is commendable. Weaker spots: the "share reaching the orbit" is an empirical attraction fraction under one specific sampling law (log-uniform |y₀| in [10⁻⁴, 30], v₀ = 0), not a basin measure — the paper says so, but readers may over-generalize.
- **Reproducibility.** Good in principle (deterministic full-batch settings, seeds and budgets stated, code promised), but at review time no code link is given. The LLM-assistance disclosure is unusually candid; given the verification-style numerics (machine-precision matches to closed forms), the risk of fabricated results seems low.
- **Significance.** As optimizer engineering, the payoff is deliberately deflated by the paper's own controls: on ResNet-18 the matched-effective-lr control explains most of the Adam-map effect and about half of Kinetic Muon's +0.17 pp, with the remainder "not resolved by five seeds"; on the GPT benchmark everything is within seed noise. The durable contribution is diagnostic understanding (why a damper fails, when the edge orbit forms, why loss of stability lags) rather than a better optimizer.
- **Red flags / caveats.** (i) The ResNet-18 control rows reuse the tuned-comparison seeds, so they are "not an independent replication" (paired, not fresh). (ii) Benchmarks are small — 1,000-image CIFAR subsets, a 51.5M GPT, 10 random-search trials — no ImageNet or production-scale LM; claims about what happens at scale are extrapolation. (iii) The theory freezes Adam's preconditioner and is full-batch; minibatch noise enters only as an injected isotropic perturbation in the delay test. (iv) The cubic map, the theory's most robust map, trained worse than the quartic/relativistic maps in the MLP — the paper's recommendations rest partly on this acknowledged tension. (v) The grokking negative result uses only 3 seeds per method, so "no effect" is weakly powered. (vi) Single-author + heavy LLM assistance means fewer independent eyes than a typical team paper, though the disclosure mitigates this.

## Strengths

- A single invariant — elasticity ε — organizes the whole zoo of physics-inspired dampers and yields an exact, falsifiable stability criterion, with closed-form orbit amplitudes confirmed to relative error ~10⁻⁹ on a real-data quadratic.
- The Neishtadt delay test is exemplary experimental dynamics: an imposed sweep in a real network, a prediction with zero fitted parameters, agreement to ~0.04% median, plus a clean demonstration that noise erases the entry-point memory (σ from 10⁻⁹ to 10⁻⁵).
- Intellectual honesty is the paper's signature: matched effective-learning-rate controls that deflate its own headline gains, an explicit negative grokking result, and a limitations section that concedes the theory governs transients, not end states, in nonlinear networks.
- The VRAdam diagnosis is actionable: the non-convexity is located precisely (z > 1), the failure threshold is exact (A = 4), and the Legendre dual of the same Lagrangian fixes it.
- Practical recommendations (per-tensor maps on the raw first moment, absolute scales small enough to engage early, report step multipliers and effective learning rates) follow directly from the analysis rather than from benchmark lore.

## Limitations

- The reduced model freezes the preconditioner and ignores minibatch noise, batch-norm scale invariance, and the large-amplitude cycles reachable from large initial momenta; the global-attraction "theorem" is numerical.
- In nonlinear networks, self-stabilization returns the sharpness to the plain edge, so the orbit theory governs the transient only (except the cubic map in the MLP, which ends at R up to 3.32); in ResNet-32 the transient is too fast for the orbit to form at all, so the map is an early brake, not an edge regulator.
- Practical gains are modest or non-significant after controls: Kinetic Muon +0.17 pp on ResNet-18 shrinks to +0.10 pp (p = 0.33) against matched effective learning rates; on the GPT benchmark all methods are within 0.032 nats; only VRAdam's extreme-learning-rate robustness (~30× AdamW's best lr) stands out.
- All experiments are small-scale (1,000-image subsets, 51.5M parameters, 3–5 seeds per setting, 10-trial tuning budgets); no evidence at LLM-training scale, and the stochastic (minibatch) regime — the practically relevant one — is only touched via injected noise and one batch-1,000 observation.
- The overshoot of ordinary ResNet-32 training follows the predicted trend (Spearman 0.82 over 15 runs) but is systematically larger than the reduced model predicts, and the authors state they have not identified what sets its size.

## Relevance

与"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"方向高度相关：论文严格刻画了大学习率训练中稳定性丢失的机制（EoS 周期轨道、失稳延迟、阻尼器失效阈值），其"报告步长乘子与有效学习率"的建议、以及对损失尖峰/发散/catapult 塌缩的动力学解释，可直接用于训练不稳定性的监测与归因分析；但实验规模较小，距离生产级 LM 训练还有差距。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the elasticity criterion survive minibatch training at scale — e.g., do Kinetic-Adam variants on the raw first moment delay divergence in real LLM pretraining runs with loss spikes, where the amplitude-blindness of preconditioned maps (Proposition 7) should matter?
- What sets the overshoot size in ordinary (non-swept) ResNet training, where the measured delay exceeds the reduced-model prediction while following its trend (Spearman 0.82)?
- Can the capture-into-resonance picture make the β₁ → 1 basin structure quantitative, predicting which fraction of runs land on period-four/chaotic attractors at practical momentum values?
- Would a convex damper with the right elasticity remove the "slingshot" loss spikes of adaptive optimizers in ways clipping cannot, and does that matter for grokking or LLM training instabilities beyond the null result reported here (which used only 3 seeds)?
- Since matching effective learning rates explains much of the observed gains, could the same control reconcile other recent optimizer wins (e.g., Lion/Cautious-style updates) with plain learning-rate effects?
