of steps that failed while the record claimed success still end in stop / proceed / buy-now — measured across 12 model × domain configurations.

Why this matters

Agents increasingly act via one-pass decision models; when records silently override reality, long autonomous runs break — and a near-free internal readout catches it.

⚠ Critical edge

Live gains rest on injected 30–40% silent-failure rates; natural prevalence is unknown, so headline gains may not reflect deployment.

— Critical Assessment, point 3

Methodology · distilled

Four one-pass typed decision models — decider (fine-tuned Qwen3.5, option-letter logits), Kev (frozen Qwen3.5 + rank-16 LoRA + pointer head), Laya (ModernBERT), and closed-weight hosted Jev — choose between a terminal option a★ (complete / proceed / buy now) and continuing.

Around each of 1,235 decision points, five matched counterfactual variants vary one input at a time, isolating paired effects on log p(a★):

DONEaction logged, screen shows effect
DONE+CLAIM+ explicit success sentence
HONESTnothing logged, screen unchanged
LOGGEDaction logged, no effect
LOGGED+CLAIMfailed action + false success claim

Δobs = DONE − LOGGED  ·  Δlog = LOGGED − HONEST  ·  Δclaim = LOGGED+CLAIM − LOGGED

6,175 states span AndroidControl phone screens (3,200 states, 245 apps), ALFWorld (1,475), and WebShop (1,500). ℓ2-regularized probes are fit at the answer position with GroupKFold holding out entire apps / tasks / goals; activation patching localizes where each input wins. EviGate reads layer 20: one linear readout s = σ(w·ᵀh̃₂₀ + b) — if the model proposes a terminal option while s < τ, it is vetoed for the best non-terminal option. One dot product, no extra forward pass.

The bias is realistic and pre-existing. A Qwen3.5-9B summarizer using T3A's own prompt claims success after 39% of no-effect steps; a false summary raises premature completion by 46–53 points. Base Qwen3.5-4B already follows the claim (+0.51) over the observed outcome (+0.02, n.s.) — decision tuning amplifies it (+1.12).

Calibration domain decides everything. The identical gate fit on offline contrast sets vetoes 95% of live WebShop purchases → 2.5% success, below the ungated baseline; on ALFWorld it matches random veto.

Strengths

  • Causal, counterfactual design — five matched variants, three isolated effects, paired bootstraps in every config — not correlational error counting.
  • Full arc: behavior → representation (probes) → mechanism (layerwise patching) → intervention (EviGate) → control remedy (LoRA, with its transfer failure documented).
  • Deployment realism: latency measured in a production serving engine; fair baselines — second question, random veto at matched rate, contrastive decoding, 9B judge, record deletion, steering.
  • The readout generalizes beyond vetoing: best-of-N verifier picks a successful trajectory 40.0% vs 28.0% for the model's own judgment (N = 16; oracle 44.0%).
  • Unusual honesty: probe inflation in ALFWorld/WebShop, simulator-not-AndroidWorld, and unknown natural prevalence are called out by the authors themselves.

Limitations

  • Live results ride on injected 30–40% silent-failure rates; deployment-time prevalence and gains are unquantified.
  • Phone-domain live evidence is a 24-task text simulator with template records — not a real Android environment or per-step LLM summarizer.
  • EviGate needs mid-layer access (ruling out hosted Jev) plus a few hundred states from the deployed agent itself; offline/synthetic calibration provably does not transfer (WebShop 2.5%).
  • Recovery quality caps gains: WebShop wanders after vetoed purchases (78 → 46); in ALFWorld random veto nearly matches EviGate (58.2 vs 61.2, CI [−1.5, 7.5]).
  • No live-agent ablations on τ threshold, layer choice, or calibration drift; the 39% summarizer false-success rate was auto-judged, not human-verified.

Questions for further exploration

  1. How often do record–state mismatches arise naturally, and does the 0.77–0.80 phone probe AUROC hold at that base rate, where false vetoes are relatively costlier?
  2. Can calibration sensitivity be removed — phrasing-invariant probes, or mid-layer access exposed in hosted decision-model APIs?
  3. Would gating non-terminal decisions catch cascading errors earlier, and how does τ shift as the agent's state distribution drifts?
  4. Does record-over-observation preference appear in general-purpose LLM action selectors, and is it reducible by training-data curation?
  5. Can EviGate combine with a learned recovery policy, given WebShop gains were capped by post-veto wandering?
  6. Is the claim influence spreading into later tokens a mechanistically identifiable circuit — attention heads or recurrent state updates — that could be patched directly?
