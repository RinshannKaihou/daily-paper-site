> **一句话总结：** 本文通过 LoRA 特征更新的"信号–噪声分解"证明，标准 Init[A] 初始化在学习率比 ηB/ηA 增大时会把随机初始化 A0 的噪声与有用信号一起放大，导致高比率崩溃；只需把 A 的初始化方差从 σA²=Θ(1/n) 降到 Θ(1/n²)（Init[A²]，保持 B0=0），即可将初始化噪声能量再压低 1/n 因子，在 Llama 3-8B 算术任务 ρ=4000 时把精度从约 56% 稳定提升到约 77%（+20 个百分点），并让 α/r=12 等激进超参变得可用。

> **TL;DR:** LoRA+ style asymmetric learning rates (ηB ≫ ηA) are brittle under standard Init[A] because ηB amplifies both the useful optimizer signal and a non-vanishing initialization-noise term from the random A0. This NeurIPS 2026 paper proves (via an exact signal–noise decomposition of LoRA features) that simply shrinking A's init variance to σA²=Θ(n⁻²) — Init[A2], keeping B0=0 — suppresses noise energy by an extra 1/n factor while preserving feature learning. On Llama 3-8B arithmetic fine-tuning (10k math examples from LLM-Adapters), at LR ratio ρ=4000 Init[A] stalls at 56.7% while Init[A2] reaches 77.6% (+20.9 pts); at α/r=12 the five-seed gap is 76.9% vs 71.4% (95% CI [4.68, 6.20] pts). The gains are largest in stress regimes; after matched LR retuning the gap narrows to ~1.3 pts, so the main value is a wider stable LR region (e.g., on RoBERTa/SST-2 at β=8: 16/25 vs 8/25 grid cells above 90% accuracy), not uniformly higher peak accuracy.

## 核心贡献

- **识别出非对称学习率的失效机制**：对 LoRA 特征更新做精确的信号–噪声分解（Lemma 1），证明在标准 Init[A]（σA²=Θ(1/n)、B0=0）下，学习后的 B 与随机初始 A0 的交互产生一项不随宽度消失的"初始化噪声"，且该项与有用信号同样被 ηB 放大——这就是 LoRA+ 比率 ρ=ηB/ηA 脆弱、大比率下性能崩溃的根源。
- **提出 Init[A²]**：一个极简的初始化改动——把 A 的初始化方差从 Θ(n⁻¹) 降为 Θ(n⁻²)，保持 B0=0。在不改动 LoRA 架构、目标函数、零初始输出和训练开销的前提下，把初始化噪声能量额外压低 1/n 因子；定理 2 证明其仍满足 LoRA+ 的稳定性与效率条件（feature learning 不受损）。
- **解释 Init[AB] 的内部不稳定**：Init[AB]（对称 LR ηA=ηB=Θ(n^{-1/2})）会使内部特征更新 ∥ΔZA∥∝Θ(√n) 随宽度爆炸，而 Init[A²] 与 Init[A] 一样用 ηA=Θ(n⁻¹) 保持瓶颈内部特征 Θ(1)。
- **大规模实证**：在 MNIST 玩具 MLP、RoBERTa-base（GLUE：MNLI/QQP/SST-2/QNLI）以及 Llama 3-8B / Llama 3.2（1B/3B）、Qwen2.5-3B、Qwen3-0.6B、Mistral-v0.3 的算术与常识生成任务上，Init[A²] 全面拓宽稳定 LR 区域、消除高比率崩溃、减少最优比率漂移。
- **实用副产物**：让大 LoRA 乘子（α/r=10–12）变得可用（Init[A] 在 α/r=3 后退化），并把"宽度启发式 ηB≈nηA"变成有限模型上可用的起点；对 Orthogonal/EVA/PiSSA 等结构化初始化做方差压缩同样能在 ρ=4000 下避免崩溃。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnose the failure mode of asymmetric LRs.** An exact signal–noise decomposition of the LoRA feature update (Lemma 1) splits Z_B^t into an optimizer-driven signal term and an initialization-noise term (learned B interacting with the random A0). Under standard Init[A] (σA² = Θ(1/n), B0 = 0) the noise energy E_N = Θ(ηB²β²m_N²t²) does not vanish with width at fixed rank — so raising ηB amplifies noise along with signal, making large ratios ρ = ηB/ηA, large init scale β, and large multiplier α/r brittle.
- **Propose Init[A2]: shrink A's variance by one extra width factor.** σA² = Θ(n⁻²) with B0 = 0 reduces initialization-noise energy by an additional factor 1/n while preserving the signal's scaling order, the zero initial adapter output, the architecture, and training cost. Theorem 2 shows Init[A2] still satisfies LoRA+'s stability + efficiency (feature-learning) conditions at ηA = Θ(n⁻¹), ηB = Θ(1), whereas Init[A] needs an extra non-cancellation assumption.
- **Explain Init[AB]'s internal instability.** With ηA = ηB = Θ(n^{-1/2}), internal feature updates scale as Θ(√n) and explode with width; Init[A2] keeps ηA = Θ(n⁻¹) so the bottleneck feature Z_A evolves at Θ(1), avoiding the feature-norm blowup seen empirically for Init[AB] at large β.
- **Broad empirical validation.** On a controlled MNIST toy MLP (widths 16→4096), RoBERTa-base on GLUE (MNLI, QQP, SST-2, QNLI), and decoder-only arithmetic/commonsense fine-tuning across Llama 3-8B, Llama 3.2-1B/3B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen3-0.6B, and Mistral-v0.3 (plus QLoRA), Init[A2] widens the stable LR region over β, rank r, ratio ρ, and multiplier α/r, and removes the high-ratio collapse of Init[A].
- **Generalize the recipe to structured initializers.** Variance-compressing the A scale of Orthogonal, EVA, and PiSSA (preserving their chosen directions/low-rank product) rescues them under aggressive asymmetric LRs — empirical evidence that controlling A0's scale, not Gaussianity, is what matters.

## Methodology

The authors study a width-n LoRA layer Z̄ = WZ + (α/r)BAZ with frozen W, rank r ≪ n, and per-matrix learning rates ηA, ηB. Writing updates with a normalized optimizer-direction model (A^{t+1} = A^t − ηA U_A^t, covering SignSGD/Adam/AdamW), they unroll A^t and B^t and obtain an exact decomposition: the one-step feature update ΔZ_B^t = −(αβ/r)ηB U_B^t Ã0 Z + (α/r)√(ηAηB) S^t Z, i.e., a noise term proportional to the random init Ã0 and a signal term S^t built purely from accumulated optimizer updates. Feature energy (width-normalized squared norm) splits into E_N (noise), E_S (signal), and a signed cross term bounded via Cauchy–Schwarz, so E_N/E_S → 0 implies signal dominance. Under rank-contraction assumptions (exponents γN, γS covering LLN-like and CLT-like regimes), Theorem 1 gives E_N = Θ(ηB²β²m_N²t²·σA²) — under Init[A] this stays Θ(1) in width, while Init[A2] (σA² = Θ(n⁻²)) earns a 1/n factor. Robustness (Definition 3) requires sup_t (1/n)‖Z_B^t‖² = O(1) over horizons T(n) = o(√n), justified via Kaplan et al. scaling laws (S ∝ n^{0.08}). A complementary spectral-condition analysis (Appendix A.5) independently recovers the σA² = Θ(n⁻²) choice, and Appendix A.6 explains why γ-calculus alone can miss aligned directions where random init gets amplified. Experiments: MNIST toy MLP with µP-scaled frozen input/output layers (momentum disabled to isolate the clean normalized-update regime); RoBERTa-base + LoRA on q/v projections of GLUE tasks (AdamW, r = α = 8); decoder-only fine-tuning on the LLM-Adapters 10k arithmetic/math and 170k commonsense sets (AdamW, LoRA on q/k/v/up/down projections), sweeping β ∈ {0.25…8}, ρ ∈ {1…4000} (ηA = 1e−5), rank r ∈ {4…128}, and α/r, with QLoRA, Init[A3]/Init[A4], structured-initializer, long-horizon (up to 10 epochs), and 3–5-seed validation runs.

## Results & Highlights

- **High-ratio stability (Llama 3-8B arithmetic, ρ = 4000, β = 1, r = α = 8):** over 1/3/6/10-epoch runs, Init[A] plateaus at 55.06–56.71% accuracy while Init[A2] reaches 75.61–77.57% — a persistent +20.1 to +21.6 point gap (e.g., 3 epochs: 77.57% vs 56.71%, Table 3).
- **Large LoRA multiplier becomes usable (Llama 3-8B arithmetic, β = 0.5, ηB = 2e−3):** Init[A] peaks at α/r = 3 (76.4%) and falls to 72.0% at α/r = 12; Init[A2] improves up to α/r = 10 (77.5%) and holds 77.3% at α/r = 12 (+5.3 pts over Init[A] at 12). Five seeds at ρ = 200: α/r = 12 gives 76.87 ± 0.48% vs 71.44 ± 0.52% for Init[A] (95% CI of difference [4.68, 6.20] pts); at α/r = 1 the CI [−0.28, 0.43] includes zero, i.e., no gain in the mild regime.
- **Collapse and rescue under QLoRA / large β (Llama 3-8B, ρ = 2000):** raising β from 0.5 to 8 under Init[A] crashes accuracy from 71.9% to 1.8% (full-precision LoRA) and 70.7% to 4.5% (QLoRA); Init[A2] reaches 80.2% at β = 0.5 and stays within 77.7–79.3% across all tested β at ratio 2000.
- **Mechanism verified directly (energy decomposition, Math-10K, 3 seeds):** under Init[A] the initialization-noise term contributes 117.2% / 117.4% of final total feature energy on Llama-3.2-1B / Llama-3-8B (with a destructive −22.1%/−25.5% cross term), whereas under Init[A2] noise falls to 3.1% / 1.5% and the optimizer signal dominates at 85.9% / 90.4% — matching the theory's dominance prediction (the asymptotic 1/n rate itself is not verified).
- **Wider usable LR region, not higher peak (RoBERTa-base/SST-2, β = 8, 25-cell LR grid, 5 seeds):** best accuracy is statistically similar (93.53 ± 0.52% vs 93.28 ± 0.64%), but Init[A2] doubles the cells above 90% accuracy (16/25 vs 8/25), raises near-best cells from 5/25 to 8/25, and cuts chance-level cells from 14/25 to 5/25.
- **Cross-family generalization (arithmetic, high-ratio columns):** Init[A] collapses to near-zero accuracy in the high-ρ × large-β corner for Llama 3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen3-0.6B, and Mistral-v0.3; Init[A2] keeps 58–60% (Llama 3.2-1B), 72–74% (Llama 3.2-3B), 57–66% (Qwen2.5-3B), 72–74% (Qwen3-0.6B), and 71–73% (Mistral-v0.3).
- **Structured initializers rescued (Llama 3-8B arithmetic, ρ = 4000, single runs):** Orthogonal, EVA, and PiSSA collapse to 2.49%, 5.10%, and 48.10%; their variance-compressed A2 variants retain 77.74%, 77.19%, and 73.80%. On the MNIST toy example at β = 5, Init[A]'s optimal LR drifts at width n = 4096 while Init[A2] transfers hyperparameters cleanly across widths.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core idea — one extra 1/n in A's init variance — is deliberately minimal, but the signal–noise decomposition that justifies it is a genuinely new lens on why LoRA+'s ratio is brittle; prior work (Hayou et al.'s LoRA+, Li et al.'s Init[AB]) analyzed scaling exponents but not this noise term. The diagnosis is more original than the fix.
- **Rigor:** Theory is careful (exact decomposition identities hold for any update sequence; scaling claims isolated under stated exponents). Empirics include 5-seed CIs for the headline claims and matched LR retuning — the retuned gap at α/r = 12 shrinks to +1.33 pts (76.84 ± 0.31 vs 75.51 ± 0.34, Table 7), which the paper itself concedes; but most LR-ratio sweeps (the heatmaps) are single-run, so cell-level differences should not be over-read (the authors say so explicitly).
- **Reproducibility:** Good — code released, hyperparameters and hardware (NVIDIA L40S, ~1h/run encoder, ~3h/run decoder) documented, and the change is a one-line init edit in PEFT. Results are deterministic sweeps over small grids, easy to replicate.
- **Significance:** The practical payoff is robustness/usability (fewer blowups, wider LR region, ηB ≈ nηA as a usable starting point, α/r > 3 unlocked), not large peak-accuracy gains — best tuned scores are within noise of Init[A] on SST-2 and equal-LR math (Table 9, where Init[A2] trails Init[A] at mid LRs, e.g., 51.48% vs 57.83% at η = 3e−4, β = 1). Engineers doing aggressive LoRA+ sweeps will benefit more than those already tuning carefully.
- **Red flags / caveats:** (1) The alignment condition |U_A Z| = Θ(n) is not generally valid in minibatch training — their own measurements (Fig. 8) show module- and stage-dependent alignment, so the theory's clean regime is an idealization; (2) the 1/n asymptotic suppression is never directly verified — only dominance at two finite widths (1B, 8B); (3) evaluation is limited to SFT on small datasets (10k math, 170k commonsense, GLUE) with models ≤ 8B — no instruction tuning, no RLHF/RL post-training, no long-context tasks; (4) the toy MLP disables momentum to match the theory, diverging from the AdamW used everywhere else; (5) for the ηB ≈ nηA guideline, n = 4096 implies ρ ≈ 4096, yet their best cells usually sit at ρ = 200–2000 — the guideline is a starting point, not an optimum.

## Strengths

- Identifies a concrete, previously uncharacterized mechanism (init-noise amplification by ηB) behind a widely observed practical failure (high-ratio LoRA+ collapse), with direct energy measurements confirming it (noise 117% → 1.5–3.1% of feature energy).
- The fix is essentially free: no architecture change, no extra compute, keeps zero initial adapter output, one line in standard PEFT code.
- Exceptionally honest presentation: pre-registered style caveats, seed-level CIs, matched-retuning controls separating "robustness at fixed HPs" from "best tuned accuracy," and explicit acknowledgment that most sweeps are single-run.
- Generalizes beyond Gaussian init: variance-compressing Orthogonal/EVA/PiSSA achieves the same rescue (2.49% → 77.74% at ρ = 4000), showing the principle is about A0's scale.
- Cross-family and cross-precision consistency (Llama, Qwen, Mistral; full-precision and QLoRA) makes the claim credible rather than a single-backbone artifact.

## Limitations

- Most LR-ratio heatmap sweeps are single runs; the authors themselves warn that small score differences in those grids are not established improvements.
- Only supervised fine-tuning is studied; RL post-training (RLVR/GRPO), instruction tuning at scale, and larger models (> 8B) are untested, and the paper names this as future work.
- No direct rule for choosing ηB/ηA per model/task — Init[A2] stabilizes the high-ratio regime but does not tell you the optimal ratio; rank–multiplier–LR interactions (r^{2(γN−γS)} term) remain only partially characterized.
- Trade-off at symmetric/equal LRs: the smaller initial A scale slows early adaptation (Table 9), and Init[A3]/Init[A4] show that over-suppressing A0 requires very large ratios to recover signal (≈30% accuracy at ρ = 1).
- Theory rests on assumptions (alignment, contraction exponents, T(n) = o(√n)) whose finite-practice validity is probed but not proven; the asymptotic 1/n rate is unverified at finite width.

## Relevance

该论文直接命中"LLM Training Stability & Observability"兴趣方向：它从理论上定位了 LoRA 微调中初始化引发的训练不稳定与高学习率比崩溃机制，并给出确定性诊断量（噪声/信号能量分解）与一行代码级修复，属于微调阶段的初始化失败与稳定性问题研究。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does Init[A2] change the optimal ratio landscape for RL post-training (RLVR/GRPO with LoRA), where LR sensitivity and instability are known to be severe?
- Can the noise/signal energy ratio (E_N/E_S) be developed into a practical online probe — e.g., an early-warning metric that tells practitioners when ηB is too large before accuracy collapses?
- Why does the best ratio in practice (ρ ≈ 200–2000) fall well below the width-based guideline ηB ≈ nηA (ρ ≈ 4096 for n = 4096)? Is there a width- and data-aware correction?
- How does Init[A2] interact with rsLoRA's α/√r multiplier and with rank-transfer rules (e.g., Chen et al. 2026) — does joint (r, α/r, ρ) tuning admit a single closed-form prescription?
- Does the variance-compression recipe extend to other structured initializers with nonzero B0 (CorDA, LoftQ) and to quantized backbones beyond the tested QLoRA setup, without breaking their initialization-time alignment?
