> **一句话总结：** 该论文把 MoE 模型投机解码一步的时间建模为"请求字节数 ÷ 各内核的有效带宽 + 固定时间"，用 Lean 4 形式化证明何时投机更快、最优草稿长度（阶梯函数）以及去重内核的加速条件 ū(n) < r(n)，并在 llama.cpp Metal/CUDA 和 MLX-LM 上实测发现：逐槽（per-slot）内核的有效带宽反而更高（r = 0.32–0.75），导致去重仅在每步 5–8 个 token 时才更快甚至从不更快，推翻了"复用专家就必然省时"的单一带宽成本模型。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author paper (ReClan AI Studio) that decomposes the MoE speculative-decoding step time into requested bytes over per-kernel effective bandwidth, proves the speedup condition, the acceptance-rate "staircase" of best draft length, and the dedup condition ū(n) < r(n) in Lean 4, and validates on llama.cpp Metal/CUDA and MLX-LM with OLMoE-1B-7B, Qwen3-30B-A3B, gpt-oss-20b and DeepSeek-V2-Lite. Headline: on WikiText-103 traces next-token expert reuse reaches 0.363–0.492 (vs 0.0625–0.125 under the uniform-routing baseline of MoESD), yet the deduplicating kernel is SLOWER than the per-slot kernel at n=2 on every engine/model pair (ū(2)=0.72–0.83 > r=0.37–0.75) because its effective bandwidth is 1.3–3.1x lower — contradicting single-bandwidth cost models (S2-MoE, SpecFit) which predict reuse always helps. On DeepSeek-V2-Lite/CUDA, a pre-registered prediction from an <1-hour per-model calibration gives the best draft length within one at all 22 acceptance values, while a fit run afterwards wrongly places a crossover at n=7.

## 核心贡献

- **将步时间分解为"字节 ÷ 带宽 + 固定时间"两个可独立测量的输入**：验证一遍（pass）要读的专家字节数只由路由决定（可用 trace 离线算出），而每个内核搬这些字节的速度（有效带宽）只由内核实现决定（可用单层专家算子基准在每模型 1 小时内标定）。
- **推广了 Leviathan et al. (2023) 的投机解码加速条件**：从仿射步时间 1+χk 推广到任意正的步时间，证明最优草稿长度 k*(a) 随接受率单调不降（阶梯函数），并给出每个"阶梯点"a_j 的精确表达式；全部结果在 Lean 4 (Mathlib) 中形式化证明。
- **给出去重内核的精确胜负条件 ū(n) < r(n)**：去重引擎请求的字节份额 ū(n) 低于其带宽比 r(n) 时才更快，节省时间 = 逐槽专家时间 × (1 − ū(n)/r(n))。单一带宽的成本模型（r≡1）会错误地认为"只要有复用就更快"。
- **带宽误差的传播界（Lean 证明）**：10% 的带宽误差最多使预测加速比偏移 25%；最优草稿长度对误差最不敏感，节省量（saving）在交叉点附近最敏感（放大 3.2–340 倍）。
- **对 12 个"引擎×模型×上下文深度"配置做端到端预测验证**，并对 DeepSeek-V2-Lite 做了预注册式预测（跑投机实验前写死预测和评分规则），CUDA 上最优草稿长度在全部 22 个接受率处命中（误差 ≤1）。

## Key Contributions

- **Separates the two inputs of a step's time**: the expert bytes a verification pass requests (fixed by routing alone, computable offline from traces) and the effective bandwidth at which each kernel moves them (fixed by the kernel, measurable by a one-layer expert-operation benchmark in under an hour per model — 22 min on CUDA and 48 min on Metal for DeepSeek-V2-Lite, vs 3.8 and ~8 hours of whole-model speculative runs).
- **Generalizes Leviathan et al. (2023)** beyond the affine step time 1+χk to any positive step time: an exact condition for when a draft length beats plain decoding (Eq. 8), a proof that the best draft length k*(a) is a monotone non-decreasing "staircase" in acceptance rate, and exact step points a_j — all formalized and proved in Lean 4 against Mathlib.
- **Gives the exact deduplication condition**: a deduplicating kernel (reads each distinct expert once per pass) is faster than a per-slot kernel (one expert read per slot) exactly where its byte share ū(n) < its bandwidth ratio r(n); its saving is (per-slot expert time) × (1 − ū(n)/r(n)). Single-bandwidth cost models (r≡1) wrongly imply reuse always helps.
- **Proved error bounds for bandwidth noise** (Table 2): a 10% bandwidth error moves the predicted best speedup by at most 25%; the best draft length is the least sensitive prediction, the saving the most sensitive (error amplified 3.2–3.8x on MLX-LM, 6–220x on CUDA, up to 340x on Metal, worst at the crossover).
- **Validates prediction end-to-end** on 12 configurations (llama.cpp Metal, MLX-LM, llama.cpp CUDA — the latter two/three modified by the author to deduplicate small passes) × {OLMoE-1B-7B, Qwen3-30B-A3B, gpt-oss-20b} × depth {512, 4096}, plus a pre-registered prediction on DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat (Q4_K_M) whose scoring rules were written before any speculative run.

## Methodology

The paper models the verify time of an n-token pass on kernel p as τ_p(n) = t0 + D(n)/β_D + E_p(n)/β_p(n) + φ_p(n): fixed time outside memory, dense reads (attention/KV) at dense bandwidth β_D, expert bytes E_p(n) at the kernel's effective bandwidth, plus launch overhead. Expert bytes follow from routing: for a stationary routing process the expected distinct experts per layer is U_l(n) = m·Σ(1−ρ_l(j)), where ρ_l(n) is consecutive-token reuse, so a per-slot kernel requests m·n·W bytes while a deduplicating kernel requests Σ w_l·U_l(n). Reuse is measured on the 60 WikiText-103 test articles (32 held out for fitting). Effective bandwidths come from a standalone benchmark of one MoE layer's expert operation running each engine's own kernels, fed two kinds of expert IDs: chosen IDs (controlled number of distinct experts U per pass, to fit t = t_op + c_u·U·b + c_re·(nm−U)·b by least squares and separate first-read from cached re-read cost) and recorded-routing windows (realistic). The theory section derives, in this step time and under geometric acceptance at rate a: the speedup condition V(k)−V(0) < (a+…+a^k)·V(0), the monotone staircase and exact step points, the dedup reuse threshold ρ̄_k > ρ*_k(a) (Eq. 14), and the five error bounds of Table 2 — proofs in Lean 4 (Appendix B/C). Experiments add one dedup switch to each engine; each configuration measures whole-model verify times at n=1..9 and speculative throughput at draft lengths k=0..8 × 11 acceptance values (0.2–0.98) using an oracle draft (target's own greedy token corrupted with probability 1−a) on six WikiText-103 validation prompts truncated to depth 512 or 4096. Predictions from bytes-over-bandwidths are compared against these runs; on CUDA a hardware counter (Nsight Compute) verifies byte counts without timing.

## Results & Highlights

- **Reuse is high but uniform-routing models badly underestimate it**: layer-mean next-token reuse ρ(2) on WikiText-103 test articles is 0.363 (OLMoE-1B-7B), 0.480 (Qwen3-30B-A3B) and 0.492 (gpt-oss-20b), versus 0.125/0.0625/0.125 under the independent-uniform-routing assumption used by MoESD; at n=9 tokens reuse climbs to 0.762/0.780/0.856.
- **Deduplication is slower at n=2 on every engine and model measured**: the byte share ū(2) = 0.72–0.83 exceeds the bandwidth ratio r(2) = 0.37–0.75 in all eight engine/model pairs, so dedup only becomes faster from 5–8 tokens per pass — or never. Example numbers (Table 1): llama.cpp CUDA OLMoE at n=2 has per-slot 618 GB/s vs dedup 464 GB/s (r=0.75); llama.cpp Metal Qwen3 at n=8 has 280 vs 98 GB/s (r=0.35); the best ratio anywhere is r=0.75.
- **Hardware counters confirm the byte model on CUDA**: across 70 Nsight Compute profiles, the deduplicating kernel reads its union bytes from DRAM within 0.2–2.5%, while the per-slot kernel reads between 1× and 2.35× the union bytes — its L2 cache serves the repeats, and its median per-slot bandwidth on CUDA (933–1018 GB/s at n=8) exceeds the RTX 4070 Ti SUPER's 672 GB/s DRAM peak from n=4.
- **Prediction accuracy**: with WikiText-103 routing, the saving error (largest |Ŝ−S| over n) is 0.23–0.52 ms on CUDA and 0.43–3.7 ms on the Apple engines, and the predicted dedup crossover lands within one pass size in 10 of 12 configurations (11 of 12 with recorded routing of the verified text; the measured-time version matches all four CUDA crossovers exactly). The measured saving lies inside the proved error band at every point on CUDA and MLX-LM and at 36 of 48 points on Metal.
- **Pre-registered DeepSeek-V2-Lite prediction holds on CUDA**: the measured-time version, written down with six scoring rules before any speculative run, correctly predicts the dedup kernel slower at every n=2..8, keeps verify time within 10.1%, the saving inside its band at all 14 points, and the best draft length within one at all 22 acceptance values (throughput loss at k̂* only 2.9–4.1%). On Metal it misses — verify time up to 34% low, k̂* losing up to 22.5% of best throughput — and the pre-registered calibrated version brings verify time to 4.3–10.5% and k* within one at 20 of 22.
- **Post-hoc fits can mislead**: on DeepSeek-V2-Lite CUDA, the prediction from fits run after the whole-model runs places a crossover at n=7 where none is measured (saving error 0.95–1.1 ms vs 0.23–0.28 ms for the pre-registered version) — a concrete demonstration that the pre-registration mattered.
- **Practical bottom line**: with a real draft model on llama.cpp Metal, plain decoding (no speculation) is fastest on 33 of 37 held-out MT-Bench prompts; and the oracle draft emits 2–5% fewer tokens per step than the geometric-acceptance expectation G_a(k), putting measured speedups 1–21% below prediction on DeepSeek-V2-Lite CUDA.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Yuzheng Lan, at ReClan AI Studio, LLC — an independent/industry studio rather than an academic lab, with no public institutional track record behind this work. Compensating for that, the methodology is unusually self-scrutinizing: main results are mechanically proved in Lean 4 against Mathlib; byte counts are verified against hardware DRAM counters (Nsight Compute) rather than inferred from timings; error bounds are proved and then checked point-by-point; and the paper openly discloses (Section 5 "Limits") that the OLMoE/Qwen3/gpt-oss predictions were computed after the whole-model measurements existed, that one post-hoc correction (taking the one-token time from the per-slot fit) flipped crossover placement from wrong in 10/12 to right, and that only the DeepSeek-V2-Lite measured-time and calibrated versions were pre-registered. The "Use of AI" section states AI agents produced the code, figures, Lean formalization and initial draft under the author's direction — increasingly common but worth noting for a solo-authored systems paper. Code, data and the Lean formalization are "available from the author on request" rather than in a public repository.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The decomposition "bytes (routing) × per-kernel bandwidth" and the exact dedup condition ū(n) < r(n) are genuinely new relative to prior MoE speculative-decoding cost models — MoESD (Huang et al., 2025), Li et al. (2026), S2-MoE (Huang et al., 2026) and SpecFit (Cao et al., 2026) all charge every expert read the same bandwidth, which this paper shows empirically flips the sign of the saving at n=2 on all eight engine/model pairs. The Lean formalization of the staircase theory is a strong rigor signal rare in systems papers.
- **Rigor**: High on the measurement side (hardware counters, pre-registration on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, proved error bounds, per-point band checks). Weaker on the prediction claims for 3 of the 4 models: predictions for OLMoE, Qwen3 and gpt-oss were computed after the whole-model data existed and one post-hoc change was made — the paper says so, but a reader should treat "10 of 12 crossovers" as post-hoc accuracy, not forecasting skill; only DeepSeek-V2-Lite is a genuine held-out test.
- **Reproducibility**: Mixed. Engines, benchmark, traces, timing files and the Lean formalization are only "available on request" — no public artifact, which for an engine-modification paper (they changed llama.cpp Metal, MLX-LM and llama.cpp CUDA) is a real barrier. Single machine per platform; batch size one only; four models spanning only 7B–30B total parameters; DRAM byte verification exists only on CUDA (Apple byte counts rest on source reading). Bandwidth also does not transfer across models — fitted on one model it misses a held-out model by 9–206% — so the <1h calibration must be redone per model, which the paper honestly flags.
- **Significance**: The negative headline (dedup mostly does not pay at speculation pass sizes; a per-slot kernel with L2-cached re-reads can beat a byte-minimal kernel) is a useful corrective for engine developers who currently hand-tune this switch, and the staircase/bounds give a principled replacement. But deployment relevance is tempered by the oracle-draft setup: acceptance is enforced geometric and draft cost h(k) is only measured, and the one real-draft check (MT-Bench, 33/37 prompts fastest with plain decoding) suggests the whole regime may rarely matter in practice on Metal.
- **Red flags**: The Metal gpt-oss d=4096 configuration (saving error 9.7 ms, both crossovers missed) traces to a modeling assumption failure — greedy continuations that repeat themselves (81% of 4-grams recur vs 48% at d=512) violate stationary routing; the paper handles it honestly but it exposes that the byte model's weakest point is exactly the speculative-decoding setting (drafted/rejected tokens) it is meant to predict. The pre-registered Metal DeepSeek-V2-Lite prediction failing 3 of 6 scoring rules (best draft length, throughput lost, verify time) is disclosed but caps the strength of the strongest-evidence claim.

## Strengths

- Clean conceptual separation of bytes (routing-determined) from bandwidth (kernel-determined), each measured by the tool suited to it — traces for bytes, a sub-hour single-layer benchmark for bandwidth, hardware counters as an independent check.
- The Lean 4/Mathlib formalization of the theory (speedup condition, staircase, dedup threshold, five error bounds) is rare rigor for an inference-systems paper.
- Honest, unusually explicit treatment of its own evidence: post-hoc vs pre-registered predictions are kept distinct; the failed scoring rules and the one post-hoc correction are named.
- Actionable engineering output: the crossover and best draft length follow from a <1-hour calibration instead of multi-hour whole-model sweeps or hand-tuned switches.

## Limitations

- Oracle draft with enforced geometric acceptance; no real draft model except one small MT-Bench check — deployment acceptance is neither geometric nor stationary (the gpt-oss d=4096 failure shows what that breaks).
- Predictions for three of four models were computed after the measurements, with one post-hoc correction.
- Batch size one, one machine per platform, 7B–30B models; DRAM-byte validation only on CUDA; bandwidths must be refit per model (9–206% cross-model miss).
- No public code/data artifact ("on request"); single author with AI-agent assistance disclosed.
- The theory does not predict bandwidth from kernel structure — r(n) is always measured, never derived.

## Relevance

与「大规模 ML 计算基础设施（算子与内核）」高度相关：论文直接分析 llama.cpp/MLX 的 MoE 专家内核带宽行为，给出何时切换去重内核、如何选最优草稿长度的可操作判据。同时对推理可靠性方向有间接价值——它证明仅凭字节数（成本模型）判断加速会给出错误符号，内核层面的缓存效应才是决定性因素。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can r(n) be predicted from kernel structure (tile shapes, gather patterns, quantization format) instead of measured per model, given the 9–206% cross-model transfer failure?
- Does the staircase prediction survive a real draft model with non-geometric acceptance (e.g., acceptance decaying in draft position) and a nonzero, n-dependent h(k)?
- How do the ū(n) vs r(n) trade-offs change at batch sizes >1, where the routing union grows and both kernels amortize launches differently?
- Would hardware with lower DRAM bandwidth or weaker compute (edge NPUs, mobile SoCs) shift r(n) toward 1 and make deduplication win at all pass sizes?
- Can the gpt-oss d=4096 failure mode (self-repeating greedy continuations violating stationarity) be modeled explicitly — e.g., conditioning the byte estimate on the draft's n-gram repetition statistics?
