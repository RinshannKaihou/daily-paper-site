> **一句话总结：** 该文在矩阵二次型上对 Muon 的核心更新（PolarGD）给出闭式分析：正交化更新的收敛速度由"当前迭代点的条件数 κ(X)"而非 Hessian 条件数决定，它在迭代点行近正交时极快、近秩亏时反而远慢于 GD，而 Muon 实际使用的有限步 Newton–Schulz 恰好以阻尼方式规避了这一坏情形——在 90M 参数 transformer 的注意力矩阵中约四分之一奇异值正落入该阻尼区间，说明 NS 步数本身就是一个值得调的设计维度。

> **TL;DR:** This theory paper isolates the orthogonalized update at the heart of Muon by analyzing PolarGD (vs GD and SignGD baselines, compared under exact line search to remove learning-rate tuning) on matrix quadratics, plus a diagnostic on a 90M-parameter transformer trained with Muon on 1.8B Nemotron-CC tokens. Key results: PolarGD's contraction is governed by the condition number of the iterate (κ(X)) rather than the Hessian — it matches GD's rate with κ(H) replaced by κ(X) — so it is fast near row-isotropic iterates but provably slower than GD near rank deficiency; and the 5 Newton–Schulz steps used in Muon act as damped polar, multiplying tiny singular values by a bounded factor (ℓ₅ ≈ 485) instead of inverting them (up to 3.7×10⁶ amplification observed), which explains why fewer NS steps can converge faster than exact orthogonalization. In the transformer, about 25% of attention-matrix singular values fall in this GD-like damping range (89% for the layer-0 query matrix), while MLP/embedding/head matrices have none.

## 核心贡献

- **闭式的一步几何刻画（定理 1）。** 在矩阵二次型 f(X) = ½ tr((X−X⋆)ᵀH(X−X⋆)) 上，给出 GD、SignGD、PolarGD 在精确线搜索下的一步下降闭式，并刻画各自"一步到最优"的条件：GD 当且仅当 H = λI；SignGD 需要误差各元素等幅且 H 保号（对角占优 H + 顶点初始化即可）；PolarGD 对任意正定 H，只要误差的行正交且等范数（EEᵀ = cI）即可一步收敛——这解释了为何正交初始化对 Muon 类方法特别有利。
- **旋转等变性（定理 2）。** PolarGD（以及带动量、有限步 NS 的完整 Muon 更新）在正交基变换下等变，SignGD 则不然。在模拟语言数据 Zipf 频谱（λk ∝ k^{−ζ}）的二次型上，随机旋转 H 后 SignGD 性能大幅退化，而 PolarGD 完全不受影响——sign/Adam 的优势依赖曲率对齐于坐标轴，而极分解更新不依赖。
- **隐式预条件视角（命题 2、定理 3）。** PolarGD 等价于用由当前迭代 X 决定的预条件子 A(X) = (HΣH)^{†/2}H 做梯度步，其条件数 κ(A(X)) = κ(X)。精确进度系数 p(X) = ‖HX‖*²/(tr(H)·tr(HXXᵀ)) 满足 p(X) ≤ 1（当 XXᵀ = cI 时取等、一步收敛），下界为 GD 的 Kantorovich 型因子 4κ(X)/(1+κ(X))² ≈ 4/κ(X)，即 GD 的最坏收缩率 1−1/κ(H) 中 κ(H) 被替换成 κ(X)。因此 Hessian 病态本身不可怕，迭代点病态才致命。
- **识别出精确极分解的失效模式并给出确定性的补救机制。** 构造近秩亏实例：H = I 下 GD 一步收敛，而精确 PolarGD 每步仅按 (1−2/d)² 的固定比率收缩；其根源是极分解把微小非零奇异值放大到 1。Muon 实际使用的 q 步 Newton–Schulz 等价于阻尼极分解（damped polar，阻尼尺度 1/ℓq）：低于 1/ℓq 的奇异值被乘以固定因子 ℓq（ℓ₅ ≈ 485），行为如同 GD；高于它则如同精确极分解。因此在近秩亏情形下，更少的 NS 步反而收敛更快——NS 误差不应只被视为需要缩小的近似误差，而是一个有功能意义的软化机制。
- **真实训练中的验证。** 在用 Muon 训练的 90M transformer（Nemotron-CC 1.8B tokens）上测量全部 50 个被正交化的动量矩阵的奇异谱，证明上述"GD-like 阻尼区间"在实践中真实出现且高度异质（集中在注意力矩阵、随深度递减）。

## Key Contributions

- **Closed-form one-step geometry (Theorem 1).** On matrix quadratics with exact line search, the paper derives closed-form one-step decreases for GD, SignGD, and PolarGD, and characterizes one-step exactness: GD from every start iff H = λI; SignGD iff the error entries share one magnitude and H preserves signs (diagonally dominant H + vertex initialization suffices); PolarGD, for any positive-definite H, iff the rows of the error are orthogonal with equal norms (EEᵀ = cI). This explains why orthogonal initialization is special for Muon-style methods and vertex initialization for sign methods.
- **Rotation equivariance (Theorem 2).** PolarGD — and full Muon with momentum and finite Newton–Schulz — is equivariant under orthogonal changes of basis; SignGD is not. On a Zipf-spectrum quadratic (λk ∝ k^{−ζ}, ζ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1}) modeling next-token prediction over one-hot inputs (Kunstner & Bach's setup), randomly rotating H degrades SignGD severely while PolarGD is provably/empirically unaffected: the sign descent advantage requires coordinate-aligned curvature, the polar advantage does not.
- **Implicit preconditioning by the iterate (Prop. 2, Theorem 3).** PolarGD is exactly a gradient step with left preconditioner A(X) = (HΣH)^{†/2}H built from the iterate covariance Σ = XXᵀ, with κ(A(X)) = κ(X). The exact line-search contraction is f(X⁺)/f(X) = 1 − p(X) with p(X) = ‖HX‖*²/(tr(H)·tr(HXXᵀ)); p(X) = 1 iff XXᵀ = cI, and the tight Kantorovich-style lower bound replaces κ(H) by κ(X) in GD's classical rate. A badly conditioned Hessian can thus be harmless, and a well-conditioned one harmful, depending solely on the iterate's spectrum.
- **A deterministic failure mode of exact polar — and why finite Newton–Schulz fixes it (Sec. 4.2).** Near rank deficiency, exact polar normalization inverts tiny singular values; on a constructed family (X₀ = Oᵀdiag(1, δ, …, δ)O, δ = 10⁻⁴, H = I, d = 10), GD is one-step exact while exact PolarGD contracts at the fixed rate (1−2/d)² per step. q Newton–Schulz steps equal a damped polar update with damping scale 1/ℓq (ℓ₅ ≈ 485 for Muon's coefficients (3.4445, −4.7750, 2.0315)): below it the update scales singular values like GD, above it like exact polar — so fewer NS steps can converge *faster* than exact orthogonalization, contradicting analyses that treat NS error purely as degraded approximation.
- **Empirical relevance to real training (App. H).** Measuring all 50 momentum matrices of a 90M-parameter decoder-only transformer (8 layers, width 576, Nemotron-CC 1.8B tokens, 3433 steps) shows the GD-like regime genuinely occurs: ~25% of attention-matrix singular values sit below 1/ℓ₅ ≈ 2.1×10⁻³, none for MLP/embedding/head matrices, decreasing with depth.

## Methodology

The paper strips Muon down to its core direction — Xₖ₊₁ = Xₖ − γₖ·polar(∇f(Xₖ)), dubbed PolarGD — deliberately removing momentum and stochasticity so that only the *direction* effect remains. Three directions are compared: GD (D = G), PolarGD (D = UVᵀ, the polar factor), and SignGD (D = sign(G), Adam's sign-like core). To compare directions rather than step-size tuning, all methods use exact line search on matrix quadratics f(X) = ½tr((X−X⋆)ᵀH(X−X⋆)), where line-search steps and decreases have closed form (γ*(D) = ⟨G,D⟩/tr(DᵀHD), Δ(D) = ⟨G,D⟩²/2tr(DᵀHD)). Section 3 derives one-step-exactness conditions, then tests them in a 3×3 grid of regimes (Gaussian/orthogonal/vertex initializations × isotropic/dense Marchenko–Pastur (κ = 386)/matched-spectrum diagonal Hessians) with tuned fixed steps. Section 3.3 proves orthogonal equivariance and runs the Zipf-quadratic experiment (diagonal vs randomly rotated H, median/range over 5 seeds). Section 4 rewrites polar(G) = (GGᵀ)^{†/2}G = A(X)X using G = HX, establishing PolarGD as implicitly preconditioned GD governed by κ(X), with exact progress identities extended to rank-deficient iterates via the projector onto range(G); it also shows rank(Xₖ) is non-increasing along exact polar trajectories. Section 4.2 analyzes q-step Newton–Schulz as a singular-value map ϕq that coincides with damped polar (εq = ℓq⁻²), i.e., soft spectral clipping in the regime opposite to spectral clipping of AdamW updates. Appendix H trains the 90M transformer with a standard Muon recipe (momentum 0.9, no Nesterov, weight decay 0, per-matrix scaling 0.2√max(dₒᵤₜ,dᵢₙ), LR 8×10⁻³ warmup then cosine to 10⁻⁶, bf16, 1×A100), checkpoints weights/momentum/raw-gradient spectra at 25/50/75/100% of training, and quantifies what fraction of each matrix's spectrum lies in the GD-like damping range.

## Results & Highlights

- **PolarGD's speed is governed by κ(X), not κ(H) (Theorem 3).** With line search, GD contracts as f(Xₖ₊₁) ≤ (1−1/κ(H))f(Xₖ); PolarGD satisfies the same bound with κ(H) replaced by κ(Xₖ). The worst-case lower bound on the progress coefficient p(X) can be as small as ≈1/(κ(H)·d) (tight, attained by a constructed near-rank-deficient family with p(X) → λmin(H)/tr(H)), roughly d/4 times smaller than the Kantorovich bound 4/κ(X).
- **Orders-of-magnitude wins — and one provable loss.** On the dense Marchenko–Pastur quadratic with κ(H) = 386, PolarGD beats GD by orders of magnitude for *every* initialization tested, and the gap widens with κ(H) (Fig. 1/10c). But under rank deficiency (Fig. 4: H = I, d = 10, one singular value 10⁻⁴), GD converges in one step while exact PolarGD contracts at the fixed rate (1−2/d)² per step — exact line search does not make PolarGD uniformly better.
- **~25% of attention singular values are in the Newton–Schulz damping range (Fig. 5).** In the 90M Muon-trained transformer (final checkpoint), about a quarter of the singular values of attention momentum matrices normalized by ‖M‖F fall below the damping scale 1/ℓ₅ ≈ 2.1×10⁻³, where 5 NS steps act like GD rather than polar; MLP matrices, the embedding, and the LM head have none. The fraction is largest in layer 0 (89% of query-matrix and 60% of output-projection singular values) and decreases with depth. Across all 50 matrices, exact polar would place ~16% of the update's squared Frobenius mass on these directions versus ~5% for 5 NS steps.
- **Extreme amplification avoided by NS.** Five attention matrices have smallest normalized singular values between 5.6×10⁻⁹ and 2.7×10⁻⁷; exact polar would amplify these directions by 1.8×10⁸ to 3.7×10⁶, whereas 2, 3, and 5 Newton–Schulz steps multiply them by bounded factors ℓq ≈ 11.9, 40.9, and 485. NS becomes fully polar-like on attention only at q ≥ 9–18 steps, versus 4–5 steps for MLP matrices.
- **Greedy line search is not the best trajectory.** With dual-adapted fixed steps, the tuned rate α ≈ 1.7 keeps the progress coefficient p(Xₖ) near 0.6, whereas exact line search (α = 1) drives p(Xₖ) down to 0.01 within 150 iterations — subtracting the spectral mean creates the very tiny singular values that stall polar updates.
- **Rotation robustness on Zipf data (Fig. 2).** On the Zipf quadratic, PolarGD's loss curve is unchanged when H is randomly rotated; SignGD degrades by many orders of magnitude (relative loss gap up to ~10¹⁶ range over 1000 iterations), consistent with sign descent's reliance on diagonal curvature.
- **Momentum stabilizes the spectrum.** Raw attention gradients have ~40% of singular values in the GD-like range at 25–50% of training, falling to momentum-like levels by the end; the momentum spectra that NS actually sees are nearly stationary over training.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The iterate-covariance (κ(X)) view of polar updates and the "finite NS = damped polar" equivalence are genuinely new and clarifying; prior work (Kovalev 2025; Pethick et al. 2025; Li & Hong 2025) only gave worst-case non-Euclidean rates, and prior inexact-Muon analyses (Shulgin et al. 2026; Kim & Oh 2026) treat NS error as a degradation factor — this paper shows the opposite can hold, deterministically. The rotation-equivariance observation is simple but delivers a sharp argument that Muon's Zipf-regime robustness is basis-independent, unlike Adam/SignGD.
- **Rigor:** One-step and trajectory results are exact closed-form identities for any positive-definite Hessian, with tight lower bounds matched by explicit constructions — no worst-case hand-waving. The transformer diagnostic honestly reports that other cutoffs than 1/ℓ₅ shift the percentages (5%–28% for attention), and that the paper did not verify NS reproduction on the embedding/LM head.
- **Reproducibility:** Quadratic experiments are described in enough detail (App. A gives tuned-step grids, seeds, Marchenko–Pastur construction) to reimplement, and the transformer config (Table 2) is fully specified; however, no code release is mentioned, and the diagnostic relies on one 1.8B-token run on one A100.
- **Significance & caveats:** The central limitation is the quadratic lens itself — momentum and stochasticity, arguably the dominant features of real LLM training, are deliberately excluded, and the authors acknowledge the trajectory results rely on quadratic structure (only equivariance and NS softening carry over to general objectives). The link between "GD-like range in momentum spectra" and actual training quality is correlational: the paper shows the regime *occurs*, not that exploiting it (e.g., tuning the NS step count) *improves* downstream loss — no experiment varies q in the transformer run to test the implied design recommendation. The 90M/1.8B-token scale is far below the regimes (7B+, hundreds of B tokens) where Muon's wins are established, and layer-0 attention concentration may look different at scale. A missing ablation the reader will want: damped polar with tuned ε versus 5-step NS in the actual transformer, and the effect of the p-matrix scaling factor on the observed spectra.

## Strengths

- Isolates the update *direction* from step-size confounds via exact line search with closed-form decreases — a clean experimental design that makes the comparisons fair rather than tuning-dependent.
- The κ(X)-not-κ(H) message is a compact, memorable, and actionable insight: what matters for Muon-type updates is the conditioning of the weight matrix itself, not the loss curvature.
- The Newton–Schulz-as-damped-polar equivalence gives a principled, deterministic mechanism for an empirical phenomenon (fewer NS steps sometimes helping) that prior theory could only treat as error.
- The transformer diagnostic is unusually concrete: per-layer, per-matrix-type percentages, extreme amplification factors, a verification that the training implementation reproduces NS₅(M) to 10⁻⁴, and a control showing raw-gradient vs momentum spectra differ.
- Honest scoping: no single optimizer is claimed to dominate (Fig. 1 shows each method's preferred regime), and local one-step superiority is explicitly shown not to imply trajectory superiority.

## Limitations

- Deterministic, full-batch matrix quadratics omit momentum and gradient noise — the two ingredients Muon actually uses — so the quantitative rates should not be read as predictions for LLM pretraining.
- The practical implication "treat NS step count / damping as a design choice" is supported by quadratic evidence and a spectral diagnostic, but not by an end-to-end training experiment that varies the NS count and measures downstream loss.
- One model scale (89.8M parameters, 8 layers, width 576) and one dataset (Nemotron-CC 1.8B tokens); findings such as the 89% layer-0 query fraction may be scale- and architecture-dependent.
- The GD-like-range percentages depend on the cutoff choice (5%–28% across cutoffs), and the paper itself notes the quadratic-model link (tiny gradient singular values from tiny iterate singular values) does not transfer literally: the GD-like directions of M are not aligned with the bottom singular directions of the attention weights (mean squared cosine ≈ random-subspace value).

## Relevance

该文属于"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"方向：它把 Muon 这一大模型预训练主力优化器的快慢机理归结为可观测的量（迭代矩阵的奇异谱、NS 阻尼区间占比），并给出逐层逐矩阵的诊断方法，可直接用于监控大规模预训练运行中优化器的数值行为。其 Newton–Schulz 阻尼分析也触及"极端性能优化下的数值可靠性"——有限步 NS 的有界放大（ℓ₅≈485）恰恰避免了精确极分解对 10⁻⁹ 量级奇异值的 10⁸ 倍放大这一数值隐患。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does varying the Newton–Schulz step count q (or replacing NS with explicitly damped polar at tuned ε) change final loss or convergence speed in the 90M transformer, and does the optimum q track the measured fraction of singular values in the GD-like range?
- How do the momentum spectra and the layer-wise GD-like fraction evolve at 1B+ parameter scale, with different attention variants (GQA, MLA) and with MoE routing, where matrix shapes and conditioning differ?
- Can the κ(X) governing rate be connected to known spectral evolution of weight matrices during training (e.g., heavy-tailed log-singular-value distributions) to predict *when during training* polar updates stall?
- Does stochasticity rescue exact polar from the near-rank-deficient slow regime (Gonon et al. suggest random perturbations restore reachability), and does momentum's spectral stabilization interact with the damping analysis?
- Since SignGD's advantage provably requires coordinate-aligned curvature while PolarGD's does not, what does a hybrid (e.g., polar on rotated blocks, sign on the rest) imply for Adam-vs-Muon componentwise behavior inside a single architecture?
