Why this matters

Reading hidden states to catch deception is a direct safety lever for autonomous agents — but only if detectors survive domain shift.

Critical edge

No adversarial evasion is tested, and adaptation presumes ~10 already-caught deceptions per new domain — 0.97 AUROC is not an adversarially robust guarantee.

01 Methodology — DDSN in three phases

1

IOSD — build the subspace

Fit a linear probe, deflate activations into its orthogonal complement, repeat → 7 orthogonal deception directions (matching-pursuit style; stops below norm ε), from globally shared to subtle facets. Supervision: two instructed-deception datasets (Instructed Pairs, Roleplaying Scenarios), activations taken at the fact tokens.

2

Domain-adaptive gating

A small network Gθ produces per-direction weights αi(x). At test time, θ is adapted by gradient descent on ~10 labeled examples from the new domain — so each domain emphasizes the directions that matter to it.

3

Inference & calibration

Score Ŷ = Σ αi(x)·wiTx; token scores aggregated by mean over the sequence; threshold calibrated to a 5% FPR operating point against 10k benign Alpaca samples.

Monitored model is frozen (LLaMA-3.3-70B-Instruct; Qwen3-32B for cross-family checks). Metrics: AUROC + Recall@5%FPR across strategic scheming, instrumental deception, context-conflicted honesty, and two comprehensive benchmarks.

03 Strengths

  • Real failure mode, cleanly shown. Single-direction probe brittleness demonstrated across seven diverse, mostly third-party benchmarks — not just self-built data.
  • Extremely cheap. Linear algebra over activations + a tiny gating net + 10 examples; no model modification — a realistic monitoring budget.
  • Metric discipline. AUROC plus recall at a strict 5% FPR calibrated on out-of-domain benign controls, with an extra FPR-on-target-honests check.
  • Tests its own story. Gating-weight analysis and cross-dataset transfer probe the causal claim, not just headline accuracy.
  • Early-intent evidence. Score elevation before the first explicit deceptive utterance is a compelling safety observation.

04 Limitations

  • Chicken-and-egg data need. Requires ~10 labeled deceptive examples per new domain; the claimed shift-detection classifier is never evaluated.
  • Aggregate masks weak spots. On Qwen3-32B, insider-trading recall stays 0.34–0.52 despite 0.93–0.98 AUROC (representation overlap).
  • No adversarial evaluation against probe-aware evasion; no genuinely self-generated (non-instructed, non-roleplay) deception tested.
  • Under-documented. Theorems asserted without proof; layer choice, ε, and K selection unspecified; no code release.
  • Weak baseline variant. Few-shot static probes show identical numbers on Qwen3-32B — possibly understating a properly retrained per-domain probe.

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. Can a probe-aware model — fine-tuned or RL-trained to evade — push deception activity into the orthogonal complement of all 7 directions?
  2. What do the directions mean semantically? Activation steering along each wi could test whether they encode intent, persona, reward-seeking — or dataset artifacts.
  3. Can unsupervised domain-shift detection plus automatic support-set mining remove the 10-labeled-examples requirement?
  4. Do results survive on frontier reasoning models, where deception may be planned in latent space hundreds of tokens before any output?
  5. Would combining IOSD directions with sparse-autoencoder features yield more interpretable, more transferable detectors?
