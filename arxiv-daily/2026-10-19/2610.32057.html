Selection outputs indices, not activations — so the score table and re-selection vanish, and million-token training runs 2.07× faster.

HyScale: Scaling Long-Context Training for Learned Sparse Attention

Methodology

How it works

01

Diagnose the new bottleneck

Profiling GLM-5.1 under full recomputation: selection (scoring + top-k) eats 47% of a training step at 1M tokens — 6.6× the sparse attention itself. Two wastes: a materialized 32 GiB/GPU/layer score table whose kept indices are only 64 MiB (0.2%), and a full re-run of selection during recomputation.

02

The insight

Selection's output is a discrete set of indices, not continuous activations. Top-k is non-differentiable, so backward needs neither the table nor re-selection — Appendix A derives that the indexer's KL gradient flows only through the k selected scores.

03

HyTopK — fused, exact, deterministic top-k

An optimistic threshold from strided samples of each query's context (M = min(max(4096, L/128), 8192), ~3k candidates pass into a 16K-slot buffer); every score is compared on-chip and never written to memory — the score table disappears. Failed queries are recovered host-free (fixed-grid no-op launches; ≤64 rescored exactly), and radix select + radix sort restore bitwise determinism.

04

Selection replay — per-entry recomputation

The forward pass saves each query's k indices; recomputation just gather-and-scores those k entries, shrinking recomputed selection from O(L) to O(k) per query while backward runs unchanged. Index replay costs ≤1.6% of a step and is ~5 GiB/GPU cheaper than score replay.

05

System glue

Decoupled CP overlap (indexer-key vs. KV collectives each hidden under non-consuming compute); locality-aware kernels batching adjacent query pairs; caches killing per-layer host syncs; kernel fusion; permutation-free KV gather.

Results & highlights

Beyond the 2.07× headline

Gains grow with context length, compound across three sparse-attention families, and come at essentially no memory penalty (≤11% more peak memory without index offloading).

  • Weak scaling 128K → 8M context: HyScale layer time grows 11× vs. Megatron-LM's 190×; per-layer speedup climbs to 22.3× at 8M; strong CP scaling reaches 11.7× at 256K over 8→512 GPUs.
  • Kernel replay of all 81.8M selections in one real 1M-token step: HyTopK peaks at 1.5–1.9 GiB; cuDNN selection runs 1.22–1.71× slower — and stays 3–10% slower even with 8–26× more memory.
  • Sampling quality: >5,000× fewer failed queries than LiteTopK / DoSA with trained weights; under a random indexer HyTopK fits every query in 5.9K slots vs. 10.5K / 12.3K.
  • Exactness is cheap: deterministic sort adds 0.8%; recovery adds 1.8–4.3% even at the 64-failed-query cap — no run in the paper exceeded 4 failures.
  • Ablation at 1M: 66% of layer time removed — HyTopK 37 pts, selection replay 18, implementation 11; at 256K HyTopK's share drops to 8%.
Strengths

What holds up

  • Real, quantified bottleneck — 47% of a production step, 6.6× the attention itself — traced to a single root cause, not a synthetic microbenchmark.
  • The insight is formally justified (Appendix A gradient derivation) and yields two composable techniques, not one point fix.
  • Takes training seriously where prior sampling top-k stopped at inference: CP compatibility, bitwise reproducibility (replayable loss spikes), host-free recovery — at a measured 0.8–4.3% overhead.
  • Evaluation breadth: end-to-end training, strong/weak CP scaling to 512 GPUs and 8M context, ablations on DSA/CSA/QSA, 81.8M-selection kernel replay, fair head-to-head vs. LiteTopK/DoSA.
  • Speedups grow with context — exactly the regime the system targets — with essentially no memory penalty.
Limitations

What to watch

  • No convergence evidence: equivalence is argued mathematically; timing uses steps 4–10, momentum-free SGD, balanced MoE routing — a somewhat artificial configuration.
  • Generality unproven beyond one framework (Megatron-LM), two NVIDIA GPU types, and partially reimplemented variants; MSA is described but never evaluated.
  • Heuristic constants — 3× threshold margin, 16K-slot buffer, 4096–8192 samples, 64-query recovery cap — validated empirically, not analyzed for worst-case distributions.
  • Gains concentrate at ≥256K context; at 256K the end-to-end speedup is 1.23× and HyTopK's ablation share only 8%.
  • No public code or artifact release, limiting independent verification; nothing yet reported for HBM-denser GPUs (e.g., B200).
Open threads

Questions for further exploration

  1. How do HyTopK's failure rate and recovery overhead behave in the earliest phase of pretraining (random indexer) at 4M+ context — is the 3× margin still enough, or does it need an adaptive schedule?
  2. What worst-case selection distribution pushes failures past the 64-query recovery cap, and what does the full-rescore fallback really cost in production?
  3. Does index replay interact correctly and profitably with partial/selective recomputation schedules, e.g., when only some layers recompute?
  4. Can the paired-query locality kernel extend to larger groups (71% overlap suggests diminishing but nonzero returns), and how does the trade-off shift with larger L2 or HBM3e bandwidth?
  5. Would decoupled CP overlap transfer to asynchronous/elastic CP or disaggregated training where indexer keys and KV live on different memory tiers?
  6. What is the cumulative host-memory and PCIe footprint of per-layer index offloading for very deep models at 8M context — still near-zero-overhead?
