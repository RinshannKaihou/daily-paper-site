Methodology

How the capability is built

Sage-A1.5 trains how execution is organized, not just what tasks are covered — orchestration and verification become learned, in-model policy instead of hardcoded scaffolding rules.

  • Two composition modes. Exploratory (rival hypotheses on one question, A∨B∨C) vs. Constructive (complementary subproblems, A∧B∧C). Subagents run in isolated contexts and return structured reports — answer / status / per-URL evidence / unresolved issues — that the main agent reconciles.
  • Orchestration training data. Constraint-Guided Long-Horizon Search (starlike constraint graphs with masked identifying details) plus Multi-Source Research Synthesis; main-agent and subagent trajectories get role-specific supervision.
  • Verification as a conditioned skill. A supervisor injects reflections at stalled search or key conclusions, then the prompts are stripped — the reflection lives in the model's own reasoning, gated by Verification protocol:enabled. No online supervisor or external verifier at inference.
  • Unified post-training. Five domain teachers (science, MLE, SWE, search, real-world workflows) merged via multi-harness SFT, then sample-level mixing of outcome RL with multi-teacher on-policy distillation; the pVisor sandbox pauses/swaps/resumes environments (~80% memory reduction in static tests).
  • Data machinery. Kaggle-MLE Factory, SWE-rebench-V2 with Git-object scrubbing and no-op reward checks, and a >1,000-environment professional-workflow pool grown from a few dozen seeds with builder/reviewer/judger separation.

Strengths

What holds up

  • Reusable framing. Coverage vs. composition; A∨B∨C exploratory vs. A∧B∧C constructive parallelism — vocabulary the field can adopt.
  • Verification that pays for itself. +4.5 pts on BrowseComp while cutting tokens −5.52% and turns −3.15% — a rare "added reasoning reduced cost" result.
  • Serious data hygiene. Leakage audits, no-op reward checks, builder/reviewer/judger role separation, exclusion of benchmark-overlapping competitions.
  • Honest and broad. 20 benchmarks with losses reported alongside wins; 10 professional-workflow artifacts and 6 scientist-aligned case studies — far more evidence than a typical model card.

Limitations

What doesn't

  • Trails larger rivals on roughly half the table: DRB-II 43.7 vs 57.9, HLE 55.1 vs 64.5, CritPt 20.0 vs 32.3, ALE-CLI 19.1 vs 28.6, plus SWE-Bench Pro/Multilingual, SciCode, HiPhO, VitaBench.
  • Teacher peaks not retained. Unified post-training is consistently below its best teachers: FS-O 89.0 → 81.0; MLE any-medal 72.7% → 65.2%.
  • Showcases are simulations and historical replays. No active physical experiments, no repeated-run statistics; orchestration training still centers on search/research synthesis with heavy context overhead on ultra-long trajectories.
  • Replication is hard. Training-data assets and evaluation-harness quirks (subagent 50-turn cap, 300-tool-call context resets) remain internal; no public release confirmed in the PDF.

Open questions for further exploration

  1. How much of the FS-O / CMT edge comes from the science teacher's ExoMind-style task selection rather than orchestration/verification themselves? A swap ablation is missing.
  2. Can verification-point context compression close the DRB-II gap (43.7 vs teacher 53.6), or is multi-teacher distillation loss the binding constraint?
  3. Does Verification protocol:enabled transfer across harnesses, or does it overfit to the training scaffold?
  4. What are repeated-run success rates and cost distributions for the scientific showcases — e.g., is the 12-hour Psi4 workflow reliable or a curated best-of run?
  5. Would orchestration gains survive removing the 50-turn subagent cap and 300-tool-call context reset — is the model or the harness doing the long-horizon management?
  6. MoE-A3B vs. dense 35B under identical post-training: does sparsity itself matter for agentic horizons?
