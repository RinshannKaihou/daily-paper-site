> **一句话总结：** 本文给出了多类 PAC 学习在任意固定 oracle 风险 $L^\star$ 下的最优（差对数因子）超额风险刻画 $\widetilde{\Theta}(\sqrt{L^\star d_{\mathrm{N}}/n} + d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n)$，用一个"面向比较者"的相对样本压缩定理把 Natarajan 维度项局部化到 $L^\star$、把 DS 维度项压到可实现阶，并配上一个在每个 $L^\star$ 上同时逼出两项的下界（常数 1/12 与 1/15），从而把此前只在 $L^\star=0$ 与 $L^\star=\Theta(1)$ 两个端点已知的结果连成一条完整曲线，并推广到 list learning（顺带把 [HMZ24] 可实现 list 下界中的因子 $r$ 去掉）。

> **TL;DR:** For finite-alphabet multiclass classification the paper closes the gap between the realizable rate $d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n$ [Pab26] and the agnostic rate $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{N}}/n}+d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n$ [CEH+26, HMZ24]: a parameter-free learner RMC attains $L(\mathrm{RMC}(S)) \le L^\star + C(\sqrt{L^\star(d_{\mathrm{N}}\log^3(en)+\log(1/\delta))/n} + (d_{\mathrm{N}}\log^3(en)+d_{\mathrm{DS}}\log^2(en)+\log(1/\delta))/n)$ (Thm 3.1), improving the previous best worst-case bound $\widetilde{O}(d_{\mathrm{DS}}/\varepsilon + (d_{\mathrm{N}}+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2)$ which charges the same $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{N}}/n}$ whether $L^\star=10^{-12}$ or $L^\star=1/3$; a matching lower bound $\frac{1}{12}\mathbf{1}\{L^\star \ge d_{\mathrm{N}}/n\}\sqrt{L^\star d_{\mathrm{N}}/n} + \frac{1}{15}\min\{d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n, 1/4\}$ (Thm 4.1) shows both terms are forced by a single class and a single distribution. This is a pure theory paper with machine-checked (Lean 4) main theorems and no experiments.

## 核心贡献

- **关闭了多类 optimistic rate 的开放区间。** 在每个固定 oracle 风险 $L^\star$ 上给出 $\widetilde{\Theta}(\sqrt{L^\star d_{\mathrm{N}}/n} + d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n)$ 的上下界匹配（差对数因子），且**关于字母表大小 $K$ 一致**。此前只有两个端点已知：$L^\star=0$ 的 $d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n$（[Pab26]）与 agnostic 的 $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{N}}/n}+d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n$（[CEH+26, HMZ24]）。
- **新的"面向比较者"的相对压缩定理（Thm 3.3）。** 若一个大小为 $k$ 的确定性压缩规则在样本上的经验损失不超过固定比较者 $h$，则 $L(A(S)) \le L(h) + 12\sqrt{L(h)\Gamma_N(k,\delta)} + 20\Gamma_N(k,\delta)$，其中 $\Gamma_N = ((k+1)\log(N+1)+\log(4/\delta))/N$。关键是**在 $L(h)$ 上保留系数 1**、允许消息有序且索引重复、且**不需要任何 stability 假设**。
- **把二元证明中"能迁移的部分"与"不能迁移的部分"分离。** 作者明确指出 [MQZ26] 的二元锐化结果里，Boolean cube 的边等周几何**无法**提升到多类标签（$K$-ary Hamming graph 的自然容量是 Graph dimension，在 DS 有限时可能无穷），真正可迁移的只有"系数一比较原理"。
- **两个维度的分工被下界证实是内在的。** $d_{\mathrm{DS}}$ 只管"正确标签是否进入学习到的 menu"（coverage，$1/n$ 阶），$d_{\mathrm{N}}$ 只管"menu 内部选错标签"的局部化波动。下界用一个乘积类（noisy Natarajan cube ⊕ 来自 [BCD+22] 分离的 Natarajan-free pseudo-cube）在同一分布上同时逼出两项。
- **推广到 list learning 并顺带改进已知下界。** Thm 5.1/5.2 把比较者升级为最优 $r$-元组；Corollary 5.3 去掉了 [HMZ24] 可实现 list 下界中的因子 $r$，得到 $\frac{1}{15}\min\{D/n,1/4\}$，即可实现 list 学习样本复杂度 $\Omega(d^r_{\mathrm{DS}}/\varepsilon)$。
- **主定理经 Lean 4 机器验证**（Section D 记录验证内容与形式，代码在 GitHub: xiaoyulics/multiclass-pac-learning）。

## Key Contributions

- **A two-dimensional optimistic rate for multiclass PAC learning.** At every fixed oracle risk $L^\star$, the optimal excess risk is $\widetilde{\Theta}(\sqrt{L^\star d_{\mathrm{N}}/n} + d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n)$, uniformly in alphabet size $K$, attained by a learner that knows neither $L^\star$ nor $\delta$.
- **A comparator-facing relative sample-compression theorem (Thm 3.3)** with explicit constants (12 and 20), ordered messages with repeated indices, no stability assumption, and coefficient one on the comparator's *population* risk — the interface (not the mechanism, which is counting + one-sided Bernstein, cf. [GKN17, Thm 8]) is the claimed novelty.
- **A structural separation:** the paper isolates which half of the binary argument of [MQZ26] transfers (the coefficient-one comparison principle) and which does not (the Boolean-cube edge-isoperimetric geometry), and explains *why* — Graph dimension, not DS or Natarajan dimension, is the natural capacity of the $K$-ary Hamming-graph construction.
- **A fixed-$L^\star$ lower bound (Thm 4.1)** built from a noisy pair-Assouad scheme calibrated to $L^\star$ plus a fiber/leave-one-out argument on pseudo-cubes, forcing both terms with one class and one distribution.
- **List-learning extension (Thms 5.1, 5.2, Cor 5.3)**, including a factor-$r$ improvement to the known realizable list lower bound of [HMZ24].
- **Machine-checked main theorems in Lean 4**, with the development publicly linked.

## Methodology

The upper bound reuses the cover–menu–compression skeleton of [CEH+26, Algorithm 4] and replaces only its final statistical interface. Algorithm 1 (RMC, "relative menu compression") splits an i.i.d. sample $S$ into three consecutive blocks of near-equal size: $S_1$ builds a compression cover, replacing a possibly infinite class $\mathcal{H}$ by a finite family $\widehat{\mathcal{F}}$ with $\log|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}| \lesssim d_{\mathrm{DS}}\log^2(en)$ (via one-inclusion/boosting-to-compression plus the sharp realizable conversion of [Pab26, Cor 1.1]); $S_2$ runs a multiplicative-weights menu learner for $T=|S_2|$ rounds, producing a set-valued map $\mu(x)$ of size $\le T-1$ whose correct-label miss probability is $\lesssim (d_{\mathrm{DS}}\log^2(en)+\log(1/\eta))/n$; $S_3$ fits a fixed-menu, empirical-risk-dominating compression rule of description length $k \lesssim d_{\mathrm{N}}\log^2(en)$ under the *inside-menu* binary loss $\ell_\mu(f,(x,y)) = \mathbf{1}\{f(x)\ne y,\, y\in\mu(x)\}$.

The conceptual hinge is Lemma 2.4 (selective-menu decomposition), an elementary pointwise identity giving $L_{\mathcal{D}}(g)-L_{\mathcal{D}}(h) \le (L_\mu(g)-L_\mu(h)) + \alpha_\mu(h)$ with $\alpha_\mu(h)=\mathbb{P}(h(X)=Y,\ Y\notin\mu(X))$ and $L_\mu(h)\le L_{\mathcal{D}}(h)$. Because the second term charges only *correct-label coverage failures*, and the first term is a purely binary comparison on a frozen menu, the two dimensions never merge. Applying a standard worst-case compression bound at the last stage would give $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{N}}/n}$ and erase all information that the oracle is nearly correct; Thm 3.3 instead anchors the fluctuation at the comparator's own Bernoulli variance scale. The ordering of blocks is load-bearing (the cover must be frozen before the menu rounds, and the menu before $S_3$), and the paper is careful that the menu is independent of the final sample and that empty/singleton menus admit a size-zero finalizer (Lemma A.6).

The lower bound (Thm 4.1) uses a product class $\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{cube}} \oplus \mathcal{W}$ on disjoint domains, with $\mathrm{Nat}(\mathcal{H}) \in \{d_{\mathrm{N}}, d_{\mathrm{N}}+1\}$ and $\mathrm{DS}(\mathcal{H}) = d_{\mathrm{N}} + d_{\mathrm{DS}}$ (Lemma 4.4: both dimensions add over disjoint domains). The cube factor carries a multiclass transplant of the classical Assouad construction of [DL95], with per-point mass $\tau \asymp L^\star/d_{\mathrm{N}}$ and label bias $\gamma \asymp \sqrt{d_{\mathrm{N}}/(nL^\star)}$, and an anchor point absorbing the remaining mass so the oracle risk equals $L^\star$ *exactly* for every sign pattern. The second factor imports the Natarajan-free pseudo-cube of [BCD+22] (Lemma 4.2: $\mathrm{Nat}(\mathcal{W})\le 1$, $\mathrm{DS}(\mathcal{W})=D$) and applies a realizable leave-one-out argument whose combinatorial core is the fiber lemma (Lemma 4.5: within a cloud, every prediction is wrong with conditional probability $\ge 1/2$).

## Results & Highlights

No empirical experiments are reported — this is a pure theory paper; all claims below are theorems with explicit constants.

- **Main upper bound (Thm 3.1):** $L_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathrm{RMC}(S)) \le L^\star + C\big(\sqrt{L^\star(d_{\mathrm{N}}\log^3(en)+\log(1/\delta))/n} + (d_{\mathrm{N}}\log^3(en)+d_{\mathrm{DS}}\log^2(en)+\log(1/\delta))/n\big)$, with **coefficient exactly one on $L^\star$**. The strongest prior baseline is [CEH+26, Pab26, Cor 1.2]: $\widetilde{O}(d_{\mathrm{DS}}/\varepsilon + (d_{\mathrm{N}}+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2)$, i.e. excess-risk scale $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{N}}/n} + d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n$ — the delta is replacing $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{N}}/n}$ by $\sqrt{L^\star d_{\mathrm{N}}/n}$, a factor $\sqrt{L^\star}$ improvement (e.g. $10^{-6}\times$ at $L^\star=10^{-12}$). The corresponding sample complexity (Cor 3.4) is $\widetilde{O}(L^\star(d_{\mathrm{N}}+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2 + (d_{\mathrm{DS}}+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon)$.
- **Matching lower bound (Thm 4.1):** for all $1\le d_{\mathrm{N}}\le d_{\mathrm{DS}}$, every $n\ge 1$, every $L^\star\in[0,1/8]$ and every (possibly randomized) learner, $\mathbb{E}[L_{\mathcal{D}}(A(S))]-L^\star \ge \frac{1}{12}\mathbf{1}\{L^\star\ge d_{\mathrm{N}}/n\}\sqrt{L^\star d_{\mathrm{N}}/n} + \frac{1}{15}\min\{d_{\mathrm{DS}}/n,\,1/4\}$. The prior lower bounds ([CEH+26] worst-case agnostic, [Pab26] realizable, [HMZ24]) covered only the endpoints $L^\star=\Theta(1)$ and $L^\star=0$; the delta here is that both terms are forced *at every intermediate $L^\star$* by one class and one distribution.
- **Relative compression theorem (Thm 3.3):** $L^\ell_{\mathcal{D}}(A(S)) \le L^\ell_{\mathcal{D}}(h) + 12\sqrt{L^\ell_{\mathcal{D}}(h)\Gamma_N(k,\delta)} + 20\Gamma_N(k,\delta)$ with $\Gamma_N(k,\delta)=((k+1)\log(N+1)+\log(4/\delta))/N$. Baseline: generic agnostic compression of size $k$ cannot beat $\sqrt{k\log(n/k)/n}$ [HK19], and removing the log requires stability; this theorem keeps the log and the stability-free interface but moves the anchor to the comparator's population risk with coefficient one.
- **Coverage-term improvement lineage:** [CEH+26] originally paid a $d_{\mathrm{DS}}^{3/2}$ coverage term; via [Pab26, Cor 1.1] this becomes $d_{\mathrm{DS}}$, and the paper inherits $d_{\mathrm{DS}}\log^2(en)$.
- **List learning (Thm 5.1):** $L^{\mathrm{list}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathrm{RMC}_r(S)) \le L^\star_r + C(\sqrt{L^\star_r(r d_{\mathrm{N}}\log^3(en)+\log(1/\delta))/n} + (r(d_{\mathrm{N}}\log^3(en)+d_{\mathrm{DS}}\log^2(en))+\log(er/\delta))/n)$, versus the prior single-comparator rates $\sqrt{r^1 d^r_{\mathrm{N}}/n} + r d^r_{\mathrm{DS}}/n$ of [CP23, Pab26]; Thm 5.2 gives the matching-shape lower bound $\frac{1}{48}\mathbf{1}\{L^\star_r \ge d/n\}\sqrt{L^\star_r d/n} + \frac{1}{15}\min\{D/n,1/4\}$ for $L^\star_r \in [0, 1/(8(r+1))]$.
- **Corollary 5.3 (factor-$r$ removal):** realizable $r$-list learning has $\mathbb{E}[L^{\mathrm{list}}_{\mathcal{D}}(A(S))] \ge \frac{1}{15}\min\{D/n, 1/4\}$, improving the best known $\Omega((d^r_{\mathrm{DS}}+\log(1/\delta))/(r\varepsilon))$ of [HMZ24] by exactly the factor $r$, and leaving only a single factor $r$ against the $O(r(d^r_{\mathrm{DS}}+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon)$ upper bound of [Pab26, Cor 1.3]. The mechanism is concrete: doubling the hard structures' width to $2r$ so an $r$-list covers at most half of each fiber.
- **Explicit constant tracking:** Remark 3.2 states $C = 12\sqrt{5(c_3+2)} + 100(c_3+2) + 5(c_2+4)(2636c_1+4)$, where the factor 2636 bounds $(d_{\mathrm{RE}}(\mathcal{H})+1)/d_{\mathrm{DS}}$ via Lemma A.1 — an unusually explicit (if enormous) constant for this literature.

## Authors & Credibility

Junbin Gao is a well-established senior researcher (business analytics / machine learning, Sydney) and Andi Han works on Riemannian optimization and geometric ML — neither is a household name in the PAC-learning-theory community specifically, which makes the density of correct engagement with the recent multiclass literature ([BCD+22], [CEH+26], [Pab26], [HMZ24], [MQZ26], [HMMS26], [AHV25]) notable. The credibility signal that matters more than affiliation here: the main theorems are stated to be machine-checked in Lean 4, with Section D documenting exactly what was verified and in which form, and a public GitHub development. For a paper whose whole content is a chain of constant-tracking inequalities composed across three sample blocks, that is the right form of evidence, and it substantially de-risks the "is the proof actually correct" question — though a reader should still check *what* was formalized versus what was assumed as an imported interface.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-high, honestly scoped).** The authors are refreshingly explicit that Thm 3.3's *mechanism* is not new: "the proof route is the counting-plus-Bernstein one of the compression literature, and the claim of novelty is confined to the interface, not the mechanism," and they name [GKN17, Thm 8] as the closest antecedent. Likewise the three-block skeleton is [CEH+26, Algorithm 4] verbatim, the first-stage sharpening is [Pab26, Cor 1.1], and the pseudo-cube is [BCD+22]. The genuinely new pieces are (i) the comparator-facing form of the compression bound, (ii) the composition argument that preserves coefficient one through a *random* menu, and (iii) the fixed-$L^\star$ lower bound. That is a real but incremental contribution built on a lot of imported machinery.

**Rigor (high, with explicit self-flagged gaps).** Constants are tracked end-to-end; measurability is handled by an explicit assumption (2.1); the sigma-field bookkeeping for the three-block conditioning is deferred to Section A.3 rather than waved at. Remark 4.7 is a good example of intellectual honesty: the exact-$L^\star$, single-distribution form of Thm 4.1 *genuinely requires* the constructed product class, because "for an entangled class the two structures need not admit a common distribution whose infimum risk is computable exactly" — i.e. the lower bound is about a specific engineered family, not about every class with those dimensions.

**Paper-specific weaknesses.**
- *The upper and lower bounds do not match on logs.* The upper bound carries $d_{\mathrm{N}}\log^3(en)$ and $d_{\mathrm{DS}}\log^2(en)$; the lower bound has none, and the paper states plainly that Thm 4.1 "does not address the sharp $\log(1/\delta)$ dependence." So "optimal" means optimal up to polylogs, not sharp — weaker than the binary state of the art it is modeled on.
- *A real gap in the list section (Remark 5.5).* The list upper bound's fluctuation scales with $r\,d_{\mathrm{N}}$ while the lower bound scales with $d^r_{\mathrm{N}}$, and $d^r_{\mathrm{N}} \le d_{\mathrm{N}}$ "with arbitrarily large gaps possible." The authors conjecture the truth is $d^r_{\mathrm{N}}$ up to $\mathrm{poly}(r)$ but leave it open; similarly the deterministic terms are stated at $d_{\mathrm{DS}}$ (upper) versus $d^r_{\mathrm{DS}}$ (lower). The list section is therefore a demonstration that the architecture composes, not a tight result.
- *The lower-bound family needs a growing alphabet* (Remark 4.6): a separation $d_{\mathrm{DS}} \gg d_{\mathrm{N}}$ requires $K \ge \exp(\Omega(d_{\mathrm{DS}}/d_{\mathrm{N}}))$. At bounded alphabet the two dimensions collapse to within a log factor and "the interpolation question degenerates." The headline two-dimensional result is thus most meaningful in a regime (astronomically many labels) that is far from typical practice — the paper says so, but a casual reader could easily over-read the practical relevance.
- *Lower bound is in expectation, not high probability.* Thm 4.1 bounds $\mathbb{E}[L(A(S))]-L^\star$; the paper converts to "excess at least half the RHS with probability at least half the RHS" via boundedness, which matches Thm 3.1 only "at fixed confidence, up to logarithmic factors."
- *Computational cost is unbounded.* Thm 3.1 explicitly says the learner "may be computationally inefficient"; the compression and one-inclusion routines need not be efficient. There is no discussion of whether any efficient variant exists.
- *Constant $C$ is absurd in practice* ($\ge 2636 \cdot 5 c_1 c_2$-scale). This is normal for the genre, but it means the bound is a statement about rates only.

**Reproducibility.** As a theory paper, reproducibility means proof-checkability; here it is unusually strong (Lean 4 + public repo). No datasets, code for experiments, or hyperparameters are involved.

**Significance.** Within multiclass PAC learning this settles a clean, well-posed open question and gives the field the multiclass analogue of the binary fixed-risk benchmark $\sqrt{L^\star(d+\log(1/\delta))/n} + (d+\log(1/\delta))/n$. Outside learning theory the practical impact is essentially zero in the short term.

**Red flags.** No serious overclaiming detected. The abstract's "We close the gap" is defensible given that both directions are proved, and the "up to logarithmic factors" qualifier appears in the abstract-adjacent text and repeatedly in the body. The one place to watch is the word "optimal" in Thm 3.1's discussion, which is optimality up to polylogs and up to the $\log(1/\delta)$ dependence.

## Strengths

- Both directions (upper and lower) are proved in the same paper, at the same $L^\star$-localized granularity — rarer than it sounds in this literature, where upper and lower bounds usually come from different groups and different constructions.
- The selective-menu decomposition (Lemma 2.4) is a genuinely clean idea: two logically different failure modes (correct label missing from the menu; wrong choice inside the menu) are charged to two different dimensions and provably never merge, and the lower bound certifies that this division of labor is intrinsic rather than an artifact of the analysis.
- Excellent scholarly hygiene: every imported result is restated as a formal lemma in Section A.1 "with its source locator, convention mapping, and applicability check," so the reader does not have to reconcile notation across five papers.
- Explicit negative result about method transfer: the paper explains *why* the $K$-ary Hamming-graph route fails (it measures Graph dimension, which can be infinite when DS is finite), which is useful methodological knowledge independent of the main theorem.
- Machine-checked main theorems plus a fully explicit constant chain.
- Honest, well-placed remarks (4.6, 4.7, 5.4, 5.5, 5.6) that pre-empt most of the obvious objections rather than hiding them.

## Limitations

- Optimality holds only up to polylogarithmic factors; the sharp $\log(1/\delta)$ dependence is not resolved.
- The finite-alphabet assumption is required, and the interesting regime ($d_{\mathrm{DS}} \gg d_{\mathrm{N}}$) forces $K \ge \exp(\Omega(d_{\mathrm{DS}}/d_{\mathrm{N}}))$.
- The learner is information-theoretic and possibly computationally intractable; no efficient implementation or empirical validation is offered.
- The list-learning bounds have an unresolved $r\,d_{\mathrm{N}}$ vs $d^r_{\mathrm{N}}$ (and $d_{\mathrm{DS}}$ vs $d^r_{\mathrm{DS}}$) dimension gap, explicitly left open; the $r=1$ specialization also has worse constants than the dedicated Thms 3.1/4.1.
- Theorem 5.2 restricts to even $D$ with $2 \le D \le 2d$, and Remark 5.6 notes this mirrors Thm 4.1's unconditional diagonal only because the width-$2r$ separation family is imported as an interface (verified by hand only at $D=2$).
- Lower bound is stated in expectation over a constructed product class; it does not imply anything about a specific "entangled" class with the same dimensions.
- This is not a fast-rate result in the local-Rademacher sense of [BBM05]: localization enters only through $L^\star$, with no Massart or Tsybakov noise condition.

## Relevance

如果你（用户）关注学习理论中的样本复杂度刻画、样本压缩（sample compression）与 PAC 可学性的组合维度（Natarajan / DS / Graph dimension），这篇是本年度多类 PAC 理论线上的一块关键拼图，且其"把随机 menu 与保系数一的相对压缩组合起来"的技术手法对任何需要在非平凡中间阶段做局部化泛化界的工作都有借鉴价值。若你的兴趣偏向可落地的深度学习方法或实验驱动研究，这篇纯理论文章（无实验、学习器计算不可行）参考价值有限，可只读 Section 1 与 Table 1/2。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Can the $\log^3(en)$ and $\log^2(en)$ factors in Thm 3.1 be removed? Stability-based compression [HK19] removes logs in the binary case — is there a stable variant of the menu finalizer, and would stability survive the random-menu conditioning that Thm 3.3 was specifically designed to avoid needing?
2. Remark 5.5's open problem: is there a fixed-menu list compression scheme whose empirical domination holds against every $r$-tuple with description length $\widetilde{O}(d^r_{\mathrm{N}})$ rather than $O(r\,d_{\mathrm{N}})$? The authors suggest replacing the block-$S_1$ cover with one built from a realizable list learner [CP23] to reach $d^r_{\mathrm{DS}}$ — how much of the imported machinery breaks?
3. Is the exponential alphabet requirement (Remark 4.6) an artifact of the construction or genuinely necessary? Does a two-dimensional optimistic rate exist for infinite label spaces where Graph dimension is infinite but DS is finite?
4. Can the exact-$L^\star$ lower bound be extended beyond constructed product classes, i.e. is there a distribution-dependent statement of the form "for every class and every $L^\star$ realizable by that class"? Remark 4.7 shows the current proof needs disjoint supports.
5. Does the coefficient-one relative compression theorem give anything for real-valued or partial-concept classes [AHHM21], where the menu abstraction plausibly still makes sense?
6. What exactly is covered by the Lean 4 formalization — the composition and constant tracking, or also the imported [CEH+26]/[Pab26]/[BCD+22] interfaces? The latter would be a much stronger claim and is worth checking against Section D.
7. Is there an efficient learner achieving even the $\sqrt{L^\star d_{\mathrm{N}}/n}$ term for structurally simple multiclass classes (e.g. linear multiclass predictors), where the general compression machinery is overkill?
