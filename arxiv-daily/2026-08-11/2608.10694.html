Why this matters

Compile a prompt once on a nano-tier or self-hosted model, deploy anywhere — removing the main cost objection to reflective prompt optimization.

⚠ read critically
Critical edge

The pooled +2.8% headline is half-composed of one family (Mixed Qwen, 24 of 48 points); a variance-weighted mean sits slightly below zero — the CI is not four independent replications.

Methodology

cost model · 3-role split · zero-shot upward transfer

The work reframes GEPA-style reflective prompt evolution as multi-fidelity optimization, splitting the LLM's role in the search loop into three independently-priced parts. A measured cost model shows >96% of search tokens go to answering, so the answerer's price tier — not the deploy model's — sets the bill.

A · ANSWERER
Cheapest-tier fitness
gpt-4.1-nano / gemini-2.5-flash-lite / self-hosted Qwen3-8B runs every validation evaluation.
B · REFLECTOR
Strong variation op
Sonnet-5 / gpt-5.5 / gemini-3.1-pro reads rollout traces and rewrites prompts — rare but decisive.
C · DEPLOY
Zero-shot upward
Evolved prompt deployed unchanged on any equal-or-stronger tier; one search amortizes many.

Transfer is measured as target regret R = J_t(π*_t) − J_t(π*_s): the cheap-source prompt vs. the tier's own full-cost prompt, both scored on the target tier. "Positive" is judged against the no-transfer baseline, not the untrained seed — so the +27-pt nano→luna jump on IFBench is attributed to deploy capability, not transfer. Every dollar figure multiplies logged per-call tokens by three independent vendor price tables, so costs re-derive under any future schedule without re-running.

Strengths & Limitations

both sides of the ledger

Strengths +

  • Measured, not assumed cost backbone: >96% of tokens on answering; reflection is 0.08–4.95% of calls yet a median 27% of spend.
  • Three independent vendor ladders (Claude / GPT / Gemini) plus a self-hosted Qwen family — genuine third-party replication.
  • Neutral deploy target gpt-4.1-mini (seen by no reflector) shows transfer is reflector-family agnostic (Table 12).
  • Both sides reported: a break-even query volume N* for the recurring cost of longer prompts, with caching shown to cut both ways.
  • Honest framing throughout: nano→luna gains attributed to deploy capability; worst shortfall labeled descriptive, not guaranteed.

Limitations −

  • Bounded below by cheap-tier competence: if the answerer scores ≈0 the fitness landscape flattens and search stagnates — no experiment locates this floor.
  • Bounded above by prompt-insensitive tiers: LiveBench-Math seed already scores 41.5–72.1, so "parity" there is a near-null result.
  • n=3 seeds; single-benchmark role ablation; single-benchmark weak-reflector arm; pooled statistic half-driven by one family.
  • Qwen serving compute excluded from all cost figures; longer evolved prompts add recurring inference cost not folded into headline multipliers.
  • Evolved prompts on both arms embed memorized validation-set answers — complicates interpreting the val→test gap.

Open Questions

for further exploration

Does the explicitness account survive a real intervention — mechanically expanding the full-cost prompt into a checklist at matched content and re-measuring transfer?

How far down can the answerer floor go before the fitness landscape flattens — a 1–2B model, or a deliberately degraded one?

Does the recipe hold when the deploy tier is genuinely stronger in capability, not merely more expensive? (The Gemini inversion: 3.5-flash > 2.5-pro.)

How much of the transferred gain is validation-set memorization baked into the prompt? A decontamination test would settle instruction quality vs. leakage.

Does the decoupling hold for agentic programs with tool calls, where a cheap answerer may fail structurally (malformed calls) and break the ranking assumption?

Can the residual be predicted ex ante — a cheap diagnostic (e.g. seed-prompt score gap) forecasting whether a (task, tier) pair will transfer?
