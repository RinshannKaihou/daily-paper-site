> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 MOSAIC，把 MoE 缩放律与一个算子级 MFU/显存性能模型耦合成单步混合整数非线性规划，证明在纯 model-FLOPs 预算下 MoE 稀疏度最优解永远贴在数据支撑上边界（无内点解），只有换成"集群可交付 FLOPs"预算后才出现内点最优（约 S*≈0.915–0.963），并在 32 节点 B200 × 20 天预算下选出 Nact=14.5B、S=0.956、G=4 的配置（loss 1.3898、1.23×10²³ model FLOPs），比"消耗 model FLOPs 最多"的候选（1.94×10²³，多花约 36%）loss 更低。

> **TL;DR:** MOSAIC folds a systems performance model into the scaling-law stage for sparse MoEs. Fit on ~150 from-scratch MoE runs (Ntot 0.22B–79.24B, Nact 0.104B–2.72B, joint-law R²=0.99877, held-out RMSE 0.00779 nats), the paper shows the model-FLOPs-optimal sparsity is boundary-seeking (S*=Smax), while the cluster-aware optimum is interior and 0.031 nats better on a 4-node × 5-day B200 envelope. On a 32-node/20-day B200 budget the MOSAIC optimum (Nact=14.5B, S=0.956, G=4, L=1.3898 at 1.23×10²³ model FLOPs) beats the max-model-FLOPs candidate that burns 1.94×10²³ FLOPs (~36% more) — i.e. the Chinchilla/Abnar-style compute-optimal baseline misorders configurations. The MFU predictor lands at 9.6%/9.0%/13.2% MAPE for 700M/5.4B/18B active-parameter sweeps.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 MOSAIC 框架**：把架构选择 Z、token 预算 D、并行执行方案 Pℓ 写成一个双层、非凸的混合整数非线性规划，目标是缩放律预测 loss，约束是 `Cmodel(Z,D) ≤ max_{Pℓ} Cdeliverable(Z,Pℓ,B)`，其中 `Cdeliverable = Cpeak · MFU(Z,Pℓ,B) · ηgood`。关键概念转变：算力预算不再是与架构无关的常数，而是随架构和并行布局变化的量。
- **新拟合四维 MoE 缩放律** `L(Ntot, S, D, G)`：在 Chinchilla 形式上加入稀疏度 S = 1 − Nact/Ntot 与"专家切分因子" G = dff/dexpert，并把 G 放进 (1−S)^δ · Ntot^γ 的交互项分母里。G 是本文相对 Abnar et al. (2025) 新增的轴。
- **给出边界性结论**：在标定区间内，固定 model FLOPs 预算下 ∂L*/∂S = 0 无内点解，L*(S) 单调下降，最优稀疏度永远等于数据支撑上界 Smax；也就是说纯 FLOPs 优化退化成"稀疏度越大越好"，无法给出可部署的处方。
- **算子级性能模型（开源为 ScalePlan）**：用微基准（dense GEMM、grouped GEMM、SDPA/FlashAttention、routing、vocab-parallel 交叉熵、NCCL 各集合通信带宽曲线、per-kernel CPU dispatch gap）驱动的解析迭代时间模型，只保留一个显式标定项 χsys（跨节点 straggler / 单节点深流水线 overlap）。
- **真实集群验证**：在 AWS p6-B200 上做 MFU 预测验证与分阶段预训练验证，展示 loss 排序在 model-FLOPs 轴与硬件算力轴之间会翻转。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes architecture–systems co-design as a single mixed-integer nonlinear program (MINLP) rather than the conventional two-stage pipeline (scaling law → systems tuning), as exemplified by the Kimi-K2 report which treats the two separately.
- Fits a G-augmented joint MoE scaling law `L = a/Ntot^α + b/D^β + c/(1−S)^λ + j/((1−S)^δ Ntot^γ G^η) + e`, with G entering only the interaction term so that finer experts discount the sparsity penalty at fixed capacity.
- Proves empirically that the compute-optimal sparsity has no interior optimum inside the calibrated range, motivating the switch from `Cmodel` to `Cdeliverable`.
- Releases an operator-level MFU/memory/layout predictor (ScalePlan, github.com/dmlc/ScalePlan) that explicitly prices expert dispatch/combine, all-to-all as a function of EP topology, 1F1B pipeline bubbles, vocabulary-stage imbalance, and CPU-dispatch-bound execution — overheads a pure roofline analysis (the closest prior work, Sun et al. 2026) misses.
- Derives a systems design rule: the pipeline fill ratio r_fill = n_mb/P_PP dominates layout choice, and expert parallelism should stay inside the scale-up (intra-node) domain.

## Methodology

The pipeline has three interlocking components. **(1) The scaling law.** ~150 from-scratch decoder-only MoE pretraining runs (first block dense, all others MoE, one always-active shared expert à la DeepSeek-V3, SwiGLU experts, Switch-style token-choice routing with Gumbel proposal and a loss-free load-balancing bias controller, AdamW, bf16, single pass over tokens). Coverage: Ntot ∈ [0.22, 79.24]B, Nact ∈ [0.104, 2.72]B, S ∈ [0.499, 0.981], G ∈ {1,2,4,6,8}, E ∈ {8…512}, K ∈ {1…8}, D ∈ [2.88, 56.38]B tokens (TPP ∈ [10.8, 70]), across four (Llayers, d) families. Fitting minimizes a Huber objective (δ=0.1) on log L via L-BFGS-B in log-coefficient space; the top-10% of runs by Nact within each G-stratum are held out for upward extrapolation. Confidence intervals come from a warm-started wild bootstrap, and each coefficient is labeled *identified* or *ridge*.

**(2) The performance model.** Rather than a closed-form roofline, the iteration time is assembled from measured lookup tables: achieved GEMM utilization as a function of (M,N,K) and precision; grouped-GEMM efficiency keyed by the per-expert tile M_expert = ⌈T_seq·B_micro·K/E⌉ (the paper notes costing this as one dense GEMM over-credits utilization by up to an order of magnitude in the dropless regime); NCCL bus-bandwidth curves that deviate from analytic link-peak estimates by up to 8× in both directions. Three departures from naive roofline are modeled explicitly: a CPU-dispatch floor `t_disp = L_stage·κ(E_loc)·t_gap + t_host` (with a kernel-count law linear in local experts) taken as `max(t_cmp, t_disp)`; a corrected 1F1B bubble fraction `f_bubble = (P_PP−1)/(P_VPP·n_mb)` that removes an earlier double-counting of fill/drain; and a vocabulary-stage imbalance term for the last-stage LM head plus fused cross-entropy. A single fitted multiplier χ_sys captures inter-node A2A straggler, deep-pipeline hops, and intra-node overlap credit; single-node shallow-pipeline configs get χ_sys = 1.

**(3) The search.** A "geometry scaling ladder" pins how depth and width co-scale (Llayers = n_head = 4q, d_head = 128, dff = 2.5d), so a single seed q climbs the model-scale axis while (E, K, G) are searched on top; S, Ntot, Nact are *derived*, not set. The MINLP is solved by structure-exploiting enumeration (with a closed-form elimination of D) over q ∈ {8…18}, E ∈ {4…512}, K ∈ {1,2,4,6,8}, G ∈ {1,2,4,8}, TPP ∈ [40,150], with a guard S ≥ 0.7 to stay inside the calibrated region.

## Results & Highlights

- **Scaling-law fit quality.** The joint L(Ntot,S,D,G) law attains R² = 0.99877 over all runs and a held-out RMSE of **0.00779 nats** on the largest 10% of runs by Nact; the earlier per-G Abnar-style fits of Eq. (9) reach held-out RMSE **0.0002–0.0037** (under half a percent relative error) but require different coefficients per G, which is the stated justification for adding the G axis. Only two of eleven coefficients are *identified*: β = 0.2070 [0.206, 0.208] and η = 0.9513 [0.911, 0.954]; the other nine sit on a flat ridge.
- **MFU prediction accuracy.** Mean absolute percentage error is **9.6% / 9.0% / 13.2%** for the 700M / 5.4B / 18B active-parameter sweeps on p6-B200 (4–64 nodes), all under the claimed 15% bar. Top-5 predicted-vs-measured rank overlap is **100%** for every 5.4B and 18B sweep and **80%** for 700M; Top-10 is 100% / 100% / 90%. Layout choice matters: measured MFU spreads by up to **3.1×** across parallelism configurations, almost all explained by r_fill = n_mb/P_PP (collapsing as r_fill → 1, saturating at r_fill ≥ 4).
- **Compute-optimal vs cluster-optimal (headline).** Under a fixed 32-node / 20-day B200 envelope (Cpeak = 9.95×10²³ FLOPs, ηgood = 1.0), the MOSAIC optimum is q=12, Llayers=48, E=96, K=2, G=4, Nact=14.5B, S=0.956, predicted MFU 12.38%, **loss 1.3898 on 1.23×10²³ model FLOPs**. The candidate that converts the *same* hardware ceiling into the most model FLOPs — a denser G=2, E=32 geometry at **1.94×10²³ FLOPs (~36% more compute)** — lands at a *higher* loss. So the standard compute-optimal ranking (the baseline here is model-FLOPs-only Chinchilla/Abnar-style allocation) misorders the candidates.
- **Systems cost of the FLOPs-optimal prescription.** At world size 512, the Cmodel-optimal configuration pays **1.70× wall-clock per delivered model FLOP** when sweeping S at G=4, and **2.66×** when sweeping G at S=0.958, relative to the densest/coarsest configuration — i.e. the loss-optimal-under-FLOPs config is the systems-slowest. Median MFU is ~8% for G=8 geometries versus **13–20%** for G=2.
- **Interior optimum appears.** On a 4-node × 5-day envelope with Cmodel = 3.3×10²¹, the FLOPs prescription S* = Smax = 0.985 is infeasible (feasible boundary S_feas^max = 0.96); re-optimizing under the hardware envelope yields an interior sparsity whose predicted loss is **0.031 nats lower**. Across budget sweeps (10–40 days at 32 nodes; 16–128 nodes at 30 days), S* sits on a **plateau of ~0.915–0.963** with no monotone budget trend, G* = 4 at most budgets, and Nact* stays at **14–15B** across the day sweep but grows to **34B and 58B** at the two largest node budgets.
- **Staged real-run validation.** Four configurations selected on an 8-node/10-day envelope (each trained >200B tokens) reproduce the predicted MFU ordering **exactly**, though normalized predicted MFU (0.60 / 0.50 / 0.94 / 1.00) overstates the measured spread (0.84 / 0.69 / 0.91 / 1.00). ID 1 sustains 0.84 vs ID 2's 0.69 normalized MFU, so the same hardware buys ID 1 ~**1.2×** the model computation; ID 2 leads on the model-FLOPs axis while ID 1 leads on the Cmodel/MFU hardware-cost axis — the claimed ordering flip. These runs reach **15–20× the largest budget in the fitting set** (which tops out at 8.1×10²⁰ FLOPs).

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors — Soumajyoti Sarkar, Yuxin Tang, and Sheng Zha — are at Amazon AGI Foundations. Sheng Zha is a long-standing contributor to the DMLC/Apache MXNet and GluonNLP ecosystem, and the released estimator lives under `github.com/dmlc/ScalePlan`, consistent with that lineage. Sarkar has prior published work on sparse MoE training (self-cited as Sarkar et al., 2024). The acknowledgments name a substantial internal infrastructure team (including Volkan Cevher and Leonard Lausen), and the experiments consume a genuinely industrial budget: ~150 from-scratch pretraining runs plus multi-hundred-billion-token staged validation runs on AWS p6-b200.48xlarge nodes. This is credible frontier-adjacent infrastructure work; the compute scale is beyond what an academic group could replicate, which cuts both ways for verification.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-high).** The specific move — making the compute budget itself a function of the architecture via `Cdeliverable = Cpeak · MFU · ηgood`, then solving one MINLP over discrete geometries — is a clean and genuinely new formulation. The G (expert split factor) axis and the demonstration that the model-FLOPs optimum is boundary-seeking are incremental relative to Abnar et al. (2025), Krajewski et al. (2024) and Ludziejewski et al. (2025), all of whom already gesture at memory- or granularity-aware budgets. The paper is honest that Sun et al. (2026) already couple roofline analysis to architecture selection; the differentiator is the operator-level detail (expert dispatch/combine, kernel-launch overhead under fine-grained experts, EP-topology-dependent all-to-all).

**Rigor (mixed, with specific soft spots).**
1. *Only two coefficients are identified.* Nine of eleven scaling-law coefficients sit on a flat ridge, and the authors state outright that "uncertainty in the non-identified scaling law coefficients is not propagated through the discrete architecture search." The headline configuration is therefore the argmin of a surface whose parameters are mostly unpinned.
2. *Extreme extrapolation.* The 32-node/20-day optimum sits "about **150×** beyond our largest fit run." The staged runs sit 15–20× beyond. A 0.031-nat predicted gap at 150× extrapolation is not a number one should act on without error bars.
3. *The law contradicts its own data at the G boundary.* The fitted law predicts loss decreasing monotonically in G at matched (Ntot, S, D), but the observed runs "decrease up to G = 4 and flatten or slightly reverse at G = 8." η ≈ 0.95 is nonetheless labeled the paper's "robust observation." That is a real tension: the one identified geometry coefficient encodes a trend the measurements stop supporting exactly at the edge of the sweep.
4. *The validation is four runs.* The whole "loss ordering flips" claim rests on ID 1 vs ID 2 on a single 8-node envelope, and the paper concedes the two curves "converge and the margin narrows to the width of the smoothed trajectories." No seeds, no variance bands, no held-out validation loss — the fits use **smoothed training loss of the final checkpoint**, single-epoch, and there are **zero downstream/benchmark evaluations** anywhere in the paper.
5. *Weak/absent comparators.* The only baseline is the abstract "model-FLOPs-optimal" allocation, i.e. a straw target the authors themselves construct from their own law. There is no head-to-head against Ludziejewski's memory-aware budget or Sun et al.'s roofline co-design, both cited as the closest prior work. A reader cannot tell how much of the gain comes from *any* systems awareness versus MOSAIC's particular operator-level fidelity.
6. *Data quirks and unexercised machinery.* ηgood is fixed to 1.0 in every experiment, so the goodput term of Eq. (2) — checkpointing, failures, input-pipeline stalls, the very things that dominate real 512-GPU runs — is never actually tested. The TPP grid extends to 150 while the training data only reaches TPP = 70, roughly 2× beyond calibration, and the overtrained regime that frontier labs actually operate in (TPP ≫ 100) is explicitly out of scope. Layout search restricts P_TP = 1 and P_CP = 1, and the MFU design rules are stated "under no memory pressure."
7. *Missing ablation.* There is no ablation isolating which component of the performance model actually flips the recommendation. Given the authors' own admission that "the absence of a clear monotone trend in the optimal expert split factor G may itself be an artifact of that [basic Megatron-Core] stack's systems penalty," an ablation over, say, fused/overlapped all-to-all kernels would be the single most informative missing experiment — as written, the interior optimum could be an artifact of an unoptimized MoE stack rather than a law of hardware.

**Reproducibility (good by industrial standards).** ScalePlan is released with calibration data and the layout search, so the estimator numbers are regenerable from a model + cluster spec. The scaling-law dataset coverage is documented in detail (Tables 6–8), the ladder is fully specified, and the routing/LFLB algorithm is given as pseudocode. But the ~150 pretraining runs and the >200B-token staged runs are not reproducible outside a large GPU fleet, and it is unclear whether the fitted coefficients or run-level losses are released.

**Significance (high, if the principle rather than the number is taken).** The framing "practitioners do not buy model FLOPs, they buy GPU-hours on a fixed cluster over a fixed wall-clock window" is correct and under-served by the scaling-law literature. The concrete deliverable — treat MFU as an architecture-dependent multiplier inside the scaling-law optimization — is directly actionable for anyone planning an MoE pretraining run.

**Red flags.** The title ("Compute-Optimal Is Not Cluster-Optimal") overstates what four staged runs and a 0.031-nat predicted delta establish; the authors partially retract this themselves in the limitations, writing that "our evidence more strongly supports the hardware-aware ranking and co-design principle than any single geometry as a universal cluster optimum" — a commendably honest sentence that a reader skimming only the abstract would miss. The absence of any evaluation beyond training loss is the largest gap, since sparsity and expert granularity are known to affect downstream task performance differently from perplexity.

## Strengths

- The core reframing (architecture-dependent deliverable FLOPs budget) is simple, correct, and immediately usable by practitioners planning a pretraining run.
- The performance model is unusually well-engineered for a scaling-law paper: measured lookup tables instead of hand-tuned constants, an explicit single calibration term, and correctly identified failure modes of naive roofline (grouped-GEMM tile size, CPU dispatch floor, vocab-stage imbalance, NCCL curves off by up to 8× from analytic estimates).
- It is released as open source (ScalePlan), which is rare for internal systems tooling of this kind.
- The limitations section is genuinely candid — it names the extrapolation problem, the non-propagated coefficient uncertainty, the geometry-ladder dependence, and the possibility that the G result is an artifact of the Megatron-Core stack.
- The paper distinguishes MFU from HFU and refuses credit for activation recomputation, which is the correct (and often fudged) convention.
- Reporting the corrected 1F1B bubble formula, including the double-counting bug in the earlier expression, is a small but useful systems contribution on its own.

## Limitations

- Every recovered optimum is conditional on one geometry ladder (Llayers = n_head = 4q, d_head = 128, dff = 2.5d), one hardware platform (B200 / p6 with EFA), and one training stack (Megatron-Core), with P_TP = P_CP = 1.
- G ≤ 8 throughout, so the finest-grained-expert regime used by DeepSeek-scale models is not probed; the law's G-monotonicity is unverified past the point where observations flatten.
- No overtrained regime (TPP capped at 150, data only to 70) — arguably the regime that matters most for deployed models.
- Global batch size fixed at 1024 across all runs, so batch-size/parallelism co-optimization is untouched; the authors list it as future work.
- Compute and exposed communication are costed largely additively, with overlap only for DP collectives — modern stacks with persistent/fused overlapping kernels would change the MFU landscape and possibly the recommendation.
- No downstream evaluation, no validation loss, no seed variance, ηgood ≡ 1.

## Relevance

如果你（用户）关注 MoE 架构设计、缩放律拟合，或大规模预训练的系统效率（MFU、EP all-to-all、流水线气泡），这篇是少见的把"缩放律"和"并行布局搜索"写进同一个优化问题的工作，且附带开源的算子级性能估计器 ScalePlan，可直接用于自己集群上的架构选型。其对"稀疏度最优解在纯 FLOPs 预算下贴边界"的论证，也对任何在复现 DeepSeek/Kimi 式 MoE 配置时纠结 E/K/粒度取值的人有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the interior sparsity optimum survives a modern MoE stack with fused, overlapped dispatch/combine kernels (DeepEP-style)? The authors flag this as a possible artifact — an ablation swapping the all-to-all cost curve for an overlapped one would settle whether MOSAIC recovers a property of hardware or of Megatron-Core.
- What happens to the recommendation once ηgood is realistic (0.85–0.95 with checkpointing and failure recovery) and node-count-dependent? Larger clusters have worse goodput, which should push the optimum toward smaller Ntot in a way the current sweeps (ηgood = 1) cannot see.
- Does the (Ntot, S, G) representation transfer across backbone geometry families, or is η ≈ 0.95 specific to the dff = 2.5d, d_head = 128 ladder?
- If the nine ridge coefficients were bootstrapped through the discrete search, how often would the argmin configuration change? A posterior over the top-k configurations would be far more actionable than a single point optimum 150× outside the fit regime.
- Do the cluster-optimal geometries hold up on downstream benchmarks, or does the loss-equivalent but sparser/denser trade-off change task performance (e.g. reasoning vs recall) in ways perplexity hides?
- Can the MOSAIC objective be extended to jointly price inference cost, given that an S = 0.956, 250B-total-parameter model is far more expensive to serve than an equal-loss denser one?
