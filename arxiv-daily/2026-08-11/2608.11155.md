> **一句话总结：** 作者用 LLTFI 对自研 C++ CKKS 实现（C-CKKS）做穷举式单比特翻转注入，发现 Vanilla/RNS CKKS 的客户端错误容忍度只有两种固定模式（加法模式与乘法模式），其边界由 log∆=25 与 logQ=60 直接决定（64 位系数中约 25–59 位为脆弱区），一旦服务端做同态乘法，乘法模式就会压倒加法模式；而启用 NTT 或 RNS+NTT 后任何单比特翻转都会导致解密/解码彻底失败，最严重的 MREP 可达 2.74e+13%。

> **TL;DR:** This 3-page characterization study exhaustively flips every bit of every polynomial coefficient in a custom CKKS implementation (C-CKKS, built on OpenFHE/HEaaN/SEAL/PyFHE) using the LLTFI fault injector, and measures Maximum Relative Error Percentage (MREP) after the full encode→encrypt→compute→decrypt→decode pipeline. In the Fig. 3 configuration (N=64, Gaps=2, logQ=60, log∆=25, ε=0.1), Vanilla CKKS masks bit-flips below the scaling factor (bit index <25) and above the modulus (bit index ≥60) — i.e. roughly 35 of 64 bit positions are vulnerable — while NTT and RNS+NTT configurations mask essentially nothing and turn every single bit-flip into catastrophic decryption failure, with severe SDC reaching 2.74e+13% MREP. There is no competing baseline method: the paper is a first-of-its-kind vulnerability characterization rather than a defense, and it reports no error-detection or mitigation numbers.

## 核心贡献

- **把 FHE 客户端的错误容忍度归纳为两种模式**：加法模式（Addition pattern）与乘法模式（Multiplication pattern），并在 Vanilla CKKS 与 RNS CKKS 两种算术后端上分别刻画。
- **指出同态乘法是最敏感的操作**：只要服务端执行了一次乘法，整条流水线的容错画像就退化为乘法模式（"multiplication pattern domination"），加法带来的掩蔽效应被抹掉。
- **揭示优化本身破坏容错性**：NTT 与 RNS+NTT 这两种为性能而生的标准优化会把局部比特错误扩散到整条多项式，使任何单比特翻转都造成灾难性的解密/解码失败，连"模式"都无法区分。
- **给出 ptx / c0 / c1 的非对称性解释**：gap 系数与第 N/2 个系数的翻转在明文与 c0 中被完全掩蔽、但在 c1 中不被掩蔽，原因是 gap 只存在于被编码进 c0 的明文里，而 c1 是依赖密钥采样得到的分量。
- **声称容错画像与参数、数据无关**：作者主张这些 resilience heuristics 是 CKKS 方案本身的内禀性质，而非某组 (Q, ∆, N) 或某份输入数据的偶然结果。
- **提供可控实验平台**：自研 C-CKKS 支持逐项开关 RNS / NTT / RNS+NTT 与 64 位系数表示，使得"消融式"地隔离每个优化对可靠性的影响成为可能。

## Key Contributions

- A taxonomy of client-side FHE error resilience into exactly two profiles — an *Addition pattern* and a *Multiplication pattern* — observed consistently across Vanilla CKKS and RNS CKKS.
- Identification of homomorphic multiplication as the dominant sensitivity driver: whenever a multiplication runs on the server, the overall resilience profile collapses to the multiplication pattern regardless of the surrounding additions.
- A concrete mechanism for masking in Vanilla CKKS: flips whose magnitude falls below the scaling factor ∆ or above the modulus Q are absorbed and never reach the decoded output; RNS CKKS loses this ∆/Q masking because each coefficient is spread over multiple limbs.
- A negative result for performance optimizations: NTT and RNS+NTT arithmetic are *fully* susceptible — any single bit-flip yields catastrophic decryption/decoding failure, so no addition/multiplication pattern can even be defined there.
- An explanation of the ptx/c0 vs c1 asymmetry rooted in scheme structure (gaps live in the plaintext and are encoded into c0; c1 is the sampled secret-key-dependent polynomial).
- A claim of parameter- and data-independence for the observed heuristics, positioning them as properties of CKKS itself rather than artifacts of the tested configuration.

## Methodology

The threat model is a *single transient bit-flip* on the client side, motivated by silent data corruption (SDC) at scale (Dixit et al., 2021) — the concern being that in HE the data is *already* noisy by construction (LWE), so a hardware-induced error is statistically indistinguishable from the intrinsic encryption noise and slips past conventional detection.

The fault-injection campaign is exhaustive rather than sampled: each bit of every polynomial coefficient — in both the plaintext p(X) and both ciphertext components c = (c0(X), c1(X)) — is flipped *in sequence*, one at a time. After each injection the entire HE pipeline (encode → encrypt → server-side computation → decrypt → decode) is executed, and the recovered data is compared against the original using **Maximum Relative Error Percentage (MREP)**. Outcomes are bucketed into four severity classes: Masked (MREP ≤ ε), Minor SDC (≤10%), Moderate SDC (10%–100%), and Severe SDC (≥100%, with the color scale topping out at 2.74e+13%).

The substrate is **C-CKKS**, a custom C++ CKKS implementation the authors wrote based on OpenFHE, HEaaN, SEAL and PyFHE, supporting native RNS, NTT, combined RNS+NTT, and 64-bit coefficient representation. "Vanilla CKKS" denotes both RNS and NTT disabled. Because each optimization is independently toggleable, the study can attribute reliability differences to a specific optimization — effectively an ablation over the arithmetic backend. Fault injection uses the open-source **LLTFI** framework (LLVM-level, framework-agnostic). Pipeline configurations swept include NoComp, Add, Mult, Rot, and compositions (Add+Rot, Add+Mult, Rot+Mult) plus bootstrapping (Add+Mult+Boot). All experiments ran on a single Intel i7-11700 with 32 GB RAM under Arch Linux 257.5-1.

## Results & Highlights

- **Vulnerable bit window is set by log∆ and logQ.** In the headline configuration (CKKS + Mult₁, Gaps=2, logQ=60, log∆=25, N=64, ε=0.1), Fig. 3 shows Vanilla CKKS masking flips in the low bits below the scaling factor (bit index < 25) and in the high bits at/above the modulus (bit index ≥ 60), leaving roughly **35 of 64 bit positions (~55%)** in the Minor/Moderate/Severe SDC bands. No baseline scheme is compared; the implicit reference point is the untouched (fault-free) pipeline.
- **Severity spans 14 orders of magnitude.** The MREP severity scale in Fig. 3 runs from Masked (≤ ε = 0.1) through Minor SDC (≤10%) and Moderate SDC (10%–100%) up to Severe SDC at **100% → 2.74e+13%**, i.e. a single flipped bit can inflate the decoded output's relative error by more than 13 orders of magnitude over the tolerance threshold.
- **NTT/RNS+NTT: masking rate drops to essentially zero.** Whereas Vanilla CKKS masks the sub-∆ and super-Q bit ranges, the NTT and RNS+NTT configurations are reported as **fully susceptible — every single bit-flip produces catastrophic decryption and decoding failure (100% failure, 0% masked)**, because the NTT butterfly diffuses one corrupted coefficient across the whole polynomial. This is the paper's sharpest delta: the same scheme, same parameters, differing only by an optimization flag, moves from ~45% masked bit positions to ~0%.
- **RNS removes ∆/Q resilience per coefficient.** In RNS CKKS individual coefficients show *no* scaling-factor or modulus masking (each coefficient is split across multiple limbs), and the gap / (N/2)-th coefficients that were resilient in Vanilla CKKS "exhibit no resilience and are completely corrupted."
- **Gap-coefficient asymmetry with Gaps=2.** With Gaps=2 (half the coefficient slots are gaps at N=64), flips on gap coefficients and on the N/2-th coefficient are *fully masked* in the plaintext and in c0 but **not** in c1 — visible as the periodic green stripes in the ptx/c0 panels of Fig. 3 versus the near-uniform red of the c1 panel.
- **Multiplication dominance.** Under Vanilla CKKS the gap and N/2 coefficients stay resilient only up to an error threshold once a multiplication is involved, and the authors state that whenever a server-side multiplication occurs the overall profile "consistently follows this pattern" — additions never rescue the profile.

Note on rigor: these are read off a single figure for a single configuration. The paper reports **no aggregate SDC rates, no per-operation tables, no variance across seeds, and no numbers at all for the Rot, Add+Rot, Rot+Mult, or bootstrapping configurations it says it swept.**

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** Fault-injection characterization of cryptographic kernels is not new, and the ∆/Q masking behavior is arguably a direct corollary of CKKS's approximate-arithmetic design — low-order bits sit under the rescaling noise floor, high-order bits get reduced mod Q. The genuinely useful and less obvious finding is the *inversion of the reliability/performance tradeoff*: the very optimizations everyone deploys (NTT, RNS+NTT) destroy the masking that makes Vanilla CKKS partially fault-tolerant. That deserves wider attention than the abstract's generic "multiplication is most sensitive" framing suggests.

**Rigor (weak, and the paper is honest that it is preliminary).** Concrete problems: (1) Only *one* figure of results for *one* configuration (N=64, logQ=60, log∆=25, Gaps=2) is shown, yet the paper asserts parameter- and data-independence in Section II-C with **zero supporting evidence** — no sweep over Q, ∆, N, or gap count is reported, and no data distribution is varied. That is the single biggest overclaim. (2) N=64 is a toy ring dimension; real CKKS deployments use N = 2^13–2^17, and the gap/(N/2) coefficient effects could plausibly scale differently. (3) The bootstrapping configuration (Add+Mult+Boot) is listed as evaluated but never reported — bootstrapping is exactly where noise-budget interactions with injected faults would be most interesting, so its absence is conspicuous. (4) There is no baseline at all — no comparison against other HE schemes (BFV/BGV/TFHE), no comparison against an existing HE fault-tolerance technique, and no detector to measure against. (5) The severity thresholds (ε=0.1, 10%, 100%) are asserted, not justified against any downstream application's accuracy requirement, so "Minor SDC" has no operational meaning for, say, an encrypted CNN inference.

**Reproducibility (poor as published).** C-CKKS is described as a "custom C++ implementation" with a footnote but **no repository link or artifact statement** in the text; LLTFI is open-source but the injection configs are not provided. Numbers are conveyed only through a colormap, so nothing can be checked numerically. Also, the writing shows signs of haste ("boostrapping", "Multiplication pattern dominates addition pattern" stated without a supporting plot), which lowers confidence in editorial care.

**Significance (potentially high, currently unrealized).** If the NTT-destroys-masking result holds at realistic parameters, it has real design implications: HE accelerators that pipeline NTT butterflies would need coefficient-level protection (ECC/residue checks) rather than relying on the scheme's intrinsic noise tolerance. But the paper stops at characterization — it proposes no detector, no cost model, and no mitigation.

**Red flags.** The parameter/data-independence claim unsupported by any sweep; a single figure carrying the entire empirical argument; evaluated-but-unreported configurations; absence of an artifact link; and an abstract that promises characterization of "how faults propagate and amplify" through multiplication while the body offers a qualitative mechanism rather than a propagation model.

## Strengths

- Attacks a real and under-studied gap: HE's intrinsic noise makes it structurally hostile to conventional SDC detection, and almost nobody has quantified that.
- Exhaustive (not sampled) single-bit injection over all coefficients of ptx, c0 and c1 gives complete coverage of the fault space at the tested configuration.
- The toggleable C-CKKS backend is the right experimental design — it isolates RNS, NTT, and RNS+NTT as independent variables against a Vanilla control.
- The mechanistic explanations (∆/Q masking; gaps encoded into c0 but absent from the sampled c1) are physically grounded, not just curve-fitting to the data.
- The NTT/RNS+NTT susceptibility result is actionable for hardware architects and is the kind of finding that reframes a performance decision as a reliability decision.
- The paper labels its own results "preliminary" rather than dressing them as definitive.

## Limitations

- Single-bit, single-transient fault model only — no multi-bit upsets, no stuck-at faults, no permanent faults, no faults injected during server-side computation (the title scopes it to *client-side*, but that means the most exposed party, the untrusted server, is unmodeled).
- Toy parameters: N=64, logQ=60, one scaling factor, Gaps=2, one CPU, one OS.
- No quantitative summary statistics — no percentage of injections leading to Masked/Minor/Moderate/Severe SDC per configuration, which is the natural headline metric and is trivially derivable from the data they already have.
- Parameter- and data-independence asserted without a sweep.
- Bootstrapping, rotation, and composite pipelines evaluated but not reported.
- No mitigation, detector, or overhead analysis; no link to end-application impact (e.g. how much a Severe SDC perturbs an encrypted logistic-regression or CNN output).
- No artifact/code release stated.

## Relevance

如果关注隐私保护机器学习（encrypted inference、FHE 加速器设计），这篇工作提示了一个常被忽略的耦合：为吞吐而启用的 NTT/RNS 优化会把 CKKS 原本"低位比特被 ∆ 掩蔽、高位比特被 Q 掩蔽"的天然容错性彻底抹掉，因此 FHE 加速器的可靠性预算不能沿用明文域的直觉。对做系统/硬件-算法协同设计的读者价值最大；纯算法或纯建模方向的读者可只取"乘法主导 + NTT 破坏掩蔽"这两个结论。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the ∆/Q masking boundaries hold at deployment-scale parameters (N = 2^13–2^16, multi-limb RNS with logQ in the hundreds)? The masked fraction should shrink or shift predictably — is there a closed-form model for the vulnerable bit window as a function of log∆ and logQ?
- What is the *end-to-end application* impact? A Severe SDC at 2.74e+13% MREP on one slot may or may not flip an encrypted classifier's decision; mapping MREP to task accuracy is the missing link.
- Since gap coefficients are masked in ptx and c0 but not c1, can gap slots be repurposed as a nearly free residue/checksum channel for SDC detection?
- What happens under bootstrapping, which the authors ran but did not report — does the noise refresh amplify or launder an injected fault?
- Can NTT be made fault-tolerant cheaply (e.g. algorithm-based fault tolerance / residue checks on the butterfly network), and what is the throughput cost relative to the resilience recovered?
- Does the same two-pattern taxonomy transfer to exact schemes (BFV/BGV) and to TFHE's bootstrapped gates, where there is no scaling-factor noise floor to hide sub-∆ flips?
- Is a fault distinguishable from intrinsic LWE noise by a statistical test on the ciphertext, or is the indistinguishability the paper posits actually provable?
