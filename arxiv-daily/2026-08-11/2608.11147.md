> **一句话总结：** 作者用自研 C++ CKKS 实现（C-CKKS）配合 LLTFI 注入单比特瞬时故障，发现同一个密文多项式 `c0^(1)` 在同态乘法中被"对称"污染（d0 和 d1 两处都翻转）时，低 19 个比特位（bit 0–18）的翻转全部被掩蔽、最大相对误差 MREP ≤ ε=0.1；而只污染 d1 一处时，全部 61 个可用比特位（bit 0–60）都产生 >100% 的严重静默数据损坏（SDC），最高 MREP 达 3.96e+03%——说明故障"何时/何处"发生比"是否发生"更决定 FHE 输出的正确性。

> **TL;DR:** A 2-page characterization study injecting single-bit transient faults into unoptimized CKKS ciphertext–ciphertext multiplication (own C++ implementation "C-CKKS", LLTFI injector, N=64, log Q=60, log Δ=25, gaps=2, ε=0.1). Measured by Maximum Relative Error Percentage (MREP), a fault that hits *both* algebraic uses of `c0` (in d0 and d1) leaves bits 0–18 fully masked (MREP ≤ 0.1), while the *same* operand faulted in only `d1` yields severe SDC (MREP > 100%, peaking at 3.96e+03%) across all 61 usable bit positions — i.e. a ~19-bit-plane collapse of the masking region. There is no external baseline system; the paper's own symmetric-injection case serves as the comparison point, and no aggregate SDC rates are reported.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"对称性破坏"作为 CKKS 乘法故障传播的核心机制。** `c0` 在乘法中被用了两次（贡献 d0 和 d1），`c1` 也被用了两次（贡献 d1 和 d2）。若单比特翻转只影响其中一次使用，代数对称性被破坏，解密时的抵消性质失效，残留随机项导致严重 SDC；若两次使用被一致污染，注入的故障与一个合法密文相乘，结果仍是"正确乘积 + 一个低幅度误差项"，掩蔽模式保持不变。
- **给出解析模型。** 把故障建模为误差多项式 `e_{i,j} = 2^j X^i`（第 i 个系数的第 j 位翻转），并展开重线性化后的密文：对称情形得到 `(c0^m, c1^m) + e_{i,j} × (c0', c1')`（仍是合法密文形式）；只污染 d1 的情形得到 `c_mult + e_{i,j} × (0, c1')`（不构成合法密文，故解密时无法抵消）。
- **实证刻画 12 个注入位点的完整比特×系数热力图。** 覆盖 `c0^(1)`、`c0^(2)`、`c1^(1)`、`c1^(2)`、中间量 `d0`、`d2` 以及部分积 `c0^(1)c1^(2)`、`c1^(1)c0^(2)`，按 MREP 分为 Masked (≤ε)、Minor SDC (≤10%)、Moderate SDC (10–100%)、Severe SDC (>100%，上限 3.96e+03%) 四档。
- **报告若干"反直觉"结构性现象**：缩放因子 Δ 的保护在某些注入点完全消失、slot gap 结构消失、以及第 N/2 个系数的抗错性丢失。
- **明确的方法论边界。** 只研究未优化（无 NTT、无 RNS）的 CKKS 乘法，作为后续研究"一阶理解"的基线。

## Key Contributions

- **A mechanistic explanation of CKKS multiplication fault propagation via algebraic symmetry.** Because `c0` feeds both `d0` and `d1`, and `c1` feeds both `d1` and `d2`, a single-bit flip that corrupts only one of the two uses breaks the cancellation structure that decryption relies on; corrupting both uses consistently preserves it.
- **A closed-form fault model.** The fault is a sparse error polynomial `e_{i,j} = 2^j X^i`. Expanding relinearized multiplication gives `c'_mult = (c0^m, c1^m) + e_{i,j} × (c0', c1')` for the symmetric case (a valid ciphertext ⇒ the sk-multiplication still cancels) versus `c'_mult = c_mult + e_{i,j} × (0, c1')` for the d1-only case (not a valid ciphertext ⇒ residual randomness survives decryption).
- **An empirical bit-level × coefficient-level resilience map** for 12 injection sites inside homomorphic multiplication, produced with a purpose-built C++ CKKS implementation (C-CKKS, modeled on OpenFHE/HEaaN/SEAL/PyFHE) plus the LLTFI LLVM-level fault injector.
- **Identification of structural anomalies** under fault: disappearance of the scaling factor Δ, loss of the slot gap structure, and loss of the N/2-th coefficient's usual resilience.
- **A deliberately scoped baseline**: unoptimized CKKS multiplication only (no NTT, no RNS), which the authors frame as a first-order study, with the optimized-kernel resilience profile deferred to companion work (refs [21], [22]).

## Methodology

The authors implemented their own CKKS scheme in C++ ("C-CKKS", built after OpenFHE, HEaaN, Microsoft SEAL, and PyFHE) specifically so they could observe intermediate polynomial state and reconstruct error-propagation paths — something a black-box library run would not expose. Faults are injected with **LLTFI** (Agarwal et al., ISSRE 2022), an LLVM-IR-level fault injector, as single-bit transient flips in the ciphertext polynomials involved in ciphertext–ciphertext multiplication (Eq. 2) and relinearization (Eq. 3).

Each injection is followed by a full end-to-end run of the pipeline: server-side homomorphic multiplication → client-side decryption → decoding. The decoded output is compared to the fault-free reference using **Maximum Relative Error Percentage (MREP)**. Outcomes are bucketed into four classes: Masked (MREP ≤ ε), Minor SDC (≤10%), Moderate SDC (10–100%), and Severe SDC (>100%, with the observed maximum at 3.96e+03%). This taxonomy follows the standard silent-data-corruption framing from Dixit et al.'s hyperscale SDC study [16].

The reported configuration is a single small parameter point: **N = 64, log Q = 60, log Δ = 25, gaps = 2, ε = 0.1**. The injection space per panel is 64 coefficients × the 64-bit register width, with the 12 panels of Fig. 2 covering the input ciphertext components `c0^(1)`, `c0^(2)`, `c1^(1)`, `c1^(2)`, the intermediates `d0` and `d2`, and the partial products `c0^(1)c1^(2)` and `c1^(1)c0^(2)`. Crucially, two panels are labeled with the *same* operand (`c0^(1)` appears twice in the top row, `c1^(1)` twice in the bottom row) but injected at different points in the dataflow — that pairing is the experimental instantiation of the "when matters" claim. Analysis proceeds by diffing polynomial execution traces between clean and faulty runs to build explicit error-propagation trees, then cross-checking those trees against the analytic expansion.

## Results & Highlights

All numbers below are read from Fig. 2 (the paper reports no results table; bit-plane boundaries are read off the heatmap and are therefore ±1 bit).

- **Timing dominates location — the same operand, two outcomes.** For `c0^(1)` injected symmetrically (both `d0` and `d1`), flips in bits 0–~18 are **fully masked (MREP ≤ ε = 0.1)**, i.e. ~19 of the 61 usable bit planes (~31%) are error-free, with additional masking on alternating coefficients (the gap=2 slot structure) and a fully green column at the N/2 = 32nd coefficient. For the *same* `c0^(1)` injected asymmetrically (into `d1` only), the panel is **Severe SDC (MREP > 100%) at essentially every bit position 0–60 and every coefficient** — the masked region collapses from ~19 bit planes to ~0. Delta: ~19 bit planes of protection lost, and the LSB (bit 0) alone goes from harmless to >100% output error.
- **Peak corruption magnitude: MREP = 3.96e+03% (≈3960%)**, versus the masking threshold of ε = 0.1 — a roughly 4×10⁴× ratio between the worst observed corruption and the "acceptable" boundary. This is the top of the Severe SDC color scale.
- **Symmetry-breaking is not operand-specific.** `c0^(2)` (bottom-left panel) and `c1^(1)`/`c1^(2)` in their asymmetric placements are likewise **Severe SDC across all bits 0–60**, whereas `c1^(1)` in its symmetric placement is masked up to bit ~18, Minor SDC (≤10%) in bits ~19–27, Moderate SDC (10–100%) at bits ~28–29, and Severe only above bit ~30. Delta between the two `c1^(1)` panels: ~30 bit planes that are ≤100% error in one case and >100% in the other.
- **Post-multiplication operands are ~26 bit planes more resilient, exactly as the scaling factor predicts.** `d0`, `d2`, and the partial products `c0^(1)c1^(2)` and `c1^(1)c0^(2)` are masked up to bit ~44–46, versus bit ~18–20 for pre-multiplication ciphertext components. That ~26-bit shift matches the transition from a log Δ = 25 scale to a log Δ² = 50 scale, i.e. the relative weight of a flip at bit j is 2^(j−50) rather than 2^(j−25).
- **Structurally dead bits.** In all 12 panels, register bits ~60–63 are green (masked), consistent with log Q = 60: those bit positions of the 64-bit register cannot carry a meaningful residue and a flip there is either impossible or removed by modular reduction.
- **d2 is the mildest intermediate**: masked up to bit ~44, Minor SDC through ~51, Moderate at ~52–53, Severe only above ~54 — narrower Severe band than `d0`'s.

What the paper does **not** report: any aggregate SDC rate (e.g. "X% of all injections caused SDC"), any confidence intervals or repetition counts, any comparison against an external protection scheme or a prior fault-tolerance baseline, and any result at a cryptographically realistic parameter size.

## Authors & Credibility

The author list spans three institutions with directly relevant track records. **Matías Mazzanti**, **Esteban Mocskos** (University of Buenos Aires), and **Augusto Vega** (IBM T. J. Watson) are co-authors of the two immediate predecessors cited here: "Characterizing the sensitivity to individual bit flips in client-side operations of the CKKS scheme" (arXiv:2507.20891, 2025) and "Quantifying the bit-error resilience of FHE compute" (HPCA 2025 workshop). **Karthik Swaminathan** (IBM Research) works on resilient and energy-efficient architecture. **Radha Venkatagiri** (Georgetown) has a substantial body of work on approximate computing and application-level error resilience, which is exactly the analytical lens used here. **Vattana Chan** (Georgetown) is presumably the student first author. The methodology choices — LLTFI, the SDC taxonomy from Dixit et al., MREP — are standard in the architecture-reliability community. Credibility of the mechanism claim is high; the evidence base backing it in *this* paper is thin by design (2 pages, workshop/poster format).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** The symmetry-breaking argument — that `c0` and `c1` are each consumed twice and that consistent versus inconsistent corruption of those two uses determines whether decryption's cancellation still works — is a clean, non-obvious insight, and the analytic derivation in Eqs. 5–6 is the paper's real contribution. However, the surrounding bit-level resilience characterization overlaps heavily with the authors' own prior work: footnote 3 explicitly defers the resilience of Δ, Q, and the gap/slot structure to refs [21] and [22]. This paper is best read as an incremental extension of that line from client-side operations to the server-side multiply.

**Rigor (weak, and self-acknowledged).** Section III is literally titled as presenting "preliminary results." The evaluation is a **single parameter point (N = 64, log Q = 60, log Δ = 25, gaps = 2)** — N = 64 is orders of magnitude below any deployable CKKS ring dimension (2^13–2^17), so it is genuinely unknown whether the masking boundaries (bit ~18 vs bit ~46) scale or whether the N/2-coefficient resilience artifact survives at realistic N. There is no baseline in the usual sense — no prior fault-tolerance technique, no ECC/ANT comparison, no alternate library — so all deltas are internal contrasts between the paper's own injection sites. There are **no aggregate statistics at all**: no SDC rate, no per-panel percentage breakdown, no variance across repeated runs, no statement of how many messages/inputs were tested (apparently one). The MREP metric itself is a *maximum* over slots, which is the most pessimistic possible summary and hides the distribution; a mean or percentile view is missing.

**Missing ablations (several, and they matter).** (a) No sweep over log Δ, even though the ~26-bit shift between pre- and post-multiplication panels is clearly a Δ² effect and a two-point Δ sweep would have confirmed the mechanism directly. (b) No sweep over gaps, despite "gaps = 2" being the cause of the striping in the symmetric panels. (c) No NTT/RNS variants — the authors state this up front, but since every practical CKKS implementation (including OpenFHE and SEAL, which C-CKKS is modeled on) uses RNS, and RNS decomposition changes which bits are architecturally live, the results may not transfer to any deployed system at all. (d) No test of whether a *double* fault or a fault in `evk` behaves differently.

**Reproducibility (poor as published).** "C-CKKS" is described only in a footnote as being based on OpenFHE/HEaaN/SEAL/PyFHE; **no repository link, no license, no version, no random seed, no injection count** is given. LLTFI is public, which helps, but the custom scheme implementation is the crux — a reader cannot re-derive the heatmaps. Given that a bespoke reimplementation could itself contain bugs that manifest as "unexpected behaviors such as the absence of the scaling factor Δ," releasing C-CKKS is essential.

**Significance (potentially high, currently unrealized).** If the symmetry argument holds at realistic parameters, it has a concrete architectural payoff: an FHE accelerator would not need to protect every register uniformly. It could protect only the operand *reuse* path (the register holding `c0` between its d0 and d1 consumption, and `c1` between d1 and d2), because a fault that hits both uses is self-masking. That is a much cheaper hardening target than full ECC on all datapaths. The paper does not take this step — there is no proposed protection scheme, no area/energy estimate, no coverage number.

**Red flags.** The abstract's framing ("we present an in-depth analysis") oversells a two-page study with one figure, one parameter point, and no aggregate statistics; "in-depth" is not warranted. The claim that homomorphic multiplication "is highly susceptible to faults" is supported for the asymmetric injection cases but is stated without the qualifier that the symmetric cases mask roughly a third of the bit space. Reading exact bit boundaries requires eyeballing a color heatmap, which is not a substitute for reported numbers.

## Strengths

- The theoretical explanation is genuinely illuminating and falsifiable: Eqs. 5 and 6 make a crisp structural prediction (valid ciphertext ⇒ cancellation preserved; `(0, c1')` term ⇒ residual randomness), and the heatmaps are consistent with it.
- Building a transparent CKKS implementation rather than instrumenting a production library was the right call for tracing propagation paths, and the trace-diff-to-propagation-tree methodology is sound.
- The ~26-bit-plane offset between pre- and post-multiplication injection sites is a strong internal consistency check: it lines up with the log Δ = 25 → log Δ² = 50 scale change without being fit to it.
- Intellectually honest scoping — the authors state the no-NTT/no-RNS restriction and the "preliminary" status explicitly rather than burying them.
- The finding is architecturally actionable in principle (selective hardening of the operand-reuse path).

## Limitations

- Toy parameter regime: N = 64 and a single (log Q, log Δ, gaps, ε) setting; no scaling study.
- Unoptimized CKKS only — no NTT, no RNS — which is not how CKKS is actually deployed or accelerated.
- No aggregate quantitative results: no SDC rates, no distributions, no error bars, no injection counts.
- No external baseline and no proposed mitigation; the work characterizes but does not defend.
- C-CKKS is not released (no link in the paper), so the central artifact is unreproducible.
- Only ciphertext–ciphertext multiplication and relinearization are studied; addition, key switching, rotation, and bootstrapping are left to future work, and bootstrapping in particular is where most real FHE runtime and most fault exposure lives.
- MREP (a max-over-slots metric) is the sole quality measure; there is no downstream task metric (e.g. accuracy of a privacy-preserving inference), so the practical severity of "Moderate SDC" is unquantified.
- Single-bit, single-fault model only; multi-bit and multi-site faults, and faults in `evk`, are untested.

## Relevance

如果你关注隐私保护机器学习的系统栈（FHE 加速器、CKKS 推理、可信执行），这篇文章给出的"操作数复用路径才是关键保护对象"的结论，可能直接影响 FHE ASIC 的容错预算分配——不必对所有寄存器做全量 ECC，只需保护 `c0`/`c1` 在两次消费之间的存活区间。但目前证据只到 N=64 的未优化实现，距离能指导真实加速器设计还有相当距离，建议与作者的 HPCA'25 workshop 论文和 arXiv:2507.20891 一起读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the ~19-bit masked floor for symmetric injection scale with N, or is it purely a function of log Δ and the message magnitude? A two-point sweep (N = 64 vs N = 4096) would settle whether any of this transfers to deployable parameters.
2. Under RNS decomposition, `c0` is represented across multiple small moduli — does the symmetry argument survive, or does RNS *increase* the chance that a fault hits only one use because the two consumptions are separated in time and residue channel?
3. Can the symmetry insight be turned into a cheap hardening scheme — e.g. computing `d0` and `d1` from the same physically-protected copy of `c0`, or deliberately *duplicating* the fault into both uses (a "fault mirroring" trick) to convert severe SDC into a masked outcome?
4. Why is the N/2-th coefficient normally resilient, and what exactly destroys that resilience under asymmetric injection? This is asserted from the heatmap but never explained analytically.
5. What is the actual SDC rate over the full injection space, and how does it break down per operand? A single table of (operand, % masked / % minor / % moderate / % severe) would substantially raise the paper's evidentiary value at negligible cost.
6. How do these corruption magnitudes translate into end-task damage — e.g. top-1 accuracy drop for an encrypted CNN inference — where a 10% MREP might be harmless and a 3960% MREP obviously catastrophic, but the boundary is unknown?
7. Does bootstrapping amplify or attenuate these faults? Since bootstrapping re-encrypts and refreshes noise, it might either wash out low-bit corruption or propagate a broken ciphertext structure indefinitely.
