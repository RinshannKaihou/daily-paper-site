> **一句话总结：** ReRound 用一个在 LLM 自身权重上训练的条件扩散模型重建连续权重，只对落在量化区间中点附近（模糊区）的权重改写 RTN 的向上/向下取整决策，再用「去量化矩阵的前 k 个奇异值与 FP16 最接近」这一纯权重谱准则挑选容差参数；在 8 个小模型上 3/4-bit 权重量化平均精度稳定超过同参数 RTN 0.1–1.6 分，且在 Gemma 2 2B 上以 67.4 的四任务均值反超需要校准数据的 SignRound（67.0）。

> **TL;DR:** ReRound is a calibration-free weight-only PTQ post-processing step that flips at most 1% of round-to-nearest (RTN) assignments — only those near quantization-interval midpoints — using a per-model conditional diffusion prior trained on the LLM's own 64×64 weight patches, then picks the tolerance parameter whose de-quantized matrix best matches the full-precision leading singular values. On Gemma 2 2B W4A16 it lifts group-wise RTN from 66.4 to 67.4 average over WinoGrande/PIQA/BoolQ/SIQA, beating the strongest calibration-based baseline SignRound (67.0) and GPTQ (66.7) without any C4 calibration data; on OLMo 2 1B W4 it gives the largest RTN gain (+1.3, 58.1→59.4) yet still trails calibration-free BNB FP4 (61.1).

## 核心贡献

- **提出「中点歧义」这一被忽略的最后一步问题**：在 scale、zero-point、group size、量化层集合都固定之后，每个权重仍要在下界 $\ell$ 和上界 $u$ 之间二选一。论文推导出翻转代价 $c_{\text{flip}}(r)=\Delta^2|1-2r|$，在中点 $r=0.5$ 处最小——这正是可以低代价改写、但 RTN 完全靠标量距离盲选的区域。
- **用模型自身权重训练扩散先验**：对每个目标 LLM 训练一个条件扩散模型（DeepFloyd IF Stage-II + ControlNet），输入是 2-bit 随机舍入（SR）条件、目标是 FP32 的 64×64 权重 patch；推理时以确定性 2-bit RTN patch 为条件反向扩散，得到连续重建矩阵 $W_{\text{rec}}$。$W_{\text{rec}}$ 从不作为部署权重，只作为「该往上还是往下取整」的证据。
- **位置相关容差度量**：$\tau_{\text{pos}}(r;\tau)=\tau\exp[-\beta((0.5-\delta-d(r))_+/(0.5-\delta))^2]$，在中点区 $|r-0.5|\le\delta$（$\delta=0.1$，即 $r\in[0.4,0.6]$）保持满容差，向两侧衰减，从而「越靠近中点越信扩散重建，越靠近整数越信 RTN」。只有当 $q_{\text{rec}}=q_{\text{alt}}$ 且重建偏移 $\rho=|\tilde w_{\text{rec}}-\tilde w|\le\tau_{\text{pos}}$ 时才接受翻转，并给出了每次翻转标量误差上界 $c_{\text{flip}}\le 2\Delta^2\tau$。
- **纯权重空间的谱选择准则**：扫描 $\tau\in\mathcal{T}$ 得到多个候选整数矩阵，逐矩阵选取去量化后前 $k=\min\{m,128,\max(32,\lfloor m/16\rfloor)\}$ 个奇异值与原 FP 矩阵相对 $\ell_2$ 距离最小的候选，全程不需要激活样本、校准文本或下游标签。
- **完全离线、零推理开销、可叠加**：最终模型与 RTN 共享同一套量化参数与低比特表示，只有部分整数赋值不同；也可直接作用于别的 PTQ 方法（论文用 SINQ 的 scale 做了验证）产出的量化参数之上。

## Key Contributions

- Isolates the *final rounding assignment* as an independent, under-exploited degree of freedom in weight-only PTQ, and characterizes where it is cheap to change (near-midpoint entries, where the flip cost $\Delta^2|1-2r|$ vanishes).
- Trains a conditional diffusion model (DDPM, DeepFloyd IF-II-M-v1.0 U-Net frozen + trainable ControlNet, empty-prompt T5 embedding) on the target LLM's own weight patches to invert the many-to-one low-bit map, producing continuous reconstructions used purely as guidance.
- Introduces a position-dependent tolerance metric that smoothly interpolates between "trust the diffusion prior" at the midpoint and "keep RTN" near quantization levels, with a proof that any accepted revision satisfies $|r-1/2|\le\rho\le\tau_{\text{pos}}(r;\tau)\le\tau$.
- Introduces a weight-only spectral selection criterion (leading-singular-value matching) to pick one candidate matrix per layer, avoiding any activation- or text-based calibration.
- Demonstrates consistent RTN improvement across 9 small LLMs (Gemma 2 2B, Gemma 3 1B, Qwen3 1.7B, OLMo 2 1B, SmolLM2 1.7B, Llama 3.2 1B, Pythia 1.4B, Phi-2 2.7B, Falcon 3-1B) at both W3A16 and W4A16, with released code.

## Methodology

The pipeline has three offline stages, all executed after the quantization parameters are frozen.

**Stage 1 — conditional weight reconstruction.** All 2-D weight matrices of the target LLM (including `lm_head`, excluding token embeddings and norms) are converted to FP32 and cut into 64×64 patches; each quantization group is min–max normalized to $[0,1]$. Training pairs a normalized FP patch $P$ with a 2-bit *stochastic-rounding* condition $P_{\text{low}}$ (SR picks $\ell$ with prob. $1-r$, $\ell+1$ with prob. $r$), and optimizes the standard $\epsilon$-prediction loss $\mathbb{E}\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(P_t,t,P_{\text{low}})\|_2^2$. Only the ControlNet is updated; the IF Stage-II U-Net and its text-encoder projections stay frozen. Training runs 5 epochs × 1M sampled patches, batch 32/GPU on 2× RTX 4090, AdamW at constant LR 2e-5. At inference the condition switches to *deterministic* 2-bit RTN, and DDPM sampling with the `super27` respacing schedule (27 reverse steps, batch 128, 1 GPU) reconstructs every patch in raster order; patches are un-normalized per group and reassembled into $W_{\text{rec}}$.

**Stage 2 — reconstruction-guided rounding.** Both $W$ and $W_{\text{rec}}$ are mapped into the *same* quantized coordinate space using the fixed $\Delta,z$. A revision from $q_{\text{rtn}}$ to the opposite neighbor $q_{\text{alt}}$ is proposed only when $\mathrm{round}(\tilde w_{\text{rec}})=q_{\text{alt}}$, and accepted only when the deviation $\rho=|\tilde w_{\text{rec}}-\tilde w|$ falls inside $\tau_{\text{pos}}(r;\tau)$. Sweeping $\tau$ over a small per-model candidate set (Table 8: typically $\{0,0.15,0.25\}$ for linear layers, often $\{0\}$ for `lm_head`; $\beta\in\{8,32,64,128\}$) yields several candidate integer matrices. Notably $\tau=0$ is always in the set, so ReRound can always fall back to exact RTN.

**Stage 3 — spectral candidate selection.** Each candidate $Q_\tau$ is de-quantized to $W_\tau$ and scored by relative leading-singular-value discrepancy $D_{\text{spec}}(\tau)=\|\sigma_{1:k}(W_\tau)-\sigma_{1:k}(W)\|_2/(\|\sigma_{1:k}(W)\|_2+\epsilon)$; the argmin is kept per matrix. Because selection runs per matrix and per bit width, W3 and W4 end up with different revision patterns even though they reuse the *same* $W_{\text{rec}}$ (Figures 7–8). Overall at most 1% of RTN assignments are changed in any matrix.

Evaluation uses LM Evaluation Harness v0.4.11, zero-shot, acc for WinoGrande/BoolQ/SIQA and acc_norm for PIQA, seeds fixed at 0/1234. Baselines: channel-wise and group-wise RTN, HQQ 0.2.8, BitsAndBytes FP4 0.49.2, Hadamard-transformed RTN, CafeQ (calibration-free); GPTQ, AdaRound, SignRound (AutoRound 0.13.1) with 128×2048-token C4 calibration sequences.

## Results & Highlights

- **Gemma 2 2B, W4A16, 4-task average:** ReRound **67.4** vs. matched group-wise RTN **66.4** (+1.0) and the strongest calibration-*based* baseline SignRound **67.0** (+0.4); GPTQ 66.7, AdaRound 66.9, CafeQ 65.9, FP16 ceiling 68.1. Best calibration-free rival HQQ reaches 67.3.
- **Gemma 3 1B, W4A16:** ReRound **59.3** vs. group-wise RTN **58.8** (+0.5) and SignRound **59.1** (+0.2); GPTQ collapses to 57.8 (BoolQ 56.8 vs. ReRound's 61.9). FP16 is 60.8.
- **OLMo 2 1B, W4A16 — the honest failure case:** ReRound posts the paper's largest RTN gain, **58.1 → 59.4 (+1.3)**, yet is still beaten by calibration-free BNB FP4 (**61.1**) and HQQ (**60.4**), i.e. a different quantization parameterization dominates better rounding here.
- **W3A16 across the five main models:** ReRound has the top average on every one — Gemma 2 2B 62.0 vs. RTN 61.9 (+0.1), Gemma 3 1B 55.5 vs. 55.3 (+0.2), Qwen3 1.7B 54.2 vs. 54.1 (+0.1), OLMo 2 1B 55.1 vs. 54.3 (+0.8), SmolLM2 1.7B 56.9 vs. 56.0 (+0.9). Hadamard+RTN degrades badly at 3 bits (Qwen3 46.7, SmolLM2 48.1).
- **Phi-2 2.7B, W3A16:** the largest single gain in the paper, **66.3 → 67.9 (+1.6)**, driven by BoolQ 64.7 → 67.1 and WinoGrande 71.7 → 73.6.
- **Head-to-head vs. AdaRound at W4A16 (Table 11):** ReRound wins the average on all six tested models, by up to **+1.9** on OLMo 2 1B (59.4 vs. 57.5) and +1.0 on Llama 3.2 1B (56.5 vs. 55.5) — while AdaRound consumes 128 C4 sequences and ReRound consumes none.
- **Stacking on SINQ scales, Qwen3 1.7B, G=64:** W4 accuracy 62.1 → **62.6**, average perplexity (WikiText-2 + C4) 18.53 → **18.46**; W3 accuracy 57.1 → **57.3**, perplexity 24.26 → **24.06** (WikiText-2 23.27 → 23.14, C4 25.25 → 24.97).
- **Ablation on OLMo 2 1B (Table 4), W4:** full ReRound 59.4 vs. matched *random* flips 58.5 (same number of changes, 10 seeds), midpoint-window tolerance 58.4, weight-MSE selection instead of spectral 58.7 — every component removal costs ≥0.7 points, and random flips alone recover only 0.4 of the 1.3-point gain. At W3, random flips actually *hurt* (54.1 vs. RTN 54.3) while full ReRound reaches 55.1.
- **Offline cost (Table 6):** 1.76–3.43 GPU-hours diffusion training on 2× RTX 4090 plus 3.65–9.55 hours single-GPU diffusion inference per model; the ReRound PTQ step itself is only 42–124 s, and inference-time cost is exactly zero since the low-bit format is unchanged.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-high).** The specific combination is new: prior rounding-optimization work (AdaRound, FlexRound, SignRound, CafeQ) learns rounding via output or weight-space reconstruction objectives, whereas ReRound treats de-quantization as an *inverse problem* and imports a diffusion prior from the image-dequantization literature (Vavilala et al. 2025) into weight space. However, the diffusion machinery is almost entirely borrowed — the ControlNet-on-frozen-IF-Stage-II recipe is Vavilala et al.'s, so the genuinely new pieces are the tolerance metric and the spectral selection rule.

**Rigor (mixed).** The ablation design is better than average: "matched random flips" is exactly the right control for a method that changes ≤1% of assignments, and it shows the gains are not a generic perturbation effect. But several things undercut the headline claims. (1) **The main tables report only four zero-shot commonsense tasks and no perplexity.** Perplexity is the far more sensitive quantization metric, and it appears only in the SINQ table — where the improvements are 0.07–0.20 PPL, i.e. tiny. (2) **Several "wins" are inside the noise band.** Gemma 2 2B / Qwen3 1.7B / Gemma 3 1B at W3 improve by 0.1–0.2 points on a 4-task average; and Qwen3 1.7B W4 BoolQ shows *both* RTN (77.7) and ReRound (78.3) exceeding the FP16 model (77.5), a clear sign that BoolQ is dominated by variance rather than fidelity. Since BoolQ is by far the highest-variance of the four tasks and carries much of ReRound's margin (Gemma 2 2B W4: +2.4 on BoolQ alone drives the +1.0 average), the averages should be read with caution. (3) **Per-model hyperparameters without a stated selection protocol.** Table 8 shows $\beta$ varying over $\{8, 32, 64, 128\}$ and tolerance sets varying per model and even per layer type (`lm_head` often restricted to $\{0\}$), yet the paper never says how $\beta$ or the candidate set was chosen, and provides no $\beta$ ablation. If these were tuned on the same four evaluation tasks, the method is not as "calibration-free" as advertised — it would be free of *data* calibration but not of *benchmark* tuning. (4) **The 2-bit conditioning choice is unmotivated and unablated** for the main experiments: the diffusion model is trained with 2-bit SR conditions and its output is reused unchanged for both W3 and W4 targets, but no experiment shows 2-bit is better than 3-bit or 4-bit conditions (the SINQ experiments switch to bit-matched conditions, suggesting the authors themselves are unsure). (5) **The component ablation is run on exactly one model (OLMo 2 1B)** — which happens to be the model where ReRound loses to two calibration-free baselines.

**Reproducibility (good).** Code is released; checkpoints, package versions, seeds, patch construction, DDPM schedule, learning rate, and per-model hyperparameters are all specified. The main obstacle is compute: ~5–13 GPU-hours per LLM, and a fresh diffusion model per LLM.

**Significance (narrow but real).** The result that ~1% of rounding bits, chosen well, can be worth more than 128 C4 calibration sequences (the AdaRound comparison) is a genuinely interesting empirical claim, and orthogonality to SINQ/other PTQ parameterizations makes it composable. But the payoff ceiling is small (≤1.6 points) while the offline cost is large, and the OLMo 2 1B result shows that choosing a better quantization *parameterization* can outweigh better *rounding* entirely.

**Red flags.** Evaluation is capped at 2.7B parameters; the abstract's "particularly effective for smaller LLMs" is doing real work, since nothing here demonstrates the prior scales to 7B+ where patch-based 64×64 reconstruction of far larger matrices becomes much more expensive. SignRound is compared only at W4 and only on two models, and SignRoundV2 / CafeQ appear in Related Work but CafeQ is evaluated on just one model (Gemma 2 2B, where it is the *weakest* method at 65.9 — a suspiciously convenient single-model appearance). No W3 calibration-based comparison exists at all, even though W3 is where ReRound claims to win most consistently.

## Strengths

- Deployment-neutral by construction: identical scales, zero-points, group size, layer coverage, low-bit format, and inference kernel as RTN. Nothing changes at serving time, which makes adoption essentially risk-free if the offline cost is acceptable.
- Composable with other PTQ methods — demonstrated on SINQ scales at G=64 with both accuracy and perplexity improving.
- The "matched random flips" control is the correct skeptic's baseline and the authors ran it, including a case (OLMo 2 1B W3) where random flips make things *worse*, which strengthens the claim that the guidance signal is real.
- Clean theoretical hygiene: the flip-cost derivation, the $|r-1/2|\le\rho\le\tau$ sandwich, and the explicit caveat that the scalar error bound "does not characterize changes in model outputs or downstream task accuracy."
- The limitations section is unusually candid (per-model diffusion cost, weight-space-only selection criterion, patch-local reconstruction missing long-range/cross-layer structure, small-LLM-only evaluation).

## Limitations

- **Cost/benefit is unfavorable at scale.** 5–13 GPU-hours of diffusion work per model for ≤1.6 accuracy points, with no prior transfer across models (the authors flag transferring weight priors as future work).
- **Candidate space is capped by the frozen quantization parameters.** ReRound can only reach assignments reachable by single-neighbor flips within the given grid; joint optimization of scales and rounding is left open.
- **Spectral selection is a proxy.** Leading singular values of a de-quantized matrix say nothing about layer sensitivity or downstream task behavior; the weight-MSE ablation shows the criterion matters, but nothing shows spectral is *optimal*.
- **Patch-local reconstruction (64×64) cannot capture long-range or cross-layer weight dependencies**, and matrices smaller than the patch must be padded with validity masks.
- **Scope:** eight-to-nine models, all ≤2.7B, four commonsense tasks, no generative/instruction-following/long-context evaluation, no W2 results despite the diffusion model being conditioned on 2-bit inputs.

## Relevance

如果你关注低比特 LLM 推理与端侧部署，这篇给出了一个「零推理开销、可叠加在任何 PTQ 之上」的后处理层，尤其适合手上跑 1B–3B 小模型且不愿引入校准数据的场景。另一个值得记的点是把扩散模型当作权重空间的逆问题先验来用——这条思路（weights2weights、Neural Network Diffusion 一脉）可能比本文的量化增益本身更有延展性。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the 1-point W4 gain survive perplexity evaluation on WikiText-2/C4 for the *main* (non-SINQ) configurations, and does it survive a multi-seed / larger-benchmark protocol given how much of it rides on BoolQ?
2. How were $\beta$ and the tolerance candidate sets selected per model? If on the evaluation tasks, what does a fixed-$\beta$ (say $\beta=32$ everywhere) ablation cost?
3. Can one diffusion prior be trained once on a *family* of LLMs (or on a large mixed corpus of pretrained weight matrices) and transferred zero-shot, removing the per-model 5–13 GPU-hour tax?
4. Why 2-bit conditions? An ablation over condition bit-width (2/3/4-bit, SR vs. RTN) would clarify whether the prior is learning weight structure or merely a denoising map at a specific SNR.
5. Does the approach extend to W2A16, where midpoint ambiguity should matter far more, and to 7B+ models where per-matrix spectral selection and patch reconstruction costs grow substantially?
6. Would a sensitivity-weighted or activation-free Hessian-diagonal proxy beat leading-singular-value matching as the candidate selection criterion, without reintroducing calibration data?
7. Since ReRound composes with SINQ, does it also compose with GPTQ/AWQ/QuaRot parameterizations — and do the gains add or saturate once the parameterization is already strong (as the OLMo 2 1B / BNB FP4 result hints)?
