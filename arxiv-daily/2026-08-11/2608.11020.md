> **一句话总结：** 作者系统地把 PINN 中的导数计算从自动微分（AD）换成有限差分（FD），提出一套在训练中周期性重标定步长 ε 的经验方法（按 ε_opt ∝ ε_m^{1/(k+2)} 的理论标度），在 PINNacle 的 Poisson 2D、Burgers 1D、Heat2D-CG 三个基准上把 L2 相对误差做到与 AD 统计不可区分（如 Heat2D-CG：AD 2.56% vs eFD 2.60% vs FD 2.66%），同时全 batch 区间都更快、显存约省 2×；并指出对 BatchNorm/Attention 这类含样本间依赖的网络，PyTorch 常用的 `grad_outputs=ones` 写法是**静默错误**的。

> **TL;DR:** On three PINNacle-derived PDE benchmarks (Poisson 2D with holes, viscous Burgers 1D, Heat2D-CG), properly calibrated central finite differences match the automatic-differentiation baseline in accuracy (Heat2D-CG L2: AD 2.56% vs. eFD 2.60%; Burgers L2: AD 1.36% vs. FD 1.38%) while being ~2–3× faster and ~2× more memory-efficient for plain MLPs; a proposed Stochastic FD variant beats AD on the stationary Poisson problem (1.11% vs. 1.41% L2, a 21% relative reduction), and the paper further shows the ubiquitous `grad_outputs=ones_like(outputs)` autograd idiom is silently wrong for BatchNorm/attention networks (Laplacian error of 8.5 and 7.4 absolute units vs. the exact per-sample reference, where FD errs by only 0.79 and 2.1).

## 核心贡献

- **步长 ε 的原理化选择。** 给出一阶/二阶中心差分的截断误差与舍入误差平衡分析，导出 ε_opt^{(1)} = C₁·ε_m^{1/3}、ε_opt^{(2)} = C₂·ε_m^{1/4}，并用实验测得常数 C₁ ≈ 2–3；给出 `find_eps` 经验标定算法（在 logspace(-6,-1) 上扫 50 个候选，以与 AD 参考的 RMSE 最小为准则）。
- **训练中周期性重标定。** 指出 ε_opt 依赖 |f|/|f^{(k+2)}|，会随网络平滑度在训练中漂移，因此每隔固定 epoch 重新标定；并做了重标定间隔从 1 到 10000 epoch 的消融。
- **混合阶 PDE 的三种策略。** FD（两阶最优 ε 的几何平均）、eFD（每阶用各自最优 ε，代价是 batch 增大约 40%）、sFD（每步每点从 [ε_opt^{(1)}, ε_opt^{(2)}] 对数均匀采样 ε）。
- **识别一个静默 bug。** 对含样本间依赖的架构（BatchNorm、self-attention），PyTorch 中 `grad_outputs=ones` 得到的是行和而非逐样本导数；唯一正确的 O(B) 逐样本 autograd 循环在 PINN 所需 batch 下不可行（BN 封顶 B=2⁸，attention 封顶 B=2⁷）。
- **计算成本量化。** 在 RTX 4070（12GB）上给出时间/显存随 batch 的曲线，覆盖 plain MLP、MLP+BN、MLP+attention 三种架构。

## Key Contributions

- A truncation-vs-roundoff error analysis yielding the derivative-order-dependent optimal step size ε_opt^{(k)} ∝ ε_m^{1/(k+2)}, with a table of predicted optima across FP64/FP32/FP16/BF16, plus an empirical runtime calibration routine (`find_eps`) that closes the undetermined-prefactor gap the theory leaves open.
- Periodic in-training recalibration of ε, motivated by the fact that the optimum depends on the evolving ratio |f(x)|/|f^{(k+2)}(x)| — claimed to be the first such proposal in the FD-PINN literature.
- Three strategies for mixed-order PDEs: geometric-mean FD, order-specific eFD, and Stochastic FD (sFD), the last framed as noise-induced regularization over a family of nearby FD operators.
- Identification and quantification of the `grad_outputs=ones` failure mode for inter-sample-dependent architectures, with FD offered as a *partial* mitigation rather than a fix.
- A speed/memory microbenchmark showing FD dominates AD across the full tested batch-size range, and dramatically so when the only *correct* AD baseline (per-sample loop) is used.

## Methodology

The core substitution is simple: instead of building an autograd graph for ∂u/∂x and ∂²u/∂x², evaluate the network on a concatenated enlarged batch [X−ε, X, X+ε] in a single forward pass and apply 3-point central-difference stencils. For d-dimensional inputs the Laplacian needs 2d+1 evaluations per collocation point, all packed into one forward pass (Appendix A gives the PyTorch code). Crucially, unlike Lim et al. (2022) where ε is dictated by a fixed grid spacing, here ε is a free, calibrated parameter and collocation points are resampled uniformly each epoch.

The theory section performs the standard Taylor/roundoff balance: truncation error scales as ε²|f'''|/6 for the first derivative and ε²|f''''|/12 for the second, while roundoff scales as κ₁ε_m|f|/ε and κ₂ε_m|f|/ε², giving optima at ε_m^{1/3} and ε_m^{1/4} respectively. Since κ and the |f|/|f^{(k+2)}| ratio are unknown for a neural network, Algorithm 2 sweeps 50 log-spaced candidates and picks the one minimizing RMSE against the AD reference — note that this makes AD a *required component* of the calibration loop, not something FD fully eliminates (though only intermittently, every 4000 epochs by default).

Experiments use a 4-hidden-layer tanh MLP (width 128 for Burgers/Poisson, 256 for Heat2D-CG), Adam at lr 1e-3 with cosine annealing, loss L = L_PDE + λ_IC L_IC + λ_BC L_BC (λ = 10 for Burgers/Heat, λ_BC = 1000 for Poisson), batch size 8192 with rejection sampling on multiply-connected domains, 20k epochs, and 25–26 random seeds per configuration. Everything runs in FP32 on a single RTX 4070. Benchmarks are Poisson 2D on a square with four circular holes (second-order only, stationary), viscous Burgers 1D with ν = 0.01/π (mixed first/second order, sharp shock at t ≈ 0.3, Cole–Hopf analytical reference), and Heat2D-CG (a 17-hole heat-exchanger geometry in (x,y,t) with segment-varying Robin boundary conditions, COMSOL reference on 13,312 nodes), all from the PINNacle suite.

The inter-sample-dependency study (Appendix B) compares three Laplacian estimators — batched AD with `grad_outputs=ones`, the O(B) per-sample autograd loop (treated as ground truth), and calibrated FD — on an MLP+BatchNorm1d and an MLP+single-head self-attention, both pretrained 2000 epochs on u = sin x · cos y to stabilize BN running statistics and put output magnitudes on a comparable scale, at B = 64 over 5 random initializations.

## Results & Highlights

- **Poisson 2D (PINNacle, square with 4 holes), L2 relative error:** sFD **1.11 ± 0.39%** vs. the AD baseline **1.41 ± 0.31%** — a 21% relative reduction; max error 0.026 ± 0.010 (sFD) vs. 0.032 ± 0.008 (AD), a 20% reduction; SMAPE 13.10% vs. 13.67%. Deterministic FD is statistically tied with AD (1.40% vs. 1.41%).
- **Burgers 1D (Cole–Hopf reference), L2 relative error:** AD **1.36 ± 0.11%**, eFD 1.38 ± 0.17%, FD 1.38 ± 0.14% — differences an order of magnitude below the seed variance. sFD *fails* here: **4.23 ± 1.21%**, roughly 3× worse, with max-error std ±0.120 vs. ±0.012 for AD.
- **Heat2D-CG (PINNacle, 17-hole geometry, Robin BCs), L2 relative error:** AD **2.56 ± 0.32%**, eFD 2.60 ± 0.31%, FD 2.66 ± 0.38%, sFD 4.65 ± 2.24%. The authors explicitly benchmark their own AD baseline against PINNacle's published numbers: vanilla PINN 3.64% and best-reported LAAF 2.39%, so their AD implementation at 2.56% is a genuinely strong, not strawman, baseline — the single most credibility-enhancing number in the paper.
- **Speed/memory (RTX 4070, plain MLP):** FD beats the memory-efficient batched AD idiom by roughly **2–3× in wall-clock time** and ~**2.0× in peak memory** at the largest tractable AD batch (B = 2¹⁸, itself only half of FD's feasible B = 2¹⁹).
- **Speed/memory vs. the only correct AD baseline (per-sample loop):** ~**360×** faster for the plain MLP, ~**1600×** for MLP+BN, ~**1400×** for MLP+attention at B = 2⁷. Feasible batch size caps at B = 2⁸ (BN) and 2⁷ (attention) for per-sample AD versus 2¹⁸ and 2¹² for FD — a 1024× and 32× advantage; at a common B = 2⁷, per-sample AD costs 54× and 107× more memory.
- **The silent-bug quantification (Table 6, median max-abs Laplacian discrepancy vs. per-sample AD over 5 inits):** plain MLP — batched AD 0 (exact), FD 8.2 × 10⁻⁴; MLP+BN — batched AD **8.5**, FD **0.79** (≈10× closer); MLP+attention — batched AD **7.4**, FD **2.1** (≈3.5× closer).
- **Recalibration ablation:** L2 error is essentially flat for FD/eFD across refresh intervals from 1 to 4000 epochs (5 seeds, 20k epochs, all three problems); only at interval 10000 (i.e. ε calibrated twice in the whole run) does error degrade, catastrophically for sFD on Burgers and Heat2D-CG.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** FD-in-PINNs is well-trodden (Lim et al. 2022, Jiang et al. 2023, Xiang et al. 2022, Roy et al. 2025, Zhang et al. 2024), and the paper is honest that its differentiator is narrow: prior work treated ε as a fixed hyperparameter, while this paper contributes (i) runtime empirical calibration, (ii) periodic recalibration, (iii) stochastic ε sampling. The error-balance analysis in §3.2 is textbook numerical analysis reproduced for the NN setting; its value is contextual, not new mathematics. The genuinely fresh contribution is the `grad_outputs=ones` failure mode, and the authors correctly frame FD there as a *partial mitigation, not a solution*.

**Rigor (good, with specific blemishes).** 25–26 seeds per configuration and reported standard deviations put this above the typical PINN paper, and the explicit comparison of their AD baseline against PINNacle's published vanilla-PINN (3.64%) and LAAF (2.39%) numbers preempts the "weak baseline" objection that would otherwise dominate. Three concrete blemishes: (1) **a numerical inconsistency between the abstract/Figure 1 and Table 2** — Figure 1 reports sFD at 1.15% and AD at 1.66% relative L2 on Poisson, while Table 2 reports 1.11 ± 0.39% and 1.41 ± 0.31%. These are different numbers for the same experiment and are never reconciled; a reader cannot tell which is the reported result. (2) **Seed-count inconsistency:** §4.1 says "25 independent random seeds," while Tables 2–4 all say "26 seeds." Minor, but it is the kind of slip that suggests the tables and text were assembled at different times. (3) **The sFD Poisson win is within noise by the paper's own standard.** sFD 1.11 ± 0.39% vs. AD 1.41 ± 0.31% overlaps at one standard deviation, yet the abstract claims sFD "outperforms AD" without a significance test, and elsewhere the paper uses seed-variance overlap as its criterion for calling methods "statistically indistinguishable." Applying that criterion consistently would downgrade the headline sFD claim.

**Missing ablations.** The `find_eps` calibration itself requires an AD reference, so the "AD-free" framing is only true between recalibrations — there is no ablation of a fully AD-free calibration proxy (e.g. Richardson-style self-consistency across two ε values), which the discussion of Richardson extrapolation practically invites. There is also no ablation isolating whether sFD's Poisson gain comes from the collapse-suppression mechanism the authors hypothesize versus generic gradient noise: the obvious control — deterministic FD plus matched-variance input jitter — is not run, so the mechanistic story in §5.2 is interpretation, not evidence. No stencil-order ablation either (only 3-point central differences; a 5-point stencil would change the truncation-error exponent and hence the whole ε-scaling story).

**Reproducibility (deferred).** Code and CSVs are promised "upon publication," which is a real limitation today. On the positive side, all architectural details, hyperparameters, PyTorch/Python versions, and hardware are stated precisely enough to reimplement.

**Significance (practical, not conceptual).** The paper's real value is a defensible engineering recommendation for PINN practitioners plus one genuinely alarming correctness finding. The direct rebuttal to Chen et al. (2025) — "AD is essential" — is empirically supported here for MLP-based PINNs, though the authors' own §2.4 fairly notes that Chen et al.'s spectral argument concerns residual-minimization dynamics rather than solution accuracy, so the two results are less contradictory than the framing suggests.

**Red flags.** The abstract-vs-table discrepancy is the most serious. Secondarily, the speedup headlines (~360×, ~1600×) are computed against per-sample AD — a baseline nobody actually uses in practice precisely because it is infeasible — so quoting them without the "only correct baseline" qualifier would overclaim; the paper does mostly attach the qualifier, but the abstract's "an order of magnitude closer" phrasing compresses a 10× (BN) and 3.5× (attention) result into a single number that flatters the attention case.

## Strengths

- Explicitly benchmarks its own AD baseline against externally published PINNacle numbers, which is rare and directly forecloses the most common criticism of "our method beats AD" papers.
- 25–26 seeds with reported standard deviations, and the authors repeatedly use inter-seed variance as the yardstick for whether differences are meaningful — including when it argues *against* their own method (sFD on Burgers/Heat is reported as a clear failure).
- The negative result on sFD is not buried: it appears in the summary table, the discussion, and the recalibration ablation, with a plausible mechanistic account of why stationary elliptic problems differ from shock-forming and time-dependent ones.
- The `grad_outputs=ones` finding is actionable and affects a lot of existing code — anyone combining PINNs with transformers or BatchNorm is likely computing wrong derivatives right now.
- Practical recommendations section is concrete and appropriately hedged (use geometric-mean FD by default; recalibrate a few times; for BN either use FD or reimplement BN with running statistics in training mode).
- Honest scoping of limitations: restricted to first/second derivatives and FP32, with the fourth-order (ε⁻⁴ roundoff amplification) and FP64 cases explicitly flagged as open.

## Limitations

- Only first- and second-order derivatives are tested; for biharmonic/Kirchhoff-plate operators the roundoff amplification scales as ε⁻⁴ and whether ε_m^{1/6} calibration survives FP32 is untested and, by the authors' own analysis, doubtful.
- FP32 only. The scaling table predicts markedly better FD accuracy in FP64, but the memory/arithmetic trade-off is unexplored.
- All PDE experiments use a single fixed architecture family (4-layer tanh MLP); the inter-sample architectures (BN, attention) are studied only as *derivative-estimation* microbenchmarks, never actually trained as PINNs — so the practical claim "use FD for BatchNorm PINNs" is supported by derivative fidelity, not by any end-to-end solution-accuracy result.
- The FD "batch" contains dependent shifted copies, so the effective number of independent collocation points is smaller than the raw batch size; the authors acknowledge this partially offsets the memory advantage but do not quantify how much of the ~2× advantage survives an equal-independent-points comparison.
- Calibration still needs AD, so FD is not a fully AD-free pipeline.
- Single GPU, single hardware platform; no distributed or mixed-precision results despite the cited Wang et al. (2025) FSDP motivation.
- Code/data unavailable at submission time.

## Relevance

如果你关心 PINN/科学机器学习的训练效率，或者在把 Transformer/注意力机制塞进 PINN，这篇里的 `grad_outputs=ones` 静默错误是必须知道的——它意味着相当一部分含 BatchNorm 或 attention 的 PINN 实现算出来的逐样本导数是错的（Laplacian 偏差达 7–8 个绝对单位）。另外，"用前向 pass 替代高阶 autograd 图"的思路和扩散模型里用 FD 近似 JVP 的做法同源，对显存受限的大 batch 训练有直接迁移价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Which Poisson number is correct — Figure 1's 1.15%/1.66% or Table 2's 1.11%/1.41%? Does the sFD advantage survive a paired-seed significance test rather than an eyeball comparison of overlapping error bars?
- Can the AD dependency in `find_eps` be removed entirely, e.g. by selecting ε via cross-consistency between two FD estimates at different step sizes (Richardson-style), making the pipeline truly forward-only?
- Is sFD's Poisson gain actually collapse suppression, or just gradient noise? The control experiment — deterministic FD with matched-variance perturbation that does *not* vary the stencil width — would settle it.
- Does JAX's functional `vmap`+`grad` interface (which the authors mention but did not benchmark) give correct *and* efficient per-sample derivatives for BN/attention, which would make the FD mitigation unnecessary in that ecosystem?
- How does the picture change with higher-order stencils (5-point, 7-point)? Larger truncation exponents shift the ε optimum and could make FD strictly better than the compromise geometric-mean strategy for mixed-order PDEs.
- Do FD-PINNs retain their advantage on inverse problems and on PDEs with sharper multi-scale structure, where the optimal ε might vary spatially rather than globally — i.e. is a per-region or learned ε worth the added complexity?
- Given Langer (2026)'s result that zero-loss FD-PINN solutions correspond exactly to solutions of the discrete PDE on the stencil grid, does the randomly-resampled-collocation setting used here (no fixed grid) inherit that guarantee, or does it fall outside the theory?
