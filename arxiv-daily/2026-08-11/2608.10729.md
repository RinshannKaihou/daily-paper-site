> **一句话总结：** 该文把大模型的"生成—验证—修订"自精炼循环形式化为一个有限时域最优停止问题，证明在"分数单调 + 收益递减"两个由高斯过程辨识得到的经验性质下，最优策略退化为一个与阶段无关的单一阈值 α\*，并在 EFFIBENCH 上用 Haiku 4.5 / Gemini Flash-Lite 3.1 / GPT Codex Mini 5.1 验证：在全部 12 组（3 模型 × 4 个收益权重 β）配置中，优化阈值策略的期望净收益 E{g(x_τ) − cτ} 都优于固定迭代次数策略和 Sun et al. 的 UCB 自适应停止（如 Haiku、β=1 时 0.87 vs UCB 0.76 vs 最佳固定策略 0.81）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper casts LLM self-refinement as a finite-horizon optimal stopping problem, identifies the score dynamics with a Gaussian process on EFFIBENCH, and proves (Props. 2–4) that the optimal policy is a *single stage-independent threshold* α\*. Evaluated on 50 held-out EFFIBENCH tasks with three frontier models, the optimized threshold policy beats both fixed-iteration stopping (the standard practice in the self-refine literature) and the UCB adaptive-stopping baseline of Sun et al. in all 12 configurations — e.g. Haiku 4.5 at β=1 reaches an expected net value of 0.87 vs 0.76 for UCB and 0.81 for the best fixed-iteration policy (6 iterations), and 9.3 vs 8.8 (UCB) / 8.6 (best fixed) at β=10.

## 核心贡献

- **把自精炼形式化为最优停止问题。** 状态是当前保留的最优输出质量分 x ∈ [0,1]，动作是 Stop / Continue，Continue 付出固定成本 c > 0（按 token 计价的真实美元成本），Stop 得到收益 g(x)。目标为 max_μ E{g(x_τμ) − c·τμ}。作者称这是首次将经典最优停止理论用于分析基础模型的自精炼过程。
- **两条由数据辨识出的结构性质。** 用高斯过程（Matérn-5/2 核、均值函数 m(x)=x）从 EFFIBENCH 上三个前沿模型的真实轨迹中拟合分数转移函数 q，观察到：(1) 单调性——q̃ 在 [0,1] 上非降；(2) 收益递减——q̃(x) − x 非增。低分样本一次修订收益大，高分样本几乎无收益。
- **三个结构性定理。** Prop. 2：最优停止集是上闭集 [α_k, 1]，即最优策略是阈值策略；Prop. 3：阈值随阶段 k 非增；Prop. 4：所有阶段的停止集相等，因此存在**唯一、与阶段无关**的阈值 α\*。这把策略搜索从"求一列函数 μ_0..μ_{N−1}"降为一维标量优化。
- **可高效计算的策略求解。** 用 SPSA、交叉熵方法、差分进化三种仿真式随机逼近方法在 GP 仿真器上优化 α\*，三者收敛到同一水平，且均在数秒内收敛（M4 Pro，Python 3.11）。
- **实证对比与开源。** 在 EFFIBENCH 上以 N=10、50 道与辨识集不相交的题目、3 个随机种子对比固定迭代（1–6 次）与 UCB 自适应停止；代码开源于 GitHub（Kim-Hammar/optimal_stopping_foundation_models）。

## Key Contributions

- A decision-theoretic (rather than purely empirical) formulation of the generate → verify → refine loop as a discrete-time, finite-horizon, continuous-state Markov optimal stopping problem with a per-iteration monetary/compute cost.
- System identification of the three problem primitives from real data: continuation cost c (measured per-iteration API cost), payoff g(x) = βx (β is a *design knob*, not estimated), and transition dynamics f via a GP posterior with x_{k+1} = min{1, max{x_k, q(x_k) + w_k}}.
- Structural results proving the optimal policy collapses to a single scalar threshold, plus a geometric characterization of V\* as the smallest superharmonic majorant of g.
- An empirical characterization of self-refinement economics: quality gains saturate after 3–4 iterations while token consumption and dollar cost grow approximately linearly in the number of iterations.
- Head-to-head evaluation against the dominant practice (fixed iteration count) and one adaptive baseline (UCB, ACL 2026), across three commercial models and four cost/quality trade-off settings.

## Methodology

The use case is code optimization on EFFIBENCH. At each iteration k the model emits code, which is executed against a test suite measuring correctness, execution time and memory. These are folded into a *candidate* score x̃_k ∈ [0,1]: zero if any test fails, otherwise clip((T_max − T_k)/(T_max − T_min), 0, 1), where T_k = (1/T_ref)∫₀^{T_k} m_k(t) dt is the memory-over-time integral normalized by a reference solution — a single scalar that fuses runtime and memory. The problem state is the best-so-far score, x_k = max{x_{k−1}, x̃_k}, which is fed back to the stopping policy μ.

Data collection ran the refinement loop for N = 10 iterations on 50 tasks for each of HAIKU 4.5, GEMINI FLASH-LITE 3.1 and GPT CODEX MINI 5.1. From this, c is set to the measured mean per-iteration cost: $0.01 (Haiku), $0.0025 (Gemini Flash-Lite), $0.005 (GPT Codex Mini), priced as of March 17, 2026. The paper justifies a constant (stage-independent) c by noting per-iteration token consumption is roughly flat.

The dynamics f are estimated as a GP regression from x_k to x̃_{k+1} with prior mean m(x) = x (refinement preserves quality) and a Matérn-type kernel κ(x,x′) = (1 + √5r + 5r²/3)exp(−√5r). The posterior mean q̃ supplies Observations 1–2, which are then *treated as exact* in the proofs. Given monotone f and non-increasing f(x,w) − x, the DP recursion V_k\*(x) = max{g(x), −c + E[V\*_{k+1}(f(x,w))]} yields a non-decreasing V\* and a non-increasing V\* − g, hence an upper stopping set. Prop. 4's key step is that f(x,w) ≥ x always (best-so-far retention), so from any state in S\*_{k+1} the process cannot leave the stopping region, forcing Q\*_k = Q\*_{k+1} and thus a stage-independent α\*.

Policy computation therefore reduces to a 1-D stochastic optimization of E{g(x_{τ_{μα}}) − cτ_{μα}} over α ∈ [0,1], evaluated by simulating the identified GP model, and solved with SPSA, the cross-entropy method, and differential evolution. Final evaluation is on 50 EFFIBENCH problems disjoint from the identification set, with 3 seeds, against fixed-iteration policies (1–6 iterations) and the UCB policy of Sun et al. instantiated over thresholds {0, 0.1, …, 1}.

## Results & Highlights

- **Haiku 4.5, β = 1 (EFFIBENCH, expected net value E{g(x_τ) − cτ}, higher better):** optimized threshold policy **0.87** vs **0.76** for the UCB baseline (+14.5% relative) and **0.81** for the best fixed-iteration policy (6 iterations).
- **Haiku 4.5, β = 10:** threshold policy **9.3** vs **8.8** (UCB) and **8.6** (best fixed, 6 iterations); the worst fixed policy (1 iteration) scores 6.7, so the fixed-iteration family spans 6.7–8.6 depending on a hyperparameter the practitioner must guess.
- **Gemini Flash-Lite 3.1, β = 1:** **0.84** vs **0.76** (UCB) and **0.81** (6 fixed iterations); at β = 10, **8.5** vs **8.3** (UCB) and **8.2** (6 fixed iterations).
- **GPT Codex Mini 5.1, β = 1:** **0.79** vs **0.74** (UCB) and **0.77** (6 fixed iterations); at β = 10 the margin narrows to **8.2** vs **8.1** for both UCB and the 6-iteration policy — essentially within the reported one-standard-deviation error bars.
- **Cost-regime reversal (Haiku, β = 0.1):** threshold policy **0.069**, UCB **0.067**, and fixed policies *decrease* monotonically with more iterations — 0.062 (1 iter) down to 0.03 (6 iters) — i.e. a practitioner who hard-codes 6 iterations loses over half the achievable value when refinement is expensive relative to payoff. Gemini at β = 0.1: **0.073** for the threshold policy vs 0.068 for 6 fixed iterations.
- **Learned thresholds (Fig. 6)** move as theory predicts: α\* ≈ 0.22 (Haiku) / 0.28 at β = 0.01, rising to 0.52–0.65 at β = 0.1, and 0.93 / 0.85 / 0.80 (Haiku / Gemini / GPT) at β = 10. Haiku has the *lowest* threshold at small β, consistent with its 4× higher per-iteration cost ($0.01 vs $0.0025).
- **Diminishing returns (Fig. 3):** all three models gain roughly up to ~10% in execution time and memory reduction, with most of the improvement inside the first 3–4 of 10 iterations, while cumulative tokens (up to ~60k) and dollar cost (~$0.1 for Haiku at 10 iterations) grow approximately linearly.
- **Compute:** all three optimizers (SPSA, cross-entropy, differential evolution) converge to statistically indistinguishable values within seconds on an M4 Pro, over 3 seeds.

## Authors & Credibility

Kim Hammar (Imperial College London, supported by Swedish Research Council contract 2024-06436) has a substantial track record applying optimal stopping and POMDP/game-theoretic control to security problems (IEEE TNSM 2022 intrusion prevention; TIFS 2025/2026; AAAI 2026), so the stopping-theory machinery here is squarely in-house expertise. Tansu Alpcan (University of Melbourne) is well known for security games and networked control; Emil C. Lupu (Imperial) leads a long-standing systems-security group. The paper is formatted as a 6-page IEEE conference submission (eess.SY, likely CDC/ECC-track) and links a public code repository. The proofs are elementary but complete and correct as far as I can check by inspection. One credibility wrinkle: the model citations are stale/mismatched — "HAIKU 4.5" cites the *Claude 3 model family* report, "GPT CODEX MINI 5.1" cites the *GPT-4* technical report, and "GEMINI FLASH-LITE 3.1" cites the original 2024 Gemini paper. This is sloppy rather than substantive, but it signals the empirical section got less care than the theory.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The theoretical framing is genuinely new for this application: nobody has, to my knowledge, given a stopping-time formalism for LLM self-refinement with a proof that stage-dependence collapses. But the mathematics itself is textbook (Snell envelope, superharmonic majorant, monotone-DP threshold arguments); the contribution is transfer, not invention. Prop. 4 — the headline "single threshold" result — is largely a consequence of a modeling choice rather than a deep property: because the state is defined as the best-so-far score, f(x,w) ≥ x, so the state process is non-decreasing and can never re-enter the continuation region. Under that construction, stage-independence is nearly immediate, and the finite horizon N essentially stops mattering.

**Rigor.** Reasonable but with a circularity that deserves naming: the threshold α\* is optimized *on simulations of the GP model* and the evaluation metric is E{g(x_τ) − cτ}, i.e. exactly the objective the policy was tuned for, computed under a payoff function g(x)=βx the authors themselves chose. No task-level metric is reported (pass rate, mean speedup at matched dollar cost, fraction of tasks where the policy stopped too early). A reader cannot tell whether the threshold policy actually produces better code at equal spend, only that it maximizes the authors' scalarization. The identification/evaluation split (50 disjoint problems) is a real and welcome control, but 50 tasks × 3 seeds is thin, and at GPT Codex Mini / β=10 the claimed win (8.2 vs 8.1) is inside the plotted standard deviation — the abstract's "significantly more cost-efficient" is not supported in that cell.

**Baseline strength.** Fixed-iteration stopping is a deliberately weak straw man (it is standard practice, but no one would argue it is adaptive), and the paper's own data show its ranking flips entirely with β. The single serious baseline is UCB (Sun et al., ACL 2026), and it is instantiated over a coarse grid {0, 0.1, …, 1} — a 0.1-resolution threshold grid against a continuously optimized threshold is not a fair fight, and much of the reported gap (e.g. α\*=0.65 vs a grid that can only pick 0.6 or 0.7) may simply be quantization. Missing baselines that would have been cheap: "stop when the score stops improving for one iteration", "stop when the verifier reports all tests passing", and an oracle-threshold upper bound.

**Missing ablations.** No sensitivity study on N (only N=10); no ablation of the GP kernel or of the prior mean m(x)=x, which directly encodes Observation 1 and therefore partly *assumes* the monotonicity the paper later "observes"; no analysis of sim-to-real gap between the GP simulator and actual EFFIBENCH rollouts; no test of the constant-cost assumption beyond a one-sentence appeal to Fig. 3, even though input tokens visibly accumulate as the refinement transcript grows. The authors do honestly flag the biggest theoretical caveat themselves: Props. 2–4 require Observations 1–2 to hold *exactly*, whereas they are read off a GP posterior mean with non-trivial variance (Fig. 4 shades up to three standard deviations, and the bands are wide at the extremes of x).

**Data quirks.** The score definition collapses correctness and efficiency into one number with a hard zero for any test failure, so a state of x=0 conflates "broken code" with "correct but catastrophically slow code" — the GP is asked to regress through that discontinuity. Normalization constants T_min, T_max and the reference integral T_ref are inherited from EFFIBENCH and never varied, yet they set the entire scale on which β is interpreted.

**Significance.** Real but bounded. The practical payload is a one-line rule ("stop when the verifier score exceeds α\*"), with α\* obtainable in seconds — that is deployable and the framing (per-iteration dollar cost vs marginal quality) is the right one for agentic pipelines. The absolute savings on 50 EFFIBENCH tasks are cents; the argument for importance rests on the extrapolation to "thousands of daily invocations," which is asserted, not measured.

**Red flags.** (1) Evaluation metric identical to the training objective; (2) UCB baseline handicapped by a coarse threshold grid; (3) "significantly" overclaimed given overlapping error bars in several cells; (4) mismatched model citations; (5) β is a free parameter with no principled selection procedure, and the entire comparison story changes with it.

## Strengths

- Clean, honest problem formalization that makes an implicit engineering knob (iteration count) into an explicit, optimizable decision variable tied to real dollar costs.
- The structural results genuinely buy something: the reduction from a policy sequence to a scalar makes optimization trivially cheap (seconds), which is exactly what makes the method deployable.
- Grounded in real measurements from three commercial models with actual per-token pricing, rather than a synthetic cost model.
- Proper identification/evaluation split on disjoint EFFIBENCH problems, three seeds, and variance reported everywhere.
- The empirical characterization of diminishing returns (most gains within 3–4 iterations, linear cost growth) is a useful standalone finding for anyone budgeting test-time compute.
- Self-aware limitations section: the authors explicitly state that the theory depends on Observations 1–2 holding exactly and propose exploiting GP posterior uncertainty for probabilistic guarantees.

## Limitations

- Single benchmark (EFFIBENCH), single modality (code efficiency), 50 evaluation tasks; no math reasoning, no multi-modal or time-series models despite the "foundation models" framing in the title.
- The payoff g(x)=βx is linear and β is unestimated; all conclusions are stated per-β, and there is no guidance for choosing β in a real deployment.
- Constant continuation cost c ignores growing context length across iterations and any variance in output length.
- The threshold is optimized against a GP surrogate; no direct measurement of how much value is lost due to surrogate misspecification.
- The "best-so-far" state definition assumes a reliable verifier that never mis-ranks candidates; with noisy or gameable verifiers (the common case outside unit-test-checkable code) monotonicity of the state fails and Prop. 4 does not apply.
- Threshold policies act on a scalar verifier score only; richer feedback (error messages, partial test results) that a policy could condition on is discarded.

## Relevance

这篇对做 test-time compute / agent 推理成本控制的工作很对口：它给出了"什么时候该停止让模型再改一版"的可证明最优规则，且只需要一个可用几秒钟标定的标量阈值，非常容易嵌进现有的 verifier-in-the-loop 代码代理或 LLM-as-judge 精炼流水线。同时它的经验结论（3–4 轮后收益饱和、成本线性增长、不同模型阈值随单价反向移动）本身就可以直接用于给自己的 refinement loop 设预算上限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the threshold policy still win when the evaluation metric is *not* the training objective — e.g. mean speedup or pass rate at a matched dollar budget? That is the comparison a practitioner actually cares about.
2. How much of the gap to the UCB baseline disappears if UCB is given a continuous or finer threshold grid instead of {0, 0.1, …, 1}?
3. What happens when the verifier is noisy or partially adversarial, so that x_k = max{x_{k−1}, x̃_k} no longer holds? Does the stage-independence result survive a POMDP formulation with belief state over true quality?
4. Can the GP posterior variance be used, as the authors suggest, to certify the threshold structure with probability 1−δ rather than assuming Observations 1–2 exactly — and how much does the threshold move under the pessimistic posterior?
5. Does a single global α\* transfer across task difficulty strata, or would a threshold conditioned on cheap task features (input length, initial score x_0) do materially better?
6. How does the framework extend to multi-stop / restart settings where the policy may branch (sample k candidates) rather than only continue-or-stop, which is closer to modern best-of-n + refine agent designs?
7. Since cost c is measured in dollars at a fixed date, how brittle are the learned thresholds to the fast-moving price/quality frontier of frontier model APIs?
