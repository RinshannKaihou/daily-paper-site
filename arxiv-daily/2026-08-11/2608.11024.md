> **一句话总结：** 该文用「真实图像 vs. 空白灰图」双前向传播把 VLM 的 yes/no 属性判断拆成视觉 logit 差 δvis 与语言先验 logit 差 δprior，在 VAW 的 10,791 条负样本上发现 δvis 与幻觉的 Spearman 相关达 0.755–0.835 而 δprior 仅 0.066–0.177（仅用先验的分类器 58.5–59.0%，几乎等于 57.9% 的多数类基线），据此否定「语言先验主导」的通行解释，并按诊断结果路由三种修复算子（Calib/Abstain/Adapt），把 color FPR 降低 11.6–17.4 pp，而 VCD/ICD 在同一设置下为 0 pp 甚至变差。

> **TL;DR:** On a 10,791-sample negative-ground-truth attribute probe built from VAW, the visual logit margin predicts attribute false positives (Spearman ρ = 0.755/0.760/0.835 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B / InternVL3.5-4B / LLaVA-1.5-7B) while the null-image language prior is near chance (ρ = 0.177/0.066/0.066); the routed VISOR framework cuts color FPR from 33.8% to 22.2% on Qwen and from 47.2% to 29.8% on LLaVA, whereas the prior-suppression baselines VCD and ICD make color FPR *worse* (38.0% and 36.5% on Qwen), and per-word visual LoRA adaptation drops six-word material FPR from 38.1% to 23.9% (shared hard-negative LoRA only reaches 31.1%).

## 核心贡献

- **直接检验了「语言先验主导」假说并给出否定证据。** 通过配对的真实图像 / 空白灰图前向传播，把每个属性预测分解成 δvis 与 δprior 两个标量；在三个模型族、三类属性、10,791 条负样本上，δprior 与幻觉几乎无关（ρ = 0.066–0.177），只用 δprior 的分类器精度 58.5–59.0%，多数类基线是 57.9%。
- **给出属性幻觉的两机制分类法。** 机制 A「视觉犹疑」（color/state）：δvis 方向正确但裕度不足，属于阈值放置错误；机制 B「视觉表征反转」（material）：δvis 低信噪比甚至方向错误，输出层减先验在数学上无法翻转决策（B2 材质词的 δ̂prior ≤ 0，而幻觉样本 δvis > 0）。
- **层间 SNR 诊断把机制 B 定位到解码器末段。** 定义 SNR(w,l) = |µ[δvis^(l)]| / σ[δvis^(l)]，B2 材质词的 SNR 从 L17 附近约 35 掉到 L36 的 0.9 以下，高 SNR 词维持在 2–4，说明信息在「视觉→Yes/No logit 投影」阶段丢失而非中层就不存在。
- **诊断驱动的路由式修复，而非拼装的多方法集合。** 同一个 VSNR 坐标同时用于假设检验、层间回溯和算子选择：Calib（校准阈值 1[δvis − γδprior > 0]）、Abstain（免训练、按 SNR 阈值弃答）、Adapt（按词训练视觉侧 LoRA）。
- **提供了负对照实验表（Table IX）。** 语言层 DPO 使 FPR 反而上升 2.0–2.1 pp（视觉编码器梯度为零），共享视觉 LoRA 退化为一律答 “No”（FNR +100 pp），推理时隐状态编辑不稳定（−1.7 到 +60 pp），只有 L28 监督探针能恢复部分信号（FPR −10.8 pp）。

## Key Contributions

- **A falsification test for the prior-dominance hypothesis at the attribute level.** Prior work (VCD, ICD, HA-DPO) assumes language priors override visual evidence; this paper measures the two contributions separately and finds the prior contributes almost nothing to sample-level attribute false positives.
- **A two-mechanism taxonomy** separating threshold-correctable low-margin errors (color/state) from directional visual-signal failure (material), backed by layer-wise SNR trajectories, word-level FPR regressions, and annotation-noise filtering.
- **A Bayesian argument for why contrastive decoding cannot work on material attributes:** for an output-level correction δ'vis = δvis − αδ̂prior to flip a hallucinating sample requires δ̂prior > δvis/α > 0, but measured δ̂prior ≤ 0 for the tested B2 material words across all three models.
- **Three routed operators with different deployment profiles** — Calib (no parameter change, only a decision rule), Abstain (training-free, trades coverage), Adapt (per-word visual LoRA for the highest-risk vocabulary).
- **Mechanistic validation of the repair, not just outcome metrics:** after Adapt training, final-layer SNR rises for rubber (+0.307), steel (+0.407), metal (+0.177), ceramic (+0.246), paper (+0.256), concentrated in L28–L36 — i.e., the fix moves the quantity the diagnosis said was broken, not just the yes-rate.

## Methodology

The setup is a controlled yes/no probe: "Is the [object] [attribute]? Answer yes or no." built from VAW (Visual Attributes in the Wild, 620 attribute words, dense per-instance positive/negative annotations from Visual Genome). Negatives come from `negative_attributes`, positives from `positive_attributes`. For Qwen2.5-VL-3B the splits are color 2,992 samples (1,302 pos / 1,690 neg, 61 words), material 2,417 (969 / 1,448, 45 words), state 803 (344 / 459, 11 words). FPR on negative-ground-truth samples is the primary metric, with F1 / Accuracy / Yes-Ratio reported in POPE format.

Each (image, query) pair gets two forward passes. The first yields δvis = logit(yes | I, q) − logit(no | I, q); the second replaces the image with a uniform gray 128×128×3 null image to yield δprior. Logits are taken as the max log-probability over surface forms {"Yes","yes"," Yes"," yes"} vs. the "No" variants at the final token position. Under greedy decoding the first answer token fixes the label, so δvis ≥ 0 is exactly the hallucination condition for a negative sample. The same final norm and LM head are applied to intermediate hidden states to get a per-layer δvis^(l) and hence a per-layer SNR.

Equation 4 decomposes the log-odds into a visual likelihood ratio plus a language prior; in the null-image limit the expression reduces to δprior, so δvis − δprior approximates the visual likelihood contribution. This is what licenses the claim that prior subtraction only helps when the visual direction is already correct.

Routing: words with directionally correct but low-margin δvis get Calib, a calibrated rule 1[δvis − γδprior > 0]. Low-SNR material words get either Abstain (SNR threshold τ, no training) or Adapt (one visual LoRA per target word, deliberately not a shared adapter). Models: Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, InternVL3.5-4B-Flash, LLaVA-1.5-7B, spanning Qwen-VL ViT / InternViT-300M / CLIP ViT-L-14 encoders. Greedy decoding, temperature 0, seed 42, single A100 80GB.

## Results & Highlights

- **Diagnostic separation (Table II).** ρ(δvis, hallucination) = 0.755 / 0.760 / 0.835 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B / InternVL3.5-4B / LLaVA-1.5-7B, versus ρ(δprior, hallucination) = 0.177 / 0.066 / 0.066. A classifier using δprior alone reaches 58.5–59.0% accuracy against a 57.9% majority-class baseline — a delta of 0.6–1.1 pp, i.e. essentially chance.
- **Color FPR, VISOR vs. VCD/ICD (Table I(a)).** Qwen2.5-VL-3B: baseline 33.8% → VISOR 22.2%, while VCD *rises* to 38.0% and ICD to 36.5%. InternVL3.5-4B: 31.6% → 15.4% (VCD/ICD both 35.6%). LLaVA-1.5-7B: 47.2% → 29.8% (VCD/ICD both 51.3%). So the strongest prior-suppression baseline is worse than doing nothing on color, by +2.7 to +4.2 pp.
- **State FPR.** Qwen 25.9% → 19.0%, InternVL 25.7% → 19.6% (VCD/ICD 28.1%, i.e. +2.4 pp worse than baseline), LLaVA 32.5% → 32.5% (no gain; VCD/ICD 37.0%).
- **Material FPR (Table I(b)).** VISOR gives modest gains — Qwen 15.7% → 14.2%, InternVL 19.5% → 17.8%, LLaVA 25.8% → 24.5% — while VCD and ICD report *exactly* the baseline FPR (15.7 / 19.5 / 25.8) on all three models, i.e. 0.0 pp.
- **Abstain operating curve (Table VII).** On Qwen material queries: τ = 0.3 → FPR 14.1% (−1.6 pp) at 6.2% abstention; τ = 0.6 → 10.2% (−5.5 pp) at 14.2% abstention; τ = 1.0 → 4.8% (−10.9 pp) but 45.5% abstention.
- **Adapt on six target material words (Table VIII).** Mean FPR 38.1% → 23.9% on Qwen; the shared hard-negative LoRA baseline only reaches 31.1% and actively harms leather (26.0% → 34.0%), while per-word Adapt takes leather to 14.0%. Cross-model: InternVL 44.7% → 32.8% (5/6 words improved), LLaVA 34.2% → 26.8% (6/6 improved). MME regression stays within ±1.25 pp.
- **SNR is the dominant word-level predictor of material FPR (Table V).** Across 46 material words, ρ(SNR, FPR) = −0.916 (p < 0.0001), beating either component alone: mean δvis alone −0.765, σ(δvis) alone +0.633.
- **Prior-positive conflict cases (Table III).** In all 278 cases where δprior > 0 but δvis < 0 (147 on Qwen, 74 on InternVL, 57 on LLaVA), the hallucination rate is 0.0% — the real image reliably overrides a positive blank-image prior.
- **Annotation-noise separation (Table IV).** steel's observed FPR 45.5% collapses to a "true" FPR of 4.5% once 18/20 hallucinating negatives judged visually metallic are removed (a B1 label-granularity artifact), whereas rubber (44.4%), paper (41.9%), and leather (26.0%) are unchanged — genuine representation failures.
- **Word-level asymmetry.** ρ(mean δprior, FPR) = +0.420 (p = 0.003, n = 49) for color but +0.034 (p = 0.83) for material, while δvis distribution properties explain material FPR at ρ = +0.752 (p < 0.0001).
- **End-to-end material gain is small by design:** the six adapted words are 16.1% of material negatives, so estimated full-material FPR on Qwen falls only 15.7% → 13.5%.

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors (Yufei Zhang, Chenlu Zhan, Hongwei Wang, corresponding) are at Zhejiang University. The related-work coverage is current and appropriate (VCD, ICD, DAMRO, DeCo, V-DPO, HA-DPO, RLHF-V, POPE, CHAIR, HallusionBench, VAW, OVAD, ABO). Experimental hygiene is stated explicitly — greedy decoding, temperature 0, seed 42, single A100 80GB — and the paper reports negative controls and a scoped-claim paragraph that explicitly declines to generalize to "language models have no priors." No code or adapter release is mentioned anywhere in the main text, which is the largest credibility gap.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-high).** The null-image contrast trick is not new (it is essentially how VCD constructs its contrastive branch, and "blind" VLM baselines have been probed before, e.g. the cited Mirage work). What is new is turning that contrast into a *routing* coordinate and using it to argue that the field's default causal story is wrong for attributes specifically. The Bayesian impossibility argument for output-level prior subtraction on B2 words is the sharpest contribution.

**A structural concern about the headline claim.** The paper states that "sign(δvis) matches the hallucination label across all 10,791 negative-ground-truth samples" — under greedy yes/no decoding, δvis ≥ 0 *is* the prediction. So ρ(δvis, hallucination) = 0.755–0.835 is close to tautological; it measures how well a monotone function of the decision variable predicts the decision, not an independent causal finding. The honest comparison would be ρ(δvis − δprior, hallucination) versus ρ(δprior, hallucination), since Eq. 4 itself says δvis already contains the prior term. Calling δvis "the visual signal" in the abstract while defining it as the full log-odds in Eq. 4 is a real slippage, and the correlational headline is weaker than it reads.

**An internal inconsistency in the baseline numbers.** Table I(a) reports VCD color FPR on Qwen as 38.0% (worse than the 33.8% baseline), but Table VI reports VCD color on Qwen as "33.8% (0 pp)". These cannot both be right. More broadly, VCD and ICD produce FPR *identical to the baseline to the decimal* on all three models for material (15.7 / 19.5 / 25.8) and, in Table VI, on color too. Contrastive decoding perturbing zero first-token decisions across thousands of samples is implausible; it suggests either a misconfigured α/cutoff or that the reimplementation is degenerate. Since "VCD/ICD achieve 0 pp" is a load-bearing claim for the whole prior-dominance refutation, this is the paper's most serious red flag.

**Baseline weakness.** The only compared methods are two training-free contrastive-decoding schemes. There is no comparison against a plain calibrated-threshold baseline (Platt scaling or a tuned constant bias on δvis) — which is important because VISOR-Calib *is* a threshold shift, and a constant offset with no δprior term might match it. There is no comparison against DAMRO, DeCo, OPERA, or any preference-optimized model, despite all being cited. The Adapt comparison is against a shared hard-negative LoRA that degenerates to always answering "No" (FNR +100 pp) — a straw baseline.

**Missing ablation on γ.** The paper says γ is calibrated offline but never states whether calibration and evaluation use disjoint splits. If γ is fit on the same VAW negatives that are then scored, the 11–17 pp color gains are partly in-sample. Similarly, the τ sweep for Abstain is reported on the same test set. No held-out or cross-dataset calibration is shown (the GQA color check only validates the δvis ≥ 0 decision rule, not the tuned operator).

**The FPR-only framing hides a trade.** VISOR improves the bolded FPR column but degrades F1 on color for every model (Qwen 73.7 → 71.4, InternVL 74.7 → 71.7, LLaVA 67.8 → 65.2) and pushes Yes-Ratio far below balance (Qwen 55.6 → 43.7, InternVL 54.4 → 37.8). Accuracy rises only 1.2–3.1 pp. Table VI concedes the LLaVA material case increases FNR by 7.1 pp. Some of the gain is therefore a bias shift, not strictly better discrimination — the same critique the paper levels at VCD/ICD ("Yes-Ratio increases reveal worsening yes-bias masked by stable F1/Accuracy") applies symmetrically in the other direction.

**Data quirk, acknowledged but under-controlled.** The steel case (45.5% → 4.5% "true" FPR) shows VAW's negative annotations are substantially wrong for granularity reasons. The relabeling is 18 of 20 samples judged by the authors, with no second annotator and no agreement statistic — a very small, subjective correction that nonetheless determines which words are classified B1 versus B2, and hence which get adapters.

**Scale and scope.** All three models are 3B–7B. There is no check on a frontier-scale VLM, where the balance between prior and visual signal plausibly differs. Adapt covers six words (16.1% of material negatives); the paper is candid that open-vocabulary scaling needs an unevaluated target-word selection and adapter-management policy. Per-word 95% CIs are wide (steel [10.3%, 35.1%] against a 45.5% baseline), so several individual word-level improvements are not separately significant; the paper defers stratified CMH tests to the supplement.

**Reproducibility (medium).** Seeds, decoding settings, hardware, null-image construction, and surface-form logit extraction are all specified precisely enough to reimplement, and VAW is public. But no code link, no adapter weights, no γ/τ values, and several key robustness results (null-image sensitivity r > 0.90, CMH tests, per-word tables, free-form transfer) live only in a supplement not included here.

**Significance.** If the core claim survives the baseline-implementation question, it is genuinely useful: it says a large family of hallucination-mitigation methods is aimed at the wrong causal pathway for fine-grained attributes, and it supplies a cheap two-forward-pass diagnostic to decide when they will help. The late-layer SNR-collapse localization (L28–L36 on a 36-layer decoder) is a concrete, checkable interpretability result.

## Strengths

- Asks a falsifiable mechanistic question and reports the answer that contradicts the field's default assumption, rather than proposing yet another decoding trick.
- The diagnosis-to-intervention loop is tight: the same scalar that classifies the failure mode also selects the operator, and post-hoc layer-wise SNR measurement confirms the fix moved the diagnosed quantity (rubber +0.307, steel +0.407 in L28–L36) rather than just suppressing "yes".
- Table IX's negative controls (language-layer DPO, shared visual LoRA, hidden-state edits, L28 supervised probe) are unusually honest — the authors publish four things that did not work and use them to justify the design.
- Explicit annotation-noise audit separating label-granularity artifacts (B1) from real representation failure (B2), instead of reporting the inflated raw FPR.
- Careful scoping language: the paper states outright that it does not deny language bias in general, does not claim δvis is independent of language pre-training, and does not explain the pre-training origin of Mechanism B.
- Three architecturally distinct encoder families, with the qualitative pattern reproducing on all three.

## Limitations

- Binary yes/no probing over a closed vocabulary may not transfer to free-form captioning hallucination, which is where CHAIR-style metrics live; free-form transfer results are deferred to the supplement.
- Adapt requires one LoRA per target word and per-word supervised negatives; six words is a demonstration, not a deployable system, and end-to-end material FPR moves only 15.7% → 13.5%.
- Abstain's headline −10.9 pp comes at 45.5% abstention, which is unusable outside narrow safety-critical settings; the practical τ = 0.6 point is −5.5 pp at 14.2% abstention.
- No evidence disentangling pre-training coverage from decoder projection as the source of Mechanism B; the L28 probe only shows the information is recoverable, not why it is lost.
- Null image is a single uniform gray; robustness across black/white/noise is asserted (r > 0.90) but only in supplementary material.
- VAW/Visual Genome attribute negatives are noisy enough that one of six target words (steel) turned out to be mostly mislabeled, which raises the question of how many of the remaining FPR numbers are label error.

## Relevance

对于关注 VLM 幻觉与机制可解释性的读者，这篇是少见的「先证伪主流因果假设、再据此设计干预」的工作，其 δvis / δprior 双前向分解和层间 SNR 回溯可以直接搬到自己的评测流程里做诊断，成本只是每条样本多一次前向。若你在用 VCD / ICD / DPO 这类先验抑制方法且发现对细粒度属性无效，本文给出了「为什么无效」的数学解释（B2 词的 δ̂prior ≤ 0，输出层减先验无法翻转符号），值得在采纳其结论前先自查 baseline 实现是否退化。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is ρ(δvis − δprior, hallucination) — the actual visual likelihood term from Eq. 4 — as opposed to ρ(δvis, hallucination)? Without it, the headline correlation is confounded by the fact that δvis is the decision variable itself.
2. Why do VCD and ICD produce FPR identical to the baseline to one decimal place on material for all three models, and why does Table I(a) (VCD color 38.0% on Qwen) disagree with Table VI (33.8%, 0 pp)? Resolving this determines whether the prior-suppression refutation holds.
3. Was γ calibrated on a held-out split? How much of the 11–17 pp color gain survives cross-dataset calibration (e.g., fit γ on VAW, evaluate on GQA color)?
4. How does VISOR-Calib compare to a single tuned constant bias on δvis, with no δprior term at all? If they match, the null-image pass buys diagnosis but not remediation.
5. Does the near-chance prior signal persist at frontier scale (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL 38B+, GPT-4o), or is prior dominance a property that emerges — or disappears — with capacity?
6. Can the low-SNR words be predicted *before* deployment from pre-training frequency or encoder-side attribute separability, enabling a target-word selection policy for open-vocabulary Adapt?
7. Does the L28–L36 SNR collapse reproduce across decoder depths and architectures, i.e. is it a fixed fraction of depth or tied to Qwen's specific projection design?
8. What happens to positive-ground-truth samples and FNR under Adapt? Only MME regression (±1.25 pp) is reported, which is a coarse proxy for whether the per-word adapters damage true-positive material recognition.
