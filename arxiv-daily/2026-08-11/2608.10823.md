> **一句话总结：** 面向华为昇腾平台上 MoE 大模型 RL 后训练的故障复现难题，作者提出"多视角+频率感知"的专家剪枝法（融合 Router 参数距离、专家共激活距离、路由上下文原型距离，再做 K-Medoids 聚类选代表专家），构造保持骨干网络与 Top-k 路由结构不变的小型代理模型：DeepSeek-V3.2 从 256 专家/512 NPU 压到 16 专家/64 NPU，单步成本从 426.7 NPU-hours 降到 12.8（33.3×），GSM8K 保住 58.5%（频率-only 基线 53.0%，随机/First-K 仅 3.0%），并能在注入 Rollout LogP 精度故障与 Actor 更新缺失故障时复现与原模型一致的异常方向和趋势。

> **TL;DR:** MoE proxy models built by multi-view (router-parameter + co-activation + routed-context) K-Medoids expert pruning let you reproduce RL post-training failures on 8 NPUs instead of 16 (Qwen3-30B-A3B) or 64 instead of 512 (DeepSeek-V3.2, 426.7 → 12.8 NPU-hours per step, 33.3×). On DeepSeek-V3.2 at a 16-expert budget the method holds 58.5% GSM8K vs 53.0% for frequency-only selection and 3.0% for random/First-K (original: 95.0%); on Qwen3-30B-A3B at 64 experts it reaches 86.0% vs 81.0% for the best pairwise-view ablation (original: 93.5%). Both a Rollout LogP precision fault and an Actor-update-omission fault reproduce with matching anomaly directions, while the Sub-MoE expert-merging baseline fails to even sustain stable training. Worth reading if you run large MoE RLHF/GRPO pipelines and burn cluster-hours chasing intermittent training bugs; skip if you want a general-purpose MoE compression method — the 48-expert Qwen proxy collapses to 48.0% GSM8K.

## 核心贡献

- **RL 后训练故障分类学**：基于昇腾平台大规模 RL 后训练的实战经验，总结出四类代表性故障（框架/模型状态一致性、算子实现与数值一致性、专家路由一致性、优化与训练稳定性），列出典型症状与主导成因（Table 1）。
- **明确"故障复现"的判据**：不要求代理模型与原模型产生数值相同的指标，只要求在同一故障条件下出现**相同的异常类型、指标变化方向和时间趋势**——这是一个刻意放宽但可操作的定义。
- **多视角、频率感知的专家剪枝**：从三个互补视角建模专家相似度——Router 权重向量的 L2 距离（静态路由偏好）、Top-k 共激活的余弦式距离（动态路由/专家利用模式）、路由权重加权隐状态原型的距离（专家接收的输入表征）——用 95 分位归一化后加权融合，做 K-Medoids 聚类，每簇取激活频率最高的专家作为代表。
- **结构保持**：直接拷贝被选专家的 MLP 与对应 Router 行，不做参数平均、不做知识蒸馏、不做任何微调，因此保留原始骨干、Top-k 路由规则与标准 MoE 执行路径（这是与 Sub-MoE 等 merging 方法的关键区别）。
- **低支持度专家的兜底策略**：若某专家的累计路由质量 m_i = Σ_t p_{t,i} 低于阈值 τ_m，则用 Router 距离替换其路由上下文距离，避免用少量样本估出的噪声原型污染聚类。

## Key Contributions

- A practitioner-grounded taxonomy of four RL post-training failure classes observed on Huawei Ascend clusters, mapped to symptoms and dominant root causes.
- A relaxed but usable fault-reproduction criterion: matching anomaly *type*, metric-change *direction*, and temporal *trend* — explicitly not numerical equivalence.
- A multi-view, frequency-aware expert-pruning algorithm (Algorithm 1) that fuses router-parameter distance, co-activation distance, and routed-context prototype distance into a joint distance for per-layer K-Medoids clustering, with medoids initialized at the most-activated experts.
- Structure-preserving reconstruction: experts and their router rows are copied verbatim, so the proxy keeps the original backbone, Top-k rule, and MoE dispatch path — no parameter averaging, no fine-tuning, no calibration-time gradient work.
- End-to-end validation across two model families (Qwen3-30B-A3B, DeepSeek-V3.2, DeepSeek-V4-Flash) covering cost, training-dynamics fidelity, two injected faults, and expert-selection ablations.

## Methodology

The pipeline has three stages. **(1) Routing statistics collection.** A calibration set (disjoint from test data) is run forward through the original MoE model to log, per layer and per token: input hidden states h_t, the Top-k selected expert set E_t, routing weights p_{t,i}, and cumulative activation frequency. **(2) Multi-view similarity and pruning.** For layer l, three distance matrices are computed over the N experts: D_r(i,j) = ||w_i − w_j||₂ on router weight vectors (bias concatenated if present); D_c(i,j) = 1 − (co-activation count) / sqrt(count_i · count_j + ε), a cosine-style co-activation dissimilarity derived from Top-k joint selections; and D_f(i,j), the L2 distance between L2-normalized routing-weight-weighted hidden-state prototypes Σ_t p_{t,i} h_t. D_r and D_f are normalized by the 95th percentile of their off-diagonal entries and clipped to [0,1] (D_c is already bounded), then fused as D_joint = α_r·D̃_r + α_c·D_c + α_f·D̃_f with α_r + α_c + α_f = 1. K-Medoids on D_joint yields K clusters; the most-frequently-activated expert in each cluster becomes the representative. **(3) Reconstruction.** Selected experts' MLP parameters and router rows are copied into the proxy; each MoE layer goes from N to K experts with the Top-k rule untouched.

Experimental setup: Qwen3-30B-A3B (128 routed experts, Top-8) on a Huawei A3 server with 16 Ascend 910 NPUs; proxies at 48 and 64 experts. DeepSeek-V3.2 (256 experts, 512 NPUs across 32 nodes) with a 16-expert proxy on 64 NPUs. DeepSeek-V4-Flash with 64- and 32-expert proxies for training-dynamics curves. RL post-training uses VERL + vLLM with GRPO; GSM8K is both the capability benchmark and the RL task environment. Two faults are injected: the **Rollout LogP Precision Fault** (reduces numerical precision of the scaling factor in rollout log-prob computation, creating a generation/training probability mismatch) and the **Actor Update Omission Fault** (actor parameter updates disabled while the rest of the pipeline runs). Baselines: pairwise-view ablations, frequency-only selection, random selection, fixed First-K retention, and Sub-MoE-style expert grouping/merging (Li et al. 2026).

## Results & Highlights

- **DeepSeek-V3.2 cost (Table 2):** original needs 32 nodes / 512 NPUs at ~50 min per RL step = 426.7 NPU-hours/step; the 16-expert proxy needs 4 nodes / 64 NPUs at ~12 min = 12.8 NPU-hours/step — a **33.3× per-step cost reduction** and an 87.5% cut in accelerator count.
- **Qwen3-30B-A3B cost (Table 2):** 16 → 8 Ascend 910 NPUs (50% fewer), per-step time 128.93 s → 114.83 s, i.e. 0.573 → 0.255 NPU-hours/step (**≈55.5%** reduction). Note the wall-clock gain alone is only ~11%; nearly all the saving comes from halving the device count, so the headline "50%–87.5%" is a deployment-scale claim, not a throughput claim.
- **Expert-selection comparison on DeepSeek-V3.2 at 16 experts/layer (Table 4):** proposed method **58.5% GSM8K** vs **53.0%** frequency-only (**+5.5 pts**), **3.0%** random selection, **3.0%** fixed First-K, against **95.0%** for the original model. The random/First-K collapse to 3.0% is the strongest evidence in the paper that relationship-aware selection matters — but it also means the proxy retains only ~62% of the original's accuracy.
- **Multi-view ablation on Qwen3-30B-A3B (Table 3), original = 93.5% GSM8K:** at 64 experts the full three-view method gets **86.0%** vs 81.0% (Router+Co-activation, **+5.0 pts**), 77.5% (Context+Router), 76.0% (Context+Co-activation). At 48 experts the ordering holds but all numbers crater: **48.0%** for the full method vs 46.5% best pairwise (**only +1.5 pts**) — the multi-view advantage nearly vanishes at the aggressive budget, and a proxy at 48.0% has lost half the original capability.
- **Fault reproduction, Rollout LogP Precision Fault (Fig. 3):** both the original model and the 48-expert proxy show the same qualitative signature after injection — an upward shift in the rollout–training LogP discrepancy (roughly the 0.010–0.015 healthy band moving to ~0.020) and an increase in KL divergence (healthy ~0.002–0.004 rising to ~0.006–0.008) — with matching direction and temporal trend but different magnitudes.
- **Baseline comparison under fault injection:** at the same 48-expert budget, both the proposed method and frequency-only selection reproduce the LogP-shift and KL-increase signatures, while the **expert grouping/merging baseline fails to sustain stable training at all** — supporting the paper's argument that parameter averaging destroys fault-relevant routing structure.
- **Actor Update Omission Fault (Fig. 4):** disabling actor updates flattens reward in both models — the original's reward stays near ~0.80 instead of climbing to ~0.92, and the proxy's stays near ~0.45–0.55 instead of climbing to ~0.85 — same suppression direction, different magnitude and different absolute reward level.
- **Training-dynamics fidelity (Fig. 2, DeepSeek-V4-Flash):** the 64-expert proxy tracks the original's reward trajectory more closely than the 32-expert proxy, and all three show monotonically increasing actor KL loss, though the proxies differ from the original in absolute KL magnitude. This is a purely visual/trend-level claim; no correlation coefficient or trajectory-distance metric is reported despite "training-dynamics consistency" being listed as an evaluation metric.

## Authors & Credibility

A Nanjing University × Huawei collaboration: the academic side is the State Key Laboratory for Novel Software Technology (Chen Tian, Sheng Zhong, Zhibin Wang are established systems/networking researchers there), and the industrial side is a Huawei team with direct access to Ascend 910 clusters and to pre-release DeepSeek-V3.2/V4-Flash-scale RL pipelines. The claim of "experience with large-scale RL post-training on the Huawei Ascend platform" is credible given the 512-NPU numbers and the specificity of the failure taxonomy — these are the kinds of bugs (weight-sync errors, reduction-order inconsistencies, Top-k dispatch mismatches) that only show up when you actually operate such a stack. The paper is formatted as an AAAI submission; no code, calibration data, or expert-selection artifacts are released.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** The individual ingredients are all prior art: router-based expert pruning (MoE-Pruner), frequency/activation-based selection (Lu et al. 2024), clustering-driven expert pruning (Guo et al. 2025b), and small-scale proxies for training instabilities (Wortsman et al. 2023). The genuinely new move is the *objective shift* — pruning for fault-response fidelity rather than task accuracy — plus the routed-context prototype view. That reframing is well argued and the merging baseline's failure to train is a nice empirical payoff for it.

**Rigor (weak in specific, checkable ways).** (a) The taxonomy in Table 1 lists **expert routing consistency issues** as one of four failure classes, and preserving routing behavior is the entire motivation for the method — yet neither injected fault is a routing fault. Both tested faults (a scalar precision change and disabling the optimizer) are architecture-agnostic and would plausibly reproduce in *any* smaller model, including a dense one; a dense-small-model control is conspicuously absent. (b) Hyperparameters α_r, α_c, α_f and the routing-mass threshold τ_m are never given numerical values, and there is no sensitivity study over them — the fusion weights are the method's only real knob. (c) The ablation covers only *pairwise* view combinations; single-view results (router-only, co-activation-only, context-only) are missing, so we cannot tell which view carries the weight. Frequency-only appears in Table 4 but on a different model at a different budget, so it is not directly comparable to Table 3. (d) "Detailed fault-reproduction comparisons are provided in appendix" — the arXiv PDF has no appendix. (e) All RL curves are single runs with no seeds or variance bands, in a setting the paper itself describes as high-variance; distinguishing a reproduced fault from run-to-run noise by eyeballing a smoothed curve is exactly the failure mode this domain is prone to. (f) Fig. 4's smoothing uses "a wider smoothing window for the original model" — an asymmetric visualization choice that makes trend comparison less trustworthy.

**Reproducibility (low).** No code, no calibration set description beyond "independent," no expert-mapping artifacts, no fusion weights, and the hardware (Ascend 910 A3 servers, 32-node DeepSeek-V3.2 RL) is unavailable to essentially all readers. DeepSeek-V4-Flash appears only as a 2026 arXiv preprint reference. Reproducing even the Qwen3-30B-A3B row requires 16 Ascend NPUs.

**Significance (real but bounded).** For a team already burning 426.7 NPU-hours per RL step, a 33.3× cheaper surrogate that shows the same anomaly direction is operationally valuable even if imperfect — you use it to bisect a bug, not to publish a number. The bounding issue is capability: the 48-expert Qwen proxy sits at 48.0% GSM8K, and a proxy that solves half as many problems may not enter the reward regimes (reward hacking, over-optimization, late-stage divergence) where the most expensive real failures actually live — a limitation the paper itself flags in its background section and then does not revisit.

**Red flags.** The abstract's "50%–87.5% accelerator reduction" and "33.3× reduction" are drawn from two different models and presented as a single range; "preserving major training dynamics" is supported only by visual trend agreement; and "retaining basic task capabilities" is doing heavy lifting for a 93.5% → 48.0% drop.

## Strengths

- The problem is real and under-served: nobody publishes on how to *debug* trillion-parameter RL pipelines cheaply, and the failure taxonomy alone is useful field knowledge.
- Deliberately refusing parameter averaging is the right call for this objective, and the paper earns that choice empirically — the merging baseline cannot even complete stable training.
- The three views are genuinely complementary in intent (static preference / dynamic co-selection / input representation), and the ablation confirms the full combination wins at both budgets.
- The low-support fallback (substituting D_r for D_f when routing mass < τ_m) is a thoughtful detail showing the authors hit real estimation noise on rarely-routed experts.
- The random/First-K → 3.0% result is a clean, unambiguous negative control that many pruning papers omit.
- Honest framing of the reproduction criterion (direction and trend, not magnitude) rather than overclaiming numerical equivalence.

## Limitations

- No routing-consistency fault is tested, despite routing preservation being the method's core justification.
- No dense-small-model or naive-smaller-checkpoint control, so the marginal value of *structure-preserving* pruning over just using a small model is unquantified.
- Severe capability loss at aggressive budgets (48.0% vs 93.5% on GSM8K), which may prevent the proxy from reaching the training regimes where subtle late-emerging faults appear.
- Only two faults, both injected synthetically; no case study of an organic, previously-unexplained production bug being diagnosed via the proxy — which would have been the paper's most convincing evidence.
- Fusion weights, τ_m, calibration-set size, and K-Medoids initialization sensitivity are all unreported.
- Single-seed RL curves in a high-variance setting; no quantitative trajectory-similarity metric.
- Evaluation confined to GSM8K math reasoning; no code, instruction-following, or multi-task RL environments.
- Ascend-specific throughput results may not transfer to NVIDIA stacks where MoE dispatch kernels behave differently.

## Relevance

如果你关注 MoE 大模型的 RL 后训练系统工程（VERL/GRPO 流水线、训练-推理数值不一致、专家路由异常）或做 MoE 压缩研究，这篇提供了一个少见的"为故障复现而剪枝"的视角，Table 1 的故障分类学和 merging 基线直接训崩的结论都有实践参考价值。但若你想要的是 MoE 推理压缩或严谨的 benchmark 结果，48 专家代理在 GSM8K 上掉到 48.0%、且缺关键超参与消融，参考价值有限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a dense model of comparable FLOPs, or simply a smaller off-the-shelf MoE from the same family, reproduce these two faults equally well? Without that control, the case for structure-preserving expert pruning rests on the merging baseline alone.
- Can the proxy reproduce an actual **routing-consistency** fault (e.g. a Top-k dispatch mismatch or an expert-mapping bug) — the fault class the method was designed for?
- How do α_r, α_c, α_f trade off? Is there a regime where co-activation dominates for fault sensitivity while routed-context dominates for task accuracy, implying you should build *different* proxies for diagnosis vs. capability checks?
- Is there a minimum expert budget below which fault sensitivity — not just accuracy — breaks down? The paper shows accuracy degrading with K but never tests whether fault reproduction itself fails at some K.
- Could a quantitative fidelity score (e.g. DTW distance or rank correlation between original and proxy metric trajectories) replace visual trend inspection, and could it be validated against a held-out set of faults?
- Does the proxy remain faithful for faults that emerge only late in training (reward hacking, over-optimization), given that the 48-expert proxy plateaus at a much lower reward level and may never enter that regime?
- Are the selected experts stable across calibration sets and across RL training steps, or does the optimal expert subset drift as the policy updates — which would undermine using one fixed proxy across a long run?
