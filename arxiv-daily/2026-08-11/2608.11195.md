> **一句话总结：** 作者用「推理模型(GPT-5.5-Pro/5.6-Sol) + 编码智能体(Claude Code, Opus/Fable 5) + 文件式记忆」的双智能体研究系统，在 240 个会话、2091 次调用、约 1.52 亿 token、约 5400 美元的成本下，把 Grothendieck 常数 $K_G$ 的下界从沿用四十余年的 Davie–Reeds 值 1.6769566742 抬升到 $6\pi/11 \approx 1.7135$（首个不构造困难实例的下界证明），并给出上界 $\pi/(2\log(1+\sqrt2)) - 3.47\times10^{-4} \approx 1.78187$（此前最好改进量级仅 $10^{-5}$），从而首次确定了 $K_G$ 的十分位为 7；同时用完整运行记录论证了当前 AI 的能力不对称——技术执行强，研究判断与研究状态维护弱。

> **TL;DR:** A two-agent research harness (GPT-5.5-Pro/5.6-Sol as reasoning brain + Claude Code with Opus/Fable 5 as executor, file-based memory, ~240 sessions, 2,091 model calls, ~152M tokens, ~$5,400) improved the Grothendieck constant lower bound from the 40-year-old Davie–Reeds baseline 1.6769566742 to $6\pi/11 = 1.7135\ldots$ (a $+0.0366$ jump, and the first lower bound not built from a hard instance), with a companion upper bound $\pi/(2\log(1+\sqrt2)) - 3.47\times10^{-4} = 1.78187$ versus prior best improvements of only $\sim10^{-5}$ over Krivine's 1.7822 — pinning the tenths digit of $K_G$ at 7. The paper's own analysis of the run shows the system was strong at technical execution but needed ~40 human directives for research judgement, and one machine record (upper bound 1.7802243 at session 44) was later withdrawn.

## 核心贡献

- **对一个 1953 年以来的开放问题给出新方法**：上界侧引入「极限 Krivine 方案」(limiting Krivine schemes)，把舍入算法的搜索空间从固定低维方案扩展到维数趋于无穷的极限；下界侧首次把「所有舍入方案都存在的障碍」转化为 $K_G$ 的下界，不再依赖构造困难矩阵实例。
- **长时程 AI 研究的系统方法论**：一套「推理智能体 + 编码智能体」的 harness，会话之间不保留任何模型上下文，靠文件（problem statement、单一 summary 工作记忆、append-only 转录与实验日志）承接连续性；每个会话结尾做校准审计，把每条断言标注为 proven / numerically supported / conjectural / heuristic。
- **完整运行记录与能力剖面分析**：把长时程数学研究拆成「研究状态 → 研究判断 → 高层动作 → 技术执行 → 状态再表示」的循环（对应 Newell–Simon、Pólya、Schoenfeld、Schön 的经典框架），并用真实失败案例定位 AI 在其中三个环节的强弱。
- **对能力不对称给出可检验的成因假设**：训练语料记录的是「数学作为产物」而非「数学作为过程」，失败路径、放弃决策、证据强度都被论文体裁抹掉，因此研究判断与状态维护缺训练信号；这也预测了状态被反复重写约 200 次后「结论数值比其证据等级更容易存活」的退化方向。

## Key Contributions

- New mathematics on a 73-year-old open constant: limiting Krivine schemes (upper bound) and an obstruction-to-lower-bound duality via Naor–Regev optimality (lower bound), with proofs in a companion paper [SLX+26].
- A documented long-horizon research harness: bounded sessions with zero context carryover, file-based working memory, a calibrated four-level claim-tagging verification protocol, and an asynchronous text-file channel for human steering.
- A quantified run record (Tables 1–2) that is unusually transparent about cost, failure, and withdrawal — including a machine-recorded upper bound that was later retracted by internal audit.
- A systems-level decomposition of mathematical research (research state / research judgement / technical execution / state representation) used as the analytic lens for where the AI succeeded and failed.
- An explicit hypothesis, with citations to the broken-telephone / transmission-chain literature [Bar32, AS23, MGVS25], for *why* iterated state rewriting loses caveats faster than it loses headline numbers.

## Methodology

The target is $K_G$, the worst-case integrality gap between the NP-hard bilinear $\pm1$ optimization $\mathrm{OPT}(A)$ and its SDP relaxation $\mathrm{SDP}(A)$. Every upper bound is a rounding scheme; the relevant family is Krivine schemes, i.e. pairs of odd sign partitions $f, g : \mathbb{R}^k \to \{\pm1\}$ applied to correlated Gaussian points, whose entire performance is encoded by the normalized correlation function $H(t) = \frac{\pi}{2}\mathbb{E}[f(X)g(Y)]$. Hyperplane rounding gives $H(t) = \arcsin t$ and Krivine's $1.7822\ldots$; Naor–Regev showed mixed Krivine schemes are asymptotically optimal, so the whole problem can in principle be fought inside this one family.

The AI system is a harness coupling two agents in distinct roles. The **reasoning agent** (GPT-5.5-Pro at maximal effort, swapped mid-run for GPT-5.6-Sol) selects directions, develops arguments, specifies experiments, and audits claims. The **coding agent** (Claude Code on Opus, later Fable 5) maintains the repository, implements and runs experiments, retrieves literature, and drives the reasoning model through a scripted interface. Sessions last a few hours, 2–5 run concurrently on mutually distinct directions, and no agent context survives a session boundary — continuity lives entirely in files. Floating-point search ran on a single four-GPU node; every Arb-certified computation was redone in CPU interval arithmetic. The run was *not* cold-started: it was seeded with the authors' cubic–quintic scheme, coefficient formulas, certification machinery, months of notes, and a problem statement that already suggested the obstruction-dualization idea.

The lower-bound discovery path is the paper's centerpiece. Sessions 12–17 opened six mechanically distinct escape routes for pushing the inverse-majorant parameter $\gamma$ past $\approx0.882$; all six failed against the same obstruction (cubic coefficient mass cannot be cancelled without shrinking the linear coefficient), and the system's response was to start a seventh variant. Human intervention at session 18 redirected it to prove the obstruction universally. Sessions 32–51 then stalled again by repeatedly proving sub-lemmas in restricted settings and recording them as global progress. A second intervention forced several theory-only sessions; session 56 produced the decisive reframe — constrain the Hermite coefficients $(b_1, b_3)$, which are *linear* functionals of the scheme, so an affine constraint automatically survives mixtures and coefficientwise limits, yielding the family $b_3 \ge (1+\lambda)(b_1 - \lambda + \tfrac56)$ with barrier $\Gamma_\lambda = (\lambda + \tfrac56)/(1+\lambda)$. A third intervention overrode the system's preference for the strongest rung ($\lambda = \tfrac12$, worth $9\pi/16 \approx 1.767$, blocked by an open chaos inequality) in favor of certifying $\lambda = 1$ completely, giving $\Gamma_1 = 11/12$ and $K_G \ge 6\pi/11$.

## Results & Highlights

- **Lower bound: $K_G \ge 6\pi/11 = 1.7135\ldots$**, versus the Davie–Reeds baseline of $1.6769566742$ that stood for over four decades — an absolute gain of $\approx 0.0366$. The two most recent competing improvements over Davie–Reeds [Hei26a, JM26] moved the bound by only $10^{-26}$ and $10^{-12}$ respectively, so this is roughly 24 orders of magnitude larger a step than the concurrent state of the art. This is the only run output the authors state as a theorem (independently human-verified).
- **Upper bound: $\pi/(2\log(1+\sqrt2)) - 3.47\times10^{-4} = 1.7818666070$**, against Krivine's classical $1.7822\ldots$; the two prior explicit improvements (Heilman [Hei26b] and the authors' own [LSX+26]) were on the order of $10^{-5}$, i.e. this is a ~35x larger improvement and the first obtained by letting scheme dimension grow, answering a question of Braverman et al. [BMMN11] affirmatively. Note this upper bound came from conversation with GPT-5.5-Pro and *predates* the harness.
- **Combined interval narrows from $[1.6769\ldots, 1.7822\ldots]$ to $[1.7135\ldots, 1.78187\ldots]$**, determining the tenths digit of $K_G$ to be 7 for the first time since 1953.
- **Machine-verified (not human-verified) further results**: lower bounds $27\pi/49 \approx 1.7311$ (session 92) and $51\pi/92 \approx 1.7415$ (session 117); upper bounds $1.781801841033$ and then $1.7813319810625639$, both improving on the cubic–quintic value and the latter passing adversarial review.
- **Run economics**: June 16 – July 24, 2026; $\approx 240$ research sessions; 2,091 reasoning-model calls; $\approx 152$M tokens; $\approx \$5{,}400$ list-price API cost (coding agent on a flat-rate Claude Max subscription, only partially metered); $\approx 40$ dated human directives. The decisive reframe (session 56) to first complete write-up took about one day.
- **Ceiling-parameter trajectory**: hyperplane $\gamma = \log(1+\sqrt2) \approx 0.8814$ → stalled at $\approx 0.882$ → two-dimensional certified ceiling $\Gamma \approx 0.8866$, numerically sharpened to $\approx 0.8846$; the run separately computed that *any* universal ceiling $\Gamma \le 0.93669$ already beats Davie–Reeds, which is why a loose general bound sufficed.
- **A negative result reported honestly**: the session-44 numerical upper bound $1.7802243$ held the internal record for 25 days before being withdrawn — a session-4 evaluator's "exploration only" caveat did not survive state rewrites, and a feasibility criterion proved in session 8 was lost in a handoff and had to be reproved by the audit that retracted the record.

## Authors & Credibility

Strong and well-matched author list. Raghu Meka (UCLA), Pravesh K. Kothari (Princeton), and Adam Klivans (UT Austin) are established theorists in approximation algorithms, SoS/SDP hardness, and learning theory; Swarat Chaudhuri (UT Austin) works at the neurosymbolic/AI-for-reasoning boundary and is also a co-author on AlphaEvolve [NVE+25], which the paper positions against. The mathematical claims are deferred to a companion paper [SLX+26] and, for the theorem-level result, independently re-verified by the human authors — an appropriate division. The paper is careful to label which outputs are theorems versus machine-verified claims, which is the right epistemic hygiene for this genre.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Genuine on two axes. Mathematically, the obstruction-to-lower-bound duality is the first $K_G$ lower bound not proceeding through a gap instance, and the affine-in-$(b_1,b_3)$ transportability trick is a clean idea. Methodologically, this is plausibly the first detailed, quantified case study of a *program-level* (not task-level) human–AI math collaboration, versus AlphaEvolve/FunSearch (fixed objective + auto-scorer) or AlphaProof (fixed formal statement).

**Rigor and honest scoping.** The paper is unusually disciplined about attribution, and this cuts against its own headline framing. Theorem 3.1 (the upper bound) was *not* produced by the system — it came from plain conversation with GPT-5.5-Pro and predates the harness. Of the harness's own outputs, exactly one ($6\pi/11$) is human-verified; the four numerically stronger results are machine-verified only, and the run's own track record on machine-verified claims includes a 25-day-lived withdrawn record. So the strict claim supported by the evidence is narrower than "AI improved both bounds": it is "AI produced one human-verified lower bound after three decisive human redirections."

**The counterfactual is untested.** The run was seeded with the authors' framework, certification machinery, months of notes, *and* a problem statement that already suggested the dualization; the paper itself notes session 18's intervention "supplied no new mathematics." There is no ablation — no unseeded run, no run with fewer directives, no run with a single agent or no file memory — so the harness's contribution over "GPT-5.5-Pro plus a diligent postdoc following an existing research plan" is not isolated. Given that a companion-paper-quality upper bound came out of ordinary chat, this is the missing comparison that matters most.

**Weak/absent baselines.** There is no comparison run against AlphaEvolve-style evaluator search on the same $\gamma$-optimization (which looks like a natural fit, since $\gamma$ *is* an automatic score), nor against a plain long-context frontier model with the same seed files. The capability-asymmetry conclusion (execution strong, judgement weak) is drawn from a single run on a single problem with a single harness configuration that was, by the authors' own account, "adjusted repeatedly over the run" — so the run is not a controlled artifact and the $n{=}1$ generalization to "current AI systems" is an overreach.

**Data quirks worth flagging.** The $\approx\$5{,}400$ figure understates true cost, since coding-agent usage was on a flat-rate subscription and "only partially metered," and GPU search time is not priced. The "$\approx 40$ directives" count treats interventions as fungible, but the case study makes clear that ~3 of them were load-bearing and the rest were priorities/audits; a directive count is therefore a poor proxy for human effort or contribution.

**Reproducibility.** Effectively zero at the artifact level: no repository, no harness code, no transcripts, no prompt templates, and two of the four models named (GPT-5.6-Sol, Claude Fable 5) are moving targets. The mathematics is reproducible via the companion paper; the *AI-systems* claims are not.

**Red flags.** The title foregrounds "Long-Horizon AI Research" while the most decisive contributions in the narrative are human. That is arguably the paper's honest thesis rather than overclaiming — but the abstract's "improvements were achieved using an AI research system" reads more strongly than Section 4's careful attribution.

## Strengths

- Reports failures at the same resolution as successes: the withdrawn session-44 bound, the six failed escape routes, the fourteen-session restricted-lemma loop. This kind of negative record is exactly what the field lacks and what the authors argue models need for training.
- Clear separation of epistemic status (theorem / machine-verified / numerically supported / conjectural / heuristic) applied consistently, including to the run's own headline numbers.
- The failure diagnosis is mechanistically specific, not vibes-based: the archive retained the original facts, and it was the *compressed state governing decisions* that lost the caveat — a directly actionable design lesson for agent memory systems.
- The mathematical payoff is real and independently checkable regardless of one's view of the AI claims.

## Limitations

- Single problem, single run, no ablations; the capability profile may be specific to analysis-flavored math with cheap numerical feedback.
- The harness evolved during the run, so the reported configuration is a post-hoc description rather than a fixed experimental condition.
- No artifact release; model versions are proprietary and already superseded.
- The strongest numerical results (two upper bounds, two lower bounds) remain unverified by humans, and the run's own history shows machine verification has failed before.
- The proposed remedy — train on "mathematics as process" — is stated as a direction, with no dataset, benchmark, or experiment attached.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM agent 长时程规划、agent memory / context 压缩失效、以及 AI-for-science 评测的读者极有价值：它给出了一个罕见的、把 agent 失败模式量化到会话级别的真实案例，尤其是「压缩后的工作记忆会系统性丢弃 caveat 而保留数值结论」这一退化机制，直接对应当前 agent harness 设计中的 summary/handoff 环节。同时它也是评估「AI 是否真的独立做出数学发现」时一个校准良好的参照样本——归因粒度比多数同类宣称都要细。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What does an unseeded run look like? If the problem statement had *not* suggested obstruction dualization, would the system ever have pivoted — and can that be tested cheaply on a held-out open problem?
- Can the state-representation failure be fixed architecturally rather than by training? E.g. append-only claim ledgers with immutable provenance and evidence tags, retrieval over the archive instead of rewriting a summary, or a separate auditor agent whose only job is caveat propagation.
- Is "knowing when to activate a strategy" (the Mason–Spence framing the authors invoke) learnable from synthetic process traces, or does it require reward signals over multi-week horizons that no current RL setup provides?
- Would an evaluator-guided search (AlphaEvolve-style) over the limiting-scheme space have beaten the LLM harness on the *upper* bound, where $\gamma$ is an automatic score? The paper positions against these systems but never runs one.
- How much of the ~$0.0366$ lower-bound gain is attributable to the affine reframe versus to the pre-existing Naor–Regev transfer, and would a domain expert given the same six recorded failures have found the reframe faster than 38 sessions?
- Do the machine-verified rungs $27\pi/49$ and $51\pi/92$ survive human verification, and if any fails, what does that imply about the calibration of the four-level tagging protocol?
