A two-line output-layer natural gradient—not new tricks—stabilizes heteroscedastic regression by fixing the geometry, not the loss.

一句话总结：把高斯 NLL 异方差回归的训练不稳定归因于“用欧氏几何走 KL 流形”；只在 2 维输出层 (μ, s=lnσ²) 上乘以精确的 2×2 逆 Fisher 矩阵（∇μ→eᵠ∇μ、∇s→2∇s）并做批量 L2 归一化，零额外超参。

Frozen-feature downstream digit accuracy where the primary task only regresses rotation angle — vs 35–52% for every baseline. Faithful collapses to 37.4%.

Why this matters

Collapses a zoo of ad-hoc heteroscedastic stabilizers into one geometric correction — and shows gradient-severing cripples representation quality (Faithful falls to 37%).

Critical edge

Gains hold only under plain SGD at a deliberately short 100-step budget — under Adam they vanish, and no ablation separates the Fisher correction from its unit-norm batch step.

Methodology — the whole fix lives at the output head

A standard Nix–Weigend network trains mean µ and log-variance s = ln σ² under Gaussian NLL. Vanilla SGD implicitly constrains each update inside a Euclidean ball in (µ, s)-space — but because s is a log-variance, equal Euclidean steps induce wildly unequal KL shifts in the output Gaussian.

Fisher8 swaps that Euclidean constraint for a second-order KL one. For the (µ, s) parameterization the Fisher matrix is diagonal and exact, F = diag(e⁻ᵠ, ½), so the natural-gradient correction collapses to two scalar multiplies. Per-point corrected gradients are stacked across the batch, each channel unit-L2 normalized, then back-propagated — no gradient severing, no KFAC-style weight-space approximation, and no data-dependent hyperparameter beyond the learning rate.

That yields a batched KL trust-radius readout, ½e⁻min(s)η² + ¼η², an interpretable knob where β-NLL's β and Faithful's architectural severing previously sat. The trunk still receives ordinary backprop of the corrected signal.

The Fisher8 update rule
# output head θ = (µ, s), s = ln σ²
F⁻¹ = diag(eᵠ, 2)
 
∇μ → eᵠ · ∇μ
∇s → 2 · ∇s
 
# stack across batch, unit-L2
# normalize each channel, backprop

Strengths & limitations

+ Strengths

  • The unifying argument is checkable, not hand-waved: Table 1 shows β-NLL(β=1) and Faithful both recover the eᵠ∇μ mean step.
  • Zero data-dependent hyperparameters beyond learning rate — a real simplification vs β-NLL's β and Wong-Toi's corridor search.
  • The learning-rate sweep is a falsification test: the authors predicted high-lr degradation from their theory, then reported it.
  • The local KL-variance diagnostic distinguishes churning (features move, output stalls) from ordinary underfitting — useful independent of Fisher8.
  • SGD scoping is deliberate; Adam's preconditioning confound is named and tested in the appendix, not swept away.
  • Publishing the λ=0 run (needs essentially no weight regularization) is a falsifiable claim they didn't have to make.

– Limitations

  • Restricted to univariate Gaussian likelihoods with a 2-parameter head; multivariate, other families, dense prediction are all future work.
  • Inherits second-order learning-rate brittleness; trades a 2–3-axis grid for a finer 1-axis search — real, but not “no tuning.”
  • Gains shown under vanilla SGD at short budgets; the Adam appendix shows the advantage largely disappears in the optimizer most practitioners use.
  • No released code, and no ablation separating Fisher preconditioning from the unit-norm batch step — the most conspicuous missing experiment.
  • Coverage on the cosmology task stays below nominal (0.610 vs 0.68) for all methods — uncertainty is still under-dispersed in absolute terms.
  • Only output-layer curvature is modeled; trunk-curvature interaction vs KFAC is a single MNIST reference row.

Questions for further exploration

  1. What survives with Fisher preconditioning but without the unit-L2 batch normalization, and vice versa? The single most informative missing experiment.
  2. Does the advantage hold at convergence, not just at 100 steps? A matched-wall-clock comparison separates “faster” from “better.”
  3. Can the analytic KL bound ½e⁻min(s)η² + ¼η² become an adaptive learning rate, curing the lr=0.1 brittleness?
  4. How does Fisher8 compose with Adam if the second-moment estimate is applied to the already-corrected vs the raw gradient?
  5. Does the construction extend cleanly to Student-t or heteroscedastic Laplace, where the Fisher matrix is analytic but no longer diagonal?
  6. Why do β-NLL and Faithful underperform even the plain baseline on weak lensing? A synthetic σ²_sys injection would test the hypothesis.
