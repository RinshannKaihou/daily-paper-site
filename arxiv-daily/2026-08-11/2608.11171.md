> **一句话总结：** 作者用「人工标注 + Claude Sonnet 5 + Nova Lite 2.0」三方多标签标注了 TrustNLP 六届共 144 篇存档论文，按 TrustLLM/DecodingTrust 改编的六个可信维度统计出：真实性（Truthfulness）从 2021–2022 的 0 篇涨到 2025–2026 的 13–14 篇/年（累计 38 篇，占语料 26%），可解释性走出「先降后升」的 U 型（2025 年仅 2 篇 → 2026 年 13 篇，由稀疏自编码器/线性探针/激活引导驱动），并与 ACL/NAACL/EACL/EMNLP 共 2,169 篇「可信」论文对比证明该工作坊的主题分布贴合领域均值——结论是可信性研究是被能力事件牵着走的滞后反应，而非内生理论积累。

> **TL;DR:** A descriptive meta-analysis of all 144 TrustNLP archival papers (2021–2026), labeled along six TrustLLM/DecodingTrust-derived trust dimensions by one human annotator plus two LLM annotators (Claude Sonnet 5 at 92.2% decision-level accuracy / κ=0.79 overall; Amazon Nova Lite 2.0 at 90.5% / κ=0.72 — Nova Lite is the weaker "baseline" annotator, trailing Sonnet by ~1.7 points of accuracy and by κ=0.11 on Fairness & Bias). Truthfulness goes from 0 papers in 2021–2022 to 38 total (26% of the corpus); Explainability collapses from 9 papers in 2021–2022 to 2 in 2025 and rebounds to 13 in 2026; a cross-venue check against 2,169 keyword-filtered ACL/NAACL/EACL/EMNLP papers shows TrustNLP tracks the field average. No new models or benchmarks are proposed.

## 核心贡献

- **六维可信分类体系的语料化改造**：以 TrustLLM 的六维为起点，用 DecodingTrust 的细粒度边界收敛范围——把 DecodingTrust 的 Toxicity 并入 Fairness & Bias，把 TrustLLM 的 Safety 与 Machine Ethics 合并为 Machine Ethics & Safety，并额外补入语料驱动的 Explainability（因为 TrustLLM 只把 Transparency 当原则而未操作化，但 2021–2022 的 TrustNLP 语料被可解释性主导）。
- **144 篇全量多标签标注**：三方独立标注（1 位作者 + Claude Sonnet 5 + Nova Lite 2.0，通过 Amazon Bedrock、限 256 输出 token、结构化 JSON），首轮 87/144（60%）三方完全一致，112/144（78%）至少一个 LLM 与人类完全一致；剩余 40% 由人类裁决。
- **纵向趋势的量化结论**：Truthfulness 增长最快（38 篇），Fairness & Bias 最稳定（每届 2–7 篇，共 30 篇），Explainability 呈 U 型（共 29 篇），Robustness 在 2023 年后稳定在 5–8 篇/年（27 篇），Privacy 呈现「2023 年 6 篇孤峰、其余年份 0–1 篇」的散点分布（9 篇）。
- **跨会场对照**：用 30 个可信关键词过滤 ACL Anthology 主会论文得到 2,169 篇候选，全部由 Sonnet 5 用同一 prompt 分类；为保证一致性，TrustNLP 论文在此对比中也改用 Sonnet 标签而非人工裁决标签。
- **四条结构性洞见 + 局限自述**：主题滞后于能力事件；可信证据形态随交互范式（直接权重访问 → API → agentic）而变；输出层审计系统性低估内部行为；缺少统一理论框架导致每次能力涌现都长出一条互不相连的研究支线。

## Key Contributions

- A corpus-motivated six-dimension trust taxonomy (Fairness & Bias, Robustness & Adversarial, Privacy, Machine Ethics & Safety, Truthfulness, Explainability) that merges TrustLLM and DecodingTrust categories rather than adopting either wholesale.
- A full-corpus, multi-label annotation of every TrustNLP archival paper across six editions, with inter-annotator agreement reported per dimension — an unusually transparent methodology section for a workshop retrospective.
- A chronological technical synthesis organized into five phases: Interpretability & Bias (2021–2022), the Generative Pivot (2023), Trust as a Trade-off Problem (2024), Agentic & Multimodal Frontiers (2025), and Mechanistic Trust & Safety at Scale (2026).
- Four structural insights, of which the most actionable is "Outputs ≠ Internals": output-level audits repeatedly missed failure modes that only internal probes surfaced (representational bias missed by output-distribution fairness audits; contamination-driven benchmark inflation; latent attack surfaces found only by automated red-teaming; function-calling brittleness under toolkit expansion).
- A cross-venue sanity check establishing that the workshop is not topically idiosyncratic relative to the four main *CL conferences.

## Methodology

The study is descriptive and bibliometric, not experimental. The unit of analysis is a paper title+abstract, not the full text. Labels come from three sources applied to the same written annotation instructions (reproduced in Appendix A, prompts in Appendix B): one author annotating manually, plus Claude Sonnet 5 (`us.anthropic.claude-sonnet-5`) and Amazon Nova Lite 2.0 (`us.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0`) served through Amazon Bedrock, each capped at 256 output tokens and asked for structured JSON. Labels are multi-label; the instructions explicitly restrict assignment to the PRIMARY focus and note that most papers carry 1–2 dimensions.

Adjudication is human-final: papers with unanimous three-way agreement pass through untouched; the remaining ~40% are resolved by the same human annotator, who may adopt an LLM's label when its rationale is judged better. This is a single-annotator ground truth with LLM assistance, not a multi-human consensus — worth naming plainly, because the reported κ values measure human-vs-LLM agreement, not human-vs-human reliability, so there is no estimate of the human annotator's own noise floor.

For the cross-venue arm, the pipeline is: (1) scrape all main-track papers from ACL Anthology volumes for ACL/NAACL/EACL/EMNLP 2021–2026; (2) retain any paper whose *title* contains one of 30 curated trust keywords (bias, fairness, robustness, adversarial, jailbreak, privacy, safety, alignment, hallucination, explainability, interpretability, toxicity, backdoor, unlearning, …); (3) classify each survivor's title+abstract with Sonnet 5 using the identical taxonomy prompt. This yields 2,169 classified papers (ACL n=965, EMNLP n=771, NAACL n=264, EACL n=169). The title-only keyword gate is the weakest link in the design: any trust paper whose title avoids those 30 terms is invisible, which likely under-counts mechanistic-interpretability and calibration work that tends to be titled after its method rather than its trust dimension.

## Results & Highlights

- **Truthfulness growth.** 0 papers in both 2021 and 2022 → 6 (2023) → 5 (2024) → 14 (2025) → 13 (2026), totaling 38 papers, which §3.3 states is 26% of the 144-paper corpus. The abstract instead claims truthfulness comprises "37% of papers by 2025–2026"; recomputing from Table 2, 27 truthfulness-labeled papers out of 79 papers published in 2025–2026 is 34.2%, and 27 out of the 85 dimension-labels assigned in those two years is 31.8% — neither reproduces 37%. This is an internal inconsistency between the abstract and the table.
- **Explainability U-curve.** 5 (2021) + 4 (2022) = 9 papers at the early peak, falling to 2 in 2025, then rebounding to 13 in 2026 (29 total) — a 6.5× year-over-year jump attributed to sparse autoencoders, linear probes, and activation steering. Note §4.5 says "11 papers" for the 2026 explainability resurgence while Table 2 says 13; the paper does not reconcile the two.
- **Annotator agreement.** Overall decision-level accuracy: Claude Sonnet 5 92.2% vs. Amazon Nova Lite 2.0 90.5% (delta +1.7 pts to Sonnet); overall Cohen's κ 0.79 vs 0.72 (delta +0.07). Per-dimension κ for Sonnet ranges from 0.532 (Machine Ethics & Safety) to 0.892 (Fairness & Bias); Nova Lite ranges 0.506 to 0.882. The largest per-dimension gap is Fairness & Bias (0.892 vs 0.782, delta 0.11); Nova Lite actually edges Sonnet on Truthfulness (0.673 vs 0.645) and Privacy (0.882 vs 0.881).
- **Raw agreement.** 87/144 papers (60%) had perfect three-way agreement; 112/144 (78%) had at least one LLM fully matching the human.
- **Corpus growth.** 8 archival papers (2021) → 8 (2022) → 28 (2023) → 21 (2024) → 38 (2025) → 41 (2026), i.e. a 5.1× increase, with the largest single-year jump (8 → 28, +250%) landing in the first edition after the November 2022 chat-model release.
- **Dimension totals across six editions.** Truthfulness 38, Fairness & Bias 30, Explainability 29, Robustness & Adversarial 27, Machine Ethics & Safety 20, Privacy 9. Privacy's distribution is 1/0/6/0/1/1 by year — 6 of its 9 papers land in 2023 alone.
- **Cross-venue comparison.** 2,169 trust-keyword papers across four venues vs. TrustNLP n=136 in Figure 2 (not 144 — 8 papers are silently absent from the comparison figure, presumably unlabeled or dropped, and the paper does not explain the gap). The reported finding is qualitative: TrustNLP "closely traces" the four-venue average, with no distance metric, KL divergence, or correlation coefficient given.
- **Substantive findings quoted from the surveyed corpus.** Safety compliance rates vary from 15% (human trafficking) to 86% (surveillance design) across seven ethical domains (Bugaud, 2026a); Bakman et al. (2026) argue static black-box evaluation cannot guarantee post-update alignment, with models passing all safety tests yet becoming severely misaligned after a single benign gradient update.

## Authors & Credibility

The author list is the TrustNLP organizing committee itself: Rahul Gupta, Jwala Dhamala, Tharindu Kumarage, Yang Trista Cao and Aram Galstyan (Amazon AGI), Anaelia Ovalle, Anil Ramakrishna and Ninareh Mehrabi (Meta), Anubrata Das (Autodesk), Apurv Verma (NJIT), Yada Pruksachatkun (Salesforce), and Kai-Wei Chang (UCLA). These are the people who chaired the six editions being analyzed, and several are also cited as proceedings editors within the paper's own reference list. That is both the paper's credibility (nobody knows this corpus better; they can speak to CfP intent and non-archival volume) and its central conflict of interest: the organizers are assessing their own venue's representativeness and concluding it is representative. Kai-Wei Chang's group and the Amazon Responsible-AI team have a long publication record in fairness and trustworthy NLP, so the domain expertise is genuine.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** Longitudinal venue retrospectives are a known genre; the specific contribution is the trust-dimension time series and the "trust topics trail capability events" framing. The taxonomy is derivative by design — merged from TrustLLM and DecodingTrust with one corpus-motivated addition.

**Rigor (mixed, honestly reported).** The annotation protocol is above average for this genre: written instructions, three annotators, per-dimension κ, and an appendix dumping all 144 label triples (Table 3). But the weakest dimension, Machine Ethics & Safety, sits at κ=0.532/0.506 — barely moderate — and the paper's own explanation (conceptual overlap with Robustness and Truthfulness) means the Machine Ethics & Safety time series, which underpins the "safety peaks in 2026" claim, is the least trustworthy series in Table 2. There is no human-vs-human reliability estimate, no bootstrap or confidence interval on any trend, and no significance test that the truthfulness rise or the explainability U-shape exceeds what a 144-paper sample would produce by chance. With 8–41 papers per year, single-paper noise moves percentages by several points.

**Paper-specific red flags.** (1) The abstract's "37% of papers by 2025–2026" does not reconcile with Table 2 under either denominator (34.2% by papers, 31.8% by labels). (2) §4.5 says explainability rose to 11 papers in 2026 while Table 2 says 13; §4.5 says Machine Ethics & Safety "reaches its highest count (8 papers)" while Table 2 says 9. (3) Figure 2 reports TrustNLP n=136 against a corpus stated everywhere else as 144, unexplained. (4) The cross-venue arm swaps the human-adjudicated labels for raw Sonnet labels "for consistency," meaning the headline representativeness claim rests entirely on a single LLM's unaudited output. (5) §5 promises to "identify underexplored directions" as a co-equal half of the section, but delivers only one short paragraph on benchmark saturation — the gap analysis is thinner than the section title advertises. (6) Missing ablation: no check of whether labeling from title+abstract versus full text changes the distribution, which matters most for the Truthfulness/Machine Ethics boundary the authors themselves flag as contested.

**Reproducibility (partial).** Model IDs, decoding constraints, prompt appendices, keyword list, and the complete 144-row label table are provided, which is more than most surveys give. What is missing: the exact paper-ID list and per-paper labels for the 2,169 cross-venue papers, any code or scraping script, and the full 30-keyword list is only described as "including" 14 examples. The keyword list itself is not fully enumerated, so the cross-venue arm is not exactly reproducible.

**Significance (community-facing, not technical).** Insight 3 ("Outputs ≠ Internals") is the one claim with real prescriptive force, and it is supported by both in-corpus evidence and outside work (Cao et al. 2022 on intrinsic/extrinsic fairness metric misalignment; the KEEN probe predicting factuality from internals without generating text; parametric-knowledge-trace probing showing unlearning suppresses rather than removes knowledge). For anyone designing a trust evaluation suite, "add internal probes as first-class evidence" is directly usable. The rest is orientation and agenda-setting rather than measurable advance.

## Strengths

- Fully transparent annotation artifact: Appendix D publishes all 144 papers with the human label and both LLM labels side by side, so a reader can independently audit any trend claim.
- The paper does not hide its weakest agreement numbers — κ=0.532 for Machine Ethics & Safety is printed in the main table and discussed rather than buried.
- The phase-by-phase technical synthesis (§4) is genuinely informative as a reading list, tracing concrete threads (attention-based attribution → fairness–explainability trade-offs → activation steering; input perturbation attacks → model-co-constructed attacks → cross-modal PBI-Attack).
- The Limitations section is unusually candid: it states outright that the paper makes no experimental contribution, that it is anglocentric, that the reactive-delay pattern is a retrospective observation over only four capability events, and that readers wanting benchmark numbers should go read the primary papers.
- Using two LLM annotators from different vendors, rather than one, gives a crude but real check against single-model idiosyncrasy.

## Limitations

- Single venue, archival papers only; non-archival posters and tutorials (which Figure 1 shows are substantial — e.g. +23 additional non-archival papers in 2024, nearly doubling that edition) are excluded, and the trends could differ there.
- Title+abstract-only classification cannot see a paper's actual methodological contribution, which is precisely where the interesting "post-hoc vs. mechanistic" distinction lives.
- Title-keyword prefiltering for the cross-venue set biases toward papers that self-identify with trust vocabulary; a mechanistic-interpretability paper titled after its technique would be missed, which could artificially flatten the very cross-venue difference the paper reports as absent.
- Counts are small (8–41 papers/edition) and no uncertainty is quantified on any trend.
- The causal language of Insight 1 ("trust topics trail capability events") is supported only by temporal adjacency across four events with no counterfactual, and the authors acknowledge they cannot say whether the regularity generalizes.
- Findings may not transfer to FAccT, AIES, ICLR, or NeurIPS, where much trustworthy-AI work is published.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 可信性、机制可解释性与评测方法论的读者，这篇提供了一份带完整标注数据的六年主题地图，尤其是「输出层审计系统性低估内部行为、必须把内部探针当一等证据」这条洞见可直接用于设计评测套件。另外其标注流程本身（人工 + Claude Sonnet 5 + Nova Lite 2.0 三方多标签、κ 分维报告、人类裁决 40% 分歧样本）是一个可复用的 LLM-as-a-judge 标注模板，与你此前在批量文本标注/GT 质量审计上的工作直接呼应。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would full-text (rather than title+abstract) labeling change the Truthfulness/Machine Ethics boundary enough to alter the 2026 "safety peaks" claim, given κ is only ~0.52 there?
- What does the trend look like with confidence intervals — is the explainability U-shape distinguishable from Poisson noise on 2–13 counts?
- Can the "reactive delay" be quantified rather than asserted? Fitting a lag between model-release dates and dimension-share inflections, ideally on the 2,169-paper cross-venue set where monthly resolution exists, would turn Insight 1 into a testable claim.
- Does the title-keyword filter systematically miss mechanistic-interpretability papers, and how much does the "TrustNLP tracks the field average" conclusion move under an abstract-level filter or an embedding-based retrieval instead?
- The two LLM annotators disagree most on Fairness & Bias (κ 0.892 vs 0.782) — is that a prompt-sensitivity artifact, and would self-consistency sampling or a third arbiter model close it?
- What would a unified framework (Insight 4) actually look like formally — is there a tractable multi-objective formulation where fairness, calibration, robustness, and refusal behavior are constraints on a shared representation-level object, rather than separate benchmarks?
