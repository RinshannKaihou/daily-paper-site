> **一句话总结：** 作者在 1,867 个 GitHub 仓库、247,694 条指令生命周期上证明 CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md 类上下文文件单调膨胀（生命周期内 +226%、每次 commit 净增 4.9 条），根因是"删除理由"的遗忘（删除风险随指令年龄下降，对数风险斜率 −0.032/commit）而非指令过时；随后通过"反转 IFEval"构造已知最优提示的可验证世界，证明给每条指令加上记录其潜在推理的**提示注释（prompt comments）**能在 51 步后把冗余规模从 +211.3% 压到 +1.4%（消除 99.3% 冗余）且约束满足率持平（44.0% vs 44.0%），在 WildIFEval 上把指令遵循率从 50.4% 提到 62.0%（+11.6pp，相对 +23.1%）。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author empirical + causal study naming "catastrophic remembering" — the inverse of catastrophic forgetting — as why agentic context files grow without bound. Across 1,867 GitHub repos and 247,694 instruction lifetimes, prompts triple (+226%) and deletion hazard *falls* with instruction age (log-hazard −0.032/commit, 95% CI [−0.047, −0.019]), which falsifies staleness and content-fragility accounts. In an inverted-IFEval testbed with a known minimum cover, adding per-instruction comments that record latent reasoning cuts excess prompt size from **+211.3% (no-comment control)** and **+147.9% (comment-shaped-noise placebo)** to **+1.4%** at T=51 with constraint satisfaction at parity (44.0% vs 44.0%); on inverted **WildIFEval**, comments lift constraint satisfaction from **50.4% → 62.0%** (+11.6pp, +23.1% relative, 95% CI [5.1, 18.3]pp) against an identically-seeded uncommented maintainer.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"灾难性记忆"（catastrophic remembering）概念**：与持续学习中的灾难性遗忘互为对偶——梯度学习器覆盖了本该保留的东西，而提示维护者保留了本该覆盖的东西；两者失败原因相同：授权更新的信息已经丢失。
- **给出闭式理论**：添加指令成本 O(1)，而在 |D| 条指令的提示中安全删除一条需要 O(2^|D|) 次子集反事实探测（冗余性使一次一个的探测失效）。若写入时记录潜在推理 r_d，该成本坍缩为 O(1)，但 r_d 的可恢复率 ρ̄(a) 随年龄衰减，流量平衡给出 |D_∞| = A/(ρ̄·s) → ∞。
- **首次在单条指令粒度上做实证**：1,867 个仓库、1,801 个多版本文件、299,440 次版本转移、247,694 条指令 spell、28,426 次可归因删除，估计出删除风险函数 h(a) 并用它证伪竞争机制。
- **发现"棘轮"（ratchet）动力学**：77.3% 的指令死亡来自整文件重写或迁移；重写把文件砍到 59.5%，10 次 commit 内回到 91.5%，且重写后增速更快（4.9%/commit vs 重写前 4.1%/commit）。
- **提出可验证的新评测范式**：反转 IFEval——隐藏题目指令使其成为已知的最小覆盖 D*，保留验证器作为隐藏约束集，用更强模型生成一句话目标；于是"冗余规模"首次可测量。
- **给出可落地方案**：提示注释（#，对执行器不可见，只给下一任维护者），并做了消融证明必须携带"结果"而非仅叙述。

## Key Contributions

- **A named phenomenon and a closed-form model.** Prompt maintenance is framed as online estimation of an unobservable constraint set from censored, noisy feedback under five properties (instructability, interference, redundancy, censoring, stochasticity). Deletion hazard factorizes as h(a) ≈ ρ̄(a)·s(a), and flow balance yields |D_∞| = A/(ρ̄·s) → ∞ as recoverability decays — unbounded growth even with a *stationary* task and constant addition rate.
- **Instruction-level longitudinal measurement**, not file-level. Prior work (Chatlatanagulchai et al. 2025; Tafreshipour et al. 2025) reported file growth and addition-dominant edits but could not track individual instructions; this paper segments every historical version and matches across versions with a three-stage cascade (exact → normalized → rapidfuzz ≥70), validated at 1.000 precision / 0.933 recall on 50 hand-annotated transitions.
- **Mechanism discrimination via hazard shape.** Instruction staleness predicts hazard *rising* with age; content fragility and imperfect recall predict falling. A gamma-frailty refit absorbs at most 30.8% of the slope, and imperfect recall's unique conditional prediction — the age slope steepens where more humans have edited the file — holds (β_multi-author×age = −0.021, z = −11.7).
- **Inverse-IFEval / Inverse-WildIFEval testbeds** where the minimum cover is known a priori, making excess size measurable at all.
- **The comment protocol (COMMIT schema)**: round counter, quoted failure + recurrence count, falsification lineage carried forward from deleted instructions, and a verbatim restore quote. Comments are stripped by code before the executor sees the prompt, and instructions citing past failures are rejected, so the comment is the only channel for provenance.

## Methodology

**Corpus half.** The repository frame is inherited from Chatlatanagulchai et al. (2025) — public GitHub repos carrying `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, or `copilot-instructions.md` — but every byte of content is re-derived by blobless clone and forward walk of each tracked file's full commit history (walking forward avoids conditioning on survival, which is the quantity under study). An "instruction" is one clause-level imperative; headings, fenced code, tables, whole-line bold labels and fragments under 2 words go to "payload" and never enter the risk set. Two competing risks are censored: a *rewrite* (≥50% of live instructions die in one commit, floor of 5 at risk) and a *migration* (text reappearing in a sibling file at the same commit). Hazard is estimated with Nelson–Aalen on an age-in-commits clock (risk per review opportunity, not calendar days), kernel-smoothed at bandwidth 2, with a repository-stratified bootstrap (200 draws) rather than a spell-level one.

**Controlled half.** Each IFEval item (D_j, C_j) with ≥2 verifiers becomes a "world": D_j is hidden and becomes the reference minimum cover D*; C_j becomes the hidden constraint set run only by the harness; a stronger model (claude-sonnet-5) lossily summarizes D_j into a one-line objective, frozen with the world. A fresh maintainer instance reconstructs the prompt over T steps seeing only *censored* feedback — a complaint naming a dimension ("the response's word count is off") but never a parameter, threshold, or verifier identity. Maintainer and executor are both claude-haiku-4-5, deliberately chosen to be closer in capability to real README authors than a frontier model. Three arms: CONTROL (no comments), PLACEBO (comment-shaped noise drawn from a fixed pool of vague phrases), TREATMENT (the COMMIT schema). The author reports that arms separate only when five conditions hold jointly — vague verdicts, censored passes, withheld constraint identifiers, no brevity objective, and a horizon ≥10 steps — and that disclosing the verifier spec collapses the uncommented arm to +3.0% excess with the effect gone entirely.

**Real-prompt half.** WildIFEval items are inverted the same way, but the maintainer is *seeded* with |D_j|−K true instructions plus G noisy ones sampled from other items. Since WildIFEval's constraints are human prose with no code verifiers, satisfaction is scored by an arm-blind LLM judge (claude-haiku-4-5) that sees one constraint and one response and nothing else, then re-scored end-to-end under a second judge (gpt-5.6-luna) at temperature 0.

## Results & Highlights

- **Growth magnitude (observational).** Over a file's own normalized lifetime, mean instruction count rises **+226%** relative to its first version, against only **+10%** in mean instruction length and **+140%** in non-instruction payload — so growth is genuinely more rules, not text migrating between classes or existing rules getting wordier. Median final file holds **39 instructions** (90th pct **131**); **64.3%** of 1,576 multi-version repos grow vs **26.6%** that shrink (median net **+7**); excluding mass rewrites, each commit adds a net **+4.9 instructions** across 19,267 commits.
- **Deletion hazard falls with age.** Nelson–Aalen over **28,426 deletions**: log-hazard slope **−0.032/commit**, 95% CI **[−0.047, −0.019]** — opposite in sign to what instruction staleness predicts (flat is what age-independent deletion predicts). A gamma-frailty model sharing frailty by *instruction text* across repositories absorbs **30.8%** of the no-frailty slope but leaves it at **−0.0355 [−0.0414, −0.0296]**; repository- and file-level clustering absorb only **4.9%** and **4.3%**.
- **The ratchet survives rewrites.** Aligning on each file's first mass rewrite, count drops to **59.5%** of pre-rewrite level then recovers to **91.5%** within 10 commits; growth is **4.9%/commit after** vs **4.1%/commit before**. Across inter-rewrite segments, growth accelerates monotonically (median ×1.33 in segment 0 → ×1.46 in segment 3+).
- **Inverse-IFEval, T=51 (N=184 worlds, |D*|=2.2).** Excess size: CONTROL **+211.3% ± 105.3**, PLACEBO **+147.9% ± 79.9**, TREATMENT **+1.4% ± 22.1** — a **99.3%** removal of excess — at constraint satisfaction **44.0% / 42.6% / 44.0%** (i.e., exact parity with the strongest baseline).
- **Inverse-IFEval, T=15 (N=552, 3 seeds).** CONTROL **+60.4% ± 18.4**, PLACEBO **+53.2% ± 14.6**, TREATMENT **−5.8% ± 6.6** (a **66.2pp** swing) at satisfaction 39.0 / 40.3 / 38.2 — CIs disjoint on size, overlapping on satisfaction.
- **Inverse-WildIFEval (64 worlds, K=1, G=16).** Seeded distractors cost **24.1pp** of correctness on the true instructions already present (**65.6%** at G=0 vs **41.5%** at G=16; 95% CI [−33.4, −14.9]pp). Comments lift satisfaction **50.4% → 62.0%** over 3 rounds — **+11.6pp**, **+23.1% relative**, 95% CI [5.1, 18.3]pp — against an uncommented maintainer given the *identical* seeded prompt. The placebo lands **2.7pp** from control (CI [−4.4, 10.1]pp, covering zero).
- **Judge robustness.** All 6,336 verdicts re-scored under gpt-5.6-luna: the instruction-following effect becomes **+7.8pp [+0.6, +14.9]** vs **+11.6pp**, with per-world DID **+3.8pp [−1.9, +9.6]pp** (does not exclude zero). Raw agreement 82.1%, κ = 0.639, and κ does not vary by arm (0.631–0.653), so judge noise cannot have manufactured the gap.
- **Capability scaling.** Holding executor, task stream and horizon fixed and moving only the maintainer, the uncommented arm's excess rises from **+67.7%** to **+571.9%** across three tiers; Δ from COMMIT is −42.1% (Haiku 4.5), −61.7% (Sonnet 5), −52.3% (Opus 5), and at the top tier comments yield strictly Pareto gains (satisfaction **+18.4%**, z = 2.12).
- **Ablations.** Comment-shaped noise: Δ = −3.1% (z = −0.33, null). A *narrative of attempts without their outcomes* is the **worst arm at +70.0%** — worse than no comments at all. Within the schema, dropping the recurrence count alone costs **37%** of the size reduction. Lifting the comment strip (rendering comments into the executor's prompt too) yields **+28.5%** excess vs **−0.1%** in the canonical run, at a satisfaction difference straddling zero.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: **Kushal Chakrabarti**, affiliated with **South Park Commons** (a founder community / seed fund, not a research lab), contact at a personal domain. There is no institutional co-author, no lab-scale compute claim (49,680 model calls, 37.9M tokens, 45 model-hours, CPU-only containers), and no prior track record cited in this line of work. Against that, the paper's methodological hygiene is unusually strong for a solo preprint: pre-registered acceptance gates reported with thresholds and observed values (Table 3), pre-registered criteria of record evaluated *per seed* rather than only pooled (Table 11), competing-risk censoring made explicit, a repository-stratified rather than spell-level bootstrap, a lockfile-frozen dependency set, a stated statement that a programmatic generator emits every reported number from canonical artifacts with no LLM-produced values, and an ethics statement that names the paper's own recommendation as the main risk. The LLM-use statement is candid about delegating literature synthesis, LaTeX, and code co-generation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (high).** The catastrophic-forgetting/remembering duality is a genuinely clarifying reframe, and the inversion trick — hide a benchmark's instructions so they become a known minimum cover — is the kind of evaluation design that makes a previously unmeasurable quantity (excess prompt size) measurable. Nobody has previously estimated a per-instruction deletion hazard over agentic context files.

**Rigor (mixed, but honest about it).** The load-bearing weakness is the **gap between the corpus and the experiment**: the controlled worlds have covers of **2 or 3 instructions** (423 and 129 histories) against a **median real file of 39** — an order of magnitude. The author states this plainly, and Appendix C.1 shows the strata disagree: at |D*|=2 the control sits at **+74.2% [+52.2, +98.8]**, but at |D*|=3 the control's interval is **+15.2% [+0.5, +31.8]**, barely excluding cover, so *the pooled ratchet is essentially a two-constraint result at this horizon*. The author's own explanation — interference makes deletion pay, and interference is weak at 2–3 constraints and strong at 39 — is plausible but untested, and it cuts both ways: if interference is what makes pruning pay, the regime where the intervention was validated is precisely the regime where it matters least.

**Second problem: the treatment over-prunes.** The protocol empties **12.1%** of prompts entirely (1 in 8) against **2.4%** under control and **1.1%** under placebo, and on exactly those emptied worlds it scores lowest (0.259 vs control 0.279 and placebo 0.328, the placebo gap **−0.070 [−0.114, −0.027]** excluding zero). At |D*|=3 it prunes *below* cover (**−21.2%**) at satisfaction 35.7% vs control's 40.3%. The pooled "parity satisfaction" headline therefore averages over a real failure tail. To the author's credit, this is Appendix C.5, not buried.

**Third: the T=51 headline is one seed.** The 99.3%-excess-removal number in the abstract comes from a single-seed run at N=184; the three-seed replication is only at T=15, where the effect is smaller (66.2pp) though consistent across seeds (control sd 10.9pp).

**Weak/awkward baselines.** The only comparators are "no comments" and "comment-shaped noise". Missing: a periodic dedup/compression pass, a maintainer explicitly instructed to minimize prompt length (the author reports dropping the minimality objective left the delete-to-add rate essentially unchanged, which is informative but is a knob-check, not a baseline), or an auditor that re-derives rationale post hoc. The Sonnet 5 row in the capability table is explicitly **not** a capability reading — that maintainer overran the 1,024-character comment budget on **two of every five additions**, i.e., it did not run the protocol it was given, and reporting it as a table row invites misreading.

**Data quirks.** **77.3% of all instruction deaths are censored** as rewrites or migrations, so the hazard is fit on a minority of events — rewrite deaths outnumber the deletions used for estimation by 3.4:1. The 50% rewrite threshold is applied *inside the tracker* and was **never swept** (the author says so and calls the audit missing). The matcher is validated on **50 hand-annotated transitions annotated by the author himself** against 299,440 tracked ones; the author concedes an independent stratified re-annotation would fix this. Segmentation grammar is fixed per corpus and named as the largest untested degree of freedom. The mitigating argument — that matcher error adds spurious hazard to the old-age tail and thus biases the slope *toward zero*, making the decline a lower bound — is sound.

**Overclaiming to watch.** The abstract's "improve real-world agentic instruction-following by up to 23.1%" is doing work the experiment doesn't quite support: Appendix D.4 states the effect is a **recovery from seeded noise**, not a general gain — at D=0 the arms do not separate consistently (treatment above control at N=5, below at N=6). The gain only appears at D=16 where there are distractors to prune. Similarly, "real-world" here means *benchmark prose seeded with synthetic distractors*, not actual repository files; the ratchet is never shown to be reversed in any real CLAUDE.md. The O(2^|D|) deletion cost is a worst-case combinatorial argument, not an empirically measured quantity. Finally, maintainer, executor and judge 1 are all claude-haiku-4-5 — same model family throughout — and the second judge's smaller effect (7.8pp) is the honest reminder that neither judge is ground truth.

**Reproducibility (good).** Derived per-instruction tables and rebuild code are released, verifiers are reimplemented from scratch and flagged as not comparable to published IFEval numbers, model versions and decoding settings are pinned in a resolved config, and env.lock freezes dependencies. What is *not* reproducible from the artifact alone: hosted-API model weights, and the corpus content itself (only URLs are redistributed).

**Significance (high for practice).** If the mechanism holds, the prescription — write down *why* an instruction exists, at write time, in a channel the model never reads — is nearly free and immediately actionable for anyone maintaining an AGENTS.md.

## Strengths

- The theory makes a **falsifiable, directional prediction** (hazard slope sign) that cleanly separates three rival mechanisms, and the data are collected at the granularity needed to test it.
- The multi-author × age interaction is the paper's sharpest move: a fixed per-instruction frailty gives no reason for the age slope to differ between files many people edit and files one person edits, and it does differ (z = −11.7).
- Pre-registration is real, not decorative: gates with thresholds fixed before pipeline code, criteria evaluated per seed, and a stated no-frailty denominator for every "absorbed share".
- The placebo arm is a genuine control for *surface form* rather than a strawman, and the ablation showing "narrative without outcomes" is **worse than no comment at all** (+70.0%) is the most interesting negative result in the paper — it says the channel is only useful when it carries falsification, not intent.
- The comment strip is enforced **in code**, not by asking the executor to ignore comments, and Appendix C.6 measures what lifting it costs.
- Limitations and ethics sections name the specific failure modes an adversarial reviewer would raise, including the unswept threshold, the single hand-annotation pool, and the deletion risk of automating the recommendation.

## Limitations

- Controlled experiment operates at covers of 2–3 instructions vs a median real file of 39; the regime where retention is nearly free is not the regime the corpus describes.
- The abstract's flagship number (+211.3% → +1.4%) rests on **one seed**; the multi-seed evidence is the smaller T=15 effect.
- 77.3% of deaths censored; 50% rewrite threshold never swept; matcher validated once on 50 author-annotated transitions.
- WildIFEval satisfaction is a rate under a named LLM judge, not ground truth; no human-annotated subsample was run, and the two judges' point estimates differ by 3.8pp with an interval admitting up to 9.6pp.
- The protocol empties 1 in 8 prompts and scores worst on those worlds; no floor on pruning was imposed, and the author explicitly warns operators to keep a human in the deletion path.
- Corpus language distribution was never measured — no result speaks to non-English instructions. Whether catastrophic remembering reaches system prompts or agent skill files is open.
- Section 3.4's multi-author covariate counts *who has edited a file*, not whether the instruction's own author has left, so it proxies turnover rather than measuring it; the author labels this analysis exploratory with no pre-registered criterion.
- Observational half is correlational; the causal half is synthetic. There is no field deployment showing the ratchet reversed in a real repository.

## Relevance

这篇论文与你关注的**自演化 agent 与自动化研究循环**高度相关：它把"agent 自己维护自己的提示"形式化为一个带删失反馈的在线估计问题，并给出了一个可证伪的失败模式（潜在推理衰减导致提示单调膨胀），这正是任何长期运行的自改进 agent 都会遇到的记忆治理问题。同时其"反转基准以获得已知最优解"的评测构造方法，对**LLM 推理评测与可靠性度量**也有直接借鉴价值——它把一个原本不可计算的量（最小覆盖）变成了可测量的实验设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the ratchet reverse in an actual repository? The strongest missing experiment is a field trial: instrument N real repos with a comment syntax for 6 months and measure the hazard slope, rather than inferring it from 2–3-instruction synthetic worlds.
- Does the effect survive at realistic cover sizes (20–40 instructions)? The author's own interference argument predicts it should get *stronger*, but Appendix C.1 shows the direction of the strata disagreement runs the other way at the sizes tested — that tension needs resolving before the headline generalizes.
- What is the right floor on pruning? The protocol empties 12.1% of prompts; a minimal safeguard (never delete below k instructions, or require the comment's failure to have recurred m times) is untested and would likely recover the emptied-world deficit cheaply.
- Is the comment the right channel, or would a *deletion log* work better? The current design deliberately kills a rationale when its instruction dies ("a rationale not attached to a live instruction has nowhere to live"). That is a modeling choice, not a physical constraint — a file-level graveyard section would preserve falsification lineage without the copy-forward burden the COMMIT schema imposes.
- Can the comment be generated post hoc? The whole argument rests on O(1) write-time capture vs O(2^|D|) read-time reconstruction, but an LLM with access to the commit that introduced an instruction may recover much of the rationale cheaply — that would be a strong, cheap baseline and it is absent.
- Does this transfer to system prompts, tool descriptions, and skill files, where the maintainer is often the model vendor rather than a repo owner and turnover dynamics differ entirely?
- Would rehearsal or regularization analogues from continual learning (the paper's stated open question) outperform comments — e.g., periodically replaying past failures against the current prompt to re-derive which instructions still earn their place?
