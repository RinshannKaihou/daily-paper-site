> **一句话总结：** RLMOpt 把提示词优化的「外层搜索策略」本身交给一个递归语言模型（RLM）智能体去自主决定（看什么证据、评估哪个候选、何时停），再用一个确定性 harness 强制执行按字段打分、Pareto 选择和「不许回退」的下限约束；在 Chia / HotpotQA / IFBench-2025 / BFCL 多轮四个基准上四项全胜，四任务均值 0.610 对 GEPA 的 0.589，且只用 500 次候选 rollout（GEPA 约 780–840），产出的提示词长度只有 GEPA 的 27–79%。

> **TL;DR:** RLMOpt replaces the hand-coded outer loop of prompt optimizers with a recursive-language-model agent that controls the search itself (introspection, failure analysis, candidate generation, budget allocation, stopping), while a deterministic harness owns scoring, Pareto selection, and a per-field no-regression floor. Against GEPA-light on Chia, HotpotQA, IFBench-2025, and BFCL multi-turn, RLMOpt wins all four held-out columns and the four-task mean (0.610 vs 0.589, +0.021), with the biggest single gap on BFCL multi-turn (0.686 vs 0.653, +0.033 = 1.83 paired SE), using 500 rollouts instead of GEPA's ~780–840 and producing prompts 27–79% the size. Caveat: most per-benchmark margins sit inside the ~0.03–0.05 per-run standard error, and no ablation isolates the adaptive policy from the harness.

## 核心贡献

- **把搜索策略本身做成语言模型驱动的。** 现有优化器（GEPA 的反思变异 + Pareto 选择、MIPROv2 的贝叶斯搜索、OPRO 的固定循环）中，算法决定探索什么、如何推进，LM 只负责生成候选提示词。RLMOpt 反过来：RLM 智能体在 REPL 里写代码调用工具，自己决定先看数据还是先花评估预算、攻击哪个失败模式、什么时候停。
- **「决策」与「执行」分离的 harness 设计。** 智能体只能通过 harness 触达任务；harness 是程序而非第二个智能体，独占数据集、执行 task LM、计算全部分数，并强制两条约束：每字段回退下限 f_floor = 0.05（不许靠牺牲某个字段换总分）与显著性门槛（相对当前最优的配对提升须超过 1.65 个标准误，单边 95%）。
- **对抗「原地打转」的噪声带拒绝机制。** 若连续三个候选的分数落在当前最优的噪声带内且中间没有做过诊断，下一次 `run_candidate` 直接返回错误且不扣预算，强制智能体先对最弱字段调用 `synthesize_failures`。这个门只约束「何时允许继续评估」，不规定「该搜什么」。
- **把优化对象从单个字符串推广到 component map。** 一个工具型智能体由 system prompt、每个工具的 description、以及可优化的 demonstrations 共同决定；RLMOpt 让这些组件各自独立编辑，复合打分 / Pareto / 字段下限原样适用。轨迹级评分包含工具精确率与召回率、参数匹配、终态相等、tool-call JSON 合法性，并对「本不该调工具却调了」扣分、「正确地拒绝行动」给分。
- **给出「什么时候提示词优化有用」的判据。** 增益主要由种子提示词留下的 headroom 决定，而非搜索预算；接近模型 prompting 天花板的任务（IFEval 式指令跟随约 0.91、BFCL 单轮约 0.77）两种优化器都推不动，此时 RLMOpt 的无回退下限会原样退回种子提示词。

## Key Contributions

- **LM-driven search policy, not just LM-driven candidate proposal.** The RLM agent acts by writing short programs in a REPL that call harness tools (`describe_task`, `peek_examples`, `search_traces`, `describe_failure_patterns`, `synthesize_failures`, `run_candidate`, `commit_prompt`, `pareto_frontier_status`, scratchpad), choosing both which operation to run and when.
- **Deterministic harness as the guarantee layer.** Composite score S(p) = Σ w_f s_f(p); Pareto frontier over the per-field score vector; commit acceptance requires both s_f(p) ≥ b_f − 0.05 for every field and a paired improvement exceeding 1.65 SE. A rejected commit changes nothing regardless of the agent's own assessment.
- **Structured feedback instead of a scalar.** `run_candidate` returns the composite, its per-field decomposition, the mismatched fields, and any LM-judge rationale, so the agent can target the next edit without a separate diagnostic call.
- **Multi-component candidates for agentic tasks.** A candidate is a map {name → text}; a single-prompt task exposes only `system_prompt`, a tool-using agent additionally exposes `tool_description_<name>` per tool plus a `demonstrations` component. Trajectories are scored by subset matching (permits exploration/recovery) or final-state matching.
- **A headroom-vs-ceiling account of when optimization pays**, backed by explicitly-labeled negative controls (capability-saturated tasks) rather than only by benchmarks where the method wins.

## Methodology

The formal objective is the standard one — maximize mean metric m(θ(p, x_i), y_i) over a prompt p, starting from seed p₀ under a budget B of task-LM candidate evaluations. What changes is who runs the loop.

**Agent side.** The RLM agent (gpt-5.1 in all experiments) is initialized with the seed prompt, task description, tool list, budget, and a persistent scratchpad, and is conditioned by a fixed "skill prompt" (Appendix A) that is never modified during optimization. That skill prompt encodes three load-bearing disciplines: orient once (static introspection calls are made a single time), diagnose from aggregated failures rather than a 3-example skim, and treat a within-noise result as uninformative — do not commit, do not re-run to confirm, attack a different failure mode. It also carries an explicit "Bigger is NOT better" instruction telling the agent to test a minimal variant when elaborated candidates stop winning, which the authors credit for the short optimized prompts. Crucially, the skill is advisory: only the harness can refuse a commit.

**Harness side.** The harness evaluates the seed first (not charged to B), executes every task-LM rollout, computes per-field metrics and the composite, maintains candidate history, and enforces the regression floor and significance gate. The floor is additionally auto-enabled on datasets of ≤20 records, where a single-field regression is most easily mistaken for aggregate progress. Two harness-enforced limits apply independently of the agent's own stopping decision: the hard budget cap B, and the noise-band refusal that forces a diagnosis after three uninformative candidates.

**Finalization.** After the agent stops, the harness generates "polish" variants of its own running-best candidate, then scores the seed, all committed candidates, the agent's claimed best, and the polish variants on validation, forms the Pareto frontier, and returns the test score of the frontier member with the highest composite. Polish evaluations are also outside B.

**Setup.** Task LM is gpt-4o-mini everywhere except Chia (gpt-4.1, because many-field extraction saturates the smaller model's format control before the prompt becomes the binding constraint); temperature = 0 throughout. Optimizer LM is gpt-5.1 for both RLMOpt and GEPA. RLMOpt uses a fixed B = 500 across all benchmarks with no per-benchmark tuning; GEPA uses its native `auto="light"` setting (~780–840 rollouts). GEPA cannot natively drive the BFCL multi-turn loop, so the authors wrote a thin wrapper exposing the agentic system prompt as the optimizable instruction while still scoring with the real multi-turn rollout. RLMOpt's cross-run skill library is disabled for all head-to-head comparisons so neither method carries information between runs.

## Results & Highlights

- **Four-task mean held-out score: RLMOpt 0.610 vs GEPA-light 0.589 vs seed prompt 0.542** (single seed 7; Table 2). RLMOpt is top of every column: Chia 0.568 vs 0.562, HotpotQA 0.727 vs 0.702, IFBench-25 0.460 vs 0.440, BFCL-mt 0.686 vs 0.653.
- **BFCL multi-turn is the largest and only clearly-significant lift:** 0.686 vs GEPA's 0.653 (+0.033 = 1.83 paired standard errors). HotpotQA's +0.025 is 1.04 paired SE; Chia (+0.006 = 0.48 SE) and IFBench-25 (+0.020 = 0.39 SE) sit *inside* one paired SE and are, by the paper's own stated convention, ties.
- **Validation-to-test transfer on Chia is the sharpest qualitative finding:** GEPA scores *higher* on validation (0.636 vs RLMOpt's 0.593) but *lower* on test (0.562 vs 0.568) — the authors read this as a prompt fitted to the split the optimizer can see.
- **Multi-seed robustness (11 matched benchmark–seed runs): RLMOpt wins 9/11**, losing HotpotQA seed 13 (0.715 vs 0.744) and Chia seed 13 (0.676 vs 0.698). Multi-seed means: HotpotQA 0.731 ± 0.019 vs 0.693 ± 0.056; BFCL-mt 0.699 ± 0.030 vs 0.677 ± 0.027; IFBench-25 0.390 ± 0.089 vs 0.367 ± 0.102; Chia 0.622 ± 0.076 vs GEPA's 0.630 ± 0.097 (the one benchmark-level loss). Overall mean 0.611 vs 0.592.
- **Zero regressions vs two for GEPA.** Across all 11 runs RLMOpt never returned a prompt below its seed; GEPA did twice (HotpotQA seed 42: 0.634 vs seed 0.669; IFBench-25 seed 13: 0.250 vs seed 0.290). On the IFBench run RLMOpt held the seed *exactly* at 0.290 — winning the comparison without improving anything.
- **Variance reduction:** RLMOpt's HotpotQA standard deviation across seeds is 0.019, about one third of GEPA's 0.056.
- **Compute:** fewer downstream rollouts on every benchmark (500 or fewer vs 639–842), and on 3 of 4 also fewer tokens and less wall-clock. Widest wall-clock gap is BFCL-mt at 1,854s vs 5,344s (and 5.2M vs 7.05M tokens). On Chia, GEPA-light is actually *faster* (1,244s vs 1,357s) and cheaper in tokens (3.81M vs 4.58M) than the B=500 RLMOpt run.
- **Small budgets recover most of the gain:** HotpotQA at B=200 reaches 0.717 vs 0.727 at B=500 — a quarter of GEPA's rollouts for ~93% of the delta over the seed. On Chia a light run self-stops at 135 rollouts and reaches 0.553, within one SE of GEPA-light's 0.562 at fewer tokens (3.11M vs 3.81M).
- **Prompt size:** RLMOpt's optimized instruction is 27–79% of GEPA-light's on every benchmark. BFCL-mt: 5,419 vs 19,818 characters (−14,399). Chia: 12,605 vs 24,556. IFBench-25: 3,073 vs 7,744. HotpotQA: 3,961 vs 5,043.
- **Negative controls (honestly reported):** on capability-saturated tasks — IFEval-style instruction following at ~0.91 seed, single-turn BFCL at ~0.77 — *neither* RLMOpt nor GEPA improved anything; RLMOpt returned the seed unchanged.
- **A reported failure of the multi-component extension:** on a small mixed function-calling probe (~10 validation, ~8 test examples), optimization improved validation on *every* seed (+0.067 mean) but held-out changed by roughly −0.03.

## Authors & Credibility

Three of four authors are at Autonomize AI (Subhash Bangalore Satheesha, Deepthi Duddempudi, Bharath Dandala), with Nirvik Pande at Carnegie Mellon University. Bharath Dandala has a substantial prior publication record in clinical NLP and biomedical information extraction, which is consistent with Chia (clinical-trial eligibility criteria extraction) being the flagship benchmark and with the care taken over multi-field extraction scoring. The work is a single-lab industry paper with an academic collaborator; the writing shows unusual methodological self-awareness (it labels its own ties as ties, reports the one benchmark where GEPA wins on multi-seed mean, flags its own miscalibrated stopping thresholds, and reports a negative result on the multi-component extension). No code or artifact release is mentioned anywhere in the paper, and there is no funding/conflict statement in the extracted text.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate–good).** "Let an agent run the outer loop" is in the air — the paper itself cites ADAS (meta-agent programming agents in code) and concurrent SkillOpt — so the conceptual move is not unprecedented. What is genuinely new here is the *division of labor*: making the search policy fully LM-controlled while hard-wiring the things an LM cannot be trusted to maintain (objective scoring, Pareto dominance, per-field regression floors, budget accounting) into a program the agent cannot bypass. The noise-band refusal that returns an error *without consuming budget* and redirects the agent to `synthesize_failures` is a specific, well-motivated mechanism against a real agentic failure mode.

**Rigor (mixed, but honestly presented).** The headline table is a single seed, and the authors' own paired-SE column shows only 2 of 4 margins exceed one paired SE — Chia's +0.006 at 0.48 SE is noise and is labeled as such. The multi-seed table partially repairs this (9/11 wins), but the seed counts are unbalanced: three seeds for the gpt-4o-mini benchmarks and only *two* for Chia, and Chia is precisely the benchmark where GEPA leads the mean (0.630 vs 0.622). A 2-seed mean with sd ≈ 0.08–0.10 carries essentially no information. The 11 comparisons are also not independent draws — three benchmarks × three seeds — so "9 of 11" is not a clean binomial test.

**The baseline story is thinner than the intro implies.** The introduction and related work set up GEPA, MIPROv2, and OPRO as the landscape, but **only GEPA-light is actually run**. MIPROv2 and OPRO never appear in a results table. And GEPA is run at its *light* preset, not a matched-compute or best-effort configuration — the compute comparison is then framed as RLMOpt's advantage (500 vs ~780–840 rollouts), which cuts the other way: a GEPA-medium/heavy run might close the gap, and we never see one. Additionally, Section 5.3 concedes that rollouts are not total cost — on Chia only ~32% of RLMOpt's 1,556 task-LM calls are `run_candidate`; ~67% is harness-side scoring — so "more efficient" is a claim about downstream candidate evaluations specifically, not about dollars.

**The missing ablation is named by the authors themselves.** RLMOpt differs from GEPA along at least four axes simultaneously: adaptive search policy, composite per-field scoring, no-regression floor, and an optimizable demonstrations component. The paper explicitly declines to run the obvious controlled study (hold harness, budget, task LM, and optimizer LM fixed; swap the adaptive policy for a fixed procedure). This matters most on BFCL multi-turn — the one benchmark with a >1 SE margin — where **RLMOpt optimizes a demonstrations component that the GEPA wrapper does not expose at all**. The largest reported win may therefore be an artifact of the comparison surface rather than evidence for adaptive search. The authors say as much; readers should weight it accordingly.

**A data quirk worth naming:** Chia runs on gpt-4.1 while the other three run on gpt-4o-mini, chosen because "many-field extraction saturates the smaller model's format control." That is a defensible engineering reason, but it means the strongest-headroom benchmark (+0.133 over seed) is also the one with a different task model, and it is the benchmark with the fewest seeds and the only benchmark-level loss.

**The "no regressions" claim is partly definitional.** RLMOpt's harness will return the seed prompt when nothing clears the significance gate — as it did on IFBench-25 seed 13, holding 0.290 exactly and "taking the comparison without improving on it." So "0 runs below seed" is largely a property of the acceptance rule, not evidence that the search is better. It is a real practical benefit (an optimizer that cannot hurt you is deployable), but it is not the same claim as "finds better prompts."

**Overclaiming check:** low. The abstract's framing ("best held-out score on all four") is technically true but leans on two margins the paper's own SE analysis calls ties; the body immediately says so ("the comparison therefore rests on consistency rather than on any one column"). The limitations section is one of the more candid ones in this literature — it admits the stopping conditions are miscalibrated (a 0.85 per-field target that is unattainable given observed composites; a 0.02 improvement threshold *below* the 0.03–0.05 per-run SE), admits the budget cap is what actually terminates runs, and admits larger budgets sometimes *lowered* held-out accuracy.

**Reproducibility (weak).** No code, no artifact link, no dataset preprocessing release in the extracted text. The optimizer skill prompt is excerpted (with elisions) in Appendix A and optimized prompts are reproduced in Appendix B, which helps. But the whole system depends on gpt-5.1 as the optimizer LM and gpt-4.1/gpt-4o-mini as task LMs — closed, versioned, drifting APIs — so exact replication is not possible for outsiders, and the ~0.03–0.05 per-run SE against ~0.02 typical margins means small reproduction attempts will be uninformative.

**Red flags:** (1) single-seed headline table with several sub-SE margins; (2) only one baseline actually run, at its lightest preset; (3) the biggest win comes from a benchmark where the baseline's optimization surface is strictly smaller; (4) 2 seeds on Chia; (5) IFBench-2025 is cited to a benchmark suite entry with no authors listed, which is hard to independently verify; (6) test sets are 50–150 examples.

## Strengths

- The decision/enforcement split is a genuinely good architectural answer to "how do you let an agent drive search without letting it fool itself," and the mechanisms (Pareto frontier over per-field vectors, 0.05 regression floor, 1.65-SE significance gate, budget-free refusal after three uninformative candidates) are concrete and implementable rather than hand-wavy.
- Reporting negative controls (capability-saturated IFEval-style and single-turn BFCL splits where *nothing* helps) is rare in the prompt-optimization literature and is the paper's most useful contribution to practitioners deciding whether to bother.
- The headroom framing is a falsifiable claim with support: gains rank-order with seed weakness (Chia +0.133 from 0.435; BFCL +0.084 from 0.602; HotpotQA +0.027 from 0.700; IFBench +0.030 from 0.430).
- Prompt-size reduction is a real practical win that is usually ignored: 5,419 vs 19,818 characters on BFCL-mt means lower per-call inference cost forever after, not just during optimization.
- The Chia validation-vs-test inversion is a nice piece of evidence that the paper turns into a genuine argument about optimizer overfitting rather than burying.
- The paper reports its own failure cases (small-validation-set overfitting probe: +0.067 val, ≈ −0.03 test) instead of omitting them.

## Limitations

- **No component ablation.** The contribution of the adaptive policy versus the harness's scoring/floor/demos machinery is unidentified, by the authors' own admission.
- **BFCL comparison is asymmetric.** GEPA's wrapper does not expose the demonstrations component that RLMOpt optimizes, so the paper's largest margin is a system-vs-system comparison, not a policy-vs-policy one.
- **Only GEPA-light is run.** MIPROv2, OPRO, TextGrad, and the evolutionary methods are discussed but never benchmarked; no GEPA at a matched or higher budget.
- **Stopping policy is broken and known to be broken.** Two of the three stated conditions are miscalibrated (0.85 per-field target unattainable; 0.02 threshold below the SE), so termination is effectively driven by the budget cap, and stopping points vary wildly (27 vs 21 rollouts in the two synthetic-diagnostic runs, fewer elsewhere). Raising the budget cap did not reliably increase search and in one diagnostic *lowered* test accuracy.
- **Small evaluation sets.** 50–150-example test sets with 0.03–0.05 per-run SE against typical margins of 0.006–0.033.
- **Narrow model coverage.** Two closed task LMs, one closed optimizer LM; no open-weight or smaller models, so nothing is established about whether an open model can act as a competent search controller.
- **Benchmark selection is admitted to be headroom-biased.** All four headline benchmarks were chosen to leave exploitable headroom.
- **Multi-component extension is fragile on small validation sets**, per the authors' own probe.
- **No cost-parity comparison.** The optimizer LM is a stronger, separate model whose cost is only reported in tokens; a dollar-normalized comparison is absent.

## Relevance

如果你关注 agentic 系统的自动优化 / LLM-as-optimizer / prompt 工程自动化，这篇给出了一个可直接照搬的工程范式：让智能体控制搜索策略，但把「打分、Pareto 选择、不许回退」这类不能交给 LM 的部分硬编码进确定性 harness——这个 decision/enforcement 分离的思路对任何 LLM 驱动的自动搜索循环（包括自演化 skill / agent pipeline 优化）都有借鉴价值。另外「增益由 seed headroom 决定而非搜索预算」以及明确报告 negative control 的做法，对判断「某个任务值不值得上 prompt optimizer」很实用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. The decisive ablation the paper skips: hold harness, budget, task LM, and optimizer LM fixed, and replace the RLM policy with GEPA's reflective-mutation policy. How much of the +0.021 mean survives? My prior is that the per-field floor and Pareto machinery carry a large share.
2. Re-run BFCL multi-turn with the demonstrations component *disabled* for RLMOpt (or exposed to GEPA). Does the +0.033 — the only clearly-super-SE margin — hold up?
3. Does the RLM controller still work with an open-weight optimizer LM (e.g. a mid-size open model instead of gpt-5.1)? The whole premise is that the controller can form and test hypotheses; that is exactly the capability likely to degrade first.
4. Can the no-regression floor be turned into a formal guarantee rather than a heuristic? With a 1.65-SE gate on a 50–150-example validation split, the false-accept rate over many commits is not negligible — what is the actual empirical rate of committed candidates that regress on test?
5. The proposed fix for stopping — terminate on remaining per-field headroom plus the commit-significance criterion — is stated but never evaluated. Does it actually reduce the run-to-run variance the paper identifies as the dominant source of instability?
6. GEPA at matched *rollout* budget (500) rather than its light preset (~780–840): is RLMOpt's efficiency claim really about the policy, or about GEPA-light being budget-inefficient at its default?
7. Does the cross-run skill library (disabled for all head-to-heads) actually compound? That is arguably the more interesting long-run claim and it is entirely untested here.
8. How does the headroom hypothesis interact with model upgrades — does a prompt optimized for gpt-4o-mini's failure modes transfer to a stronger task LM, or does it become dead weight that the "bigger is not better" rule was fighting in the first place?
