> **一句话总结：** SQuaT 在无标签 QAT 中把教师的中间特征用**学生自己的量化参数**投影到学生的量化格点上再做特征蒸馏，从理论上消除了"不可达残差"造成的蒸馏损失下界；在 CIFAR-100/ResNet-32 W1A1 上比 SQAKD 提升 +1.74 pp（59.38 → 61.12），在 DeiT-Tiny/CIFAR-100 W4A4 上提升 +3.42 pp（86.78 → 90.20），但在 ImageNet-1K 上的增益仅 +0.02~+0.40 pp。

> **TL;DR:** SQuaT is a label-free quantization-aware training (QAT) framework that quantizes the *teacher's* intermediate features using the *student's* own learned quantization parameters before feature distillation, provably removing the irreducible lower bound on distillation loss caused by FP-vs-quantized range mismatch. Against the label-free QAT+KD baseline SQAKD, it gains +1.74 pp on CIFAR-100/ResNet-32 W1A1 (59.38 → 61.12), +3.42 pp on DeiT-Tiny/CIFAR-100 W4A4 (86.78 → 90.20), and +8.48 pp on GLUE-RTE with BERT-base W4A4 (57.40 → 65.88) — though on ImageNet-1K/ResNet-18 the gains shrink to +0.40 pp (W1A1) and +0.02 pp (W8A8). Accepted at AISTATS 2026.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"学生感知投影"(student-aware projection) 范式转换**：以往特征蒸馏是把学生往教师的表示空间拉（teacher-aware），SQuaT 反过来，用学生第 ℓ 层的量化参数 φ_S,ℓ = {a⁻, a⁺, b} 把教师特征 f_T,ℓ 投影到学生的量化格点上，蒸馏目标变为 f̃_T,ℓ = Π_{φ_S,ℓ}(f_T,ℓ)。
- **给出定理 1 与证明**：用 FP 教师特征做目标时，期望平方误差不可能低于 E‖Π(f_T)−f_T‖²（即学生可达集 G_{φ_S} 到教师特征的距离），构成不可约的损失下界；换成学生量化后的目标，该下界严格为 0。并指出：教师特征越超出学生学到的截断范围 [a⁻, a⁺]，该下界单调增大。
- **完全无标签的联合目标**：L = λ_feat·L_feat（学生量化特征与投影后教师特征的 L2）+ λ_logit·L_logit（温度平滑的 KL，用教师分布当伪标签），全程不接触 ground-truth 标签，教师冻结。
- **跨架构/跨任务/跨量化器的广泛验证**：CNN（ResNet-20/32/18）、ViT（DeiT-Tiny）、NLP Transformer（BERT-base on GLUE）；量化器覆盖 EWGS、LSQ、STATSQ、TwnQuantizer、SymQuantizer；蒸馏损失覆盖 L1/L2/KL/cosine。
- **投影方式的直接消融**：Non-aware（直接用 FP 特征）、Teacher-aware（复现 QFD 的教师侧标定量化器）、Student-aware 三者对比，并给出特征蒸馏损失的训练曲线佐证理论。

## Key Contributions

- **A paradigm shift from teacher-aware to student-aware projection.** Prior feature-level KD for quantized models (notably QFD) attaches a quantizer to the teacher and calibrates it independently; SQuaT instead reuses the student's *learned* per-layer quantization parameters φ_S,ℓ to project teacher features, so both models are read on the identical quantization lattice and quantized values carry the same semantics.
- **Theorem 1 (lower-bound removal).** With FP targets, inf over the student's attainable set G_{φ_S,ℓ} of E‖h − f_T,ℓ‖² equals E‖Π_{φ_S,ℓ}(f_T,ℓ) − f_T,ℓ‖² > 0; with student-quantized targets it equals exactly 0. The residual is shown to equal E[dist(f_T,ℓ, G_{φ_S,ℓ})²], which grows monotonically as teacher activations fall outside the student's learned clipping range.
- **Strictly label-free QAT.** Joint feature + logit objective (Eq. 12) with a frozen FP teacher; no ground-truth labels anywhere in training, addressing privacy/copyright/cost constraints on labels.
- **Architecture- and quantizer-agnostic evidence.** Same projection rule applied under EWGS-based QAT (CNNs) and STE-based QAT (Transformers), with four different quantizers and four different feature-loss functions.
- **Deployment check.** INT8 ResNet-18 on Jetson Nano: 294.32 → 2046.03 FPS, 3.442 → 0.53 ms, a 6.49× speedup over FP32.

## Methodology

SQuaT builds on EWGS (Element-Wise Gradient Scaling) for the CNN experiments and on plain STE for Transformers. The forward quantization path is standard: normalize x to [0,1] using learnable range parameters (a⁻, a⁺), round onto the b-bit uniform grid {0, 1/(2^b−1), …, 1}, then apply a type-specific linear map (identity for activations, 2(x−0.5) for weights). Backward uses the EWGS rescaling ∂L/∂x_norm ≈ g_q(1 + η·sign(g_q)·e), where e is the quantization error.

The novelty is a single operator: Π_{φ_S,ℓ}(z) ≜ ẑ_{φ_S,ℓ}, i.e. run the teacher's layer-ℓ feature through the *student's* forward quantization path. The distillation target becomes f̃_T,ℓ = Π_{φ_S,ℓ}(f_T,ℓ) and L_feat = (1/|K|) Σ_{ℓ∈K} ‖f̂_S,ℓ − f̃_T,ℓ‖²₂. Logit alignment is the usual T²·KL(p_T ‖ p_S). Both θ_S and the quantization parameters φ_S are updated by gradients from the joint loss — note that φ_S therefore appears on both sides of the feature loss, since it defines the target as well as the student output (the paper does not discuss whether the target is detached, which matters for gradient flow).

Where the projection is applied is deliberately minimal: for ResNets, the input feature of the last conv layer in the final residual block; for DeiT/BERT, the input to the first linear layer of the FFN in the last Transformer block, distilled over all tokens. So in practice |K| = 1 despite the general multi-layer formulation.

Training setup (Appendix A): CIFAR-10 ResNet-20/32 for 1200 epochs (batch 256, Adam, LR 1e-3 model / 1e-5 quant params); CIFAR-100 for 720 epochs (batch 64); DeiT-Tiny 300 epochs (AdamW); ImageNet-1K ResNet-18 100 epochs from PyTorch pretrained weights (SGD for weights, Adam for quant params); BERT-base 3 epochs on GLUE with BertAdam. All on a single RTX A6000, PyTorch 2.8.0. Loss weights are λ_logit = λ_feat = 1 everywhere except the CIFAR-100/ResNet-32 4-bit setting, which uses λ_logit = 100, λ_feat = 10.

## Results & Highlights

- **CIFAR-100, ResNet-32, W1A1 (Table 2):** SQuaT 61.12 ± 0.19 vs SQAKD 59.38 ± 0.04 (**+1.74 pp**); supervised EWGS gets 59.76, so SQuaT beats even the label-*using* baseline here by +1.36 pp. At W4A4 SQuaT reaches 71.94 vs SQAKD 71.65 (+0.29) and exceeds the FP teacher (71.40).
- **CIFAR-10, ResNet-20, W1A1 (Table 1):** SQuaT 86.97 ± 0.14 vs SQAKD 86.52 ± 0.05 (**+0.45 pp**), EWGS 86.42. Gains shrink monotonically with bit-width: +0.35 at W2A2, +0.07 at W4A4. On ResNet-32 W4A4, SQuaT (93.72) is actually *below* supervised EWGS (93.75).
- **ImageNet-1K, ResNet-18 (Table 3):** SQuaT vs SQAKD is 49.86 vs 49.46 at W1A1 (**+0.40 pp**), 67.81 vs 67.62 at W3A3 (+0.19), 68.77 vs 68.56 at W4A4 (+0.21), and 69.27 vs 69.25 at W8A8 (**+0.02 pp**, i.e. noise). No supervised EWGS column is reported at ImageNet scale.
- **DeiT-Tiny (Table 4)** — the largest gains: CIFAR-100 W4A4 90.20 ± 0.23 vs SQAKD 86.78 ± 0.15 (**+3.42 pp**), W3A3 +3.27 pp; CIFAR-10 W4A4 87.98 vs 85.88 (+2.10). But both methods sit ~9–11 pp below the quoted FP accuracy (97.26 / 97.33), suggesting the ViT QAT recipe is weak overall.
- **BERT-base on GLUE (Table 5):** RTE W4A4 65.88 ± 0.25 vs SQAKD 57.40 ± 1.02 (**+8.48 pp**); W3A3 57.22 vs 54.15 (+3.07); W8A8 69.13 vs 65.70 (+3.43, above the FP 68.95). SST-2 and QNLI gains are far smaller: +0.28/+0.69/+0.29 and +0.20/+1.26/+0.33.
- **Projection ablation (Table 7):** on CIFAR-100/ResNet-32 W1A1, Student-aware 61.02 > Non-aware 60.77 > Teacher-aware 59.59. Teacher-aware (the QFD-style scheme) is *worse than doing nothing* in 5 of 6 configurations — the paper's key empirical support for its central claim.
- **Loss-function robustness (Table 6):** all four feature losses beat SQAKD at W1A1 (L1 86.78, L2 86.97, KL 86.74, Cos 86.74 vs 86.47), with a spread of only 0.23 pp among them.
- **Tiny-ImageNet (Table 11, appendix):** +0.34 pp at W1A1, +0.15 at W4A4, +0.17 at W8A8, but **−0.20 pp at W3A3** — the one reported regression.
- **On-device (Table 9):** 6.49× throughput speedup (294.32 → 2046.03 FPS) for INT8 vs FP32 ResNet-18 on Jetson Nano.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** The operator is a one-line change — route teacher features through the student's quantizer — and its benefit is intuitive. Theorem 1 is mathematically correct but nearly tautological: the nearest point of a set to a vector is its projection, and once you redefine the target as an element of the attainable set the infimum is trivially zero. Crucially, driving the *distillation loss* to zero is not the same as improving *accuracy*; a degenerate target (e.g. all-zeros) would also have zero attainable residual. The paper never engages with this gap between its theory and its objective, and the empirical result that Non-aware often beats Teacher-aware is exactly the kind of evidence that the loss value is a poor proxy for downstream accuracy.

**Rigor (mixed).** The baseline set is thin: only SQAKD (label-free) and EWGS (supervised). QFD — the closest prior work and the direct motivation for the paper — is never run as a full published baseline; it appears only as a re-implemented "Teacher-aware" ablation row, which is convenient because that row underperforms even the naive Non-aware variant. Numbers are also internally inconsistent: Table 2 reports CIFAR-100/ResNet-32 W1A1 SQuaT = 61.12, while Table 7's Student-aware row for the same configuration says 61.02. Table 4 lists DeiT-Tiny FP accuracy as 97.33 on CIFAR-100 — higher than its own CIFAR-10 FP number (97.26) and far above any plausible DeiT-Tiny CIFAR-100 result, which strongly suggests a copy error; that makes the ~90% quantized numbers hard to interpret. The number of seeds behind the ± values is never stated, yet several headline deltas (+0.02, +0.07, +0.13, +0.19) are at or below the reported standard deviations. The BERT/RTE +8.48 pp is on a 2.5k-example task notorious for variance, reported with an implausibly tight ±0.25.

**Reproducibility (good).** Code is public, hyperparameters are tabulated per dataset/model (Table 10), the single-GPU A6000 setup is modest, and library versions are pinned. The main ambiguity is implementation-level: whether f̃_T is detached before the loss, and whether teacher features are passed through the *activation* or *weight* branch of the type-specific mapping.

**Significance (narrow but real).** Label-free QAT is a genuinely useful setting, and the method costs essentially nothing — no extra parameters, no teacher-side calibration pretraining (a concrete advantage over QFD). But the value proposition weakens exactly where deployment matters: ImageNet gains are ≤ 0.40 pp, and the claim that SQuaT "even surpasses the pretrained full-precision models" at 4-bit holds only on small CIFAR models where supervised EWGS also surpasses FP (93.75 vs 93.71), so it is a property of the QAT regularization, not of SQuaT.

**Red flags.** (1) Selective framing: "SQuaT outperforms the supervised QAT baseline EWGS in several configurations" glosses over CIFAR-100/ResNet-20 W1A1, where EWGS (56.86) beats SQuaT (56.74), and CIFAR-10/ResNet-32 W4A4, where EWGS wins. (2) The single Tiny-ImageNet regression (−0.20 pp at W3A3) is buried in the appendix. (3) The on-device table measures generic FP32→INT8 TensorRT speedup and has nothing to do with SQuaT; presenting it as "SQuaT-trained models provide substantial efficiency gains" is mild overclaiming. (4) Missing ablations: no multi-layer distillation study despite the formulation over a set K (all experiments use a single layer); no ablation on *which* layer; the λ_feat sweep covers exactly one configuration (CIFAR-100/ResNet-32 4-bit) and reports a 0.23 pp total spread across λ ∈ [10,100], which undercuts the decision to use a special λ setting only there; and no ablation removing L_logit to show feature distillation alone works.

## Strengths

- The core idea is simple, parameter-free, and cheap: no teacher-side quantizer to pretrain, unlike QFD, and it drops into any QAT pipeline that already learns quantization ranges.
- The projection-scheme ablation (Table 7) is the right experiment and is genuinely informative — the finding that teacher-side calibration underperforms even naive FP-target distillation is a useful negative result for the field.
- Breadth of settings is unusually good for a small-lab paper: CNN + ViT + BERT, vision + NLP, five quantizers, four distillation losses, four bit-widths, plus a real edge-device measurement.
- Gains concentrate exactly where the theory predicts (extreme low-bit, where teacher features most often fall outside the student's clipping range), which is a coherent theory-experiment link.
- Theorem statement and proof are short, correct, and honestly scoped to the loss rather than dressed up as an accuracy guarantee.

## Limitations

- Improvements on ImageNet-1K are within or near noise for ≥ 3 bits (+0.19 to +0.02 pp), so large-scale evidence is weak.
- QFD is never benchmarked as published; only a re-implementation used as an ablation arm.
- Only one distillation layer is actually used, leaving the multi-layer generalization of the framework untested.
- Table inconsistencies (61.12 vs 61.02) and a likely-wrong DeiT-Tiny CIFAR-100 FP number (97.33) reduce confidence in the reported ViT block.
- Seed count is unreported; several deltas are smaller than the stated standard deviations.
- No analysis of the coupling between φ_S and the target it defines (moving-target dynamics), nor any stability/collapse discussion.
- No LLM-scale or generative-model results — everything is classification-style, and the largest model is BERT-base.

## Relevance

如果关注**低比特模型压缩与端侧部署**，这篇给出了一个几乎零成本、可直接叠加到现有 QAT 流程上的技巧：把教师特征先过一遍学生的量化器再做 MSE 蒸馏，尤其在 1–2 bit 和无标签场景下值得一试。同时它对 **KD 目标可达性**的分析框架（"目标必须落在学生的可达集内"）可以迁移到其它 student-teacher 容量/表示空间不匹配的蒸馏问题上，比如稀疏化蒸馏或跨架构蒸馏。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Is the projected target f̃_T detached from the computation graph? If not, gradients flowing into φ_S through the target create a moving-target objective that could be minimized by shrinking the range — did the authors check that (a⁻, a⁺) does not degenerate?
- Since zero attainable residual is trivially achievable by degenerate targets, what prevents the feature loss from being satisfied by uninformative alignment? An experiment matching a *randomly quantized* teacher would be a useful negative control.
- Does the method still help when |K| > 1, i.e. distilling at several depths, where quantization lattices differ per layer?
- How does SQuaT compare to simply widening the student's learned clipping range, or to clipping/normalizing the FP teacher features to the student's range without discretization? That would isolate range mismatch from lattice discretization.
- Why does Teacher-aware projection underperform Non-aware so consistently — is it the calibration budget, or genuine lattice misalignment? An experiment sweeping the teacher-side calibration epochs would settle it.
- Do the large GLUE/RTE gains survive multiple fine-tuning seeds and a larger task suite (MNLI, CoLA), or are they small-dataset variance?
- Does the benefit persist for post-training-quantization-style or weight-only quantization of LLMs, where activation outliers are the dominant failure mode?
