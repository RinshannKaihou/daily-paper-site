> **一句话总结：** 该文用同一套 10,000 条 prompt 的回答（32 个模型、6 大家族），在句子级嵌入上构造三种互补的模型间距离（对齐的逐 prompt 平均距离、PCA 压缩的分歧得分、无需对齐的 Gromov–Wasserstein 差异），画出一张"行为地图"：模型家族自成簇（平均距离下 21/32 的最近邻同族）、gpt-2 是全局离群点（到其他模型平均距离 ≈88.5，而最近的一对 gpt-3.5-turbo↔gpt-4.1-mini 仅 ≈6.8）、跨家族距离随发布日期显著下降（斜率 −1.37e−02/天，r=−0.52，p≤0.0011），并用 token 级 MMD 交叉验证（与句子级平均距离 Spearman ρ=0.98）。

> **TL;DR:** Using one shared 10,000-prompt bank stratified over 13 public benchmarks (MMLU, ARC, BIG-Bench, GSM8K, MATH, TruthfulQA, HumanEval, ...), the authors embed the responses of 32 LLMs from 6 families and build three label-free cross-model dissimilarities. The aligned mean per-prompt pseudometric recovers family structure best (21/32 same-family nearest neighbours) versus the PCA-compressed variant (17/32) and the alignment-free Gromov–Wasserstein discrepancy (14/32, ρ=0.77 against the mean); an independent token-level MMD construction agrees at Spearman ρ=0.98 / Pearson 0.97 over all 496 model pairs and reproduces the same negative cross-family time trend (r≈−0.52, one-sided p≤0.0011), which also survives swapping the 8B Qwen3 encoder for a 73× smaller 110M all-mpnet-base-v2 (ρ=0.930, 19/32 same-family NN).

## 核心贡献

- **三种互补的句子级模型间差异度量**：(1) 保持 prompt 对齐的逐 prompt 欧氏距离均值 `D^mean`，作者证明它是观测嵌入矩阵上的伪度量（对"编码后回答完全相同"取商后成为度量，Proposition 1）；(2) 对每个模型对的 N 维逐 prompt 分歧向量做 rank-one PCA 得到的压缩得分 `D^pca`（不保证满足度量公理）；(3) 用余弦代价矩阵在模型自身回答云上做 Gromov–Wasserstein 最优耦合的 `D^gw`，不需要 prompt 对齐、对正交变换与正缩放不变。
- **一张 32 模型 × 6 家族的"行为地图"**：用 metric MDS / t-SNE / UMAP 可视化，并配合家族内累计漂移、逐代步长漂移、按家族+日期排序的距离热图、平均连接层次聚类、跨家族平均距离、以及模型自身回答云的离散度（`C_m` 的非对角均值）。
- **token 级独立交叉检验**：保留每条回答的 token 嵌入，用 RBF 核的有偏 plug-in 平方 MMD 逐 prompt 比较，得到 N×M×M 张量（约 40MB），其均值矩阵与句子级平均距离 ρ=0.98、28/32 模型的最近邻一致。
- **测度论视角 + 训练侧充分条件**：Theorem 1 证明两模型部署分布的期望 TV 距离 ≤ 有效目标分布差异 η + 两个 √(κ·ε/2) 学习误差项（Pinsker + 密度比覆盖条件 dξ/dP_X ≤ κ），并给出语义编码器下的 W1 版本（Corollary 1）与条件均值嵌入版本（Corollary 2）。
- **几何结构诊断**：四点缺陷（Gromov δ-hyperbolicity）归一化均值 0.015（句子与 token 一致），最大值 0.125–0.155，说明"平均近树、局部偏离树"；prompt 级 token-MMD 张量的首主成分解释 63% 的 prompt 间方差，前 5 个到 77%，前 10 个到 85%。

## Key Contributions

- A **label-free** pipeline for comparing LLMs purely from generated text — no gold labels, no weights, no activations — which is what makes the closed frontier models (GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.7, Gemini 3.1) measurable at all.
- Three sentence-level constructions that deliberately vary along **three axes**: representation (pooled response vector vs. token empirical measure), aggregation (mean vs. rank-one PCA), and alignment (fixed prompt indices vs. optimized GW coupling). Their high rank agreement is framed as a robustness check rather than a claim of mathematical equivalence.
- An **empirical characterization** of 32 models across GPT / Claude / Gemini / Qwen / Llama / Mistral, yielding three findings claimed to be stable across constructions: family coherence, decreasing cross-family distance over release date, and comparatively compact response clouds for several recent reasoning-oriented models.
- An **architecture-agnostic sufficient condition** (Theorem 1 and Corollaries 1–2) linking behavioral similarity to inference-prompt coverage, small excess population log-loss, and similar effective target distributions — explicitly presented as a *possible training-side account*, not an empirical explanation.
- **Encoder-robustness evidence**: all 32 × 10⁴ responses are re-encoded with bge-m3, multilingual-e5-large, and all-mpnet-base-v2 (110M, 768-d, ~73× smaller than Qwen3-Embedding-8B), none sharing a vendor with an evaluated model.

## Methodology

Every one of the 32 models answers the same canonical bank of N = 10,000 prompts, drawn as a stratified sample from 13 public benchmarks (BIG-Bench 877, ARC 877, MMLU 876, HellaSwag 876, WinoGrande 876, MATH 876, GSM8K 876, SQuAD 2.0 876, TriviaQA 876, Natural Questions 876, TruthfulQA 817, MBPP 257, HumanEval 164). The bank stores prompts only, with no gold labels. Generation is routed through OpenRouter (gpt-2 runs locally), one response per model–prompt pair under a single decoding configuration.

Each response is encoded with Qwen3-Embedding-8B (max 1024 input tokens, 4096-d output, mean-pooled over token positions, no L2 normalization), giving a row-aligned matrix `E_m ∈ R^{10000×4096}` per model. `D^mean` averages Euclidean per-prompt distances (Eq. 1); `D^pca` projects each pair's N-dimensional prompt-wise disagreement profile onto the leading principal direction (Eq. 2); `D^gw` solves the squared Gromov–Wasserstein objective (Eq. 3) with POT's conditional-gradient solver over per-model cosine cost matrices, using a common seeded subsample of **256 prompts** because full N×N costs are prohibitive.

The token-level cross-check keeps at most 256 tokens per response, uses an RBF kernel with global-median bandwidth, and applies the biased plug-in squared MMD (Eq. 5) uniformly to all response lengths — chosen because the unbiased U-statistic is undefined for one-token responses. Models are placed on a release-date axis (Table 3), with several dates marked approximate because they were post-cutoff or unannounced at run time; these affect only horizontal placement, not distances. Projections use metric MDS (SMACOF, 300 iters, 4 inits), t-SNE (perplexity 5), UMAP (n_neighbors 5, min_dist 0.1), all with `metric=precomputed`, seed 0.

## Results & Highlights

- **Family coherence, ranked by construction.** Under `D^mean`, **21/32** models have a same-family nearest neighbour; the PCA-compressed score gives **17/32** and GW only **14/32**. Spearman correlation with `D^mean` over the 32-choose-2 = **496** distinct pairs: PCA **ρ=0.92**, GW **ρ=0.77**, token MMD **ρ=0.98** (Pearson 0.97); GW correlates no higher than **0.83** with any other construction (Table 5).
- **Outliers and closest pair.** `gpt-2` has the largest average distance to all other models (**≈88.5**) and is the global outlier; the closest pair is `gpt-3.5-turbo`↔`gpt-4.1-mini` at `D^mean` **≈6.8**, a ~13× gap. Ordering the matrix by family and date exposes two high-distance rows: `gpt-2` and `qwen-2.5-72b-instruct`, which the average-linkage dendrogram splits off first.
- **Convergence trend.** Mean cross-family distance falls with release date at slope **−1.37e−02/day** (within-family **−1.80e−02/day**), correlation **r = −0.522** with one-sided **p ≤ 0.0011**. The `gpt-2 → gpt-3.5-turbo` step drift dwarfs every later step; 2024–2026 step distances are smaller and broadly comparable across families.
- **Token-level agreement.** The mean token-MMD matrix matches the sentence mean at **ρ=0.98 / Pearson 0.97**, with **28/32** models sharing a nearest neighbour under both constructions, and reproduces the gpt-2 and qwen-2.5-72b outliers plus the downward cross-family trend (Fig. 16).
- **Encoder robustness (Table 7).** Against the Qwen3-Embedding-8B reference (21/32 same-family NN, trend r=−0.522): bge-m3 **ρ=0.933, 21/32, r=−0.521**; multilingual-e5-large **ρ=0.922, 22/32, r=−0.551**; all-mpnet-base-v2 (110M, ~73× smaller) **ρ=0.930, 19/32, r=−0.522**. All four encoders return the same two global outliers.
- **Geometry diagnostics.** Normalized four-point defect: mean **0.015** for both sentence and token matrices, maximum **0.125** (sentence, diameter 111.8) and **0.155** (token, diameter 0.317). The prompt-wise token-MMD tensor is highly compressible: PC1 explains **63%** of prompt-to-prompt variance, rising to 69% / 72% / **77%** / **85%** at ranks 2 / 3 / 5 / 10.
- **Dispersion.** Several recent reasoning-oriented models — `claude-opus-4-7-think`, `gpt-5.2` — have lower response-cloud dispersion (mean off-diagonal cosine dissimilarity, roughly 0.40–0.54 across the panel) than some mid-generation predecessors.

## Authors & Credibility

Dong Qiao, Chris Ding, and Jicong Fan (corresponding) are all at the School of Data Science, CUHK-Shenzhen. Chris Ding is a well-known figure in matrix factorization / spectral clustering; Jicong Fan publishes regularly on subspace clustering, low-rank recovery, and kernel/OT methods. The technical framing — pseudometrics, quotient spaces, Gromov–Wasserstein, MMD, Pinsker-based transfer bounds — is squarely in this group's wheelhouse and is executed carefully, with unusually explicit qualifications about when each construction is or is not a formal metric. The paper is formatted as an AAAI-style submission; no conference acceptance is claimed in the text.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** The individual ingredients — response embeddings, MDS maps, MMD, GW — are all standard. The novelty is the *comparison design*: deliberately varying representation, aggregation, and alignment and showing the induced rank geometry is stable. Prior work is close: LLM DNA (Wu et al., ICLR 2026) already builds a behavioral phylogeny from responses, and PhyloLM (Yax et al., ICLR 2025) infers LLM phylogeny. The paper distinguishes itself by retaining pairwise geometry and covering closed frontier models rather than mostly open-weight ones.

**Rigor — the central gap.** *No prior method is ever run on this 32-model panel.* LLM DNA and PhyloLM are discussed in Related Work but never used as an empirical baseline, so there is no evidence the three proposed constructions recover family structure better than an off-the-shelf n-gram or response-overlap similarity. The de facto "baselines" are the paper's own weaker constructions (GW at 14/32 same-family NN), which is self-referential.

**Specific weaknesses worth naming:**
- **The time trend rests on a leverage point.** `gpt-2` is dated 2019-02-14, sits ~13× further from everything than the closest pair, and anchors the earliest end of a 32-point regression. No leave-`gpt-2`-out (or leave-`gpt-3.5-turbo`-out) refit of the r=−0.52 convergence trend is reported. Given that the authors themselves say the gpt-2→gpt-3.5 step "dwarfs modern steps" and that 2024–2026 steps are "broadly comparable," the convergence claim may be almost entirely a pre-2023 vs. post-2023 contrast rather than a continuous trend.
- **Half the release dates are guessed.** In Table 3, every date for gpt-5.2, claude-sonnet-4-5-think onward, gemini-3-flash/3.1-pro, qwen3.5/3.6/3.7, mistral-medium-3.1 and mistral-large-2512 is asterisked as approximate (post-cutoff or unannounced). The headline finding is a *time trend*, and the x-axis for the newest ~14 models is partly imputed to month granularity.
- **One sample per model–prompt pair.** Distances therefore mix genuine model difference with decoding stochasticity, and no repeated-sampling or temperature ablation quantifies that floor. This directly weakens Eq. (4)'s interpretation, which the paper notes differs from the distance between conditional means unless within-prompt variability is negligible — exactly the quantity never measured.
- **The reference encoder is the odd one out.** The three alternative encoders agree with *each other* at ρ=0.99 but only 0.92–0.93 with Qwen3-Embedding-8B, which is the encoder used for every main-text figure. The authors flag this honestly, but it means the main results come from the least-typical encoder in the panel. Two of the alternatives also truncate at 512 tokens (4.5% of responses affected), a confound they acknowledge.
- **Response length is an uncontrolled nuisance variable.** The plug-in MMD's bias depends on response length (median 74–80 tokens), and no length-matched or length-regressed analysis rules out "recent models converge" being partly "recent models converge in verbosity and markdown formatting." Since the bank has no labels, nothing separates semantic convergence from stylistic/format convergence — arguably the single most plausible alternative explanation for the entire headline.
- **GW is undersampled.** It uses 256 of 10,000 prompts (2.6%), is the least concordant construction, and is nonetheless cited as corroborating evidence when the cross-vendor pairings it produces (gemini-3.1-pro ↔ qwen3.7-max, an Opus-class Claude ↔ gpt-5.2) are exactly the cases where noise would matter most.
- **The theory is decorative.** Theorem 1 is a triangle inequality on TV plus Pinsker plus a change-of-measure bound. Every quantity on the right-hand side (κ, ε, η) is unobserved for closed models, so the bound is empirically vacuous. The authors say so explicitly — commendable honesty, but it means Section 6 does not support Section 4.

**Reproducibility (weak).** No code or data release is mentioned anywhere in the paper. Hyperparameters are documented well (seeds, solver settings, batch sizes, bandwidth choice), but reproducing this requires re-generating 320,000 responses from closed APIs — including model versions (gpt-5.2, claude-opus-4-7-think) whose availability and behavior will drift. The `gpt-2` outlier and family blocks would probably replicate; the exact slopes almost certainly would not.

**Significance (real but bounded).** As a descriptive instrument — a label-free way to ask "how different is this new release from its predecessor and from its competitors?" — it is genuinely useful, especially for closed models where CKA/SAE-based comparisons are impossible. As evidence in the Platonic-Representation-Hypothesis debate it is weaker than it first appears, because output convergence under a fixed benchmark-derived prompt bank is exactly what shared RLHF conventions and shared benchmark contamination would also produce.

**Red flags:** no external baseline; convergence claim not stress-tested against its most obvious confound (style/format) or its most obvious leverage point (gpt-2); imputed dates on the axis carrying the headline result; no released artifacts.

## Strengths

- Unusually candid mathematical bookkeeping: the paper repeatedly states where a construction is *not* a metric (PCA compression, squared MMD, GW over cosine costs) instead of pretending uniformity.
- The encoder-swap experiment is the right ablation and is done at full scale (all 32 × 10⁴ responses re-encoded, not a subsample), including a 73× smaller model with no vendor overlap — which neatly defuses the "a Qwen encoder favors Qwen models" objection, since every encoder still flags `qwen-2.5-72b-instruct` as an outlier.
- Two genuinely independent representations (pooled sentence vector vs. token empirical measure) agreeing at ρ=0.98 with 28/32 shared nearest neighbours is a strong internal-consistency result.
- Limitations section names the real problems (generation variability, 256-prompt GW subsample, observational trends, unobserved training-side quantities) rather than hiding them.
- Engineering detail is practical: streaming token embeddings so only a ~40 MB tensor hits disk, caching per-response embeddings so any distance can be recomputed without re-encoding.

## Limitations

- Behavioral similarity is explicitly *not* correctness; the maps say nothing about capability, and cross-vendor GW pairings are warned against as capability comparisons.
- Absolute distances depend on prompt bank, encoder, and decoding configuration; only the rank geometry is claimed to be stable.
- The 32-model panel is not exhaustive (no DeepSeek, no Grok, no open reasoning models like R1-class systems), and family sizes are unbalanced (Claude 8 models vs. Gemini 4).
- Release-date analyses are purely observational — no causal mechanism for convergence is identified, and the paper says the distance alone cannot attribute drift to data, training, or decoding changes.
- Theorem 1's quantities are unobservable for the closed models studied, so the theory is a sufficient scenario, not an explanation.
- Deterministic greedy decoding would need an extra stability condition (a positive probability margin) for the distributional corollaries to transfer.

## Relevance

如果你关注 LLM 评测方法论、模型指纹/血缘追踪、或"前沿模型是否趋同"这一议题（Platonic Representation Hypothesis 之争），这篇提供了一套无需标签、无需权重的输出侧几何工具，对闭源模型尤其可用。其 OT/MMD/伪度量的构造方式与表示相似性度量（CKA、GW）的工程实践也可直接迁移到自己的模型对比实验中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the r = −0.52 convergence trend survive dropping `gpt-2` and `gpt-3.5-turbo`? If the slope collapses within the 2024–2026 window, "frontier homogenization" becomes a much narrower claim.
2. How much of the cross-family distance is explained by surface style — response length, markdown density, hedging boilerplate? A partial correlation controlling for length, or a comparison on length-normalized / style-stripped responses, would separate semantic from format convergence.
3. With repeated sampling (say 5 responses per model–prompt), what is the within-model noise floor of `D^mean`, and how does it compare to the ~6.8 distance of the closest observed pair? Any pair closer than the noise floor is behaviorally indistinguishable.
4. Do LLM DNA or PhyloLM distances, run on this same 32-model panel, produce a different dendrogram or a different time trend — and if so, which construction should be believed?
5. Is `qwen-2.5-72b-instruct`'s outlier status a genuine behavioral property or an artifact of refusal/formatting behavior on a specific benchmark slice? A per-benchmark decomposition of its distance profile would settle this.
6. Can the low-rank structure (PC1 = 63% of prompt-to-prompt variance) be exploited to select a few hundred high-leverage prompts that reproduce the full 10k-prompt geometry? The paper suggests this but does not evaluate prompt selection.
7. Does GW's cross-vendor pairing of recent models persist when the subsample grows from 256 to, say, 2,048 prompts, or is it subsample noise?
