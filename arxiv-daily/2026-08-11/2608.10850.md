> **一句话总结：** 作者对 OLMo 2 系列（1B/7B/13B/32B）做了系统的 SVD 频谱解剖，发现继续预训练（CPT）几乎不改变权重矩阵的奇异值谱、适应主要由奇异向量旋转承载，并据此提出"缩放 Frobenius 范数差"的注意力头重要性判据——回滚约 15% 的低重要性头即可让 7B 数学 CPT 模型的 GSM8K 提升约 4%，回滚 60% 的头或截断 CPT delta 中 50%（7B）/70%（13B）的奇异值均无明显掉点。

> **TL;DR:** Using SVD on OLMo 2 checkpoints (1B/7B/13B/32B), the paper shows continual pre-training leaves singular *value* spectra invariant (transplanting pre-train spectra into the CPT model leaves GSM8K unchanged) while adaptation lives in singular *vectors*, then turns the resulting "head heterogeneity" into a ranking criterion: rewinding ~15% of heads gains up to +4% GSM8K on 7B math CPT (+6.3% on the released OLMo 2 13B CPT checkpoint, +3.6% on 32B), and its AUC-diff of 6.0 on 7B beats the greedy oracle ordering's 5.0 and random's 0.0; 60% of head updates or 50–70% of CPT-delta singular values can be discarded without measurable loss.

## 核心贡献

- **谱不变性**：CPT 阶段权重矩阵的奇异值谱几乎不变，把预训练模型的奇异值"移植"回 CPT 模型（保留 CPT 的 U、V）后 GSM8K 精度不变；真正承载领域适应的是奇异向量的旋转，且变化集中在谱峰附近的窄频带。
- **预训练阶段的复杂谱结构**：随预训练 token 预算增加（20B→100B→400B→4T），注意力头矩阵的谱从 Marchenko–Pastur 体 → 幂律尾 → 出现多峰与离群值，明显偏离 heavy-tailed self-regularization (HTSR) 理论；而 MLP 层始终基本符合幂律尾模型。作者据此论证"幂律尾的形成是模型质量提升的必要而非充分条件"。
- **Head heterogeneity（头异质性）**：长预训练模型中，部分注意力头在所有 CPT 领域都大幅变化，另一部分只对特定领域变化；20B 预训练的小模型则表现为均匀变化。这一现象随预训练预算增大而增强。
- **头重要性判据与部分回滚**：以 text CPT 作为参考域，用 `scale[0,1](||W_domain − W_pretrain||_F) − scale[0,1](||W_ref − W_pretrain||_F)` 对头排序，选择性地把低重要性头回滚到预训练状态，反而能提升下游精度。
- **CPT delta 的低秩冗余**：模型越大冗余越多（7B 可截断 50% 奇异值、13B 可截断 70% 而不掉点），但冗余度并不随预训练 token 预算单调增加。
- **Domain connectivity（领域连通性）**：不同领域 CPT 检查点之间的线性插值（model soup）质量随预训练预算与模型规模从凹（20B）→线性（400B）→凸（4T/5T）演化。
- **开源工具 Diffract**：面向十亿参数级模型的可扩展谱分析库，外加完整的 pre-train/CPT 训练代码与 OLMES 评测命令。

## Key Contributions

- A systematic SVD-based dissection of the continual pre-training (CPT) stage — the least-studied stage of the modern LLM pipeline relative to SFT — across OLMo 2 1B, 7B, 13B and 32B, and four CPT domains (math, instruct, code, text).
- The empirical decoupling of *spectrum* from *direction*: CPT preserves singular value spectra (verified causally by singular-value transplantation), so the adaptation signal is carried by singular vectors localized near spectral peaks.
- Identification and quantification of **head heterogeneity**, plus a cheap, training-free head importance criterion (difference in scaled Frobenius norms of CPT deltas, measured against a text-CPT reference domain) that outperforms both spectral heuristics and a greedy leave-one-head-out oracle at 7B scale.
- Demonstration of **domain connectivity**: linear interpolation between differently-adapted CPT checkpoints degrades smoothly (and eventually convexly) rather than catastrophically, with the caveat that soups still lose to CPT on data mixtures.
- An open-source toolkit (Diffract) plus a separate training repo, covering norms, stable/effective rank, MP and power-law fits with KS goodness-of-fit, and singular vector agreement.

## Methodology

Models follow the OLMo 2 architecture and training stack (SwiGLU, RoPE θ=500k, QK-norm, RMSNorm, post-norm, z-loss, AdamW β=(0.9, 0.95), wd 0.1, bf16). Stage 1 pre-trains 1B models from scratch on DCLM samples at 20B/100B/400B token budgets; larger checkpoints (1B/7B at 4T, 13B at 5T, 32B at 6T) are taken from the OLMo team. Stage 2 runs CPT starting from the pre-train LR endpoint (4e-5) linearly annealed to zero, over six mixture families: DolminoMath-only (MATH), balanced DCLM+DolminoMath, DCLM-heavy replay (TEXT), FLAN + StackExchange (INST), FLAN+DolminoMath, and StarCoder (CODE). CPT budget is 20B tokens for most runs (10B for 13B), chosen because 1B math accuracy plateaus at 20B.

The analysis toolkit computes, per weight matrix and per attention head: Frobenius/spectral norms, stable rank ‖W‖²_F/‖W‖²₂, effective rank (entropy of the normalized spectrum), Marchenko–Pastur bulk-edge estimation via a randomization-and-rescaling heuristic, power-law exponent α by MLE with Kolmogorov–Smirnov goodness-of-fit, and **singular vector agreement** A_u = |Uᵢᵀ Uⱼ| (reported as diagonal, row-maximum, and matrix-level mean). Attention projections are split per head before any metric is computed — this granularity is what surfaces head heterogeneity.

Two intervention experiments convert the descriptive analysis into causal claims. (1) **Head-wise rewind**: order all heads by a scalar criterion, then incrementally reset heads to their pre-train weights and re-evaluate. Candidate orderings include random, a greedy leave-one-out "ground truth", PL-KS distance and PL exponent α of the pre-train checkpoint, delta Frobenius norm, mean singular vector agreement, and the proposed difference-in-scaled-Frobenius-norms score s_{l,h}. Curve separation is summarized by AUC-diff, the trapezoidal area between the descending- and ascending-order curves. (2) **CPT delta SVD truncation**: compute SVD of ΔW = W_domain − W_pretrain per attention head and MLP matrix, zero the smallest singular values, and evaluate W_pretrain + ΔW̃. Evaluation uses OLMES: 5-shot ARC-Easy/HellaSwag/WinoGrande for "language", 8-shot GSM8K and 4-shot MATH-500 for math, DROP/SQuAD for reading comprehension, HumanEval for code. An 80% bootstrap over 5 seeds quantifies evaluation noise, and head-selection RNG is re-seeded up to 5 times.

## Results & Highlights

- **Head rewind gains, 7B math CPT**: rewinding roughly 15% of heads by the proposed criterion improves GSM8K by **up to +4 percentage points** over the fully-trained CPT baseline (Fig. 4b), and up to **60% of heads** can be rewound with no significant drop. The same protocol on the *released* OLMo 2 checkpoints gives **+6.3% GSM8K on 13B** and **+3.6% on 32B** (Fig. 18), with the 32B case reportedly improving even at complete head rewind. On the authors' own 13B (5T pre-train, 10B CPT) the gain is **+2.2% on MATH-500**, and over 60% of heads rewind with no SQuAD F1 loss (Fig. 17).
- **Criterion vs. baselines (Table 4, AUC-diff, higher = better ordering)**: on 7B, difference-in-scaled-Frobenius-norms scores **6.0** vs. greedy oracle **5.0**, agreement-difference 5.1, delta Frobenius norm 4.9, mean agreement 4.5, PL-KS **1.1**, random **0.0**. On 1B the greedy oracle still wins (**19.1** vs. 11.0 for the proposed metric and 11.7 for agreement-difference), i.e. the proposed criterion only beats greedy at the larger scale. Purely spectral pre-train heuristics (PL-KS at 0.9/1.1) are barely better than random.
- **CPT delta compressibility (Fig. 5)**: 7B tolerates removing **50%** of CPT-delta singular values with no measurable GSM8K drop (pronounced degradation only past 80%); 13B tolerates **70%** (degradation past 90%). By contrast, 1B models lose **≈10% relative GSM8K** at only **30%** truncation, and this is true for both the 100B- and 4T-token pre-trained 1B models — so redundancy scales with parameters, not with pre-train tokens.
- **Spectrum is inert**: transplanting pre-train singular values into the math-CPT model leaves GSM8K accuracy unchanged (Fig. 15); shuffling only the MP bulk singular values causes no significant loss, while shuffling the full spectrum collapses quality to near zero — a clean control separating "bulk = noise" from "outliers = signal".
- **CPT quality dynamics (Fig. 1)**: the authors' 1B 20B-token math CPT reaches higher GSM8K than the released OLMo 2 0425 1B checkpoint (which used a 50B CPT with 20% math), and math accuracy plateaus by 20B CPT tokens. Longer pre-training yields better post-CPT math despite indistinguishable pre-CPT math.
- **Domain connectivity (Fig. 6)**: math↔instruct and math↔text interpolation curves are concave at 20B pre-train, roughly linear at 400B, mildly convex at 4T (1B), and clearly convex at 4T (7B) and 5T (13B). However, interpolants **consistently underperform CPT trained directly on the corresponding data mixture** across all budgets and sizes.
- **Evaluation noise (Table 5)**: 7B GSM8K 0.698 ± 0.005 and DROP F1 0.437 ± 0.015; 1B GSM8K 0.534 ± 0.009, DROP F1 0.232 ± 0.006 over 5 bootstrap seeds.
- **Code domain is a negative result**: 1B HumanEval pass@1 goes from **0.04 → 0.09** after StarCoder CPT, which the authors themselves call a limited improvement attributable to OLMo 2 needing SFT with chat templates before HumanEval becomes meaningful.

## Authors & Credibility

All nine authors are affiliated with Risk AI Research Lab (one additionally with the Applied AI Institute); correspondence goes to Dmitry Vinichenko. This is an industrial/independent lab rather than a well-known academic group, and the author list carries no marquee names in the spectral-analysis or CPT literature. Credibility instead rests on the artifacts: the paper is formatted for ICML 2026 (PMLR 306, Seoul), releases two repositories (`Risk-AI-Research/diffract` for analysis and `Risk-AI-Research/diffract-training` for pre-train/CPT/OLMES commands), and builds entirely on the fully open OLMo 2 stack with published data mixes (DCLM, DolminoMath, FLAN, StackExchange, StarCoder) and documented hyperparameters (Tables 1–3). Training 1B models from scratch at 20B–400B tokens plus 20B-token CPT runs at 7B and 13B is a non-trivial compute commitment for a small lab, and the fact that key results replicate on OLMo's own released 13B/32B CPT checkpoints — which the authors did not train — is the strongest independent-verification signal in the paper.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-to-good).** The individual ingredients are not new — HTSR/random-matrix analysis of weight spectra (Martin & Mahoney), spectral dynamics during training (Yunis et al.), linear mode connectivity and model soups, delta sparsification à la DARE/TIES — but applying them jointly to the *CPT* stage, at head granularity, and finding that the spectrum is causally inert while singular vectors carry the adaptation, is a genuinely useful negative-plus-positive result. "Head heterogeneity" and "domain connectivity" are new names for phenomena that are plausibly implicit in prior merging work, so the terminological framing is somewhat inflated.

**Rigor (mixed).** The singular-value transplantation and bulk-shuffle ablations are exactly the right controls, and reporting 5-seed bootstrap variance (GSM8K std 0.005–0.009) makes the +4% claim comfortably larger than eval noise. But several weak points stand out. First, the flagship "+4%" is a *single-point maximum on a rewind curve* — the curve is scanned over rewind fractions and the best point is reported, which is a selection-on-the-test-set procedure with no held-out validation split for choosing the rewind fraction; the same objection applies to the +6.3%/+3.6% numbers on 13B/32B. Second, the proposed criterion requires an extra **reference-domain text CPT run**, i.e. its cost is a whole additional training run, and this cost is never priced against the "greedy" oracle it is compared to; the comparison is therefore not compute-matched. Third, Table 4 shows the proposed metric *loses* to greedy at 1B (11.0 vs. 19.1) and only wins at 7B (6.0 vs. 5.0) — with a single number per cell and no error bars, a 6.0-vs-5.0 gap on one model is thin evidence for "surpasses greedy baseline". Fourth, the strongest per-domain result is math/GSM8K; on instruct (7B DROP, Fig. 16a) the paper concedes "rewinding heads according to any heuristic has only a minor effect", and the code domain is effectively a failed experiment (0.04 → 0.09 pass@1 on a benchmark the authors admit is uninformative for base models). Benchmark selection thus leans on the one domain where the effect is large.

**Reproducibility (strong).** Two public repos, the fully open OLMo 2 base, exact mixture tables, LRs, batch sizes, and OLMES eval commands. The main barrier is compute, not information. The AUC-diff metric is defined explicitly (trapezoidal, Eq. 17–18), though it is a nonstandard summary whose absolute scale is hard to interpret across models — note AUC-diff values are systematically ~4x larger at 1B than 7B for every method, which suggests the metric is not normalized across scales and cross-model comparisons of it should be avoided.

**Significance (moderate).** If the head importance criterion holds up, it is a nearly free post-hoc improvement to any CPT pipeline, and the 50–70% delta-truncation result has direct implications for storing and serving many domain adapters. The domain-connectivity finding is arguably the most consequential *negative* claim in the paper: since interpolants always lose to mixture training, the authors explicitly warn that DARE-style task-vector merging will struggle for CPT — a useful corrective to merging hype.

**Red flags.** (a) Everything is one model family and one optimizer (OLMo 2 + AdamW); the authors flag this themselves, but "consistent across 1B/7B/13B/32B" reads as broader robustness than "four checkpoints from the same recipe" warrants. (b) The claim that complex spectral structure *causes* better adaptability is repeatedly labeled a hypothesis, yet the abstract's framing invites a causal reading. (c) Not all phenomena are measured at all scales — head rewind at 32B uses only released checkpoints, truncation at 13B uses only GSM8K/DROP — so the scale trends are assembled from partially overlapping evidence. (d) No ablation isolates whether the reference-domain subtraction in Eq. 1 matters versus simply using the raw delta Frobenius norm at a better-tuned rewind fraction; Table 4 shows raw delta norm at 4.9 vs. 6.0, close enough that this ablation is genuinely needed.

## Strengths

- Causal controls, not just correlations: singular-value transplantation and bulk-vs-outlier shuffling directly test the "spectrum is inert" hypothesis instead of inferring it from curves.
- Head-level rather than matrix-level granularity, which is what makes the heterogeneity phenomenon visible at all.
- Replication on checkpoints the authors did not train (released OLMo 2 13B and 32B CPT models) substantially de-risks the head-rewind result.
- Honest reporting of failures: the code-domain result, the instruct-domain flatness, and the "soups lose to mixtures" finding are all stated plainly rather than buried.
- Explicit uncertainty quantification for both evaluation noise and head-selection RNG.
- Genuinely useful released tooling with a documented five-stage visualization pipeline (box plots, cluster bars, sparklines, agreement maps, RMT fits).

## Limitations

- Single model family (OLMo 2) and single optimizer recipe (AdamW with the OLMo 2 schedule); no Llama/Qwen/Mistral validation, no Muon/Shampoo/second-order ablation.
- Benchmark coverage is narrow: GSM8K and MATH-500 for math, DROP/SQuAD for comprehension, three cloze-style tasks for "language". No MMLU, no long-context, no held-out reasoning suite; base-model cloze evaluation may not track post-SFT behavior at all.
- The rewind fraction and the reference domain are hyperparameters chosen post hoc on the evaluated benchmark; no validation split.
- The proposed criterion needs an extra text-domain CPT run as reference, so it is not free.
- 1B-scale conclusions frequently invert the 7B/13B ones (delta is high-rank, greedy wins, curves are concave), so the practical recipe applies only to sufficiently large models with sufficiently long pre-training — the paper does not identify where the crossover is.
- Domain connectivity is only characterized qualitatively (concave/linear/convex) with no scalar measure of the interpolation barrier, making the "improves with scale" claim hard to falsify.
- Code CPT is preliminary by the authors' own description and contributes essentially no evidence.

## Relevance

如果关注 LLM 训练动力学、领域自适应与模型合并（task arithmetic / model soup / delta 压缩），这篇是少见的把 CPT 阶段的权重变化在注意力头粒度上讲清楚的实证工作，"谱不变、向量旋转"与"delta 可截断 50–70%"两条结论对多领域适配器的存储与服务有直接工程价值。其"插值永远打不过混合数据训练"的结论也为 DARE/TIES 一类合并方法在 CPT 场景的适用性划出了边界。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the head importance criterion transfer to non-OLMo families (Qwen3, Llama 3/4) and to non-AdamW optimizers, or is head heterogeneity partly an artifact of QK-norm + post-norm + the OLMo LR schedule?
- Can the rewind fraction be selected on a held-out split without touching GSM8K, and does the +4% survive that? What happens if the rewind fraction is fixed a priori at, say, 15% across all domains and scales?
- Is the reference-domain subtraction in Eq. 1 necessary? A direct ablation against the raw delta Frobenius norm at matched rewind fractions would settle whether the gain comes from the contrast or merely from magnitude ranking.
- Do the +4%/+6.3% gains survive downstream SFT/RLHF, or does subsequent instruction tuning wash out the rewind benefit — and correspondingly, does head rewind change post-SFT HumanEval where base-model CPT did not?
- What is the mechanistic identity of the domain-specific outlier heads? Do they coincide with known circuit-level structures (induction heads, retrieval heads), and can they be predicted before CPT rather than after?
- Why does CPT-delta redundancy scale with parameter count but not with pre-train token budget? A width-vs-depth decomposition would test whether this is about head dimension or layer count.
- Given that soups underperform mixture-trained models everywhere, is there a spectrally-informed merge (e.g., merging only the singular directions near spectral peaks, or Procrustes-aligned merging) that closes the gap?
- Does the transition from concave to convex interpolation curves have a measurable threshold in the attention-head spectral complexity statistics, which would turn the current hypothesis into a testable predictor?
