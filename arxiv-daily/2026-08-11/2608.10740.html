Key Finding

Mining convergent gaps across branching citation evolution trees produces research ideas that LLM judges rate equal to real published papers.

Tree-of-Ideas: Automated Research Ideation via Cross-Trajectory Reasoning over Scholarly Evolution
Xun Li, Yiying Yang, Pengtao Li et al.  ·  Guangdong Lab of AI & Digital Economy (SZ) · Melbourne · Sydney · NTU · Tsinghua · Tencent Youtu
Key Metric
6.27/10
LLM-judge score — within 0.02 of real published papers (6.29) and +0.91 over the strongest baseline, ResearchAgent (5.36).
Why This Matters

Two portable primitives — gap-status edge annotations and the ≥2-evidence provenance rule — for any ideation agent; plus a sharp warning that LLM judges flipped the method ranking three places versus human experts.

Critical Edge

Human evaluators invert the headline: CoI-Agent — 4th under LLM judges — beats ToI 6.71 to 6.62, an unquantified 0.09 gap the abstract never mentions.

Methodology — distilled

pipeline: (topic q, literature L) → graph Gq → evolution tree Tr → ideas I
  1. EvoGraph. Retrieve seed papers for q; expand 3 hops backward along citations (top-30 leaf expansion, ≤30 refs/node), prune topic drift & surveys. A frontier router scores depth/reach/branching/span to keep the k=5 most recent frontier papers that each have an ancestral subgraph to reason over.
  2. EvoTrace. For each parent→child edge infer the mechanism delta + gap-status transition — what the child silently inherited and what it failed to close — using a fixed taxonomy (4 gap types × 6 evolution directions). Chaining edges yields a branching tree whose every edge carries explicit gap-evolution semantics.
  3. EvoAgent. Scan four cross-trajectory signals — convergent gap, convergent action, capability–gap bridge, frontier race — under a hard ≥2-evidence-unit provenance rule. Then run an anti-obviousness gate (rejects "method X + method Y on new domain Z"), novelty check against broader literature, and embedding dedup to emit structured method-paper proposals.

Setup: DeepSeek-V4-Flash backbone shared by all automatic methods; 6 topics × 5 ideas = 30 ideas per method; LLM-judge ensemble of GPT-5.5 / Claude Opus 4.7 / Grok 4.3 plus 5 human experts.

Strengths

  • Gap-centered edge annotation (what a paper silently inherited & failed to close) is a genuinely better unit of analysis — backed by the −0.72 w/o-Gap ablation.
  • The ≥2-evidence provenance rule is checkable grounding, and plausibly explains the Groundedness jump (7.00 vs 3.83 for Direct Prompting).
  • All baselines share the backbone & literature space — rules out the most common confound in this literature.
  • The diffusion case study is auditable: a named frontier paper, two branches, an identified shared assumption, a concrete proposal (NF-Diff).
  • Reports the unfavorable human-eval result and lower Feasibility in the body; appendix-level prompt & hyperparameter disclosure above the norm.

Limitations

  • Evaluation stops at the idea stage: no implementation, no experiments — "6.27 vs 5.36" measures judged plausibility, not research value (authors cite Si et al. 2025 on post-execution score drops).
  • Seeds restricted to post-May-2025 top-venue ML papers — interdisciplinary, non-English, sparsely-cited areas untested.
  • 30 ideas/method, single backbone (DeepSeek-V4-Flash), single judge ensemble, no variance or significance tests.
  • No cost/latency analysis for a per-citation-edge LLM pipeline — plausibly an order of magnitude pricier than RAG.
  • "Cross-trajectory" may reduce to cross-branch within one tree: appendix states k=1 is the default ideation mode; no k-sweep is reported.
  • Non-obviousness gate & anti-pattern list are un-ablated guardrails tuned on the same six eval topics.

Open questions for further exploration

from the analysis
  1. What is the actual default k? A k=1 vs k=3 vs k=5 sweep would settle whether "cross-trajectory" or "cross-branch" is doing the work — the single most important missing experiment.
  2. Why does CoI-Agent jump from 5.25 (LLM, 4th) to 6.71 (human, 1st)? Per-judge scores and judge–human correlation per dimension would reveal whether the ensemble over-rewards ToI's explicit provenance formatting.
  3. How much Novelty comes from the non-obviousness gate vs evolution tracing? Removing those prompts from ToI and adding them to RAG/CoI baselines would isolate it.
  4. What is the cost curve — LLM calls, tokens, wall-clock per idea vs RAG and ResearchAgent — and does the quality-per-dollar ordering survive?
  5. Do any of the 30 ideas (e.g. NF-Diff) survive implementation? Pair with an MLR-Bench- / ideation–execution-gap-style follow-up.
  6. Does the gap taxonomy transfer outside recent ML venues — to fields with slower citation dynamics or weaker Semantic Scholar coverage?
  7. Does the embedding diversity filter suppress genuinely distinct ideas that share vocabulary, and what is the retention rate at each review/novelty/dedup stage?
