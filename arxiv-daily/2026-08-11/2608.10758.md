> **一句话总结：** 作者把语言模型可解释性里的 activation patching / causal tracing 搬到 VLM 的视觉编码器上，用因果贡献 Γ 与 token 图像块和目标框的 IoU 做相关性分析，发现在 CLIP（100 个样本，Corr(Γ, IoU)=0.062）以及 DeepSeek-VL、Qwen-2.5、LLaVA-1.5/NeXT、InternVL、SmolVLM 上相关性一律接近 0（DDPM 与 blur 两种破坏方式下大多在 −0.02 ~ 0.09 之间），即"因果上最重要的视觉 token 往往根本不在目标物体所在区域"，说明多模态性能强并不等于表征空间对齐。

> **TL;DR:** This work-in-progress paper adapts causal tracing (activation patching) to the ViT vision encoder of VLMs, defining a per-(layer, token) causal contribution Γ = (s_patch − s_corr)/(s_clean − s_corr) on image–text similarity, and correlating it with the IoU between each token's image patch and the referring-expression ground-truth box. On 100 referring-expression samples the CLIP baseline yields Corr(Γ, IoU) = 0.062, and the same near-zero pattern holds for six larger VLMs under both DDPM and blur corruption (e.g., Qwen-2.5: 0.0912 ± 0.0422 DDPM vs 0.0294 ± 0.0100 blur; LLaVA-NeXT even flips to −0.0217 ± 0.0052 under blur), while individual tokens can be strongly causal yet spatially irrelevant (Γ = 0.718 with IoU = 0.049). A companion string-tracing probe with Gemini-3.1 Pro shows models trace strings when colors differ but fail when all strings share a color — reported qualitatively only, with no accuracy numbers.

## 核心贡献

- **把 causal tracing 迁移到视觉编码器内部。** 以往 VLM 的因果追踪主要落在语言侧或跨模态融合层；本文对 ViT 的中间激活做 clean / corrupted / patched 三次前向，直接测量单个 (layer, token) 对最终 image-text 相似度的因果恢复率 Γ。
- **提出"因果重要性 vs. 空间对齐"的联合度量框架。** 把每个 vision token 映射回其固定图像 patch，用与 referring expression 真值框的 IoU 表征空间相关性，然后计算 Corr(Γ, IoU)，并逐层拆解成 Corr_l，用于判断空间 grounding 是否随层数逐渐涌现。
- **给出跨模型、跨破坏方式的一致负结果。** 在 CLIP、DeepSeek-VL、Qwen-2.5、LLaVA-1.5、LLaVA-NeXT、InternVL-2、SmolVLM 上，无论用 DDPM 加噪还是模糊，Corr(Γ, IoU) 都停留在弱相关区间，且多数模型跑了 6–10 个随机种子，结论对种子稳健。
- **区分"没有强因果 token"与"强因果 token 不在目标上"两种解释。** 论文强调低相关不是因为缺乏因果 token：Qwen-2.5 在 blur 下 Top-1 最大 Γ 达 0.8034 ± 0.0868，LLaVA-1.5 在 DDPM 下达 0.9451 ± 0.3710，只是这些 token 与目标框无关。
- **附录提出 string puzzle 结构推理探针。** 同一几何拓扑下只改变线条颜色（同色 vs 异色），把"外观线索"与"结构连通性"解耦，用于检验视觉编码器是否保留长程结构信息。

## Key Contributions

- Extends activation patching from language-model interpretability into the vision encoder itself, measuring each ViT (layer, token) activation's causal effect on the image–text similarity score rather than on token logits.
- Introduces a joint causal–spatial framework: Γ_{l,t} from three forward passes (clean, corrupted, patched) is correlated against IoU(P_t, B) between each token's patch and the referring-expression ground-truth box, plus a layer-wise variant Corr_l to test whether grounding emerges progressively.
- Reports a consistent near-zero causal–spatial correlation across seven models and two corruption procedures, with multi-seed repeats (10 runs for DeepSeek-VL, Qwen-2.5, LLaVA-NeXT, LLaVA; 6 for InternVL).
- Separates the two competing explanations for weak correlation by reporting per-model maximum Γ among top-ranked tokens, showing highly causal tokens do exist but are not spatially localized to the query object.
- Proposes a synthetic string-tracing task with matched geometry and only appearance (color) varied, isolating whether structural connectivity survives visual encoding; defines Δ_color = Accuracy_distinct − Accuracy_same as a model-independent measure of appearance-cue dependence.

## Methodology

The core measurement is a three-pass activation-patching protocol applied inside the vision tower. A clean run feeds the original image I and records s_clean = ⟨f_img(I), f_text(T)⟩. A corrupted run feeds a degraded image (Gaussian noise, and later DDPM-based corruption or blur) and records s_corr. A patched run feeds the corrupted image but restores one intermediate activation at (layer l, token t) from the clean run, recording s_patch. The causal contribution is Γ_{l,t} = (s_patch − s_corr)/(s_clean − s_corr), i.e. the fraction of the corrupted-to-clean similarity gap recovered by that single activation. The authors explicitly note Γ can fall outside [0, 1] under over-recovery.

For spatial grounding, each ViT patch token t is mapped back to its fixed image region P_t, and IoU(P_t, B) is computed against the ground-truth box B for the referring expression. The headline statistic is Corr(Γ, IoU) aggregated over tokens, with the layer-resolved version Corr_l = Corr(Γ_{l,t}, IoU_t) used to ask whether alignment increases with depth. Notably, the paper never states whether Pearson or Spearman correlation is used, and never names the source dataset for the images and referring-expression boxes (RefCOCO-style annotations are implied but not cited).

Experiments proceed in three stages: (1) establish the causal–spatial relationship on CLIP as a clean contrastive testbed; (2) transfer the identical procedure to DeepSeek-VL, Qwen-2.5, LLaVA-NeXT, LLaVA-1.5, InternVL-2, and SmolVLM under DDPM and blur corruption, with multiple seeds; (3) a *planned* longitudinal study across model generations and across ViT- vs SigLIP-based encoders — this third stage is explicitly future work, not executed here.

The appendix adds a controlled string-tracing probe. A custom Python generator produces puzzles with multiple overlapping strings; the model is given one endpoint and must name the endpoint at the other end of the same string. Two matched conditions share identical geometry and connectivity: all-same-color (only geometric continuity is usable) versus color-distinct (color acts as a persistent local identity feature). Endpoint accuracy is defined as the fraction of correctly identified endpoints, and Δ_color quantifies appearance dependence. The authors also propose attention-map analysis at string crossings to distinguish "vision encoder loses structure" from "language model fails to reason over preserved structure," but this analysis is described as planned rather than reported.

## Results & Highlights

- **CLIP causal–spatial correlation is essentially null.** Over 100 image–query samples, Corr(Γ, IoU) = 0.062 — against the natural null hypothesis of 0.0 for "causal importance is unrelated to the target region," this is indistinguishable from chance; the paper reports no p-value or confidence interval.
- **The near-zero pattern replicates across six larger VLMs under DDPM corruption:** DeepSeek-VL 0.0531 ± 0.0334, Qwen-2.5 0.0912 ± 0.0422, LLaVA-NeXT 0.0419 ± 0.0166, LLaVA-1.5 0.0509 ± 0.0329, InternVL 0.0397 ± 0.0224, SmolVLM 0.0011 ± 0.0000 — all within the same weak-correlation regime as the CLIP reference point of 0.062, i.e. deltas of at most ±0.03 relative to CLIP.
- **Switching the corruption operator does not rescue alignment, and can flip its sign.** Under blur: DeepSeek-VL 0.0520 ± 0.0041 (vs 0.0531 DDPM, Δ ≈ −0.001), Qwen-2.5 0.0294 ± 0.0100 (vs 0.0912 DDPM, Δ ≈ −0.062, a ~3× drop), LLaVA-NeXT −0.0217 ± 0.0052 (vs +0.0419 DDPM, a sign reversal), InternVL 0.0121 ± 0.0033 (vs 0.0397, Δ ≈ −0.028).
- **Strongly causal tokens exist but are spatially wrong.** Max Γ among top-ranked tokens reaches 0.8034 ± 0.0868 for Qwen-2.5 under blur (vs 0.5365 ± 0.3822 under DDPM) and 0.9451 ± 0.3710 for LLaVA-1.5 under DDPM (vs 0.1726 ± 0.0000 for the weakest model, SmolVLM — a 5.5× gap), while the correlation with IoU stays near zero.
- **Single-token illustration:** a representative token attains Γ = 0.718 while its patch overlaps the ground-truth box with IoU = 0.049; several other top-ranked tokens have exactly zero overlap with the annotated region.
- **Layer-wise analysis finds no emergence point.** Corr_l stays low across all CLIP vision-encoder layers, with only a small rise in later layers that remains weak at its maximum — arguing against the intuition that grounding crystallizes near the encoder output.
- **String-puzzle results are qualitative only.** The authors report that Gemini-3.1 Pro traces the correct endpoint in the color-distinct condition but "frequently fails" in the same-color condition, illustrated by two figures. No endpoint-accuracy numbers, no N, and no Δ_color value are reported — the metric is defined but never instantiated. The auxiliary Table 1 columns "Spatial Heuristic Top-1," "Intersection Top-k," and "Jaccard Top-k" (e.g. LLaVA-1.5 Intersection Top-k = 2.0176 ± 0.0417) are never defined anywhere in the text.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are Naren Kumar S, Tirth Bhatt, and Mayank Singh of the LINGO Research Group at IIT Gandhinagar, a lab with a track record in NLP and interpretability. The methodological lineage is properly credited: activation patching / causal tracing from LM interpretability, Palit et al. (2023)'s causal tracing tool for BLIP as the closest prior work in the multimodal setting, and Schaumlöffel et al. (2026) / Esmaeilkhani & Latecki (2026) on object localization and attention-based grounding in VLMs. The write-up reads as an early-stage or workshop submission: multiple sections are framed as "we plan to," Section 4.7–4.8 describe unrun experiments, and the appendix probe has no numbers. No code or data release is mentioned.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate).** Applying activation patching to intermediate ViT activations and correlating recovery against patch–box IoU is a clean, sensible instrument, and the explicit separation of "causal importance" from "spatial grounding" is a useful conceptual framing. But the empirical finding — that attention/importance maps in VLMs diverge from task-relevant regions — is close to what the cited grounding literature already reports; the contribution is a causal (rather than attention-based) confirmation, which is genuinely stronger evidence but not a new phenomenon.

**Rigor (weak in several concrete places).** (a) The evaluation dataset is never named. Ground-truth boxes for "referring expressions" imply RefCOCO/RefCOCOg, but with no citation, no split, and no sample count beyond the 100 used for CLIP, the numbers are not reproducible as stated. (b) The correlation estimator is unspecified (Pearson vs Spearman matters a lot here, since Γ has heavy tails and IoU is zero-inflated — most tokens have IoU = 0, which mechanically compresses any correlation toward zero). This is the single biggest threat to the headline claim: a near-zero Pearson correlation between a heavy-tailed Γ and a mostly-zero IoU is close to a statistical artifact, and the paper offers no rank-based or top-k-restricted robustness check to rule it out. (c) No significance testing, confidence intervals, or random-token control is reported; there is no positive control showing the pipeline *would* detect alignment if it existed (e.g., a supervised grounding model such as GLIP or a segmentation-aware encoder as an upper-bound reference). (d) SmolVLM is reported from a single run with ±0.0000 standard deviations — the authors correctly flag this, but the row still appears in the headline table, and its blur-condition Max Γ of −0.4906 is anomalous and unexplained. (e) Three of the five table columns are undefined, and "Intersection Top-k" values above 1 (1.1737, 2.0176) cannot be intersections in any normalized sense.

**Overclaiming.** Section 4.4 states the analysis "proves that a visual token can be causally important without being spatially localized" — "proves" is too strong for a 100-sample, single-dataset, unnamed-estimator correlation study. Section 4 also claims "We investigate all VLMs from 2023 to the present," while seven models are actually evaluated (and none of the strongest current systems except a qualitative Gemini-3.1 Pro anecdote). The abstract's claim that models "exploit visual cues to understand visual structures" rests entirely on two illustrative figures.

**Reproducibility (low).** No code, no dataset name, no model checkpoints/revisions, no patching granularity details (single token vs token-set, residual stream vs attention output vs MLP output is never specified), no corruption hyperparameters (noise σ, DDPM step, blur kernel).

**Significance (potentially high if completed).** If the finding survives a rank-correlation reanalysis and a positive control, "strong VLM benchmark performance without spatially localized causal representations" is an important negative result for the grounding and interpretability communities, and directly relevant to anyone building attribution or region-level explanation tools on top of VLM vision towers.

**Red flags:** unnamed dataset; undefined table columns; undefined correlation statistic; single-seed row presented alongside multi-seed rows; a defined metric (Δ_color) that is never computed; a substantial fraction of the paper written in future tense.

## Strengths

- The Γ formulation is a genuinely causal intervention, not a correlational saliency/attention heuristic — a real methodological step up from attention-map grounding analyses.
- Two independent corruption operators (DDPM, blur) plus 6–10 random seeds for most models, with mean ± std reported, is more robustness discipline than typical interpretability notes provide.
- The paper explicitly anticipates and rebuts the most obvious counter-explanation (that there simply are no causally important tokens) by reporting max Γ per model.
- The string-puzzle design is elegantly controlled: identical geometry, only appearance varies, so the accuracy gap cleanly isolates appearance dependence — a good instrument if the numbers get filled in.
- Intellectual honesty in places: the authors flag that Γ can exceed [0, 1], that SmolVLM's single run cannot support statistical claims, and that ViT-based conclusions may not transfer to SigLIP encoders.

## Limitations

- ViT-only; SigLIP-based encoders (used by many current VLMs) are deferred to future work, so architectural generality is untested.
- IoU is computed against axis-aligned boxes at fixed patch granularity — coarse patches around a small object guarantee low IoU regardless of representation quality, biasing the correlation downward.
- The zero-inflated IoU distribution is not addressed; a top-k or rank-based analysis is needed before the null correlation can be interpreted as a claim about representations rather than about the estimator.
- Only image–text similarity is used as the causal target for CLIP; extending Γ to autoregressive VLMs requires a similarity surrogate whose definition is not spelled out for models like Qwen-2.5 or LLaVA.
- The string-puzzle probe evaluates a single proprietary model on "a single instance of the dataset," with no accuracy table, no baseline models, and no human ceiling.
- The proposed attention analysis that would disambiguate encoder information loss from LM reasoning failure is described but not performed.

## Relevance

若你关注 VLM 的机制可解释性、视觉 grounding 的可信度，或者在做基于注意力/归因的多模态解释工具，这篇给出了一个可直接复用的因果探针（Γ 与 IoU 的联合分析），并提示：把 VLM 的注意力或重要性图当作"模型在看哪里"的证据可能是站不住的。不过它目前更像 work-in-progress 报告，若要引用其结论，建议自行补做秩相关与正对照实验。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Is the near-zero correlation an artifact of zero-inflated IoU? What happens with Spearman correlation, or with a top-k analysis restricted to tokens whose patches touch the box at all?
2. What is the positive control? If the same pipeline is run on an explicitly grounded model (GLIP, Grounding DINO, or a segmentation-supervised encoder), does Corr(Γ, IoU) rise substantially — and if not, the instrument itself is suspect.
3. Which sub-module is being patched — residual stream, attention output, or MLP output — and does the causal–spatial correlation differ across them?
4. Do high-Γ / low-IoU tokens carry global/context information (register tokens, background-scene tokens, or the CLS-adjacent "attention sink" tokens documented in ViTs)? Clustering high-Γ tokens by what they encode would turn a negative result into a mechanistic account.
5. For autoregressive VLMs, how exactly is the image–text "similarity" surrogate defined, and does the conclusion change if Γ is defined over answer-token logits instead?
6. What is the actual Δ_color on the string puzzles, across a panel of open models rather than a single Gemini-3.1 Pro anecdote, and does it shrink with model scale?
7. Does the weak alignment predict downstream failures — i.e., do samples with lower Corr(Γ, IoU) correlate with referring-expression comprehension errors? That would connect the interpretability finding to task behavior.
