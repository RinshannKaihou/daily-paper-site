> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了 LoDNA（Do-Not-Answer 数据集在 Twi/Hausa/Amharic/Swahili 上的"字面翻译 + 文化本地化"配对版本），并用"英语拒绝方向"作内部对照做隐层几何探测，发现在 Qwen2.5-7B / Llama-3.1-8B / Mistral-7B 的第 16 层上，绝大多数语言-模型组合保留的英语拒绝信号不足 10%（Hausa 在 Qwen2.5 上仅 0.97%，Mistral/Llama 上甚至为负），唯一例外是 Llama 上的 Swahili（cosine 0.4626、保留 27.31%）；同时字面与本地化提示彼此语义高度一致（cosine 0.95–0.996），说明模型"理解了危害概念却没有把它路由到安全机制"。

> **TL;DR:** On LoDNA — a new paired literal/culturally-localized extension of Do-Not-Answer into Twi, Hausa, Amharic and Swahili — hidden-state probing of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 at layer 16 shows target-language harmful prompts recover <10% of the English refusal direction for nearly every language–model pair (Hausa/Qwen2.5: 0.97% retained; Hausa/Mistral: −0.60%; Swahili/Mistral: −7.42%), with the single outlier Swahili/Llama at 27.31% (cosine 0.4626). This directly contradicts the "refusal direction is universal across safety-aligned languages" claim (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2025), and the behavioral proxy agrees: the average probability of emitting any standard refusal string is 1.05% (literal) / 1.15% (cultural), i.e. no refusal is initiated in >98.8% of cases.

## 核心贡献

- **LoDNA 数据集**：把 Wang et al. (2024b) 的 Do-Not-Answer 扩展到 Twi、Hausa、Amharic、Swahili 四种非洲低资源语言，每条危害提示都配一条"字面翻译"和一条"文化本地化改写"（保留危害意图但换成本地隐喻、习语和话语风格），由母语者共同作者交叉校验。
- **Twi 社区化英文提示**：把 Rashid et al. (2026) 的社区众包英文提示改写成加纳语境下的 DNA 式问题，用于检验"提示熟悉度"本身是否影响安全行为。
- **Latent Geometric Framework**：不看生成文本，直接在残差流上度量安全概念的跨语言映射，提出/组合了四个指标——Signed Retained Component、逐层 Dot Product、Mean Drift（DotLit − DotCtx）、以及在英语 PCA 平面上的投影，并用英语线性探针作为"英语安全边界确实被学到了"的内部对照。
- **回答四个 RQ**：英语安全对齐在表征层几乎不迁移（RQ1）；不存在完全语言无关的"危害流形"，几何是语言特异的（RQ2）；失败同时来自跨语言结构映射缺陷和提示形式敏感性（RQ3）；不同架构失败方式不同（RQ4）。
- **行为侧接地**：用 tokenization-aware 的 Normalized Sequence Log-Likelihood (SLL) 和 Probability of Refusal 替代在尾部语言上不可靠的 LLM-as-judge ASR，并显示行为漂移与隐层几何漂移方向一致。

## Key Contributions

- **LoDNA**, a safety dataset that pairs literal translations with culturally localized rewrites of Do-Not-Answer prompts in Twi, Hausa, Amharic and Swahili — the pairing is the methodological point, since it isolates *semantic contextualization* from *cross-lingual representational mismatch*.
- A **Latent Geometric Framework** that scores cross-lingual safety transfer from hidden states rather than generations: English refusal direction r_k^en = μ_k(U^en) − μ_k(S^en), target displacements r_k^{ℓ,lit} = μ_k(L^ℓ) − μ_k(S^ℓ) and r_k^{ℓ,ctx}, then cosine similarity, Signed Retained Component, layer-wise drift, English-trained logistic probes evaluated zero-shot, and PCA projection onto the English safety plane.
- **Empirical refutation of the universal harm manifold** in the extremely low-resource regime, specifically contradicting Wang et al. (2025) and Krasnodębska et al. (2026), whose universality claims were established on safety-aligned, moderate-to-high-resource languages using literal translations.
- **Two tokenization-aware behavioral metrics** (Probability of Refusal via LogSumExp over refusal candidate sequences; length-normalized SLL) proposed as substitutes for LLM-as-judge ASR in tail languages.
- A **qualitative multi-dimensional failure taxonomy** across AfriqueQwen-8B, Mistral, Llama and Qwen2.5 covering semantic understanding, safety alignment, repetition/degeneration, language switching and script corruption, produced by native-speaker annotation with iterative thematic coding.

## Methodology

The pipeline is deliberately generation-free at its core. For each target language ℓ the authors assemble five prompt sets: safe English (S^en), unsafe English (U^en), safe target-language (S^ℓ, drawn from existing HF corpora — GhanaNLP English-Twi parallel text, Henok/aya_amharic_dataset, CohereForAI/aya_dataset for Hausa and Swahili, SalKhan12/prompt-safety-dataset for English), literal harmful (L^ℓ) and culturally contextualized harmful (C^ℓ). LoDNA construction is two-stage: Google Translate seeds the translation, native-speaker co-authors correct grammaticality and semantic fidelity to produce the literal set, then the same annotators rewrite each literal prompt into a culturally grounded variant under a shared, iteratively refined guideline, with cross-annotator review and consensus resolution of disagreements.

For every prompt the final-input-token hidden state h_k(x) is extracted from mid-to-late layers, with layer 16 used as the headline layer (50% relative depth for the 32-layer Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, 57% for the 28-layer Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct). AfriqueQwen-8B, a continued-pretraining model with no safety tuning, is included **only for PCA visualization** at layer 20, as a "pre-alignment geometry" reference.

Alignment is quantified by (a) cosine between r_k^en and each target displacement, (b) the Signed Retained Component (r_k^en)^T r_k^{ℓ,t} / (r_k^en)^T r_k^en × 100, i.e. the signed percentage of the English refusal direction recovered, (c) per-prompt raw dot products onto r_k^en and their literal-minus-cultural difference (Drift_k), and (d) a per-layer English logistic probe p(unsafe | H_k(x)) evaluated zero-shot on target-language prompts. PCA is fit on English hidden states only, and target sets are projected onto that English-defined plane, so clustering behavior is read relative to the English safe/unsafe geometry. A decision rule then classifies each case as successful transfer, semantic-complexity failure (high drift), or cross-lingual structural failure (low cosine/retained component). Behavioral grounding uses refusal candidate strings in-language ("I'm sorry", "Samahani", "Ba zan iya"), scored both as an aggregate LogSumExp refusal probability and as a length-normalized SLL, with Drift = SLL(cultural) − SLL(literal).

## Results & Highlights

- **Retained English refusal signal is below 10% for nearly every language–model pair at layer 16.** Hausa is worst: **0.97% literal retention in Qwen2.5**, **−0.60% in Mistral** and **−1.58% in Llama**. Amharic is architecture-dependent and often negative (**−7.44% literal in Llama**, **−1.22% in Qwen2.5**, +2.12% in Mistral). Twi is the best of the non-Swahili cases at **8.57% in Qwen2.5** and 5.85% in Llama. The paper's own internal control — the English self-projection — is 100% by construction, so these are the numbers to compare against.
- **The single anomaly is Swahili on Llama-3.1-8B: cosine 0.4626 (literal) / 0.4577 (cultural), Signed Retained Component 27.31% / 27.73%** — roughly 28× the Hausa/Qwen2.5 figure. Crucially this does **not** replicate across architectures: the same Swahili prompts give cosine **−0.1482 and −7.42% retention in Mistral** and **−0.0215 / −1.06% in Qwen2.5**, so the authors correctly frame it as architecture-specific rather than a language property.
- **Literal vs. cultural prompts are near-identical semantically but not behaviorally:** literal-vs-cultural cosine is **0.9518–0.9957** (Hausa/Qwen2.5 lowest at 0.9518, Twi/Qwen2.5 highest at 0.9957) across all 12 language–model cells, while their projections onto the English refusal direction diverge — **Mean Drift = +9.90 for Hausa/Qwen2.5, −5.44 for Amharic/Qwen2.5, +4.65 for Twi/Qwen2.5**, versus drifts of order 0.01–0.33 in Mistral and Llama. This is the paper's core evidence for "concept encoded, not routed."
- **Behavioral proxy agrees with the geometry.** Averaged over all prompts, the probability of emitting any standard refusal sequence is **1.05% for literal and 1.15% for cultural prompts — no refusal pathway is initiated in >98.8% of cases**. Per-model refusal probabilities: Llama 1.83%/2.23%, Mistral 1.00%/0.87%, Qwen2.5 0.32%/0.33%. Per-language: Hausa is highest at 3.57%/3.95%, Twi 0.23%/0.23%, Amharic 0.18%/0.16%, Swahili 0.22%/0.24%.
- **Cultural localization measurably lowers refusal likelihood in at least one model:** Mistral shows SLL drift **−0.044** with refusal probability dropping **1.00% → 0.87%**; Amharic (−0.013) and Swahili (−0.015) show the same sign. The overall drift, however, is **−0.002** (SLL −1.774 literal vs −1.777 cultural), which is essentially nil.
- **Tokenization cost is visible in the SLL scale:** Twi has the worst normalized refusal log-likelihood at **−2.512**, versus Amharic −1.280 and Hausa −1.563; Qwen2.5 is lowest per-model at **−2.610** vs Llama −1.280.
- **The English control holds.** English-trained linear probes reach high validation accuracy and AUROC across layers and all architectures (Figure 8; numbers are not stated in the text), so the failures cannot be attributed to an unlearned English safety boundary.
- **Raw dot-product magnitudes differ by two orders of magnitude across architectures** (English self-dot: Mistral 4.70–6.54, Llama 14.42–17.32, Qwen2.5 233.8–325.4), which the authors explicitly flag as making raw alignment useless for cross-architecture comparison — the Retained Component normalization is what carries the comparison.
- **Qualitative:** AfriqueQwen-8B shows unsafe compliance even in English; Qwen2.5 exhibits severe Amharic script corruption and switches into French/Wolof/Yoruba for Twi and Chinese/Japanese for Amharic; the Mistral-Swahili example is the cleanest illustration — a grammatical, coherent Swahili response that fully complies with a harmful request, i.e. comprehension without safety routing.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-high).** The pairing of literal and culturally localized prompts *within the same language*, combined with a geometric rather than generation-based readout, is a genuinely new lever: it lets the authors separate "the model doesn't understand the language" from "the model understands but doesn't route to safety." The individual geometric tools (refusal direction, retained component, PCA projection, linear probes) are all borrowed from Arditi et al. (2024) and Zou et al. (2025); the contribution is the experimental design and the target languages, not the interpretability machinery.

**Rigor (mixed, with one significant hole).** The most important missing control is a **null distribution for cosine similarity**. The paper's headline evidence is that English↔target cosine is "near zero or negative," but in a 4096-dimensional residual space, two unrelated difference-of-means vectors have expected cosine ≈ 0 by default. Without a permutation baseline, a random-concept-direction baseline, or a within-English cross-split baseline, "cosine = −0.0162 for Hausa/Mistral" cannot be distinguished from "these vectors are simply not the same vector" — which is the null hypothesis, not evidence for a specific mechanism. Second, **Table 4 has no English row**: the reader is told refusal probability is 1.05% averaged over target-language prompts, but is never shown what that number is for *English harmful prompts on the same models*, so the "catastrophically low" characterization has no reference point. Third, **no variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests appear anywhere** — and the overall SLL drift of −0.002 is precisely the kind of number that needs them; Mistral's −0.044 drift and 0.13-percentage-point refusal drop are likewise reported as a single point estimate. Fourth, **the dataset size of LoDNA is never stated** in the main text — no prompt counts per language, per harm category, or per literal/cultural split — which is a surprising omission for a paper whose first contribution is a dataset. Fifth, **AfriqueQwen-8B appears in the abstract, methodology and qualitative Table 3 but has no row in Table 2**; it is restricted to PCA visuals because it lacks safety tuning, which is defensible, but the abstract's "across Mistral, Qwen2.5, AfriqueQwen, and Llama" reads as broader coverage than the quantitative results actually deliver.

**Reproducibility (moderate).** Model checkpoints, layers (16, and 20 for AfriqueQwen), safe-prompt source datasets and metric definitions are all specified precisely enough to reimplement. Code and data are promised but not yet available. The refusal candidate string set Y is only exemplified ("I'm sorry", "Samahani", "Ba zan iya"), not enumerated — and since Probability of Refusal is a LogSumExp over exactly that set, the headline 1.05% number is sensitive to a list the reader cannot see.

**Significance (high, if it replicates).** "Safety alignment does not transfer to low-resource languages" is not itself new, but a representation-level diagnosis that separates comprehension failure from routing failure is actionable: it argues that translating safety benchmarks is the wrong fix and that alignment must target the routing pathway. The Mistral-Swahili qualitative example — fluent, correct, and fully compliant with a harmful request — is the single most persuasive artifact in the paper.

**Red flags / overclaiming.** The title word "Illusion" is stronger than the evidence, which is correlational by the authors' own admission. Tokenization fragmentation (severe in Amharic) is acknowledged as a confounder that "likely distorts geometric analysis before safety routing occurs" — which means the Amharic results in particular may be measuring tokenizer pathology rather than safety geometry, yet Amharic numbers are reported alongside the others without qualification in Table 2. The absence of a safety-aligned moderate-resource control language is disclosed and reasonably justified (no such 7B–8B open-weight model exists for African languages), but it means the paper cannot fully separate "structural mapping failure" from "no alignment ever existed for these languages" — the English internal control establishes only that the English boundary is learnable, not that the target-language representations were ever supposed to map into it. Finally, the framing as a refutation of Wang et al. (2025) is somewhat asymmetric: that work studied *safety-aligned* languages, and these four languages are almost certainly not in any of these models' alignment data, so the two results may not actually be in conflict.

## Strengths

- The literal-vs-cultural pairing is a clean experimental control that most multilingual safety papers lack, and the 0.95–0.996 within-language cosine is exactly the sanity check that makes the divergence in refusal projection interpretable.
- Native-speaker co-authors performed all translation correction, localization and cross-validation, with a shared guideline and consensus resolution — a real answer to the MT-only critique the paper levels at prior benchmarks.
- Honest handling of a cross-architecture measurement artifact: the authors notice Qwen2.5's ~50× larger raw dot-product magnitudes and explicitly refuse to compare raw alignment across architectures.
- The Swahili/Llama outlier is reported prominently rather than buried, and is correctly characterized as architecture-specific after checking that it inverts in Mistral and Qwen2.5.
- Replacing LLM-as-judge ASR with tokenization-aware likelihood metrics is a well-motivated choice for tail languages, and the paper is candid that depressed SLL may partly reflect generation degradation rather than safety failure.
- Ethics section is substantive: annotator exposure minimization, a purpose-built annotation tool, research-only release.

## Limitations

- Restricted to 7B–8B open-weight instruction-tuned models; nothing is known about whether frontier-scale models with much stronger multilinguality show the same siloing.
- Purely observational geometry — no activation patching, steering, or ablation to establish that the measured misalignment *causes* the refusal failures.
- Subword tokenization inefficiency, catastrophic for Amharic (script corruption, prompt restatement, non-lexical output), is an admitted confounder upstream of the geometry.
- No safety-aligned moderate-resource control language, so structural mapping failure and simple absence of alignment cannot be fully disentangled.
- The Twi community-sourced English subset is explicitly small, weakening the "prompt familiarity affects safety sensitivity" claim.
- The localized dataset is described as limited in scale, and the actual counts are never reported.
- SLL depends on the model being able to produce in-language refusal tokens at all, so language switching (refusing in French instead of Twi) is scored as a non-refusal.
- Only layer 16 (and 20 for AfriqueQwen) is reported quantitatively; layer-wise evidence lives in appendix figures with no tabulated numbers.

## Relevance

如果你关注 LLM 安全对齐的机制可解释性（refusal direction、activation steering、表征层探针），这篇提供了一个反例证据：拒绝方向在极低资源语言上并不通用，且"理解但不路由"的失败模式可以被几何指标定位——对多语言 guardrail 设计和越狱风险评估都有直接参考价值。另外它示范了如何用 tokenization-aware 的似然指标绕开 LLM-as-judge 在尾部语言上的失效，这个方法论对任何做非英语评测的工作都可复用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the cosine between the English refusal direction and a *random* difference-of-means direction, or between refusal directions estimated from two disjoint English splits? Without that null, how much of the "near-zero cosine" finding is just high-dimensional geometry?
- What is the Probability of Refusal for English harmful prompts on these same three models? Is 1.05% catastrophic relative to, say, 60%, or is the absolute scale of the metric simply small?
- Does activation patching — injecting r^en into the residual stream at layer 16 during a Hausa or Twi harmful prompt — actually restore refusal? That would convert the correlational diagnosis into a causal one and directly test the "routing" hypothesis.
- Is the Swahili/Llama transfer (27.3% retained) explained by Swahili's presence in Llama-3.1's pretraining mix, by tokenizer efficiency, or by post-training data? Comparing tokens-per-word across the four languages against retained component would be a cheap first test.
- Do the Amharic results survive a tokenizer-controlled reanalysis (e.g., byte-level model, or restricting to prompts with comparable token counts)? Given the documented script corruption, Amharic may be measuring something other than safety geometry.
- How does retained component scale with model size — is the 27% Swahili/Llama figure a preview of what 70B models look like for all four languages, or is siloing scale-invariant?
- Do the geometric metrics predict actual jailbreak success under a real attack (e.g., adversarial metaphor / adversarial poetry attacks in Twi), which would give the framework predictive rather than merely diagnostic value?
