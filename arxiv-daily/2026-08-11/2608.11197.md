> **一句话总结：** 作者把 SAE 的「活跃 latent 集合」（signature）当作分析单元，发现它在人造玩具模型中确实满足并集式组合性（Jaccard/recall/precision 0.8–1.0），但在真实 LLM 上既不比稠密残差流表征更贴近人类类别边界（Gemma 3 270M 逐层峰值 AMI：SAE-Jaccard 0.421 vs 残差流 cosine 0.471；Llama 3.1 8B 0.297 vs 0.429），也不满足最基本的组合直觉——给名词加一个语义兼容的形容词，就会让名词位置上 20%–60% 的原有 latent 直接熄灭。

> **TL;DR:** The paper re-runs Shani et al. (2026)'s human-concepts typicality benchmark (Rosch/McCloskey categories) using SAE active-latent-set overlap (Jaccard) instead of dense cosine similarity, across Gemma 3 270M–27B, GPT-2 Small, Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral 7B with Gemma Scope 2 / Llama Scope / OpenAI TopK SAEs. SAE set similarity does *not* beat the dense residual-stream baseline at recovering human category boundaries (peak layerwise AMI 0.421 vs 0.471 on Gemma 3 270M, 0.297 vs 0.429 on Llama 3.1 8B), within-category typicality Spearman ρ hovers around zero for both, and a controlled "add one compatible adjective" probe shows 20%–60% of the base noun's active latents disappear — falsifying a naive union-of-properties / bag-of-features reading of SAE signatures. Read it if you build tooling on top of SAE feature sets; skip if you only care about single-latent interpretability.

## 核心贡献

- **把「活跃 latent 集合」正式化为分析单元**：定义 signature $S^\ell(w)=\bigcup_t\{k \mid z_k>0\}$ 与 Jaccard 集合相似度，并先做两项前置检查证明它不是噪声——LRH 对齐的玩具模型上 SAE 满足并集一致性，Pile 上 30,000 条 snippet 的 latent-set 重叠聚类能给出语义可读的邻域（如「政治会议」簇）。
- **复现并扩展 Shani et al. (2026) 的人类概念实验**：把稠密 cosine 换成 SAE-Jaccard，结论定性一致但定量更差——SAE 集合相似度在类别边界（AMI）和类别内典型性（Spearman ρ）两项上都没有超过稠密表征。
- **提出并证伪 union-of-properties 假设**：构造 200 条「名词 → 形容词+名词 → …」的手工序列（$k_{\max}=5$），发现加形容词会大量删除名词自身的 latent，直接违反「组合 = 集合并」的预期。
- **对「丢失 latent」做机制解剖**：区分 (i) 上游是否曾激活、(ii) pre-activation 属于 near-zero 还是强负值、(iii) SAEBench 自动可解释性得分、(iv) 用随机 latent 方向 steering 模拟干扰，逐一排除单一解释（干扰、feature absorption、跨层探针缺陷）。
- **跨 8 个模型 / 多种 SAE 家族的稳健性检查**：Matryoshka BatchTopK、JumpReLU、TopK、ReLU 四类 SAE，16k–262k 宽度，三档 L0 稀疏度，结论定性一致。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the SAE *signature* (set of active latents, unioned over target-word token positions) as a unit of analysis, with Jaccard overlap as the induced similarity, and validates it before using it.
- Preliminary studies establish the measure is meaningful: a frozen LRH-aligned toy model shows near-perfect union consistency once SAE width exceeds the ground-truth feature count, and an APM-based clustering of 30k Pile snippets yields semantically coherent neighborhoods.
- Replicates the Shani et al. (2026) conceptual-similarity protocol with SAE set similarity, finding qualitatively similar but quantitatively *weaker* alignment with human conceptual structure than raw cosine.
- Introduces a controlled compositional-consistency probe (prefixing compatible adjectives to a noun under a fixed template "This is a {t}._") and quantifies a "lost-latent rate" $\mathcal{R}^{(k)}_\ell$.
- Provides a four-way diagnostic decomposition of lost latents (upstream presence, pre-activation regime, auto-interpretability, steering-based interference simulation), showing no single mechanism accounts for the phenomenon.

## Methodology

Two similarity measures are compared on identical inputs. Dense baseline: mean-pooled residual-stream states $\bar x^\ell(w)$ for the target-word tokens under the neutral template `"This is a {word}._"`, compared with cosine. SAE measure: the same states are encoded, activations binarized, and the union of active latent indices across target-word tokens forms $S^\ell(w)$; similarity is $|S\cap S'|/|S\cup S'|$.

**Experiment 1 (human concepts).** Using the human-concepts dataset from Shani et al. (2026) — semantic categories (bird, fish, weapon, …) with instances and human typicality scores from Rosch (1973, 1975) and McCloskey & Glucksberg (1978) — two evaluations are run: (a) category-boundary recovery via $K$-medoids on $d=1-s$ with $K$ = number of ground-truth categories, scored by Adjusted Mutual Information; (b) within-category typicality via Spearman correlation between category–instance similarity rankings and human typicality rankings. Both are computed layerwise.

**Experiment 2 (set consistency).** 200 hand-curated noun sequences $t_0=\texttt{noun}$, $t_1=\texttt{adj}_1\,\texttt{noun}$, …, $t_5$, with five available adjectives per noun (color, size, condition, …). The lost-latent rate compares the noun-position signature under $t_0$ with the noun-position signature under $t_k$: $\mathcal{R}^{(k)}_\ell = |\overline F_\ell(t_0)\setminus\overline F_\ell(t_k)| / |\overline F_\ell(t_0)|$. Lost latents are then classified by (i) whether the layer-$\ell$ SAE, used as a cross-layer probe on layers $0..\ell$, detects them upstream; (ii) whether their pre-activations $\alpha_{\text{pre}}=W_e(x-b_d)+b_e$ fall in a near-zero band $[-\theta,\theta]$ ($\theta$ = learned JumpReLU offset, else 0.1) or a negative regime $<-\theta$; (iii) SAEBench auto-interpretability scores (GPT-4o-mini explanations, 100 latents per group per layer, groups = lost / surviving / random control); (iv) synthetic steering where $k$ random SAE decoder directions with coefficients drawn uniformly around $\mu\in\{\beta/2,0,-\beta/2\}$, $\beta=10$, are added to the residual state and renormalized.

**Models/SAEs.** Gemma 3 270M/1B/4B/12B/27B with Gemma Scope 2 (Matryoshka BatchTopK and JumpReLU, widths 16k and 262k, target-$L_0$ small≈10–20 / medium≈30–60 / big≈60–150); GPT-2 Small with OpenAI v5 TopK (32k, 128k); Llama 3.1 8B with Llama Scope JumpReLU (32k, 131k); Mistral-7B with a 65k ReLU SAE. Toy models: $D=50$ with $N\in\{10,50,100\}$, $D=1000$ with $N\in\{500,1000,5000\}$, and $D=640$ with ground-truth directions sampled from a real Gemma-3-270M decoder (pairwise cosine < 0.2); firing probability $p=0.2$, 3 seeds, 15M–50M training activations.

## Results & Highlights

- **Category boundaries, contextual (Table 4, peak AMI across layers).** SAE-Jaccard does not beat dense residual-stream cosine on most models: Gemma 3 270M 0.421 (16k big) / 0.463 (262k big) vs **0.471** for cosine; Gemma 3 1B 0.470 (262k big) vs **0.488**; GPT-2 Small 0.218 (32k) vs **0.304** (−0.086); Llama 3.1 8B 0.297 (32k) vs **0.429** (−0.132, the largest gap); Mistral 7B 0.289 vs **0.311**. Honest counterpoint the paper itself reports: on the larger Gemmas the sign flips — Gemma 3 27B SAE-Jaccard 0.444 (262k big) vs 0.397 cosine (+0.047), and Gemma 3 4B 0.466 vs 0.438 — so the "SAEs are worse" headline is model-dependent, not universal.
- **Category boundaries, static/uncontextualized (Table 3).** Applying the lowest-layer SAE to raw token embeddings is far worse than the embedding geometry itself: Gemma 3 270M 0.0294 (16k big) vs 0.1074 embedding AMI; Gemma 3 12B bottoms out at 0.0034 (262k medium) vs 0.1119 — essentially chance-level. Across all 8 models the static SAE-Jaccard AMI never exceeds 0.098 while embeddings sit at 0.060–0.124.
- **Within-category typicality.** Per-category Spearman ρ between human typicality and SAE-Jaccard rankings "fluctuates around zero for most categories" (Fig. 2b, Fig. 10b), i.e. SAE sets recover essentially none of the graded typicality structure — the same negative result Shani et al. report for dense states, so the more-interpretable representation buys nothing here. Meanwhile ρ(SAE-Jaccard, residual cosine) is strongly positive (Fig. 2c, broadly 0.5–0.9), showing SAE sets track model-internal geometry rather than human semantics.
- **Lost-latent rates under semantic refinement.** Adding a single compatible adjective removes **20%–60%** of the noun's active latents (Fig. 4a), rising with $k$ and with model depth; the largest single drop is $t_0\to t_1$ (Fig. 29). This is measured at the *noun* token positions with the surrounding template fixed, so it is not a positional artifact.
- **Where lost latents go.** Up to ~**60%** of lost latents were active upstream in middle layers when the layer-$\ell$ SAE is used as a cross-layer probe (Fig. 4b), so much of the "loss" is local disappearance rather than information deletion. On the noun signature, ~**80%** of previously-active lost latents fall in the near-zero pre-activation band with only a few in the strongly negative regime; over the full sequence the negative fraction is only **10%–20%** (Fig. 4c). Interpretation: latents are dropped below threshold, not actively suppressed.
- **Toy-model union consistency (Fig. 5–7).** With $D=50$ and $N\in\{10,50,100\}$, Jaccard/recall/precision are **0.8–1.0** once SAE width is sufficient; the residual degradation at $N=10$ is recall-driven (precision stays ≈1), consistent with feature hedging rather than spurious latent recruitment. In the $D=1000$ sweep, union consistency is poor when $L\ll N$ and improves sharply near $L\approx N$; at $N=5000$ it is recovered even for $L<N$. With decoder-sampled (realistic) feature geometry the worst regime is *intermediate* width ≈200, where Jaccard dips to ≈0.4 before recovering.
- **Auto-interpretability (Fig. 39, SAEBench + GPT-4o-mini, 100 latents/group/layer).** Lost latents score slightly lower than surviving and control latents at 25/50/65/85% depth, but the paper explicitly states means fall within each other's error bars — "weak supporting evidence" only, not a separation.
- **Interference cannot explain it.** Positive-only steering ($\mu=\beta/2$) reproduces realistic loss rates for Gemma in early-to-mid layers, but for GPT-2, Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral 7B *all three* sign settings produce steep, near-saturating loss rates as $k$ grows, and the steering-induced layerwise trend does not match the real-input trend. The authors correctly conclude interference is a contributing factor, not the mechanism.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty (moderate-good).** The specific move — treating the *set* of active latents as the object and testing whether it composes as a union — is a clean, previously under-tested reframing. It is close in spirit to Chanin et al.'s absorption work and Leask et al.'s "SAEs do not find canonical units", and the paper is candid about that lineage rather than claiming a new phenomenon.

**Rigor (good, with caveats).** The multi-model, multi-SAE-family sweep and the four-way diagnostic decomposition are more careful than typical negative-result interpretability papers, and the authors repeatedly flag when evidence is weak (auto-interp bars overlap; steering "remains speculative"). Two concrete problems, though. First, Table 4 contains results that partially contradict the abstract's framing: Gemma 3 4B, 12B and 27B all have SAE-Jaccard peak AMI *above* the residual-stream baseline, yet the main text summarizes with "the highest SAE-based AMI remains below the highest embedding-based AMI, a pattern observed in many investigated settings" — "many" is doing a lot of work, and the main-text figures use the 270M model where the claim holds. Second, the headline evaluation model is **Gemma 3 270M**, the smallest model in the suite; choosing the weakest backbone for the flagship figures is a defensible compute decision but makes the negative result easier to obtain.

**Missing ablations.** (a) No comparison against a *random-set* or shuffled-latent control for the Jaccard AMI numbers — we see SAE-Jaccard vs cosine, but not what AMI a size-matched random signature would give, so the absolute interpretability of 0.42 is unclear. (b) The lost-latent probe uses a single template and a single kind of semantic refinement (adjective prefixing); the paper acknowledges this in Limitations but it means the 20–60% figure has one construction behind it. (c) Only residual-stream SAEs — no BatchTopK-at-MLP, no gated SAEs, no transcoders — despite the claim being about SAE features generally. (d) The Jaccard measure is size-sensitive; two words with very different $L_0$ can never have high Jaccard, and no normalization or $L_0$-matched control is reported, which is a real confound when comparing across sparsity targets (small/medium/big).

**Data quirks.** The human-concepts dataset is small, English-only, and built from 1970s psychology norms; the "crime" category is called out as an anticorrelation outlier across many models, which suggests category-level noise is non-trivial relative to the effect sizes. The 200 noun–adjective sequences are hand-curated by the authors with no inter-annotator check on whether the adjectives are truly "semantically compatible."

**Reproducibility (good).** Everything is built on public models and SAE suites; hyperparameters are tabulated (Table 1, Table 2); seeds are specified ({0,1,2}); the pragmatic $\theta=0.1$ threshold for non-JumpReLU SAEs is flagged as a choice rather than hidden. Code release is promised but not yet available, which is the main gap.

**Significance (moderate).** This is a useful constraint rather than a discovery: it tells anyone building set-based SAE tooling (retrieval over signatures, latent-set diffing, concept-set steering) that the union assumption fails on natural text. It does not propose a fix.

**Red flags.** Mild overclaiming in the abstract's "substantial mismatch" framing given the model-dependent Table 4 reversals; and the title's "instability" is somewhat loaded for what is, mechanistically, latents falling into a near-zero band rather than being deleted — the paper's own Fig. 4c evidence argues against the strongest reading of its title.

## Strengths

- Tests its own measure before using it (toy-model union consistency + Pile clustering), which most negative-result papers skip.
- Breadth: 8 backbones from 270M to 27B, 4 SAE nonlinearity families, 2–6 width/sparsity variants each, with $k$-sensitivity analysis (Fig. 28) showing conclusions do not hinge on the $k=1$ choice used in main figures.
- The pre-activation decomposition (near-zero vs strongly negative) is a genuinely informative diagnostic that reframes "lost" as "sub-threshold", and the authors let that nuance override their own headline.
- Actively tries to kill its own result with three competing explanations (interference, absorption, cross-layer probe artifacts) and reports that none suffices — including the observation that Matryoshka SAEs, designed to mitigate absorption, show the same lost-latent rates.
- Transparent about compute, AI assistance, and the pragmatic threshold choice.

## Limitations

- Conclusions are tied to one typicality dataset (Shani et al. / Rosch norms), one semantic-refinement construction (adjective prefixing), and residual-stream SAEs only; paraphrase, task-relevant abstraction, and other semantic-consistency forms are untested.
- No BatchTopK, gated, or non-residual hook-location SAEs are exhaustively evaluated, so the set-level instability claim is not established for those families.
- The paper offers no positive proposal — no repaired similarity measure, no SAE training objective that would restore union consistency.
- Jaccard's dependence on signature cardinality is unaddressed as a confound across sparsity levels.
- Toy-model results are the *positive* half of the story and rely on a synthetic generative process (independent firing at $p=0.2$, unit magnitude) that is far simpler than real activations; the authors note the decoder-sampled variant already degrades at intermediate widths.

## Relevance

如果你的工作涉及 SAE / 机制可解释性、或者依赖「特征集合」做检索、diffing、概念编辑，这篇给出了一个必须知道的负面约束：latent 集合不能当作可加的语义 bag 来用。对 LLM 表征评测方法论（AMI / Spearman 对齐人类判断）也有直接参考价值，尤其是它诚实展示了「更可解释的表征未必更贴近人类语义」这一反直觉结果。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would an $L_0$-matched or size-normalized set similarity (e.g., overlap coefficient, or weighted Jaccard using activation magnitudes instead of binarization) close the gap to dense cosine, or is the deficit intrinsic to discarding magnitude?
- Since ~80% of lost latents sit in the near-zero pre-activation band, does a soft signature (top-$p$ by pre-activation, or a temperature-softened membership) restore union consistency on the adjective probe?
- The Gemma 3 27B reversal (SAE-Jaccard 0.444 > cosine 0.397) is unexplained — is there a scale threshold beyond which SAE sets *do* become the better semantic probe, and does it correlate with dictionary width per parameter?
- Do transcoders or cross-layer features (which explicitly model computation rather than a single residual snapshot) reduce the lost-latent rate, testing the paper's own "single point in the residual stream" hypothesis?
- Would a training objective that explicitly penalizes signature loss under compositional inputs (a union-consistency regularizer on synthetic adjective-noun pairs) trade off reconstruction/sparsity in an acceptable way?
- Human typicality correlations are near zero for *both* representations — is the failure in the representation, or in using single-token/word-level probes for a judgment humans make over rich episodic knowledge?
