Why This Matters

A reusable blueprint for any LLM-driven auto-search loop: let the agent control strategy, hard-code the guarantees it can't maintain itself.

Critical Edge

No ablation separates the policy from the harness — and the biggest win comes from a benchmark where GEPA can't even touch the demos RLMOpt optimizes.

Methodology

decision ↔ enforcement split

Standard objective — maximize mean metric over prompt p under a budget B of task-LM rollouts. What changes is who runs the loop.

  • Agent sideRLM agent (gpt-5.1) writes short REPL programs calling harness tools — peek_examples, synthesize_failures, run_candidate, commit_prompt — choosing both which operation and when. A fixed "skill prompt" (Appendix A) is advisory: it encodes "orient once," "diagnose from aggregated failures," and "bigger is NOT better." Only the harness can refuse a commit.
  • Harness sideA program the agent cannot bypass. Owns the dataset, executes every rollout, computes composite S(p)=Σwfsf, keeps candidate history, and enforces a per-field 0.05 regression floor plus a 1.65-SE paired significance gate (one-sided 95%). The floor auto-enables on datasets ≤20 records.
  • Noise-band refusalIf three candidates in a row land within the current best's noise band with no diagnosis in between, the next run_candidate errors without consuming budget and redirects the agent to synthesize_failures. Gates when to keep evaluating, not what to search.
  • Multi-componentA candidate is a map {name→text}: system_prompt always; tool_description_<name> per tool and demonstrations for agentic tasks. Trajectories scored by subset / final-state matching; spurious tool calls penalized, correct refusals rewarded.
  • FinalizationAfter the agent stops, the harness generates "polish" variants of its own running-best, scores seed + all commits + claimed-best + polish on validation, forms the Pareto frontier, and returns the test score of the frontier member with the highest composite. Polish evaluations are outside B.

SETUP

  • Task LMgpt-4o-mini everywhere except Chia (gpt-4.1 — many-field extraction saturates the smaller model's format control). Temperature = 0.
  • Optimizer LMgpt-5.1 for both RLMOpt and GEPA. RLMOpt uses fixed B=500, no per-benchmark tuning.
  • BaselineGEPA at native auto="light" (~780–840 rollouts). GEPA can't drive the BFCL multi-turn loop natively → a thin wrapper exposes the agentic system prompt as the optimizable instruction.
  • FairnessCross-run skill library disabled for all head-to-heads — neither method carries information between runs.

Negative controls (honestly reported)

On capability-saturated tasks — IFEval-style instruction following (~0.91 seed), single-turn BFCL (~0.77) — neither method improved anything; RLMOpt returned the seed unchanged.

Headroom, not budget, drives gains

Lifts rank-order with seed weakness: Chia +.133 (from .435) > BFCL +.084 (from .602) > IFBench +.030 > HotpotQA +.027. B=200 on HotpotQA reaches .717 vs .727 at B=500.

Strengths & Limitations

side-by-side

Strengths

  • The decision/enforcement split is a genuinely good architectural answer to "let an agent drive search without letting it fool itself" — Pareto over per-field vectors, 0.05 floor, 1.65-SE gate, and budget-free refusal after three uninformative candidates are all concrete and implementable.
  • Negative controls on capability-saturated splits (where nothing helps) are rare in this literature and the most useful contribution for practitioners deciding whether to bother.
  • The headroom framing is falsifiable and supported: gains rank-order with seed weakness.
  • Prompt-size reduction (5,419 vs 19,818 chars on BFCL-mt) is a permanent inference-cost cut usually ignored.
  • The Chia val-vs-test inversion (GEPA higher on val, lower on test) is turned into a real overfitting argument, not buried.
  • Reports its own failure cases (small-val probe: +0.067 val, ≈−0.03 test) instead of omitting them.

Limitations

  • No component ablation. Adaptive policy vs harness scoring/floor/demos contribution is unidentified — by the authors' own admission.
  • BFCL comparison is asymmetric. GEPA's wrapper doesn't expose the demos RLMOpt optimizes → the largest margin is system-vs-system, not policy-vs-policy.
  • Only GEPA-light is run. MIPROv2, OPRO, TextGrad discussed but never benchmarked; no GEPA at matched or higher budget.
  • Stopping policy is broken and known broken (0.85 target unattainable; 0.02 threshold below the SE) — termination is effectively the budget cap; larger budgets sometimes lowered test accuracy.
  • Small evaluation sets (50–150) with 0.03–0.05 SE against typical 0.006–0.033 margins.
  • Narrow model coverage (two closed task LMs, one closed optimizer LM); no open-weight controller tested. No code/artifact release. No cost-parity (dollar) comparison.

Questions for Further Exploration

the decisive studies the paper skips

Run the ablation the paper declines. Hold harness, budget, task LM, and optimizer LM fixed; swap the RLM policy for GEPA's reflective-mutation policy. How much of the +0.021 mean survives? Prior: the per-field floor and Pareto machinery carry a large share.

Re-run BFCL multi-turn with the demonstrations component disabled for RLMOpt (or exposed to GEPA). Does the +0.033 — the only clearly-super-SE margin — hold up? decisive

Does an open-weight optimizer LM still work as the controller? Forming and testing hypotheses is exactly the capability likely to degrade first when gpt-5.1 is swapped for a mid-size open model.

Can the no-regression floor become a formal guarantee rather than a heuristic? With a 1.65-SE gate on a 50–150-example split, the false-accept rate over many commits is non-trivial — what's the empirical rate of committed candidates that regress on test?

GEPA at matched rollout budget (500) rather than its light preset (~780–840): is RLMOpt's efficiency claim really about the policy, or about GEPA-light being budget-inefficient at default?

Does the cross-run skill library actually compound? Disabled for all head-to-heads — arguably the more interesting long-run claim, and entirely untested here.
