Methodology & Results

02

Methodology · training-free, frozen encoder

  1. Cache the frozen embedding xᵢ = f_V(preprocess(Vᵢ)) ∈ ℝᴰ. No weight updates, no generative decoding — all later steps operate on this vector.
  2. Rank channels by prevalence-invariant selectivity (Eq. 2). Per-coordinate win-rate ≈ per-channel AUROC, centered so chance = 0 — robust to multi-label CT imbalance (prevalence ~7%–45%).
  3. Take top-K = 10 coordinates, K* chosen by grid search on a held-out dev split (smallest K within 0.005 mean AUROC of full-D), reused for all backbones.
  4. Fit a closed-form direction (Eq. 3) from class means on top-K channels w_c = (μ₊ − μ₋)/‖μ₊ − μ₋‖ — avoids least-squares bias from imbalance. Threshold τ_c calibrated on held-out data; test volume never enters the means (no leakage).
  5. Causal ablation (Eq. 5): zero a finding's top-K channels and measure the drop matrix Δ over all findings. On-target vs off-target gap quantifies causal selectivity.
  6. Detection-grounded reporting: binary detections drive a deterministic corpus-derived template (split / tag / negate T₀, append sentences per d=1). The verbalizer can never hallucinate findings beyond dᵢ.

Strengths vs Limitations

03

+ Strengths

  • Genuinely training-free. No weight updates, no opaque decoding — produces both predictions and channel-level explanations, well-aligned with auditing motivation.
  • Clean math. Prevalence-invariant selectivity (Eq. 2), closed-form mean-difference direction (Eq. 3), principled causal-ablation metric (Eq. 5).
  • Strong causal evidence. ~20× on-target vs off-target AUROC drop, with per-finding circuit tables that make the sparsity claim inspectable.
  • Honest experimental design. Three transfer regimes disentangle backbone-portability from anatomy-alignment.
  • Rare rigor. Bootstrap uncertainty (B=2000) with shared-resampling p-values; ~23× latency reduction is practically useful.

! Limitations

  • CT-CHAT is the only trained baseline and its F1 is anomalously low (0.184); broader report-generation comparison would strengthen claims.
  • Rare findings = high-variance channels. Few positive calibration volumes; abstention mitigates but doesn't solve uneven per-finding reliability.
  • Anatomy-mismatch degrades performance (regime C AUROC 0.605) — "general property" applies to matched-anatomy settings only.
  • Template-bound reporting. Abdomen RadGraph-F1 (0.262) lags trained Merlin+RadLlama (0.293).
  • Calibration-set dependence. Behavior on smaller calibration sets or under calibration-domain shift is uncharacterized; CT-CLIP baselines taken from original paper (checkpoint unavailable).

Open Questions for Further Work

04

How does channel-ranking stability behave as the calibration set shrinks (600 → 200 → 50 volumes) — is there a sample-efficiency floor where the sparse circuit breaks down? calibration

Would ensembling CCP's top-K set with a learned supervised linear head recover the remaining ~0.035 AUROC gap to ClassFine while preserving interpretability? hybrid

The off-diagonal ablation entries (arterial/coronary calcification, emphysema/atelectasis) hint at shared sub-circuits — can these be decomposed into monosemantic channels via sparse-overcomplete reparameterization? mechanistic

Are the channel sets robust but directions w_c fragile under calibration-domain shift (scanner vendor, slice thickness, contrast phase)? robustness

Could a frozen retrieval-augmented LLM that respects dᵢ exactly replace the template, recovering abdomen RadGraph-F1 without reintroducing hallucination? reporting

Does the ~10-channel sparsity replicate on 2D radiography (CheXagent, RadDINO) or MRI foundation models — a modality-independent principle? generalization
