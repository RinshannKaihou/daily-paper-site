How Many Bits Can an Adapter Write?

arxiv.org/abs/2607.21351 ↗
Kaizhen Tan (CMU) · Heqing Du (Columbia) · Yang Feng (Columbia)
Key Finding

LoRA adapters store only ~1.7–2.8 bits per trainable parameter vs 3.6 for full fine-tuning — and leakage tracks written bits, not parameter count.

⌗ Key Metric
~11 bits

Canary exposure jump on Qwen2.5-0.5B at the rank 16→64 step — where written bits climb ~50%. Rank 4→16 (4× params) moves neither.

◆ Why This Matters

Capacity is computable before any gradient step, letting data owners weigh a dataset against an adapter's capacity and design against leakage — not audit it afterward.

▲ Critical Edge

Scale is extrapolation. All capacity numbers come from 2M/8M GPT-style bases plus a single 0.5B Qwen audit; 7B-scale claims rest on protocol portability, not direct measurement.

Methodology · the compression meter

The instrument is a compression-based memorization meter. Codelength is the negative log-likelihood of a sequence under a model, realized by arithmetic coding. Unintended memorization is the bits a tuned model shaves off a reference: mem_U(x) = H_K(x|θ_ref) − min(H_K(x|θ_ref), H_K(x|θ̂)).

The probe trains on uniformly random 64-token sequences over a 2048-symbol vocab (704 bits each, nothing predictable), so every saved bit is a stored bit — held-out sequences must read mem_U ≈ 0, a check that passes across the whole spine.

  • Artifact cost CL(Δθ) — entropy-coded size of the quantized update; what a hub physically transmits.
  • Behavior-write W(D) — likelihood shift on canonical answers; makes SFT & RL comparable.
  • mem_U on random data — the ground-truth capacity meter.

Fixed 16,000-step budget, no early stopping, LR envelope reported. Extending to 40K steps lifts readings only 10–24%, so every number is a conservative lower bound.

Results · the numbers

  • ~1.7–2.8 bits/param for LoRA vs ≥3.07 budget-matched full FT and the 3.6 full-model line (Table 1).
  • Placement > count: at fixed ~37K params, MLP-only holds 2.43 bits/param vs attention-only 1.30 — near-doubling with count flat (Fig 3).
  • Substrate sets the ceiling: same LoRA memorizes 29% on a random base, 79% on synthetic, 98% on WikiText (Fig 4).
  • Leakage follows bits: rank 16→64 jumps exposure ~11 bits; rank 4→16 moves neither bits nor exposure (Fig 5).
  • SFT vs GRPO at matched accuracy: SFT excess bits +96 → +271; GRPO sits at −35 to −56 (zero in noise). Planted secrets: SFT extracts at rank 1, GRPO stays at chance (1.1 bits).

Strengths & Limitations

▲ Strengths
  • Reframes "is a LoRA a harmless diff?" into a measurable, pre-training quantity with a defensible random-string ground truth.
  • Clean dissociations: placement & substrate move capacity while count is held flat.
  • First bit-level evidence in the elicitation-vs-injection debate; secret asymmetry is mechanism-level.
  • Conservative by construction — understates, never inflates.
▲ Limitations
  • Scale: 2M/8M bases + one 0.5B Qwen audit; 7B claims are extrapolation.
  • MIA sits at chance at the corpus size used — bits→leakage link is extraction-only.
  • SFT-vs-GRPO secret test is a stress test, not a controlled bitrate (SFT perfect acc, GRPO high-90s).
  • Random strings calibrate storage but are not themselves a threat model.
  • Reward-entropy dial does not move GRPO here (bound is slack) — conjecture untested.

Open Questions

Q1Do the 1.7–2.8 bits/param and MLP-vs-attention gap hold at 7B–70B scale, or does substrate width saturate the effect?
Q2Can the bits→leakage link extend to membership inference at corpus sizes where MIA resolves above chance?
Q3What happens when the reward-entropy dial is actually engaged — on tasks where GRPO operates near an entropy bound?
Q4Does publishing a measured capacity alongside a checkpoint require also characterizing the frozen base? How should hubs standardize?
Q5Could the same meter rank VeRA / DoRA / prefix tuning into a unified, privacy-meaningful "writable bits" leaderboard?
Q6How does capacity interact with DP-SGD — does the noise clamp show up as a designable reduction in writable bits?
