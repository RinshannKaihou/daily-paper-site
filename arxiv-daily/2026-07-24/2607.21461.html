Why this matters
Reframes verification as a transition operator between research rounds; open 4B & 122B-A10B weights provide directly usable baselines.
Critical edge
No compute, latency, or throughput metrics — critical missing evidence for the "efficient deep research" claim.

M Methodology

AREX runs a bi-level loop: an inner research loop samples search/visit/update_context steps and emits (answer, evidence, confidence ∈ [0,100]) via finish. An outer loop applies the rule:

s ≥ τ → Accept  ·  s < τ, recoverable → Refine  ·  else → Restart  (max 5 rounds, 300 turns)

  1. Progressive mid-training — browse-intensive → reasoning-intensive → mixed-capability consolidation.
  2. Key-step supervision — rule-flagged evidence-discovery / path-rejection / context-update steps get masked loss; their token loss runs +19–29% above ordinary steps.
  3. Step-aware RL — turn-level geometric-mean policy ratio + hierarchical normalization; key-step bonus gated on trajectory success.
  4. Autonomous Context Update (ACU) — policy self-invokes update_context to compress history into a research-objective-organized state (verified findings, unresolved constraints, rejected candidates, next-step plan).

Strengths

  • Conceptual clarity — discovery–verification asymmetry → concrete Accept/Refine/Restart rule with a learnable confidence signal.
  • ACU is genuinely novel — model-invoked, objective-organized, retains negative evidence and rejected candidates, not budget-driven summarization.
  • Comprehensive ablations — Table 3 / 4 / Fig. 4 isolate each component under matched budgets.
  • Step-aware RL — geometric-mean turn-level ratio addresses real long-trajectory credit assignment.
  • Open-weight 4B & 122B-A10B; honest self-critical appendix on self-distillation.
  • Best-in-class WideSearch-en among all compared models, including GPT-5.4 & Gemini-3.1-Pro.

Limitations

  • No compute/latency/throughput — critical missing evidence for efficiency claims.
  • Reproducibility gap — synthetic tasks, teacher trajectories, key-step annotations not clearly released.
  • Apples-to-oranges HLE — AREX on text-only subset; closed-source on full benchmark (denoted *).
  • MiroThinker-H1 outscores AREX on BrowseComp (88.2) & GAIA (88.5); H1's param count undisclosed.
  • 33% over-confidence tail in incorrect outputs even with ACU — could corrupt the outer-loop rule.
  • Generous budget — 300 turns / 5 rounds; gains not shown under tighter compute-matched limits.

? Questions for further exploration

Do gains hold under a strictly compute-matched inference budget vs GPT-5.4 / Gemini-3.1-Pro / MiroThinker-H1?
What is MiroThinker-H1's activated-param count, and is AREX still "comparable-scale" if it isn't?
Can calibration training shrink the 33% over-confidence tail, and how much would that lift the outer loop?
Can the rule-based key-step detector be replaced by a learned, domain-general step-utility estimator?
How does AREX transfer to non-web domains (codebase navigation, multi-doc legal/medical) where constraint decomposition is less crisp?
Does the open release include training tasks & key-step annotations, or only weights — and how does that affect reproduction?
