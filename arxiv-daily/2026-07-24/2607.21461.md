> **一句话总结：** 来自 BAAI 的 AREX 提出一种"递归自改进 (RSI)"的深度研究智能体范式——内层研究循环产出临时答案，外层验证循环按约束审计并定向重启研究；通过自学习 `update_context` 工具压缩轨迹、关键步骤监督与 step-aware RL，122B-A10B MoE 模型在 BrowseComp 上达 82.5、WideSearch-en 上达 82.0、HLE(text-only) 上达 52.4，超过 Qwen3.5-397B 等大参数对手。

> **TL;DR:** AREX is a recursively self-improving deep research agent that interleaves an inner evidence-gathering loop with an outer constraint-wise verification loop that refines or restarts based on an answer-level confidence score; trained via multi-stage agentic mid-training, key-step focused supervision, and step-aware RL on synthetic research tasks, the 122B-A10B MoE (AREX-Base) reaches 82.5 BrowseComp (beating Qwen3.5-397B's 78.6), 89.9 DeepSearchQA (vs DeepSeek-V4-Pro 88.7), and 82.0 WideSearch-en (the best reported score, beating GPT-5.4's 77.5 and Gemini-3.1-Pro's 66.4), while a dense 4B variant (AREX-Turbo) beats Qwen3.5-35B on 5 of 6 benchmarks.

## 核心贡献

- **递归自改进 (RSI) 框架**：将多约束深度研究建模为"发现—验证不对称"问题——发现难、验证易。把部分验证过的答案转换为"改进状态"（保留已验证证据、隔离未决约束），作为下一轮定向研究的起点，验证不再是终点过滤器而是研究轮次间的转移算子。
- **双层循环架构**：内层研究循环积累证据、调用 search/visit 工具、产出带置信度的临时答案；外层自改进循环按置信度判定 Accept / Refine / Restart，最多 5 轮外循环、300 步内循环。
- **自主学习上下文更新工具 (ACU)**：模型自主决定何时调用 `update_context`，把长交互历史压缩为面向当前研究目标的紧凑状态（已验证发现、当前候选、未决约束、被否候选、下一步计划），无需外部模型做摘要。BrowseComp 上 80.3% 的案例调用了该工具，平均在 25,721 tokens 时触发，远低于 128K 上限。
- **关键步骤 (key-step) 监督**：用规则检测器标注"证据发现、路径否决重定向、关键上下文更新"等决策关键步，在单轮全轨迹训练后专门对它们做掩码损失训练——这些步骤的 token loss 比普通步高 19%–29%，说明确为学习难点。
- **Step-aware RL**：用 turn-level 几何均值的策略比率、层级化归一（先 step 后 trajectory）防止长轨迹主导梯度；用关键步骤的有界辅助奖励塑造 advantage，比标准 GRPO 在 BrowseComp 上高 3.1 点。
- **4B 与 122B-A10B 双尺度交付**：开源权重 (HuggingFace BAAI/arex)，并提供 homepage、app、benchmark 结果。

## Key Contributions

- A **discovery–verification asymmetry** principle: discovering a multi-constraint answer is costly, but verifying a candidate decomposes into tractable per-constraint checks. Verification is repurposed as the *transition operator* between research rounds rather than a final filter or local action critic.
- A **bi-level RSI architecture** (Fig. 2): an inner research loop externalizes a provisional answer plus evidence and an answer-level confidence score via a `finish` tool; an outer loop uses the confidence score (threshold τ) to decide Accept / Refine (recoverable) / Restart (uninformative), bounded by a max round count.
- A **learned autonomous context-update (ACU) tool** that the policy itself invokes to compress the interaction history into an improvement state holding verified findings, current candidates, unresolved constraints, validity concerns, rejected candidates, and a next-step plan — organized around the current research objective, not generic summarization.
- A **training pipeline** consisting of: (i) progressive multi-round capability mid-training (browse-intensive first, then reasoning-intensive, then mixed-capability consolidation); (ii) key-step focused supervision with prefix-conditioned loss masking on rule-detected critical steps; (iii) step-aware group policy optimization with hierarchical normalization and key-step reward shaping.
- **Two instantiations**: AREX-Turbo (dense 4B, Qwen3.5-4B backbone) and AREX-Base (122B total / 10B activated MoE, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B backbone), released open-weight.

## Methodology

**Architecture.** Given input x, AREX derives a research objective q⁽¹⁾. In recursive round k, the inner loop samples steps (m, a, o) from a policy π_θ over a search/visit environment, building trajectory h_t⁽ᵏ⁾. The model may invoke `update_context` at chosen τ to produce a refreshed state z_τ⁽ᵏ⁾, after which subsequent conditioning uses effective context h̄_t⁽ᵏ⁾ = z_τ ⊕ (post-τ interactions) instead of the full history (Eq. 5–7). At the end of the loop a structured `finish` interface emits r⁽ᵏ⁾ = (y⁽ᵏ⁾, E⁽ᵏ⁾, s⁽ᵏ⁾) — answer, evidence, and a confidence score in [0,100]. The outer loop (Eq. 10) applies: Accept if s ≥ τ; Refine if s < τ and trajectory is recoverable, refreshing h_0⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁾ with preserved findings P⁽ᵏ⁾ and issue set I⁽ᵏ⁾; Restart otherwise, reinitializing from x alone.

**Data construction.** Tasks are synthesized in three categories (browse-intensive, reasoning-intensive, scientific literature). Experts write templates that extract a latent answer y and a constraint set C(y); constraints are transformed into indirect multi-hop descriptions and verified for uniqueness, evidence availability, and difficulty (rollouts filter shallow-retrieval or unsolvable items). Teacher trajectories from strong models in the same environment pass multi-stage QC: iterative-behavior retention, valid-tool-use check, evidence-grounded answer filtering, and a confidence threshold s_conf ≥ τ_conf.

**Training pipeline.**
1. *Progressive multi-round capability mid-training*: first browse-intensive multi-round trajectories (tool use, navigation, evidence acquisition), then reasoning-intensive data (long-form thinking, hypothesis verification), then a *mixed-capability consolidation* stage that replays key steps from browse trajectories plus academic-paper and knowledge-intensive reasoning tasks to counter capability interference.
2. *Key-step focused supervision*: rule-based detectors flag (a) the first tool call yielding answer-relevant evidence after exploration, (b) the first step rejecting an incorrect hypothesis and redirecting search, (c) key context-update steps. Loss is applied only to the key step's assistant tokens with full prefix context preserved (Eq. L_key).
3. *Step-aware RL*: turn-level geometric-mean policy ratio (Eq. ρ_i,j), hierarchical averaging (within-trajectory then within-group) so longer trajectories don't dominate (Eq. L_step), plus a KL penalty. Step advantage A_i,j = A_i^out + λ_key · B̃_i,j, where the key-step bonus is gated on trajectory success (𝟙[R_i>0]·B_i,j) to avoid rewarding spurious behavior.

## Results & Highlights

- **BrowseComp** (accuracy, deep web research): AREX-Base = **82.5**, beating Qwen3.5-397B (78.6), MiroThinker-H1 (88.2 but vastly more params), Kimi-K2.6 (83.2), GLM-5 (75.9), and within 3.4 points of GPT-5.4 (82.7) / Gemini-3.1-Pro (85.9). AREX-Turbo (4B) = 70.7, beating Qwen3.5-35B (61.0) by +9.7.
- **DeepSearchQA** (F1): AREX-Base = **89.9**, beating DeepSeek-V4-Pro (88.7), GPT-5.4 (88.5), Kimi-K2.6 (92.5), trailing only Gemini-3.1-Pro (93.3). The 4B AREX-Turbo (78.5) beats Qwen3.5-35B (68.5) by +10.0.
- **WideSearch-en** (Item-F1): AREX-Base = **82.0, the highest reported score overall**, beating GPT-5.4 (77.5) by +4.5, Gemini-3.1-Pro (66.4) by +15.6, Kimi-K2.6 (80.8) by +1.2, and Qwen3.5-397B (74.0) by +8.0.
- **GAIA**: AREX-Base = **85.4**, beating Kimi-K2.6 (80.6), Qwen3.5-397B (83.5), Gemini-3.1-Pro (80.6), and within 3.1 points of MiroThinker-H1 (88.5).
- **xbench-DeepSearch-2510**: AREX-Base = **71.0**, beating Qwen3.5-35B (50.3) by +20.7, DeepSeek-V4-Pro (80.0) leads at the top, but AREX-Base uses only 10B activated params.
- **HLE (text-only)**: AREX-Base = **52.4**, beating DeepSeek-V4-Pro (48.2), Qwen3.5-397B (48.3), GLM-5 (50.4), and within range of Gemini-3.1-Pro (51.4).
- **ACU ablation on BrowseComp** (Table 3): without ACU and without outer loop = 59.6; ACU alone raises it to 71.4 (+11.8); outer loop alone raises to 69.8 (+10.2); full system = **82.5**, i.e. +22.9 absolute over the stripped baseline.
- **Confidence score calibration** (Fig. 3): 95.9% of correct outputs land in 90–100 bin (with ACU), while 55.2% of incorrect outputs fall below 60 — supporting the τ-thresholded Accept/Refine/Restart decision rule.
- **Key-step loss analysis** (Fig. 4): ordinary steps = 0.232 loss; evidence discovery 0.277 (+19%), path rejection/redirection 0.298 (+28%), key context-update 0.300 (+29%) — confirming decision-critical steps remain under-fit after full-trajectory training.
- **Training-recipe ablations** (Table 4): replacing progressive multi-round capability training with mixed training → 77.5 (−5.0); replacing key-step supervision with random-step replay → **74.1 (−8.4, largest drop)**; replacing step-aware RL with standard GRPO → 79.4 (−3.1); full AREX = 82.5.
- **Trajectory self-distillation (preliminary)** (Table 5): under a simplified 122B-A10B config without ACU/outer-loop/key-step, self-distillation improves BrowseComp from 52.3 to 57.1 (+4.8), but the authors flag this as a future direction, not a final component.
- **Runtime context-update behavior** (Table 2): on BrowseComp 80.3% of cases call `update_context`; mean active-context size at invocation = 25,721 tokens (median 25,386), only 0.01% at the 128K hard limit — indicating ACU is proactive rather than budget-triggered. Trigger shares: revise search strategy 66.9%, reject candidate 13.6%; content shares: unresolved constraints 95.5%, next-step plan 96.4%, rejected candidates 81.5%.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core novelty is positioning verification as the *transition operator between research rounds* (Refine vs Restart) rather than the more common use as outcome ranker or step-level reward. The framing is a real conceptual reframing but builds closely on prior asymmetric-verification work (Zeng et al. 2025b) and MindDR/Team et al. 2026; the contribution is more of a systematization + control mechanism than a fundamentally new idea. ACU (learned self-invoked `update_context`) is more novel than the outer loop.
- **Rigor**: Solid. The two-part ablation in Table 3 cleanly isolates ACU and the outer loop with matched budgets. Key-step supervision is grounded in verifiable tool observations, not self-reported reasoning, and the loss analysis (Fig. 4) directly supports the design choice. The largest ablation drop (−8.4 from random-step replay) is convincing.
- **Reproducibility**: Partial. Weights are open (HuggingFace BAAI/arex) and the unified interface (search/visit/update_context/finish) is documented. However: (i) the synthetic task dataset, teacher trajectories, and key-step annotations are not explicitly released as of the paper; (ii) evaluation uses internally-controlled protocols (300 inner turns, 5 outer rounds) that may not match third-party harnesses; (iii) the paper does not report token/compute cost or wall-clock latency per task — a real gap for "efficient deep research" claims; (iv) τ (confidence threshold), λ_key, and τ_conf are mentioned but not numerically specified in the main text.
- **Significance**: High for the open-source deep-research-agent community. WideSearch-en's best reported score (82.0) at 10B activated params is notable. But note **baselines are heterogeneous**: GPT-5.4, Opus-4.6, Gemini-3.1-Pro are reported on the *full HLE* (marked with *), while AREX is reported on the *text-only* HLE subset — so HLE comparisons understate closed-source competitors. BrowseComp baselines like Tongyi-DR (43.4) and Quest-35B (64.6) look weak; the strongest named competitors are MiroThinker-H1 (88.2 on BrowseComp, 88.5 GAIA) which actually beats AREX-Base on those two benchmarks, somewhat undercutting the "substantially outperforms comparable-scale baselines" claim.
- **Red flags**:
  - HLE comparison mixes full-benchmark vs text-only subset (denoted *), making direct comparison favorable to AREX.
  - MiroThinker-H1 outscores AREX-Base on BrowseComp (88.2 vs 82.5) and GAIA (88.5 vs 85.4) but uses more activated parameters; the paper frames these as "comparable-scale" without disclosing H1's parameter count.
  - No latency/cost/inference-budget report — critical for "efficient" long-horizon agent claims.
  - The 5 outer-round, 300-turn budget is generous; relative gains might shrink under tighter compute-matched budgets the paper does not test.
  - Confidence calibration (Fig. 3) shows 33.0% of incorrect outputs in the 90–100 bin *with ACU* — a non-trivial over-confidence tail that could derail the Restart/Refine decision rule in practice.
  - xbench-2510 lacks results for several baselines, making cross-comparison thin on that benchmark.

## Strengths

- Clean conceptual framing (discovery–verification asymmetry) translated into a concrete Accept/Refine/Restart control rule with a learnable confidence signal.
- ACU is genuinely novel relative to budget-driven summarization: model-invoked, objective-organized, retaining negative evidence and rejected candidates (not just summaries).
- Comprehensive ablation suite (Table 3, Table 4, Fig. 4) directly supports each design component with matched budgets.
- Step-aware RL with hierarchical normalization addresses a real long-trajectory credit-assignment problem; the geometric-mean turn-level policy ratio is a sensible adaptation.
- Open-weight release of both 4B and 122B-A10B variants, plus a thorough and self-critical appendix on self-distillation.
- Best-in-class WideSearch-en score (82.0) among all compared models, including GPT-5.4 and Gemini-3.1-Pro.

## Limitations

- No compute/latency/throughput metrics — critical missing evidence for efficiency claims.
- Synthetic task dataset, teacher trajectories, and key-step annotations are not clearly released, limiting full reproduction.
- HLE comparison conflates full-benchmark (closed-source, marked *) with text-only subset (AREX) — direct comparison is not apples-to-apples.
- MiroThinker-H1 outscores AREX-Base on BrowseComp and GAIA; the "comparable-scale" framing depends on activated-parameter accounting that is not disclosed for H1.
- Confidence calibration has a 33% over-confidence tail in incorrect outputs even with ACU, which could corrupt the outer-loop decision rule.
- Evaluation protocol (300 turns, 5 outer rounds) is generous; gains are not shown under tighter compute-matched budgets.
- The "preliminary exploration" on self-distillation is acknowledged as not isolated and not adopted into the final system.
- Benchmarks like xbench-2510 have sparse baseline coverage (DeepSeek-Pro appears only there).

## Relevance

本文与"深度研究智能体 / 长程 RL / 自我改进 Agent"主线高度契合：它把验证从被动过滤器提升为研究轮次间的转移算子，并提出自主上下文更新工具与关键步骤监督——对研究长程任务中的上下文管理、稀疏信用分配与递归式 Agent 训练有直接借鉴价值。其开源 4B 与 122B-A10B 权重也为后续在自有评测集上做对比实验提供了直接可用的基线。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do AREX's gains change under a strictly compute-matched inference budget (e.g., fixed tokens or wall-clock per task) against GPT-5.4, Gemini-3.1-Pro, and MiroThinker-H1?
- What is MiroThinker-H1's activated parameter count, and does AREX-Base still "substantially outperform comparable-scale baselines" if H1 is actually comparable?
- Could the 33% over-confidence tail (Fig. 3, 90–100 bin for incorrect outputs *with ACU*) be reduced via calibration training, and how much would that further improve the outer-loop decision rule?
- Can the key-step detector (rule-based) be replaced with a learned, domain-general step-utility estimator that the paper flags as future work?
- How does AREX transfer to non-web domains (codebase navigation, multi-document legal/medical research) where constraint decomposition is less crisp?
- Does the open-weight release include the synthesized training tasks and key-step annotations, or only the model weights — and how does this affect third-party reproduction?
- Are there failure modes where the outer loop's Restart decision discards recoverable information despite the recoverability classifier, and how frequently does the model converge to the highest-confidence-but-wrong answer within the max-round budget?
