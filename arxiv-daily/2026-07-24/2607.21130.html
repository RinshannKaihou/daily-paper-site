1 Key metric
2 Why this matters

A ready-to-use open float16 training framework for RISC-V single-cores — plus concrete FPGA evidence that Zfh is cheap enough to be a default hardware feature.

3 Critical edge

The Zvfh VPU is designed but never benchmarked — the headline 4× speedup is a VLEN/LMUL projection, not a measured result. All software numbers come from the ISA Simulator; no wall-clock, no silicon.

Methodology01

Two-track co-design, both built on existing open-source artifacts rather than greenfield:

  • Software — extend AIfES. Plug in NMSIS-DSP math primitives (Zfh scalar / Zvfh vector), selected at compile time. AIfES-Converter imports PyTorch & TensorFlow models into RISC-V float16 C, with optional layer freezing for transfer learning.
  • Hardware — extend NaxRiscv. A dual-issue RV64GC super-scalar out-of-order core (SpinalHDL). Decoder / LSU / FPU extended for the 36 Zfh instructions; float16 reuses the f32/f64 NaN-boxed unpacking path.
  • Zvfh VPU proposal. 4 lanes, VLEN=64, input/output crossbars; LMUL register grouping split into micro-ops at issue/rename to feed out-of-order execution — except (LMUL)² cases like vrgather.vv.
  • Evaluation. Accuracy & memory measured on the RISC-V ISA Simulator (MLP on MNIST / FashionMNIST / EMNIST). FPGA resources from Vivado 2021.1 on XC7K325T-2FFG900C.

Strengths & limitations03

▲ Strengths

  • Fills a real gap: only framework with RISC-V + full float16 ODT + batch≥1 + open-source, all at once.
  • Concrete silicon cost: +1.15% LUT6 / +0.05% FF on a named FPGA part, 175 MHz unchanged.
  • Honest scoping — measured vs designed are clearly separated; missing VPU eval named as future work.
  • Micro-op LMUL strategy for out-of-order execution is a defensible, non-trivial architectural choice.
  • PyTorch/TF converter + layer freezing fits the realistic "pretrain off-device, fine-tune on-device" flow.

▼ Limitations

  • Zvfh VPU is described, not benchmarked — the 4× figure is a projection (biggest gap vs the "co-design" title).
  • Software numbers are from the ISA Simulator: no wall-clock time, no real-memory, no power.
  • Narrow models/data: only MLPs on MNIST-family. No CNNs, transformers, or ImageNet-scale.
  • No multi-seed variance — worst cell (−0.54) is within plausible seed noise.
  • No head-to-head vs PULP-Trainlib or quantized-INT8 ODT; Table 1 is feature-presence only.

Open questions for further exploration04

  • Does the Zvfh VPU actually deliver the projected 4× over scalar once synthesized — and at what area/frequency cost above the +1.15% / +0.05% Zfh baseline?
  • How does float16 ODT hold up on CNNs / small transformers (CIFAR, ImageNet), where float16 is less benign than on MLPs — is loss-scaling or mixed precision needed?
  • What are the real wall-clock training times and energy numbers on actual RISC-V silicon, versus PULP-Trainlib and quantized-INT8 ODT of comparable footprint?
  • How seed-sensitive is Table 2 — would a multi-seed run with confidence intervals confirm "minimal degradation" or expose it as noise?
  • Does the LMUL micro-op split scale cleanly to LMUL=8, and is there a threshold above which (LMUL)² cases need a dedicated handler?
