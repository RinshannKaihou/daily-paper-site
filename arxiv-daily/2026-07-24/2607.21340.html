From Plausible to Bankable

Three frontier closed-source LLMs cluster within a 0.22-point CM-LRS band on capital-markets workflows, while open-weights Llama trails by ~1 point.

Methodology 01

CM-LRS scores each (workflow, model) output on seven dimensions against reviewer-anchored rubrics (0 = unusable/fabricated, 5 = production-grade). A capital-markets workflow taxonomy — Extraction, Retrieval, Synthesis, Comparison & Reasoning, Drafting — maps each class to default dimension weights (e.g. Extraction emphasises D3 = 0.25; Retrieval emphasises D2 = 0.30).

Five reproducible workflows are built entirely on public/synthetic material: W1 DCM terms, W2 precedent retrieval, W3 issuer-profile synthesis, W4 M&A comparables, W5 ECM terms. A four-judge, three-family protocol — Sonnet 4.6 & GPT-5.5 (in-panel), Haiku 4.5 & Gemini 2.5 Pro (out-of-panel) — scores 104 outputs at temperature 0, all models fed identical 6,000-token inputs.

Deployment principle "Predict with the LLM, calculate with code, decide with the human." Gate for high-stakes regulated workflows: CM-LRS ≥ 4.0 with no dimension < 3.

Open-weights gap is workflow-class-specific Llama trails Sonnet by 2.23 pts on W2 retrieval (2.51 vs 4.74) and 2.15 pts on W3 synthesis (2.33 vs 4.48), but only 0.84 pts on W1 single-doc extraction — the gap concentrates on retrieval/synthesis, not extraction.

Inter-judge agreement (Spearman ρ, 20 per-cell aggregates)

GPT-5.5 ↔ Gemini · ρ = 0.03 · "effectively independent" Sonnet ↔ Haiku · ρ = 0.94 · same family

The two cross-family judges sharing neither panel- nor family-overlap produce independent rankings — the strongest empirical signal that a single-judge protocol would not have surfaced this blind spot.

Buyer takeaway At the current frontier the choice between Sonnet, Opus, and GPT-5.5 is determined by cost, latency, and workflow-class fit — not headline reliability.

Strengths & limitations 03

Strengths

  • Operates at the workflow-output layer where deployment decisions are made — fills the gap left by FinanceBench/FinQA (QA-pair) and RAGAS (component).
  • Four-judge, three-family cross-validation; the ρ = 0.03 finding is itself useful LLM-as-judge methodology evidence.
  • Fully public & reproducible: prompts, rubrics, raw outputs, deterministic verification script under CC BY 4.0.
  • Falsifiable deployment gate slots into NIST AI RMF / EU AI Act / ISO 42001 governance frameworks.

Limitations

  • LLM-as-judge, no human raters — absolute scores are a baseline until inter-rater work vs bankers/compliance.
  • 6,000-token truncation biases toward front-matter; deep-document failure modes (buried covenants) untested.
  • Gemini absent from scored panel (judge only); Mistral / Qwen / DeepSeek untested.
  • Single-turn; no agentic / tool-use / retrieval-pipeline workflows exercised.

Open questions 04

  • Do scores shift under a full chunking/retrieval pipeline vs 6k-token truncation — does the open-weights gap widen or narrow?
  • What is the inter-rater reliability between the four LLM judges and a panel of practising bankers, and does D6 keep its "cleanest separator" status?
  • How does adding Gemini + Mistral/Qwen/DeepSeek to the scored panel affect the frontier-cluster and gap findings?
  • Can dimension weights be empirically derived from revealed-preference regression on real reviewer accept/reject decisions?
  • Does CM-LRS generalise to agentic tool-use workflows, and do D3 / D7 improve when numerical claims are forced through deterministic code?
Scope boundary CM-LRS does not cover MNPI handling, information-barrier integrity, or trade-surveillance / market-abuse controls — these remain compliance-framework responsibilities.
