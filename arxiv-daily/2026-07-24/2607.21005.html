Critical edge

The stability boundary derives from GD / Edge-of-Stability theory, but the headline LLM experiments run on AdamW — the theory–optimizer gap is acknowledged but not closed.

Methodology

Parameters are split into a scale-invariant block u (weights feeding BN/LN, where L(αu,v)=L(u,v)) and the rest v. Normalization makes the loss insensitive to rescaling u, but local curvature is not invariant — so weight decay, which shrinks ‖u‖, should inflate sharpness on the u-subspace.

Three theoretical pillars

  • Curvature explosion (Thm 5.1). λ_max(H(αu,v)) ≥ α⁻²·λ_max(H_uu) — sharpness grows at least quadratically as norms collapse. Verified by controlled rescaling on FNN+BN and ResNet-50.
  • Stability boundary (Prop 5.2). Combining the EoS criterion η·λ_max ≤ 2 with the scaling law yields c* = √(ηρ/2), ρ=‖u‖²·λ_max(H_uu) — a directly monitorable norm threshold.
  • Spike boundary (Prop 5.3). Replacing λ_max with gradient-direction curvature λ_grad sharpens to a single-step-spike threshold c*_spike = √(ηρ_grad/2).

Probes & scale

  • Minimal probes. MNIST FNN ± {BN, LN} with a wd sweep; a 3-layer FNN+BN on y=x₁+2x₂; PCA visualizations showing drift toward a zero-norm limit point.
  • Large scale. 187M LLaMA-style Transformer (16L/16H, one epoch over 100B tokens); ResNet-50 / CIFAR-100 (SGD); synthetic 3x→x with AdamW.
  • Alignment test. Sustained ‖W₁‖ excursions below c*_spike are filtered (merge gap <30 iters, drop <200 iters) and checked against observed spikes.

Results & highlights

  • Curvature explosion, verified. λ_max at the trained endpoint: 15 → 28 → 383 → 3863 as wd sweeps 0 → 0.001 → 0.01 → 0.03 — directly confirming the α⁻² lower bound.
  • Spike-boundary alignment. Sustained excursions of ‖W₁‖ below c*_spike line up temporally with abrupt loss spikes (green = first crossing, red = return to stability).
  • Scale-invariant block dominates. By mid-late training, the u-block λ_max overtakes the non-scale-invariant block and dominates global sharpness.
  • Normalization is necessary. A wd sweep produces no spikes without normalization; pronounced spikes appear with either BN or LN — isolating the interaction as the cause.
  • Module-level mitigation (187M). wd=3 on all modules is highly unstable (final loss 2.720); excluding MLP modules cuts spike frequency and lowers loss to 2.548. Eigenvector decomposition confirms the MLP subspace dominates leading curvature.
  • General. Reproduced across Transformer/AdamW, ResNet-50/SGD, FNN/SGD; across LN and BN; autoregressive and classification.

Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • Clean minimal probes isolate the normalization × wd interaction from confounders.
  • Per-layer / per-module boundaries turn a global phenomenon into an attributable signal.
  • Cross-architecture & cross-optimizer validation (BN+LN, SGD+AdamW).
  • MLP-decay ablation is an immediately deployable pretraining mitigation.
  • Honest framing as a complement to learning-rate criticality.

Limitations

  • Theory is GD-based; LLM experiments use AdamW — theory–optimizer gap open.
  • Instability detection relies on ad hoc thresholds (30-iter merge, 200-iter min); robustness unanalyzed.
  • Only two final-loss values for the headline ablation — no variance / significance.
  • Residual connections break strict layer-wise scale invariance (ResNet boundary is approximate).
  • Spikes ↔ generalization link explicitly left open.

Open questions for further exploration

01

Does c* stay predictive under adaptive optimizers (AdamW, Lion, Sophia) if ρ uses the optimizer's preconditioned curvature rather than the raw Hessian?

02

How does the phenomenon interact with RMSNorm, GroupNorm, weight-normalized layers, or μParam scaling?

03

Can c* drive an online monitor that adaptively schedules weight decay (decay-off as ‖u‖→c*), beating static MLP-decay exclusion?

04

What is the seed-variance of the 2.720 → 2.548 gain, and does it persist at billion-parameter scale with realistic data mixes?

05

Is there a connection between weight-norm-criticality spikes and downstream generalization?

06

Does scaling residual-branch weights (not only Conv→BN) change the boundary's predictive power for ResNets?
