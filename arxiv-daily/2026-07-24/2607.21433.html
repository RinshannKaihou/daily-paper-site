Token Budget Saturation and Mechanistic Early-Detection of Reasoning Non-Convergence in Chain-of-Thought Models

Renuka Oladri / Niveda Jawahar / Abdirisak Mohamed · Univ. of Maryland CMNS; SAP Labs LLC
chain-of-thought test-time compute interpretability early-exit reasoning models
Key finding

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, thinking accuracy saturates at just 256 tokens on GSM8K / MATH-500 — and a layer-20 activation probe predicts AIME non-convergence before any surface cue appears.

2 Why this matters

Quantifies when chain-of-thought stops paying off, and shows internal activations can flag doomed reasoning before it loops — directly actionable for inference-cost reduction in deployed reasoning LLMs.

3 Critical edge

Every generation uses greedy decoding (do_sample=False) — which DeepSeek explicitly advises against. The headline 43.5% non-convergence rate may be a decoding artifact, not an intrinsic model property.

i. Methodology

Two interconnected studies on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (float16, NVIDIA RTX A5000, greedy decoding), each evaluated on 200-problem subsets (seed=42) of GSM8K, MATH-500, and AIME 1983–2024.

Study 1 — Budget forcing. A custom LogitsProcessor counts tokens inside <think>…</think> and, at budget B, forces </think> by setting all other logits to −∞. Sweep B ∈ {256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096} + uncapped 10,000-token ceiling. Minimum sufficient budget B* = smallest B reaching ≥95% of uncapped accuracy, with 1,000-iter bootstrap CIs.

Study 2 — Mechanistic early detection. Regenerate the 200 AIME problems with forward hooks: hidden states on all 28 layers at token 150, and entropy at 9 checkpoint positions (50–300 tokens). Three logistic probes (L-BFGS, 5-fold stratified CV, AUC-ROC with label-flip correction): an activation probe (dim 3,584), a behavioral baseline (7 surface features), and a combined probe. Significance via paired permutation tests (B=10,000 per checkpoint; B=100,000 sweep-level).

Contamination check. All 30 AIME 2025 problems (released Feb 2025, after the model's Jul 2024 cutoff) run uncapped + greedy to rule out memorization.

iii. Results & highlights

  • B* = 256GSM8K · MATH-500
    Both easy/medium benchmarks hit minimum-sufficient budget at the smallest tested setting; accuracy is flat across all higher budgets. 256 tokens already capture ≥95% of uncapped performance.
  • 56.5 / 43.5 %AIME bimodal split
    Of 200 AIME generations: 113 converge (96.5% acc, ~4,100 tok avg) while 87 never emit </think> within 10k tokens (11.5% acc). ~43.5% of AIME compute is spent on doomed generations an oracle could reclaim.

  • r² ≈ 0.186difficulty → convergence
    Problem difficulty explains only ~19% of variance (point-biserial r=0.431, p<0.0001). Within the hard tercile, converged generations hit 100% acc (n=18) vs. 0% for non-converged (n=41) — convergence and correctness are nearly co-extensive on hard problems.
  • +0.074 ∆AUCprobe vs. behavior @ 50 tok
    Activation probe beats the behavioral baseline at 8 of 9 checkpoints (mean ∆AUC +0.035); peak advantage at 50 tokens, where activation AUC=0.615 vs. behavioral 0.541. Sweep-level permutation p=0.063 — directionality is consistent, n=200 is the binding constraint.
  • 100% / 0%AIME 2025 contamination check
    On post-cutoff AIME 2025: converged accuracy 11/11 (100%), non-converged 0/19 (0%). Memorization ruled out — the convergence–correctness link holds on data the model cannot have seen.

iv. Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • Cleanly separates two questions — does more thinking help? and is failure predictable? — and answers each with a purpose-built study.
  • Well-powered statistics for the budget study (bootstrap CIs, Wilson intervals); appropriately conservative for the probe study (permutation tests, dependence-aware sweep test, label-flip correction).
  • Post-cutoff AIME 2025 contamination check — a credibility step many comparable interpretability papers omit.
  • The 256-token saturation result has immediate, actionable inference-cost implications for deployment.
  • Reframes probing from input/current-token properties to a future trajectory property of the full generation.

Limitations

  • Single model (R1-Distill-Qwen-7B); bimodality, 256-token saturation, and the layer-20 peak are not shown to generalize.
  • Greedy decoding conflicts with DeepSeek's recommended settings (temp ~0.6) and may inflate the non-convergence rate.
  • Practical 10,000-token ceiling means some "non-converged" generations might terminate given more budget.
  • n=200 with fold sizes ~40 leaves per-checkpoint tests underpowered; some bootstrap cells reach n=4.
  • Probe shows predictiveness, not causality — activation-patching interventions are out of scope.
  • Historical AIME baseline slightly inflated by partial memorization (6.6% non-converged acc vs. 0% on AIME 2025).
  • Code/data release gated on acceptance, limiting immediate third-party reproduction.

v. Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the bimodal pattern — and the 43.5% non-convergence rate — persist under DeepSeek's recommended temperature (~0.6) and across seeds, or is it largely a greedy-decoding artifact?
  2. Do nonlinear probes (MLP / attention-based) or multi-layer windowed aggregation push layer-20 AUC from ~0.61 to operationally useful levels (≥0.85) for a real early-exit system?
  3. Does the layer-20 signal generalize to other distilled reasoners (QwQ, R1-Distill-Llama) and larger R1 checkpoints, or is it specific to the Qwen-7B distillation?
  4. Can activation patching at layer 20 causally rescue non-converging trajectories — or does the probe detect a symptom rather than the cause of looping?
  5. Is the 256-token saturation threshold stable under non-greedy decoding and harder OOD math, or does it shift upward when the model samples?
  6. What exactly does the layer-20 probe read out — a coherent problem representation, an early strategy choice, or implicit confidence calibration?
