> **一句话总结：** 作者发现 LLM 后训练量化在让拒绝率、过度拒绝和多选题偏差控制保持不变的同时，会让开放式生成中的刻板印象输出率攀升到约 24%–27%；他们提出 QuantiBias 基准，用多语言开放式生成探针配对短形式安全控制、按实测 bpw 评分、对比是否启用推理，并量化所生成内容的危害严重度。

> **TL;DR:** Across the Qwen3.6-27B precision ladder (16 to 1.13 measured bpw), quantization leaves refusal (~0.98 on StrongREJECT), over-refusal (~0.33 on XSTest), and multiple-choice bias (~0.97 on BBQ) flat, while an independent judge (Claude Sonnet-5) flags a stereotype in ~24%–27% of open-ended MBTP answers across eight languages; reasoning roughly halves this rate on Qwen but leaves Gemma unchanged, and CEB independently confirms the rise (lean judge 3.5%→24.9%, independent 13.5%→37.6% from Q4 to one bit).

## 核心贡献

- **揭示"选择性盲区"现象**：在 Qwen3.6-27B 与 Gemma-4-31B 两个骨干、五个模型家族和八个基准上，量化让标准评估所测的短形式安全控制（拒绝、过度拒绝、多选题偏差）几乎不变，而开放式生成中的偏差却悄悄升高——一个被既有评测漏掉的缺口。
- **提出 QuantiBias 基准**：将生成式、多语言（八种语言）刻板印象探针 MBTP，与拒绝、过度拒绝、多选题控制配对，加上推理开关对比与 0–4 分内容严重度评分，组成可复用的评测协议。
- **统一用实测 bits-per-weight (bpw) 作为横轴**：直接读取模型文件中每个张量的大小与量化类型得到真实精度，避免标称标签（如"4-bit"、"2-bit"）将不同压缩程度的构建混淆——名义标签会比实测值高估量化程度 11%–40%。
- **给出机理性解释**：基于 logit 边界模型说明为何训练直接强化的行为（拒绝、多选题）有大边界而能幸存，而"在开放生成中不主动陈述刻板印象"这种从未被直接训练的行为边界窄、易被量化扰动击穿；量化器在编码/聊天校准数据上分配精度，本身不携带偏差信号，进一步加剧这种不对称。
- **定位推理作为家族依赖的缓解手段**：在 Qwen 上开启推理把 MBTP 偏差率大致减半并把压缩斜率从 +3.6 压到 +0.7，但在 Gemma 上几乎无效（IQ2 处 0.069 off vs 0.064 on），说明推理只在会"回头修正刻板印象决策"的家族上奏效。

## Key Contributions

- **Documents a "selective blindness" effect**: across two anchor backbones (Qwen3.6-27B, Gemma-4-31B), a five-family screen, and eight benchmarks, quantization keeps the short-form safeguards standard evaluations measure (refusal, over-refusal, multiple-choice bias avoidance) flat while the bias a model volunteers in open-ended generation rises unmonitored.
- **Introduces the QuantiBias benchmark**: a reusable protocol pairing a generative, multilingual stereotype probe (MBTP, 8 languages, 13 social categories) with refusal / over-refusal / multiple-choice controls, a reasoning-on/off contrast, and a 0–4 content-severity rubric scored by an independent judge.
- **Re-indexes every result to measured effective bits per weight (bpw)**: nominal quantizer labels overstate the degree of compression by 11–40% because they hide the still-high-precision embedding/attention/output-head tensors; reading bpw directly from the artifact prevents conflating differently-compressed builds under the same label.
- **Provides a mechanistic account**: a logit-margin model (R(b)=∫ρ(m)Φ(−m/σ(b))dm) explains why alignment-reinforced behaviors (refusal, MCQ) survive behind wide margins while untargeted "do not volunteer a stereotype" behavior sits on a narrow margin and degrades, with excess endorsement growing as ~2^(−2b).
- **Shows reasoning is a family-dependent, not universal, safeguard**: deliberation roughly halves MBTP endorsement on Qwen and flattens the compression slope (+3.6 → +0.7 points), but barely moves it on Gemma, a contrast the authors attribute to whether the deliberation actually revisits the stereotype decision.

## Methodology

The paper builds a measurement instrument rather than a new model. The pipeline has four pieces.

**(1) Effective-bpw measurement.** Each model artifact is read directly: the size and quantization type of every stored weight tensor are read, total weight bytes are divided by parameter count, yielding measured effective bpw. This matters because conventional low-bit builds (llama.cpp k-quant schemes) keep embeddings, attention, and the output head at higher precision, so e.g. the "2-bit" UD-IQ2_XXS build mixes nine quantization types with MLP weights at 2.14 bits but output-head/attention at 4.53/3.55 bits, giving a measured bpw of 2.789. Nominal labels overstate compression by 11–40%. The resolved backend (kernel precision) is also recorded per run.

**(2) The QuantiBias protocol** has four components: a generative, paired, multilingual probe (MBTP — a frozen 320-item balanced subset across Arabic/Chinese/English/French/German/Japanese/Russian/Spanish and 13 social categories, where each item is an open-ended request plus a stereotype-endorsing claim and a group-swapped counterpart that fixes the axis a judge scores, plus a parallel-translated variant holding content fixed while varying only language); scoring against measured effective bpw rather than nominal label; a reasoning-load contrast (deliberation on vs off at run time); and short-form controls (refusal on StrongREJECT, over-refusal on XSTest, multiple-choice bias on BBQ, next-token bias on StereoSet) whose flatness establishes the effect is selective rather than a general behavioral change. Capability anchors MMLU-Redux and GSM8K separate safety-specific degradation from general capability loss, and CEB provides a disjoint-item generative bias instrument.

**(3) Judging.** The full-ladder probe is scored by an in-family Qwen3-8B lean judge (validated to recover the binary neutral-vs-endorsing distinction at ~94% against MBTP's labeled reference), with two independent never-quantized frontier judges — Claude Sonnet-5 (Anthropic) and Gemini-2.5-pro (Google) — used as out-of-family validation. The claim the authors defend is the *selective gap* (safeguards flat, open-ended bias high), not the absolute endorsement level, which is judge-family-dependent; the lean judge systematically understates it. On a controlled parallel probe (two middle rungs, n=160), the two independent judges agree on 85% of items (Krippendorff's α = 0.69).

**(4) Severity.** Each one-bit lean endorsement is re-scored on a 0–4 content-severity rubric by a judge independent of the evaluation set: 0 = no biased content, 4 = a biased generalization asserted as fact. This separates frequency of endorsement from per-generation harm.

**(5) Mechanistic model.** Quantization perturbs weights by ∆b = Qb(W) − W with E‖∆b‖² ∝ 2^(−2b). Linearizing the logit margin m favoring the non-endorsing continuation gives an induced shift ε = ∇W m · ∆b that is approximately zero-mean with variance σ²(b) ∝ 2^(−2b), so the population endorsement rate is R(b) = ∫ρ(m) Φ(−m/σ(b)) dm — a Gaussian-smoothing tail probability. Under a margin distribution skewed toward declining, three properties follow that the data confirm: rate is non-decreasing as precision falls; behaviors with margins above some mmin ≫ σ stay exponentially flat (selectivity is a property of margin location, not perturbation size); and at the high-precision end the excess over the clean rate grows as 2^(−2b).

## Results & Highlights

- **Selective gap on the Qwen3.6-27B anchor**: refusal of harmful requests stays at 0.98 across the full ladder (16 → 1.13 measured bpw), XSTest over-refusal near 0.33, and BBQ multiple-choice bias accuracy near 0.97 at every rung — yet under the independent Claude Sonnet-5 judge the open-ended MBTP endorsement rate is 0.238, 0.267, 0.266 across the reasoning-off ladder (Q4 to one bit), roughly one open-ended answer in four. The in-family lean judge, by contrast, reports a low but rising rate of 0.042, 0.053, 0.078.
- **StereeoSet next-token control is flat at 58–60 / 50-ideal across the ladder**, so a study measuring only short-form outcomes would conclude quantization is safety-neutral.
- **Capability anchors hold through IQ2**: MMLU-Redux drops from 0.952 only to 0.877 at the one-bit extreme, separating safety-specific degradation from general capability loss; from Q4 to IQ2 capability is flat while generative bias already rises.
- **Reasoning roughly halves the effect on Qwen** and flattens the compression slope from +3.6 to +0.7 points per rung (Table A3 / Figure A4); on the Gemma-4-31B backbone the compression effect replicates (MBTP reasoning off 0.038 at Q4 rising to 0.069 at IQ2, n=480) but reasoning barely moves it — Q4 identical either way, IQ2 at 0.069 off vs 0.064 on — making deliberation a family-dependent safeguard rather than a universal one.
- **Independent benchmark agreement (CEB)**: on items disjoint from MBTP (n ≈ 960 per rung, reasoning off), the lean judge reports 3.5% / 21.9% / 24.9% and the independent judge 13.5% / 23.0% / 37.6% from Q4 to one bit — same direction and the same judge-dependence, ruling out an MBTP-construction artifact.
- **Content severity of one-bit endorsements**: across 48 lean-judge endorsements, mean severity is 1.83 on the 0–4 rubric, with 33/48 scoring ≥ 2 (a stated group generalization), none reaching the severity-4 ceiling, and 10/48 scoring 0 (bounding the false-positive rate of the binary lean at about a fifth).
- **Five-family screen resolves the level but not the per-family slope**: baselines differ about twofold across families (8B class above the 27B anchor, consistent with size fragility); an independent re-scoring (Claude Sonnet-5, n=60) puts every family at 20–30%, three to four times the in-family lean rate, while every Q8-to-Q2 change lies inside its 95% interval, so per-family compression slope is unresolved at this sample size.
- **Nominal-label correction**: measured bpw for the Qwen ladder is 5.235 (nominal 4-bit), 2.789 (nominal 2-bit), 2.128 (ternary), 1.128 (1-bit), so nominal labels overstate compression by 11–40% — a measurement-hygiene point the paper makes central.

## Authors & Credibility

- Sole author **Emilio Ferrara**, Thomas Lord Department of Computer Science, University of Southern California. Ferrara is a well-cited researcher on AI bias and network science; two of his own prior results are load-bearing in the paper's motivation — the "butterfly effect" account of AI bias (Ferrara, 2024) and a multi-dimensional audit of bias-reduction methods (Chand et al., 2026) — and he cites both critically rather than self-promotionally.
- Code, rubrics, probe, and baseline results are released at `https://huggingface.co/datasets/emilioferrara/quantibias`, satisfying the reproducibility bar for a benchmark paper.
- Methodology uses a checkpointed, idempotent harness that writes verbatim generations to an append-only store, with every number tracing to a job identifier and content hash; decoding (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, fixed token budgets, the model's own chat template) is held constant across precisions.
- All quantitative claims are reported with Wilson 95% intervals; the dose-response finding is explicitly tagged "provisional" where the independent judge contradicts the lean judge, which is an unusually honest handling of a measurement disagreement.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine. The "selective gap" framing — short-form safeguards flat while open-ended generation carries a high stereotype rate that no checklist samples — is sharper than prior compression-safety work (Xu et al. 2024; Marcuzzi et al. 2026; Rath & Maliakkal 2026; Hua et al. 2026), which mostly report aggregate effects. The added content-severity axis and the measured-bpw re-indexing are non-trivial measurement contributions.
- **Rigor**: Strong on instrumentation (effective bpw read from the artifact, never the nominal label; resolved backend recorded; verbatim generations with content hashes; Wilson intervals) but **weaker than it claims on the headline statistic**. The abstract's "~24% to ~27%" rests on a single independent judge (Claude Sonnet-5) on the anchor backbone; the dose-response claim — "whether open-ended bias further increases with compression is less certain" — is the paper's own hedge, and the independent re-scoring returns a roughly flat profile (0.238/0.267/0.266) that *contradicts* the rising 0.042/0.053/0.078 the in-family lean judge reports. The honest reading is: the **level** is robust, the **slope** is not.
- **Missing ablations**: (a) The full-sample diagonal cells have *not* been re-scored with the independent ensemble — the Limitations section flags this explicitly, and it is the single biggest caveat on the abstract's level claim. (b) The power analysis (Cohen's h ≈ 0.15) says several hundred to >1000 paired prompts per cell are needed to resolve the per-family slope; the screen used n ≤ 320 (lean) and n=60 (independent), which is underpowered and leaves every family's compression slope unresolved. (c) Activation precision is held constant and not varied, so the treatment is only weight precision. (d) The parallel-translated probe pilot covers only two middle rungs at 80 items each and does not reach the one-bit rung where the slope concentrates.
- **Reproducibility**: High for the benchmark itself — the probe, scoring code, and harness are released — but the artifact ladder includes a private "Ternary-Bonsai" build and end-to-end ternary/one-bit builds whose exact recipes are not fully specified in the body; some precision choices depend on hardware (kernels) the current cluster cannot vary.
- **Significance**: Practically important. The paper directly challenges the deployment assumption that "quantization is safety-neutral because capability benchmarks hold," and gives a concrete instrument to re-evaluate quantized builds before release.
- **Red flags**: The in-family lean judge (Qwen3-8B) is *uncorrelated* with both independent judges on the controlled probe (Limitations), yet the lean judge's rising-with-compression profile is what drives the abstract's slope claim and Figure 2's lower panel — a dependence on a known-biased instrument (Wataoka et al. 2024 self-preference) that the paper acknowledges but does not fully defuse. The Mechanistic Section 7 is explicitly "an account, not a proof," and its prediction that mean severity s̄(b) rises with compression is *not measured* (only frequency is); the paper says so, but readers should not treat the severity channel as a result.

## Strengths

- The measured-effective-bpw re-indexing is a real methodological improvement that other compression papers should adopt; it exposes that "2-bit" builds are actually 2.2–2.8 bpw, which materially changes how one reads every prior result.
- The selective-gap framing — pairing the open-ended probe with the short-form controls that establish *selectivity* rather than a general behavioral change — is a clean experimental design.
- Intellectual honesty about judge dependence: the paper reports the lean/independent disagreement openly, tags the slope as provisional, and pre-registers the independent ensemble as the reference measurement that supersedes the lean judge.
- Multilingual scope across eight languages with parallel-translated items (back-translated for drift control) is unusual for a compression study and directly tests the Al Hakim et al. (2026) finding that damage is worst in non-English settings.
- Independent corroboration via CEB (disjoint items, same direction) rules out an MBTP-construction artifact.

## Limitations

- Full-sample bias results rest on a single in-family judge whose absolute level is judge-family-dependent; the independent-ensemble re-scoring needed to settle the level has been run only on a small pilot (two middle rungs, 80 items each).
- The dose-response (slope) finding is judge-dependent: the lean judge shows a rise, the independent judge shows a roughly flat high rate — so the per-family and per-rung slopes remain genuinely unresolved.
- The full-sample evidence rests on one backbone (Qwen3.6-27B); the Gemma-4-31B replication and the CEB instrument point the same way but are underpowered (Cohen's h ≈ 0.15 implies several hundred to >1000 paired prompts per cell).
- Activation precision is held constant; varying it would require hardware not available on the current cluster, so the treatment is partial.
- The screening probe is sensitive to the fast path by design (reasoning off), so families whose degradation lives on the deliberative path would be missed.
- The multilingual probe is a diagonal design in which language of presentation, national target, and content are entangled; between-language comparisons are confounded, though the within-language compression slopes are not.
- Section 7's mechanistic account is consistent with the data but is explicitly not a proof, and the severity channel is a prediction rather than a measured result.

## Relevance

这份论文对量化部署与安全评测都有直接价值：它指出当前常用的"能力基准保持即视为安全"的量化验收方式在开放式生成场景下存在系统性盲区，并给出可立即使用的 QuantiBias 基准，可用于在发布量化模型前重新评估多语言开放式偏差。对于关注 LLM 后训练量化、模型评测、对齐鲁棒性与多语言安全的研究工作高度相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the open-ended bias slope with compression replicate once the full ladder is re-scored by the independent ensemble (rather than only the in-family lean judge)? The current evidence is split: lean judge rising, independent judge roughly flat.
- Would protecting bias-critical weights during quantization (à la Al Hakim et al. 2026) — using bias-signal-bearing calibration data rather than coding/chat data — close the selective gap, and at what capability cost?
- Why does deliberation halve the bias on Qwen but not on Gemma? Is the difference the reasoning trace structure, the family's training, or whether the deliberation actually revisits the stereotype decision?
- Does the predicted severity channel (s̄(b) rising with compression, H(b)=f(b)·s̄(b)) hold up under a graded severity rubric, given that the current instrument measures only frequency?
- How do the slopes change once activation precision (not just weight precision) is varied on appropriate hardware?
- Can a parallel-translated probe that fully disentangles language of presentation from national target and content confirm that the bias damage is indeed worse in non-English settings, as the diagonal-design data hint?
