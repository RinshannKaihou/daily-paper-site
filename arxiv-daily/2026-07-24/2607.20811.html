01

Methodology

parameterized complexity × convex QP

ReLU pipeline

  1. Sign normalization (L1–2) — rescale so every deep edge has weight in {−1,+1}; collapse the deep-weight search to 2w branches.
  2. Hyperplane partitions (L3) — enumerate all partitions of D induced by an affine hyperplane in |D|O(n) via LP-extremal points; repairs a gap in Arora et al. 2018.
  3. Deeper neurons (L4) — bound distinct active/dead partitions at u by |D|O(n·2x).
  4. Branch & solve (Thm 5) — over sign + partition choices, solve a least-squares QP via the ellipsoid method.

Linear pipeline

  1. Bias sliding (Obs 11) — in linearly-activated nets, biases migrate to output neurons only.
  2. Untangling — partition nodes into blocks so each input is a unique source and each output a unique sink; block adjacency mirrors reachability.
  3. Regression (Thm 12) — in/out-branchings select active edges; remaining path-sums reduce to a multivariate QP in poly(|D|+|V|).
  4. Finding untanglings (Thm 13–15) — NP-hard in general; FPT-linear for k hidden neurons and for treewidth-t.

Results & Highlights

asymptotic, no experiments
|D|O(n·w·2w)·poly(L) Global optimum for every constant-size ReLU arch with hidden out-degree ≤ 1; matches the Froese et al. 2022 lower bound.
poly(|D|+|V|) LIN-NNT solved exactly given an untangling, beating general ∃R-hardness (Abrahamsen et al. 2021).
o·λℓ+1 Blow-up size bound on hidden neurons (ℓ layers of λ each, o outputs); same depth, no loss in training accuracy.
kO(k)·|V|  /  f(t)·|V| Untangling recognition: NP-hard generally, but FPT-linear in hidden-neuron count and in treewidth (MSOL + Courcelle).

Strengths

  • First non-trivial poly-time algorithms in two genuinely open regimes: multi-output / multi-hidden-layer ReLU, and non-fully-connected linear nets.
  • Rigorous, self-contained proofs; corrects a gap in Arora et al. 2018's hyperplane enumeration.
  • Blow-up construction is an elegant bridge from the out-degree-1 class to arbitrary constant-size archs, with explicit depth- and expressivity-preservation.
  • Clean dichotomy: upper bounds paired with the matching Froese et al. 2022 lower bound.

Limitations

  • Polynomial only for constant n, w. The 2w exponent rules out use beyond toy networks.
  • No empirical evaluation — all results asymptotic, no implementation or runtime demo.
  • Untangling recognition is NP-hard in general; even FPT cases rarely match real architectures.
  • Blow-up can change the hypothesis class (improper-learning-like) and may worsen generalization error.
  • Thm 15's Courcelle dependence is non-elementary in treewidth; does not extend to sigmoid/tanh.

Can ReLU-NNT be solved for constant-size architectures with hidden out-degree > 1? — authors, §7

Do these results extend to other loss functions (ℓ₁, logistic)? — authors, §7

Can the 2w factor in the ReLU runtime be reduced, or is it also lower-bound-matching beyond the single-neuron case? — reader

Is there a polynomial-time algorithm for finding untanglings on wider classes — bounded depth or bounded cut-width DAGs? — reader

Does the blow-up preserve any non-trivial generalization bound (PAC-Bayes, Rademacher) beyond training loss, given the improper-learning caveat? — reader

Does sign-normalization (L1) extend to leaky-ReLU or piecewise-linear activations with more than two affine pieces? — reader
