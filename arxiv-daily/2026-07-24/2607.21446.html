§ 01 Methodology

Because AdaLN sits between DiT blocks, the LLM "absorption trick" breaks — so the activation transform must run online at every denoising step, making it both cheap and expressive.

  • Structure. T = diag(K⁽¹⁾…K⁽⌈d/32⌉⁾), each K = G₁⊗G₂⊗G₃⊗G₄⊗G₅ — a Kronecker product of five 2×2 unit-determinant LU factors.
  • Parametrization. Three free scalars (aₖ, bₖ, cₖ) per factor → 15 params per 32×32 block (68× smaller than dense GL(32)). Inversion = five independent 2×2 inversions.
  • Init. Each factor starts at the 2×2 Hadamard rotation → T₀ is a block-diagonal Hadamard, a strong outlier-suppressing start.
  • Layer-wise optimization. Minimize L = L_out + λₐLₐ + λ_wL_w with auto-scaled λ; Adam + STE through the quantizer. Joint multi-layer training was unstable — left to future work.
  • Composable. Drop-in replacement for SmoothQuant inside the LoRaQ weight-correction pipeline; combination beats either alone.

  • LPIPS — strongest headline. Best on every SDCI configuration; −17.5% relative on FLUX.1-schnell/SDCI.
  • Ablation isolates the transform. Without LoRaQ, KroQuant cuts FID 130.6 → 41.8 on PixArt-Σ/MJHQ. Holding LoRaQ fixed, the 15 learnable params beat a fixed Hadamard-32 by ΔLPIPS 0.035 and +0.8 dB PSNR.
  • Kernel. On MI350/FP16, K1 is up to 14% faster than per-channel Scale; K2 reaches 2.45× over FWHT while keeping 68× parameter compression.
  • Honest weak spot. On FLUX.1-schnell/MJHQ-30K: best FID (19.8) & PSNR (16.9) but loses IR (0.880 vs. 0.952) and LPIPS (0.336 vs. 0.331) — attributed to double-stream/joint-attention heterogeneity.

§ 03 Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • Occupies a genuinely empty design point: first learned, non-diagonal, online transform for DiT PTQ.
  • Parameter-efficient & hardware-native: 15 scalars/block, single fused tensor-core GEMM, supports any d divisible by 32.
  • Strong results on 2/3 models and across all three on SDCI; clean ablation.
  • Honest reporting — includes a bare-quantizer lower bound (Q4) and explicit failure modes.

Limitations

  • Tied to n = 32; Kronecker factor count grows too slowly to track GL(n) at larger blocks.
  • Mixed results on the largest model (FLUX.1-schnell 12B) on MJHQ-30K — diagnosed, not resolved.
  • Layer-wise optimization only; joint multi-layer unstable under the unit-det LU parametrization.
  • Higher-contrast output artifact left theoretically unexplained.
  • No LLM evaluation — DiT-specific by design.

§ 04 Questions for further exploration

  • Q1End-to-end wall-clock? Full denoising-loop image/sec of K1/K2 over SmoothQuant & FWHT — how much headroom survives once the fused Q4 quantizer dominates?
  • Q2Joint-attention-aware variant? Separate Kronecker factors for text vs. image token slices — can it recover FLUX.1-schnell/MJHQ losses?
  • Q3Better parametrization? Stiefel-manifold or QR-based instead of unit-det LU — would it stabilize joint multi-layer optimization?
  • Q4Scale to n ∈ {64, 128}? Can factor count grow fast enough before hitting the MXFP4 block-size constraint?
  • Q5Higher-contrast artifact? Quantization-induced magnitude redistribution biasing the decoder — can a small output-side recalibration remove it?
  • Q6Transfer across checkpoints? Calibrate once, deploy widely — or per-checkpoint recalibration required?
  • Q7Non-DiT regimes? Could the same construction help streaming/norm-blocked LLM layers where absorption also fails?
