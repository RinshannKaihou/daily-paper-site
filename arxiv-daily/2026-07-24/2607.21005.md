> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"权重范数临界性"(weight-norm criticality)这一新机制，揭示归一化层引入的尺度不变分量在权重衰减持续收缩下会逼近零范数，导致 Hessian 曲率以 α⁻² 量级爆炸并触发损失尖峰；据此推导出逐层稳定性边界 c* = √(ηρ/2)，并在 187M Transformer 与 ResNet-50 上验证：当权重范数跌破该边界时损失尖峰恰好出现，且关闭 MLP 模块的权重衰减可将尖峰大幅减少并使最终损失从 2.720 降至 2.548。

> **TL;DR:** Beyond learning-rate criticality (Edge of Stability), the authors identify a "weight-norm criticality": in networks with normalization (BN/LN), weight decay persistently shrinks the norms of scale-invariant weights toward zero, which provably amplifies the Hessian's top eigenvalue as λ_max ∝ ∥u∥⁻² and destabilizes training. They derive a layer-wise stability boundary c* = √(ηρ/2) and validate it on a 187M-parameter LLaMA-style Transformer (100B-token corpus) and ResNet-50 (CIFAR-100): on synthetic data with BN, λ_max grows from 15 (wd=0) to 3863 (wd=0.03) — a ~257× explosion — and excursions of the first-layer norm below c* align with observed loss spikes; ablating weight decay on MLP modules reduces spike frequency and lowers the final loss from 2.720 to 2.548.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"权重范数临界性"概念**：作为对传统学习率临界性 (Edge of Stability) 的补充机制，揭示归一化与权重衰减的相互作用是损失尖峰的一个被忽视的来源。
- **理论：曲率爆炸定理 (Theorem 5.1)**：对正尺度不变分量 u，λ_max(H(αu, v)) ≥ α⁻² · λ_max(H_uu(u, v))，给出 Hessian 曲率随权重范数收缩而爆炸的下界。
- **理论：权重范数稳定性边界 (Proposition 5.2)**：c* = √(ηρ/2)，其中 ρ = ∥u∥² · λ_max(H_uu)；将 EoS 的曲率阈值翻译为可监测的权重范数阈值。
- **理论：基于梯度方向曲率的尖峰边界 (Proposition 5.3)**：c*_spike = √(ηρ_grad/2)，刻画实际触发单步损失增加的更精细条件。
- **逐层 / 逐模块可归因性**：边界可在每个尺度不变分量上独立计算，从而将尖峰定位到具体层或模块（而非整网），并用模块级 Hessian 特征向量分解定位到 MLP 子空间。
- **大规模实验验证并给出实用调参启示**：在 187M LLaMA-style Transformer 上证明，仅关闭 MLP 模块的权重衰减即可显著降低尖峰频率并降低最终损失，为正则化不能任意增强提供机理性解释。

## Key Contributions

- **Introduces "weight-norm criticality"** as a complementary mechanism to the prevailing learning-rate criticality (Edge of Stability) view of loss spikes, identifying the normalization–weight-decay interaction as an overlooked source of training instability in architectures containing scale-invariant components (e.g., BN, LN).
- **Theoretical curvature-explosion result (Theorem 5.1)**: For a positively scale-invariant component u, shrinking it by α amplifies curvature as λ_max(H(αu, v)) ≥ α⁻² · λ_max(H_uu(u, v)), giving a rigorous lower bound on sharpness growth as norms collapse.
- **Weight-norm stability boundary (Prop. 5.2)**: c* = √(ηρ/2) with ρ = ∥u∥² · λ_max(H_uu), translating the EoS curvature threshold η·λ_max ≤ 2 into a directly monitorable weight-norm threshold.
- **Refined spike boundary (Prop. 5.3)**: Uses gradient-direction curvature λ_grad(H_uu) = g_uᵀ H_uu g_u / ∥g_u∥² to derive c*_spike = √(ηρ_grad/2), characterizing when a single GD step actually increases the loss.
- **Per-layer / per-module attribution**: The boundary decomposes across scale-invariant components, allowing instability to be localized (the framework does not require global scale invariance, so it applies to Transformers with LN). Module-wise top-Hessian-eigenvector decomposition further localizes the dominant curvature to the MLP subspace.
- **Practical, actionable mitigation at scale**: On a 187M-parameter LLaMA-style Transformer, simply disabling weight decay on MLP modules substantially reduces spike frequency and lowers the final loss (2.720 → 2.548), demonstrating the mechanism informs real regularization choices.

## Methodology

The paper combines theory, controlled probes, and large-scale experiments.

**Setup and motivation.** The authors partition parameters of L(·) into a scale-invariant block u (e.g., weights feeding into BN/LN, where L(αu, v) = L(u, v) for α > 0) and the rest v. Because normalization makes the loss insensitive to rescaling u while local curvature is *not* invariant, weight decay — which persistently shrinks ∥u∥ — should inflate the Hessian's sharpness on the u-subspace.

**Theory (Section 5).**
1. *Homogeneity of the Hessian (Theorem 5.1).* Under positive scale invariance, λ_max(H(αu, v)) ≥ α⁻² · λ_max(H_uu(u, v)). Verified by controlled parameter rescaling on a 3-layer FNN+BN and on ResNet-50 (Conv→BN weights rescaled).
2. *Stability boundary (Prop. 5.2).* Combining the EoS criterion η·λ_max(H(u,v)) ≤ 2 with the scaling law yields a critical norm c* = √(ηρ/2) below which linear stability is lost.
3. *Spike boundary (Prop. 5.3).* Replacing λ_max with the gradient-direction curvature λ_grad gives a sharper single-step-spike threshold c*_spike = √(ηρ_grad/2).

**Controlled probes.** (i) MNIST FNN with Norm ∈ {Identity, BN, LN} and a weight-decay sweep shows spikes appear *only* when normalization is present (Fig. 2). (ii) A minimal 3-layer FNN+BN on y = x₁ + 2x₂ reproduces the phenomenon in isolation (Fig. 4). (iii) PCA-subspace visualizations (Figs. 3, 4b) show converged solutions drift along a principal direction toward a "zero-limit point" (scale-invariant params set to 0) where curvature is enormous.

**Large-scale validation.** (a) LLaMA-style 187M Transformer (16 layers, 16 heads) trained for one epoch on a 100B-token corpus, sweeping wd ∈ {0, 0.5, 1} (Fig. 1a). (b) ResNet-50 on CIFAR-100 with SGD, sweeping wd (Fig. 1b). (c) Synthetic 3x→x next-token task with a Transformer trained by AdamW (Fig. 8), plus a 187M Transformer with wd=3 applied to all modules vs. excluding MLP (Fig. 9). (d) Module-wise decomposition of the top Hessian eigenvector quantifies each block's contribution to the dominant curvature direction.

**Alignment tests (Fig. 7).** A filtering protocol (merge adjacent unstable intervals separated by <30 iterations, drop intervals shorter than 200 iterations) is applied to identify "sustained" excursions of ∥W₁∥ below c*_spike; these excursions are then checked for temporal alignment with pronounced loss spikes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Curvature explosion with weight decay on a 3-layer FNN+BN (synthetic data, η = 0.03):** the top Hessian eigenvalue λ_max at the trained endpoint grows from **15 (wd=0)** to **28 (wd=0.001)**, **383 (wd=0.01)**, and **3863 (wd=0.03)** — roughly a **257× increase**, directly verifying the α⁻² lower bound of Theorem 5.1 (Fig. 5a; values from Fig. 3 legend).
- **Spike-boundary alignment on MNIST+BN and synthetic+BN (Fig. 7):** sustained excursions of the first layer's weight norm ∥W₁∥ below the predicted c*_spike (red shaded regions) line up with abrupt loss spikes, with green dashed lines marking the first crossing and red dashed lines the return to stability — empirical confirmation that violations of the derived boundary coincide with spike onset.
- **Scale-invariant block dominates global sharpness (Fig. 6):** by mid-to-late training, the λ_max of the scale-invariant Hessian block (orange) surpasses the non-scale-invariant block (green) and becomes the dominant contribution to the global λ_max (blue), consistent with the homogeneity analysis.
- **Normalization is necessary for wd-induced spikes (Fig. 2):** on MNIST, sweeping wd produces no spikes without normalization, but pronounced spikes appear with either BatchNorm or LayerNorm — isolating the interaction as the cause rather than wd alone.
- **Module-level mitigation on a 187M Transformer (Fig. 9):** applying wd=3 to all modules gives highly unstable training with frequent spikes and a final loss of **2.720**; excluding MLP modules from weight decay substantially reduces spike frequency and lowers the final loss to **2.548**, while module-wise Hessian eigenvector decomposition (Fig. 8) confirms the MLP subspace dominates the leading-curvature direction and that removing wd from MLP removes the steadily increasing curvature trend.
- **Generality across architectures and optimizers:** the phenomenon and its mitigation are reproduced on a 187M Transformer (AdamW), ResNet-50/CIFAR-100 (SGD), and small FNNs (SGD), spanning LN and BN, autoregressive and classification settings.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: High.** The framing of weight-norm criticality as a *complementary* axis to learning-rate criticality is fresh. The technical lift over prior work (e.g., Li et al. 2020b, Lyu et al. 2022) is the explicit per-component stability boundary c* = √(ηρ/2) and the gradient-direction spike variant, plus the extension to networks that are only *partially* scale invariant (containing normalization layers) rather than globally scale invariant.
- **Rigor: Strong.** Theorems are stated precisely with proofs in appendices; the α⁻² lower bound is verified empirically via controlled parameter rescaling (Fig. 5), and the boundary is tested against held-out training trajectories (Fig. 7). The module-wise eigenvector decomposition (Fig. 8) provides a causal-looking handle that is then intervened on (Fig. 9).
- **Reproducibility: Moderate-to-Good.** Architectures, optimizers, learning rates, and wd sweeps are specified; appendix references (A.1.1–A.1.5, A.4–A.7) are cited for full detail. The synthetic regression and MNIST FNN experiments are minimal and easy to replicate; the 187M-Transformer / 100B-token setting is heavier but standard. Code release is not explicitly promised in the main text.
- **Significance: Practical and conceptual.** The MLP-weight-decay ablation (Fig. 9) is a directly actionable recipe insight for LLM pretraining stability, and the per-layer c* monitor offers a diagnostics tool for attributing spikes to specific modules.
- **Red flags / caveats.** (i) The instability-detection pipeline relies on hand-tuned thresholds (merge gap <30 iterations, drop intervals <200 iterations); sensitivity to these choices is not reported. (ii) The stability boundary is grounded in GD/EoS theory, while the headline LLM experiments use AdamW — the gap between the GD-based theory and AdamW dynamics is acknowledged but not closed. (iii) The connection between spikes and generalization is explicitly left open. (iv) Fig. 9 reports only two final-loss numbers (2.720 vs 2.548) without variance or statistical significance.

## Strengths

- Clean, minimal probes (synthetic regression, MNIST ± normalization) that isolate the normalization × weight-decay interaction from confounders.
- A genuinely useful decomposition: per-layer / per-module boundaries turn a global phenomenon into an attributable, monitorable signal.
- Cross-architecture and cross-optimizer validation (BN+LN, SGD+AdamW, FNN+ResNet+Transformer).
- The MLP-decay ablation provides an immediately deployable mitigation for large-model pretraining.
- Honest framing as a *complement* to learning-rate criticality rather than a replacement, with explicit acknowledgment of open questions.

## Limitations

- The core theory is derived for GD on (partially) scale-invariant losses; the AdamW-based LLM experiments are only empirically aligned with the boundary, leaving a theory–optimizer gap.
- Empirical instability detection depends on ad hoc filtering thresholds (30-iteration merge, 200-iteration minimum) whose robustness is not analyzed.
- Only two final-loss values are reported for the headline MLP-decay ablation, with no variance/significance; longer/multiple-seed runs would strengthen the claim.
- Residual connections break strict layer-wise scale invariance (acknowledged — the authors rescale only Conv→BN weights), so the boundary is an approximation for ResNets.
- The paper does not address the implications of spikes for generalization, and notes that the mechanisms in fully general settings remain incompletely understood.

## Relevance

对关注大模型训练动力学与优化理论的研究者，本文提供了一条可落地、可监测的损失尖峰归因与缓解路径（逐层 c* 阈值与"关闭 MLP 权重衰减"的实用配方），与 Edge of Stability、Adam 尖峰等近期工作互补，对预训练稳定性与正则化调参具有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the weight-norm criticality boundary c* = √(ηρ/2) remain predictive under adaptive optimizers (AdamW, Lion, Sophia) if ρ is computed from the optimizer's effective preconditioned curvature rather than the raw Hessian?
- How does the phenomenon interact with other scale-invariance-inducing modules (RMSNorm, GroupNorm, weight-normalized layers, μParam scaling)?
- Can c* be used as an online monitor to adaptively schedule weight decay (decay-off when ∥u∥ approaches c*) and does this recover or exceed the gains of static MLP-decay exclusion?
- What is the variance and seed-sensitivity of the 2.720 → 2.548 final-loss improvement, and does the benefit persist at billion-parameter scale with realistic data mixes?
- Is there a connection between weight-norm-criticality-induced spikes and downstream generalization (the paper explicitly leaves this open)?
- Does scaling the residual-branch weights (rather than only Conv→BN) change the boundary's predictive power for ResNets where strict layer-wise scale invariance fails?
