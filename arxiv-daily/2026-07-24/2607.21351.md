> **一句话总结：** 本文用基于压缩的"比特写入"测量法量化了 LoRA 适配器的记忆容量——发现每个可训练参数仅存约 1.7–2.8 比特（远低于全量微调的 3.6），且容量更多取决于权重放在 MLP 还是 attention、以及冻结基座的结构，而非参数数量；并据此证明隐私泄露随写入比特而非参数量增长，且 GRPO 强化学习在同等精度下几乎不写入可记忆的秘密，而 SFT 会逐字抄录。

> **TL;DR:** Weighing LoRA adapters in *bits* (a compression-based memorization meter), the authors find adapters store only ~1.7–2.8 bits per trainable parameter on WikiText-pretrained 2M/8M GPT-style bases (vs ~3.07 for matched full fine-tuning and the 3.6 full-model line), and that capacity is governed by where weights sit (MLP holds nearly 2× per-parameter vs attention at a matched ~37K count) and what the frozen base knows (29% on a random base vs 98% on WikiText); on Qwen2.5-0.5B, canary exposure climbs ~11 bits only at the rank step that actually writes more bits (rank 16→64), and at matched accuracy GRPO writes ~0 excess bits where SFT writes +271–471 bits and copies planted secrets verbatim.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"适配器写入比特"测量协议**：把 Morris 等人的压缩式记忆测量法从全模型扩展到"冻结基座 + 可训练适配器"场景，给出三种互补读数——artifact cost CL(Δθ)（文件实际传输的比特）、behavior bits W(D)（最终策略的似然位移）、unintended memorization mem_U（相对参考编码器的压缩优势）。这套协议以随机字符串探针标定（64 个均匀 token，词表 2048，每条 704 比特，无任何可预测性），确保读出的每比特都是真正"记住"的比特。
- **给出与"经验之谈"相反的量化结论**：适配器确实远比全量微调"漏"（每参数 1.7–2.8 vs 3.6 比特），但容量并非参数数量的属性——同等 ~37K 可训练参数下，MLP 适配器每参数容量 2.43 比特，是 attention-only 的 1.30 比特的近两倍；bf16 仅损失约 1% 记忆而文件减半，fp16 几乎无损。
- **将比特读数与真实隐私泄露挂钩**：在 Qwen2.5-0.5B 上植入 canary，证明 rank 4→16（参数 4 倍但写入比特几乎不变）泄露不动，而 rank 16→64（写入比特增半）使频繁重复的秘密直接被提取（exposure 升 ~11 比特）。参数量在两步中同等增长，却无法区分两者——**写入比特才是泄露的真正预测量**。
- **首次比特级对比 SFT vs GRPO**：在匹配精度下，GRPO 适配器（binary/graded/noisy 奖励均如此）的 excess 训练集比特为零，而 SFT 在学会任务后仍持续写入（+96 → +271 bits）。植入八位数秘密测试中，SFT 达到 rank-1 提取（exposure 12–13 bits），GRPO 全部留在随机水平（exposure 1.1 bits），为"RLVR 诱发而非注入"的争论给出比特级证据。
- **提供可前置的隐私工程目标**：由于容量可在训练前计算，从业者可把数据集信息量与适配器容量直接比对，落在"总记忆"区间还是"稀释"区间，并据此选择 rank、放置位置、基座结构——而非训练后再攻击。

## Key Contributions

- A compression-based measurement protocol for PEFT that reads memorization directly in **bits**, extending Morris et al. (2026b) into the frozen-base regime and defining three complementary quantities (artifact cost `CL(Δθ)`, behavior-write bits `W`, unintended memorization `mem_U`) calibrated on uniformly random strings so every recovered bit is provably a stored bit.
- The empirical finding that LoRA adapters hold **only ~1.7–2.8 bits per trainable parameter** versus the 3.6 line for full models and ≥3.07 for budget-matched full fine-tuning — and that this per-parameter number is governed by **where** the weights live and **what** the frozen base represents, not by how many weights there are.
- A demonstration on Qwen2.5-0.5B that **canary exposure tracks written bits, not parameter count**: the rank 16→64 step is where leakage jumps (~11 bits of exposure, repeated secrets extractable) because that is where bits jump, while the rank 4→16 step moves parameters but neither bits nor leakage.
- The **first bit-level comparison of SFT vs GRPO** at matched accuracy, showing GRPO writes essentially zero excess bits (across binary/graded/noisy rewards) while SFT keeps writing after it has learned the task, and that planted secrets SFT copies verbatim (rank-1 extraction, exposure 12–13 bits) GRPO never records (exposure 1.1 bits).
- A privacy-engineering reframing: because capacity is computable before any gradient step, one can weigh an adaptation set's information content against the adapter's measured capacity in advance and choose rank/placement/base to design *against* leakage rather than audit it afterward.

## Methodology

The instrument is a **compression-based memorization meter**. Codelength is the negative log-likelihood of a sequence under a model (realized by arithmetic coding). Unintended memorization is the bits a tuned model shaves off a reference: `mem_U(x) = H_K(x|θ_ref) − min(H_K(x|θ_ref), H_K(x|θ̂))`. To make the reference exact, the probe trains on **uniformly random 64-token sequences over a 2048-symbol vocabulary** (704 bits each, nothing predictable), so every saved bit is a stored bit; held-out random sequences must read `mem_U ≈ 0` (a check that passes across the whole spine).

Three readings answer different questions:

1. **Artifact cost `CL(Δθ)`** — the entropy-coded size of the quantized weight update itself; what a hub or federated aggregator physically transmits.
2. **Behavior-write bits `W(D)`** — the likelihood shift on canonical answers, defined on the final policy alone, which makes SFT and RL adapters comparable.
3. **`mem_U`** on random data — the ground-truth capacity meter.

A clean capacity reading requires optimization, not storage, to stop being the bottleneck. The protocol fixes a **16,000-step budget with no early stopping**, sweeps the learning rate and reports the envelope, and places dataset sizes relative to estimated capacity. Extending to 40,000 steps lifts readings by only 10–24%, so every number is read as a conservative lower bound. Bases are GPT-style decoders (2M and 8M non-embedding parameters) pretrained on WikiText-103 then frozen; adapters are LoRA and weight-tied TinyLoRA. The untouched base bounds each reading from below, full fine-tuning of the same architecture bounds it from above.

For the privacy audit, the authors fine-tune Qwen2.5-0.5B on a document corpus with planted **secret-sharer canaries** (short random secrets at controlled repetition), measuring exposure as the log-rank of the true secret against 10,000 decoys, plus loss-based and Min-K% membership inference. For the SFT-vs-GRPO study they hold base and rank-4 LoRA fixed and train both arms to matched accuracy on 3-digit arithmetic and key-value extraction (SFT on gold answers, GRPO on a verifiable reward), then add a planted eight-digit-code secret that appears only as a supervised target, never in any prompt.

## Results & Highlights

- **Adapters store ~1.7–2.8 bits per trainable parameter** (Table 1): the 2M-base rank-16 LoRA (196,608 params) reaches 540K peak `mem_U` at `α̂_ad = 2.75`; the 2M rank-1 (12,288 params) reads 21.1K bits at `α̂_ad = 1.71`. Budget-matched full fine-tuning of the same 2.2M-param base stores **6.81M bits, ≥3.07 bits per parameter** — confirming the adapter falls well short of the full-model 3.6-bit line.
- **Placement dominates parameter count**: at a fixed ~37K trainable parameters, per-parameter capacity runs **1.30 (attention-only) → 2.43 (MLP-only) bits**, a near-doubling with parameter count held flat (Figure 3, left). bf16 casting costs ~1% of memorization while halving the file; fp16 costs essentially nothing.
- **The frozen base sets the ceiling**: the same LoRA memorizes **29% of its data on a randomly initialized base, 79% on a structureless-synthetic base, and 98% on a WikiText-pretrained base**, while full fine-tuning of the random base reaches 99% — so the shortfall is specific to writing through frozen weights (Figure 4).
- **Leakage tracks written bits, not parameter count** on Qwen2.5-0.5B (Figure 5): the rank 4→16 step quadruples parameters but barely moves bits or exposure; the rank 16→64 step quadruples parameters again, written bits jump ~50%, and **canary exposure climbs ~11 bits** with the frequently-repeated secrets becoming extractable outright (rank 1 among 10,000 decoys).
- **GRPO writes essentially nothing where SFT keeps writing** (Table 2): on 3-digit arithmetic at matched accuracy (~92–95%), SFT's excess training-set bits climb **+96 → +271** from 300→800 steps; GRPO (binary/graded/noisy) sits at **−35 to −56** — zero within estimator noise. On the secret-injection test, SFT reaches **rank-1 extraction with exposure 12.0–13.3 bits**, while GRPO leaves every secret at chance (**exposure 1.1 bits**).
- Excluding the planted records drops the SFT adapter's measured surplus from ~5,000 bits to essentially nothing, showing the surplus is the secrets themselves, not a general memorization habit.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper has three authors spanning **Carnegie Mellon University** (Kaizhen Tan) and **Columbia University** (Heqing Du; Yang Feng). It directly extends the compression-based memorization framework of Morris et al. (2026b) — "How Much Do Language Models Memorize?" (ICML 2026) — and engages the same line that produced "Learning to Reason in 13 Parameters" (Morris et al. 2026a), placing the work inside an active, high-profile research conversation on memorization and what RLVR actually deposits in weights. The methodological care is notable: a declared optimization budget with no early stopping, learning-rate envelopes, dataset grids placed relative to estimated capacity, and a random-string probe that doubles as a calibration sanity check (held-out sequences read zero across the whole spine). Quantitative claims are consistently bracketed with seed spreads (2–10% in Table 1) and conservative lower-bound framing. The accompanying harness and measurement protocol are offered as a released artifact.

## Critical Assessment

The paper's central move — turning memorization from a post-hoc attack into a pre-training quantity — is genuinely useful, and the dissociations it reports are non-obvious. The capacity plateau reading (~1.7–2.8 bits/param) survives a matched budget against full fine-tuning, and the placement result (1.30 vs 2.43 bits/param at fixed ~37K count) is a clean experimental design that rules out the obvious "more parameters = more room" confound. The base-substrate result (29% random → 98% WikiText) is the most conceptually important: it reframes adapter capacity as a joint property of update and substrate, and the authors are honest that a bits-per-parameter number measured on one base should not be transferred to another.

Two honest edges the authors themselves flag deserve weight in any assessment. First, the SFT-vs-GRPO secret test is a **stress test, not an accuracy-matched comparison** (SFT reaches perfect accuracy where GRPO spans the high nineties), so it shows what each method *does* with a memorable target rather than a controlled bitrate; the elicitation-vs-injection claim is therefore directional, not decisive. Second, **loss-based and Min-K% membership inference sit at chance across the entire rank sweep** at the corpus size used — only extraction resolves the difference, and whether bits predict membership at larger scale is explicitly left open. The reward-entropy dial that the "13-parameter" conjecture predicts would move GRPO's writing does not measurably do so, because on these tasks GRPO operates far below any entropy bound. Finally, the capacity numbers themselves are lower bounds at a declared budget (extending to 40K steps lifts them 10–24%), and they are obtained on small GPT-style bases (2M/8M) plus a single 0.5B Qwen audit — generalization to production-scale adapters is asserted by protocol portability rather than demonstrated across scales.

## Strengths

- Reframes a folkloric privacy question ("is a LoRA a harmless diff?") into a measurable, pre-training quantity, with a calibration (random strings) that gives the scale a defensible ground truth.
- Clean experimental dissociations: placement (MLP vs attention) and base substrate (random vs WikiText) each move capacity while parameter count is held flat, ruling out the trivial confound.
- The bit-level SFT-vs-GRPO comparison is a genuinely new piece of evidence in the elicitation-vs-injection debate, and the secret-injection asymmetry (SFT copies, GRPO never does even when the secret is made learnable) is a sharp, mechanism-level result.
- The privacy engineering is actionable and concrete: the smallest *privacy-protective* rank is the smallest that still writes the data, and placement offers a second lever — both are knobs practitioners already turn for accuracy.
- Conservative by construction: every capacity is a stated-budget lower bound, so a measurement can understate but never inflate what an adapter holds — the safe direction for a privacy claim.

## Limitations

- **Scale**: capacity numbers come from 2M/8M GPT-style bases pretrained on WikiText-103, with the privacy audit on a single 0.5B Qwen model. Production 7B-scale claims (the "ten megabytes of text" back-of-envelope) rest on extrapolation, not direct measurement.
- **Membership inference under-resolved**: at the corpus size used, MIA sits at chance for every rank, so the bits→leakage link is established only for extraction, not for membership — the authors flag this as a question their corpus is too small to answer.
- **SFT-vs-GRPO is a stress test, not a controlled bitrate**: SFT reaches perfect accuracy on the secret task where GRPO spans the high nineties, so the asymmetry shows what each method does with a memorable target rather than a matched-information comparison.
- **Random strings are a calibration, not a threat model**: they isolate storage by stripping away everything learnable; the real-text leakage argument is carried by separate canary/extraction audits rather than by the random-string meter itself.
- **Reward-entropy dial does not move GRPO**: the mechanism the "13-parameter" conjecture predicts would change GRPO's writing is not observed here (the bound is slack on these tasks), leaving the conjecture's quantitative form untested.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接关系到所有把 LoRA 适配器作为可分享资产的工作流——HuggingFace 式模型中心、联邦学习客户端上传、团队间任务适配器交换。它给出一个可前置计算的"数据携带量"指标，让数据所有者、平台和监管方在分发适配器前就能评估其潜在泄露面，对医疗/客服/法律等敏感语料的微调场景尤其重要。它也为当前 RLVR 社区"SFT 记忆、RL 泛化"的争论提供首个比特级证据，对设计隐私友好的后训练 pipeline（优先用 GRPO 而非 SFT 处理敏感数据）有直接指导意义。对做 PEFT 隐私、模型水印与成员推断攻击、以及 RL 理论的研究者，这是一篇方法论与实证都值得借鉴的工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the per-parameter capacity numbers (1.7–2.8 bits) and the MLP-vs-attention placement gap hold at 7B–70B scale, or does the substrate's representational width saturate the effect?
- Can the bits→leakage link be extended to **membership inference** at larger corpus sizes where MIA resolves above chance, and does the placement lever protect membership as cleanly as it protects extraction?
- What happens when the reward-entropy dial is actually engaged — i.e., on tasks where GRPO operates near an entropy bound rather than far below it? Does GRPO then start writing measurable bits?
- Does the substrate-dependence result (29% random → 98% WikiText) imply that publishing a *measured capacity alongside a checkpoint* (as the authors suggest) requires also publishing/characterizing the frozen base, and how should hubs standardize this?
- Could the same compression meter be applied to other PEFT forms (VeRA, DoRA, prefix tuning) to produce a unified "writable bits" leaderboard that is more privacy-meaningful than parameter count?
- How does adapter capacity interact with **differentially private fine-tuning** (Yu et al. 2022) — does DP-SGD's noise clamp manifest as a predictable, designable reduction in measured writable bits?
