> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 CM-LRS，一个面向资本市场工作流输出层的 LLM 可靠性评估指标，从七个维度对四款模型在五类典型投行工作流上打分，发现三家闭源前沿模型可靠性几乎无差异，开源模型主要在检索与综合类任务上掉队，而"决策可用性"维度是区分生产可用性最干净的信号。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces CM-LRS, a 7-dimension rubric that scores LLM outputs at the workflow-output layer across five capital-markets tasks (DCM extraction, precedent retrieval, issuer-profile synthesis, M&A comparable reasoning, ECM extraction) using four cross-family LLM judges; the three frontier closed-source models (Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.5) cluster within a 0.22-point band (4.31 / 4.30 / 4.09) on a 5-point scale while the open-weights Llama 3.3 70B trails by ~1.16 points at 3.15, with the gap concentrated on retrieval/synthesis workflows and Decision Usefulness (D6) showing the widest cross-model dispersion (4.0-point spread on issuer-profile).

## 核心贡献

1. **定义了七个工作流输出层可靠性维度**：事实准确性（D1）、证据可追溯性（D2）、数值一致性（D3）、工作流完整性（D4）、来源纪律（D5）、决策可用性（D6）、可审查性/可审计性（D7），每个维度采用 0–5 分锚定评分，并给出可调权重的聚合公式。

2. **提出资本市场工作流分类法**：抽取（Extraction）、检索（Retrieval）、综合（Synthesis）、比较与推理（Comparison & Reasoning）、起草（Drafting）五类，每类对应默认维度权重，将评估与具体工作流绑定而非一刀切。

3. **构建五个公开可复现的示范工作流**：W1 DCM 债券条款抽取（15 份 SEC EDGAR + 3 份合成北欧高收益债）、W2 先例检索、W3 发行人画像综合（Tesla/Equinix/Spotify/Ferrari/CME）、W4 M&A 可比交易推理（以 Synopsys/Ansys 为标的，20 笔可比交易）、W5 ECM 股权条款抽取；全部基于公开或合成材料。

4. **采用四评委、跨三个模型家族的交叉验证协议**：Sonnet 4.6 与 GPT-5.5（面板内）、Haiku 4.5 与 Gemini 2.5 Pro（面板外），用于控制自评与家族偏差，对 104 个输出进行评分。

5. **公开发布全部评分细则、提示词、示范任务与原始输出**（CC BY 4.0），支持从业者扩展与批判。

## Key Contributions

1. **Formalises seven workflow-output reliability dimensions** — Factual Accuracy (D1), Evidence Traceability (D2), Numerical Consistency (D3), Workflow Completeness (D4), Source Discipline (D5), Decision Usefulness (D6), Reviewability/Auditability (D7) — each scored 0–5 against reviewer-anchored rubrics, with a weight-tunable aggregate formula.

2. **Introduces a capital-markets workflow taxonomy** (Extraction, Retrieval, Synthesis, Comparison & Reasoning, Drafting) that maps each class to default dimension weights, binding evaluation choices to the workflow rather than treating LLM evaluation as one-size-fits-all.

3. **Builds five reproducible demonstration workflows** entirely on public-domain or synthetic material: W1 DCM terms extraction (15 SEC EDGAR prospectus supplements + 3 synthetic Nordic term sheets), W2 precedent retrieval, W3 issuer-profile synthesis (Tesla, Equinix, Spotify, Ferrari, CME), W4 M&A transaction-comparable reasoning (Synopsys/Ansys target with a 20-deal comparable corpus), W5 ECM terms extraction (5 IPOs, 3 follow-ons, 2 convertibles + 2 synthetic Nordic deals).

4. **Employs a four-judge, three-model-family cross-validation protocol** — Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.5 (in-panel), Haiku 4.5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro (out-of-panel) — to control for self-judge and family bias across 104 scored outputs.

5. **Releases all rubrics, prompts, demonstration tasks, raw outputs, and a deterministic verification script** under CC BY 4.0 to support extension and critique.

## Methodology

CM-LRS scores each (workflow, model) output on seven dimensions using a 0–5 ordinal rubric (Table 2 anchors: 0 = Unusable/fabricated; 5 = Production-grade). The aggregate is a weighted mean `CM-LRS = Σ wᵢ·sᵢ ∈ [0,5]` where weights sum to 1; default weights are given per workflow class (e.g., Extraction emphasises D3 Numerical = 0.25; Retrieval emphasises D2 Traceability = 0.30; Drafting emphasises D6 Use = 0.20). Headline scoring uses equal weighting for simplicity, since the frontier-vs-open-weights gap is large enough that no plausible weight vector inverts it. A deployment-readiness gate of CM-LRS ≥ 4.0 with no dimension below 3 is recommended for high-stakes regulated workflows.

**Models evaluated** (all at temperature 0): Claude Opus 4.7 (Radical gateway), GPT-5.5 (Codex CLI, reasoning effort high), Claude Sonnet 4.6 (production workhorse), Llama 3.3 70B Instruct (Groq). All documents are pre-processed to a fixed 6,000-token budget per document so all four models receive identical inputs — a deliberate constraint that biases the test toward cover-page/front-matter failure modes.

**Judging protocol**: LLM-as-judge (Sonnet 4.6 primary) following Zheng et al. (2023) and Liu et al. (2023), with three additional cross-family judges. The author positions the rubric as reflecting what banker/analyst/compliance review actually catches in UK, Nordic, and US capital markets, and frames the LLM-judge protocol as a reproducible baseline to be later calibrated against human raters, not a substitute.

**Guiding deployment principle**: "predict with the LLM, calculate with code, decide with the human" — numerical claims should trace to deterministic computation, not token prediction.

## Results & Highlights

- **Frontier cluster within 0.22 points** (four-judge averaged CM-LRS across all five workflows): Claude Sonnet 4.6 = 4.31, Claude Opus 4.7 = 4.30, GPT-5.5 = 4.09, versus Llama 3.3 70B = 3.15 — a 0.94–1.16 point open-weights gap that all four judges (spanning three model families) consistently rank last.

- **Open-weights gap is workflow-class-specific** (primary-judge per-cell, Table 5): Llama trails Sonnet by 2.23 points on W2 retrieval (2.51 vs 4.74) and by 2.15 points on W3 issuer-profile synthesis (2.33 vs GPT-5.5's 4.48), but only by 0.84 points on W1 single-document DCM extraction (4.11 vs 4.95) — i.e., the gap is concentrated on retrieval/synthesis, not extraction.

- **D6 Decision Usefulness shows the widest cross-model dispersion** in any cell: on W3 issuer profile, GPT-5.5 scores 5.00 while Opus and Sonnet score 2.67 and Llama scores 1.00 under the primary judge — a 4.0-point spread — while simultaneously sitting in the top tier of inter-judge agreement (mean Pearson r = 0.52 across six judge pairs, with the strongest cross-family agreement Sonnet–GPT-5.5 at r = 0.82 on D6).

- **Inter-judge agreement signals family-bias matters**: pairwise Spearman ρ on the 20 per-cell aggregates ranges from Sonnet–Haiku ρ = 0.94 (same family) down to GPT-5.5–Gemini ρ = 0.03 (two cross-family judges sharing neither panel- nor family-overlap — "effectively independent rankings"), the strongest single empirical signal that a single-judge protocol would not have surfaced.

- **Cost/latency trade-off (Table 4)**: Opus 4.7 = 18 s / $0.20–0.30 per call (CM-LRS 4.30); GPT-5.5 high-reasoning = 84 s / $0.02–0.04 (4.09); Sonnet 4.6 = 21 s / $0.04–0.06 (4.31, the cost-latency-reliability sweet spot); Llama 3.3 70B = 10 s / $0.004–0.008 (3.15). GPT-5.5 with high reasoning effort is materially slower per call, a factor in agentic/batch settings.

- **Deployment-relevant takeaway for buyers**: at the current frontier the choice between Sonnet, Opus, and GPT-5.5 is determined by cost, latency, and workflow-class fit — not headline reliability.

## Critical Assessment

The paper's central methodological bet — scoring at the workflow-output layer rather than the QA-pair layer — is well-motivated and the failure-mode taxonomy (D1–D7) maps cleanly onto what compliance review actually rejects. Three points merit scrutiny:

1. **Automated judging is the load-bearing weakness.** Despite the four-judge, three-family design, this is still an LLM-as-judge study, not a human-rater study. The author flags this honestly (Limitation 1; Section 5.4) and notes GPT-5.5 grades 0.3–1.1 points stricter than Sonnet and Gemini grades GPT-5.5 specifically lower (mean 3.38 vs 4.19–4.44 from the other judges). The headline in-cluster ranking among the three frontier models is judge-dependent (Sonnet first under Sonnet/Haiku judges, GPT-5.5 first under self, Opus first under Gemini) — so the 0.22-point cluster claim is real, but the precise ordering inside it is not stable.

2. **The 6,000-token truncation is the single biggest external-validity threat** (acknowledged in Section 5.2 and Limitation 5). By design all four models see identical front-matter-biased inputs, which means failure modes that surface 90 pages into a prospectus (a buried covenant, a deep risk-factor paragraph) are not exercised. Per-cell scores should be read as "front-matter reliability," not full-document reliability — a production deployment using chunking/retrieval could shift the rankings.

3. **The 4.0-point D6 dispersion on W3 (issuer profile) is a single-workflow, single-judge-per-cell measurement.** It is a striking and useful signal, and D6's top-tier inter-judge agreement (r̄ = 0.52) supports its diagnostic value, but the strongest evidence (the 5.00 vs 1.00 split) comes from the primary judge's per-cell table; the four-judge aggregate would tighten the extremes. The author is careful to frame D6 as the "cleanest" rather than the "highest-agreement" dimension, and the reasoning (dispersion × agreement) is sound.

The position is internally consistent: CM-LRS is presented as a deployment-readiness metric complementary to QA benchmarks, not a replacement, and the author does not overclaim exhaustiveness.

## Strengths

- Operates at the **workflow-output layer** where deployment decisions are actually made, filling a genuine gap left by FinanceBench/FinQA/ConvFinQA (QA-pair) and RAGAS (component-level).
- **Four-judge, three-family cross-validation** is a meaningfully stronger bias-control design than typical single-judge studies; the GPT-5.5–Gemini ρ = 0.03 finding is itself useful evidence about LLM-as-judge methodology.
- **Fully public and reproducible**: prompts, rubrics, worked anchors, raw outputs, and a deterministic verification script under CC BY 4.0 — rare for a finance-domain evaluation.
- **Workflow taxonomy with per-class default weights** gives practitioners an actionable starting point and an honest acknowledgement that equal-weight headline scoring is a simplification.
- **Practitioner-grounded rubric anchors** tied to what banker/analyst/compliance review actually catches, with explicit positionality disclosure.
- Clean, falsifiable deployment recommendation (CM-LRS ≥ 4.0, no dimension < 3) that slots into NIST AI RMF / EU AI Act / ISO 42001 governance frameworks.

## Limitations

- **Automated judging, no human raters**: the priority extension is inter-rater reliability against practising bankers/compliance reviewers; until then the absolute scores are an LLM-judge baseline.
- **Five workflows do not span the full taxonomy** — drafting workflows (pitch evidence, compliance memos) are absent.
- **Tight, non-exhaustive model panel**: Google's Gemini family is absent from the scored models (it appears only as a judge); alternative open-weights families (Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek) are not evaluated.
- **W4 depth heterogeneity**: 18 of 20 comparable transactions are full DEFM14A/SC 13E-3 proxies (200+ pages) but 2 (Adobe/Figma, Thoma Bravo/Darktrace) are announcement-class HTML; scoring adjusts rather than penalises, which is defensible but introduces cell-level asymmetry.
- **6,000-token truncation** biases results toward cover-page/front-matter content; deep-document failure modes are untested.
- **Single-turn, no agentic/tool-use workflows** — the rubric is compatible but the harness does not exercise tool calls, code execution, or retrieval pipelines.
- **Equal-weighted aggregate ignores failure-cost asymmetry** (a wrong coupon vs an omitted ESG paragraph carry very different costs); default weights are practitioner-informed, not empirically derived.
- **Ordinal rubric subjectivity**; no binary sub-criteria checklists yet.
- **Static frozen corpus** will need refreshing as models advance; no adversarial-robustness evaluation.
- **Domain scope**: designed for capital markets; adjacent regulated domains (credit risk, asset management, insurance) would require a domain-tuned variant.
- CM-LRS does not cover MNPI handling, information-barrier integrity, or trade-surveillance/market-abuse controls — these remain compliance-framework responsibilities.

## Relevance

对评估方向的研究者与金融/受监管行业的 LLM 落地团队都有较高参考价值。对于评估方法学，它把"评委间一致性 × 跨模型离散度"作为挑选关键维度的判据（而非单看一致性），并以 GPT-5.5–Gemini ρ=0.03 这一实例揭示了单评委协议的盲区，是一个可借鉴的 LLM-as-judge 实验设计范本。对于 RAG/检索增强生成方向，CM-LRS 明确定位在 RAG-component 评估（如 RAGAS）的下游——先评估组件可靠性，再用 CM-LRS 评估工作流输出是否"可入册"，构成互补的两层评估。对于金融 AI 治理从业者，它给出了可直接嵌入 NIST AI RMF 的 measure 功能与 EU AI Act 第 9/13 条合规证据的可落地指标，以及一条明确的部署门槛（CM-LRS ≥ 4.0 且无维度低于 3）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do CM-LRS scores and the D6 dispersion pattern change when documents are routed through a full chunking/retrieval pipeline instead of being truncated to 6,000 tokens — does the open-weights gap on retrieval/synthesis workflows widen or narrow?
- What is the inter-rater reliability between the four LLM judges and a panel of practising bankers/compliance reviewers, and does D6 retain its "cleanest separator" status under human scoring?
- How does adding Google's Gemini family and additional open-weights models (Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek) to the scored panel affect the frontier-cluster and open-weights-gap findings?
- Can dimension weights be empirically derived from revealed-preference regression on real reviewer accept/reject decisions, and would that change the deployment gate?
- Does CM-LRS generalise to agentic/tool-use workflows where a model calls search and code-execution tools, and do D3 (Numerical Consistency) and D7 (Reviewability) scores improve when numerical claims are forced through deterministic code computation?
- How should CM-LRS be adapted for drafting-class workflows (pitch evidence, compliance memos) and for adjacent regulated domains (credit risk, asset management, insurance)?
